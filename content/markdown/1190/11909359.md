# Timeline of algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Timeline of algebra"}

The following is a timeline of key developments of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923):

:::

::: {#o2 .table cue=table}

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| c. 1800 BC | An [Old Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Babylonian_dynasty) tablet (Strasbourg 363) seeks the solution of a [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175).[^o3] |
| c. 1800 BC | The *[Plimpton 322](wikipedia://en/2983547)* tablet gives a table of [Pythagorean triples](wikipedia://en/24172) in [Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia) [Cuneiform script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform_script).[^o4] |
| 1800 BC | [Berlin Papyrus 6619](wikipedia://en/4233960) (19th dynasty) contains a [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175) and its solution.[^o5][^o6] |
| 800 BC | [Baudhayana](wikipedia://en/47408155), author of the Baudhayana [Sulba Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulba_Sutras), a [Vedic Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_Sanskrit) geometric text, contains quadratic equations, and calculates the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682) correct to five [decimal places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_place) |
| c. 300 BC | [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s *[Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)* gives a geometric construction with Euclidean tools for the solution of the quadratic equation for positive real roots.[^o7] |
| c. 300 BC | A geometric construction for the solution of the cubic is sought (doubling the cube problem). It is now well known that the general cubic has no such solution using [Euclidean tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_tools). |
| 150 BC | [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) mathematicians in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India) write the “Sthananga Sutra”, which contains work on the [theory of numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_numbers), arithmetical operations, [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), operations with [fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractions), simple equations, [cubic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_equations), [quartic equations](wikipedia://en/251591), and [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) and [combinations](wikipedia://en/5308). |
| 250 BC | Algebraic equations are treated in the Chinese mathematics book *[Jiuzhang suanshu](wikipedia://en/204207)* (*The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art*), which contains solutions of linear equations solved using the [rule of double false position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_position_method), geometric solutions of quadratic equations, and the solutions of matrices equivalent to the modern method, to solve systems of [simultaneous linear equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_linear_equations).[^o8] |
| 1st century AD | [Hero of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/68274) gives the earliest fleeting reference to [square roots of negative numbers](wikipedia://en/52358). |
| c. 150 | Greek mathematician [Hero of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/68274), treats algebraic equations in three volumes of mathematics. |
| c. 200 | Hellenistic mathematician [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110), who lived in Alexandria and is often considered to be the "father of algebra", writes his famous *[Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetica)*, a work featuring solutions of algebraic equations and on the theory of numbers. |
| 499 | Indian mathematician [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204), in his treatise *Aryabhatiya*, obtains whole-number solutions to linear equations, describes the general solution of the indeterminate linear equation. |
| c. 625 | Chinese mathematician [Wang Xiaotong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xiaotong) finds numerical solutions to certain cubic equations. |
| c. 7th century Dates vary from the 3rd to the 12th centuries.[^n1] | The *[Bakhshali Manuscript](wikipedia://en/1848052#Bakhshali_Manuscript)* written in [ancient India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_kingdoms_of_India) uses a form of algebraic notation using letters of the alphabet and other signs, and contains cubic and quartic equations, algebraic solutions of [linear equations](wikipedia://en/17570) with up to five unknowns, the general algebraic formula for the quadratic equation, and solutions of indeterminate quadratic equations and simultaneous equations. |
| 7th century | [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206) invents the method of solving indeterminate equations of the second degree . He also develops methods for calculations of the motions and places of various planets, their rising and setting, conjunctions, and the calculation of eclipses of the sun and the moon |
| 628 | [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206) writes the *[Brahmasphuta-siddhanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmasphutasiddhanta)*, where zero is clearly explained, and where the modern [place-value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place-value) [Indian numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_numerals) system is fully developed. It also gives rules for manipulating both [negative and positive numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_and_positive_numbers), methods for computing [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208), methods of solving [linear](wikipedia://en/17570) and [quadratic equations](wikipedia://en/25175), and rules for summing [series](wikipedia://en/15287), [Brahmagupta's identity](wikipedia://en/5161105), and the [Brahmagupta theorem](wikipedia://en/1884241) |
| 8th century | [Virasena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virasena) gives explicit rules for the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918), gives the derivation of the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of a [frustum](wikipedia://en/89405) using an [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) procedure, and also deals with the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) to [base 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_2) |
| c. 800 | The [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) patrons of learning, [al-Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mansur), [Haroun al-Raschid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haroun_al-Raschid), and [al-Mamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mamun), has Greek, Babylonian, and Indian mathematical and scientific works translated into Arabic and begins a cultural, scientific and mathematical awakening after a century devoid of mathematical achievements.[^n2] |
| 820 | The word *algebra* is derived from operations described in the treatise written by the [Persian mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_in_medieval_Islam), [Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Ḵhwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Musa_al-Khwarizmi), titled *[Al-Kitab al-Jabr wa-l-Muqabala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing)* (meaning "The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing") on the systematic solution of [linear](wikipedia://en/17570) and quadratic equations. Al-Khwarizmi is sometimes credited with the "father of algebra" by some, for treating algebra as an independent discipline and for introducing the methods of "[reduction](wikipedia://en/1033865)" and "balancing" (the transposition of subtracted terms to the other side of an equation, that is, the cancellation of like terms on opposite sides of the equation) which was what he originally used the term *al-jabr* to refer to.[^n3] His algebra was also no longer concerned "with a series of [problems](wikipedia://en/256700) to be resolved, but an [exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expository_writing) which starts with primitive terms in which the combinations must give all possible prototypes for equations, which henceforward explicitly constitute the true object of study." He also studied an equation for its own sake and "in a generic manner, insofar as it does not simply emerge in the course of solving a problem, but is specifically called on to define an infinite class of problems."[^o13] |
| c. 990 | Persian mathematician [Al-Karaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karaji) (also known as al-Karkhi), in his treatise *Al-Fakhri*, further develops algebra by extending Al-Khwarizmi's methodology to incorporate integral powers and integral roots of unknown quantities. He replaces geometrical operations of algebra with modern arithmetical operations, and defines the [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416) x, x<sup>2</sup>, x<sup>3</sup>, .. and 1/x, 1/x<sup>2</sup>, 1/x<sup>3</sup>, .. and gives rules for the products of any two of these.[^o15] He also discovers the first numerical solution to equations of the form ax<sup>2n</sup> + bx<sup>n</sup> = c.[^n4] Al-Karaji is also regarded as the first person to free algebra from [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) operations and replace them with the type of [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) operations which are at the core of algebra today. His work on algebra and [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000), gave the rules for arithmetic operations to manipulate polynomials. The [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) F. Woepcke, in *Extrait du Fakhri, traité d'Algèbre par Abou Bekr Mohammed Ben Alhacan Alkarkhi* ([Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), 1853), praised Al-Karaji for being "the first who introduced the theory of algebraic [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176)". Stemming from this, Al-Karaji investigated [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficients) and [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497). |
| 895 | [Thabit ibn Qurra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabit_ibn_Qurra): the only surviving fragment of his original work contains a chapter on the solution and properties of [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787). He also generalized the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034), and discovered the [theorem](wikipedia://en/1817053) by which pairs of [amicable numbers](wikipedia://en/3259) can be found, (i.e., two numbers such that each is the sum of the proper divisors of the other). |
| 953 | [Al-Karaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karaji) may be regarded as the first person to free [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) from geometrical operations and to replace them with the arithmetical type of operations which are at the core of algebra today. He defined the [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416) $x$, $x^2$, $x^3$, … and $1/x$, $1/x^2$, $1/x^3$, … and to give rules for [products](wikipedia://en/24977) of any two of these. He start\[s\] a school of algebra which flourished for several hundreds of years”. He also discovers the [binomial theorem](wikipedia://en/4677) for [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent), which “was a major factor in the development of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) based on the decimal system.” |
| c. 1000 | [Abū Sahl al-Qūhī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_Sahl_al-Qūhī) (Kuhi) solves [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) higher than the [second degree](wikipedia://en/25175). |
| c. 1050 | Chinese mathematician [Jia Xian](wikipedia://en/15141098) finds numerical solutions of polynomial equations of arbitrary degree. |
| 1070 | [Omar Khayyám](wikipedia://en/92550) begins to write *Treatise on Demonstration of Problems of Algebra* and classifies cubic equations. |
| 1072 | Persian mathematician [Omar Khayyám](wikipedia://en/92550) gives a complete classification of cubic equations with positive roots and gives general geometric solutions to these equations found by means of intersecting conic sections.[^n5] |
| 12th century | [Bhaskara Acharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskara_II) writes the “[Bijaganita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijaganita)” (“[Algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923)”), which is the first text that recognizes that a positive number has two square roots |
| 1130 | [Al-Samawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Samawal) gives a definition of algebra: “\[it is concerned\] with operating on unknowns using all the arithmetical tools, in the same way as the arithmetician operates on the known.”[^o18] |
| c. 1200 | [Sharaf al-Dīn al-Tūsī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharaf_al-Dīn_al-Tūsī) (1135–1213) writes the *Al-Mu'adalat* (*Treatise on Equations*), which deals with eight types of cubic equations with positive solutions and five types of cubic equations which may not have positive solutions. He uses what would later be known as the "[Ruffini](wikipedia://en/766014)–[Horner](wikipedia://en/14263) method" to [numerically](wikipedia://en/21506) approximate the [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) of a cubic equation. He also develops the concepts of the [maxima and minima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima) of curves in order to solve cubic equations which may not have positive solutions. He understands the importance of the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) of the cubic equation and uses an early version of [Cardano](wikipedia://en/13145)'s formula[^o20] to find algebraic solutions to certain types of cubic equations. Some scholars, such as Roshdi Rashed, argue that Sharaf al-Din discovered the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of cubic polynomials and realized its significance, while other scholars connect his solution to the ideas of Euclid and Archimedes.[^o21] |
| 1202 | [Leonardo Fibonacci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Fibonacci) of [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) publishes his *[Liber Abaci](wikipedia://en/167699)*, a work on algebra that introduces Arabic numerals to Europe.[^o22] |
| c. 1300 | Chinese mathematician [Zhu Shijie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Shijie) deals with [polynomial algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_algebra), solves quadratic equations, simultaneous equations and equations with up to four unknowns, and numerically solves some quartic, [quintic](wikipedia://en/303368) and higher-order polynomial equations.[^o23] |
| c. 1400 | Indian mathematician [Madhava of Sangamagrama](wikipedia://en/2793007) documents infinite series approximations of trigonometry functions.[^o24] |
| 15th century | [Nilakantha Somayaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilakantha_Somayaji), a [Kerala school](wikipedia://en/3099132) mathematician, writes the “Aryabhatiya Bhasya”, which contains work on infinite-series expansions, problems of algebra, and spherical geometry |
| 1412–1482 | Arab mathematician [Abū al-Hasan ibn Alī al-Qalasādī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_al-Hasan_ibn_Alī_al-Qalasādī) takes "the first steps toward the introduction of [algebraic symbolism](wikipedia://en/277184)." He uses "short Arabic words, or just their initial letters, as mathematical symbols." |
| 1535 | [Scipione del Ferro](wikipedia://en/4560966) and [Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Fontana_Tartaglia), in Italy, independently solve the general cubic equation.[^o26] |
| 1545 | Girolamo [Cardano](wikipedia://en/13145) publishes *Ars magna* -*The great art* which gives del Ferro's solution to the cubic equation[^o26] and [Lodovico Ferrari](wikipedia://en/18720)'s solution to the quartic equation. |
| 1572 | [Rafael Bombelli](wikipedia://en/1548091) recognizes the complex roots of the cubic and improves current notation.[^o27] |
| 1591 | [Franciscus Vieta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscus_Vieta) develops improved symbolic notation for various powers of an unknown and uses vowels for unknowns and consonants for constants in *In artem analyticum isagoge*. |
| 1608 | [Christopher Clavius](wikipedia://en/633645) publishes his *Algebra* |
| 1619 | [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) discovers [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202). ([Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) claimed that he also discovered it independently.) |
| 1631 | [Thomas Harriot](wikipedia://en/780445) in a posthumous publication is the first to use symbols \< and > to indicate "less than" and "greater than", respectively.[^n6] |
| 1637 | Pierre de Fermat claims to have proven [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) in his copy of [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110)' *Arithmetica*, |
| 1637 | René Descartes introduces the use of the letters *z*, *y*, and *x* for unknown quantities.[^o29][^o30] |
| 1637 | The term [imaginary number](wikipedia://en/14554) is first used by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525); it is meant to be derogatory. |
| 1682 | [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz) develops his notion of symbolic manipulation with formal rules which he calls *characteristica generalis*.[^o31] |
| 1683 | Japanese mathematician [Kowa Seki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowa_Seki), in his *Method of solving the dissimulated problems*, discovers the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468),[^o32] discriminant, and [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964). |
| 1693 | [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) solves systems of simultaneous linear equations using matrices and determinants. |
| 1722 | [Abraham de Moivre](wikipedia://en/57327) states [de Moivre's formula](wikipedia://en/57326) connecting [trigonometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) and [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), |
| 1750 | [Gabriel Cramer](wikipedia://en/499331), in his treatise *Introduction to the analysis of algebraic curves*, states [Cramer's rule](wikipedia://en/166008) and studies [algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curves), matrices and determinants. |
| 1797 | [Caspar Wessel](wikipedia://en/21418268) associates vectors with [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and studies complex number operations in geometrical terms, |
| 1799 | Carl Friedrich Gauss proves the [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414) (every polynomial equation has a solution among the complex numbers), |
| 1799 | [Paolo Ruffini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Ruffini_%28mathematician%29) partially proves the [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518) that [quintic](wikipedia://en/303368) or higher equations cannot be solved by a general formula, |
| 1806 | [Jean-Robert Argand](wikipedia://en/543727) publishes proof of the [Fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414) and the [Argand diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argand_diagram), |
| 1824 | [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573) proves that the general quintic equation is insoluble by radicals.[^o26] |
| 1832 | [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) is developed by [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815) in his work on abstract algebra.[^o26] |
| 1843 | [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) discovers [quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions). |
| 1853 | [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) provides a modern definition of groups. |
| 1847 | [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298) formalizes [symbolic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_logic) in *The Mathematical Analysis of Logic*, defining what now is called [Boolean algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_algebra_%28logic%29). |
| 1873 | [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) proves that [e](wikipedia://en/9633) is transcendental. |
| 1878 | Charles Hermite solves the general quintic equation by means of elliptic and modular functions. |
| 1926 | [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) extends Hilbert's theorem on the finite basis problem to representations of a finite group over any field. |
| 1929 | [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) combines work on structure theory of [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) and the representation theory of groups into a single arithmetic theory of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) and [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) in [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) satisfying [ascending chain conditions](wikipedia://en/3189), providing the foundation for modern algebra. |
| 1981 | [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29) develops the theory of [hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817), revolutionizing both infinite group theory and global differential geometry, |
