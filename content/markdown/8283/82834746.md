# Equitable partition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equitable partition"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **equitable partition** of the vertex set *V* of a graph *G* = (*V, E*) is a [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) of *V* such that, for any pair of vertices *u* and *v* in the same set of the partition and any set *B* of the partition, both *u* and *v* have the same number of neighbors in *B*.

:::

More precisely, one represents $V = V_1 \cup V_2 \cup \cdots \cup V_r$ where every vertex is contained in exactly one "cell" $V_i$, the edges within each cell form a [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821), and for any two distinct cells $V_i$ and $V_j$ and every vertex $v_i \in V_i$, the number of edges $v_iv_j$ such that $v_j \in V_j$ is a constant $b_{ij}$, independent of the choice of $v_i \in V_i$.

The *characteristic matrix* $P$ of the partition has a row for each vertex and a column for each cell, with 1 in row $v$ and column $V_i$ if $v \in V_i$, otherwise 0.

Equitable partitions are important for simplifying calculations involving [adjacency](wikipedia://en/244463) and related matrices of large graphs.

## Equitable partitions from automorphisms {#Equitable_partitions_from_automorphisms}

The orbits of a group of [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/15094186) of *G* form an equitable partition of *V*.[^o3] This fact can assist in studying eigenvalues of graphs with [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175) automorphism group. It can also assist in determining [isomorphism of graphs](wikipedia://en/1950766).[^o4]

## Matrix use {#Matrix_use}

Equitable partitions allow collapsing $|V| \times |V|$ graphical matrices into smaller, $r \times r$ matrices while preserving the set of [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices). Thus, they can facilitate [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914).[^o6] For example, if $\pi$ is an equitable partition of $G$, then the characteristic polynomial of $A(G)$ is divisible by that of $A(G/\pi)$, where $G/\pi$ is a weighted [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) formed by collapsing each cell to a single vertex.[^o7] Thus, the eigenvalues of $A(G/\pi)$ are also eigenvalues of $A(G)$, but $A(G/\pi)$ may be a much smaller matrix so easier to compute with.

This is especially relevant to [distance regular graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_regular_graph), where the partition based on distance from a chosen vertex is equitable. That makes for, besides simplified computation of eigenvalues, a simple diagram of the numerical properties of a [distance regular graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_regular_graph).[^o8]
