# Michele Rajna

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michele Rajna"}

**Michele Rajna** (September 28, 1854 – September 29, 1920) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18831) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer). He led astronomy at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) for nearly two decades, publishing studies of [eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse), [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field), and [geodetic astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodetic_astronomy). He was the director of the [Observatory of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory_of_Bologna) from 1903 until his death.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rajna was born in [Sondrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sondrio), a city in the [Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy–Venetia) in the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire), in 1854.[^o4] He was the second-born son of Eugenio Paolo Rajna and Costanza Simonetta. His older brother [Pio Rajna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_Rajna), born 1847, was an Italian [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology) and [literary critic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_criticism) who later served in the [Senate of the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Kingdom_of_Italy).

He attended [Ghislieri College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghislieri_College) in [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia), where he studied under astronomer [Giovanni Schiaparelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Schiaparelli) at the [Brera Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brera_Astronomical_Observatory).[^o4][^o5] Rajna attended the [University of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia), graduating with a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_science) in mathematics in 1878.[^o6][^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Upon graduating from the University of Pavia, Rajna joined the Brera Astronomical Observatory in 1878.[^o9] In 1882, he was appointed Third Astronomer at the observatory and recognized for his mathematical work on [Cartesian coordinate](wikipedia://en/7706) calculation by an invitation to join the Italian Geodetic Commission.[^o4] He became a lecturer in astronomy and geodesy at the [Polytechnic University of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Milan) in 1890.[^o4] Wishing to remain in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan), in 1897 he rejected an offer to chair the astronomy department at the [University of Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Palermo).

Rajna became chair of astronomy at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) in 1903.[^o5][^o6] He wrote a letter supporting the [Vatican Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Observatory) in 1907.[^o10] He directed the [Observatory of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory_of_Bologna) until his death, attempting unsuccessfully to have it transferred to Villa Aldini, a more suitable position on a nearby hill.[^o11] A branch was later built in [Loiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loiano) in 1933 by his successor, [Guido Horn d'Arturo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Horn_d'Arturo).[^o12]

Rajna's work focused primarily on [geodetic astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodetic_astronomy) and the influence of [solar activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_phenomena) on [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field).[^o13][^o14][^o15] He found a method to graphically predict [solar eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipses) in 1891.[^o16][^o17][^o18] He published a volume of [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) tables to five decimals with Otto Müller through [Hoepli Editore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoepli_Editore) in 1883 which became one of the most commonly used computational aids in Italy for decades.[^o19] It was republished in 1886 and again in 1891.[^o20] Rajna also published a volume calculating the true geographical [local time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_time) of any place in Italy.[^o21] He was a member of the [Italian National Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei).[^o22]

After frequent periods of [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_%28mood%29) and illness,[^o4] Rajna died on September 20, 1920, in [Teglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teglio), Italy.[^o5][^o22]
