# Darmois–Skitovich theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Darmois–Skitovich theorem"}

The **Darmois–Skitovich theorem** is one of the most well-known characterization theorems in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711). It characterizes the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) (the Gaussian distribution) by the independence of two linear forms of $n$ independent [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685). The theorem was proved independently in 1953 by [V. P. Skitovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Pavlovich_Skitovich)[^o2] and [Georges Darmois](wikipedia://en/28719892)[^o3], see also [^o4], where some of its generalizations can be found.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $\xi_j, j = 1, 2, \dots, n$, be independent random variables, and let $\alpha_j, \beta_j$ be nonzero constants. If the linear forms $L_1 = \alpha_1\xi_1 + \dots + \alpha_n\xi_n$ and $L_2 = \beta_1\xi_1 + \dots + \beta_n\xi_n$ are independent, then the random variables $\xi_j$ are normally distributed (Gaussian).

The Darmois–Skitovich theorem is a generalization of the [Kac–Bernstein theorem](wikipedia://en/62469857), in which the normal distribution is characterized by the independence of the sum and difference of two independent random variables.

A result closely related to the Darmois–Skitovich theorem was proved in 1970 by [C. C. Heyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Heyde), where the Gaussian distribution is characterized by the symmetry of the [conditional distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_distribution) of one [linear form](wikipedia://en/214137) of $n$ independent random variables given another[^o7].

:::

## Generalizations to locally compact Abelian groups {#Generalizations_to_locally_compact_Abelian_groups}

The Darmois–Skitovich theorem has been extended in various directions. In particular, many works are devoted to its generalization to the case where independent random variables take values in a locally compact [Abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) and the coefficients of the linear forms are topological automorphisms of the group. A survey of a significant part of these results can be found in [^o9]. A typical example of a group analogue of the Darmois–Skitovich theorem is the following result.

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**[^o11]. Let $X$ be a second countable locally compact Abelian group containing no subgroups topologically isomorphic to the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177). Let $\xi_j, j = 1, 2, \dots, n$, be independent random variables taking values in $X$ with distributions $\mu_j$ whose characteristic functions do not vanish. Let $\alpha_j, \beta_j$ be topological automorphisms of $X$. If the linear forms $L_1 = \alpha_1\xi_1 + \dots + \alpha_n\xi_n$ and $L_2 = \beta_1\xi_1 + \dots + \beta_n\xi_n$ are independent, then all distributions $\mu_j$ are [Gaussian](wikipedia://en/66573106).

The proof of the theorem relies on group analogues of [Cramér's decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/13509534) and the Marcinkiewicz theorem.

:::
