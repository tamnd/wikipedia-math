# Daisuke Takahashi (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Daisuke Takahashi"}

**Daisuke Takahashi** is a professor of computer science at the [University of Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tsukuba),[^o3] specializing in [high-performance numerical computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Takahashi received a bachelor's degree in engineering in 1993 and a master's degree in engineering in 1995, both from [Toyohashi University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyohashi_University_of_Technology). He completed a Ph.D. in information science from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1999. After working as a researcher at the University of Tokyo and at [Saitama University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_University), he joined the University of Tsukuba in 2001.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Takahashi's works include several records of the [number of digits of the approximation of Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology_of_computation_of_π).[^o7] His work on the computation of Pi has inspired his former student [Emma Haruka Iwao](wikipedia://en/60230131), who broke a new record on March 14, 2019.[^o8]

In 2011, he was part of a team from the University of Tsukuba that won the [Gordon Bell Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Bell_Prize) of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) for their work simulating the [quantum states](wikipedia://en/30876419) of a [nanowire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanowire) using the [K computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_computer).[^o9]

He is also known for his research on the [Fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512),[^o10][^o11][^o12] and is one of the developers of the [HPC Challenge Benchmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPC_Challenge_Benchmark).[^o13]
