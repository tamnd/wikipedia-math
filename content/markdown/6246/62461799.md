# Clawson point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clawson point"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **Clawson point** is a special point in a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) defined by the [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) $\tan \alpha : \tan \beta : \tan \gamma$,[^o10] where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are the interior angles at the triangle vertices $A, B, C$. It is named after [John Wentworth Clawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wentworth_Clawson), who published it 1925 in the [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly). It is denoted *X*(19) in [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593).

:::

The Clawson point, the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244), the [mittenpunkt](wikipedia://en/14941140) and the [Spieker center](wikipedia://en/35728942) are collinear.[^o10]

## Geometrical constructions {#Geometrical_constructions}

There are at least two ways to construct the Clawson point, which also could be used as coordinate free definitions of the point. In both cases you have two triangles, where the three lines connecting their according vertices meet in a common point, which is the Clawson point.

### Construction 1 {#Construction_1}

*Image: Clawson punkt3.svg.* **Construction 1:** Clawson point as a homothetic center solid black dashed grey 2px solid black 1px solid red dashed red 2px

For a given triangle $\triangle ABC$, let $\triangle H_{A}H_{B}H_{C}$ be its [orthic triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthic_triangle) and $\triangle T_{A}T_{B}T_{C}$ the triangle formed by the outer tangents to its three [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle_and_excircles_of_a_triangle). These two triangles are similar and the Clawson point is their [center of similarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_similarity), therefore the three lines $T_{A}H_{A}, T_{B}H_{B}, T_{C}H_{C}$ connecting their vertices meet in a common point, which is the Clawson point.[^o4][^o5]

### Construction 2 {#Construction_2}

*Image: Clawson punkt2.svg.* **Construction 2:** Clawson point as a center of perspective solid #994c1a dashed #994c1a 2px solid black 1px solid blue dashed red 2px

For a triangle $\triangle ABC$, its circumcircle intersects each of its three excircles in two points. The three lines through those points of intersections form a triangle $\triangle A'B'C'.$ This triangle and $\triangle ABC$ are perspective triangles with the Clawson point being their [perspective center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_center). Hence the three lines $AA', BB', CC'$ meet in the Clawson point.[^o10]

## History {#History}

The point is now named after J. W. Clawson, who published its trilinear coordinates 1925 in the American Mathematical Monthly as problem 3132, where he asked for geometrical construction of that point.[^o8] However the French mathematician [Émile Lemoine](wikipedia://en/15956333) had already examined the point in 1886.[^o9] Later the point was independently rediscovered by R. Lyness and G. R. Veldkamp in 1983, who called it *crucial point* after the Canadian math journal Crux Mathematicorum in which it was published as problem 682.[^o10]
