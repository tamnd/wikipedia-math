# Benjamin Rossman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin Rossman"}

**Benjamin E. Rossman** is an American mathematician and theoretical computer scientist, specializing in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543).[^o2] He is currently an associate professor of computer science and mathematics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He graduated from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) with B.A. in 2001 and M.A. in 2002.[^o4] He received in 2011 his Ph.D. with advisor [Madhu Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhu_Sudan) from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) with thesis *Average-Case Complexity of Detecting Cliques*.[^o6] From 2010 to 2013 Rossman was a postdoc at the [Tokyo Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Institute_of_Technology). From 2013 to 2016 he was an assistant professor in the [Kawarabayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken-ichi_Kawarabayashi) Large Graph Project of the [National Institute of Informatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Informatics). For the academic year 2014–2015 he was a Simons-Berkeley Research Fellow at the [Simons Institute for the Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Institute_for_the_Theory_of_Computing). He was an assistant professor in the departments of mathematics and computer science of the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) until early 2019, before joining [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University).[^o4] In the fall of 2018 he was a visiting scientist at the Simons Institute for the Theory of Computing.[^o7]

His research seeks to quantify the minimum resources required to solve basic problems in combinatorial models such as [Boolean circuits](wikipedia://en/7087423). Through creative techniques based in [logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and the probabilistic method, Ben has derived groundbreaking lower bounds on the [complexity](wikipedia://en/6511) of detecting [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466) and determining [connectivity](wikipedia://en/1614492) in [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477). His other notable results include size and depth [hierarchy theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy_theorem) for [bounded-depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree-depth) circuits, answering longstanding questions.[^o8]

Rossman was a [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellow) for the academic year 2017–2018. He won the [Aisenstadt Prize](wikipedia://en/8534576) in 2018.[^o8] He was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 2018 in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro).[^o9]
