In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **11-cell** is a [self-dual](wikipedia://en/609737) [abstract regular 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1211056) ([four-dimensional polytope](wikipedia://en/24979)). Its 11 cells are [hemi-icosahedral](wikipedia://en/10393682). It has 11 vertices, 55 edges and 55 faces. It has [Schläfli type](wikipedia://en/489394) {3,5,3}, with 3 hemi-icosahedra (Schläfli type {3,5}) around each edge.

:::

Its automorphism group has 660 elements. The automorphism group is isomorphic to the [projective special linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) of the 2-dimensional vector space over the finite field with 11 elements, $L_{2}(11)$.

It was discovered in 1976 by [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976),[^n1] who constructed it by pasting hemi-icosahedra together, three at each edge, until the shape closed up. It was independently discovered by [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) in 1984, who studied its structure and symmetry in greater depth.[^n2] It has since been studied and illustrated by [Carlo H. Séquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_H._Séquin).[^n3][^n4]

Looking only at the vertices and cells, its abstract structure is [geometric configuration](wikipedia://en/7200539) (11<sub>6</sub>) and can be defined with a [cyclic configuration](wikipedia://en/7200539#Cyclic_configurations), with a generator "line" as {0,1,2,4,5,7}<sub>11</sub>. (Sequential lines increment vertex indices by 1 modulo 11.)

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

*Image: 10-simplex t0.svg.* [Orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052) of [10-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-simplex) with 11 vertices, 55 edges

The abstract 11-cell contains the same number of vertices and edges as the 10-dimensional [10-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-simplex), and contains 1/3 of its 165 faces. Thus it can be drawn as a regular figure in 10-space, although then its hemi-icosahedral cells are skew; that is, each cell is not contained within a flat 3-dimensional [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_subspace).
