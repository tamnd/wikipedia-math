# Associator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Associator"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the term **associator** is used in different ways as a measure of the [non-associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity) of an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364). Associators are commonly studied as [triple systems](wikipedia://en/19287276).

:::

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Nucleus (algebra)"}

## Ring theory {#Ring_theory}

For a [non-associative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-associative_ring) or [algebra](wikipedia://en/6533841) *R*, the **associator** is the [multilinear map](wikipedia://en/221525) $[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot] : R \times R \times R \to R$ given by

$$[x,y,z] = (xy)z - x(yz).$$

Just as the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193#Ring_theory)

$$[x, y] = xy - yx$$

measures the degree of [non-commutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity), the associator measures the degree of non-associativity of *R*. For an [associative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_ring) or [algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) the associator is identically zero.

The associator in any ring obeys the identity

$$w[x,y,z] + [w,x,y]z = [wx,y,z] - [w,xy,z] + [w,x,yz].$$

The associator is [alternating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_form) precisely when *R* is an [alternative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_ring).

The associator is symmetric in its two rightmost arguments when *R* is a [pre-Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/30722934).

The **nucleus** is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of elements that associate with all others: that is, the *n* in *R* such that

$$[n,R,R] = [R,n,R] = [R,R,n] = \{0\} \ .$$

The nucleus is an associative subring of *R*.

:::

## Quasigroup theory {#Quasigroup_theory}

A [quasigroup](wikipedia://en/25223) *Q* is a set with a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) $\cdot : Q \times Q \to Q$ such that for each *a*, *b* in *Q*, the equations $a \cdot x = b$ and $y \cdot a = b$ have unique solutions *x*, *y* in *Q*. In a quasigroup *Q*, the associator is the map $(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot) : Q \times Q \times Q \to Q$ defined by the equation

$$(a\cdot b)\cdot c = (a\cdot (b\cdot c))\cdot (a,b,c)$$

for all *a*, *b*, *c* in *Q*. As with its ring theory analog, the quasigroup associator is a measure of nonassociativity of *Q*.

## Higher-dimensional algebra {#Higher-dimensional_algebra}

In [higher-dimensional algebra](wikipedia://en/19515158), where there may be non-identity [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) between algebraic expressions, an **associator** is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)

$$a_{x,y,z} : (xy)z \mapsto x(yz).$$

## Category theory {#Category_theory}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the associator expresses the associative properties of the internal product [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) in [monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/330604).
