# John Wishart (statistician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Wishart"}

**John Wishart** (28 November 1898 – 14 July 1956) was a Scottish mathematician and agricultural statistician.

:::

He gave his name to the **[Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/364996)** in statistics.[^o3]

## Life {#Life}

Wishart was born at 24 Melville Lane in [Montrose, Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montrose,_Scotland) on 28 November 1898, the son of Elizabeth Millar Scott of Edinburgh and John Wishart of [Montrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montrose,_Angus). His father was a bespoke boot and shoe maker.[^o7] The family moved from Montrose to Perth around 1903, living at 36 Robertsons Buildings on Barrack Street.[^o5] He was educated at [Perth Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Academy).[^o6]

In the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) he was conscripted into the [Black Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Watch) in 1917 and served two years in France.[^o7]

He studied mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) under [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), graduating with an MA and BSc. He then went on to study at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) where he gained a further MA. He then gained a doctorate (DSc) at the [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) under [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695). After a year of teacher training at [Moray College of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moray_House_School_of_Education) in Edinburgh he then worked for two years as a Mathematics Teacher at West Leeds High School.

In 1927 he joined [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station) with [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824), and then (from 1931) as a Reader in Statistics in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) where he became the first Director of the [Statistical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Laboratory,_University_of_Cambridge) in 1953. He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1931,[^o8] his proposers being [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [Malcolm Laurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Laurie), [Alexander Craig Aitken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Craig_Aitken) and [Robert Schlapp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Schlapp).[^o9]

He edited *[Biometrika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometrika_%28journal%29)* from 1937. In 1950 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o10] He first formulated a generalised product-moment distribution named the [Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/364996) in his honour, in 1928.

In the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) he first served as a Captain in the [Intelligence Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Corps_%28United_Kingdom%29) then in 1942 became assistant secretary at the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty).[^o11]

Wishart drowned at the age of 57 on 14th July 1956, having suffered a stroke while swimming in the sea at Revolcadero Beach, [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acapulco). He was in Acapulco as a representative of the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization), and on a mission to set up a research centre.[^o7]

## Family {#Family}

In September 1924 he married Olive Pullan Birdsall at All Souls, Leeds. They had two sons, John Neil in 1928 and Phillip Keith in 1931, both born in St Albans, Hertfordshire.
