# Schreier refinement theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schreier refinement theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Schreier refinement theorem** of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) states that any two [subnormal series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnormal_series) of [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of a given group have equivalent refinements, where two series are equivalent if there is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between their [factor groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_group) that sends each factor group to an [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) one.

:::

The theorem is named after the [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Otto Schreier](wikipedia://en/4355201) who proved it in 1928. It provides an elegant proof of the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan–Hölder_theorem). It is often proved using the [Zassenhaus lemma](wikipedia://en/877209). (Baumslag 2006) gives a short proof by intersecting the terms in one subnormal series with those in the other series.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3$, where $S_3$ is the [symmetric group of degree 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_group_of_degree_3). The [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) $A_3$ is a normal subgroup of $S_3$, so we have the two subnormal series

$$\{0\} \times \{(1)\} \; \triangleleft \; \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \{(1)\} \; \triangleleft \; \mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3,$$

$$\{0\} \times \{(1)\} \; \triangleleft \; \{0\} \times A_3 \; \triangleleft \; \mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3,$$

with respective factor groups $(\mathbb{Z}_2,S_3)$ and $(A_3,\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2)$. The two subnormal series are not equivalent, but they have equivalent refinements:

$$\{0\} \times \{(1)\} \; \triangleleft \; \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \{(1)\} \; \triangleleft \; \mathbb{Z}_2 \times A_3 \; \triangleleft \; \mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3$$

with factor groups isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}_2, A_3, \mathbb{Z}_2)$ and

$$\{0\} \times \{(1)\} \; \triangleleft \; \{0\} \times A_3 \; \triangleleft \; \{0\} \times S_3 \; \triangleleft \; \mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3$$

with factor groups isomorphic to $(A_3, \mathbb{Z}_2, \mathbb{Z}_2)$.

:::
