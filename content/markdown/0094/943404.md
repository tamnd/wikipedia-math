# Adriaan van Wijngaarden

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adriaan van Wijngaarden"}

van WijngaardenVan Wijngaarden **Adriaan** "**Aad**" **van Wijngaarden** (2 November 1916 – 7 February 1987) was a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). Trained as a mechanical engineer, Van Wijngaarden emphasized and promoted the mathematical aspects of computing, first in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), then in [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015) and finally in design principles of such languages.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Van Wijngaarden's university education was in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering), for which he received a degree from [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology)[^o4] in 1939. He then studied for a doctorate in [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics), but abandoned the field. He joined the [Nationaal Luchtvaartlaboratorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Aerospace_Centre) in 1945 and went with a group to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) the next year to learn about new technologies that had been developed there during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

Van Wijngaarden was intrigued by the new idea of automatic computing. On 1 January 1947, he became the head of the Computing Department of the brand-new [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063) (CWI), which was at the time known as the Mathematisch Centrum (MC), in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam).[^o4] He then made further visits to England and the United States, gathering ideas for the construction of the first Dutch computer, the [ARRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARRA_%28computer%29), an electromechanical device first demonstrated in 1952. In that same year, Van Wijngaarden hired [Edsger W. Dijkstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra), and they worked on software for the ARRA.

in 1958, while visiting [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh), Scotland, Van Wijngaarden was seriously injured in an automobile accident in which his wife was killed. After he recovered, he focused more on programming language research. The following year, he became a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o12]

In 1961, he became the director of the Mathematisch Centrum in Amsterdam and remained in that post for the next twenty years.

He was one of the designers of the original [ALGOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL) language, and later [ALGOL 68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL_68),[^o5] for which he developed a two-level type of [formal grammar](wikipedia://en/18020716) that became known as a [Van Wijngaarden grammar](wikipedia://en/1544998).

In 1962, he became involved with developing [international standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_standard) in programming and informatics, as a member of the [International Federation for Information Processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_for_Information_Processing) (IFIP) [IFIP Working Group 2.1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFIP_Working_Group_2.1) on Algorithmic Languages and Calculi,[^o6] which [specified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specification_%28technical_standard%29), maintains, and supports the [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015) [ALGOL 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL_60) and [ALGOL 68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL_68).[^o7]

## Van Wijngaarden Awards {#Van_Wijngaarden_Awards}

The Van Wijngaarden Awards are named in his honor and are awarded every 5 years from the 60th anniversary of the [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063) in 2006. The physical award consists of a bronze sculpture.

- 2006: Computer scientist [Nancy Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Lynch) and mathematician-magician [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467).[^o13]
- 2011: Computer scientist [Éva Tardos](wikipedia://en/2979149) and numerical mathematician [John C. Butcher](wikipedia://en/3265725).[^o14]
- 2016: Computer scientist [Xavier Leroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_Leroy) and statistician [Sara van de Geer](wikipedia://en/48021043).[^o15]
- 2021: Computer scientist [Marta Kwiatkowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kwiatkowska) and statistician [Susan Murphy](wikipedia://en/40783721).[^o16]
