# Calkin algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Calkin algebra"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Calkin algebra**, named after [John Williams Calkin](wikipedia://en/62855104),[^o2] is the [quotient](wikipedia://en/898784) of *B*(*H*), the [ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_%28algebra%29) of [bounded linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) on a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) infinite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H*, by the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *K*(*H*) of [compact operators](wikipedia://en/6690773).[^o3] Here the addition in *B*(*H*) is addition of operators and the multiplication in *B*(*H*) is composition of operators; it is easy to verify that these operations make *B*(*H*) into a ring. When scalar multiplication is also included, *B*(*H*) becomes in fact an algebra over the same field over which *H* is a Hilbert space.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Since *K*(*H*) is a maximal norm-closed ideal in *B*(*H*), the Calkin algebra is [simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_algebra). In fact, *K*(*H*) is the only closed ideal in *B*(*H*).
- As a quotient of a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) by a two-sided ideal, the Calkin algebra is a C\*-algebra itself and there is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence)

$$0 \to K(H) \to B(H) \to B(H)/K(H) \to 0$$

which induces a [six-term cyclic exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity) in [K-theory](wikipedia://en/13314265). Those operators in *B*(*H*) which are mapped to an invertible element of the Calkin algebra are called [Fredholm operators](wikipedia://en/279722), and their [index](wikipedia://en/18102#Index) can be described both using K-theory and directly. One can conclude, for instance, that the collection of unitary operators in the Calkin algebra consists of homotopy classes indexed by the integers **Z**. This is in contrast to *B*(*H*), where the unitary operators are path connected.

- As a C\*-algebra, the Calkin algebra is not isomorphic to an algebra of operators on a separable Hilbert space. The [Gelfand-Naimark-Segal construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand-Naimark-Segal_construction) implies that the Calkin algebra is isomorphic to an algebra of operators on a nonseparable Hilbert space, but while for many other C\*-algebras there are explicit descriptions of such Hilbert spaces, the Calkin algebra does not have an explicit representation.
- The existence of an outer automorphism of the Calkin algebra is shown to be independent of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC), by work of Phillips and Weaver, and Farah.[^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

- One can define a Calkin algebra for any infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, not just separable ones.
- An analogous construction can be made by replacing *H* with a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), which is also called a Calkin algebra.[^o10]
- The Calkin algebra is the [Corona algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_algebra) of the algebra of compact operators on a Hilbert space.

:::
