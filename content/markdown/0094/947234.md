# Dunkl operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dunkl operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly the study of [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_groups), a **Dunkl operator** is a certain kind of [mathematical operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_operator), involving [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886) but also [reflections](wikipedia://en/295829) in an underlying space.

:::

Formally, let *G* be a [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) with reduced root system *R* and *k*<sub>*v*</sub> an arbitrary "multiplicity" function on *R* (so *k*<sub>*u*</sub> = *k*<sub>*v*</sub> whenever the reflections σ<sub>*u*</sub> and σ<sub>*v*</sub> corresponding to the roots *u* and *v* are conjugate in *G*). Then, the **Dunkl operator** is defined by:

$$T_i f(x) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i} f(x) + \sum_{v\in R_+} k_v \frac{f(x) - f(x \sigma_v)}{\left\langle x, v\right\rangle} v_i$$

where $v_i$ is the *i*-th component of *v*, 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *N*, *x* in *R*<sup>*N*</sup>, and *f* a smooth function on *R*<sup>*N*</sup>.

Dunkl operators were introduced by (txt). One of Dunkl's major results was that Dunkl operators "commute," that is, they satisfy $T_i (T_j f(x)) = T_j (T_i f(x))$ just as partial derivatives do. Thus Dunkl operators represent a meaningful generalization of partial derivatives.
