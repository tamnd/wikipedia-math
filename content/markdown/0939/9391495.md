# Multivariate interpolation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multivariate interpolation"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), **multivariate interpolation** or **multidimensional interpolation** is [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) on *[multivariate functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_function)*, having more than one variable or defined over a [multi-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-dimensional) domain.[^o2] A common special case is **bivariate interpolation** or **two-dimensional interpolation**, based on [two variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivariate_function) or [two dimensions](wikipedia://en/70691232). When the variates are [spatial coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_coordinate), it is also known as **spatial interpolation**.

:::

The function to be interpolated is known at given points $(x_i, y_i, z_i, \dots)$ and the interpolation problem consists of yielding values at arbitrary points $(x,y,z,\dots)$.

Multivariate interpolation is particularly important in [geostatistics](wikipedia://en/51531), where it is used to create a [digital elevation model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_elevation_model) from a set of points on the Earth's surface (for example, spot heights in a [topographic survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_survey) or depths in a [hydrographic survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrographic_survey)).

### Tensor product splines for *N* dimensions {#Tensor_product_splines_for_N_dimensions}

Catmull–Rom splines can be easily generalized to any number of dimensions. The [cubic Hermite spline](wikipedia://en/656586) article will remind you that $\mathrm{CINT}_x(f_{-1}, f_0, f_1, f_2) = \mathbf{b}(x) \cdot \left( f_{-1} f_0 f_1 f_2 \right)$ for some 4-vector $\mathbf{b}(x)$ which is a function of *x* alone, where $f_j$ is the value at $j$ of the function to be interpolated. Rewrite this approximation as

$$\mathrm{CR}(x) = \sum_{i=-1}^2 f_i b_i(x)$$

This formula can be directly generalized to N dimensions:[^o8]

$$\mathrm{CR}(x_1,\dots,x_N) = \sum_{i_1,\dots,i_N=-1}^2 f_{i_1\dots i_N} \prod_{j=1}^N b_{i_j}(x_j)$$

Note that similar generalizations can be made for other types of spline interpolations, including Hermite splines. In regards to efficiency, the general formula can in fact be computed as a composition of successive $\mathrm{CINT}$-type operations for any type of tensor product splines, as explained in the [tricubic interpolation](wikipedia://en/14556113) article. However, the fact remains that if there are $n$ terms in the 1-dimensional $\mathrm{CR}$-like summation, then there will be $n^N$ terms in the $N$-dimensional summation.

## Irregular grid (scattered data) {#Irregular_grid_(scattered_data)}

Schemes defined for scattered data on an [irregular grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irregular_grid) are more general. They should all work on a regular grid, typically reducing to another known method.

- [Nearest-neighbor interpolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest-neighbor_interpolation)
- [Triangulated irregular network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulated_irregular_network)-based [natural neighbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_neighbor)
- [Triangulated irregular network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulated_irregular_network)-based [linear interpolation](wikipedia://en/160010) (a type of [piecewise linear function](wikipedia://en/444224))
  - n-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) (e.g. tetrahedron) interpolation (see [barycentric coordinate system](wikipedia://en/762954))
- [Inverse distance weighting](wikipedia://en/1445176)
- [ABOS - approximation based on smoothing](https://surgeweb.mzf.cz/AOSIM/ABOS.htm)
- [Kriging](wikipedia://en/477026)
- [Gradient-enhanced kriging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient-enhanced_kriging) (GEK)
- [Thin-plate spline](wikipedia://en/8136546)
- [Polyharmonic spline](wikipedia://en/6160804) (The thin-plate spline is a special case of a polyharmonic spline.)
- [Radial basis function](wikipedia://en/2310753) ([Polyharmonic splines](wikipedia://en/6160804) are a special case of radial basis functions with low degree polynomial terms.)
- Least-squares [spline](wikipedia://en/457680)
- [Natural-neighbor interpolation](wikipedia://en/9032191)

*Gridding* is the process of converting irregularly spaced data to a regular grid ([gridded data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridded_data)).
