# Aubin–Lions lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aubin–Lions lemma"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Aubin–Lions lemma** (or **theorem**) is the result in the theory of [Sobolev spaces](wikipedia://en/611964) of [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989)-valued functions, which provides a [compactness](wikipedia://en/6042) criterion that is useful in the study of nonlinear evolutionary [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). Typically, to prove the existence of solutions one first constructs approximate solutions (for example, by a [Galerkin method](wikipedia://en/2664839) or by [mollification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollifiers) of the equation), then uses the compactness lemma to show that there is a convergent subsequence of approximate solutions whose limit is a solution.

:::

The result is named after the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [Jean-Pierre Aubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Aubin) and [Jacques-Louis Lions](wikipedia://en/3735500). In the original proof by Aubin,[^o2] the spaces *X*<sub>0</sub> and *X*<sub>1</sub> in the statement of the lemma were assumed to be [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091), but this assumption was removed by Simon,[^o3] so the result is also referred to as the **Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma**.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Statement of the lemma"}

## Statement of the lemma {#Statement_of_the_lemma}

Let *X*<sub>0</sub>, *X* and *X*<sub>1</sub> be three Banach spaces with *X*<sub>0</sub> ⊆ *X* ⊆ *X*<sub>1</sub>. Suppose that *X*<sub>0</sub> is [compactly embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_embedded) in *X* and that *X* is [continuously embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_embedded) in *X*<sub>1</sub>. For $1\leq p, q\leq\infty$, let

$$W = \{ u \in L^p ([0, T]; X_0) \mid \dot{u} \in L^q ([0, T]; X_1) \}.$$

(i) If $p<\infty$ then the embedding of $W$ into $L^p([0,T];X)$ is compact.

(ii) If $p=\infty$ and $q>1$ then the embedding of $W$ into $C([0,T];X)$ is compact.

:::
