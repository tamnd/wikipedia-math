# Hypernetted-chain equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypernetted-chain equation"}

In [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) the **hypernetted-chain equation** is a [closure](wikipedia://en/293802) relation to solve the [Ornstein–Zernike equation](wikipedia://en/9791475) which relates the direct correlation function to the total correlation function. It is commonly used in fluid theory to obtain e.g. expressions for the [radial distribution function](wikipedia://en/4538599). It is given by:

:::

$$\ln y(r_{12}) =\ln g(r_{12}) + \beta u(r_{12}) =\rho \int \left[h(r_{13}) - \ln g(r_{13}) - \beta u(r_{13})\right] h(r_{23}) \, d \mathbf{r_{3}}, \,$$

where $\rho = \frac{N}{V}$ is the [number density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_density) of molecules, $h(r) = g(r)-1$, $g(r)$ is the [radial distribution function](wikipedia://en/4538599), $u(r)$ is the direct interaction between pairs. $\beta = \frac{1}{k_{\rm B} T}$ with $T$ being the [Thermodynamic temperature](wikipedia://en/41789) and $k_{\rm B}$ the [Boltzmann constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_constant).

## Derivation {#Derivation}

The direct correlation function represents the direct correlation between two particles in a system containing *N* − 2 other particles. It can be represented by

$$c(r)=g_{\rm total}(r) - g_{\rm indirect}(r) \,$$

where $g_{\rm total}(r)=g(r) = \exp[-\beta w(r)]$ (with $w(r)$ the [potential of mean force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_of_mean_force)) and $g_{\rm indirect}(r)$ is the radial distribution function without the direct interaction between pairs $u(r)$ included; i.e. we write $g_{\rm indirect}(r)=\exp\{-\beta[w(r)-u(r)]\}$. Thus we *approximate* $c(r)$ by

$$c(r)=e^{-\beta w(r)}- e^{-\beta[w(r)-u(r)]}. \,$$

By expanding the indirect part of $g(r)$ in the above equation and introducing the function $y(r)=e^{\beta u(r)}g(r) (= g_{\rm indirect}(r) )$ we can approximate $c(r)$ by writing:

$$c(r)=e^{-\beta w(r)}-1+\beta[w(r)-u(r)] \, = g(r)-1-\ln y(r) \, = f(r)y(r)+[y(r)-1-\ln y(r)] \,\, (\text{HNC}),$$

with $f(r) = e^{-\beta u(r)}-1$.

This equation is the essence of the hypernetted chain equation. We can equivalently write

$$h(r) - c(r) = g(r) - 1 -c(r) = \ln y(r).$$

If we substitute this result in the [Ornstein–Zernike equation](wikipedia://en/9791475)

$$h(r_{12})- c(r_{12}) = \rho \int c(r_{13})h(r_{23})d \mathbf{r}_{3},$$

one obtains the **hypernetted-chain equation**:

$$\ln y(r_{12}) =\ln g(r_{12}) + \beta u(r_{12}) =\rho \int \left[h(r_{13}) -\ln g(r_{13}) - \beta u(r_{13})\right] h(r_{23}) \, d \mathbf{r_{3}}. \,$$
