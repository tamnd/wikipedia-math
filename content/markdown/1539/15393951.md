# Vague topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vague topology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in the area of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752), the **vague topology** is an example of the [weak-\* topology](wikipedia://en/33662) which arises in the study of [measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) on [locally compact Hausdorff spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_Hausdorff_space).

:::

Let $X$ be a [locally compact Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_Hausdorff_space). Let $M(X)$ be the space of [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) [Radon measures](wikipedia://en/521933) on $X,$ and $C_0(X)^*$ denote the dual of $C_0(X),$ the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) of complex [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on $X$ [vanishing at infinity](wikipedia://en/461986) equipped with the [uniform norm](wikipedia://en/349755). By the [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/38948504) $M(X)$ is [isometric](wikipedia://en/254777) to $C_0(X)^*.$ The isometry maps a measure $\mu$ to a [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) $I_\mu(f) := \int_X f\, d\mu.$

The **vague topology** is the [weak-\* topology](wikipedia://en/33662) on $C_0(X)^*.$ The corresponding topology on $M(X)$ induced by the isometry from $C_0(X)^*$ is also called the vague topology on $M(X).$ Thus in particular, a sequence of measures $\left(\mu_n\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ converges vaguely to a measure $\mu$ whenever for all test functions $f \in C_0(X),$

$$\int_X f d\mu_n \to \int_X f d\mu.$$

It is also not uncommon to define the vague topology by duality with continuous functions having compact support $C_c(X),$ that is, a sequence of measures $\left(\mu_n\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ converges vaguely to a measure $\mu$ whenever the above convergence holds for all test functions $f \in C_c(X).$ This construction gives rise to a different topology. In particular, the topology defined by duality with $C_c(X)$ can be metrizable whereas the topology defined by duality with $C_0(X)$ is not.

One application of this is to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542): for example, the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) is essentially a statement that if $\mu_n$ are the [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) for certain sums of [independent random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables), then $\mu_n$ converge weakly (and then vaguely) to a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462), that is, the measure $\mu_n$ is "approximately normal" for large $n.$
