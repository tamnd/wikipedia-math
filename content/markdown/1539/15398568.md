# David Rees (mathematician)

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="David Rees"}

**David Rees** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o5] (29 May 1918 – 16 August 2013) was a British professor of pure mathematics at the [University of Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Exeter), having been head of the Mathematics / Mathematical Sciences Department at Exeter from 1958 to 1983.[^o7] During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Rees was active on [Enigma](wikipedia://en/9256) research in [Hut 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_6) at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037).[^o8][^o9]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Rees was born in [Abergavenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abergavenny) to David Rees (1881–), a corn merchant, and his wife Florence Gertrude (Gertie) née Powell (1884–1970), the 4th out of 5 children. Despite periods of ill health and absence, he successfully completed his early education at [King Henry VIII Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Henry_VIII_School,_Abergavenny).[^o7]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rees won a scholarship to [Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Sussex_College,_Cambridge), supervised by [Gordon Welchman](wikipedia://en/2385693) and graduating in summer 1939. On completion of his education, he initially worked on [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory); the [Rees factor semigroup](wikipedia://en/22795102) is named after him. He also characterised [completely simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_simple_semigroup) and [completely 0-simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_0-simple_semigroup) semigroups, in what is nowadays known as Rees's theorem or Rees-Suschkewitsch theorem. The matrix-based semigroups used in this characterisation are called [Rees matrix semigroups](wikipedia://en/41983973).[^o4][^o12]

Later in 1939, Welchman drafted Rees into [Hut 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_6), [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), for the war effort. He was credited with the first decode using the [Herivel tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herivel#Herivel_tip). He was subsequently seconded to the Enigma Research Section, where the Abwehr Enigma was broken, and later to the [Newmanry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newmanry), where the [Colossus computer](wikipedia://en/6229) was built.[^o7][^o13]

After the war, Rees was appointed an assistant lecturer at [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University) in 1945 and a full lecturer at [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1948. In 1949, he was a Fellow of [Downing College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downing_College).

At the behest of [Douglas Northcott](wikipedia://en/9368121) he switched his research focus to [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990).[^o14] In 1954, in a joint paper with Northcott,[^o15] Rees introduced the Northcott–Rees theory of reductions and integral closures, which has subsequently been influential in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990).[^o16] In 1956 he introduced the [Rees decomposition](wikipedia://en/52095463) of a commutative algebra.

In 1958, Rees and his family moved to Exeter, where he had been appointed to the Chair of Pure Mathematics. In 1959, he was awarded a [DSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) by the University of Cambridge.

[Craig Steven Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Steven_Wright) claims that Rees was the third part of the Satoshi team that created [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265).[^o17][^o18]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1949, Rees was an [Honorary Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellow) of [Downing College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downing_College,_Cambridge).[^o20]

In 1968, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1968).[^o5]

In 1993, he was also awarded an [Honorary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_honorary_graduates_of_the_University_of_Exeter#1990s) [DSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) by the [University of Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Exeter). The same year, he was awarded the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29) by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). In August 1998 a conference on commutative algebra was held at Exeter in honour of David Rees' 80th Year.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1952, Rees married Joan S. Cushen,[^o22] who became a Senior Lecturer in Mathematics at Exeter, with four children:

1. (Susan) [Mary Rees](wikipedia://en/18517772) FRS, Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), b. 1953[^o23][^o24]
1. Rebecca Rees, b. 1955[^o25]
1. [Sarah Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Rees_%28professor%29), Professor of Pure Mathematics at [Newcastle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University), b. 1957[^o26][^o27]
1. Deborah Rees, b. 1960[^o28]
