# Bonita V. Saunders

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bonita V. Saunders"}

**Bonita Valerie Saunders** is an American mathematician specializing in [mathematical visualization](wikipedia://en/25948250). She works at the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology) in the Applied and Computational Mathematics Division of the Information Technology Laboratory, where she contributes to the [Digital Library of Mathematical Functions](wikipedia://en/12647205) as the Visualization Editor and the principal designer of visualizations and graphs.[^o3]

:::

## Professional career {#Professional_career}

Saunders received her Ph.D. in computational and applied mathematics from [Old Dominion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dominion_University) in 1985.[^o3] She earned her M.S. in Mathematics from the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia) and her B.A. in Mathematics from the College of William and Mary.[^o5] She worked at the BDM corporation for four years and has published a number of papers.[^o5] A list of her papers can be found at the [dblp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DBLP) "Computer Science" bibliography.<https://dblp.org/pers/s/Saunders:Bonita_V=.html> Her current research interests include, "Visualization of Complex Functions Data, Numerical Software for Special Functions, Numerical Grid Generation, \[and\] Numerical Solution of Partial Differential Equations."[^o6]

Saunders has made a number of contributions to the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA). From 1999 to 2002, she served as the MD-DC-VA Section treasurer.[^o7] From 2004 to 2009, she was on the selection committee for the Etta Z. Falconer Lecture (sponsored by the MAA and the [Association for Women in Mathematics AWM](wikipedia://en/9858698)).[^o7] She was also on the MAA Business Industry Government Committee from 2012-2017,[^o7] and was the MD-DC-VA Section Representative to the MAA Congress in 2019.[^o7] As of 2020 she is on the editorial board for Mathematics Magazine[^o7] and the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) Board of Trustees.[^o8]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

The National Association of Mathematicians chose Saunders to give the 2001 Claytor Lecture at the [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143).[^o3]

The US Department of Commerce awarded her research group a Gold Medal for Distinguished Scientific/Engineering Achievement in 2011, in recognition for her work in creating the online Digital Library of Mathematical Functions.[^o10]

In 2017, Saunders was awarded the NIST Information Technology Laboratory Outstanding Contribution Award for Excellence in Technical Leadership.[^o11]

In 2018, [Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006) made Saunders a Black History Month honoree.[^o12]

In 2019 she accepted the Excellence in Research in Mathematics and Computer Science award from the Washington Academy of Sciences[^o13] and became a Fellow of the Washington Academy of Sciences.[^o3]

Saunders gave the 2021 AWM-MAA [Etta Zuber Falconer](wikipedia://en/9997388) Lecture at [MathFest](wikipedia://en/14984993) 2021.[^o14]
