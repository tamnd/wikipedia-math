# Karin Melnick

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Karin Melnick"}

**Karin Melnick** (born 1977) is an American mathematician and Professor at the [University of Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Luxembourg).[^o3] She specializes in differential geometry and was awarded the 2020–2021 Joan and Joseph Birman Fellowship for Women Scholars by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5]

:::

## Research {#Research}

Melnick's primary research area is in differential-geometric aspects of rigidity, where she focuses on global and local results relating the automorphisms of a differential-geometric structure with the geometric and topological properties of the space.[^o5] In addition, she is a leader in research in [Lorentzian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold) geometry and has done substantial work on the Lorentzian Lichnerowicz conjecture.[^o5]

Melnick also has research interests in conformal [pseudo-Riemannian](wikipedia://en/287555) structures, parabolic [Cartan](wikipedia://en/412272) geometries in general, and smooth dynamics.[^o6]

## Education {#Education}

Melnick received her PhD in Mathematics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 2006, where she also earned her Master of Science in Mathematics in 2000, while working under the guidance of doctoral advisor [Benson Farb](wikipedia://en/32518776).[^o5][^o8] Her dissertation research focused on compact Lorentz manifolds with local symmetry.[^o8] Prior to her graduate studies, Melnick received her Bachelor of Arts, also in Mathematics, from [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College) in 1999.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Melnick currently works as a professor in the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Luxembourg). She was an assistant professor beginning in 2009 at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland), where she was promoted to associate professor in 2014 and to full professor in 2021. Before working at UMD, Melnick held the position of Gibbs Assistant Professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) while on an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Postdoctoral Research Fellowship.[^o5][^o10]

## Honors {#Honors}

Melnick was awarded the 2020–2021 Joan and Joseph Birman Fellowship for Women Scholars by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for her research on differential-geometric aspects of rigidity.[^o5] This prestigious fellowship, founded by mathematician [Joan Birman](wikipedia://en/8625257) and physicist [Joseph L. Birman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_L._Birman), is awarded to talented, mid-career women with a significant record of research in a core area of mathematics.[^o12]

Melnick has previously been awarded an [NSF CAREER Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation_CAREER_Awards) (2013), an [AMS Centennial Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMS_Centennial_Fellowship) (2012–2013), and a Junior Research Fellowship from the [Erwin Schrödinger Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger_International_Institute_for_Mathematical_Physics) (2009).[^o13][^o14][^o15]
