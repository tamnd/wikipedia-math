# Circolo Matematico di Palermo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Circolo Matematico di Palermo"}

The **Circolo Matematico di Palermo** (Mathematical Circle of Palermo) is an Italian [mathematical society](wikipedia://en/1098209), founded in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) by Sicilian geometer [Giovanni B. Guccia](wikipedia://en/41801592) in 1884.[^o2] It began accepting foreign members in 1888,[^o2] and by the time of Guccia's death in 1914 it had become the foremost international mathematical society, with approximately one thousand members.[^o3] However, subsequently to that time it declined in influence.[^o2]

:::

## Publications {#Publications}

***Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo***, the journal of the society, was published in a first series from 1885 to 1941 and in a second ongoing series beginning in 1952. Since 2008 it has been published by [Springer Science+Business Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media); current editors are C Ciliberto, G. Dal Maso, and Pasquale Vetro.[^o6]

Influential papers published in the *Rendiconti* include [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740)'s *On the Dynamics of the Electron* (1906). The *Rendiconti* also provided the introduction of [normal numbers](wikipedia://en/204277),[^o7] the original publications of the [Plancherel theorem](wikipedia://en/454315)[^o8] and [Carathéodory's theorem](wikipedia://en/892014),[^o9] [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544)'s proof of the [equidistribution theorem](wikipedia://en/2541664),[^o10] and one of the appendices to Henri Poincaré's "[Analysis Situs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_Situs_%28paper%29)".[^o11]
