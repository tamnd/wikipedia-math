# Mnemonics in trigonometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mnemonics in trigonometry"}

In [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), it is common to use [mnemonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemonic) to help remember [trigonometric identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_identities) and the relationships between the various [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367).

:::

*Image: trigonometric\_function\_triangle\_mnemonic.svg.* Image mnemonic to help remember the ratios of sides of a right triangle

The *sine*, *cosine*, and *tangent* ratios in a right triangle can be remembered by representing them as strings of letters, for instance SOH-CAH-TOA in English:

- **S**ine = **O**pposite ÷ **H**ypotenuse
- **C**osine = **A**djacent ÷ **H**ypotenuse
- **T**angent = **O**pposite ÷ **A**djacent

One way to remember the letters is to sound them out phonetically (i.e. , similar to [Krakatoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakatoa)).[^o2]

## Phrases {#Phrases}

Another method is to expand the letters into a sentence, such as "Some Old Horses Chew Apples Happily Throughout Old Age", "Some Old Hippy Caught Another Hippy Tripping On Acid", or "Studying Our Homework Can Always Help To Obtain Achievement". The order may be switched, as in "Tommy On A Ship Of His Caught A Herring" (tangent, sine, cosine) or "The Old Army Colonel And His Son Often Hiccup" (tangent, cosine, sine) or "Come And Have Some Oranges Help To Overcome Amnesia" (cosine, sine, tangent).[^o5] Another way to remember it is if you drop a brick on your toe, you should Soak A Toe (SOH-CAH-TOA). Communities in Chinese circles may choose to remember it as TOA-CAH-SOH, which also means 'big-footed aunt' () in [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien).

An alternate way to remember the letters for Sin, Cos, and Tan is to memorize the syllables Oh, Ah, Oh-Ah (i.e. ) for O/H, A/H, O/A. Longer mnemonics for these letters include "Oscar Has A Hold On Angie" and "Oscar Had A Heap of Apples."[^o4]

## All Students Take Calculus {#All_Students_Take_Calculus}

*Image: trigonometric\_function\_quadrant\_sign.svg.* Signs of trigonometric functions in each quadrant.

**All S**tudents **T**ake **C**alculus is a [mnemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemonic) for the sign of each [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) in each [quadrant](wikipedia://en/7706) of the plane. The letters ASTC signify which of the trigonometric functions are positive, starting in the top right 1st quadrant and moving [counterclockwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterclockwise) through quadrants 2 to 4.[^o12]

- Quadrant 1 (angles from 0 to 90 degrees, or 0 to $\pi$/2 radians): **All** trigonometric functions are positive in this quadrant.
- Quadrant 2 (angles from 90 to 180 degrees, or $\pi$/2 to $\pi$ radians): **S**ine and cosecant functions are positive in this quadrant.
- Quadrant 3 (angles from 180 to 270 degrees, or $\pi$ to 3$\pi$/2 radians): **T**angent and cotangent functions are positive in this quadrant.
- Quadrant 4 (angles from 270 to 360 degrees, or 3$\pi$/2 to 2$\pi$ radians): **C**osine and secant functions are positive in this quadrant.

Other mnemonics include:

- **All Silver Tea Cups**
- **All S**tations **T**o **C**entral[^o9]
- **All S**illy **T**om **C**ats[^o9]
- **A**dd **S**ugar **T**o **C**offee[^o9]
- **All** **S**cience **T**eachers (are) **C**razy[^o10]
- **A** **S**mart **T**rig **C**lass[^o11]
- **All** **S**chools **T**orture **C**hildren[^o12]
- **A**wful **S**tinky **T**rig **C**ourse[^o12]
- **All** **S**tudents **T**rust **C**ambridge[^o13]

Other easy-to-remember mnemonics are the **ACTS** and **CAST** laws. These have the disadvantages of not going sequentially from quadrants 1 to 4 and not reinforcing the numbering convention of the quadrants.

- **CAST** still goes counterclockwise but starts in quadrant 4 going through quadrants 4, 1, 2, then 3.
- **ACTS** still starts in quadrant 1 but goes clockwise going through quadrants 1, 4, 3, then 2.

:::

## Sines and cosines of special angles {#Sines_and_cosines_of_special_angles}

Sines and cosines of common angles 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90° follow the pattern $\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}$ with $n = 0, 1, ..., 4$ for sine and $n = 4, 3, ..., 0$ for cosine, respectively:[^o15]

::: {#o16 .table cue=table}

| $\theta$ | $\sin \theta$ | $\cos \theta$ | $\tan \theta = \sin \theta \Big/ \cos \theta$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° = 0 radians | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{blue}{0}}}}{2} = \;\; 0$ | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{red}{4}}}}{2} = \;\; 1$ | $\;\; 0 \;\; \Big/ \;\; 1 \;\; = \;\; 0$ |
| 30° = $\tfrac{\pi}{6}$ radians | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{teal}{1}}}}{2} = \;\, \frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{orange}{3}}}}{2}$ | $\;\, \frac{1}{2} \; \Big/ \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ |
| 45° = $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ radians | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{green}{2}}}}{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{green}{2}}}}{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \Big/ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \;\; 1$ |
| 60° = $\tfrac{\pi}{3}$ radians | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{orange}{3}}}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{teal}{1}}}}{2} = \; \frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \Big/ \; \frac{1}{2} \;\, = \sqrt{3}$ |
| 90° = $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ radians | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{red}{4}}}}{2} = \;\, 1$ | $\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{\color{blue}{0}}}}{2} = \;\, 0$ | $\;\; 1 \;\; \Big/ \;\; 0 \;\;$ undefined |

:::

## Hexagon chart {#Hexagon_chart}

*Image: Trigonometric identity mnemonic.svg.* Trigonometric identities mnemonic

Another mnemonic permits all of the basic identities to be read off quickly. The hexagonal chart can be constructed with a little thought:[^o18]

1. Draw three triangles pointing down, touching at a single point. This resembles a [fallout shelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallout_shelter) [trefoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trefoil).
1. Write a 1 in the middle where the three triangles touch
1. Write the functions without "co" on the three left outer vertices (from top to bottom: *sine*, *tangent*, *secant*)
1. Write the co-functions on the corresponding three right outer vertices (*co*sine, *co*tangent, *co*secant)

Starting at any vertex of the resulting hexagon:

::: {#o19 .table cue=table}

| Identity | Example(s) | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| The starting vertex equals one over the opposite vertex. | $\sin A = \frac{{1}}{{\csc A}}$ | *Image: Trigonometría - Identidades recíprocas.svg.* Illustration that a function equals the reciprocal of the opposite function |
|  | $\tan A = \frac{{1}}{{\cot A}}$ |  |
|  | $\cos A = \frac{{1}}{{\sec A}}$ |  |
| Going either clockwise or counter-clockwise, the starting vertex equals the next vertex divided by the vertex after that. | $\sin A = \frac{{\cos A}}{{\cot A}} = \frac{{\tan A}}{{\sec A}}$ | *Image: Trigonometría - Cocientes 1.svg.* Illustration that a function equals the fraction of the next two functions going clockwise, for example $\tan A = \tfrac{\sin A}{\cos A}$ *Image: Trigonometría - Cocientes 2.svg.* Illustration that a function equals the fraction of the next two functions going counter-clockwise, for example $\cot A = \tfrac{\cos A}{\sin A}$ |
|  | $\cos A = \frac{{\cot A}}{{\csc A}} = \frac{{\sin A}}{{\tan A}}$ |  |
|  | $\cot A = \frac{{\csc A}}{{\sec A}} = \frac{{\cos A}}{{\sin A}}$ |  |
|  | $\csc A = \frac{{\sec A}}{{\tan A}} = \frac{{\cot A}}{{\cos A}}$ |  |
|  | $\sec A = \frac{{\tan A}}{{\sin A}} = \frac{{\csc A}}{{\cot A}}$ |  |
|  | $\tan A = \frac{{\sin A}}{{\cos A}} = \frac{{\sec A}}{{\csc A}}$ |  |
| The starting corner equals the product of its two nearest neighbors. | $\sin A = \cos A \cdot \tan A$ | *Image: Trigonometría - Productos.svg.* Illustration that a function equals the product of its two neighbor functions, for example $\sin A = \cos A \cdot \tan A$ |
| The sum of the squares of the two items at the top of a triangle equals the square of the item at the bottom. These are the [trigonometric Pythagorean identities](wikipedia://en/1050741). | $\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1^2 = 1 \$ | *Image: Trigonometría - Identidades pitagóricas.svg.* Illustration of the Pythagorean trigonometric identities in the hexagon chart |
|  | $1 + \cot^2 A = \csc^2 A \$ |  |
|  | $\tan^2 A + 1 = \sec^2 A \$ |  |

:::

Aside from the last bullet, the specific values for each identity are summarized in this table:

::: {#o21 .table cue=table}

| Starting function | ... equals $\tfrac{1}{\operatorname{opposite}}$ | ... equals $\tfrac{\operatorname{first}}{\operatorname{second}}$ clockwise | ... equals $\tfrac{\operatorname{first}}{\operatorname{second}}$ counter-clockwise/anticlockwise | ... equals the product of two nearest neighbors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\tan A$ | $= \frac {1}{\cot A}$ | $= \frac {\sin A}{\cos A}$ | $= \frac {\sec A}{\csc A}$ | $= \sin A \cdot \sec A$ |
| $\sin A$ | $= \frac {1}{\csc A}$ | $= \frac {\cos A}{\cot A}$ | $= \frac {\tan A}{\sec A}$ | $= \cos A \cdot \tan A$ |
| $\cos A$ | $= \frac {1}{\sec A}$ | $= \frac {\cot A}{\csc A}$ | $= \frac {\sin A}{\tan A}$ | $= \sin A \cdot \cot A$ |
| $\cot A$ | $= \frac {1}{\tan A}$ | $= \frac {\csc A}{\sec A}$ | $= \frac {\cos A}{\sin A}$ | $= \cos A \cdot \csc A$ |
| $\csc A$ | $= \frac {1}{\sin A}$ | $= \frac {\sec A}{\tan A}$ | $= \frac {\cot A}{\cos A}$ | $= \cot A \cdot \sec A$ |
| $\sec A$ | $= \frac {1}{\cos A}$ | $= \frac {\tan A}{\sin A}$ | $= \frac {\csc A}{\cot A}$ | $= \csc A \cdot \tan A$ |

:::
