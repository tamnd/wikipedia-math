# Endre Boros

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Endre Boros"}

**Endre Boros** (born 21 September 1953) is a Hungarian-American mathematician, a distinguished professor at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in New Brunswick, New Jersey, and the director of the Center for Operations Research (RUTCOR).[^o3] He is the author of 15 book chapters and edited volumes, and 165 research papers. He is associate editor of the Annals of Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, and Editor-in-Chief of both the Annals of Operations Research and Discrete Applied Mathematics.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Results {#Results}

(Boros Szőnyi 1986) settled a conjecture by [Beniamino Segre](wikipedia://en/3534031) about the cyclic structure of [finite projective planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_projective_plane), and (Boros 1988) provided the best known bound for a question posed by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) about blocking sets of Galois planes. (Boros Gurvich 1996) proved that [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531) are kernel solvable which answered a longstanding open question by C. Berge and P. Duchet (and which is independent of the [perfect graph theorem](wikipedia://en/670602)). He settled the complexity of generating all maximal frequent and minimal infrequent sets of large data sets answering questions by R.H. Sloan, K. Takata and G. Turán in (Boros Gurvich Khachiyan Makino 2003), and in (Khachiyan Boros Borys Elbassioni 2008) resolved the complexity of the longstanding open problem of generating all vertices of polyhedra.

(Boros Hammer Sun Tavares 2008) uses a [network flow](wikipedia://en/645676) based approach for quadratic binary optimization. In the area of the theory of [Horn functions](wikipedia://en/2522311), (Boros Crama Hammer 1990) proved that all "prime implicates" of a Horn CNF can be generated efficiently, extended Horn logic to q-Horn and showed that this extension forms in some sense the boundary between tractable and intractable logic.

:::
