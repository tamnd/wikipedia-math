# Riemann–Roch theorem for smooth manifolds

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riemann–Roch theorem for smooth manifolds"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Riemann–Roch theorem for smooth manifolds** is a version of results such as the [Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/2892412) or [Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/1532606) (GRR) without a hypothesis making the [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) involved carry a [complex structure](wikipedia://en/509742). Results of this kind were obtained by [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) in 1959, reducing the requirements to something like a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Let *X* and *Y* be oriented smooth [closed manifolds](wikipedia://en/669475), and *f*: *X* → *Y* a continuous map. Let *v*<sub>*f*</sub>=*f*<sup>\*</sup>(*TY*) − *TX* in the [K-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_theory) K(X). If dim(X) ≡ dim(Y) mod 2, then

$$\mathrm{ch}(f_{K*}(x)) = f_{H*}(\mathrm{ch}(x) e^{d(v_f)/2}\hat{A}(v_f)),$$

where ch is the [Chern character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_character), d(v<sub>f</sub>) an element of the integral [cohomology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) *H*<sup>2</sup>(*Y*, *Z*) satisfying *d*(*v*<sub>*f*</sub>) ≡ *f*<sup>\*</sup> *w*<sub>2</sub>(T*Y*)-*w*<sub>2</sub>(T*X*) mod 2, f<sub>K\*</sub> the [Gysin homomorphism](wikipedia://en/7095671) for K-theory, and f<sub>H\*</sub> the Gysin homomorphism for cohomology.[^o4] This theorem was first proven by Atiyah and Hirzebruch.[^o5]

The theorem is proven by considering several special cases.[^o6] If *Y* is the [Thom space](wikipedia://en/1243451) of a vector bundle *V* over *X*, then the Gysin maps are just the Thom isomorphism. Then, using the [splitting principle](wikipedia://en/15958803), it suffices to check the theorem via explicit computation for line bundles.

If *f*: *X* → *Y* is an embedding, then the Thom space of the normal bundle of *X* in *Y* can be viewed as a tubular neighborhood of *X* in *Y*, and excision gives a map

$$u:H^*(B(N), S(N)) \to H^*(Y, Y-B(N)) \to H^*(Y)$$

and

$$v:K(B(N), S(N)) \to K(Y, Y-B(N)) \to K(Y).$$

The Gysin map for K-theory/cohomology is defined to be the composition of the Thom isomorphism with these maps. Since the theorem holds for the map from *X* to the Thom space of *N*, and since the Chern character commutes with *u* and *v*, the theorem is also true for embeddings. *f*: *X* → *Y*.

Finally, we can factor a general map *f*: *X* → *Y* into an embedding

$$i: X \to Y \times S^{2n}$$

and the projection

$$p: Y \times S^{2n} \to Y.$$

The theorem is true for the embedding. The Gysin map for the projection is the Bott-periodicity isomorphism, which commutes with the Chern character, so the theorem holds in this general case also.

:::

## Corollaries {#Corollaries}

Atiyah and Hirzebruch then specialised and refined in the case *X* = a point, where the condition becomes the existence of a spin structure on *Y*. Corollaries are on [Pontryagin classes](wikipedia://en/385944) and the [J-homomorphism](wikipedia://en/3061995).
