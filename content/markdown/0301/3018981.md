# Formal moduli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formal moduli"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **formal moduli** are an aspect of the theory of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) (of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties) or [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524), for example), closely linked to [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) and [formal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_geometry). Roughly speaking, deformation theory can provide the [Taylor polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_polynomial) level of information about deformations, while formal moduli theory can assemble consistent Taylor polynomials to make a [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) theory. The step to moduli spaces, properly speaking, is an *algebraization* question, and has been largely put on a firm basis by [Artin's approximation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin's_approximation_theorem).

:::

A **formal universal deformation** is by definition a [formal scheme](wikipedia://en/18829483) over a [complete local ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_local_ring), with [special fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_fiber) the scheme over a field being studied, and with a [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) amongst such set-ups. The local ring in question is then the carrier of the formal moduli.
