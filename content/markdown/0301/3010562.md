# Biharmonic equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biharmonic equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **biharmonic equation** is a fourth-order [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) which arises in areas of [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918), including [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429) theory and the solution of [Stokes flows](wikipedia://en/2047298). Specifically, it is used in the modeling of thin structures that react [elastically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29) to external forces.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

It is written as

$$\nabla^4 \varphi = 0$$

or

$$\nabla^2 \nabla^2 \varphi = 0$$

or

$$\Delta^2 \varphi = 0$$

where $\nabla^4$, which is the [fourth power](wikipedia://en/665027) of the [del](wikipedia://en/151925) operator and the square of the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) operator $\nabla^2$ (or $\Delta$), is known as the **biharmonic operator** or the **bilaplacian operator**. In [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates), it can be written in $n$ dimensions as:

$$\nabla^4 \varphi = \sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n \partial_i\partial_i\partial_j\partial_j \varphi = \left(\sum_{i=1}^n \partial_i\partial_i\right) \left(\sum_{j=1}^n \partial_j\partial_j\right) \varphi.$$

Because the formula here contains a summation of indices, many mathematicians prefer the notation $\Delta^2$ over $\nabla^4$ because the former makes clear which of the indices of the four nabla operators are contracted over.

For example, in three dimensional [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) the biharmonic equation has the form

$${\partial^4 \varphi\over \partial x^4 } + {\partial^4 \varphi\over \partial y^4 } + {\partial^4 \varphi\over \partial z^4 } + 2{\partial^4 \varphi\over \partial x^2\partial y^2} + 2{\partial^4 \varphi\over \partial y^2\partial z^2} + 2{\partial^4 \varphi\over \partial x^2\partial z^2} = 0.$$

As another example, in *n*-dimensional [real coordinate space](wikipedia://en/1482083) without the origin $\left( \mathbb{R}^n \setminus \mathbf 0 \right)$,

$$\nabla^4 \left({1\over r}\right) = {3(15-8n+n^2)\over r^5}$$

where

$$r = \sqrt{x_1^2 + x_2^2 +\cdots + x_n^2}.$$

which shows, for *n*=3 and *n*=5 only, $\frac{1}{r}$ is a solution to the biharmonic equation.

A solution to the biharmonic equation is called a **biharmonic function**. Any [harmonic function](wikipedia://en/55762) is biharmonic, but the converse is not always true.

In two-dimensional [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates), the biharmonic equation is

$$\frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial}{\partial r} \left(r \frac{\partial}{\partial r} \left(\frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial}{\partial r} \left(r \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial r}\right) \right)\right) + \frac{2}{r^2} \frac{\partial^4 \varphi}{\partial \theta^2 \partial r^2} + \frac{1}{r^4} \frac{\partial^4 \varphi}{\partial \theta^4} - \frac{2}{r^3} \frac{\partial^3 \varphi}{\partial \theta^2 \partial r} + \frac{4}{r^4} \frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial \theta^2} = 0$$

which can be solved by [separation of variables](wikipedia://en/387750). The result is the [Michell solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michell_solution).

:::

## 2-dimensional space {#2-dimensional_space}

The general solution to the 2-dimensional case is

$$x v(x,y) - y u(x,y) + w(x,y)$$

where $u(x,y)$, $v(x,y)$ and $w(x,y)$ are [harmonic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_functions) and $v(x,y)$ is a [harmonic conjugate](wikipedia://en/910519) of $u(x,y)$.

Just as [harmonic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_functions) in 2 variables are closely related to complex [analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_functions), so are biharmonic functions in 2 variables. The general form of a biharmonic function in 2 variables can also be written as

$$\operatorname{Im}(\bar{z}f(z) + g(z))$$

where $f(z)$ and $g(z)$ are [analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_functions).
