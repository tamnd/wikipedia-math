# Cryptomeria cipher

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Cryptomeria cipher"}

The **Cryptomeria cipher**, also called **C2**, is a [proprietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_software) [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) defined and licensed by the [4C Entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4C_Entity). It is the successor to [CSS](wikipedia://en/18935238) algorithm (used for [DVD-Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD#DVD-Video)) and was designed for the [CPRM/CPPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_Protection_for_Recordable_Media) [digital rights management](wikipedia://en/18938226) scheme which are used by DRM-restricted [Secure Digital cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Digital_card) and [DVD-Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-Audio) discs.

:::

## Cipher details {#Cipher_details}

The C2 [symmetric key algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_key_algorithm) is a 10-round [Feistel cipher](wikipedia://en/352709). Like [DES](wikipedia://en/7978), it has a [key size](wikipedia://en/5749) of 56 bits and a [block size](wikipedia://en/294065) of 64 bits. The encryption and decryption algorithms are available for [peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review), but implementations require the so-called "secret constant", the values of the [substitution box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_box) (S-box), which are only available under a license from the 4C Entity.

The 4C Entity licenses a different set of S-boxes for each application (such as [DVD-Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-Audio), [DVD-Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-Video) and CPRM).[^o5]

## Cryptanalysis {#Cryptanalysis}

In 2008, an attack was published against a reduced 8-round version of Cryptomeria to discover the [S-box](wikipedia://en/240991) in a chosen-key scenario. In a practical experiment, the attack succeeded in recovering parts of the S-box in 15 hours of CPU time, using 2 plaintext-ciphertext pairs.[^o5]

A paper by Julia Borghoff, [Lars Knudsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Ramkilde_Knudsen), Gregor Leander and Krystian Matusiewicz in 2009 breaks the full-round cipher in three different scenarios; it presents a 2<sup>24</sup> time complexity attack to recover the S-box in a chosen-key scenario, a 2<sup>48</sup> [boomerang attack](wikipedia://en/2137332) to recover the key with a known S-box using 2<sup>44</sup> adaptively chosen plaintexts/ciphertexts, and a 2<sup>\53.5</sup> attack when both the key and S-box are unknown.[^o2]

## Distributed brute force cracking effort {#Distributed_brute_force_cracking_effort}

Following an announcement by Japanese [HDTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television) broadcasters that they would start broadcasting programs with the copy-once [broadcast flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_flag) starting with 2004-04-05, a distributed Cryptomeria cipher brute force cracking effort was launched on 2003-12-21. To enforce the broadcast flag, [digital video recorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video_recorder) employ CPRM-compatible storage devices, which the project aimed to circumvent. However, the project was ended and declared a failure on 2004-03-08 after searching the entire 56-bit keyspace, failing to turn up a valid key for unknown reasons.[^o8] Because the attack was based on [S-box](wikipedia://en/240991) values from [DVD-Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-Audio), it was suggested that CPRM may use different S-boxes.[^o9]

Another brute force attack to recover [DVD-Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-Audio) [CPPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_Protection_for_Recordable_Media) device keys was mounted on 2009-05-06. The attack was intended to find any of 24570 secret device keys by testing [MKB](wikipedia://en/20584449) file from Queen "The Game" [DVD-Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-Audio) disc. On 2009-10-20 such key for column 0 and row 24408 was discovered.

The similar brute force attack to recover [DVD-VR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-VR) [CPRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPRM) device keys was mounted on 2009-10-20. The attack was intended to find any of 3066 secret device keys by testing [MKB](wikipedia://en/20584449) from Panasonic LM-AF120LE [DVD-RAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-RAM) disc. On 2009-11-27 such key for column 0 and row 2630 was discovered.

By now the CPPM/CPRM protection scheme is deemed unreliable.
