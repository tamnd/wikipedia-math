# Upper and lower probabilities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Upper and lower probabilities"}

**Upper and lower probabilities** are representations of [imprecise probability](wikipedia://en/3255074). Whereas [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) uses a single number, the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), to describe how likely an event is to occur, this method uses two numbers: the event's upper probability and the event's lower probability.

:::

Because [frequentist statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequentist_statistics) disallow [metaprobabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaprobability), frequentists have had to propose new solutions. [Cedric Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Smith_%28statistician%29) and [Arthur Dempster](wikipedia://en/7040761) each developed a theory of upper and lower probabilities. [Glenn Shafer](wikipedia://en/226650) developed Dempster's theory further, now known as [Dempster–Shafer theory](wikipedia://en/226660) or Choquet (1953). More precisely, in the work of these authors, one considers in a [power set](wikipedia://en/23799), $P(S)\,\!$, a *mass* function $m : P(S)\rightarrow R$ satisfying the conditions

$$m(\varnothing) = 0 \,\,\,\,\,\,\! ; \,\,\,\,\,\, m(A) \ge 0 \,\,\,\,\,\,\! ; \,\,\,\,\,\, \sum_{A \in P(S)} m(A) = 1. \,\!$$

In turn, a mass is associated with two non-additive continuous measures called **belief** and **plausibility,** defined as follows:

$$\operatorname{bel}(A) = \sum_{B \mid B \subseteq A} m(B)\,\,\,\,;\,\,\,\, \operatorname{pl}(A) = \sum_{B \mid B \cap A \ne \varnothing} m(B)$$

In the case where $S$ is infinite there can be $\operatorname{bel}$ such that there is no associated mass function. See p. 36 of Halpern (2003). Probability measures are a special case of belief functions in which the mass function only assigns positive mass to the event space's singletons.

A different notion of upper and lower probabilities is obtained by the *lower and upper envelopes* obtained from a class *C* of probability distributions by setting

$$\operatorname{env_1}(A) = \inf_{p \in C} p(A)\,\,\,\,;\,\,\,\, \operatorname{env_2}(A) = \sup_{p \in C} p(A)$$

The upper and lower probabilities also relate to [probabilistic logic](wikipedia://en/5017608): see Gerla (1994).

Observe also that a [necessity measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessity_measure) can be seen as a lower probability, and a [possibility measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possibility_measure) as an upper probability.
