# Bitruncation

*Image: Birectified cube sequence.png.* A *bitruncated [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)* is a truncated [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635).

*Image: Bitruncated cubic honeycomb.png.* A [bitruncated cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3736299) - Cubic cells become orange truncated octahedra, and vertices are replaced by blue truncated octahedra.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bitruncation"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **bitruncation** is an operation on [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693). The original [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) are lost completely and the original [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) remain as smaller copies of themselves.

:::

Bitruncated regular polytopes can be represented by an extended [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) notation $\mathbf{t}_{1,2}\{p,q,...\}$ or $\mathbf{2t}\{p,q,...\}.$

## In regular polyhedra and tilings {#In_regular_polyhedra_and_tilings}

For regular [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) (i.e. regular 3-polytopes), a *bitruncated* form is the truncated [dual](wikipedia://en/8815). For example, a bitruncated [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is a [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654).

## In regular 4-polytopes and honeycombs {#In_regular_4-polytopes_and_honeycombs}

For a regular [4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979), a *bitruncated* form is a dual-symmetric operator. A bitruncated 4-polytope is the same as the bitruncated dual, and will have double the symmetry if the original 4-polytope is [self-dual](wikipedia://en/8815).

A regular polytope (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) {p, q, r} will have its {p, q} cells **bitruncated** into truncated {q, p} cells, and the vertices are replaced by truncated {q, r} cells.

### Self-dual {p,q,p} 4-polytope/honeycombs {#Self-dual_{p,q,p}_4-polytope/honeycombs}

An interesting result of this operation is that self-dual 4-polytope {p,q,p} (and honeycombs) remain [cell-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-transitive) after bitruncation. There are 5 such forms corresponding to the five truncated regular polyhedra: t{q,p}. Two are honeycombs on the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792), one a honeycomb in Euclidean 3-space, and two are honeycombs in hyperbolic 3-space.
