# Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence** is a [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) for calculating [generalized cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_cohomology), introduced by (txt)[^o3] in the special case of [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468). For a [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) $X$ and a generalized cohomology theory $E^\bullet$, it relates the generalized cohomology groups

:::

$$E^i(X)$$

with 'ordinary' [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) $H^j$ with coefficients in the generalized cohomology of a point. More precisely, the $E_2$ term of the spectral sequence is $H^p(X;E^q(pt))$, and the spectral sequence converges conditionally to $E^{p+q}(X)$.

Atiyah and Hirzebruch pointed out a generalization of their spectral sequence that also generalizes the [Serre spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3085825), and reduces to it in the case where $E=H_{\text{Sing}}$. It can be derived from an [exact couple](wikipedia://en/803894#Exact_couples) that gives the $E_1$ page of the Serre spectral sequence, except with the ordinary cohomology groups replaced with $E$. In detail, assume $X$ to be the total space of a [Serre fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_fibration) with fibre $F$ and base space $B$. The [filtration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtration_%28algebra%29) of $B$ by its [$n$-skeletons](wikipedia://en/2977910) $B_n$ gives rise to a filtration of $X$. There is a corresponding [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) with $E_2$ term

$$H^p(B; E^q(F))$$

and converging to the [associated graded ring](wikipedia://en/2954244) of the filtered ring

$$E_\infty^{p,q} \Rightarrow E^{p+q}(X).$$

This is the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence in the case where the fibre $F$ is a point.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Topological K-theory {#Topological_K-theory}

For example, the complex [topological $K$-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) of a point is

$KU(*) = \mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]$ where $x$ is in degree $2$

By definition, the terms on the $E_2$-page of a finite CW-complex $X$ look like

$$E_2^{p,q}(X) = H^p(X;KU^q(pt))$$

Since the $K$-theory of a point is

$$K^q(pt) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & \text{if q is even} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

we can always guarantee that

$$E_2^{p,2k+1}(X) = 0$$

This implies that the spectral sequence collapses on $E_2$ for many spaces. This can be checked on every $\mathbb{CP}^n$, algebraic curves, or spaces with non-zero cohomology in even degrees. Therefore, it collapses for all (complex) even dimensional smooth complete intersections in $\mathbb{CP}^n$.

#### Cotangent bundle on a circle {#Cotangent_bundle_on_a_circle}

For example, consider the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) of $S^1$. This is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) with fiber $\mathbb{R}$ so the $E_2$-page reads as

$$\begin{array}{c|cc} \vdots &\vdots & \vdots \\ 2 & H^0(S^1;\mathbb{Q}) & H^1(S^1;\mathbb{Q}) \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & H^0(S^1;\mathbb{Q}) & H^1(S^1;\mathbb{Q}) \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & H^0(S^1;\mathbb{Q}) & H^1(S^1;\mathbb{Q}) \\ \vdots &\vdots & \vdots \\ \hline & 0 & 1 \end{array}$$

#### Differentials {#Differentials}

The odd-dimensional differentials of the AHSS for complex topological K-theory can be readily computed. For $d_3$ it is the Steenrod square $Sq^3$ where we take it as the composition

$$\beta \circ Sq^2 \circ r$$

where $r$ is reduction mod $2$ and $\beta$ is the [Bockstein homomorphism](wikipedia://en/3116297) (connecting morphism) from the short exact sequence

$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$$

#### Complete intersection 3-fold {#Complete_intersection_3-fold}

Consider a smooth [complete intersection](wikipedia://en/3116835) 3-fold $X$ (such as a complete intersection Calabi-Yau 3-fold). If we look at the $E_2$-page of the spectral sequence

$$\begin{array}{c|ccccc} \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 2 & H^0(X; \mathbb{Z}) & 0 & H^2(X;\mathbb{Z}) & H^3(X;\mathbb{Z}) & H^4(X;\mathbb{Z}) & 0 & H^6(X;\mathbb{Z}) \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & H^0(X; \mathbb{Z}) & 0 & H^2(X;\mathbb{Z}) & H^3(X;\mathbb{Z}) & H^4(X;\mathbb{Z}) & 0 & H^6(X;\mathbb{Z})\\ -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & H^0(X; \mathbb{Z}) & 0 & H^2(X;\mathbb{Z}) & H^3(X;\mathbb{Z}) & H^4(X;\mathbb{Z}) & 0 & H^6(X;\mathbb{Z})\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ \hline & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \end{array}$$

we can see immediately that the only potentially non-trivial differentials are

$$\begin{aligned} d_3:E_3^{0,2k} \to E_3^{3,2k-2} \\ d_3:E_3^{3,2k} \to E_3^{6,2k-2} \end{aligned}$$

It turns out that these differentials vanish in both cases, hence $E_2 = E_\infty$. In the first case, since $Sq^k:H^i(X;\mathbb{Z}/2) \to H^{k+i}(X;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ is trivial for $k > i$ we have the first set of differentials are zero. The second set are trivial because $Sq^2$ sends $H^3(X;\mathbb{Z}/2) \to H^5(X) = 0$ the identification $Sq^3 = \beta \circ Sq^2 \circ r$ shows the differential is trivial.

### Twisted K-theory {#Twisted_K-theory}

The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence can be used to compute [twisted K-theory](wikipedia://en/4297226) groups as well. In short, twisted K-theory is the group completion of the isomorphism classes of vector bundles defined by gluing data $(U_{ij},g_{ij})$ where

$$g_{ij}g_{jk}g_{ki} = \lambda_{ijk}$$

for some cohomology class $\lambda \in H^3(X,\mathbb{Z})$. Then, the spectral sequence reads as

$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X;KU^q(*)) \Rightarrow KU^{p+q}_\lambda(X)$$

but with different differentials. For example,

$$E_3^{p,q} = E_2^{p,q} = \begin{array}{c|cccc} \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 2 & H^0(S^3;\mathbb{Z}) & 0 & 0 & H^3(S^3;\mathbb{Z}) \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & H^0(S^3;\mathbb{Z}) & 0 & 0 & H^3(S^3;\mathbb{Z}) \\ -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & H^0(S^3;\mathbb{Z}) & 0 & 0 & H^3(S^3;\mathbb{Z}) \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ \hline& 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \end{array}$$

On the $E_3$-page the differential is

$$d_3 = Sq^3 + \lambda$$

Higher odd-dimensional differentials $d_{2k+1}$ are given by [Massey products](wikipedia://en/2676948) for twisted K-theory tensored by $\mathbb{R}$. So

$$\begin{aligned} d_5 &= \{ \lambda, \lambda, - \} \\ d_7 &= \{ \lambda, \lambda, \lambda, - \} \end{aligned}$$

Note that if the underlying space is [formal](wikipedia://en/13998981#Formal_spaces), meaning its rational homotopy type is determined by its rational cohomology, hence has vanishing Massey products, then the odd-dimensional differentials are zero. [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414), [John Morgan](wikipedia://en/8487966), and [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608) proved this for all compact [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538), hence $E_\infty = E_4$ in this case. In particular, this includes all smooth projective varieties.

#### Twisted K-theory of 3-sphere {#Twisted_K-theory_of_3-sphere}

The twisted K-theory for $S^3$ can be readily computed. First of all, since $Sq^3 = \beta \circ Sq^2 \circ r$ and $H^2(S^3) = 0$, we have that the differential on the $E_3$-page is just cupping with the class given by $\lambda$. This gives the computation

$$KU_\lambda^k = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & k \text{ is even} \\ \mathbb{Z}/\lambda & k \text{ is odd} \end{cases}$$

### Rational bordism {#Rational_bordism}

Recall that the rational bordism group $\Omega_*^{\text{SO}}\otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is isomorphic to the ring

$$\mathbb{Q}[[\mathbb{CP}^0], [\mathbb{CP}^2], [\mathbb{CP}^4],[\mathbb{CP}^6],\ldots]$$

generated by the bordism classes of the (complex) even dimensional projective spaces $[\mathbb{CP}^{2k}]$ in degree $4k$. This gives a computationally tractable spectral sequence for computing the rational bordism groups.

### Complex cobordism {#Complex_cobordism}

Recall that $MU^*(pt) = \mathbb{Z}[x_1,x_2,\ldots]$ where $x_i \in \pi_{2i}(MU)$. Then, we can use this to compute the [complex cobordism](wikipedia://en/1033666) of a space $X$ via the spectral sequence. We have the $E_2$-page given by

$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X;MU^q(pt))$$
