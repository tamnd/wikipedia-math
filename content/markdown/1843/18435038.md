# Richard Shore

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Shore"}

**Richard Arnold Shore** (born August 18, 1946) is a professor of mathematics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) who works in [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory). He is particularly known for his work on $\mathcal{D}$, the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) of the [Turing degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_degrees).

:::

- Shore settled the [Rogers](wikipedia://en/1014272) homogeneity conjecture by showing that there are Turing degrees $a$ and $b$ such that $\mathcal{D}_a$ and $\mathcal{D}_b$, the structures of the degrees above $a$ and $b$ respectively, are not isomorphic.[^o3]
- In joint work with [Theodore Slaman](wikipedia://en/5693320), Shore showed that the [Turing jump](wikipedia://en/1681091) is definable in $\mathcal{D}$.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

He was, in 1983, an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in Warsaw and gave a talk *The Degrees of Unsolvability: the Ordering of Functions by Relative Computability*. In 2009, he was the [Gödel Lecturer](wikipedia://en/69216431) (*Reverse mathematics: the playground of logic*).[^o6] He was an editor from 1984 to 1993 of the [Journal of Symbolic Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Symbolic_Logic) and from 1993 to 2000 of the [Bulletin of Symbolic Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_Symbolic_Logic). In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7]
