# Unit measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unit measure"}

**Unit measure** is an [axiom](wikipedia://en/23539) of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542)[^o2] that states that the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) of the entire [sample space](wikipedia://en/22958) is equal to one ([unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29)); that is, *P*(*S*)=1 where *S* is the sample space. Loosely speaking, it means that *S* must be chosen so that when the experiment is performed, *something* happens. The term *measure* here refers to the [measure-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) approach to probability.

:::

Violations of unit measure have been reported in arguments about the outcomes of events[^o3][^o4] under which events acquire "probabilities" that are not the probabilities of probability theory. In situations such as these the term "probability" serves as a false premise to the associated argument.
