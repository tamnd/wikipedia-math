# Centrosymmetric matrix

*Image: Matrix symmetry qtl4.svg.* Symmetry pattern of a centrosymmetric 5 × 5 matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centrosymmetric matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and [matrix theory](wikipedia://en/20556859), a **centrosymmetric matrix** is a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) which is symmetric about its center.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

An $n \times n$ matrix $A = [A_{i, j}]$ is centrosymmetric when its entries satisfy

$$A_{i,\,j} = A_{n-i+1,\,n-j+1} \quad \text{for all }i,j \in \{1,\, \ldots,\, n\}.$$

Alternatively, if $J$ denotes the $n \times n$ [exchange matrix](wikipedia://en/7876585) with 1 on the [antidiagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidiagonal) and 0 elsewhere:

$$J_{i,\,j} = \begin{cases} 1, & i + j = n + 1 \\ 0, & i + j \ne n + 1\\ \end{cases}$$

then a matrix $A$ is centrosymmetric [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $AJ = JA$.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- All 2 × 2 centrosymmetric matrices have the form $$\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}.$$
- All 3 × 3 centrosymmetric matrices have the form $$\begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & d \\ c & b & a \end{bmatrix}.$$
- [Symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) [Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/151569) matrices are centrosymmetric.

:::

## Algebraic structure and properties {#Algebraic_structure_and_properties}

- If $A$ and $B$ are $n \times n$ centrosymmetric matrices over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $F$, then so are $A + B$ and $cA$ for any $c$ in $F$. Moreover, the [matrix product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_product) $AB$ is centrosymmetric, since $JAB = AJB = ABJ$. Since the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) is also centrosymmetric, it follows that the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of $n \times n$ centrosymmetric matrices over $F$ forms a [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239#Subalgebras_for_algebras_over_a_ring_or_field) of the [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) of all $n \times n$ matrices.
- If $A$ is a centrosymmetric matrix with an $m$-dimensional [eigenbasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenbasis), then its $m$ [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) can each be chosen so that they satisfy either $\mathbf{x} = J\mathbf{x}$ or $\mathbf{x} = -J\mathbf{x}$ where $J$ is the exchange matrix.
- If $A$ is a centrosymmetric matrix with distinct [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue), then the matrices that [commute](wikipedia://en/25264990) with $A$ must be centrosymmetric.[^o11] This follows from a theorem "If matrices A and B commute, A with distinct eigenvalues, then B is a polynomial in A".
- The maximum number of unique elements in an $m \times m$ centrosymmetric matrix is $$\frac{m^2 + m \bmod 2}{2}.$$ This is also the dimension of the vector space of all $m \times m$ centrosymmetric matrices.

## Related structures {#Related_structures}

An $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is said to be *skew-centrosymmetric* if its entries satisfy

$$A_{i,\,j} = -A_{n-i+1,\,n-j+1} \quad \text{for all }i,j \in \{1,\, \ldots,\, n\}.$$

Equivalently, $A$ is skew-centrosymmetric if $AJ = -JA$, where $J$ is the exchange matrix defined previously.

The centrosymmetric relation $AJ = JA$ lends itself to a natural generalization, where $J$ is replaced with an [involutory matrix](wikipedia://en/7917643) $K$ (i.e., $K^{2} = I$)[^o8][^o9][^o10] or, more generally, a matrix $K$ satisfying $K^{m} = I$ for an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $m > 1$.[^o11] The inverse problem for the commutation relation $AK = KA$ of identifying all involutory $K$ that commute with a fixed matrix $A$ has also been studied.[^o11]

[Symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) centrosymmetric matrices are sometimes called [bisymmetric matrices](wikipedia://en/13714248). When the [ground field](wikipedia://en/27161748) is the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), it has been shown that bisymmetric matrices are precisely those symmetric matrices whose eigenvalues remain the same aside from possible sign changes following pre- or post-multiplication by the exchange matrix.[^o9] A similar result holds for [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682) centrosymmetric and skew-centrosymmetric matrices.[^o12]
