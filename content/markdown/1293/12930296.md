# Decorrelation theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decorrelation theory"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **decorrelation theory** is a system developed by [Serge Vaudenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Vaudenay) in 1998[^o2] for designing [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594) to be [provably secure](wikipedia://en/1182348) against [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536), [linear cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/59469),[^o3] and even undiscovered cryptanalytic attacks meeting certain broad criteria. Ciphers designed using these principles include [COCONUT98](wikipedia://en/9329898) and the [AES candidate](wikipedia://en/99431) [DFC](wikipedia://en/7955867), both of which have been shown to be vulnerable to some forms of [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) not covered by the theory.

:::

According to Vaudenay, the decorrelation theory has four tasks: 1) the definition of a measurement for the decorrelation, which usually relies on a matrix norm; 2) the construction of simple primitive or "decorrelation module" with a quite good decorrelation; 3) the construction of [cryptographic algorithms](wikipedia://en/10294) with decorrelation modules so that the primitive can be inherited by the algorithm; and, 4) proving that the decorrelation provides security against attacks.[^o4]
