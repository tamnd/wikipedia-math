# Lune (geometry)

the plane geometry region

*Image: Luno ebena geom combined.svg.* In plane geometry, the [crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescent) shape formed by two intersecting circles is called a *lune*. In each diagram, two lunes are present, and one is shaded in grey.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lune"}

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), a **lune** (la) is the concave-convex region bounded by two circular [arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29).[^o2] It has one boundary portion for which the connecting segment of any two nearby points moves outside the region and another boundary portion for which the connecting segment of any two nearby points lies entirely inside the region. A convex-convex region is termed a [lens](wikipedia://en/2038396).[^o3]

:::

Formally, a lune is the [relative complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_complement) of one [disk](wikipedia://en/329542) in another (where they intersect but neither is a subset of the other). Alternatively, if $A$ and $B$ are disks, then $A \smallsetminus A \cap B$ is a lune.

## Squaring the lune {#Squaring_the_lune}

In the 5th century BC, [Hippocrates of Chios](wikipedia://en/3632522) showed that the [Lune of Hippocrates](wikipedia://en/21854422) and two other lunes could be [exactly squared](wikipedia://en/201359) (converted into a square having the same area) by [straightedge and compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straightedge_and_compass). Around 1000, [Alhazen](wikipedia://en/1645) attempted to square a circle using a pair of lunes now bearing his name. In 1766 the Finnish mathematician Daniel Wijnquist, quoting [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784), listed all five geometrical squareable lunes, adding to those known by Hippocrates. In 1771 [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) gave a general approach and obtained a certain equation to the problem. In 1933 and 1947 it was proven by [Nikolai Chebotaryov](wikipedia://en/3033696) and his student Anatoly Dorodnov that these five are the only squarable lunes.[^o6][^o2]

## Area {#Area}

The area of a lune formed by circles of radii *a* and *b* (*b>a*) with distance *c* between their centers is

$$A=2\Delta+a^2\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{b^2-a^2-c^2}{2ac}\right)-b^2\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}\right),$$

where $\text{cos}^{-1}$ is the inverse cosine or arccosine, and where

$$\Delta= \frac{1}{4} \sqrt{(a+b+c)(-a+b+c)(a-b+c)(a+b-c)}$$

is the [area of a triangle](wikipedia://en/30654#Computing_the_area_of_a_triangle) with sides *a, b* and *c*.
