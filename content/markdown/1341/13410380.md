# Mathematics and fiber arts

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematics and fiber arts"}

*Image: Moebiusstripscarf.jpg.* A [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) scarf made from crochet.

Ideas from [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) have been used as inspiration for [fiber arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_art) including [quilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilt) [making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilting), [knitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knitting), [cross-stitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-stitch), [crochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crochet), [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery) and [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving). A wide range of mathematical concepts have been used as inspiration including [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). Some techniques such as [counted-thread embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counted-thread_embroidery) are naturally [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446); other kinds of [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) provide a ready means for the colorful [physical expression of mathematical concepts](wikipedia://en/22468661).

:::

## Quilting {#Quilting}

The [IEEE Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Spectrum) has organized a number of competitions on [quilt block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilt_block) design, and several books have been published on the subject. Notable quiltmakers include Diana Venters and Elaine Ellison, who have written a book on the subject *Mathematical Quilts: No Sewing Required*. Examples of mathematical ideas used in the book as the basis of a quilt include the [golden rectangle](wikipedia://en/219374), [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673), [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)'s Claw, the [Koch curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_curve), the [Clifford torus](wikipedia://en/7950934), [San Gaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gaku), [Mascheroni](wikipedia://en/88116)'s [cardioid](wikipedia://en/642101), [Pythagorean triples](wikipedia://en/24172), [spidrons](wikipedia://en/2016606), and the six [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367).[^o3]

## Knitting and crochet {#Knitting_and_crochet}

*Image: Cross stitch embroidery.jpg.* [Cross-stitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-stitch) [counted-thread embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counted-thread_embroidery)

Knitted mathematical objects include the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), [Klein bottles](wikipedia://en/17412) and [Boy's surface](wikipedia://en/61361). The [Lorenz manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_attractor) and the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/1589135) have been crafted using crochet.[^o5][^o6] Knitted and crocheted [tori](wikipedia://en/74800) have also been constructed depicting [toroidal embeddings](wikipedia://en/1633057) of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>7</sub> and of the [Heawood graph](wikipedia://en/1396870).[^o7] The crocheting of hyperbolic planes has been popularized by the [Institute For Figuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_For_Figuring); a book by [Daina Taimina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daina_Taimina) on the subject, *[Crocheting Adventures with Hyperbolic Planes](wikipedia://en/63312770)*, won the 2009 [Bookseller/Diagram Prize for Oddest Title of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookseller/Diagram_Prize_for_Oddest_Title_of_the_Year).[^o8]

[Clifford Stoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Stoll) is an American mathematician who produces knitted [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) scarves, as well as knitted 3-Dimensional interpretations of [Klein bottles](wikipedia://en/17412)[^o9].

## Embroidery {#Embroidery}

*Image: Florentin.png.* Two [Bargello patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargello_%28needlework%29)

Embroidery techniques such as [counted-thread embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counted-thread_embroidery)[^o11] including [cross-stitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-stitch) and some [canvas work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canvas_work) methods such as [Bargello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargello_%28needlework%29) make use of the natural [pixels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel) of the weave, lending themselves to geometric designs.[^o12][^o13]

## Weaving {#Weaving}

[Ada Dietz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Dietz) (1882 – 1981) was an American [weaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) best known for her 1949 monograph *Algebraic Expressions in Handwoven Textiles*, which defines weaving patterns based on the expansion of multivariate [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000).[^o15]

(txt) used the [Rule 90](wikipedia://en/16572515) [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342) to design [tapestries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapestry) depicting both trees and abstract patterns of triangles.[^o17]

## Spinning {#Spinning}

[Margaret Greig](wikipedia://en/44216349) was a mathematician who articulated the mathematics of [worsted spinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worsted_spinning).[^o19]

## Fashion design {#Fashion_design}

The silk scarves from DMCK Designs' 2013 collection are all based on Douglas McKenna's [space-filling curve](wikipedia://en/352398) patterns.[^o21] The designs are either generalized Peano curves, or based on a new space-filling construction technique.[^o22][^o23]

The [Issey Miyake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issey_Miyake) Fall-Winter 2010–2011 ready-to-wear collection designs from a collaboration between fashion designer Dai Fujiwara and mathematician [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190). The designs were inspired by Thurston's [geometrization conjecture](wikipedia://en/220642), the statement that every [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) can be decomposed into pieces with one of eight different uniform geometries, a proof of which had been sketched in 2003 by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) as part of his proof of the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798).[^o24]
