# Elaine Kasimatis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elaine Kasimatis"}

**Elaine Ann Kasimatis** (May 25, 1952 – September 7, 2021) was an American mathematician specializing in [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468) and [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471). She was a professor in the Department of Mathematics & Statistics at [California State University, Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Sacramento).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Kasimatis was educated at the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis). She earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics there in 1976, and a master's degree in mathematics education in 1979. She returned to Davis for graduate study in pure mathematics, earning a second master's degree in 1983 and completing her Ph.D. in 1986. Her dissertation, *Dissection of Regular Polygons into Triangles of Equal Areas*, was supervised by [Sherman K. Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_K._Stein).

She joined the faculty at California State University, Sacramento in 1986.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Kasimatis was known for her work on [equidissection](wikipedia://en/36643448), the subdivision of polygons into triangles of equal area; with Stein, she made the first studies of equidissections of regular [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014), and introduced the concept of the equidissection spectrum of a polygon.

She was also the author of an algebra textbook, *Making Sense of Elementary Algebra: Data, Equations, and Graphs*, with Cindy L. Erickson, Addison-Wesley, 1999.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Kasimitis was one of the 2021 winners of the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Awards for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). The award cited her "major role in developing the first program in California to integrate mathematics content with teacher preparation", her mentorship of student teachers, her development of the middle-school Access to Algebra program and the College Preparatory Mathematics program, both used nationally, and her volunteer work developing mathematics education in [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda).
