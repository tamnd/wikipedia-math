# Roy Cameron (statistician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Roy Cameron"}

**Roy James Cameron** (11 March 19233 October 2006) was an Australian public servant and [diplomat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomat). He was the [Australian Statistician](wikipedia://en/5417205) from 1977 to 1985.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Cameron was born in [Port Pirie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Pirie), [South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Australia) on 11 March 1923. He studied at the [University of Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Adelaide), graduating with first class honours from a Bachelor of Economics course in 1948. He graduated a Master of Economics from the university in 1951, with the topic "Standard hours and the basic wage: an analysis of two aspects of the work of the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration."[^o4] He was awarded a [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) scholarship to study for his doctorate at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), starting in 1951.[^o5] He completed his PhD in 1956.[^o4]

From 1973 to 1977 Cameron was Australia's Ambassador to the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development) in Paris. His appointment announcement, which was issued by [Gough Whitlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gough_Whitlam) in 1973, noted Cameron's broad experience in economic and financial matters.[^o6]

In 1977 Cameron returned to Australia to take up his appointment as [Australian Statistician](wikipedia://en/5417205). He served in the role until his retirement in August 1985.[^o7][^o8]

Cameron died on 3 October 2006.[^o4]

## Awards {#Awards}

Cameron was made a [Companion of the Order of the Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_the_Bath) in the [1982 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Birthday_Honours).[^o10]
