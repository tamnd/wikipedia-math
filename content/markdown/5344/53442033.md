# Peter Redford Scott Lang

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Redford Scott Lang"}

*Image: Peter Redford Scott Lang.jpg.*

**Sir Peter Redford Scott Lang** [VD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_Decoration) [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (1850–1926) was a Scottish mathematician and Regius Professor at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews). In the 1880s he instituted “Common Dinners” to bring the students together for joint meals (often referred to as “commies”). This had a major impact upon student social life and was thereafter adopted by several Scottish universities. In memory of this the University of St Andrews holds an annual **Scott Lang Dinner**.[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 8 October 1850, the youngest of six children of Barbara Turnbull (née Cochrane) and Robert Laidlaw Lang (b.1808), an advocate’s clerk. They lived at 125 Fountainbridge in the south-west of the city.[^o4] He was educated at the Edinburgh Institution (now known as [Stewarts Melville College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewarts_Melville_College)) and then studied mathematics and natural philosophy (physics) at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). His university studies were interspersed with training as a life assurance clerk. He graduated MA BSc in 1872 and began assisting in lectures in natural philosophy at the University of Edinburgh.[^o5]

In 1878 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [Sir Robert Christison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christison), [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait), [David Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Stevenson_%28engineer%29), and [John Hutton Balfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hutton_Balfour). In 1879 he moved to the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews) as Professor of Mathematics.[^o6] During his time at St Andrews he purchased a house on South Street. He rose to also be Dean of the Faculty of Arts within the University.[^o7]

He was a [Lieutenant Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel_%28British_Army_and_Royal_Marines%29) in the 1st [Fifeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifeshire) [Royal Garrison Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Garrison_Artillery), a [volunteer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_Force) battalion based at the no 7 battery at [Anstruther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anstruther).[^o7] He had served as a volunteer for at least 20 years, gaining the Victorian Officer’s Decoration (VD) in 1900.[^o8] He was granted the honorary rank of [Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_%28British_Army%29) on 25 October 1902.

He was knighted in 1921 by [King George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V) on the point of his retiral. In 1922 the University of St Andrews awarded him an honorary doctorate (LLD).[^o5]

He died at home in St Andrews on 5 July 1926. He is buried with his wife and daughter in [St Andrews Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrews_Cathedral) Churchyard. The grave lies on a wall to the south of the central tower.

## Family {#Family}

He was married to Alice Mary Dickson (1858-1932) from [Colinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colinton). They had one daughter, Edith Mary Valentine Lang (1880-1936).[^o11]
