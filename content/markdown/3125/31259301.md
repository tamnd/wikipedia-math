# Theodor Schönemann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theodor Schönemann"}

**Theodor Schönemann**, also written **Schoenemann** (4 April 1812 – 16 January 1868), was a German mathematician who obtained several important results in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) concerning the theory of [congruences](wikipedia://en/20087), which can be found in several publications in [Crelle's journal](wikipedia://en/973547), volumes 17 to 40. Notably, he obtained [Hensel's lemma](wikipedia://en/1633368) before [Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565), [Scholz's reciprocity law](wikipedia://en/33627178) before Scholz, and formulated [Eisenstein's criterion](wikipedia://en/385989) before [Eisenstein](wikipedia://en/1452482).[^o2] He also studied, under the form of integer polynomials [modulo](wikipedia://en/25977) both a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) and an [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) (remaining irreducible modulo that prime number), what can nowadays be recognized as [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) (more general than those of prime order).[^o3]

:::

He was educated in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) and Berlin, where among his teachers were [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) and [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340). He obtained his doctorate in 1842, after which he became Gymnasialoberlehrer (professor at a gymnasium) in [Brandenburg an der Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_an_der_Havel). Apart from the mentioned mathematical papers, he also published, mainly after 1850, in mechanics and physical technique.
