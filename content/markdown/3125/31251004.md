# Philip Hauser

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Philip Hauser"}

**Philip Morris Hauser** (September 27, 1909 – December 13, 1994) was a [demographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographer) and pioneer in [urban studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_studies) who was a president of the [American Sociological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Sociological_Association), the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) and the [Population Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Association_of_America). For more than 30 years he was director of the Population Research Center at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) where he also served as the Lucy Flower Professor of Urban Sociology.[^o13][^o12]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Philip Morris Hauser was born September 27, 1909. He attended the University of Chicago and received a [Bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in 1929.[^o12] He was studying at Chicago at the time of the rise of the [Chicago School of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_School_of_Sociology).[^o13][^o12]

## Career {#Career}

In 1932, Hauser was first named as an instructor at the University of Chicago, a position which he retained throughout the course of his graduate studies.[^o12] In 1933 he was awarded a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) from that institution, followed by a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) in 1938.[^o12] From 1934 to 1937, Hauser went on leave to serve as a researcher for the [Federal Emergency Relief Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Emergency_Relief_Administration).[^o12] Hauser worked at the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070) from 1938 to 1947, first as Assistant Chief Statistician for Population then taking on the role of [Deputy Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Director_of_the_United_States_Census_Bureau) in 1946.[^o12] In 1944 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o11] In 1947, Hauser returned to the University of Chicago as a professor of sociology.[^o12] He also remained as acting director of the Census Bureau from 1949 to 1950.[^o12] Hauser was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1965,[^o5] the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1972,[^o6] and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1976.[^o7] Hauser was the founder of the Population Research Center at the University of Chicago, over which he served as director for some 30 years.[^o12] During the course of his career there he helped train approximately 100 future PhDs, in addition to a large number of other graduate students.[^o12]

## Death {#Death}

Hauser died on December 13, 1994. He was 85 years old at the time of his death.
