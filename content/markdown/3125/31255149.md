# Duffin–Schaeffer theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Duffin–Schaeffer theorem"}

The **Koukoulopoulos–Maynard theorem**, historically known as the **Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture**, is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558#Khinchin's_theorem_and_extensions). It was proposed as a conjecture by [R. J. Duffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Duffin) and [A. C. Schaeffer](wikipedia://en/35273660) in 1941[^o2] and [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) in 2019 by [Dimitris Koukoulopoulos](wikipedia://en/69797343) and [James Maynard](wikipedia://en/42512102).[^o3]

:::

It states that if $f : \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^+$ is a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427) taking on positive values, then for [almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) $\alpha$ (with respect to [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198)), the [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489)

$$\left| \alpha - \frac{p}{q} \right| < \frac{f(q)}{q}$$

has infinitely many solutions in [coprime integers](wikipedia://en/6556) $p,q$ with $q > 0$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922)

$$\sum_{q=1}^\infty \varphi(q) \frac{f(q)}{q} = \infty,$$

where $\varphi(q)$ is [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452).

A higher-dimensional analogue of this conjecture was resolved by Vaughan and Pollington in 1990.[^o4][^o5][^o6]

## Introduction {#Introduction}

That existence of the [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) approximations implies [divergence](wikipedia://en/876428) of the [series](wikipedia://en/15287) follows from the [Borel–Cantelli lemma](wikipedia://en/44987).[^o6] The [converse implication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converse_implication) is the crux of the conjecture.[^o4]

There have been many partial results of the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture established to date. [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) established in 1970 that the conjecture holds if there exists a constant $c > 0$ such that for every [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ we have either $f(n) = c/n$ or $f(n) = 0$.[^o4][^o8] This was strengthened by Jeffrey Vaaler in 1978 to the case $f(n) = O(n^{-1})$.[^o9][^o10]

More recently, this was strengthened to the conjecture being true whenever there exists some $\varepsilon > 0$ such that the series

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(\frac{f(n)}{n}\right)^{1 + \varepsilon} \varphi(n) = \infty.$$

This was done by Haynes, Pollington, and Velani.[^o11]

In 2006, Beresnevich and Velani proved that a [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109) analogue of the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture is [equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logically_equivalent) to the original Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture, which is a priori weaker. This result was published in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*.[^o12]
