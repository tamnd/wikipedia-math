# Kazhdan–Margulis theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kazhdan–Margulis theorem"}

In [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681), an area of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kazhdan–Margulis theorem** is a statement asserting that a [discrete subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_subgroup) in [semisimple Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) cannot be too dense in the group. More precisely, in any such [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) there is a uniform [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) such that every lattice in the group has a [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176#Conjugacy_of_subgroups_and_general_subsets) whose intersection with this neighbourhood contains only the identity. This result was proven in the 1960s by [David Kazhdan](wikipedia://en/3052833) and [Grigory Margulis](wikipedia://en/1104948).[^o2]

:::

## Statement and remarks {#Statement_and_remarks}

The formal statement of the Kazhdan–Margulis theorem is as follows.

*Let $G$ be a semisimple Lie group: there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of the identity $e$ in $G$ such that for any discrete subgroup $\Gamma \subset G$ there is an element $g \in G$ satisfying $g\Gamma g^{-1} \cap U = \{ e \}$.*

Note that in general Lie groups this statement is far from being true; in particular, in a [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569) Lie group, for any neighbourhood of the identity there exists a lattice in the group which is generated by its intersection with the neighbourhood: for example, in $\mathbb R^n$, the lattice $\varepsilon \mathbb Z^n$ satisfies this property for $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The main technical result of Kazhdan–Margulis, which is interesting in its own right and from which the better-known statement above follows immediately, is the following.[^n1]

*Given a semisimple Lie group without compact factors $G$ endowed with a norm $|\cdot|$, there exists $c > 1$, a neighbourhood $U_0$ of $e$ in $G$, a compact subset $E \subset G$ such that, for any discrete subgroup $\Gamma \subset G$ there exists a $g \in E$ such that $|g\gamma g^{-1}| \ge c|\gamma|$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma \cap U_0$.*

The neighbourhood $U_0$ is obtained as a [Zassenhaus neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zassenhaus_neighbourhood) of the identity in $G$: the theorem then follows by standard Lie-theoretic arguments.

There also exist other proofs. There is one proof which is more geometric in nature and which can give more information,[^o7][^o8] and there is a third proof, relying on the notion of [invariant random subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_random_subgroups), which is considerably shorter.[^o9]

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Selberg's hypothesis"}

### Selberg's hypothesis {#Selberg's_hypothesis}

One of the motivations of Kazhdan–Margulis was to prove the following statement, known at the time as *Selberg's hypothesis* (recall that a [lattice](wikipedia://en/15782871) is called *uniform* if its quotient space is compact):

*A lattice in a semisimple Lie group is non-uniform if and only if it contains a [unipotent](wikipedia://en/1724478) element.*

This result follows from the more technical version of the Kazhdan–Margulis theorem and the fact that only unipotent elements can be conjugated arbitrarily close (for a given element) to the identity.

:::

### Volumes of locally symmetric spaces {#Volumes_of_locally_symmetric_spaces}

A corollary of the theorem is that the [locally symmetric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_symmetric_space) and orbifolds associated to lattices in a semisimple Lie group cannot have arbitrarily small volume (given a normalisation for the Haar measure).

For hyperbolic surfaces this is due to Siegel, and there is an explicit lower bound of $\pi / 21$ for the smallest covolume of a quotient of the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane) by a lattice in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ (see [Hurwitz's automorphisms theorem](wikipedia://en/1113637)). For hyperbolic three-manifolds the lattice of minimal volume is known and its covolume is about 0.0390.[^o15] In higher dimensions the problem of finding the lattice of minimal volume is still open, though it has been solved when restricting to the subclass of [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741).[^o16]

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Wang's finiteness theorem"}

### Wang's finiteness theorem {#Wang's_finiteness_theorem}

Together with [local rigidity](wikipedia://en/50722101) and finite generation of lattices the Kazhdan-Margulis theorem is an important ingredient in the proof of Wang's finiteness theorem.[^o19]

*If $G$ is a simple Lie group not locally isomorphic to $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ or $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$ with a fixed Haar measure and $v>0$ there are only finitely many lattices in $G$ of covolume less than $v$.*

:::
