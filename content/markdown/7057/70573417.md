# Leray–Schauder degree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leray–Schauder degree"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Leray–Schauder degree** is an extension of the degree of a [base point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_point) preserving [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) between [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) $(S^n, *) \to (S^n , *)$ or equivalently to boundary-sphere-preserving continuous maps between balls $(B^n, S^{n-1}) \to (B^n, S^{n-1})$ to boundary-sphere-preserving maps between balls in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $f: (B(V), S(V)) \to (B(V), S(V))$, assuming that the map is of the form $f = id - C$ where $id$ is the [identity map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_map) and $C$ is some compact map (i.e. mapping bounded sets to sets whose closure is [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28mathematics%29)).[^o2]

:::

The degree was invented by [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430) and [Juliusz Schauder](wikipedia://en/685314) to prove existence results for partial differential equations.[^o3][^o4]
