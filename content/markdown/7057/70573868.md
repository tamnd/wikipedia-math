# Violet B. Haas

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Violet B. Haas"}

**Violet Bushwick Haas** (November 23, 1926 – January 21, 1986) was an American applied mathematician specializing in [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) and [optimal estimation](wikipedia://en/34426869) who became a professor of electrical engineering at [Purdue University College of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University_College_of_Engineering).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Haas was born November 23, 1926, in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn).[^n1] She completed a A.B. in mathematics[^n2] at [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College) in 1947.[^n1] Haas earned a M.S. (1949) and Ph.D. (1951) in mathematics from the [MIT Department of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3520170) (MIT).[^n1] Her dissertation was titled *Singular perturbations of an ordinary differential equation*. [Norman Levinson](wikipedia://en/1090122) was her doctoral advisor.[^n3] She met her future husband, Felix Haas (mathematician), a fellow mathematician at MIT.[^n4] Haas was selected as an [American Association of University Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Women) Vassie James Hill Fellow in 1951.[^n2][^n4] She was a member of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa), [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi), and [Eta Kappa Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eta_Kappa_Nu).[^n2]

## Career {#Career}

Haas was a lecturer at [Immaculata College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculata_College) from 1952 to 1955 and an instructor at the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) from 1955 to 1956.[^n2] She was a faculty member at the [University of Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Detroit) from 1957 to 1962.[^n2] Haas also taught at [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University).[^n4]

She joined the faculty at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in January 1962[^n1] as an assistant professor in the college of electrical engineering and computer engineering.[^n4] By 1978, Haas was a full professor of electrical engineering in the [Purdue University College of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University_College_of_Engineering).[^n4][^n2] Her areas of expertise included [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), [nonlinear control](wikipedia://en/3359147), and [optimal estimation](wikipedia://en/34426869).[^n2] Due to nepotism rules (her husband was a fellow mathematician), Haas took a position in electrical engineering rather than mathematics.[^n5]

Haas advocated for [women in STEM fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_STEM_fields). Some of her earlier academic environments were hostile to women.[^n5] In a few instances, she was the only department member excluded from grant proposals. This had largely improved by the early 1980s.[^n5] For 15 years, Haas was the counselor of the Purdue University student chapter of the [Society of Women Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Women_Engineers).[^n5]

Haas joined the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in 1975, serving as a coordinator for the speakers' bureau.[^n5] She was a member of the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) (IEEE) committee on professional opportunities for women and the [American Society for Engineering Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Engineering_Education) (ASEE) constituent committee on women in engineering.[^n5] For Haas' support and encouragement of women students in engineering, in 1977, she was elected as one of five "Very Important Women" on campus by the Association of Women Students.[^n6] In 1977, Haas received the D.D. Ewing Award as an outstanding teacher in the Purdue School of Electrical Engineering.[^n7] She received the 1978 Helen B. Schleman Medallion Award for her service and encouragement of women in academic and professional areas.[^n7] In the 1970s, Haas was a nominee for the distinguished science award of the Society of Women Engineers.[^n7]

From 1983 to 1984, Haas was a visiting professor at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) through the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) visiting professorships for women program. In this position, she was a full time researcher investigating [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) and infinite dimensional control problems.[^n5]

Haas was active in the [control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_engineering) community and was on the program committee of the [American Control Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Control_Conference). She was also the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) representative to the IEEE conference on decision and control.[^n5] Mathematician and colleague [Pamela G. Coxson](wikipedia://en/70585397) stated that Haas' involvement "increased the participation of the mathematical community in these two annual conferences."[^n5] She is included in biographical listings of *[Who's Who in the Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who's_Who_in_the_Midwest)*, *Who's Who of American Women*, and *[American Men and Women of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Men_and_Women_of_Science)*.[^n6] She was a former editor of the *Women in Engineering Students Newsletter*.[^n1] Haas was a member of the American Association of University Women,[^n6] [League of Women Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Women_Voters), [YWCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YWCA), and served on the board of directors of the Lafayette Symphony.[^n1][^n2]

Haas and sociologist [Carolyn C. Perrucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolyn_C._Perrucci) co-edited the book (1984). "Women in Scientific and Engineering Professions". University of Michigan Press. isbn:978-0-472-10049-1[^n4]

Eight months after leaving MIT in 1984, Haas was diagnosed with a brain tumor and was soon unconscious.[^n5]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Haas resided in [West Lafayette, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lafayette,_Indiana).[^n1] She was married to Felix Haas.[^n1] They had a daughter and two sons.[^n1]

Haas was unconscious from a [brain tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_tumor) from 1984 until her death on the morning of January 21, 1986, at St. Elizabeth Hospital.[^n1][^n6]

In 1990, the Council on the Status of Women at Purdue University established Violet B. Haas award that recognizes people who promote the status of women at the university.[^n4]
