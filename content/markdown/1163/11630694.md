# Almost symplectic manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Almost symplectic manifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), an **almost symplectic structure** on a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) $M$ is a [non-degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-degenerate) [two-form](wikipedia://en/221519) $\omega$ on $M$. If, in addition, $\omega$ is [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_differential_form), then it is a [symplectic structure](wikipedia://en/28356).[^o2][^o3]

:::

An almost symplectic manifold is equivalent to an [Sp-structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure); requiring $\omega$ to be closed is an [integrability condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrability_condition).

## Relation to other geometric structures {#Relation_to_other_geometric_structures}

An almost symplectic manifold is a pair $(M, \omega)$ of a smooth manifold and an almost symplectic structure. The manifold $M$ can be equipped with extra structures, such as a positive-definite bilinear form $g$ (i.e. a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric)) or an [almost complex structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure) $J$. Furthermore, these extra structures can be required to be compatible with each other, making the quadruple $(M, \omega, g, J)$ into an [almost Hermitian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_Hermitian_manifold).

However, this definition does not assume any further [integrability condition](wikipedia://en/968734). With increasing assumptions on integrability, one gets increasingly [rigid](wikipedia://en/1458875) (i.e. less [generic](wikipedia://en/12886758)) geometric structures:

1. Almost symplectic manifolds. Can always be extended to an [almost Hermitian structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_Hermitian_manifold).
1. [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356): $\omega$ closed. Can always be extended to an [almost Kähler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_Kähler_manifold) structure.
1. [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/514258#Integrable_almost_complex_structures): $J$ integrable. Can always be extended to an [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/2640459) structure.
1. [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538): $\omega$ closed and $J$ integrable.

Note that, for instance, an almost symplectic manifold might be extensible to inequivalent almost Hermitian manifolds, which is why they are different concepts.

The inclusion relations are $4 \subset 3 \cap 2$ and $3 \cup 2 \subset 1$. All these inclusions are strict, due to the following counterexamples:

- The Kodaira–Thurston 4-manifold is symplectic and complex but not Kähler. Indeed, it is a compact [nilmanifold](wikipedia://en/3668693) and its first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) is $b_1 = 3$, but any compact Kähler manifold must have even odd-Betti numbers.[^o5]
- [Fernández](wikipedia://en/68933833), Gotay and [Gray](wikipedia://en/1465376) described a compact 4-manifold which is symplectic but not complex, hence not Kähler.[^o6]
- The [Hopf surface](wikipedia://en/7016769), and more generally [Hopf manifolds](wikipedia://en/7016707), are compact complex manifolds but not symplectic, hence not Kähler.
- For a small $\epsilon > 0$, the 2-form $\omega_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^n d p_i \wedge d q_i+\varepsilon q_1 d p_2 \wedge d q_2$ makes $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ into an almost symplectic manifold that is not symplectic nor complex.

## From almost symplectic to almost Hermitian manifolds {#From_almost_symplectic_to_almost_Hermitian_manifolds}

Given an almost symplectic manifold $(M, \omega)$, an almost Hermitian structure $(\omega, g, J)$ can be constructed by means of a [structural group reduction](wikipedia://en/1277699) from $\mathrm{Sp}(2 n, \mathbb{R})$ to $\mathrm{U}(n)$ and the [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874) construction.

Indeed, the $\omega$-symplectic frame bundle $P_{\mathrm{Sp}} \rightarrow M$ has structure group $\operatorname{Sp}(2 n, \mathbb{R})$. The subgroup $\mathrm{U}(n)=\operatorname{Sp}(2 n, \mathbb{R}) \cap \mathrm{O}(2 n)$ is the stabilizer of a compatible pair $(J, g)$, with $J^2=-I$ and $g(\cdot, \cdot)= \omega(\cdot, J \cdot)$.

The associated bundle

$$\mathcal{J}_\omega:=P_{\mathrm{Sp}} \times_{\mathrm{Sp}(2 n, \mathbb{R})}(\mathrm{Sp}(2 n, \mathbb{R}) / \mathrm{U}(n)) \rightarrow M,$$

whose fiber at $x$ is the set of $\omega_x$-compatible almost complex structures. The homogeneous space $\mathrm{Sp}(2 n, \mathbb{R}) / \mathrm{U}(n)$ is nonempty and [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414).

A reduction $P_{\mathrm{U}} \subset P_{\mathrm{Sp}}$ exists if and only if $\mathcal{J}_\omega$ has a global section. Contractible fiber implies no obstruction classes; a section exists. Its value is a smooth bundle morphism $J: TM \to TM$ with $J^2=-I$ and $\omega(\cdot, J \cdot)$ positive-definite.
