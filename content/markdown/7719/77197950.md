# Geertruida Wijthoff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geertruida Wijthoff"}

**Geertruida** "**Truida**" **Wijthoff** (30 August 1859 – 13 March 1953) was a Dutch mathematician and teacher. In 1907 she became a member of merit of the [Royal Dutch Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/43504918).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Truida (birthname, Anna Geertruida Wijthoff) was the eldest of four children born into the wealthy family of Abraham Willem Wijthoff and Anna Catharina Frederika Kerkhoven. Truida's father was a Lutheran and son of the Amsterdam sugar refinery family Wijthoff & Son. Truida's family first lived at Lauriergracht 111, just next to the sugar refinery that burned down in 1880. (In 1911, they moved to PC Hooftstraat 28 in Amsterdam.)[^o4] Truida's younger sister was the writer Henriëtte Wijthoff and her next younger sibling was Anna Catharina Frederika Wijthoff, a painter and illustrator of children's books. The youngest child was the mathematical theorist and teacher [Willem Abraham Wythoff](wikipedia://en/8867144).[^o5]

In 1881, at the age of 22, Truida enrolled at the [Athenaeum Illustre of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenaeum_Illustre_of_Amsterdam) to study mathematics and physics. She and [Marie du Saar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_du_Saar), who registered as a student in medicine that same autumn, were two of the first women to study there, preceded only by the physician [Aletta Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aletta_Jacobs).[^o5] After graduation, Truida became a teacher at the girls' school in [Middelburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middelburg,_Zeeland) from 1884 to 1886. She then returned to Amsterdam to work for the Administration Office for the Management of American Railway Values, which was owned by the Amsterdam banker Wertheim.[^o5]

Like her only brother, Truida was an avid solver of the problems section in the *New Archive for Mathematics* (NAW). After she received an honorable mention (the highest attainable prize)[^o4] for the fifth time in the annual competition, she was appointed a member of merit of the [Royal Dutch Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/43504918) in 1907. Even after the family moved to Apeldoorn, she won the NAW competition many more times, and in 1923 she received her tenth honorable mention.[^o4]

In 1914, Truida became a member of the Apeldoorn branch of the Association against [Quackery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quackery). At the end of the nineteenth century, she also became involved in the Masonic weekly, a magazine for the Order of [Freemasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

On 23 September 1898, she married her cousin Julius Kerkhoven[^o7] who for 20 years, had worked as a civil engineer for Tjiandjoer and Tjilatjap on [West Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Java) in Indonesia, and in [Padang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang) and Batu Taba on [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra) for the Dutch-Indian Railway Company. Julius was the younger brother of Rudolf Kerkhoven, the inspiration for the main character in [Hella Haasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hella_Haasse)'s novel *De Heren van de Thee*.[^o5]

After her husband's death, Truida continued to live in [Apeldoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apeldoorn) with her sister Henriëtte. She survived the other three Wijthoff children and died on 13 March 1953.
