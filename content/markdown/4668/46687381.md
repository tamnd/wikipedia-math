# David Firth (statistician)

::: {#o10 .subject cue=lead name="David Firth"}

**David Firth** (born 22 December 1957) is a British [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661).[^o2] He is [Emeritus Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_Professor) in the Department of Statistics at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick).[^o2]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Firth was born and went to school in [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield).[^o2] He studied mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge)[^o2] and completed his PhD in statistics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London),[^o2] supervised by [Sir David Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771).[^o3]

## Research {#Research}

Firth is known for his development of a general method[^o4] for reducing the [bias](wikipedia://en/8450479) of [maximum likelihood estimation](wikipedia://en/140806) in parametric [statistical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_models). The method has seen application in a wide variety of research fields, especially with [logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631) analysis where the reduced-bias estimates also have reduced [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) and are always finite;[^o6] the latter property overcomes the frequently encountered problem of [separation](wikipedia://en/29668256), which causes maximum likelihood estimates to be infinite. The original paper published in 1993[^o4] has been cited more than 4000 times according to [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.co.uk/citations?user=rtOYz5YAAAAJ&hl=en&oi=ao).

Together with a PhD student, Renée de Menezes, Firth also established the generality of the method of [quasi variances](wikipedia://en/53493692), a device for summarizing economically the estimated effects of a categorical predictor variable in a statistical model.[^o8][^o9]

## Applied work {#Applied_work}

Firth developed (in collaboration with [John Curtice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Curtice)) a new statistical approach to the design and analysis of election-day exit polls for [UK General Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_general_elections_overview). The new methods have been used at UK General Elections since 2005 to produce the widely broadcast close-of-polls forecast of seats in the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom).[^o7]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Firth was elected as a [Fellow of the British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_British_Academy) in 2008. He was the recipient of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664)'s [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Bronze in 1998 and in Silver in 2012. With Dr Heather Turner he won the [John M Chambers Statistical Software Award](wikipedia://en/16745085#John_M._Chambers_Statistical_Software_Award) of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 2007, for the [*gnm* package](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/gnm) which facilitates working with generalized nonlinear models (a synthesis of [nonlinear regression](wikipedia://en/1045012) and [generalized linear models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_linear_models)) in [R](wikipedia://en/376707).

He is a former Editor of the [Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B (Statistical Methodology)](wikipedia://en/13327143#Journal_of_the_Royal_Statistical_Society,_Series_B_%28Statistical_Methodology%29).
