# Victor L. Shapiro

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Victor L. Shapiro"}

**Victor Lenard Shapiro** (16 October 1924, Chicago – 1 March 2013, [Riverside, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside,_California)) was an American mathematician, specializing in trigonometric series and differential equations.[^o2] He is known for his two theorems (published in 1957) on the uniqueness of multiple [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

In September 1944, he was awarded the [Combat Infantry Badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_Infantry_Badge) for action on the South Pacific island of [Bougainville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bougainville_Campaign). In April 1945, serving as a combat medic with the [132nd infantry regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/132nd_Infantry_Regiment_%28United_States%29), he was in the 6th wave of a beachhead landing on [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu) and saw much action in the ensuing campaign.[^o2]

From the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), Shapiro received B.Sc. in 1947, M.Sc. in 1949, and Ph.D. in 1952, all in mathematics.[^o6] His thesis advisor was [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477). Shapiro was from 1952 to 1960 a professor at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) and from 1960 to 1964 a professor at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon) with 3 sabbatical years (in 1953–1955 and 1958–1959) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o8] He was a professor at the [University of California, Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Riverside) from 1964 to 2010, when he retired as professor emeritus.[^o6][^o8] He was the author of several books and the author or coauthor of over 80 articles in refereed journals.[^o6]

Shapiro was elected in 2003 a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (AAAS) and in 2012 a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS).[^o2] In November 1995 in Riverside, California, a conference was held in his honor.[^o9]

Upon his death he was survived by his widow, 4 children, and 13 grandchildren.[^o2]
