# Septic equation

*Image: Septic graph.svg.* Graph of a polynomial of degree 7, with 7 [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) (crossings of the $x$ axis) and 6 [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424). Depending on the number and vertical location of the [minima and maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum), the septic could have 7, 5, 3, or 1 real root counted with their multiplicity; the number of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) non-real roots is 7 minus the number of real roots.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Septic equation"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **septic equation** is an [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) of the form

:::

$$ax^7+bx^6+cx^5+dx^4+ex^3+fx^2+gx+h=0,\,$$

where $a \ne 0$.

A **septic function** is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of the form

$$f(x)=ax^7+bx^6+cx^5+dx^4+ex^3+fx^2+gx+h\,$$

where $a \ne 0$. In other words, it is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) seven. If $a = 0$, then *f* is a [sextic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextic_function) ($b \ne 0$), [quintic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintic_function) ($b = 0, c \ne 0$), etc.

The equation may be obtained from the function by setting $f(x) = 0$.

The *coefficients* $a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h$ may be either [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers), [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) or, more generally, members of any [field](wikipedia://en/10603) or [ring](wikipedia://en/61346).

Because they have an odd degree, **septic functions** appear similar to [quintic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintic_function) and [cubic functions](wikipedia://en/243709) when graphed, except they may possess additional [local maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima) and local minima (up to three maxima and three minima). The [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of a septic function is a [sextic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextic_function), except possibly when the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of the ring containing the coefficients is divisible by 7.

## Solvable septics {#Solvable_septics}

Some seventh degree equations can be solved by factorizing into [radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_expression), but other septics cannot. [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815) developed techniques for determining whether a given equation could be solved by radicals which gave rise to the field of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316). To give an example of an irreducible but solvable septic, one can generalize the solvable [de Moivre quintic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Moivre_quintic) to get,

$$x^7+7\alpha x^5+14\alpha^2x^3+7\alpha^3x+\beta = 0\,,$$

where the auxiliary equation is

$$y^2+\beta y-\alpha^7 = 0\,.$$

This means that the septic is obtained by eliminating $u$ and $v$ between $x = u + v$, $uv + \alpha = 0$ and $u^{7} + v^{7} + \beta = 0$.

It follows that the septic's seven roots are given by

$$x_k = \omega_k\sqrt[7]{y_1} + \omega_k^6\sqrt[7]{y_2}$$

where $\omega_{k}$ is any of the 7 seventh [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950). The [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of this septic is the maximal solvable group of order 42. This is easily generalized to any other degrees $k$, not necessarily prime.

Another solvable family is,

$$x^7-2x^6+(\alpha+1)x^5+(\alpha-1)x^4-\alpha x^3-(\alpha+5)x^2-6x-4 = 0\,$$

whose members appear in Kluner's *Database of Number Fields*. Its [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) is

$$\Delta = -4^4\left(4\alpha^3+99\alpha^2-34\alpha+467\right)^3\,$$

The [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of these septics is the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of order 14.

The general septic equation can be solved with the [alternating](wikipedia://en/49726) or [symmetric](wikipedia://en/28901) [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673) $A_{7}$ or $S_{7}$.[^o3] Such equations require [hyperelliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperelliptic_function) and associated [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745) of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 3 for their solution.[^o3] However, these equations were not studied specifically by the nineteenth-century mathematicians studying the solutions of algebraic equations, because the [sextic equations](wikipedia://en/5918099)' solutions were already at the limits of their computational abilities without computers.[^o3]

Septics are the lowest order equations for which it is not obvious that their solutions may be obtained by composing *continuous functions* of two variables. [Hilbert's 13th problem](wikipedia://en/2336190) was the conjecture this was not possible in the general case for seventh-degree equations. [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490) solved this in 1957, demonstrating that this was always possible.[^o4] However, Arnold himself considered the *genuine* Hilbert problem to be whether for septics their solutions may be obtained by superimposing *algebraic functions* of two variables.[^o5] As of 2023, the problem is still open.

## Galois groups {#Galois_groups}

*Image: Fano plane.svg.* [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404)

There are seven [Galois groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_groups) for septics:[^o7]

- Septic equations solvable by radicals have a Galois group which is either the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 7, or the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of order 14, or a [metacyclic group](wikipedia://en/7515561) of order 21 or 42.[^o3]
- The $L(3, 2)$ [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) (of order 168) is formed by the [permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutations) of the 7 vertex labels which preserve the 7 "lines" in the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404).[^o3] Septic equations with this [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $L(3, 2)$ require [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) but not [hyperelliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperelliptic_function) for their solution.[^o3]
- Otherwise the Galois group of a septic is either the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) of order $7!/2=2520$ or the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of order $7!=5040.$

## Septic equation for the squared area of a cyclic pentagon or hexagon {#Septic_equation_for_the_squared_area_of_a_cyclic_pentagon_or_hexagon}

The square of the area of a [cyclic pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014#Cyclic_pentagons) is a root of a septic equation whose coefficients are [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359) of the sides of the pentagon.[^o9] The same is true of the square of the area of a [cyclic hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733#Cyclic_hexagon).[^o10]
