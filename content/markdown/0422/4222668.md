# Witt's theorem

*"Witt's theorem" or "the Witt theorem" may also refer to the [Bourbaki–Witt fixed point theorem](wikipedia://en/1011270) of order theory.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Witt's theorem"}

In mathematics, **Witt's theorem**, named after [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911), is a basic result in the [algebraic theory](wikipedia://en/24497685) of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478): any [isometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometry_%28quadratic_forms%29) between two [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) of a nonsingular [quadratic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_space) over a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28algebra%29) *k* may be extended to an isometry of the whole space. An analogous statement holds also for [skew-symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew-symmetric_form), [Hermitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form) and [skew-Hermitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew-Hermitian_form) [bilinear forms](wikipedia://en/449477) over arbitrary fields. The theorem applies to classification of quadratic forms over *k* and in particular allows one to define the [Witt group](wikipedia://en/5578523) *W*(*k*) which describes the "stable" theory of quadratic forms over the field *k*.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let (*V*, *b*) be a [finite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28algebra%29) *k* of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) different from 2 together with a [non-degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-degenerate) symmetric or skew-symmetric [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477). If *f* : *U* → *U* is an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) between two subspaces of *V* then *f* extends to an isometry of *V*.[^n1]

Witt's theorem implies that the dimension of a maximal [totally isotropic subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_isotropic) (null space) of *V* is an invariant, called the **index** or **Witt index** of *b*,[^n2][^n3] and moreover, that the [isometry group](wikipedia://en/1101694) of (*V*, *b*) [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) [transitively](wikipedia://en/12781#Transitivity_properties) on the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of maximal isotropic subspaces. This fact plays an important role in the structure theory and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/12695) of the isometry group and in the theory of [reductive dual pairs](wikipedia://en/11802934).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Witt's cancellation theorem"}

## Witt's cancellation theorem {#Witt's_cancellation_theorem}

Let (*V*, *q*), (*V*<sub>1</sub>, *q*<sub>1</sub>), (*V*<sub>2</sub>, *q*<sub>2</sub>) be three quadratic spaces over a field *k*. Assume that

$$(V_1,q_1)\oplus(V,q) \simeq (V_2,q_2)\oplus(V,q).$$

Then the quadratic spaces (*V*<sub>1</sub>, *q*<sub>1</sub>) and (*V*<sub>2</sub>, *q*<sub>2</sub>) are isometric:

$$(V_1,q_1)\simeq (V_2,q_2).$$

In other words, the direct summand (*V*, *q*) appearing in both sides of an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between quadratic spaces may be "cancelled".

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Witt's decomposition theorem"}

## Witt's decomposition theorem {#Witt's_decomposition_theorem}

Let (*V*, *q*) be a quadratic space over a field *k*. Then it admits a **Witt decomposition**:

$$(V,q)\simeq (V_0,0)\oplus(V_a, q_a)\oplus (V_h,q_h),$$

where *V*<sub>0</sub> = ker *q* is the [radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_of_a_quadratic_space) of *q*, (*V*<sub>*a*</sub>, *q*<sub>*a*</sub>) is an [anisotropic quadratic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropic_quadratic_space) and (*V*<sub>*h*</sub>, *q*<sub>*h*</sub>) is a [split quadratic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_quadratic_space). Moreover, the anisotropic summand, termed the **core form**, and the hyperbolic summand in a Witt decomposition of (*V*, *q*) are determined uniquely up to isomorphism.[^n4]

Quadratic forms with the same core form are said to be *similar* or **Witt equivalent**.

:::
