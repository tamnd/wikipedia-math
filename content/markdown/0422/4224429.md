::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Truncation"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **truncation** is an operation in any dimension that cuts [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) vertices, creating a new [facet](wikipedia://en/7186647) in place of each vertex. The term originates from [Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler)'s names for the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847).

:::

A different truncation is defined for otherwise infinite [surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28geometry%29) via the intersection with another surface, particularly a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29), such as in a [truncated cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_cone).

## Uniform truncation {#Uniform_truncation}

In general any [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) (or polytope) can also be truncated with a degree of freedom as to how deep the cut is, as shown in [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705) truncation operation.

A special kind of truncation, usually implied, is a **uniform truncation**, a truncation operator applied to a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299) (or [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693)) which creates a resulting [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223) ([uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392)) with equal edge lengths. There are no degrees of freedom, and it represents a fixed geometric, just like the regular polyhedra.

In general all single ringed uniform polytopes have a uniform truncation. For example, the [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023), represented as [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) r{5,3} or $\begin{Bmatrix} 5 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) or has a uniform truncation, the [truncated icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/286534), represented as tr{5,3} or $t\begin{Bmatrix} 5 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, . In the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), the effect of a truncation is to ring all the nodes adjacent to the ringed node.

A uniform truncation performed on the regular [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) {3,6} results in the regular [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) {6,3}.

## Truncation of polygons {#Truncation_of_polygons}

A truncated n-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) will have 2n sides (edges). A regular polygon uniformly truncated will become another regular polygon: t{n} is {2n}. A complete truncation (or [rectification](wikipedia://en/2192366)), r{3}, is another regular polygon in its [dual](wikipedia://en/19865549) position.

A regular polygon can also be represented by its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), , and its uniform truncation , and its complete truncation . The graph represents [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) I<sub>2</sub>(n), with each node representing a mirror, and the edge representing the angle π/*n* between the mirrors, and a circle is given around one or both mirrors to show which ones are active.

[Star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303) can also be truncated. A truncated [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544) {5/2} will look like a [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014), but is actually a double-covered (degenerate) [decagon](wikipedia://en/327393) ({10/2}) with two sets of overlapping vertices and edges. A truncated great [heptagram](wikipedia://en/180673) {7/3} gives a [tetradecagram](wikipedia://en/6058229) {14/3}.

## Uniform truncation in regular polyhedra and tilings and higher {#Uniform_truncation_in_regular_polyhedra_and_tilings_and_higher}

*Image: Birectified cube sequence.png.* Truncations of the cube beyond rectification

When "truncation" applies to [platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) or [regular tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilings_of_regular_polygons), usually "uniform truncation" is implied, which means truncating until the original faces become regular polygons with twice as many sides as the original form.

*Image: Cube truncation sequence.svg.*

This sequence shows an example of the truncation of a cube, using four steps of a continuous truncating process between a full [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and a [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) cube. The final polyhedron is a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280). The middle image is the uniform [truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529); it is represented by a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) ***t***{*p*,*q*,...}.

A **[bitruncation](wikipedia://en/6792316)** is a deeper truncation, removing all the original edges, but leaving an interior part of the original faces. Example: a [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654) is a bitruncated cube: t{3,4} = 2t{4,3}.

A complete bitruncation, called a **birectification**, reduces original faces to points. For polyhedra, this becomes the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815). Example: an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) is a birectification of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285): {3,4} = 2r{4,3}.

Another type of truncation, [cantellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantellation), cuts edges and vertices, removing the original edges, replacing them with rectangles, removing the original vertices, and replacing them with the faces of the dual of the original regular polyhedra or tiling.

Higher dimensional polytopes have higher truncations. [Runcination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runcination) cuts faces, edges, and vertices. In 5 dimensions, [sterication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterication) cuts cells, faces, and edges.

## Edge-truncation {#Edge-truncation}

**Edge-truncation** is a beveling, or [chamfer](wikipedia://en/43468103) for polyhedra, similar to cantellation, but retaining the original vertices, and replacing edges by hexagons. In 4-polytopes, edge-truncation replaces edges with [elongated bipyramid](wikipedia://en/3522878) cells.

## Alternation or partial truncation {#Alternation_or_partial_truncation}

Alternation or partial truncation removes only some of the original vertices.

In **partial truncation**, or [**alternation**](wikipedia://en/4349117), half of the vertices and connecting edges are completely removed. The operation applies only to polytopes with even-sided faces. Faces are reduced to half as many sides, and square faces degenerate into edges. For example, the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) is an alternated cube, h{4,3}.

[**Diminishment**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishment_%28geometry%29) is a more general term used in reference to [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) for the removal of one or more vertices, edges, or faces of a polytope, without disturbing the other vertices. For example, the [tridiminished icosahedron](wikipedia://en/1123559) starts with a regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) with 3 vertices removed.

Other partial truncations are symmetry-based; for example, the tetrahedrally diminished dodecahedron.

## Generalized truncations {#Generalized_truncations}

*Image: Types\_of\_truncation\_on\_edge.png.* Types of truncations shown on an [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) isolated from a larger polygon or polyhedron with red and blue vertices. The edge reverses direction after complete truncation.

The linear truncation process can be generalized by allowing parametric truncations that are negative, or that go beyond the midpoint of the edges, causing self-intersecting star polyhedra, and can parametrically relate to some of the [regular star polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_star_polygon) and [uniform star polyhedra](wikipedia://en/8464397).

- **Shallow truncation** - Edges are reduced in length, faces are truncated to have twice as many sides, while new [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) are formed, centered at the old vertices.
- **Uniform truncation** are a special case of this with equal edge lengths. The [truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529), t{4,3}, with square faces becoming octagons, with new triangular faces are the vertices.
- **Antitruncation** A reverse *shallow truncation*, truncated outwards off the original edges, rather than inward. This results in a polytope which looks like the original, but has parts of the dual dangling off its corners, instead of the dual cutting into its own corners.
- **Complete truncation** or [**rectification**](wikipedia://en/2192366) - The limit of a shallow truncation, where edges are reduced to points. The [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), r{4,3}, is an example.
- **Hypertruncation** A form of truncation that goes past the rectification, inverting the original edges, and causing self-intersections to appear.
- **Quasitruncation** A form of truncation that goes even farther than hypertruncation where the inverted edge becomes longer than the original edge. It can be generated from the original polytope by treating all the faces as retrograde, i.e. going backwards round the vertex. For example, quasitruncating the [square](wikipedia://en/659939) gives a regular [octagram](wikipedia://en/2667613) (t{4/3}={8/3}), and quasitruncating the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) gives the uniform [stellated truncated hexahedron](wikipedia://en/4157363), t{4/3,3}.
