# Yoshiharu Kohayakawa

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yoshiharu Kohayakawa"}

*Image: Random Structures in the Brain 060.jpg.* Yoshiharu Kohayakawa, in 2017.

**Yoshiharu Kohayakawa** (Japanese: 小早川美晴; born 1963) is a [Japanese-Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese-Brazilian) mathematician working on [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o2] He is known for his work on [Szemerédi's regularity lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szemerédi's_regularity_lemma), which he extended to [sparser graphs](wikipedia://en/2777098#Sparse_and_tight_graphs).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kohayakawa has a PhD advised by [Béla Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) with the dissertation *External Combinatorics and the Evolution of Random Graphs*.[^o6]

According to [Google Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Scholar), as of August 21, 2019, Kohayakawa's works have been cited over 3194 times, and his [h-index](wikipedia://en/3604693) is 33.[^o7]

He is a titular member of the [Brazilian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o2]

Kohayakawa has an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 1.[^o8][^o9]

He was awarded the 2018 [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916).[^o10]
