# Differential graded module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential graded module"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **differential graded module**, or **dg-module**, is a $\mathbb{Z}$-[graded module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_module) together with a differential; i.e., a square-zero graded [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) of the [module](wikipedia://en/276410) of degree 1 or −1, depending on the convention. In other words, it is a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) having a structure of a module, while a [differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863) is a chain complex with a structure of an [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring).

:::

In view of the module-variant of [Dold–Kan correspondence](wikipedia://en/39431504), the notion of an $\mathbb{N}_0$-graded dg-module is equivalent to that of a **simplicial module**; "[equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051)" in the [categorical](wikipedia://en/5869) sense; see below.

## The Dold–Kan correspondence {#The_Dold–Kan_correspondence}

Given a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*, by definition, the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [simplicial modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_module) are [simplicial objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_object) in the [category of *R*-modules](wikipedia://en/11231812); denoted by ***s***Mod<sub>*R*</sub>. Then ***s***Mod<sub>*R*</sub> can be identified with the category of differential graded modules which vanish in negative degrees via the [Dold-Kan correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dold-Kan_correspondence).[^o3]
