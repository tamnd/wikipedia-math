# Chazelle polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chazelle polyhedron"}

*Image: Chazelle polyhedron.gif.* The Chazelle polyhedron

**Chazelle polyhedron** is a non-convex [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) constructed by removing pieces of [wedges](wikipedia://en/2951640) from both top and bottom of a cube's sides, leaving the notches. Its [saddle surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_surface) can be considered as the set of line segments that lie forming the [hyperbolic paraboloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_paraboloid) with an equation $z = xy$.[^o2] This polyhedron is named after [Bernard Chazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Chazelle).[^o3]

:::

Originally, the Chazelle polyhedron was intended to prove the quadratic lower bound of complexity on the decomposition of convex polyhedra in three dimensions.[^o2] The later applications are used regarding the problem related to the construction of lower bounds as in the [binary space partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_space_partition),[^o4] [bounding volume hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounding_volume_hierarchy) for [collision detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision_detection),[^o5] decomposability of fat-polyhedra,[^o6] and optimal triangulation in [mesh generation](wikipedia://en/3050716) with its element's size.[^o7]
