# Hugo Hadwiger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hugo Hadwiger"}

*Image: Hugo Hadwiger.jpg.* Hugo Hadwiger in 1973

**Hugo Hadwiger** (23 December 1908 in [Karlsruhe, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe,_Germany) – 29 October 1981 in [Bern, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern,_Switzerland))[^o2] was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Although born in [Karlsruhe, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe,_Germany), Hadwiger grew up in [Bern, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern,_Switzerland).[^o4] He did his undergraduate studies at the [University of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bern), where he majored in mathematics but also studied physics and [actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373).[^o4] He continued at Bern for his graduate studies, and received his Ph.D. in 1936 under the supervision of Willy Scherrer.[^o5] He was for more than forty years a professor of mathematics at Bern.[^o23]

## Mathematical concepts named after Hadwiger {#Mathematical_concepts_named_after_Hadwiger}

[Hadwiger's theorem](wikipedia://en/629554) in [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336) classifies the isometry-invariant [valuations](wikipedia://en/63011965) on [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) in *d*-dimensional Euclidean space. According to this theorem, any such valuation can be expressed as a linear combination of the [intrinsic volumes](wikipedia://en/32953045#Quermassintegrals); for instance, in two dimensions, the intrinsic volumes are the [area](wikipedia://en/1209), the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636), and the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420).[^o7]

The [Hadwiger–Finsler inequality](wikipedia://en/12802352), proven by Hadwiger with [Paul Finsler](wikipedia://en/27007280), is an inequality relating the side lengths and area of any [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410).[^o8] It generalizes [Weitzenböck's inequality](wikipedia://en/10777748) and was generalized in turn by [Pedoe's inequality](wikipedia://en/601060). In the same 1937 paper in which Hadwiger and Finsler published this inequality, they also published the [Finsler–Hadwiger theorem](wikipedia://en/27007324) on a square derived from two other squares that share a vertex.

Hadwiger's name is also associated with several important unsolved problems in mathematics:

- The [Hadwiger conjecture in graph theory](wikipedia://en/1657860), posed by Hadwiger in 1943[^o9] and called by (Bollobás Catlin Erdős 1980) “one of the deepest unsolved problems in graph theory,”[^o11] describes a conjectured connection between [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) and [graph minors](wikipedia://en/353042). The [Hadwiger number](wikipedia://en/1665366) of a graph is the number of vertices in the largest [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) that can be formed as a minor in the graph; the Hadwiger conjecture states that this is always at least as large as the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number).
- The [Hadwiger conjecture in combinatorial geometry](wikipedia://en/21851601) concerns the minimum number of smaller copies of a convex body needed to cover the body, or equivalently the minimum number of light sources needed to illuminate the surface of the body; for instance, in three dimensions, it is known that any convex body can be illuminated by 16 light sources, but Hadwiger's conjecture implies that only eight light sources are always sufficient.[^o12][^o13]
- The [Hadwiger–Kneser–Poulsen conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadwiger–Kneser–Poulsen_conjecture) states that, if the centers of a system of balls in Euclidean space are moved closer together, then the volume of the union of the balls cannot increase. It has been proven in the plane, but remains open in higher dimensions.[^o14]
- The [Hadwiger–Nelson problem](wikipedia://en/3133115) concerns the minimum number of colors needed to color the points of the Euclidean plane so that no two points at unit distance from each other are given the same color. It was first proposed by [Edward Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561#Academic_work) in 1950. Hadwiger popularized it by including it in a problem collection in 1961;[^o15][^o16] already in 1945 he had published a related result, showing that any cover of the plane by five congruent closed sets contains a unit distance in one of the sets.[^o17]

## Other mathematical contributions {#Other_mathematical_contributions}

Hadwiger proved a theorem characterizing [eutactic stars](wikipedia://en/24141211), systems of points in Euclidean space formed by [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of higher-dimensional [cross polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_polytope). He found a higher-dimensional generalization of the space-filling [Hill tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/20700599).[^o19] And his 1957 book *Vorlesungen über Inhalt, Oberfläche und Isoperimetrie* was foundational for the theory of [Minkowski functionals](wikipedia://en/3510908), used in [mathematical morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_morphology).

## Cryptographic work {#Cryptographic_work}

Hadwiger was one of the principal developers of a Swiss [rotor machine](wikipedia://en/196223) for encrypting military communications, known as [NEMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEMA_%28cipher%29). The Swiss, fearing that the Germans and Allies could read messages transmitted on their [Enigma cipher machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_cipher_machine), enhanced the system by using ten rotors instead of five. The system was used by the Swiss army and air force between 1947 and 1992.[^o21]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

[Asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) [2151 Hadwiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2151_Hadwiger), discovered in 1977 by [Paul Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wild_%28Swiss_astronomer%29), is named after Hadwiger.[^o23]

The first article in the "Research Problems" section of the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* was dedicated by [Victor Klee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Klee) to Hadwiger, on the occasion of his 60th birthday, in honor of Hadwiger's work editing a column on unsolved problems in the journal *[Elemente der Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemente_der_Mathematik)*.[^o4]
