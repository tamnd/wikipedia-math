# Mahler's compactness theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mahler's compactness theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Mahler's compactness theorem**, proved by (txt), is a foundational result on [lattices](wikipedia://en/469246) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), characterising sets of lattices that are 'bounded' in a certain definite sense. Looked at another way, it explains the ways in which a lattice could [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) (*go off to infinity*) in a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of lattices. In intuitive terms it says that this is possible in just two ways: becoming *coarse-grained* with a [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) that has ever larger volume; or containing shorter and shorter vectors. It is also called his **selection theorem**, following an older convention used in naming compactness theorems, because they were formulated in terms of [sequential compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequential_compactness) (the possibility of selecting a convergent subsequence).

:::

Let *X* be the space

$$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$$

that parametrises lattices in $\mathbb{R}^n$, with its [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology). There is a [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined) function Δ on *X*, which is the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of a matrix – this is constant on the [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759), since an [invertible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible) integer matrix has [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) 1 or −1.

**Mahler's compactness theorem** states that a subset *Y* of *X* is [relatively compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_compact) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) Δ is [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) on *Y*, and there is a neighbourhood *N* of 0 in $\mathbb{R}^n$ such that for all Λ in *Y*, the only lattice point of Λ in *N* is 0 itself.

The assertion of Mahler's theorem is equivalent to the compactness of the space of unit-covolume lattices in $\mathbb{R}^n$ whose [systole](wikipedia://en/2545455) is larger or equal than any fixed $\varepsilon>0$.

Mahler's compactness theorem was generalized to [semisimple Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) by [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594); see [Mumford's compactness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumford's_compactness_theorem).
