# RC2

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="RC2"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **RC2** (also known as **ARC2**) is a [symmetric-key](wikipedia://en/53042) [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) designed by [Ron Rivest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Rivest) in 1987. "RC" stands for "Ron's Code"[^o3] (see also [RC2](wikipedia://en/632592), [RC5](wikipedia://en/102600) and [RC6](wikipedia://en/160216)), but generally called simply RC2. Other ciphers designed by Ron Rivest include [RC4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC4_%28cipher%29), [RC5](wikipedia://en/102600), and [RC6](wikipedia://en/160216).

:::

The development of RC2 was sponsored by [Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Development), who were seeking a custom [cipher](wikipedia://en/5244) that, after evaluation by the [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA), could be exported as part of their [Lotus Notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Notes) software. The NSA suggested a few changes, which Rivest incorporated. After further negotiations, the cipher was approved for export in 1989. Along with RC4, RC2 with a [40-bit](wikipedia://en/487403) [key size](wikipedia://en/5749) was treated favourably under US [export regulations for cryptography](wikipedia://en/25872370).

Initially, the details of the algorithm were kept secret — proprietary to [RSA Security](wikipedia://en/301240) — but on 29 January 1996, source code for RC2 was anonymously posted to the Internet on the [Usenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usenet) forum [sci.crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sci.crypt). Mentions of [CodeView](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CodeView) and [SoftICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoftICE) (popular [debuggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debugger)) suggest that it had been [reverse engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering). A similar disclosure had occurred earlier with RC4.

In March 1998, Ron Rivest authored an [RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Request_for_Comments) publicly describing RC2 himself.

RC2 is a [64-bit block](wikipedia://en/294065) cipher with a variable size [key](wikipedia://en/53039). Its 18 rounds are arranged as a source-heavy unbalanced [Feistel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feistel_network), with 16 rounds of one type (*MIXING*) punctuated by two rounds of another type (*MASHING*). A MIXING round consists of four applications of the MIX transformation, as shown in the diagram.

RC2 is vulnerable to a [related-key attack](wikipedia://en/1516144) using 2<sup>34</sup> [chosen plaintexts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosen_plaintext).[^n1]
