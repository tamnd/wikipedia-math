# Edinburgh Mathematical Society

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edinburgh Mathematical Society"}

The **Edinburgh Mathematical Society** is a [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) society for academics in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland).

:::

## History {#History}

The Society was founded in 1883 by a group of [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) school teachers and academics, on the initiative of [Alexander Yule Fraser](wikipedia://en/57797407) [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) and [Andrew Jeffrey Gunion Barclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jeffrey_Gunion_Barclay) [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE),[^o4][^o5] both maths teachers at [George Watson's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watson's_College), and [Cargill Gilston Knott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill_Gilston_Knott), the assistant of [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait), professor of physics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).[^o6] The first president, elected at first meeting on 2 February 1883, was J.S. Mackay, the head mathematics master at the [Edinburgh Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Academy).[^o6]

The Society was founded at a time when mathematics societies were being created around the world, but it was unusual in being founded by school teachers rather than university lecturers.[^o6] This was because, due to the very small number of mathematical academic positions in Scotland at the time, many skilled mathematics graduates chose to become schoolteachers instead.[^o7] The fifty five founding members contained teachers, ministers and students, as well as a number of academics from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o5] The proportion of teachers remained high compared to other mathematical societies, and by 1926 university members made up only one-third of the total members.[^o8] However, the dominance of teachers in the numbers of the society declined towards the 1930s, and between 1930 and 1935 no papers were presented in the *Proceedings* by teachers.[^o7] This was due to an increase in the number of academic positions available and the new requirement for teachers to undergo an additional year of vocational training.[^o7]

The Edinburgh Mathematical Society is now mainly for academics.[^o7]

## Activity {#Activity}

The Society organises and funds meetings and other research events throughout Scotland. There are normally eight meetings a year, at which talks are presented by mathematicians.[^o14]

Every four years it awards the [Sir Edmund Whittaker Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/4579429) to an outstanding mathematician with a Scottish connection.[^o10] The Society is a corporate member of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470),[^o11] and in 2008 it became a member of the [Council for the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/7779218).[^o14]

## Journals {#Journals}

The society releases an [academic journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_journal), the *Proceedings of the Edinburgh Mathematical Society*, published by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) ([ISSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Standard_Serial_Number) 0013–0915.)[^o13] The *Proceedings* were first published in 1884, and are issued three times a year, covering a range of [pure](wikipedia://en/457210) and [applied](wikipedia://en/24295969) mathematics subjects.[^o14]

Between 1909 and 1961, the Society also published the *[Edinburgh Mathematical Notes](wikipedia://en/40868144)*, on the suggestion of George Alexander Gibson, a professor at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), who wished to remove the more elementary or pedagogical articles from the *Proceedings*.[^o15]
