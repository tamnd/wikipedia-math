# Seismic moment

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Seismic moment"}

**Seismic moment** is a quantity used by [seismologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismologist) to measure the size of an [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake). The scalar seismic moment $M_0$ is defined by the equation $M_0=\mu AD$, where

:::

- $\mu$ is the [shear modulus](wikipedia://en/1575447) of the rocks involved in the earthquake (in [pascals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_%28unit%29) (Pa), i.e. newtons per square meter)
- $A$ is the area of the rupture along the [geologic fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_%28geology%29) where the earthquake occurred (in square meters), and
- $D$ is the average slip (displacement offset between the two sides of the fault) on $A$ (in meters).

$M_0$ thus has dimensions of [torque](wikipedia://en/30400), measured in [newton meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_meters). The connection between seismic moment and a torque is natural in the body-force equivalent representation of seismic sources as a [double-couple](wikipedia://en/631494#Single_couple_or_double_couple) (a pair of force couples with opposite torques): the seismic moment is the torque of each of the two couples. Despite having the same dimensions as energy, seismic moment is not a measure of energy. The [relations between seismic moment, potential energy drop and radiated energy](wikipedia://en/631494#Relations_between_seismic_moment.2C_potential_energy_released_and_radiated_energy) are indirect and approximative.

The seismic moment of an earthquake is typically estimated using whatever information is available to constrain its factors. For modern earthquakes, moment is usually estimated from [ground motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_motion) recordings of earthquakes known as [seismograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismogram). For earthquakes that occurred in times before modern instruments were available, moment may be estimated from geologic estimates of the size of the fault rupture and the slip.

Seismic moment is the basis of the [moment magnitude scale](wikipedia://en/631494) introduced by the United States Geological Survey's [Thomas C. Hanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Hanks) and Caltech's [Hiroo Kanamori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroo_Kanamori), which is often used to compare the size of different earthquakes and is especially useful for comparing the sizes of large (great) earthquakes.

The seismic moment is not restricted to earthquakes. For a more general seismic source described by a seismic moment tensor $M_{ij}$ (a symmetric tensor, but not necessarily a double couple tensor), the seismic moment is

$$M_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (M_{ij}^2)^{1/2}$$
