# Smith number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smith number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **Smith number** is a [composite number](wikipedia://en/82289) for which, in a given [number base](wikipedia://en/701207), the [sum of its digits](wikipedia://en/1598868) is equal to the sum of the digits in its [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) in the same base. In the case of numbers that are not [square-free](wikipedia://en/29525), the factorization is written without exponents, writing the repeated factor as many times as needed.

:::

Smith numbers were named by [Albert Wilansky](wikipedia://en/64651278) of [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University), as he noticed the property in the phone number (493-7775) of his brother-in-law Harold Smith:

4937775 = 3 · 5 · 5 · 65837

while

4 + 9 + 3 + 7 + 7 + 7 + 5 = 3 + 5 + 5 + (6 + 5 + 8 + 3 + 7)

in [base 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_10).[^o3]

## Mathematical definition {#Mathematical_definition}

Let $n$ be a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474). For base $b > 1$, let the function $F_b(n)$ be the [digit sum](wikipedia://en/1598868) of $n$ in base $b$. A natural number $n$ with prime factorization

$$n = \prod_{\stackrel{p \mid n,}{p\text{ prime}}} p^{v_p(n)}$$

is a **Smith number** if

$$F_b(n) = \sum_{{\stackrel{p \mid n,}{p\text{ prime}}}} v_p(n) F_b(p).$$

Here the exponent $v_p(n)$ is the multiplicity of $p$ as a prime factor of $n$ (also known as the [*p*-adic valuation](wikipedia://en/16047618) of $n$).

For example, in base 10, 378 = 2<sup>1</sup> · 3<sup>3</sup> · 7<sup>1</sup> is a Smith number since 3 + 7 + 8 = 2 · 1 + 3 · 3 + 7 · 1, and 22 = 2<sup>1</sup> · 11<sup>1</sup> is a Smith number, because 2 + 2 = 2 · 1 + (1 + 1) · 1.

The first few Smith numbers in base 10 are

[4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_%28number%29), [22](wikipedia://en/362193), [27](wikipedia://en/369235), [58](wikipedia://en/399312), [85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85_%28number%29), [94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94_%28number%29), [121](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/121_%28number%29), [166](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/166_%28number%29), [202](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/202_%28number%29), [265](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/265_%28number%29), [274](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/274_%28number%29), 319, 346, 355, 378, 382, 391, 438, 454, 483, 517, 526, 535, 562, 576, 588, 627, 634, 636, 645, 648, 654, 663, [666](wikipedia://en/463039), 690, 706, 728, 729, 762, 778, 825, 852, 861, 895, 913, 915, 922, 958, 985.

## Properties {#Properties}

W.L. McDaniel in 1987 [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that there are infinitely many Smith numbers.[^o3][^o7] The number of Smith numbers in [base 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_10) below 10<sup>*n*</sup> for *n* = 1, 2, ... is given by

1, 6, 49, 376, 3294, 29928, 278411, 2632758, 25154060, 241882509, ... .

Two consecutive Smith numbers (for example, 728 and 729, or 2964 and 2965) are called **Smith brothers**.[^o8] It is not known how many Smith brothers there are. The starting elements of the smallest Smith *n*-tuple (meaning *n* consecutive Smith numbers) in base 10 for *n* = 1, 2, ... are[^o9]

4, 728, 73615, 4463535, 15966114, 2050918644, 164736913905, ... .

Smith numbers can be constructed from factored [repunits](wikipedia://en/215056).[^o10] , the largest known Smith number in base 10 is

9 × R<sub>1031</sub> × (10<sup>4594</sup> + 3 + 1)<sup>1476</sup>

where R<sub>1031</sub> is the base 10 [repunit](wikipedia://en/215056) (10<sup>1031</sup> − 1)/9.[^o11]
