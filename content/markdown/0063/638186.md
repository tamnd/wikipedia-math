# Fixed-point theorems in infinite-dimensional spaces

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fixed-point theorems in infinite-dimensional spaces"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a number of **[fixed-point](wikipedia://en/449738) theorems in infinite-dimensional spaces** generalise the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/4101). They have applications, for example, to the proof of [existence theorems](wikipedia://en/251177) for [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564).

:::

The first result in the field was the **[Schauder fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/638200)**, proved in 1930 by [Juliusz Schauder](wikipedia://en/685314) (a previous result in a different vein, the [Banach fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/46676) for [contraction mappings](wikipedia://en/6239) in complete [metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_spaces) was proved in 1922). Quite a number of further results followed. One way in which fixed-point theorems of this kind have had a larger influence on mathematics as a whole has been that one approach is to try to carry over methods of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), first proved for finite [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686), to spaces of infinite dimension. For example, the research of [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430) who founded [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory) came out of efforts to extend Schauder's work.

> **[Schauder fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/638200):** Let *C* be a [nonempty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonempty) [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) subset of a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *V*. If *f* : *C* → *C* is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) with a [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) image, then *f* has a fixed point.

> **Tikhonov (Tychonoff) fixed-point theorem:** Let *V* be a [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844). For any nonempty compact convex set *X* in *V*, any continuous function *f* : *X* → *X* has a fixed point.

> **Browder fixed-point theorem:** Let *K* be a nonempty closed bounded convex set in a [uniformly convex Banach space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_convex_Banach_space). Then any non-expansive function *f* : *K* → *K* has a fixed point. (A function $f$ is called non-expansive if $\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq \|x-y\|$ for each $x$ and $y$.)

Other results include the [Markov–Kakutani fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/36205212) (1936-1938) and the [Ryll-Nardzewski fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/5376332) (1967) for continuous affine self-mappings of compact convex sets, as well as the [Earle–Hamilton fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/36187009) (1968) for holomorphic self-mappings of open domains. Also, Aniki & Rauf (2019) presented some interesting results on the stability of partially ordered metric spaces for coupled fixed point iteration procedures for mixed monotone mappings.

> **[Kakutani fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/2658571):** Every correspondence that maps a compact convex subset of a locally convex space into itself with a closed graph and convex nonempty images has a fixed point.
