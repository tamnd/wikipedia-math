# Herbert Seifert

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Herbert Seifert"}

**Herbert Karl Johannes Seifert** (; 27 May 1907, [Bernstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstadt_auf_dem_Eigen) – 1 October 1996, [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg)) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Seifert was born in Bernstadt auf dem Eigen, but soon moved to [Bautzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bautzen), where he attended primary school at the Knabenbürgerschule, and secondary school at the Oberrealschule.

In 1926 Seifert entered the [Dresden University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Dresden).[^o4] The next year he attended a course on [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) given by [William Threlfall](wikipedia://en/37303142). This would be the beginning both of his lifelong work in the subject and his friendship with Threlfall. In the year 1928–29 he visited the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where topologists such as [Pavel Sergeevich Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Sergeevich_Alexandrov) and [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082) were working.

In 1930 he received his doctorate with his work on three-dimensional [closed manifolds](wikipedia://en/669475) (which contains the [Seifert–van Kampen theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert–van_Kampen_theorem)). He then moved to the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig), where he received his second doctorate in 1932. It was here that Seifert submitted his dissertation, *Topologie 3-dimensionaler gefaserter Räume* (“Topology of 3-dimensional fibred spaces”), on 1 February 1932, and he was awarded with this doctorate of philosophy after his oral examination on March 3. The [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) he studied in his thesis were afterwards named [Seifert fiber spaces](wikipedia://en/1631931).

Seifert continued to collaborate with Threlfall, and in 1934 (the year Seifert received his habilitation) they published their *[Lehrbuch der Topologie](wikipedia://en/37302995)*. In 1938 they published *Variationsrechnung im Grossen*.[^o5]

In 1935, Seifert was summoned to a post at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg), where he took a position vacated by the dismissal of a Jewish professor. During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he volunteered for a position at a [Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftwaffe) research center, the Institut für Gasdynamik. After the war, Seifert was one of the few German professors whom the Allies trusted during the period of [denazification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denazification).

In the year 1948–49 Seifert visited the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). On 13 September 1949, soon after returning to Germany, he married Katharina Korn.

Seifert retired in 1975. His students include [Albrecht Dold](wikipedia://en/31337135), [Dieter Puppe](wikipedia://en/63815664), and [Horst Schubert](wikipedia://en/38487829).
