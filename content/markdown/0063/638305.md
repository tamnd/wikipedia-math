# Harald Bohr

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Harald Bohr"}

**Harald August Bohr** (22 April 1887 – 22 January 1951) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [footballer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_player). After receiving his doctorate in 1910, Bohr became an eminent mathematician, founding the field of [almost periodic functions](wikipedia://en/405512). His brother was the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize)-winning physicist [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr). He was on the [Denmark national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) for the [1908 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics), where he won a silver medal.[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bohr was born in 1887 to [Christian Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Bohr), a professor of physiology, from a Lutheran background, and Ellen Adler Bohr, a woman from a wealthy Jewish family of local renown.[^o7] Harald had a close relationship with his elder brother, which *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* likened to that between Captain Cuttle and Captain Bunsby in [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens)' *[Dombey and Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dombey_and_Son)*.[^o8]

## Mathematical career {#Mathematical_career}

Like his father and brother before him, in 1904 Bohr enrolled at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen), where he studied mathematics, obtaining his master's degree in 1909 and his doctorate a year later. Among his tutors were [Hieronymus Georg Zeuthen](wikipedia://en/1732797) and [Thorvald N. Thiele](wikipedia://en/701256).[^o10] Bohr worked in mathematical analysis; much of his early work was devoted to [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) including his doctorate, which was entitled *Bidrag til de Dirichletske Rækkers Theori* (*Contributions to the Theory of Dirichlet Series*).[^o11] A collaboration with [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen)-based [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) resulted in the [Bohr–Landau theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr–Landau_theorem), regarding the distribution of zeroes in [zeta functions](wikipedia://en/25809).[^o7]

Bohr worked in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), founding the field of [almost periodic functions](wikipedia://en/405512), and worked with the [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) mathematician [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599).

In 1915, he became a professor at [Polyteknisk Læreanstalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyteknisk_Læreanstalt) (today [Technical University of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Denmark)), working there until 1930, when he took a professorship at the University of Copenhagen. He remained in this post for 21 years until his death in 1951. [Børge Jessen](wikipedia://en/20754801) was one of his students there.

He was a visiting professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) during the academic year 1930–1931.[^o12] He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in the summer of 1948.[^o13]

Bohr was known as a good teacher, and the annual award for outstanding teaching at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) is called the Harald, in honour of Harald Bohr. With [Johannes Mollerup](wikipedia://en/49411394), Bohr wrote an influential four-volume textbook *Lærebog i Matematisk Analyse* (*Textbook in mathematical analysis*).[^o14]

Bohr was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1945.[^o15]

In the 1930s Bohr was a leading critic of the anti-Semitic policies taking root in the German mathematical establishment, publishing an article criticising [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186)'s ideas in *[Berlingske Aften](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekendavisen)* in 1934.[^o16] Following the murder of [Kaj Munk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaj_Munk) on 4 January 1944 the Danish resistance newspaper *[De frie Danske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_frie_Danske)* brought condemning reactions from influential Scandinavians, including Bohr.[^o17]

## Football {#Football}

*Image: DK football1908.jpg.* Danish football team at the [1908 Olympic games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1908_Summer_Olympics). Bohr is in the top row, 2nd from left.

Bohr was also an excellent football player. He had a long playing career with [Akademisk Boldklub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademisk_Boldklub), making his debut as a 16-year-old in 1903.[^o19] During the 1905 season he played alongside his brother [Niels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr), who was a goalkeeper. Harald was selected to play for the [Denmark national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) in the [1908 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics), where football was an official event for the first time. Though a Danish side had played at the [1906 Intercalated Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1906_Intercalated_Games), the opening match of the 1908 Olympic tournament was Denmark's first official international football match. Bohr scored two goals as Denmark beat the French "B" team 9–0.[^o20] In the next match, the semi-final, Bohr played in a 17–1 win against [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_football_team), which remains an Olympic record.[^o21] Denmark faced hosts [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_men's_Olympic_football_team) in the final, but lost 2–0, and Bohr won a silver medal.[^o7][^o11] After the Olympics he made one further appearance for the national team, in a 2–1 victory against an England amateur team in 1910.[^o22] His popularity as a footballer was such that when he defended his doctoral thesis the audience was reported as having more football fans than mathematicians.[^o23]
