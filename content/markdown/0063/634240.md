# Wheel theory

*Image: Real Wheel (Wheel theory).png.* A diagram of a wheel, as the [real projective line](wikipedia://en/46379102) with a point at nullity (denoted by ⊥)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wheel theory"}

A **wheel** is a type of [algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) (in the sense of [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200)) where division is always defined. In particular, [division by zero](wikipedia://en/185663) is meaningful. The [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) can be extended to a wheel, as can any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346).

:::

The term *wheel* is inspired by the [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) picture $\odot$ of the [real projective line](wikipedia://en/46379102) together with an extra point [⊥](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/⊥) ([bottom element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_element)) such that $\bot = 0/0$.[^n1][^n2]

A wheel can be regarded as the equivalent of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) (and [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468)) where addition and multiplication are not a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) but respectively a [commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid) and a [commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid) with [involution](wikipedia://en/293450).[^n2]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A wheel is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $(W, 0, 1, +, \cdot, /)$, in which

- $W$ is a set
- ${}0$ and $1$ are elements of that set
- $+$ and $\cdot$ are [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948)
- $/$ is a [unary operation](wikipedia://en/161006)

and satisfying the following properties:

- $+$ and $\cdot$ are each [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) and [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative), and have $\,0$ and $1$ as their respective [identities](wikipedia://en/14962).
- $/$ is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450), for example $//x = x$
- $/$ is [multiplicative](wikipedia://en/20059), for example $/(xy) = /x/y$
- $$(x + y)z + 0z = xz + yz$$
- $$(x + yz)/y = x/y + z + 0y$$
- $$0\cdot 0 = 0$$
- $$(x+0y)z = xz + 0y$$
- $$/(x+0y) = /x + 0y$$
- $$0/0 + x = 0/0$$

:::

## Algebra of wheels {#Algebra_of_wheels}

Wheels replace the usual division as a binary operation with multiplication, with a unary operation applied to one argument $/x$ similar (but not identical) to the [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) $x^{-1}$, such that $a/b$ becomes shorthand for $a \cdot /b = /b \cdot a$, but neither $a \cdot b^{-1}$ nor $b^{-1} \cdot a$ in general, and modifies the rules of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) such that

- $$\exists x: 0x \neq 0$$
- $\exists x: x/x \neq 1$, as $/x$ is not the same as the [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) of $x$.

Other identities that may be derived are

- $$0x + 0y = 0xy$$
- $$x/x = 1 + 0x/x$$
- $$x-x = 0x^2$$

where the negation $-x$ is defined by $-x = ax$ and $x - y = x + (-y)$ if there is an element $a$ such that $1 + a = 0$ (thus in the general case $x - x \neq 0$).

However, for values of $x$ satisfying $0x = 0$ and $0/x = 0$, we get the usual

- $$x/x = 1$$
- $$x-x = 0$$

If negation can be defined as above then the [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $\{x\mid 0x=0\}$ is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), and every commutative ring is such a subset of a wheel. If $x$ is an [invertible element](wikipedia://en/289666) of the commutative ring then $x^{-1} = /x$. Thus, whenever $x^{-1}$ makes sense, it is equal to $/x$, but the latter is always defined, even when $x=0$.[^n1]

## Examples {#Examples}

### Wheel of fractions {#Wheel_of_fractions}

Let $A$ be a commutative ring, and let $S$ be a multiplicative [submonoid](wikipedia://en/19652#Submonoids) of $A$. Define the [congruence relation](wikipedia://en/48167) $\sim_S$ on $A \times A$ via

$(x_1,x_2)\sim_S(y_1,y_2)$ means that there exist $s_x,s_y \in S$ such that $(s_x x_1,s_x x_2) = (s_y y_1,s_y y_2)$.

Define the *wheel of fractions* of $A$ with respect to $S$ as the quotient $A \times A~/{\sim_S}$ (and denoting the [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) containing $(x_1,x_2)$ as $[x_1,x_2]$) with the operations

$0 = [0_A,1_A]$ 10(additive identity)

$1 = [1_A,1_A]$ 10(multiplicative identity)

$/[x_1,x_2] = [x_2,x_1]$ 10(reciprocal operation)

$[x_1,x_2] + [y_1,y_2] = [x_1y_2 + x_2 y_1,x_2 y_2]$ 10(addition operation)

$[x_1,x_2] \cdot [y_1,y_2] = [x_1 y_1,x_2 y_2]$ 10(multiplication operation)

In general, this structure is not a ring unless it is trivial, as $0x\ne0$ in the usual sense – here with $x=[0,0]$ we get $0x=[0,0]$, although that implies that $\sim_S$ is an improper relation on our wheel $W$.

This follows from the fact that $[0,0]=[0,1]\implies 0\in S$, which is also not true in general.[^n1]

### Projective line and Riemann sphere {#Projective_line_and_Riemann_sphere}

The special case of the above starting with a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) produces a [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578) extended to a wheel by adjoining a [bottom element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_element) noted [⊥](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/⊥), where $0/0=\bot$. The projective line is itself an extension of the original field by an element $\infty$, where $z/0=\infty$ for any element $z\neq 0$ in the field. However, $0/0$ is still undefined on the projective line, but is defined in its extension to a wheel.

Starting with the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), the corresponding projective "line" is geometrically a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), and then the extra point $0/0$ gives the shape that is the source of the term "wheel". Or starting with the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) instead, the corresponding projective "line" is a sphere (the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799)), and then the extra point gives a 3-dimensional version of a wheel.
