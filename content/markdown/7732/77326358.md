# Balanced category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Balanced category"}

In mathematics, especially in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **balanced category** is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) in which every bimorphism (a morphism that is both a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) and [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539)) is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828).

:::

The [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) is not balanced (since continuous bijections are not necessarily homeomorphisms), while a topos is balanced.[^o2] This is one of the reasons why a topos is said to be nicer.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following categories are balanced:

- **Set**, the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829).
- **Grp**, the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092).
- An [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063).[^o6]
- **CHaus**, the category of [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) spaces (since a continuous bijection there is homeomorphic).

An [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854) may not be balanced.[^o7] Contrary to what one might expect, a balanced [pre-abelian category](wikipedia://en/62775) may not be abelian.[^o8]

A [quasitopos](wikipedia://en/53073359) is similar to a topos but may not be balanced.

:::
