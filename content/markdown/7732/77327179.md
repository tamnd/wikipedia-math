# Pyknotic set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pyknotic set"}

In mathematics, especially in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **pyknotic set** is a sheaf of sets on the [site](wikipedia://en/12910) of compact [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) (with some fixed [Grothendieck universes](wikipedia://en/1072144)). The notion was introduced by [Barwick](wikipedia://en/57157400) and Haine to provide a convenient setting for [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).[^o2] The term *pyknotic* comes from the Greek πυκνός, meaning dense, compact or thick.[^o3] The notion can be compared to other approaches of introducing [generalized spaces](wikipedia://en/77331815) for the purpose of homological algebra such as [Clausen](wikipedia://en/76038574) and [Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306)'s [condensed sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_set) or [Johnstone](wikipedia://en/13469869)'s [topological topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_topos).[^o4]

:::

Pyknotic sets form a [coherent topos](wikipedia://en/64383764), while condensed sets do not.[^o5] Comparing pyknotic sets with his approach with Clausen, Scholze writes:[^o6] In a recent preprint \[BH19\], Barwick and Haine set up closely related foundations, but using different set-theoretic conventions. In particular, they assume the existence of universes, fixing in particular a “tiny” and a “small” universe, and look at sheaves on tiny profinite sets with values in small sets; they term these *pyknotic* sets. In our language, placing ourselves in the small universe, this would be κ-condensed sets for the first strongly inaccessible cardinal κ they consider (the one giving rise to the tiny universe).
