# Chung–Erdős inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chung–Erdős inequality"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Chung–Erdős inequality** provides a lower bound on the probability that one out of many (possibly dependent) events occurs. The lower bound is expressed in terms of the probabilities for pairs of events.

:::

Formally, let $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ be events. Assume that $\Pr[A_i]>0$ for some $i$. Then

$$\Pr[A_1\vee\cdots\vee A_n] \geq \frac{ \left(\sum_{i=1}^n \Pr[A_i]\right)^2 }{ \sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n \Pr[A_i\wedge A_j] }.$$

The inequality was first derived by [Kai Lai Chung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Lai_Chung) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) (in,[^o2] equation (4)). It was stated in the form given above by Petrov (in,[^o3] equation (6.10)). It can be obtained by applying the [Paley–Zygmund inequality](wikipedia://en/9414239) to the number of $A_i$ which occur.
