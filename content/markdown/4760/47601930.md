# Dmitry Dolgopyat

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dmitry Dolgopyat"}

**Dmitry Ilyich Dolgopyat** (Дмитрий Ильич Долгопят; born November 1, 1972) is a [Russian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087),[^o3] a field that studies the time evolution of natural and abstract systems.[^o4] An internationally acclaimed lecturer, he holds the position of Distinguished University Professor at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland),[^o5] and is a foreign member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea).[^o6]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Dolgopyat graduated from [Moscow State School 57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_School_57) mathematical class in 1989.[^o8]

From 1989 to 1994, he was an undergraduate student at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University).[^o9]

From 1994 to 1997, Dolgopyat was enrolled at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he earned a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) under the guidance of [Yakov Sinai](wikipedia://en/1751626).[^o10]

## Career {#Career}

From September 1999 to June 2003, Dolgopyat served as an assistant professor at [Penn State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University).[^o12]

Dolgopyat joined the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) as an associate professor from September 2002 to June 2006. During this period, he also spent a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (IAS) in Princeton (2002-2003).[^o13]

He briefly returned to Penn State University as a professor from September 2006 to June 2007 before settling at the University of Maryland as a professor in September 2007, a position he holds to the present day.[^o14]

Additionally, Dolgopyat spent a year at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768) from 2010 to 2011.[^o15]

He has also served on the editorial boards of the [Journal of Modern Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Modern_Dynamics), [Nonlinearity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinearity_%28journal%29), [Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_Theory_and_Dynamical_Systems), [Annales Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_Henri_Poincaré), and the [Journal of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society), as well as on the Prize Committee of the International [Bolyai Prize](wikipedia://en/18706230) Committee.[^o16]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Dolgopyat was named a Distinguished University Professor in 2022 - the highest academic honor bestowed by the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park).[^o18]

In 2020, he was elected a foreign member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) - a pan-European Academy of Humanities, Letters, Law, and Sciences.[^o19]

In 2009, he was awarded the [Michael Brin Prize in Dynamical Systems](wikipedia://en/47522601) for his fundamental contributions to the theory of hyperbolic dynamics.[^o20]

In 2009, Dolgopyat received the [Annales Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_Henri_Poincaré) Prize for the article Unbounded Orbits for Semicircular Outer Billiard[^o21] coauthored with Bassam Fayad.[^o22]

Earlier academic honors included the [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) (Fall 2000 - Spring 2002) and the Miller Fellowship (Fall 1997 - Spring 1999).[^o23]
