# Separable algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Separable algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **separable algebra** is a kind of [semisimple algebra](wikipedia://en/3876151). It is a generalization to [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) of the notion of a [separable field extension](wikipedia://en/363340).

:::

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Separability idempotent"}

## Definition and first properties {#Definition_and_first_properties}

A [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) of (unital, but not necessarily [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346)) [rings](wikipedia://en/48404)

$$K \to A$$

is called *separable* if the multiplication map

$$\begin{array}{rccc} \mu :& A \otimes_K A &\to& A \\ & a \otimes b &\mapsto & ab \end{array}$$

admits a [section](wikipedia://en/10630303)

$$\sigma: A \to A \otimes_K A$$

that is a homomorphism of *A*-*A*-[bimodules](wikipedia://en/492612).

If the ring $K$ is commutative and $K \to A$ maps $K$ into the [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) of $A$, we call $A$ a *separable algebra over* $K$.

It is useful to describe separability in terms of the element

$$p := \sigma(1) = \sum a_i \otimes b_i \in A \otimes_K A$$

The reason is that a section *σ* is determined by this element. The condition that *σ* is a section of *μ* is equivalent to

$$\sum a_i b_i = 1$$

and the condition that σ is a homomorphism of *A*-*A*-bimodules is equivalent to the following requirement for any *a* in *A*:

$$\sum a a_i \otimes b_i = \sum a_i \otimes b_i a.$$

Such an element *p* is called a *separability [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent)*, since regarded as an element of the algebra $A \otimes A^{\rm op}$ it satisfies $p^2 = p$.

## Examples {#Examples}

For any commutative ring *R*, the ([non-commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_ring)) ring of *n*-by-*n* [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) $M_n(R)$ is a separable *R*-algebra. For any $1 \le j \le n$, a separability idempotent is given by $\sum_{i=1}^n e_{ij} \otimes e_{ji}$, where $e_{ij}$ denotes the [elementary matrix](wikipedia://en/5897031) which is 0 except for the entry in the (*i*, *j*) entry, which is 1. In particular, this shows that separability idempotents need not be unique. Indeed, they are not even unique up to automorphism.

### Separable algebras over a field {#Separable_algebras_over_a_field}

A [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *L*/*K* of finite [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) is a [separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340) if and only if *L* is separable as an associative *K*-algebra. If *L*/*K* has a [primitive element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_element_%28field_theory%29) $a$ with irreducible polynomial $p(x) = (x - a) \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} b_i x^i$, then a separability idempotent is given by $\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a^i \otimes_K \frac{b_i}{p'(a)}$. The tensorands are dual bases for the [trace map](wikipedia://en/457105): if $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{n}$ are the distinct *K*-monomorphisms of *L* into an [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of *K*, the trace mapping Tr of *L* into *K* is defined by $Tr(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_i(x)$. The trace map and its dual bases make explicit *L* as a [Frobenius algebra](wikipedia://en/1814652) over *K*.

More generally, separable algebras over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* can be classified as follows: they are the same as finite products of [matrix algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_algebra) over [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [division algebras](wikipedia://en/26549625) whose centers are finite-dimensional [separable field extensions](wikipedia://en/363340) of the field *K*. In particular: Every separable algebra is itself finite-dimensional. If *K* is a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) – for example a field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero, or a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), or an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) – then every extension of *K* is separable, so that separable *K*-algebras are finite products of matrix algebras over finite-dimensional division algebras over field *K*. In other words, if *K* is a perfect field, there is no difference between a separable algebra over *K* and a finite-dimensional [semisimple algebra](wikipedia://en/3876151) over *K*. It can be shown by a generalized theorem of Maschke that an associative *K*-algebra *A* is separable if for every field extension $L/K$ the algebra $A\otimes_K L$ is semisimple.

### Group rings {#Group_rings}

If *K* is commutative ring and *G* is a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) such that the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of *G* is invertible in *K*, then the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) *K*\[*G*\] is a separable *K*-algebra.[^n1] A separability idempotent is given by $\frac{1}{o(G)} \sum_{g \in G} g \otimes g^{-1}$.

## Equivalent characterizations of separability {#Equivalent_characterizations_of_separability}

There are several equivalent definitions of separable algebras. A *K*-algebra *A* is separable if and only if it is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) when considered as a left [module](wikipedia://en/276410) of $A^e$ in the usual way.[^n2] Moreover, an algebra *A* is separable if and only if it is [flat](wikipedia://en/492616) when considered as a right module of $A^e$ in the usual way.

Separable algebras can also be characterized by means of split extensions: *A* is separable over *K* if and only if all [short exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of *A*-*A*-bimodules that are [split](wikipedia://en/1531404) as *A*-*K*-bimodules also split as *A*-*A*-bimodules. Indeed, this condition is necessary since the multiplication mapping $\mu : A \otimes_K A \rightarrow A$ arising in the definition above is a *A*-*A*-bimodule [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539), which is split as an *A*-*K*-bimodule map by the right inverse mapping $A \rightarrow A \otimes_K A$ given by $a \mapsto a \otimes 1$. The converse can be proven by a judicious use of the separability idempotent (similarly to the proof of [Maschke's theorem](wikipedia://en/30876007), applying its components within and without the splitting maps).[^n3]

Equivalently, the relative [Hochschild cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochschild_cohomology) groups $H^n(R,S;M)$ of (*R*, *S*) in any coefficient bimodule *M* is zero for *n* > 0. Examples of separable extensions are many including first separable algebras where *R* is a separable algebra and *S* = 1 times the ground field. Any ring *R* with elements *a* and *b* satisfying *ab* = 1, but *ba* different from 1, is a separable extension over the subring *S* generated by 1 and *bRa*.

## Relation to Frobenius algebras {#Relation_to_Frobenius_algebras}

A separable algebra is said to be *strongly separable* if there exists a separability idempotent that is *symmetric*, meaning

$$e = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \otimes y_i = \sum_{i=1}^n y_i \otimes x_i$$

An algebra is strongly separable if and only if its trace form is nondegenerate, thus making the algebra into a particular kind of [Frobenius algebra](wikipedia://en/1814652) called a symmetric algebra (not to be confused with the [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) arising as the quotient of the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628)).

If *K* is commutative, *A* is a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) separable *K*-module, then *A* is a symmetric Frobenius algebra.[^n4]

## Relation to formally unramified and formally étale extensions {#Relation_to_formally_unramified_and_formally_étale_extensions}

Any separable extension *A* / *K* of commutative rings is [formally unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_unramified). The converse holds if *A* is a finitely generated *K*-algebra.[^n5] A separable [flat](wikipedia://en/492616) (commutative) *K*-algebra *A* is [formally étale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_étale).[^n6]

## Further results {#Further_results}

A theorem in the area is that of J. Cuadra that a separable Hopf–Galois extension *R* | *S* has finitely generated natural *S*-module *R*. A fundamental fact about a separable extension *R* | *S* is that it is left or right semisimple extension: a short exact sequence of left or right *R*-modules that is split as *S*-modules, is split as *R*-modules. In terms of G. Hochschild's relative [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), one says that all *R*-modules are relative (*R*, *S*)-projective. Usually relative properties of subrings or ring extensions, such as the notion of separable extension, serve to promote theorems that say that the over-ring shares a property of the subring. For example, a separable extension *R* of a semisimple algebra *S* has *R* semisimple, which follows from the preceding discussion.

There is the celebrated Jans theorem that a finite group algebra *A* over a field of characteristic *p* is of finite representation type if and only if its Sylow *p*-subgroup is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327): the clearest proof is to note this fact for *p*-groups, then note that the group algebra is a separable extension of its Sylow *p*-subgroup algebra *B* as the index is [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to the characteristic. The separability condition above will imply every finitely generated *A*-module *M* is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a direct summand in its restricted, induced module. But if *B* has finite representation type, the restricted module is uniquely a direct sum of multiples of finitely many [indecomposables](wikipedia://en/810549), which induce to a finite number of constituent indecomposable modules of which *M* is a direct sum. Hence *A* is of finite representation type if *B* is. The converse is proven by a similar argument noting that every subgroup algebra *B* is a *B*-bimodule direct summand of a group algebra *A*.
