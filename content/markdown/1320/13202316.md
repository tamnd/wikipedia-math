# Near-semiring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Near-semiring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **near-semiring**, also called a **seminearring**, is an [algebraic](wikipedia://en/19616384) [structure](wikipedia://en/106364) more general than a [near-ring](wikipedia://en/839900) or a [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468). Near-semirings arise naturally from [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) on [monoids](wikipedia://en/19652).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A near-semiring is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *S* with two [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948) "+" and "·", and a constant 0 such that (*S*, +, 0) is a monoid (not necessarily [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid)), (*S*, ·) is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799), these structures are related by a single (right or left) [distributive law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributive_law), and accordingly 0 is a one-sided (right or left, respectively) [absorbing element](wikipedia://en/14206224).

Formally, an algebraic structure (*S*, +, ·, 0) is said to be a near-semiring if it satisfies the following axioms:

1. (*S*, +, 0) is a monoid,
1. (*S*, ·) is a semigroup,
1. (*a* + *b*) · *c* = *a* · *c* + *b* · *c*, for all *a*, *b*, *c* in *S*, and
1. 0 · *a* = 0 for all *a* in *S*.

Near-semirings are a common abstraction of semirings and near-rings \[Golan, 1999; Pilz, 1983\]. The standard examples of near-semirings are typically of the form *M*(Г), the set of all mappings on a monoid (Г; +, 0), equipped with [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of mappings, pointwise addition of mappings, and the zero function. Subsets of *M*(Г) closed under the operations provide further examples of near-semirings. Another example is the [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) under the usual operations of [ordinal arithmetic](wikipedia://en/2139226) (here Clause 3 should be replaced with its symmetric form *c* · (*a* + *b*) = *c* · *a* + *c* · *b*. Strictly speaking, the [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of all ordinals is not a set, so the above example should be more appropriately called a *class near-semiring*. We get a near-semiring in the standard sense if we restrict to those ordinals strictly less than some [multiplicatively indecomposable ordinal](wikipedia://en/17202420#Multiplicatively_indecomposable).
