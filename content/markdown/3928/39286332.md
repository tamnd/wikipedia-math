# Almost ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Almost ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **almost modules** and **almost rings** are certain objects interpolating between [rings](wikipedia://en/61346) and their [fields of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271). They were introduced by (txt) in his study of [*p*-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853).

:::

## Almost modules {#Almost_modules}

Let *V* be a [local](wikipedia://en/153391) [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) with the [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) *m*, and *K* a [fraction field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_field) of *V*. The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of *K*-[modules](wikipedia://en/276410), *K*-**Mod**, may be obtained as a [quotient](wikipedia://en/48897477) of *V*-**Mod** by the [Serre subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_subcategory) of [torsion modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_module), i.e. those *N* such that any element *n* in *N* is annihilated by some nonzero element in the maximal ideal. If the category of torsion modules is replaced by a smaller [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959), we obtain an intermediate step between *V*-modules and *K*-modules. Faltings proposed to use the subcategory of **almost zero** modules, i.e. *N* ∈ *V*-**Mod** such that any element *n* in *N* is annihilated by *all* elements of the maximal ideal.

For this idea to work, *m* and *V* must satisfy certain technical conditions. Let *V* be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) (not necessarily local) and *m* ⊆ *V* an idempotent [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977), i.e. an ideal such that *m*<sup>2</sup> = *m*. Assume also that *m* ⊗ *m* is a [flat](wikipedia://en/492616) *V*-module. A module *N* over *V* is **almost zero** with respect to such *m* if for all *ε* ∈ *m* and *n* ∈ *N* we have *εn* = 0. Almost zero modules form a Serre subcategory of the category of *V*-modules. The category of *almost V-modules*, *V*<sup>hair*a*</sup>-**Mod**, is a [localization](wikipedia://en/48897477) of *V*-**Mod** along this subcategory.

The quotient [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) *V*-**Mod** → *V*<sup>hair*a*</sup>-**Mod** is denoted by $N \mapsto N^a$. The assumptions on *m* guarantee that $(-)^a$ is an [exact functor](wikipedia://en/453945) which has both the right [adjoint functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) $M \mapsto M_*$ and the left adjoint functor $M \mapsto M_!$. Moreover, $(-)_*$ is [full and faithful](wikipedia://en/876534). The category of almost modules is [complete](wikipedia://en/62781) and [cocomplete](wikipedia://en/62781).

## Almost rings {#Almost_rings}

The [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836) of *V*-modules descends to a [monoidal structure](wikipedia://en/330604) on *V*<sup>hair*a*</sup>-**Mod**. An almost module *R* ∈ *V*<sup>hair*a*</sup>-**Mod** with a map *R* ⊗ *R* → *R* satisfying natural conditions, similar to a definition of a ring, is called an **almost *V*-algebra** or an **almost ring** if the context is unambiguous. Many standard properties of algebras and morphisms between them carry to the "almost" world.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

In the original paper by Faltings, *V* was the [integral closure](wikipedia://en/9478630) of a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) in the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of its [quotient field](wikipedia://en/25271), and *m* its maximal ideal. For example, let *V* be $\mathbb{Z}_p[p^{1/p^{\infty}}]$, i.e. a *p*-adic [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28algebra%29) of $\operatorname{colim}\limits_n \mathbb{Z}_p[p^{1/p^n}]$. Take *m* to be the maximal ideal of this ring. Then the quotient *V/m* is an almost zero module, while *V/p* is a torsion, but not almost zero module since the class of *p*<sup>1/*p*<sup>2</sup></sup> in the quotient is not annihilated by *p*<sup>1/*p*<sup>2</sup></sup> considered as an element of *m*.
