# Symmetric probability distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symmetric probability distribution"}

*Image: Symmetric distribution for continuous probability distribution.png.* Symmetric distribution for continuous probability distribution, specifically standard normal distribution, showcasing its perfect symmetry about the mean (0).

*Image: Symmetric discrete distribution (Binomial distribution).png.* A symmetric discrete distribution, specifically a binomial distribution with 10 trials and a success probability of 0.5.

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **symmetric probability distribution** is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543)—an assignment of probabilities to possible occurrences—which is unchanged when its [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (for continuous probability distribution) or [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) (for discrete random variables) is [reflected](wikipedia://en/295829) around a vertical line at some value of the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) represented by the distribution. This vertical line is the line of [symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_%28mathematics%29) of the distribution. Thus the probability of being any given distance on one side of the value about which symmetry occurs is the same as the probability of being the same distance on the other side of that value.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A probability distribution is said to be symmetric [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) there exists a value $x_0$ such that

$f(x_0-\delta) = f(x_0+\delta)$ for all real numbers $\delta ,$

where *f* is the probability density function if the distribution is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_distribution) or the probability mass function if the distribution is [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_distribution).

:::

## Multivariate distributions {#Multivariate_distributions}

The degree of symmetry, in the sense of mirror symmetry, can be evaluated quantitatively for multivariate distributions with the chiral index, which takes values in the interval \[0;1\], and which is null if and only if the distribution is mirror symmetric.[^o5] Thus, a d-variate distribution is defined to be mirror symmetric when its chiral index is null. The distribution can be discrete or continuous, and the existence of a density is not required, but the inertia must be finite and non null. In the univariate case, this index was proposed as a non parametric test of symmetry.[^o6]

For continuous symmetric spherical, Mir M. Ali gave the following definition. Let $\mathcal{F}$ denote the class of spherically symmetric distributions of the absolutely continuous type in the n-dimensional Euclidean space having joint density of the form $f(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n)=g(x_1^2+x_2^2+\dots+x_n^2)$inside a sphere with center at the origin with a prescribed radius which may be finite or infinite and zero elsewhere.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [median](wikipedia://en/18837) and the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) (if it exists) of a symmetric distribution both occur at the point $x_0$ about which the symmetry occurs.[^o10]
- If a symmetric distribution is [unimodal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimodal_distribution), the [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127) coincides with the median and mean.
- All odd [central moments](wikipedia://en/19983) of a symmetric distribution equal zero (if they exist), because in the calculation of such moments the negative terms arising from negative deviations from $x_0$ exactly balance the positive terms arising from equal positive deviations from $x_0$.
- Every measure of [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) equals zero for a symmetric distribution.

:::
