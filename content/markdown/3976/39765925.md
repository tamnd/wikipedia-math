# Dudley Weldon Woodard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dudley Weldon Woodard"}

**Dudley Weldon Woodard** (October 3, 1881 – July 1, 1965) was a Galveston-born American mathematician and professor, and the second [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American) to earn a PhD in mathematics; the first was Woodard's mentor [Elbert Frank Cox](wikipedia://en/2814552), who earned a PhD from [Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell) in 1925.

:::

He received his B.A. degree from [Wilberforce University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilberforce_University) in Ohio (1903), his B.S. degree (1906) and M.Sc. degree (1907) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o2] He taught collegiate mathematics in Tuskegee for many years,[^o3] until finally he earned his PhD at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (1928).[^o4] His doctoral thesis was entitled, *On Two-Dimensional Analysis Situs with Special Reference to the Jordan Curve Theorem*, and was advised by [John R. Kline](wikipedia://en/21005350).[^o5][^o6]

During his lifetime, he published three papers. The second of these, *The Characterization of the Closed N-Cell* in [Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701), 13 (1929), is, according to Scott Williams, Professor of Mathematics at the [State University of New York-Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York-Buffalo), the first paper published in an accredited mathematics journal by an African American.[^o5][^o7] He also published a study for the Committee of twelve for the advancement of the interests of the Negro race on [Jackson, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson,_Mississippi) in 1909,[^o8] a textbook, *Practical Arithmetic* (1911),[^o9] and an article on geometry teaching at Tuskegee in 1913.[^o10]

Woodard was a respected mathematician, professor and mentor to his students at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University) in Washington DC, where he established the masters program in mathematics.[^o11] One of his best known students was [William Waldron Schieffelin Claytor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Waldron_Schieffelin_Claytor), who later took his PhD at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (1933), also under Woodard's former advisor, [John R. Kline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Kline).

Woodard retired in 1947, after having become chairman of the mathematics department. He died on July 15, 1965, at his home in [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio), aged 83.[^o2][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15]
