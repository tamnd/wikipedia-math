# Isotomic conjugate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isotomic conjugate"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **isotomic conjugate** of a point $P$ with respect to a triangle $\triangle ABC$ is another point, defined in a specific way from $P$ and $\triangle ABC$: If the base points of the lines $PA, PB, PC$ on the sides opposite $A, B, C$ are [reflected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28geometry%29) about the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of their respective sides, the resulting lines intersect at the isotomic conjugate of $P$.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Isotomic conjugate of a point.svg.*

We assume that $P$ is not [collinear](wikipedia://en/3189581) with any two vertices of $\triangle ABC$. Let $A', B', C'$ be the points in which the lines $AP, BP, CP$ meet sidelines $BC, CA, AB$ ([extended](wikipedia://en/43958459) if necessary). Reflecting $A', B', C'$ in the midpoints of sides $\overline{BC}, \overline{CA}, \overline{AB}$ will give points $A'', B'', C''$ respectively. The **isotomic lines** $AA'', BB'', CC''$ joining these new points to the vertices meet at a point (which can be proved using [Ceva's theorem](wikipedia://en/152428)), the *isotomic conjugate* of $P$.

:::

## Coordinates {#Coordinates}

If the [trilinears](wikipedia://en/3837790) for $P$ are $p : q : r$, then the trilinears for the isotomic conjugate of $P$ are

$$a^{-2}p^{-1} : b^{-2}q^{-1} : c^{-2}r^{-1},$$

where $a, b, c$ are the side lengths opposite vertices $A, B, C$ respectively.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The isotomic conjugate of the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) of triangle $\triangle ABC$ is the centroid itself.

The isotomic conjugate of the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) is the third [Brocard point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_point), and the isotomic conjugate of the [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point) (whose Cevian triangle is the intouch triangle) is the [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) (whose Cevian triangle is the extouch triangle).

Isotomic conjugates of lines are circumconics, and conversely, isotomic conjugates of circumconics are lines. (This property holds for [isogonal conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isogonal_conjugates) as well.)

:::
