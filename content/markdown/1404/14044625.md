# Ronald G. Douglas

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Ronald G. Douglas"}

**Ronald George Douglas** (December 10, 1938 – February 27, 2018) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), best known for his work on [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) and [operator algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_algebras).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Douglas was born in [Osgood, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osgood,_Indiana). He was an undergraduate at the [Illinois Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology), and received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1962 from [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University) as a student of Pasquale Porcelli. He was at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) until 1969, when he moved to the [State University of New York at Stony Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University). Beginning in 1986 he moved into university administration, eventually becoming Vice [Provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_%28education%29) at Stony Brook in 1990, and Provost at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University) from 1996 until 2002. At the time of his death, he was Distinguished Professor in the Department of Mathematics at Texas A&M. He is survived by three children, including [Michael R. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_R._Douglas), a noted [string theorist](wikipedia://en/28305).

## Research {#Research}

Among his best-known contributions to science is a 1977 paper with [Lawrence G. Brown](wikipedia://en/48948022) and Peter A. Fillmore (BDF theory), which introduced techniques from [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) into the theory of operator algebras. This work was an important precursor to [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917) as later developed by [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340) among others. In addition to BDF theory, two other influential theories bear his names: Douglas algebra and Cowen-Douglas operators. In recent decades, he was a prominent advocator of multivariable operator theory. His coauthored book with [Vern Paulsen](wikipedia://en/54014822) "Hilbert modules over function algebras" introduced an analytic framework for studying commuting operator tuples. Douglas-Arveson conjecture is a well-known unsolved problem in this field.

Douglas directed 23 Ph.D. students, some of whom became renowned mathematicians, and his book *Banach Algebra Techniques in Operator Theory* in the series [Graduate Texts in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7392980) is one of the classics in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]
