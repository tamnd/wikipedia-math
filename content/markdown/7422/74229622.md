# Thomas Lumsden

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Lumsden"}

**Thomas Arnot Lumsden** was a Scottish mathematician, who was educated at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), and lectured at [Birmingham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_University). He published "A certain type of Fourier-Bessel Series", a paper about Bessel functions, in 1924. Lumsden, who served during both [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) (1914–1918) and [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) (1941–1945), was killed in action in 1944.

:::

## Personal life, education, and World War I {#Personal_life,_education,_and_World_War_I}

Thomas Lumsden was born on 19 September 1895 in Edinburgh, to Mary Scott Arnott, who was born in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) in 1857, and William Lumsden, a wood turner born in 1858 in [Penicuik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicuik), Scotland. He had an older sister, Janet (1889-1935), and younger brother, Andrew (born in 1898).[^o4]

Lumsden started studying at George Heriot's in 1906, a private school in Edinburgh.[^o5] He matriculated at the University of Edinburgh in October 1912.

Lumsden took a break from his studies to fight in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) where he was assigned to a Special Brigade of the [Royal Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Engineers). He was a corporal from July 1915 to April 1917. Lumsden worked then in Munitions until December 1918.[^o6] Lumsden was awarded a BSc in absentia on 5 July 1917.[^o7] He returned from his service in 1918 to continue his studies at the University of Edinburgh, graduating with honours for his master's degree in 1919.[^o6]

On 4 August 1924, Lumsden married Florence Milne Falconer in [Abernyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abernyte), [Perthshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perthshire).[^o4] Their only child, William Fairly Lumsden was born on 6 February 1926.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Between 1919-1920, Lumsden remained at the University of Edinburgh as an Assistant in Natural Philosophy (under the Nichol Foundation). Following this, he moved to Birmingham to take a position as a lecturer in mathematics at [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham).

In 1922, he was elected as a Fellow of the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society).[^o10] In 1922, Lumsden helped [G. N. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._N._Watson), a mathematics professor at the University of Birmingham, publish a book on [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700).[^o11] Lumsden later published his own paper on Bessel functions in 1924, titled: "A certain type of Fourier-Bessel Series".[^o12] This paper was cited in [Thomas John I'Anson Bromwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_John_I'Anson_Bromwich)'s book, "An Introduction to The Theory of Infinite Series" in 1926.[^o13]

## World War II {#World_War_II}

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) (1941–1945), Lumsden once again served his country. He arrived in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on the [RMS *Queen Elizabeth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Queen_Elizabeth) on 26 December 1942, intending to stay indefinitely, but no intention to become a citizen.[^o15] He was identified as a government official and a diplomat.

Lumsden was killed in action on 17 August 1944.[^o4][^o16]
