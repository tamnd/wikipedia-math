# Gustav von Escherich

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gustav von Escherich"}

**Gustav Ritter von Escherich** (1 June 1849 – 28 January 1935) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua), he studied mathematics and physics at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna). From 1876 to 1879 he was professor at the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz). In 1882 he went to the [Graz University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz_University_of_Technology) and in 1884 he went to the University of Vienna, where he also was president of the university in 1903/04.

Together with [Emil Weyr](wikipedia://en/6022125) he founded the journal *[Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monatshefte_für_Mathematik_und_Physik)* and together with [Ludwig Boltzmann](wikipedia://en/544255) and [Emil Müller](wikipedia://en/29039777) he founded the [Austrian Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Mathematical_Society).

Escherich died in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866).

## Work on hyperbolic geometry {#Work_on_hyperbolic_geometry}

Following [Eugenio Beltrami](wikipedia://en/543760)'s (1868) discussion of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), Escherich in 1874 published a paper named "The geometry on surfaces of constant negative curvature". He used coordinates initially introduced by [Christoph Gudermann](wikipedia://en/1503772) (1830) for spherical geometry, which were adapted by Escherich using [hyperbolic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function). For the case of translation of points on this surface of negative curvature, Escherich gave the following transformation on page 510:[^o5]

$x=\frac{\sinh\frac{a}{k}+x'\cosh\frac{a}{k}}{\cosh\frac{a}{k}+x'\sinh\frac{a}{k}}$ and $y=\frac{y'}{\cosh\frac{a}{k}+x'\sinh\frac{a}{k}}$

which is identical with the relativistic [velocity addition formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity_addition_formula) by interpreting the coordinates as velocities and by using the [rapidity](wikipedia://en/4520673):

$$\frac{\sinh\frac{a}{k}}{\cosh\frac{a}{k} }=\tanh\frac{a}{k}=\frac{v}{c}$$

or with a [Lorentz boost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_boost) by using [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316):

$$(x,\ y,\ x',\ y')=\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{0}},\ \frac{x_{2}}{x_{0}},\ \frac{x_{1}^{\prime}}{x_{0}^{\prime}},\ \frac{x_{2}^{\prime}}{x_{0}^{\prime}}\right)$$

These are in fact the relations between the coordinates of Gudermann/Escherich in terms of the [Beltrami–Klein model](wikipedia://en/4792791) and the Weierstrass coordinates of the [hyperboloid model](wikipedia://en/4771189) - this relation was pointed out by [Homersham Cox](wikipedia://en/55620821) (1882, p. 186).[^o6]
