# Laguerre–Pólya class

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laguerre–Pólya class"}

The **Laguerre–Pólya class** is the class of [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372) consisting of those functions which are locally the limit of a series of polynomials whose roots are all real. [^o2] Any function of Laguerre–Pólya class is also of [Pólya class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_class).

:::

The product of two functions in the class is also in the class, so the class constitutes a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) under the operation of function multiplication.

Some properties of a function $E(z)$ in the Laguerre–Pólya class are:

- All [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) are real.
- $|E(x+iy)|=|E(x-iy)|$ for *x* and *y* real.
- $|E(x+iy)|$ is a [non-decreasing function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-decreasing_function) of *y* for positive *y*.

A function is of Laguerre–Pólya class if and only if three conditions are met:

- The roots are all real.
- The nonzero zeros *z<sub>n</sub>* satisfy

$\sum_n\frac{1}{|z_n|^2}$ converges, with zeros counted according to their [multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790#Multiplicity_of_a_root_of_a_polynomial))

- The function can be expressed in the form of a [Hadamard product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_product_%28entire_functions%29)

$$z^m e^{a+bz+cz^2}\prod_n \left(1-z/z_n\right)\exp(z/z_n)$$

with *b* and *c* real and *c* non-positive. (The non-negative integer *m* will be positive if *E*(0)=0. Note that if the number of zeros is infinite one may have to define how to take the [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006).)

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Some examples are $\sin(z), \cos(z), \exp(z), \exp(-z), \text{and }\exp(-z^2).$

On the other hand, $\sinh(z), \cosh(z), \text{and } \exp(z^2)$ are ***not*** in the Laguerre–Pólya class.

For example,

$$\exp(-z^2)=\lim_{n \to \infty}(1-z^2/n)^n.$$

Cosine can be done in more than one way. Here is one series of polynomials having all real roots:

$$\cos z=\lim_{n \to \infty}((1+iz/n)^n+(1-iz/n)^n)/2$$

And here is another:

$$\cos z=\lim_{n \to \infty}\prod_{m=1}^n \left(1-\frac{z^2}{((m-\frac{1}{2})\pi)^2}\right)$$

This shows the buildup of the Hadamard product for cosine.

If we replace *z*<sup>2</sup> with *z*, we have another function in the class:

$$\cos \sqrt z=\lim_{n \to \infty}\prod_{m=1}^n \left(1-\frac z{((m-\frac{1}{2})\pi)^2}\right)$$

Another example is the [reciprocal gamma function](wikipedia://en/4017902) 1/Γ(z). It is the limit of polynomials as follows:

$$1/\Gamma(z)=\lim_{n \to \infty}\frac 1{n!}(1-(\ln n)z/n)^n\prod_{m=0}^n(z+m).$$

:::
