# Cosmic space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cosmic space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **cosmic space** is any [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that is a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of some [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). Equivalently (for [regular](wikipedia://en/53058) [*T*<sub>1</sub> spaces](wikipedia://en/148721) but not in general), a space is cosmic if and only if it has a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) network; namely a [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) collection of [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of the space such that any [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) is the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of a subcollection of these sets.

:::

Cosmic spaces have several interesting properties. There are a number of unsolved problems about them.

## Examples and properties {#Examples_and_properties}

- Any open subset of a cosmic space is cosmic since open subsets of separable spaces are separable.
- Separable metric spaces are trivially cosmic.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="Unsolved problems"}

## Unsolved problems {#Unsolved_problems}

It is unknown as to whether *X* is cosmic if:

a) *X*<sup>2</sup> contains no uncountable [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061);

b) the countable product of *X* with itself is hereditarily separable and hereditarily [Lindelöf](wikipedia://en/310480).

:::
