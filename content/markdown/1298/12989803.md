# Non-positive curvature

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-positive curvature"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), spaces of **non-positive curvature** occur in many contexts and form a generalization of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291). In the [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) of [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652), one can consider the [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) of the manifold and require that this curvature be everywhere less than or equal to zero. The notion of curvature extends to the category of [geodesic metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space), where one can use [comparison triangles](wikipedia://en/5845706) to quantify the curvature of a space; in this context, non-positively curved spaces are known as (locally) [CAT(0) spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space).

:::

## Riemann Surfaces {#Riemann_Surfaces}

If $S$ is a closed, orientable [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) then it follows from the [Uniformization theorem](wikipedia://en/288270) that $S$ may be endowed with a complete [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) with constant [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) of either $0$, $1$ or $-1$. As a result of the [Gauss–Bonnet theorem](wikipedia://en/139229) one can determine that the surfaces which have a Riemannian metric of constant curvature $0$ $-1$ i.e. Riemann surfaces with a complete, Riemannian metric of non-positive constant curvature, are exactly those whose [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) is at least $1$. The Uniformization theorem and the Gauss–Bonnet theorem can both be applied to orientable Riemann surfaces with boundary to show that those surfaces which have a non-positive [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) are exactly those which admit a Riemannian metric of non-positive curvature. There is therefore an infinite family of [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) types of such surfaces whereas the Riemann sphere is the only closed, orientable [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) of constant Gaussian curvature $1$.

The definition of curvature above depends upon the existence of a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) and therefore lies in the field of geometry. However the Gauss–Bonnet theorem ensures that the topology of a surface places constraints on the complete Riemannian metrics which may be imposed on a surface so the study of metric spaces of non-positive curvature is of vital interest in both the mathematical fields of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). Classical examples of surfaces of non-positive curvature are the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) and [flat torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_torus) (for curvature $0$) and the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane) and [pseudosphere](wikipedia://en/60766) (for curvature $-1$). For this reason these metrics as well as the Riemann surfaces which on which they lie as complete metrics are referred to as Euclidean and hyperbolic respectively.

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The characteristic features of the geometry of non-positively curved Riemann surfaces are used to generalize the notion of non-positive beyond the study of Riemann surfaces. In the study of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) or [orbifolds](wikipedia://en/293504) of higher dimension, the notion of [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) is used wherein one restricts one's attention to two-dimensional subspaces of the tangent space at a given point. In dimensions greater than $2$ the [Mostow–Prasad rigidity theorem](wikipedia://en/2782164) ensures that a [hyperbolic manifold](wikipedia://en/1589135) of finite area has a unique complete [hyperbolic metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_metric) so the study of hyperbolic geometry in this setting is integral to the study of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

In an arbitrary [geodesic metric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space) the notions of being [Gromov hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/3172817) or of being a locally [CAT(0) space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) generalise the notion that on a Riemann surface of non-positive curvature, triangles whose sides are geodesics appear *thin* whereas in settings of positive curvature they appear *fat*. This notion of non-positive curvature allows the notion of non-positive curvature is most commonly applied to [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) and is therefore of great use in the fields of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).
