# Neil J. Calkin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Neil J. Calkin"}

**Neil J. Calkin** (born 29 March 1961) is a professor at [Clemson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemson_University) in the Algebra and Discrete Mathematics group of the School of Mathematical and Statistical Sciences. His interests are in combinatorial and probabilistic methods, mainly as applied to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

Together with [Herbert Wilf](wikipedia://en/3782688) he founded [The Electronic Journal of Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Electronic_Journal_of_Combinatorics) in 1994.[^o3] He and Wilf developed the [Calkin–Wilf tree](wikipedia://en/22672164) and the associated Calkin–Wilf sequence.[^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

Neil Calkin was born 29 March 1961, in [Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut) and moved to the UK around the age of 3. He grew up there and studied mathematics at [Trinity College Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) before moving to Canada in 1984 to study in the Department of Combinatorics and Optimization at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) where he was awarded a PhD (1988) for his thesis "Sum-Free Sets and Measure Spaces" written under the supervision of [Ian Peter Goulden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Peter_Goulden).[^o6]

He was the [Zeev Nehari](wikipedia://en/25242678) Visiting Assistant Professor of Mathematics at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) (1988—1991), an assistant professor at [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech) (1991—1997),[^o7] and joined the Algebra and Discrete Mathematics group of the School of Mathematical and Statistical Sciences at Clemson University in 1997.[^o8]

Calkin has an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 1.[^o9] He was one of the last people to collaborate with [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and once said of him, "One of my greatest regrets is that I didn't know him when he was a million times faster than most people. When I knew him he was only hundreds of times faster."[^o10]
