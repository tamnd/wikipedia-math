# CryptoHarlem

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="CryptoHarlem"}

**CryptoHarlem** is an anti-surveillance, [cybersecurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybersecurity) education and advocacy organization. Founded by Matt Mitchell in 2013, CryptoHarlem provides the predominantly African American community in [upper Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Manhattan) with free workshops on digital security, encryption, [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy), [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), digital policing and [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## History {#History}

CryptoHarlem was founded following the [Trayvon Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trayvon_Martin) murder trial. Initially a series of security workshops, Mitchell said he founded the organization “due to the feeling of profound loss, the loss of all black folks, after Trayvon Martin’s death.” [^o6] Monthly digital privacy clinics called "crypto parties" hosted by Mitchell and CryptoHarlem started in 2012.[^o7]

In 2017 the organization's name appeared as an [Easter egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_egg_%28media%29) in an episode of [Mr. Robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Robot).[^o11][^o8]

In 2017, CryptoHarlem developed an [open source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_source) tool to help organizations prepare for data breaches. The now-defunct site hosting the tool, ProtectYour.Org, was supported by the Mozilla Fellowship and the [Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation).[^o9][^o10][^o11] [Calyx Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calyx_Institute) has sponsored CryptoHarlem and its Crypto Parties since 2019.[^o12][^o13]

The [ACLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) and CryptoHarlem have worked together on issues related to surveillance and privacy legislation, including the Public Oversight of Surveillance Technology Act of 2020.[^o14]

In 2021, [Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek) profiled Mitchell and CryptoHarlem as one of "America's greatest disruptors" for their work against "digital stop and frisk".[^o14] The [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation) awarded Mitchell and CryptoHarlem with a Pioneer Award and hosted a discussion of their work.[^o6]

In 2022, Mitchell, representing CryptoHarlem, was an invited speaker on "Easy Fixes for Algorithmic Bias" at The [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College) Diversity in Computing Speaker Series.[^o15]
