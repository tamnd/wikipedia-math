# Tijdeman's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tijdeman's theorem"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Tijdeman's theorem** states that there are at most a finite number of consecutive powers. Stated another way, the set of solutions in integers *x*, *y*, *n*, *m* of the [exponential diophantine equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_diophantine_equation)

:::

$$y^m = x^n + 1,$$

for exponents *n* and *m* greater than one, is finite.[^o2][^o3]

## History {#History}

The theorem was proven by Dutch number theorist [Robert Tijdeman](wikipedia://en/9006087) in 1976,[^o5] making use of [Baker's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker's_method) in [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698) to give an [effective](wikipedia://en/250237) upper bound for *x*,*y*,*m*,*n*. [Michel Langevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Langevin_%28mathematician%29) computed a value of exp exp exp exp 730 for the bound.[^o2][^o6][^o7]

Tijdeman's theorem provided a strong impetus towards the eventual proof of [Catalan's conjecture](wikipedia://en/62382) by [Preda Mihăilescu](wikipedia://en/639556).[^o8] [Mihăilescu's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihăilescu's_theorem) states that there is only one member of the set of consecutive power pairs, namely 9=8+1.[^o9]

## Generalized Tijdeman problem {#Generalized_Tijdeman_problem}

That the powers are consecutive is essential to Tijdeman's proof; if we replace the difference of *1* by any other difference *k* and ask for the number of solutions of

$$y^m = x^n + k$$

with *n* and *m* greater than one we have an unsolved problem,[^o11] called the generalized Tijdeman problem. It is conjectured that this set also will be finite. This would follow from a yet stronger conjecture of [Subbayya Sivasankaranarayana Pillai](wikipedia://en/681150) (1931), see [Catalan's conjecture](wikipedia://en/62382), stating that the equation $A y^m = B x^n + k$ only has a finite number of solutions. The truth of Pillai's conjecture, in turn, would follow from the truth of the [abc conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108).[^o12]
