# Gottesman–Knill theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gottesman–Knill theorem"}

In [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220), the **Gottesman–Knill theorem** is a theoretical result by [Daniel Gottesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Gottesman) and [Emanuel Knill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Knill) that states that stabilizer circuits—circuits that only consist of gates from the [normalizer](wikipedia://en/145661) of the qubit [Pauli group](wikipedia://en/5045613), also called [Clifford group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_gates)—can be perfectly simulated in polynomial time on a probabilistic classical computer. The Clifford group can be [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) solely by using the [controlled NOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_NOT_gate), [Hadamard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_gate), and phase gates (CNOT, *H* and *S*);[^o2] and therefore [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) circuits can be constructed using only these gates.

:::

The reason for the speed up of quantum computers compared to classical ones is not yet fully understood. The Gottesman-Knill theorem proves that all quantum algorithms whose speed up relies on entanglement that can be achieved with [CNOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_NOT_gate) and [Hadamard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard) gates do not achieve any computational advantage relative to classical computers, due to the classical simulability of such algorithms (and the particular types of entangled states they can produce).

Since the theorem's initial statement, more efficient constructions for simulating such stabilizer (Clifford) circuits have been identified[^o2] with an implementation.[^o3]

The Gottesman–Knill theorem was published in a single-author paper by [Gottesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Gottesman), in which he credits [Knill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Knill) with the result, through private communication.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Theorem: A quantum circuit using only the following elements can be simulated efficiently on a classical computer:

1. Preparation of [qubits](wikipedia://en/25284) in computational-basis states.
1. [Clifford gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_gates) (generated by the [Hadamard gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_gate), [controlled NOT gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_NOT_gate), and phase gate *S* ).
1. Measurements in the computational basis.

The Gottesman–Knill theorem shows that even some highly [entangled](wikipedia://en/25336) states can be simulated efficiently on a classical computer. Several important types of quantum algorithms use only Clifford gates, including the standard algorithms for [entanglement distillation](wikipedia://en/20511149) and [quantum error correction](wikipedia://en/892803). From a practical point of view, stabilizer circuits on *n* qubits can be simulated in $O(n \log n)$ time using the [graph state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_state) formalism.

:::
