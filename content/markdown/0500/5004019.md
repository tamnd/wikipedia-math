# Anankastic conditional

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anankastic conditional"}

An **anankastic conditional** is a [grammatical construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_construction) of the form

:::

*If you want X, you have to do Y.*

where Y is required in order to get X. For example:

*If you wanna be my lover, you gotta get with my friends.*[^o2]

Not all conditionals of this form have an anankastic interpretation:

*If you want to eat chocolate, you should try thinking about something else.*

where thinking about something else is not required in order to eat chocolate, but is rather advice on how to avoid eating chocolate.[^o3]

The term comes from the Greek ἀναγκαστικός "compulsory", from ἀνάγκη "necessity."

Anankastic conditionals have been argued to pose problems for [compositional semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositional_semantics).[^o4] Other semanticists have argued that anankastic conditionals can be interpreted the same way as "regular, hypothetical, indicative conditionals".[^o3]
