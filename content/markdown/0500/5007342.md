# Oppenheim conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oppenheim conjecture"}

In [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), a subfield of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Oppenheim conjecture** concerns representations of numbers by [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) in several variables. It was formulated in 1929 by [Alexander Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Oppenheim) and later the [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) property was further strengthened by [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111) and Oppenheim. Initial research on this problem took the number *n* of variables to be large, and applied a version of the [Hardy-Littlewood circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy-Littlewood_circle_method). The definitive work of [Grigory Margulis](wikipedia://en/1104948), settling the conjecture in the affirmative, used methods arising from [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) and the study of [discrete subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_subgroup) of [semisimple Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

[Meyer's theorem](wikipedia://en/2115190) states that an [indefinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_quadratic_form) [integral quadratic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_quadratic_form) *Q* in *n* variables, *n* ≥ 5, nontrivially represents zero, i.e. there exists a non-zero vector *x* with integer components such that *Q*(*x*) = 0. The Oppenheim conjecture can be viewed as an analogue of this statement for forms *Q* that are not multiples of a rational form. It states that in this case, the set of values of *Q* on integer vectors is a [dense subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_subset) of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line).

## History {#History}

Several versions of the conjecture were formulated by Oppenheim and [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111).

- Let *Q* be a real nondegenerate indefinite quadratic form in *n* variables. Suppose that *n* ≥ 3 and *Q* is not a multiple of a form with rational coefficients. Then for any *ε* > 0 there exists a non-zero vector *x* with integer components such that |*Q*(*x*)| \< *ε*.

For *n* ≥ 5 this was conjectured by Oppenheim in 1929; the stronger version is due to Davenport in 1946.

- Let *Q* and *n* have the same meaning as before. Then for any *ε* > 0 there exists a non-zero vector *x* with integer components such that 0 \< |*Q*(*x*, *x*)| \< *ε*.

This was conjectured by Oppenheim in 1953 and [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by Birch, Davenport, and Ridout for *n* at least 21, and by Davenport and Heilbronn for diagonal forms in five variables. Other partial results are due to Oppenheim (for forms in four variables, but under the strong restriction that the form represents zero over **Z**), Watson, Iwaniec, Baker–Schlickewey. Early work involved [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and [reduction theory](wikipedia://en/1867129#Reduction_and_class_numbers) of quadratic forms.

The conjecture was proved in 1987 by Margulis in complete generality using methods of ergodic theory. Geometry of actions of certain [unipotent](wikipedia://en/1724478) subgroups of the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) on the [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) of the [lattices](wikipedia://en/469246) in **R**<sup>3</sup> plays a decisive role in this approach. It is sufficient to establish the case *n* = 3. The idea to derive the Oppenheim conjecture from a statement about homogeneous group actions is usually attributed to [M. S. Raghunathan](wikipedia://en/18730540), who observed in the 1970s that the conjecture for *n* = 3 is equivalent to the following property of the space of lattices:

- Any [relatively compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_compact) orbit of SO(2, 1) in SL(3, **R**)/SL(3, **Z**) is [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set).

However, Margulis later remarked that in an implicit form this equivalence occurred already in a 1955 paper of [Cassels](wikipedia://en/1709206) and [H. P. F. Swinnerton-Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._P._F._Swinnerton-Dyer), albeit in a different language.[^o4]

Shortly after Margulis's breakthrough, the proof was simplified and generalized by Dani and Margulis. Qualitative versions of the Oppenheim conjecture were later proved by Eskin–Margulis–Mozes. [Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) and [Prasad](wikipedia://en/26770296) established some *S*-arithmetic analogues. The study of the properties of unipotent and quasiunipotent flows on homogeneous spaces remains an active area of research, with applications to further questions in the theory of Diophantine approximation.
