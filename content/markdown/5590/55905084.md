# The Turing Guide

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Turing Guide"}

***The Turing Guide***, written by [Jack Copeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Copeland), [Jonathan Bowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Bowen), Mark Sprevak,[^o3] [Robin Wilson](wikipedia://en/2715277), and others and published in 2017, is a book about the work and life of the British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), and early [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) (1912–1954).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The book includes 42 contributed chapters by a variety of authors, including some contemporaries of Alan Turing. The book was published in January 2017 by [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press),[^o5] in [hardcover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcover), [paperback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paperback), and [e-book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-book) formats.

## Contents {#Contents}

*The Turing Guide* is divided into eight main parts, covering various aspects of Alan Turing's life and work:[^o7]

1. *Biography*: Biographical aspects of Alan Turing.
1. *The Universal Machine and Beyond*: Turing's universal machine (now known as a [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403)), developed while at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), which provides a theoretical framework for reasoning about computation, a starting point for the field of [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).
1. *Codebreaker*: Turing's work on [codebreaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codebreaking) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), especially the [Bombe](wikipedia://en/615354) for decrypting the German [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256).
1. *Computers after the War*: Turing's post-War work on computing, at the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory_%28United_Kingdom%29) (NPL) and at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester). He made contributions to both hardware design, through the [ACE computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACE_computer) (later implemented as the [Pilot ACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_ACE)) at the NPL, and software, especially at Manchester using the [Manchester Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Baby) computer, later the [Manchester Mark 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Mark_1) and [Ferranti Mark 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferranti_Mark_1).
1. *Artificial Intelligence and the Mind*: Turing's pioneering and philosophical contribution to machine intelligence (now known as [Artificial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence) or AI), including the [Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test).
1. *Biological Growth*: [Morphogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphogenesis), Turing's last major scientific contribution, on the generation of complex patterns through [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) processes in [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) and on the mathematics behind them, foundational in [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology).
1. *Mathematics*: Some of Turing's mathematical achievements, including one of his most significant influences, [Max Newman](wikipedia://en/19867).
1. *Finale*: Turing in a wider subsequent context, including his influence and legacy to science and in the public consciousness.

The book includes a foreword by [Andrew Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Hodges), a preface, notes on the contributors, endnotes, and an index.

## Cover {#Cover}

This artwork for the book's cover came about after a mock-up digital artwork in the style of the multiple images of [Andy Warhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol) was produced.[^o24] Jack Copeland then organized a more professional artwork,[^o25] which became the basis for the eventual book cover.[^o26] In 2023, the artwork was displayed as part of a digital art exhibition organized by the [Computer Arts Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Arts_Society) at the [BCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Computer_Society) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London).[^o27]
