# Hecke algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hecke algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hecke algebra** is the [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) generated by [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837), which are named after [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The algebra is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346).[^o4][^o5]

In the classical [elliptic modular form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_modular_form) theory, the Hecke operators *T*<sub>*n*</sub> with *n* coprime to the level acting on the space of cusp forms of a given weight are [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408) with respect to the [Petersson inner product](wikipedia://en/4028987).[^o6] Therefore, the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) implies that there is a basis of modular forms that are [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) for these Hecke operators. Each of these basic forms possesses an [Euler product](wikipedia://en/398540). More precisely, its [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) is the [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) that has [Euler products](wikipedia://en/398540) with the local factor for each prime *p* is the reciprocal of the **Hecke polynomial**, a quadratic polynomial in *p*<sup>−*s*</sup>.[^o7][^o8] In the case treated by Mordell, the space of cusp forms of weight 12 with respect to the full modular group is one-dimensional. It follows that the Ramanujan form has an Euler product and establishes the multiplicativity of *τ*(*n*).[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The classical Hecke algebra has been generalized to other settings, such as the [Hecke algebra of a locally compact group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_algebra_of_a_locally_compact_group) and spherical Hecke algebra that arise when modular forms and other automorphic forms are viewed using [adelic groups](wikipedia://en/247978#Automorphic_forms).[^o12] These play a central role in the [Langlands correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_correspondence).[^o13]

The **derived Hecke algebra** is a further generalization of Hecke algebras to [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875).[^o13][^o14][^o15] It was introduced by [Peter Schneider](wikipedia://en/64772763) in 2015 who, together with [Rachel Ollivier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Ollivier), used them to study the *p*-adic Langlands correspondence.[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16] It is the subject of several conjectures on the cohomology of [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741) by [Akshay Venkatesh](wikipedia://en/6647964) and his collaborators.[^o13][^o15][^o17][^o18][^o19]
