# Hecke algebra of a finite group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hecke algebra of a finite group"}

The **Hecke algebra of a finite group** is the [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) spanned by the [double cosets](wikipedia://en/982771) *HgH* of a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) *G*. It is a special case of a [Hecke algebra of a locally compact group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_algebra_of_a_locally_compact_group).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *F* be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero, *G* a finite [group](wikipedia://en/19447) and *H* a subgroup of *G*. Let $F[G]$ denote the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) of *G*: the space of *F*-valued functions on *G* with the multiplication given by convolution. We write $F[G/H]$ for the space of *F*-valued functions on $G/H$. An (*F*-valued) function on *G*/*H* determines and is determined by a function on *G* that is invariant under the right action of *H*. That is, there is the natural identification:

$$F[G/H] = F[G]^H.$$

Similarly, there is the identification

$$R := \operatorname{End}_G(F[G/H]) = F[G]^{H \times H}$$

given by sending a *G*-linear map *f* to the value of *f* evaluated at the characteristic function of *H*. For each double coset $HgH$, let $T_g$ denote the characteristic function of it. Then those $T_g$'s form a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) of *R*.

:::

## Application in representation theory {#Application_in_representation_theory}

Let $\varphi : G \rightarrow GL(V)$ be any finite-dimensional [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_a_finite_group) of a finite group *G*, the Hecke algebra $H = \operatorname{End}_G(V)$ is the algebra of *G*-[equivariant](wikipedia://en/19378200#Equivariant_maps_and_isomorphisms) [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of *V*. For each [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) $W$ of *G*, the [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of *H* on *V* preserves $\tilde{W}$ – the [isotypic component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotypic_component) of $W$ – and commutes with $W$ as a *G* action.
