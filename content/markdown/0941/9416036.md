# Hall's universal group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hall's universal group"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), **Hall's universal group** is a countable [locally finite group](wikipedia://en/4939270), say *U*, which is uniquely characterized by the following properties.

:::

- Every finite group *G* admits a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) to *U*.
- All such monomorphisms are conjugate by [inner automorphisms](wikipedia://en/61839) of *U*.

It was defined by [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) in 1959,[^o2] and has the universal property that *all countable locally finite groups* embed into it.

Hall's universal group is the [Fraïssé limit](wikipedia://en/62592127) of the class of all finite groups.

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Take any group $\Gamma_0$ of order $\geq 3$. Denote by $\Gamma_1$ the group $S_{\Gamma_0}$ of [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of elements of $\Gamma_0$, by $\Gamma_2$ the group

$$S_{\Gamma_1}= S_{S_{\Gamma_0}} \,$$

and so on. Since a group acts faithfully on itself by permutations

$$x\mapsto gx \,$$

according to [Cayley's theorem](wikipedia://en/105967), this gives a chain of monomorphisms

$$\Gamma_0 \hookrightarrow \Gamma_1 \hookrightarrow \Gamma_2 \hookrightarrow \cdots . \,$$

A [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) (that is, a union) of all $\Gamma_i$ is Hall's universal group *U*.

Indeed, *U* then contains a [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of arbitrarily large order, and any group admits a monomorphism to a [group of permutations](wikipedia://en/28901), as explained above. Let *G* be a finite group admitting two embeddings to *U*. Since *U* is a direct limit and *G* is finite, the images of these two embeddings belong to $\Gamma_i \subset U$. The group $\Gamma_{i+1}= S_{\Gamma_i}$ acts on $\Gamma_i$ by permutations, and conjugates all possible embeddings $G \hookrightarrow \Gamma_i$.[^o2]

:::
