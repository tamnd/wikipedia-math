# Cayley plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cayley plane"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cayley plane** (or **octonionic projective plane**) **P**<sup>2</sup>(**O**) is a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) over the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436).[^o2]

:::

The Cayley plane was discovered in 1933 by [Ruth Moufang](wikipedia://en/2003831), and is named after [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) for his 1845 paper describing the octonions.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

In the Cayley plane, lines and points may be defined in a natural way so that it becomes a 2-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135), that is, a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350). It is a [non-Desarguesian plane](wikipedia://en/21092112), where [Desargues' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desargues'_theorem) does not hold.

More precisely, as of 2005, there are two objects called Cayley planes, namely the real and the complex Cayley plane. The **real Cayley plane** is the [symmetric space](wikipedia://en/1642877) [F<sub>4</sub>](wikipedia://en/292877)/Spin(9), where F<sub>4</sub> is a compact form of an [exceptional Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exceptional_Lie_group) and Spin(9) is the [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) of nine-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) (realized in F<sub>4</sub>). It admits a cell decomposition into three cells, of dimensions 0, 8 and 16.[^o5]

The **complex Cayley plane** is a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) under the complexification of the group [E<sub>6</sub>](wikipedia://en/293725) by a [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) *P*<sub>1</sub>. It is the closed orbit in the projectivization of the minimal complex representation of E<sub>6</sub>. The complex Cayley plane consists of two complex F<sub>4</sub>-orbits: the closed orbit is a quotient of the complexified F<sub>4</sub> by a parabolic subgroup, the open orbit is the complexification of the real Cayley plane,[^o6] retracting to it.
