# Maryam Mirzakhani

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Maryam Mirzakhani"}

**Maryam Mirzakhani** (مریم میرزاخانی, ; 12 May 1977 – 14 July 2017) was an Iranian[^o5][^o6] mathematician and a professor of mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o7][^o8] Her research focused on [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). In 2014, she was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for her work in "the dynamics and geometry of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) and their [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609)", becoming the first woman and the first Iranian citizen to win the prize.[^o9][^o10] Along with the Fields Medal, her work earned numerous honors, including the [Blumenthal Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumenthal_Award), the [Satter Prize](wikipedia://en/37554140), and the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167).

:::

Born and raised in [Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran), Iran, Mirzakhani studied mathematics at [Sharif University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_University_of_Technology), graduating in 1999 before earning her [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2004 under the supervision of [Curtis T. McMullen](wikipedia://en/680168). She subsequently held positions at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), becoming a full professor at Stanford in 2009. Through her research in the 2000s and 2010s, Mirzakhani made major contributions to the understanding of hyperbolic surfaces, [Teichmüller dynamics](wikipedia://en/1835661), and the geometry of moduli spaces. In colloboration with [Alex Eskin](wikipedia://en/31883666) and later [Amir Mohammadi](wikipedia://en/81276118), she achieved breakthrough results on the dynamics of moduli spaces that became known as the "magic wand theorem".[^o11]

Mirzakhani died from [breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer) on 14 July 2017 at the age of 40.[^o12] Following her death, several initiatives and awards were established in her memory, including the [Maryam Mirzakhani New Frontiers Prize](wikipedia://en/43125933#Maryam_Mirzakhani_New_Frontiers_Prize) and the 12 May Initiative, both of which aim to promote the participation of women in mathematics.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Mirzakhani was born on 12 May 1977[^o14][^o15] in [Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran).[^o16] As a child, she attended [Tehran Farzanegan School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Farzanegan_School), part of the [National Organization for Development of Exceptional Talents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Organization_for_Development_of_Exceptional_Talents) (NODET). In her junior and senior years of high school, she won the gold medal for mathematics in the Iranian National Olympiad, thus allowing her to bypass the national college entrance exam.[^o17] In 1994, Mirzakhani became the first Iranian woman to win a gold medal at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong), scoring 41 out of 42 points.[^o18] The following year, in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), she became the first Iranian to achieve the full score and to win two gold medals in the International Mathematical Olympiad.[^o20]

Mirzakhani’s earliest published works, when she was a high school student and in early college, were in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). Her first publication, in 1995, was on decomposing complete [tripartite graphs](wikipedia://en/39469099) into [5-cycles](wikipedia://en/168609).[^o21]

Later in her life, she collaborated with friend, colleague, and [IMO](wikipedia://en/82208) silver medalist Roya Beheshti Zavareh (رؤیا بهشتی زواره) on their book *Elementary Number Theory, Challenging Problems* (in [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language)), which was published in 1999.[^o17]

On 17 March 1998, after attending a conference consisting of gifted individuals and former Olympiad competitors, Mirzakhani and Zavareh, along with other attendees, boarded a bus in [Ahvaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvaz) en route to Tehran. The bus fell off a cliff, killing seven of the passengers, all Sharif University students, in what is remembered as a national tragedy in Iran. Mirzakhani and Zavareh were two of the survivors.[^o22]

In 1999, she obtained a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics from the [Sharif University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_University_of_Technology). During her time there, she developed a simpler proof of [a theorem of Schur](wikipedia://en/1522286#Linear_algebra).[^o23][^o24] She then went to the United States for graduate work, earning a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 2004 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where she worked under the supervision of the Fields Medalist, [Curtis T. McMullen](wikipedia://en/680168).[^o25] At Harvard, she is said to have been "distinguished by determination and relentless questioning". She used to take her class notes in her native language [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language).[^o26][^o27]

## Career {#Career}

Mirzakhani was a 2004 research fellow of the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) and a professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). In 2009, she became a professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o30][^o31][^o32]

### Research work {#Research_work}

*Image: Remise de la médaille Fields à Maryam Mirzakhani.webm.* Maryam Mirzakhani receiving the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), August 2014

Mirzakhani made several contributions to the theory of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181). Mirzakhani's early work solved the problem of counting simple closed [geodesics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesics) on [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291) Riemann surfaces by finding a relationship to volume calculations on moduli space. Geodesics are the natural generalization of the idea of a "[straight line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29)" to "[curved spaces](wikipedia://en/2073470)". Slightly more formally, a curve is a geodesic if no slight deformation can make it shorter. Closed geodesics are geodesics which are also closed curves—that is, they are curves that close up into loops. A closed geodesic is [simple](wikipedia://en/89246#Definitions) if it does not cross itself.[^o34]

A previous result, known as the "[prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) for geodesics", established that the number of closed geodesics of length less than $L$ grows exponentially with $L$ – it is asymptotic to $e^L/L$. However, the analogous counting problem for simple closed geodesics remained open, despite being "the key object to unlocking the structure and geometry of the whole surface," according to University of Chicago topologist [Benson Farb](wikipedia://en/32518776).[^o35] Mirzakhani's 2004 PhD thesis solved this problem, showing that the number of simple closed geodesics of length less than $L$ is polynomial in $L$. Explicitly, it is asymptotic to $cL^{6g-6}$, where $g$ is the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) (roughly, the number of "holes") and $c$ is a constant depending on the hyperbolic structure. This result can be seen as a generalization of the [theorem of the three geodesics](wikipedia://en/48445265) for [spherical surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_surface).[^o36][^o37]

Mirzakhani solved this counting problem by relating it to the problem of computing volumes in [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609)—a space whose points correspond to different complex structures on a surface genus $g$. In her thesis, Mirzakhani found a volume formula for the moduli space of bordered Riemann surfaces of genus $g$ with $n$ geodesic boundary components. From this formula followed the counting for simple closed geodesics mentioned above, as well as a number of other results. This led her to obtain a new proof for the formula discovered by [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) and [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509) on the intersection numbers of tautological classes on moduli space.[^o7][^o38]

Her subsequent work focused on Teichmüller dynamics of moduli space. In particular, she was able to prove the long-standing conjecture that [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s [earthquake](wikipedia://en/31112690) flow on [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) is [ergodic](wikipedia://en/5456824).[^o39] One can construct a simple earthquake map by cutting a surface along a finite number of disjoint simple closed geodesics, sliding the edges of each of these cuts past each other by some amount, and closing the surface back up. One can imagine the surface being cut by [strike-slip faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_%28geology%29#Strike-slip_faults). An earthquake is a sort of limit of simple earthquakes, where one has an infinite number of geodesics, and instead of attaching a positive real number to each geodesic, one puts a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on them.

In 2014, with [Alex Eskin](wikipedia://en/31883666) and with input from Amir Mohammadi, Mirzakhani proved that complex geodesics and their closures in moduli space are surprisingly regular, rather than irregular or [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913).[^o40][^o41] The closures of complex geodesics are algebraic objects defined in terms of polynomials and therefore, they have certain rigidity properties, which is analogous to a celebrated result that [Marina Ratner](wikipedia://en/12687622) arrived at during the 1990s.[^o41] The [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) said in its press release that "It is astounding to find that the rigidity in homogeneous spaces has an echo in the inhomogeneous world of moduli space."[^o41]

### Awarding of Fields Medal {#Awarding_of_Fields_Medal}

*Image: Four\_Fields\_medallists\_plus\_epsilon.jpg.* Four Fields medallists left to right [Artur Avila](wikipedia://en/23579022), [Martin Hairer](wikipedia://en/26920158) (at back), Maryam Mirzakhani (with her daughter) and [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088) at the ICM 2014 in Seoul

Mirzakhani was awarded the Fields Medal in 2014 for "her outstanding contributions to the [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_%28mechanics%29) and geometry of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) and their moduli spaces".[^o43] The award was made in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul) at the International Congress of Mathematicians on 13 August.[^o44] At the time of the award, [Jordan Ellenberg](wikipedia://en/19765796) explained her research to a popular audience: \[Her\] work expertly blends dynamics with geometry. Among other things, she studies billiards. But now, in a move very characteristic of modern mathematics, it gets kind of meta: She considers not just one billiard table, but the universe of all *possible* billiard tables. And the kind of dynamics she studies doesn't directly concern the motion of the billiards on the table, but instead a transformation of the billiard table itself, which is changing its shape in a rule-governed way; if you like, the table itself moves like a strange planet around the universe of all possible tables ... This isn't the kind of thing you do to win at pool, but it's the kind of thing you do to win a Fields Medal. And it's what you need to do in order to expose the dynamics at the heart of geometry; for there's no question that they're there.[^o45]

In 2014, President [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani) of Iran congratulated her for winning the award.[^o46]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 2008, Mirzakhani married [Jan Vondrák](wikipedia://en/71954772), a Czech [theoretical computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_computer_scientist) and [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) who is a professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o48][^o49] They had a daughter.[^o50] Mirzakhani lived in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California).[^o51] Mirzakhani described herself as a "slow" mathematician, saying that "you have to spend some energy and effort to see the beauty of math." To solve problems, Mirzakhani would draw doodles on sheets of paper and write mathematical formulas around the drawings. Her daughter described her mother's work as "painting".[^o26][^o52]

Mirzakhani declared: I don't have any particular recipe \[for developing new proofs\] ... It is like being lost in a jungle and trying to use all the knowledge that you can gather to come up with some new tricks, and with some luck, you might find a way out.[^o26]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Mirzakhani was diagnosed with [breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer) in 2013.[^o26] In 2016, the cancer [spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metastatic_breast_cancer) to her bones and liver,[^o26][^o54] and she died on 14 July 2017 at the age of 40 at [Stanford Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Hospital) in [Stanford, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California).[^o26][^o55]

Iranian president [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani) and other officials offered their condolences and praised Mirzakhani's scientific achievements. Rouhani said in his message that "the unprecedented brilliance of this creative scientist and modest human being, who made Iran's name resonate in the world's scientific forums, was a turning point in showing the great will of Iranian women and young people on the path towards reaching the peaks of glory and in various international arenas."[^o26]

Upon her death, several Iranian newspapers, along with President Hassan Rouhani, broke taboo and published photographs of Mirzakhani with her hair uncovered. Although most newspapers used photographs with a dark background, digital manipulation, and even paintings to "hide" her hair,[^o9][^o56] this gesture was widely noted in the Western press and on social media.[^o57][^o58]

Mirzakhani's death has also renewed debates within Iran regarding [matrilineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilineality) citizenship for children of mixed-nationality parentage; [Fars News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fars_News_Agency) reported that, subsequent to Mirzakhani's death, 60 [Iranian MPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Consultative_Assembly) urged the speeding up of an amendment to a law that would allow children of Iranian mothers married to foreigners to be given [Iranian nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_nationality), in order to make it easier for Mirzakhani's daughter to visit Iran.[^o9][^o57]

*Image: تالار میرزاخانی خانه ریاضیات اصفهان.jpg.* Plaque for Mirzakhani Hall in Isfahan's House of Mathematics

Numerous obituaries and tributes were published in the days following Mirzakhani's death.[^o59][^o60] As a result of advocacy carried out by the Women's Committee within the Iranian Mathematical Society (کمیته بانوان انجمن ریاضی ایران), the International Council for Science agreed to declare Mirzakhani's birthday, 12 May, as [International Women in Mathematics Day](wikipedia://en/79951130) in respect of her memory.[^o61][^o62]

Various establishments have also been named after Mirzakhani to honor her life and achievements. In 2017, Farzanegan High School – the high school Mirzakhani formerly attended – named their amphitheater and library after her. Additionally, [Sharif University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_University_of_Technology), the institute wherein Mirzakhani obtained her bachelor's, has since named their main library in the College of Mathematics after her. Further, the House of Mathematics in [Isfahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan), in collaboration with the mayor, named a conference hall in the city after her.[^o63]

In 2014, students at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) founded the Mirzakhani Society, a society for women and non-binary students studying mathematics at the University of Oxford. Mirzakhani met the society in September 2015, when she visited Oxford.[^o64]

In 2016, Mirzakhani was made a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (of the United States), making her the first Iranian woman to be officially accepted as a member of the academy.[^o65]

On 2 February 2018, [Satellogic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellogic), a high-resolution [Earth observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_observation) imaging and analytics company, launched a [ÑuSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ÑuSat) type [micro-satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite) named in honor of Mirzakhani.[^o66]

On 4 November 2019, [The Breakthrough Prize Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize) announced that the Maryam Mirzakhani New Frontiers Prize has been created to be awarded to outstanding women in the field of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) each year. The \$50,000 [award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Award) will be presented to [early-career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_investigator) [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) who have completed their [PhDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) within the past two years.[^o67][^o68]

In February 2020, on International Day of Women and Girls in [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663), Mirzakhani was honored by [UN Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Women) as one of seven female scientists dead or alive who have shaped the world.[^o69]

In 2020, [George Csicsery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Csicsery) featured her in the documentary film *Secrets of the Surface: The Mathematical Vision of Maryam Mirzakhani*.[^o70][^o71]

The 12 May Initiative was created in Mirzakhani's honor[^o14] to celebrate women in mathematics. The Initiative is coordinated by the [European Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/58318372), [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698), [African Women in Mathematics Association](wikipedia://en/66387234), [Colectivo de Mujeres Matemáticas de Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colectivo_de_Mujeres_Matemáticas_de_Chile), and the Women's Committee of the [Iranian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/46332302). In 2020, 152 events were held.[^o72]

In 2022, following a £2.48m donation from [XTX Markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XTX_Markets), the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) launched the Maryam Mirzakhani Scholarships, which provide support for female mathematicians pursuing doctoral studies at the university.[^o73]

On 8 March 2022, the [Ecole Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecole_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) named one of its streets in honor of Mirzakhani.[^o74]
