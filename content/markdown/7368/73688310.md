# Weinstein's neighbourhood theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weinstein's neighbourhood theorem"}

In [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Weinstein's neighbourhood theorem** refers to a few distinct but related theorems, involving the [neighbourhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) of [submanifolds](wikipedia://en/525095) in [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356) and generalising the classical [Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358).[^o2] They were proved by [Alan Weinstein](wikipedia://en/7344764) in 1971.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Darboux-Moser-Weinstein theorem"}

## Darboux-Moser-Weinstein theorem {#Darboux-Moser-Weinstein_theorem}

This statement is a direct generalisation of [Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358), which is recovered by taking a point as $X$.[^o2][^o3]

> Let $M$ be a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) of dimension $2n$, and $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ two [symplectic forms](wikipedia://en/28356) on $M$. Consider a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) submanifold $i: X \hookrightarrow M$ such that $i^* \omega_1 = i^* \omega_2$. Then there exist
>
> two open [neighbourhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) $U_1$ and $U_2$ of $X$ in $M$;
>
> a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) $f: U_1 \to U_2$;
>
> such that $f^* \omega_2 = \omega_1$ and $f |_X = \mathrm{id}_X$.

Its proof employs [Moser's trick](wikipedia://en/73989969).[^o6][^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Generalisation: equivariant Darboux theorem"}

### Generalisation: equivariant Darboux theorem {#Generalisation:_equivariant_Darboux_theorem}

The statement (and the proof) of Darboux-Moser-Weinstein theorem can be generalised in presence of a symplectic action of a Lie group.[^o3]

> Let $M$ be a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) of dimension $2n$, and $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ two symplectic forms on $M$. Let also $G$ be a compact [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) [acting](wikipedia://en/39904936) on $M$ and leaving both $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ invariant. Consider a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and $G$-invariant submanifold $i: X \hookrightarrow M$ such that $i^* \omega_1 = i^* \omega_2$. Then there exist
>
> two open $G$-invariant [neighbourhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) $U_1$ and $U_2$ of $X$ in $M$;
>
> a $G$-equivariant [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) $f: U_1 \to U_2$;
>
> such that $f^* \omega_2 = \omega_1$ and $f |_X = \mathrm{id}_X$.

In particular, taking again $X$ as a point, one obtains an equivariant version of the classical Darboux theorem.

:::

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Weinstein's Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem"}

## Weinstein's Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem {#Weinstein's_Lagrangian_neighbourhood_theorem}

> Let $M$ be a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) of dimension $2n$, and $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ two [symplectic forms](wikipedia://en/28356) on $M$. Consider a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) submanifold $i: L \hookrightarrow M$ of dimension $n$ which is a [Lagrangian submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_submanifold) of both $(M,\omega_1)$ and $(M, \omega_2)$, i.e. $i^* \omega_1 = i^* \omega_2 = 0$. Then there exist
>
> two open [neighbourhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) $U_1$ and $U_2$ of $L$ in $M$;
>
> a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/312304) $f: U_1 \to U_2$;
>
> such that $f^* \omega_2 = \omega_1$ and $f |_L = \mathrm{id}_L$.

This statement is proved using the Darboux-Moser-Weinstein theorem, taking $X = L$ a Lagrangian submanifold, together with a version of the [Whitney Extension Theorem](wikipedia://en/5456164) for smooth manifolds.[^o2]

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Generalisation: Coisotropic Embedding Theorem"}

### Generalisation: Coisotropic Embedding Theorem {#Generalisation:_Coisotropic_Embedding_Theorem}

Weinstein's result can be generalised by weakening the assumption that $L$ is Lagrangian.[^o14][^o15]

> Let $M$ be a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) of dimension $2n$, and $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ two symplectic forms on $M$. Consider a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) submanifold $i: L \hookrightarrow M$ of dimension $k$ which is a [coisotropic submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropic_submanifold) of both $(M,\omega_1)$ and $(M, \omega_2)$, and such that $i^* \omega_1 = i^* \omega_2$. Then there exist
>
> two open [neighbourhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) $U_1$ and $U_2$ of $L$ in $M$;
>
> a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/312304) $f: U_1 \to U_2$;
>
> such that $f^* \omega_2 = \omega_1$ and $f |_L = \mathrm{id}_L$.

:::

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Weinstein's tubular neighbourhood theorem"}

## Weinstein's tubular neighbourhood theorem {#Weinstein's_tubular_neighbourhood_theorem}

While [Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358) identifies locally a symplectic manifold $M$ with $T^*L$, Weinstein's theorem identifies locally a Lagrangian $L$ with the zero section of $T^*L$. More precisely

> Let $(M,\omega)$ be a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) and $L$ a [Lagrangian submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_submanifold). Then there exist
>
> an open [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) $U$ of $L$ in $M$;
>
> an open neighbourhood $V$ of the [zero section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_section) $L_0$ in the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) $T^*L$;
>
> a [symplectomorphism](wikipedia://en/312304) $f: U \to V$;
>
> such that $f$ sends $L$ to $L_0$.

::: {#o19 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

This statement relies on the Weinstein's Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem, as well as on the standard [tubular neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/3192042) theorem.[^o2]
