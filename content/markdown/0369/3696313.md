# Pseudo algebraically closed field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pseudo algebraically closed field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ is **pseudo algebraically closed** if it satisfies certain properties which hold for [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018). The concept was introduced by [James Ax](wikipedia://en/6165715) in 1967.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

A field *K* is pseudo algebraically closed (usually abbreviated by **PAC**[^o8]) if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

- Each [absolutely irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_irreducible) variety $V$ defined over $K$ has a $K$-[rational point](wikipedia://en/2960200).
- For each absolutely irreducible polynomial $f\in K[T_1,T_2,\cdots ,T_r,X]$ with $\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}\not =0$ and for each nonzero $g\in K[T_1,T_2,\cdots ,T_r]$ there exists $(\textbf{a},b)\in K^{r+1}$ such that $f(\textbf{a},b)=0$ and $g(\textbf{a})\not =0$.
- Each absolutely irreducible polynomial $f\in K[T,X]$ has infinitely many $K$-rational points.
- If $R$ is a finitely generated [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) over $K$ with [quotient field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_field) which is [regular](wikipedia://en/26047190) over $K$, then there exist a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) $h:R\to K$ such that $h(a) = a$ for each $a \in K$.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Algebraically closed fields and [separably closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separably_closed) fields are always PAC.
- [Pseudo-finite fields](wikipedia://en/37613028) and [hyper-finite fields](wikipedia://en/37598729) are PAC.
- A non-principal [ultraproduct](wikipedia://en/273642) of distinct [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) is (pseudo-finite and hence[^o7]) PAC.[^o8] Ax deduces this from the [Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_hypothesis_for_curves_over_finite_fields).[^o2]
- Infinite [algebraic extensions](wikipedia://en/2125) of finite fields are PAC.[^o9]
- *The PAC Nullstellensatz*. The [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) $G$ of a field $K$ is [profinite](wikipedia://en/24266), hence [compact](wikipedia://en/750326), and hence equipped with a normalized [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645). Let $K$ be a countable [Hilbertian field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbertian_field) and let $e$ be a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). Then for almost all $e$-tuples $(\sigma_1,...,\sigma_e)\in G^e$, the fixed field of the [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) generated by the [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) is PAC. Here the phrase "almost all" means "all but a set of [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) zero".[^o10] (This result is a consequence of Hilbert's irreducibility theorem.)
- Let *K* be the maximal [totally real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) and *i* the square root of −1. Then *K*(*i*) is PAC.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) of a PAC field is trivial,[^o13] as any [Severi–Brauer variety](wikipedia://en/1669368) has a rational point.[^o14]
- The [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of a PAC field is a [projective profinite group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_profinite_group); equivalently, it has [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) at most 1.[^o14]
- A PAC field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero is [C1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C1_field).[^o15]
