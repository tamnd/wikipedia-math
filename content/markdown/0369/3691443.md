# Rhombic icosahedron

*Image: Rhombic icosahedron.stl.* A rhombic icosahedron

*Image: Rhombic icosahedron (green) 01.gif.* A rhombic icosahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rhombic icosahedron"}

The **rhombic icosahedron** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) shaped like an [oblate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblate_spheroid) [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859). Its 20 faces are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) golden rhombi;[^o3] 3, 4, or 5 faces meet at each vertex. It has 5 faces (green on top figure) meeting at each of its 2 poles; these 2 vertices lie on its axis of 5-fold symmetry, which is perpendicular to 5 axes of 2-fold symmetry through the midpoints of opposite equatorial edges (example on top figure: most left-hand and most right-hand mid-edges). Its other 10 faces follow its equator, 5 above and 5 below it; each of these 10 rhombi has 2 of its 4 sides lying on this zig-zag [skew decagon](wikipedia://en/6545461) equator. The rhombic icosahedron has 22 vertices. It has [D<sub>**5**d</sub>](wikipedia://en/2922089), \[2<sup>\+</sup>,10\], (2\*5) symmetry group, of order 20; thus it has a center of symmetry (since **5** is odd).

:::

Even though all its faces are congruent, the rhombic icosahedron is not [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive), since one can distinguish whether a particular face is near the equator or near a pole by examining the types of vertices surrounding this face.

## Zonohedron {#Zonohedron}

The rhombic icosahedron is a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402).

The rhombic icosahedron has 5 sets of 8 parallel edges, described as 8<sub>5</sub> [belts](wikipedia://en/25207334#Topological_types).

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| *Image: Rhombic icosahedron 5-color-paralleledges.png.* | The edges of the rhombic icosahedron can be grouped in 5 parallel-sets, seen in this wireframe orthogonal projection. |
|---|---|

:::

The rhombic icosahedron forms the convex hull of the vertex-first projection of a [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215) to 3 dimensions. The 32 vertices of a 5-cube map into the 22 exterior vertices of the rhombic icosahedron, with the remaining 10 interior vertices forming a [pentagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860091).

In the same way, one can obtain a [Rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) from a [4-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-cube), and a [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025) from a [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462).

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

The rhombic icosahedron can be derived from the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025) by removing a belt of 10 middle faces with parallel edges.

(\*) (For example, on the left-hand figure):

The orthogonal projection of the (vertical) belt of 10 middle faces of the rhombic triacontahedron is just the (horizontal) exterior regular decagon of the common orthogonal projection.

Removal of a further belt of 8 faces with parallel edges from the icosahedron results in the [Bilinski dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/50652823), which is topologically equivalent but not congruent to the regular [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631).
