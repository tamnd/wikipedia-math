# David Evans (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Evans"}

**David V. Evans** (27 October 1940 – 13 March 2026) was a British [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) noted for his contributions to [water waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_waves) and [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics).

:::

Together with [John Nicholas Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nicholas_Newman), he initiated the *International Workshop on Water Waves and Floating Bodies*.[^o3] He was also known for the Bristol cylinder, a [wave energy converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_energy_converter). He was an [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor) of [Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Mathematics) at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol).

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Evans obtained his BSc in mathematics from the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) in 1962 and his PhD in 1966 under the supervision of [Fritz Ursell](wikipedia://en/10408290).[^o5] After completing his PhD at the University of Manchester, Evans worked as a [post-doc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-doc) at the [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Institute_of_Technology) and [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) before going back to Bristol.[^o6]

The 21st International Workshop on Water Waves and Floating Bodies is dedicated to Evans on the occasion of his retirement.[^o7]

Evans died on 13 March 2026, at the age of 85.[^o8]
