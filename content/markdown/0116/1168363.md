# List of mathematical knots and links

*Image: Knot table.svg.* A table of all [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) with seven [crossings](wikipedia://en/18298598) or fewer (not including mirror images).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of mathematical knots and links"}

This article contains a **list of [mathematical knots](wikipedia://en/366808) and [links](wikipedia://en/1161784)**. See also [list of knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_knots), [list of geometric topology topics](wikipedia://en/348470).

:::

## Knots {#Knots}

### Prime knots {#Prime_knots}

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Unknot"}

- 0<sub>1</sub> knot/[Unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) - a simple un-knotted closed loop

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Trefoil knot"}

- 3<sub>1</sub> knot/[Trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773) - (2,3)-torus knot, the two loose ends of a common overhand knot joined together

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Figure-eight knot (mathematics)"}

- 4<sub>1</sub> knot/[Figure-eight knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/59565) - a prime knot with a crossing number four

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cinquefoil knot"}

- 5<sub>1</sub> knot/[Cinquefoil knot](wikipedia://en/16856240), (5,2)-torus knot, Solomon's seal knot, pentafoil knot - a prime knot with crossing number five which can be arranged as a {5/2} star polygon ([pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544))

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="Three-twist knot"}

- 5<sub>2</sub> knot/[Three-twist knot](wikipedia://en/26643955) - the twist knot with three-half twists

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Stevedore knot (mathematics)"}

- 6<sub>1</sub> knot/[Stevedore knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/26652059) - a prime knot with crossing number six, it can also be described as a twist knot with four twists

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="62 knot"}

- [6<sub>2</sub> knot](wikipedia://en/26677990) - a prime knot with crossing number six

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="63 knot"}

- [6<sub>3</sub> knot](wikipedia://en/26684180) - a prime knot with crossing number six

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=list-entry name="71 knot"}

- [7<sub>1</sub> knot](wikipedia://en/26657115), septafoil knot, (7,2)-torus knot - a prime knot with crossing number seven, which can be arranged as a {7/2} star polygon ([heptagram](wikipedia://en/180673))

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="74 knot"}

- [7<sub>4</sub> knot](wikipedia://en/38989391), "endless knot"

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="818 knot"}

- [8<sub>18</sub> knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrick_mat), "carrick mat"

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Perko pair"}

- 10<sub>161</sub>/10<sub>162</sub>, known as the [Perko pair](wikipedia://en/16618607); this was a single knot listed twice in Dale Rolfsen's knot table; the duplication was discovered by Kenneth Perko

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="(−2,3,7) pretzel knot"}

- 12n242/[(−2,3,7) pretzel knot](wikipedia://en/9911144)

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Torus knot"}

- (*p*, *q*)-[torus knot](wikipedia://en/1165182) - a special kind of knot that lies on the surface of an unknotted torus in **R**<sup>3</sup>

:::

### Composite {#Composite}

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Square knot (mathematics)"}

- [Square knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/26666011) - a composite knot obtained by taking the connected sum of a trefoil knot with its reflection

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Granny knot (mathematics)"}

- [Granny knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/26666182) - a composite knot obtained by taking the connected sum of two identical trefoil knots

:::

## Links {#Links}

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Unlink"}

- 02 link/[Unlink](wikipedia://en/6718898) - equivalent under ambient isotopy to finitely many disjoint circles in the plane

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hopf link"}

- 22 link/[Hopf link](wikipedia://en/1515511) - the simplest nontrivial link with more than one component; it consists of two circles linked together exactly once (L2a1)

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=list-entry name="Solomon's knot"}

- 42 link/[Solomon's knot](wikipedia://en/22830413) (a two component "link" rather than a one component "knot") - a traditional decorative motif used since ancient times (L4a1)

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Whitehead link"}

- 52 link/[Whitehead link](wikipedia://en/1966668) - two projections of the unknot: one circular loop and one figure eight-shaped loop intertwined such that they are inseparable and neither loses its form (L5a1)

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="Brunnian link"}

- [Brunnian link](wikipedia://en/2492788) - a nontrivial link that becomes trivial if any component is removed

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="Borromean rings"}

- 63 link/[Borromean rings](wikipedia://en/873118) - three topological circles which are linked and form a Brunnian link (L6a4)

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="L10a140 link"}

- [L10a140 link](wikipedia://en/31520188) - presumably the simplest non-Borromean Brunnian link

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pretzel link"}

- [Pretzel link](wikipedia://en/1286130) - a [Montesinos link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montesinos_link) with integer tangles

:::
