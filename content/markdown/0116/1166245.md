# Principle of distributivity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Principle of distributivity"}

The **principle of distributivity** states that the algebraic [distributive law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributive_law) is [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742), where both [logical conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152) and [logical disjunction](wikipedia://en/7962) are distributive over each other.

:::

## In Propositional Logic {#In_Propositional_Logic}

For any [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094) *A*, *B* and *C*, the following [equivalences](wikipedia://en/169324) hold:

$$A \land (B \lor C) \iff (A \land B) \lor (A \land C)$$

$$A \lor (B \land C) \iff (A \lor B) \land (A \lor C)$$

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof using truth tables"}

### Proof using truth tables {#Proof_using_truth_tables}

The distributive laws can be verified using truth tables.

#### Conjunction distributes over disjunction {#Conjunction_distributes_over_disjunction}

For the equivalence $A \land (B \lor C) \iff (A \land B) \lor (A \land C)$, the truth table is:

::: {#o6 .table cue=table}

| A | B | C | B ∨ C | A ∧ (B ∨ C) | A ∧ B | A ∧ C | (A ∧ B) ∨ (A ∧ C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | T | T | T | T | T |
| T | T | F | T | T | T | F | T |
| T | F | T | T | T | F | T | T |
| T | F | F | F | F | F | F | F |
| F | T | T | T | F | F | F | F |
| F | T | F | T | F | F | F | F |
| F | F | T | T | F | F | F | F |
| F | F | F | F | F | F | F | F |

:::

As seen from the table, the columns for $A \land (B \lor C)$ and $(A \land B) \lor (A \land C)$ are identical. Therefore, the equivalence is valid.

#### Disjunction distributes over conjunction {#Disjunction_distributes_over_conjunction}

For the equivalence $A \lor (B \land C) \iff (A \lor B) \land (A \lor C)$, the truth table is:

::: {#o8 .table cue=table}

| A | B | C | B ∧ C | A ∨ (B ∧ C) | A ∨ B | A ∨ C | (A ∨ B) ∧ (A ∨ C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | T | T | T | T | T |
| T | T | F | F | T | T | T | T |
| T | F | T | F | T | T | T | T |
| T | F | F | F | T | T | T | T |
| F | T | T | T | T | T | T | T |
| F | T | F | F | F | T | F | F |
| F | F | T | F | F | F | T | F |
| F | F | F | F | F | F | F | F |

:::

As seen from the table, the columns for $A \lor (B \land C)$ and $(A \lor B) \land (A \lor C)$ are identical. Therefore, the equivalence is valid.

:::

### Notes on Logic Systems {#Notes_on_Logic_Systems}

The principle of distributivity is valid in [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868), but in [quantum logic](wikipedia://en/663426) it may be both valid and invalid. The article "[Is Logic Empirical?](wikipedia://en/1166402)" discusses the case that quantum logic is the correct, empirical logic, on the grounds that the principle of distributivity is [inconsistent](wikipedia://en/75802) with a reasonable [interpretation of quantum phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretation_of_quantum_mechanics).[^o10]
