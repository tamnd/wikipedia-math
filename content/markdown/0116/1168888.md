# Zero matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zero matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), a **zero matrix** or **null matrix** is a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) all of whose entries are [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29). It also serves as the [additive identity](wikipedia://en/4178225) of the [additive group](wikipedia://en/332229) of $m \times n$ matrices, and is denoted by the symbol $O$ or $0$ followed by subscripts corresponding to the dimension of the matrix as the context sees fit.[^o2][^o3][^o4] Some examples of zero matrices are

:::

$$0_{1,1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \end{bmatrix} ,\ 0_{2,2} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} ,\ 0_{2,3} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} .\$$

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The set of $m \times n$ matrices with entries in a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) K forms a ring $K_{m,n}$. The zero matrix $0_{K_{m,n}} \,$ in $K_{m,n} \,$ is the matrix with all entries equal to $0_K \,$, where $0_K$ is the [additive identity](wikipedia://en/4178225) in K.

$$0_{K_{m,n}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0_K & 0_K & \cdots & 0_K \\ 0_K & 0_K & \cdots & 0_K \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0_K & 0_K & \cdots & 0_K \end{bmatrix}_{m \times n}$$

The zero matrix is the additive identity in $K_{m,n} \,$.[^o7] That is, for all $A \in K_{m,n} \,$ it satisfies the equation

$$0_{K_{m,n}}+A = A + 0_{K_{m,n}} = A.$$

There is exactly one zero matrix of any given dimension *m*×*n* (with entries from a given ring), so when the context is clear, one often refers to *the* zero matrix. In general, the [zero element](wikipedia://en/3476379) of a ring is unique, and is typically denoted by 0 without any [subscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscript) indicating the parent ring. Hence the examples above represent zero matrices over any ring.

The zero matrix also represents the [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) which sends all the [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) to the [zero vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_vector).[^o8] It is [idempotent](wikipedia://en/2974863), meaning that when it is multiplied by itself, the result is itself.

The zero matrix is the only matrix whose [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) is 0.

:::

## Occurrences {#Occurrences}

In [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) regression, if there is a perfect fit to the data, the [annihilator matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annihilator_matrix) is the zero matrix.
