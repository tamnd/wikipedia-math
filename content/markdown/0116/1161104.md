# Power of 10

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Power of 10"}

*Image: Visualisation 1 Trillion.svg.* Visualisation of powers of 10 from one to 1 trillion

10\<sup>10\<sup>In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **power of 10** is any of the [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [powers](wikipedia://en/99491) of the number [ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29); in other words, ten [multiplied](wikipedia://en/20845) by itself a certain number of times (when the power is a positive integer). By definition, the number one is a power (the [zeroth power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeroth_power)) of ten. The first few non-negative powers of ten are:

:::

[1](wikipedia://en/77591639), [10](wikipedia://en/208151), [100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_%28number%29), [1,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,000), [10,000](wikipedia://en/493491), [100,000](wikipedia://en/1462080), [1,000,000](wikipedia://en/1045999), [10,000,000](wikipedia://en/2847917)...

## Positive powers {#Positive_powers}

In [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) notation the *n*th power of ten is written as '1' followed by *n* zeroes. It can also be written as 10<sup>*n*</sup> or as 1E*n* in [E notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_notation). See [order of magnitude](wikipedia://en/22657) and [orders of magnitude (numbers)](wikipedia://en/260914) for named powers of ten. There are two conventions for naming positive powers of ten, beginning with 10<sup>9</sup>, called the [long and short scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_and_short_scales). Where a power of ten has different names in the two conventions, the long scale name is shown in parentheses.

The positive 10 power related to a short scale name can be determined based on its Latin name-prefix using the following formula: 10<sup>\[(prefix-number + 1) × 3\]</sup>

Examples:

- billion = 10<sup>\[(2 + 1) × 3\]</sup> = 10<sup>9</sup>
- octillion = 10<sup>\[(8 + 1) × 3\]</sup> = 10<sup>27</sup>

## Negative powers {#Negative_powers}

The sequence of powers of ten can also be extended to negative powers.

Similar to the positive powers, the negative power of 10 related to a short scale name can be determined based on its Latin name-prefix using the following formula: 10<sup>−\[(prefix-number + 1) × 3\]</sup>

Examples:

- billionth = 10<sup>−\[(2 + 1) × 3\]</sup> = 10<sup>−9</sup>
- quintillionth = 10<sup>−\[(5 + 1) × 3\]</sup> = 10<sup>−18</sup>

## Googol and googolplex {#Googol_and_googolplex}

The number googol is 10<sup>100</sup>. The term was coined by 9-year-old Milton Sirotta, nephew of American mathematician [Edward Kasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kasner). It was popularized in Kasner's 1940 book *[Mathematics and the Imagination](wikipedia://en/31052235)*, where it was used to compare and illustrate very large numbers. [Googolplex](wikipedia://en/12365), a much larger power of ten (10 to the googol power, or 10<sup>10<sup>100</sup></sup>), was also introduced in that book.

## Scientific notation {#Scientific_notation}

Scientific notation is a way of writing numbers of very large and very small sizes compactly.

A number written in scientific notation has a [significand](wikipedia://en/419250) (sometime called a mantissa) multiplied by a power of ten.

Sometimes written in the form:

*m* × 10<sup>*n*</sup>

Or more compactly as:

10<sup>*n*</sup>

This is generally used to denote powers of 10. Where *n* is positive, this indicates the number of zeros after the number, and where the *n* is negative, this indicates the number of decimal places before the number.

As an example:

10<sup>5</sup> = 100,000[^o21]

10<sup>−5</sup> = 0.00001[^o22]

The notation of *m*E*n*, known as *[E notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_notation)*, is used in computer programming, spreadsheets and databases, but is not used in scientific papers.
