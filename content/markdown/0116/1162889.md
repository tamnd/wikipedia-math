# A. A. Krishnaswami Ayyangar

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="A. A. Krishnaswami Ayyangar"}

*Image: Headshot of A. A. Krishnaswami Ayyangar.png.*

**A. A. Krishnaswami Ayyangar** (1892–1953)[^o2] was an Indian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He received his M.A. in Mathematics at the age of 18 from [Pachaiyappa's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachaiyappa's_College), and subsequently taught mathematics there. In 1918, he joined the [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) department of the [University of Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mysore) and retired from there in 1947. He was born in a [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) family.[^o3] He died in June 1953. He was the father of the Kannada poet and scholar [A. K. Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._K._Ramanujan).[^o4]

:::

## Works {#Works}

Ayyangar had a number of publications,[^o7] including an article on the [Chakravala method](wikipedia://en/4050658) where he showed how the method differed from the method of [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199). He pointed out that this point was missed by [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), who thought that the Chakravala method was only an "experimental fact" to the Indians and attributed general proofs to [Pierre de Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat) and [Joseph-Louis Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange).[^o6]

Professor [Subhash Kak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhash_Kak) of [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University), Baton Rouge first noted that Ayyangar's presentations[^o7] of the work of other Indian mathematicians was unique, and was instrumental in bringing it to the notice of the scientific community.[^o8][^o9]
