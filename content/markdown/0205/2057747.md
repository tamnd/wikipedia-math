# Substructure (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Substructure"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), an (**induced**) **substructure** or (**induced**) **subalgebra** is a [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) whose domain is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of that of a bigger structure, and whose functions and relations are restricted to the substructure's domain. Some examples of subalgebras are [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011), [submonoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submonoid), [subrings](wikipedia://en/24873145), [subfields](wikipedia://en/11634), subalgebras of [algebras over a field](wikipedia://en/191788), or induced [subgraphs](wikipedia://en/325802#Subgraphs). Shifting the point of view, the larger structure is called an **extension** or a **superstructure** of its substructure.

:::

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), the term "**submodel**" is often used as a synonym for substructure, especially when the context suggests a theory of which both structures are models.

In the presence of relations (i.e. for structures such as [ordered groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_group) or [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), whose [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156) is not functional) it may make sense to relax the conditions on a subalgebra so that the relations on a **weak substructure** (or **weak subalgebra**) are *at most* those induced from the bigger structure. Subgraphs are an example where the distinction matters, and the term "subgraph" does indeed refer to weak substructures. [Ordered groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_group), on the other hand, have the special property that every substructure of an ordered group which is itself an ordered group, is an induced substructure.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given two [structures](wikipedia://en/4055928) *A* and *B* of the same [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156) σ, *A* is said to be a **weak substructure** of *B*, or a **weak subalgebra** of *B*, if

- the domain of *A* is a subset of the domain of *B*,
- *f <sup>A</sup>* = *f <sup>B</sup>*|*<sub>A<sup>n</sup></sub>* for every *n*-ary function symbol *f* in σ, and
- *R <sup>A</sup>* $\subseteq$ *R <sup>B</sup>* $\cap$ *A<sup>n</sup>* for every *n*-ary relation symbol *R* in σ.

*A* is said to be a **substructure** of *B*, or a **subalgebra** of *B*, if *A* is a weak subalgebra of *B* and, moreover,

- *R <sup>A</sup>* = *R <sup>B</sup>* $\cap$ *A<sup>n</sup>* for every *n*-ary relation symbol *R* in σ.

If *A* is a substructure of *B*, then *B* is called a **superstructure** of *A* or, especially if *A* is an induced substructure, an **extension** of *A*.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Common\_associative\_algebras\_over\_the\_reals.svg.* Some substructure relations of common associative algebras. The upper [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) shows subalgebras and is in [Galois correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_correspondence) with the lattice of groups generated by basis elements.

In the language consisting of the [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948) + and ×, [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) \<, and constants 0 and 1, the structure (**Q**, +, ×, \<, 0, 1) is a substructure of (**R**, +, ×, \<, 0, 1). More generally, the substructures of an [ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430) (or just a [field](wikipedia://en/10603)) are precisely its subfields. Similarly, in the language (×, <sup>−1</sup>, 1) of groups, the substructures of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) are its [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011). In the language (×, 1) of [monoids](wikipedia://en/19652), however, the substructures of a group are its [submonoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submonoid). They need not be groups; and even if they are groups, they need not be subgroups.

[Subrings](wikipedia://en/24873145) are the substructures of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), and [subalgebras](wikipedia://en/45239) are the substructures of [algebras over a field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebras_over_a_field).

In the case of [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) (in the signature consisting of one binary relation), [subgraphs](wikipedia://en/325802#Subgraphs), and its weak substructures are precisely its subgraphs.

## As subobjects {#As_subobjects}

For every signature σ, induced substructures of σ-structures are the [subobjects](wikipedia://en/540476) in the [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001) of σ-structures and [strong homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/4055928#Homomorphisms) (and also in the [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001) of σ-structures and σ-[embeddings](wikipedia://en/4055928#Homomorphisms)). Weak substructures of σ-structures are the [subobjects](wikipedia://en/540476) in the [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001) of σ-structures and [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/4055928#Homomorphisms) in the ordinary sense.

## Submodel {#Submodel}

In model theory, given a structure *M* which is a model of a theory *T*, a **submodel** of *M* in a narrower sense is a substructure of *M* which is also a model of *T*. For example, if *T* is the theory of abelian groups in the signature (+, 0), then the submodels of the group of integers (**Z**, +, 0) are the substructures which are also abelian groups. Thus the natural numbers (**N**, +, 0) form a substructure of (**Z**, +, 0) which is not a submodel, while the even numbers (2**Z**, +, 0) form a submodel.

Other examples:

1. The [algebraic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_numbers) form a submodel of the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) in the theory of [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018).
1. The [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers) form a submodel of the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) in the theory of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603).
1. Every [elementary substructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_substructure) of a model of a theory *T* also satisfies *T*; hence it is a submodel.

In the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of models of a theory and [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) between them, the submodels of a model are its [subobjects](wikipedia://en/540476).
