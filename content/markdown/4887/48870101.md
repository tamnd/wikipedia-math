# Lowell E. Jones

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lowell E. Jones"}

**Lowell Edwin Jones** (born 1945) is an American professor of mathematics at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).[^o3] Jones' primary fields of interest are topology and geometry. Jones is most well known for his collaboration with [F. Thomas Farrell](wikipedia://en/46890166) on the [Farrell-Jones conjecture](wikipedia://en/20643804).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Jones received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1970 under the guidance of [Wu-Chung Hsiang](wikipedia://en/50980728). Jones' dissertation topic, assigned by Hsiang,[^o6] concerned the fixed-point theorem of [Paul Althaus Smith](wikipedia://en/4476957).

Jones joined Stony Brook University in 1975.

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

When Farrell and Jones first started collaborating they gave the very first example of an [Anosov diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/2320078) on a manifold which was not [infranil](wikipedia://en/490861#I).[^o8] Later, Jones and Farrell, also a student of Hsiang, caused a paradigm shift in higher dimensional topology when they applied ideas from differential geometry, and dynamics to questions such as the [Borel conjecture](wikipedia://en/7181855). The Farrell-Jones conjecture[^o9] implies the Borel Conjecture for manifolds of dimension greater than four.

Jones, and Farrell published about fifty papers during their 25-year collaboration.[^o10]

Jones was invited to speak at the 1990 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto).[^o11]
