# Amitsur–Levitzki theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amitsur–Levitzki theorem"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **Amitsur–Levitzki theorem** states that the algebra of *n* × *n* [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) satisfies a certain identity of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) 2*n*. It was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by (txt). In particular [matrix rings](wikipedia://en/487541) are [polynomial identity rings](wikipedia://en/25386269) such that the smallest identity they satisfy has degree exactly 2*n*.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The **standard polynomial** of degree *n* is

$$S_n(x_1,\dots,x_n) = \sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)x_{\sigma(1)} \cdots x_{\sigma(n)}$$

in non-commuting variables *x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>, where the sum is taken over all *n*! elements of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>*n*</sub>.

The Amitsur–Levitzki theorem states that for *n* × *n* matrices *A*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *A*<sub>2*n*</sub> whose entries are taken from a commutative ring then

$$S_{2n}(A_1,\dots,A_{2n}) = 0.$$

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

(txt) gave the first proof.

(Kostant 1958) deduced the Amitsur–Levitzki theorem from the [Koszul–Samelson theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koszul–Samelson_theorem) about primitive cohomology of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).

(Swan 1963) and (Swan 1969) gave a simple [combinatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial) proof as follows. By linearity it is enough to prove the theorem when each matrix has only one nonzero entry, which is 1. In this case each matrix can be encoded as a directed edge of a [graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) with *n* vertices. So all matrices together give a graph on *n* vertices with 2*n* directed edges. The identity holds provided that for any two vertices *A* and *B* of the graph, the number of odd [Eulerian paths](wikipedia://en/333219) from *A* to *B* is the same as the number of even ones. (Here a path is called odd or even depending on whether its edges taken in order give an [odd or even permutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_and_even_permutations) of the 2*n* edges.) Swan showed that this was the case provided the number of edges in the graph is at least 2*n*, thus proving the Amitsur–Levitzki theorem.

(Razmyslov 1974) gave a proof related to the [Cayley–Hamilton theorem](wikipedia://en/173547).

(Rosset 1976) gave a short proof using the [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) 2*n*.

(Procesi 2015) gave another proof, showing that the Amitsur–Levitzki theorem is the Cayley–Hamilton identity for the generic Grassman matrix. Claudio Procesi's proof used a similar technique to Rosset but is presented in a way that could make the theorem more easily transferrable to other problems. A principal property used in this proof is antisymmetry to convert a multilinear expression to an antisymmetric one.

:::
