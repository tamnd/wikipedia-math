# Lulu smoothing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lulu smoothing"}

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), **Lulu [smoothing](wikipedia://en/4155662)** is a [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear) mathematical technique for removing impulsive [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_%28signal_processing%29) from a data sequence such as a [time series](wikipedia://en/406624). It is a nonlinear equivalent to taking a [moving average](wikipedia://en/990809) (or other smoothing technique) of a time series, and is similar to other [nonlinear smoothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_smoothing) techniques, such as Tukey or [median smoothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_filter).[^o14]

*Image: LUSmoother width=1.png.* LU smoother of width 1 applied to a noisy sequence

LULU smoothers are compared in detail to median smoothers by Jankowitz and found to be superior in some aspects, particularly in mathematical properties like [idempotence](wikipedia://en/14972).[^o16]

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Lulu operators have a number of attractive mathematical properties, among them [idempotence](wikipedia://en/14972) – meaning that repeated application of the operator yields the same result as a single application – and co-idempotence. An interpretation of idempotence is that: 'Idempotence means that there is no “noise” left in the smoothed data and co-idempotence means that there is no “signal” left in the residual.'[^o15]

When studying smoothers there are four properties that are useful to optimize:[^o13]

1. Effectiveness
1. Consistency
1. Stability
1. Efficiency

The operators can also be used to decompose a signal into various subcomponents similar to wavelet or Fourier decomposition.[^o17]

:::

## History {#History}

Lulu smoothers were discovered by C. H. Rohwer and have been studied for the last 30 years.[^o11][^o12] Their exact and asymptotic distributions have been derived.[^o15]

## Operation {#Operation}

Applying a Lulu smoother consists of repeated applications of the min and max operators over a given subinterval of the data. As with other smoothers, a width or interval must be specified. The Lulu smoothers are composed of repeated applications of the *L* (lower) and *U* (Upper) operators, which are defined as follows:

### L operator {#L_operator}

For an L operator of width *n* over an infinite sequence of *x*s (..., *x*<sub>*j*</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*+1</sub>,...), the operation on *x*<sub>*j*</sub> is calculated as follows:

1. Firstly we create (*n* + 1) mini-sequences of length (*n* + 1) each. Each of these mini-sequences contains the element *x*<sub>*j*</sub>. For example, for width 1, we create 2 mini-sequences of length 2 each. For width 1 these mini sequences are (*x*<sub>*j*−1</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*</sub>) and (*x*<sub>*j*</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*+1</sub>). For width 2, the mini-sequences are (*x*<sub>*j*−2</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*−1</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*</sub>), (*x*<sub>*j*−1</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*+1</sub>) and (*x*<sub>*j*</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*+1</sub>, *x*<sub>*j*+2</sub>). For width 2, we refer to these mini-sequences as seq<sub>−1</sub>, seq<sub>0</sub> and seq<sub>\+1</sub>
1. Then we take the minimum of each of the mini sequences. Again for width 2 this gives: (Min(seq<sub>−1</sub>), Min(seq<sub>0</sub>), Min(seq<sub>\+1</sub>)). This gives us (*n* + 1) numbers for each point.
1. Lastly we take the maximum of (the minimums of the mini sequences), or Max(Min(seq<sub>−1</sub>), Min(seq<sub>0</sub>), Min(seq<sub>\+1</sub>)) and this becomes *L*(*x*<sub>*j*</sub>)

Thus for width 2, the *L* operator is:

*L*(*x*<sub>*j*</sub>) = Max(Min(seq<sub>−1</sub>), Min(seq<sub>0</sub>), Min(seq<sub>\+1</sub>))

### U Operator {#U_Operator}

This is identical to the L operator, except that the order of Min and Max is reversed, i.e. for width 2:

*U*(*x*<sub>*j*</sub>) = Min(Max(seq<sub>−1</sub>), Max(seq<sub>0</sub>), Max(seq<sub>\+1</sub>))
