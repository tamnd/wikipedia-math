# Dynkin index

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dynkin index"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Dynkin index** $I({\lambda})$ of finite-dimensional [highest-weight representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest-weight_representation) of a [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28topology%29) [simple](wikipedia://en/17944#simple_and_semi-simple) [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak g$ relates their trace forms via

:::

$$\frac{\text{Tr}_{V_\lambda}}{\text{Tr}_{V_\mu}}= \frac{I(\lambda)}{I(\mu)}.$$

In the particular case where $\lambda$ is the [highest root](wikipedia://en/277087#properties_of_the_irreducible_root_systems), so that $V_\lambda$ is the [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_group), the Dynkin index $I(\lambda)$ is equal to the [dual Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Coxeter_number).

The notation $\text{Tr}_V$ is the [trace form](wikipedia://en/982386#trace_forms) on the representation $\rho: \mathfrak{g} \rightarrow \text{End}(V)$. By [Schur's lemma](wikipedia://en/521696), since the trace forms are all invariant forms, they are related by constants, so the index is well-defined.

Since the trace forms are [bilinear forms](wikipedia://en/449477), we can take traces to obtain

$$I(\lambda)=\frac{\dim V_\lambda}{2\dim\mathfrak g}(\lambda, \lambda +2\rho)$$

where the [Weyl vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_vector)

$$\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in \Delta^+} \alpha$$

is equal to half of the sum of all the [positive roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_root) of $\mathfrak g$. The expression $(\lambda, \lambda +2\rho)$ is the value of the quadratic [Casimir](wikipedia://en/513672) in the representation $V_\lambda$.
