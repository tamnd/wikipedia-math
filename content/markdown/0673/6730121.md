# Presentation of a monoid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Presentation of a monoid"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **presentation of a monoid** (or a **presentation of a semigroup**) is a description of a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) (or a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799)) in terms of a set $\Sigma$ of generators and a set of relations on the [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) $\Sigma^{\ast}$ (or the free semigroup $\Sigma^{+}$) generated by $\Sigma$. The monoid is then presented as the [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_monoid) of the free monoid (or the free semigroup) by these relations. This is an analogue of a [group presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_presentation) in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).

:::

As a mathematical structure, a monoid presentation is identical to a [string rewriting system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_rewriting_system) (also known as a semi-Thue system). Every monoid may be presented by a semi-Thue system (possibly over an infinite alphabet).[^o2]

A *presentation* should not be confused with a *[representation](wikipedia://en/19378200)*.

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The relations are given as a (finite) [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) $R$ on $\Sigma^{\ast}$. To form the quotient monoid, these relations are extended to [monoid congruences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_congruence) as follows:

First, one takes the symmetric closure $R \cup R^{-1}$ of $R$. This is then extended to a symmetric relation $E \subset \Sigma^{\ast} \times \Sigma^{\ast}$ by defining $x \sim_{E} y$ if and only if $x$ = $sut$ and $y$ = $svt$ for some strings $u, v, s, t \in \Sigma^{\ast}$ with $(u,v) \in R \cup R^{-1}$. Finally, one takes the reflexive and transitive closure of $E$, which then is a monoid congruence.

In the typical situation, the relation $R$ is simply given as a set of equations, so that $R=\{u_1=v_1,\ldots,u_n=v_n\}$. Thus, for example,

$$\langle p,q\,\vert\; pq=1\rangle$$

is the equational presentation for the [bicyclic monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicyclic_monoid), and

$$\langle a,b \,\vert\; aba=baa, bba=bab\rangle$$

is the [plactic monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plactic_monoid) of degree 2 (it has infinite order). Elements of this plactic monoid may be written as $a^ib^j(ba)^k$ for integers *i*, *j*, *k*, as the relations show that *ba* commutes with both *a* and *b*.

:::

## Inverse monoids and semigroups {#Inverse_monoids_and_semigroups}

Presentations of inverse monoids and semigroups can be defined in a similar way using a pair

$$(X;T)$$

where

$$(X\cup X^{-1})^*$$

is the [free monoid with involution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_monoid_with_involution) on $X$, and

$$T\subseteq (X\cup X^{-1})^*\times (X\cup X^{-1})^*$$

is a [binary](wikipedia://en/3943) relation between words. We denote by $T^{\mathrm{e}}$ (respectively $T^\mathrm{c}$) the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) (respectively, the [congruence](wikipedia://en/48167)) generated by *T*.

We use this pair of objects to define an inverse monoid

$$\mathrm{Inv}^1 \langle X | T\rangle.$$

Let $\rho_X$ be the [Wagner congruence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_congruence) on $X$, we define the inverse monoid

$$\mathrm{Inv}^1 \langle X | T\rangle$$

*presented* by $(X;T)$ as

$$\mathrm{Inv}^1 \langle X | T\rangle=(X\cup X^{-1})^*/(T\cup\rho_X)^{\mathrm{c}}.$$

In the previous discussion, if we replace everywhere $({X\cup X^{-1}})^*$ with $({X\cup X^{-1}})^+$ we obtain a **presentation (for an inverse semigroup)** $(X;T)$ and an inverse semigroup $\mathrm{Inv}\langle X | T\rangle$ **presented** by $(X;T)$.

A trivial but important example is the **free inverse monoid** (or **free inverse semigroup**) on $X$, that is usually denoted by $\mathrm{FIM}(X)$ (respectively $\mathrm{FIS}(X)$) and is defined by

$$\mathrm{FIM}(X)=\mathrm{Inv}^1 \langle X | \varnothing\rangle=({X\cup X^{-1}})^*/\rho_X,$$

or

$$\mathrm{FIS}(X)=\mathrm{Inv} \langle X | \varnothing\rangle=({X\cup X^{-1}})^+/\rho_X.$$
