# Concordant pair

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Concordant pair"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **concordant pair** is a pair of observations, each on two variables, (**X**<sub>*1*</sub>,**Y**<sub>*1*</sub>) and (**X**<sub>*2*</sub>,**Y**<sub>*2*</sub>), having the property that

:::

$$\operatorname{sgn} (X_2 - X_1)\ = \operatorname{sgn} (Y_2 - Y_1),$$

where "sgn" refers to whether a number is positive, zero, or negative (its sign). Specifically, the [signum function](wikipedia://en/505449), often represented as **sgn**, is defined as:

$$\operatorname{sgn} x = \begin{cases} -1, & x < 0 \\ 0 , & x = 0 \\ 1 , & x > 0 \end{cases}$$

That is, in a concordant pair, both elements of one pair are either greater than, equal to, or less than the corresponding elements of the other pair.

In contrast, a **discordant pair** is a pair of two-variable observations such that

$$\operatorname{sgn} (X_2 - X_1)\ = - \operatorname{sgn} (Y_2 - Y_1).$$

That is, if one pair contains a higher value of *X* then the other pair contains a higher value of *Y*.

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

The [Kendall tau distance](wikipedia://en/7276069) between two series is the total number of discordant pairs. The [Kendall tau rank correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_tau_rank_correlation_coefficient), which measures how closely related two series of numbers are, is proportional to the difference between the number of concordant pairs and the number of discordant pairs. An estimate of [Goodman and Kruskal's gamma](wikipedia://en/8925986), another measure of [rank correlation](wikipedia://en/3316627), is given by the ratio of the difference to the sum of the numbers of concordant and discordant pairs. [Somers' *D*](wikipedia://en/48804237) is another similar but asymmetric measure given by the ratio of the difference in the number of concordant and discordant pairs to the number of pairs with unequal values for one of the two variables.

:::
