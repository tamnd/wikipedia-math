# Julius Richard Büchi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Julius Richard Büchi"}

**Julius Richard Büchi** (1924–1984) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people) [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

He received his Dr. sc. nat. in 1950 at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) under the supervision of [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580) and [Ferdinand Gonseth](wikipedia://en/54012910). Shortly afterwards he went to [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in [Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette,_Indiana), [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana). He and his first student [Lawrence Landweber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Landweber) had a major influence on the development of [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).

Together with his friend [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574), a student of [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580) as well, Büchi published numerous celebrated works. He invented what is now known as the [Büchi automaton](wikipedia://en/344912), a [finite-state machine](wikipedia://en/10931) accepting certain sets of infinite sequences of characters known as [omega-regular languages](wikipedia://en/3262889). The "*n* squares' problem", known also as [Büchi's problem](wikipedia://en/24471842), is an open problem from [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), closely related to [Hilbert's tenth problem](wikipedia://en/101851).

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- *Collected Works of J. Richard Büchi*. Edited by Saunders Mac Lane and Dirk Siefkes. Springer, New York 1990.
