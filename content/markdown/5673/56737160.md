# Józef Schreier

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Józef Schreier"}

**Józef Schreier** (; 18 February 1909, [Drohobycz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drohobycz), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) – April 1943, Drohobycz, [Occupied Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Poland)) was a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mathematicians) of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) origin, known for his work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). He was a member of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328) and a victim of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust).

:::

Józef Schreier was born on 18 February 1909 in Drohobycz. His father was a rabbi and doctor of philosophy. His cousin was the musician [Alfred Schreyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Schreyer). From 1927-31 he studied at the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv) in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów).

In his first published paper, he defined what later came to be known as [Schreier sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schreier_set) in order to show that not all [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) possess the weak Banach-Saks property, disproving a conjecture of [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) and [Stanisław Saks](wikipedia://en/362649). Schreier sets were later discovered independently by researchers in [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175).[^o2][^o3]

Schreier completed his master's degree *On tournament elimination systems* in 1932 under the direction of [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735). Schreier correctly conjectured that to determine the second largest number in an unordered list requires at least $n + \lceil \log_2 n \rceil -2$ comparisons.[^o4] In 1934, he completed his doctorate, *On finite base in topological groups* under Banach.[^o5]

In 1932 he married Zofia Rosenblatt. Schreier often played [blindfold chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindfold_chess).

He was a friend of [Stanisław Ulam](wikipedia://en/41531) and co-authored eight papers with him.[^o6] They were the only two undergraduates who attended the meetings at the [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651) in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów). (Schreier contributed ten questions to the [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115).)[^o7] Together they proved the [Baire–Schreier–Ulam theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baire–Schreier–Ulam_theorem) and [Schreier–Ulam theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schreier–Ulam_theorem).

According to Ulam,

With the outbreak of World War II, Eastern Poland including Drohobycz was occupied by the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) in accordance with the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov–Ribbentrop_Pact). After [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), this territory was invaded by [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany). The Jews of Drohobycz were confined to the [Drohobycz Ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drohobycz_Ghetto). In April 1943, the Germans discovered—or were informed of—an underground bunker in which Schreier was hiding with other Jews. It took three days for them to force their way in. Schreier committed suicide by [cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanide) rather than be captured. Of a prewar Jewish population of 10,000 in Drohobycz, approximately 400 survived the war.[^o9][^o10][^o11] Schreier's wife was one of them and later moved to Israel, where she remarried. Schreier's cousin Gina Schreier survived the war in Poland and later emigrated to Brazil with her husband, Bronisław Hoenig. After Ulam’s death, Ulam’s wife passed her the articles they had primarily written together.
