# Michael Frame

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Frame"}

**Michael Frame** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and retired [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) professor.[^o2] He is a co-author, along with Amelia Urry, of *Fractal Worlds: Grown, Built, and Imagined*.[^o3] At [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), he was a colleague of [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999) and helped Mandelbrot develop a curriculum within the mathematics department.[^o2]

:::

## Early years and education {#Early_years_and_education}

Michael Frame was born in 1951 and grew up in [St. Albans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Albans,_West_Virginia), [West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia).[^o5] After leaving his [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) major because the lab requirement was "something in biophysics with killing frogs,"[^o5] Frame, a vegetarian, received a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College) as a [first-generation college student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-generation_college_students_in_the_United_States).[^o5] In 1978, he completed a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University).

## Teaching career {#Teaching_career}

Michael Frame came to work at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) at the invitation of his colleague [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999). At Yale, Frame called himself "the stupidest guy in the department...the dimmest bulb in the pack here," and focused on his teaching contributions.[^o7] He received the McCredie Prize for best use of technology in teaching at Yale College, the Dylan Hixon '88 Prize for teaching excellence in the natural sciences, and the Yale [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) chapter's DeVane medal for undergraduate teaching.[^o2]

## Work with Mandelbrot {#Work_with_Mandelbrot}

Benoit Mandelbrot includes a section on Michael Frame in his posthumously published autobiography *The Fractalist: Memoir of a Scientific Maverick.*[^o9] In the section, called "Michael Frame, Friend and Colleague," he calls Frame an "indispensable" professor.[^o9]

In 1997, Mandelbrot and Frame held a meeting of teachers of [fractal geometry](wikipedia://en/10913).[^o9] According to Mandelbrot, as far as he knew, this was the "first scientific meeting totally dedicated to the teaching of fractals.[^o9] This eventually culminated in the 2002 publication of the book *Fractals, Graphics, and Mathematics Education*, which was co-authored by Frame and Mandelbrot.[^o9][^o10]
