# List of mathematical discoveries made with artificial intelligence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of mathematical discoveries made with artificial intelligence"}

Beginning in the mid-2020s, [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model), including [reasoning models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning_model), have made increasing progress in generating mathematical proofs at research level.[^o2][^o3][^o4] Most such proofs have been conducted using models from [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI) and [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic). This is a list of notable discoveries conducted primarily by such models.

:::

The degree of human intervention in AI-generated mathematical proofs may vary and remain unclear. Subjects responsible for publishing such proofs, including individuals and AI companies such as [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI), often fail to disclose used [prompts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_engineering), structure of pipelines used as a model workflow, or specific models used for generating proofs. The publication of such proofs may not satisfy [standards of science articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method). It may not have passed independent review or [peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review), and instead be published on corporate websites, personal websites, or social media.

## 2023 {#2023}

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Google DeepMind"}

- In December 2023, researchers at [Google DeepMind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_DeepMind) used a pre-trained LLM to discover a new lower bound for the [cap set problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_set_problem) and new algorithms for the [bin packing problem](wikipedia://en/287015).[^o7] The results for the cap set problem were verified by mathematician [Jordan Ellenberg](wikipedia://en/19765796).[^o8]

:::

## 2024 {#2024}

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Meta"}

- In October 2024, researchers at [Meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms) and the [Ecole des Ponts et Chaussees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecole_des_Ponts_et_Chaussees) found pre-trained [Seq2seq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seq2seq) [transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_%28deep_learning%29) could discover new global [Lyapunov functions](wikipedia://en/352267) at a higher rate than non-AI systems.[^o11]

:::

## 2025 {#2025}

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="AlphaEvolve"}

- In May 2025, Google DeepMind's [AlphaEvolve](wikipedia://en/79970630) agent discovered an optimization for the [Strassen algorithm](wikipedia://en/771965), reducing the [scalar multiplications](wikipedia://en/244373) needed for 4x4 matrices from 49 to 48. It also improved the lower bounds for 11 [kissing numbers](wikipedia://en/408555).[^o14]

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Prime number theorem"}

- In September 2025, Math Inc.'s Gauss agent formalized the strong [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) in [Lean](wikipedia://en/62889984), producing ~25,000 lines of code and formally proving >1,000 theorems.[^o16]

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Reinforcement learning"}

- In November 2025, researchers at Chinese universities constructed a [reinforcement learning](wikipedia://en/66294) algorithm which improved the lower and upper bounds for 15 kissing numbers.[^o18]

:::

## 2026 {#2026}

### Q1 {#Q1}

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Google DeepMind"}

- In February 2026, [Google DeepMind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_DeepMind) used its [Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_%28language_model%29) model to survey the 700 open [Erdős problems](wikipedia://en/336815), which proved four of them and found nine unknown published solutions.[^o22]

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Maryna Viazovska"}

- In March 2026, Math Inc.'s Gauss agent formalized [Maryna Viazovska](wikipedia://en/50004967)'s proofs of [sphere packing](wikipedia://en/368621) in 8 and 24 dimensions, in ~200 thousand lines of Lean code.[^o24][^o25]

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="AlphaEvolve"}

- In March 2026, Google DeepMind's [AlphaEvolve](wikipedia://en/79970630) agent improved lower bounds for nine classical [Ramsey numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_numbers).[^o27]

:::

### Q2 {#Q2}

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="OEIS"}

- In May 2026, Google DeepMind used an AlphaEvolve-like model, adapted for mathematical proofs, to prove nine Erdős problems and 44 out of 492 formalized [OEIS](wikipedia://en/500004) conjectures.[^o30] Epoch AI then used various LLMs to prove/disprove 147 of these 492 OEIS conjectures.[^o31]

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=list-entry name="unit distance problem"}

- In May 2026, OpenAI announced an internal model produced a disproof of the [unit distance problem](wikipedia://en/7158665),[^o33] and several mathematicians collaborated to produce a verification and exposition of the result.[^o34]

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=list-entry name="GPT-5.5"}

- In May 2026, Max Grinzstajn prompted [GPT-5.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-5.5) Pro to discover a counterexample for [Borsuk's conjecture](wikipedia://en/7765817) in dimension $n = 63$, the smallest dimension for which a counterexample is known.[^o36][^o37]

:::

### Navier–Stokes equations {#Navier–Stokes_equations}

In September 2026, OpenAI claimed an internal model more powerful than [GPT-6 Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-6_Astra) had solved the [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](wikipedia://en/6013654) problem, one of the [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234). The claim has not been verified by the wider mathematical community. The credit for the discovery is disputed.[^o86][^o87]
