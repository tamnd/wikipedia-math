# Beevers–Lipson strip

*Image: Beevers-Lipson strips at the Museum of the History of Science, Oxford.JPG.* Beevers–Lipson strips at the [Museum of the History of Science, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_History_of_Science,_Oxford),[^o6] part of the *Crystals* special exhibition in 2014. These were used by the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize) winner [Dorothy Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hodgkin) in Oxford.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beevers–Lipson strip"}

**Beevers–Lipson strips** were a computational aid for early [crystallographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) in calculating [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247) to determine the structure of [crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal) from crystallographic data,[^o2] enabling the creation of models for complex molecules.[^o3] They were used from the 1930s[^o6] until computers with enough power became generally available in the 1960s.

:::

The technique was developed by [C. Arnold Beevers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Arnold_Beevers) (1908–2001),[^o4] reader in [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), and [Henry Lipson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lipson) CBE [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (1910–1991), Professor of [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Manchester Institute of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology). The approach converted the sizable calculations of multi-dimensional Fourier summations needed in crystallography analysis into sums of more manageable one-dimensional values.[^o3] The folded card strips with numbers were typically stored in two wooden boxes,[^o5] one for [sines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine)[^o6] and one for [cosines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine). Previously it was necessary to consult sine/cosine tables, a time-consuming process. The approach was used by the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize) winner [Dorothy Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hodgkin) OM FRS (1910–1994).[^o6][^o7] The technique was used in computer programs until the invention of the [fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512).[^o8][^o9]
