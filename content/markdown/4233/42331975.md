# Crenel function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crenel function"}

In mathematics, the **[crenel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crenel) [function](wikipedia://en/185427)** is a [periodic](wikipedia://en/76848) [discontinuous](wikipedia://en/6122) function *P*(*x*) defined as [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29) for *x* belonging to a given interval and [0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29) outside of it. It can be presented as a difference between two [Heaviside step functions](wikipedia://en/87299) of amplitude 1.[^o3] It is used in [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) to account for irregularities in the occupation of atomic sites by given atoms in solids, such as periodic domain structures, where some regions are enriched and some are depleted with certain atoms.[^o4]

:::

Mathematically,

$$P(x)=\begin{cases} 1, & x \in [-\Delta/2, \Delta/2], \\ 0, & x \notin [-\Delta/2, \Delta/2], \end{cases}$$

The coefficients of its [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) are

$$P_k(\Delta,x) = \frac{\exp(2\pi i\,kx)\sin(\pi k\Delta)}{\pi k} = \Delta\cdot\mathrm{sinc}(\pi k\Delta)\cdot\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\,kx}.$$

with the [Sinc function](wikipedia://en/610583).
