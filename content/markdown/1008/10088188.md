# Order of integration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Order of integration"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **order of integration**, denoted *I*(*d*), of a [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) is a [summary statistic](wikipedia://en/27587), which reports the minimum number of [differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differencing) required to obtain a [covariance-stationary](wikipedia://en/329898#Weak_or_wide-sense_stationarity) series (i.e., a time series whose [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) and [autocovariance](wikipedia://en/1202314) remain constant over time).

:::

The order of integration is a key concept in time series analysis, particularly when dealing with non-stationary data that exhibits trends or other forms of non-stationarity.

## Integration of order *d* {#Integration_of_order_d}

A [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) is integrated of order *d* if

$$(1-L)^d X_t \$$

is a [stationary process](wikipedia://en/329898), where $L$ is the [lag operator](wikipedia://en/1481533) and $1-L$ is the first difference, i.e.

$$(1-L) X_t = X_t - X_{t-1} = \Delta X.$$

In other words, a process is integrated to order *d* if taking repeated differences *d* times yields a stationary process.

In particular, if a series is integrated of order 0, then $(1-L)^0 X_t = X_t$ is stationary.

## Constructing an integrated series {#Constructing_an_integrated_series}

An *I*(*d*) process can be constructed by summing an *I*(*d* − 1) process:

- Suppose $X_t$ is *I*(*d* − 1)
- Now construct a series $Z_t = \sum_{k=0}^t X_k$
- Show that *Z* is *I*(*d*) by observing its first-differences are *I*(*d* − 1):

$$\Delta Z_t = X_t,$$

where

$$X_t \sim I(d-1). \,$$
