::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="6-cube"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **6-cube** is a six-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) with 64 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), 192 [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), 240 square [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), 160 cubic [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29), 60 [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) [4-faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-face), and 12 [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215) [5-faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-face).

:::

It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {4,3<sup>4</sup>}, being composed of 3 [5-cubes](wikipedia://en/7184215) around each 4-face. It can be called a **hexeract**, a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) of [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) (the *4-cube*) with *hex* for six (dimensions) in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language). It can also be called a regular **dodeca-6-tope** or **dodecapeton**, being a [6-dimensional polytope](wikipedia://en/26021427) constructed from 12 regular [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647). Acronym: **ax**

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

It is a part of an infinite family of polytopes, called [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/39783). The [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope) of a 6-cube can be called a [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518), and is a part of the infinite family of [cross-polytopes](wikipedia://en/716401). It is composed of various [5-cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-cubes), at perpendicular angles on the u-axis, forming coordinates (x,y,z,w,v,u).[^o11][^o12]

Applying an *[alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117)* operation, deleting alternating vertices of the 6-cube, creates another [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392), called a [6-demicube](wikipedia://en/10275945), (part of an infinite family called [demihypercubes](wikipedia://en/6833695)), which has 12 [5-demicube](wikipedia://en/7186232) and 32 [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex) facets.

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

[Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of a 6-cube centered at the origin and edge length 2 are

(±1,±1,±1,±1,±1,±1)

while the interior of the same consists of all points (x<sub>0</sub>, x<sub>1</sub>, x<sub>2</sub>, x<sub>3</sub>, x<sub>4</sub>, x<sub>5</sub>) with −1 \< x<sub>i</sub> \< 1.

## Construction {#Construction}

There are three [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) associated with the 6-cube, one [regular](wikipedia://en/518693), with the C<sub>6</sub> or \[4,3,3,3,3\] [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004), and a half symmetry (D<sub>6</sub>) or \[3<sup>3,1,1</sup>\] Coxeter group. The lowest symmetry construction is based on [hyperrectangles](wikipedia://en/1688550) or [proprisms](wikipedia://en/27882423), [cartesian products](wikipedia://en/24104095) of lower dimensional hypercubes.

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

The 64 vertices of a 6-cube also represent a regular skew 4-polytope {4,3,4 | 4}. Its net can be seen as a 4×4×4 matrix of 64 cubes, a periodic subset of the [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318), {4,3,4}, in 3-dimensions. It has 192 edges, and 192 square faces. Opposite faces fold together into a 4-cycle. Each fold direction adds 1 dimension, raising it into 6-space.

The *6-cube* is 6th in a series of [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/39783):

This polytope is one of 63 [uniform 6-polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821728) generated from the B<sub>6</sub> [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane), including also the regular [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518).
