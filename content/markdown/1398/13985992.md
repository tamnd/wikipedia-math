# Jacobi–Anger expansion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobi–Anger expansion"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Jacobi–Anger expansion** (or **Jacobi–Anger identity**) is an expansion of exponentials of [trigonometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) in the basis of their harmonics. It is useful in physics (for example, to [convert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_wave_expansion) between [plane waves](wikipedia://en/41559) and [cylindrical waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_wave)), and in [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) (to describe [FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation) signals). This identity is named after the 19th-century mathematicians [Carl Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340) and [Carl Theodor Anger](wikipedia://en/20155404).

:::

The most general identity is given by:[^o2][^o3]

$$e^{i z \cos \theta} \equiv \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} i^n\, J_n(z)\, e^{i n \theta},$$

where $J_n(z)$ is the $n$-th [Bessel function of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_function_of_the_first_kind) and $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358), $i^2=-1.$ Substituting $\theta$ by $\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}$, we also get:

$$e^{i z \sin \theta} \equiv \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} J_n(z)\, e^{i n \theta}.$$

Using the relation $J_{-n}(z) = (-1)^n\, J_{n}(z),$ valid for integer $n$, the expansion becomes:[^o2][^o3]

$$e^{i z \cos \theta} \equiv J_0(z)\, +\, 2\, \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\, i^n\, J_n(z)\, \cos\, (n \theta).$$

## Real-valued expressions {#Real-valued_expressions}

The following real-valued variations are often useful as well:[^o5]

$$\begin{aligned} \cos(z \cos \theta) &\equiv J_0(z)+2 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^n J_{2n}(z) \cos(2n \theta), \\ \sin(z \cos \theta) &\equiv -2 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^n J_{2n-1}(z) \cos\left[\left(2n-1\right) \theta\right], \\ \cos(z \sin \theta) &\equiv J_0(z)+2 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} J_{2n}(z) \cos(2n \theta), \\ \sin(z \sin \theta) &\equiv 2 \sum_{ n=1 }^{\infty} J_{2n-1}(z) \sin\left[\left(2n-1\right) \theta\right]. \end{aligned}$$
