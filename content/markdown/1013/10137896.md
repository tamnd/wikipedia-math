# Tensor product of quadratic forms

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tensor product of quadratic forms"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **[tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478)** is most easily understood when one views the quadratic forms as *[quadratic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_space)*.[^o2] If *R* is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) where 2 is [invertible](wikipedia://en/289666), and if $(V_1, q_1)$ and $(V_2,q_2)$ are two quadratic spaces over *R*, then their tensor product $(V_1 \otimes V_2, q_1 \otimes q_2)$ is the quadratic space whose underlying *R*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) is the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836) $V_1 \otimes V_2$ of *R*-modules and whose quadratic form is the quadratic form associated to the tensor product of the [bilinear forms](wikipedia://en/449477) associated to $q_1$ and $q_2$.

:::

In particular, the form $q_1 \otimes q_2$ satisfies

$$(q_1\otimes q_2)(v_1 \otimes v_2) = q_1(v_1) q_2(v_2) \quad \forall v_1 \in V_1,\ v_2 \in V_2$$

(which does uniquely characterize it however). It follows from this that if the quadratic forms are diagonalizable (which is always possible if 2 is invertible in *R*), i.e.,

$$q_1 \cong \langle a_1, ... , a_n \rangle$$

$$q_2 \cong \langle b_1, ... , b_m \rangle$$

then the tensor product has diagonalization

$$q_1 \otimes q_2 \cong \langle a_1b_1, a_1b_2, ... a_1b_m, a_2b_1, ... , a_2b_m , ... , a_nb_1, ... a_nb_m \rangle.$$
