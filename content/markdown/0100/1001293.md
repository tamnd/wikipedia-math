# Irreducibility (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Irreducibility"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the concept of **irreducibility** is used in several ways.

:::

- A [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) may be an [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) if it cannot be factored over that field.
- In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), **irreducible** can be an abbreviation for [irreducible element](wikipedia://en/60693) of an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462); for example an [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725).
- In [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), an **[irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982)** is a nontrivial [representation](wikipedia://en/19378200) with no nontrivial proper subrepresentations. Similarly, an **irreducible module** is another name for a [simple module](wikipedia://en/29430).
- [Absolutely irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_irreducible) is a term applied to mean [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible), even after any [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) of the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of coefficients. It applies in various situations, for example to irreducibility of a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation), or of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808); where it means just the same as *irreducible over an [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129)*.
- In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* is irreducible if its [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum), that is, the topological space Spec *R*, is an [irreducible topological space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_topological_space).
- A [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) is irreducible if it is not [similar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similar_matrix) via a [permutation](wikipedia://en/238849) to a [block](wikipedia://en/457464) [upper triangular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_triangular_matrix) (that has more than one block of positive size). (Replacing non-zero entries in the matrix by one, and viewing the matrix as the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of a [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986), the matrix is irreducible if and only if such directed graph is [strongly connected](wikipedia://en/1614492).) A detailed definition is [given here](wikipedia://en/1540333#Classification_of_matrices).
- Also, a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/60876#Properties) if there is a non-zero probability of transitioning (even if in more than one step) from any state to any other state.
- In the theory of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), an *n*-manifold is [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_manifold) if any embedded (*n* − 1)-sphere bounds an embedded *n*-ball. Implicit in this definition is the use of a suitable [category](wikipedia://en/53759), such as the category of differentiable manifolds or the category of piecewise-linear manifolds. The notions of irreducibility in algebra and manifold theory are related. An *n*-manifold is called [prime](wikipedia://en/490134), if it cannot be written as a [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of two *n*-manifolds (neither of which is an *n*-sphere). An irreducible manifold is thus prime, although the converse does not hold. From an algebraist's perspective, prime manifolds should be called "irreducible"; however, the topologist (in particular the [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) topologist) finds the definition above more useful. The only compact, connected 3-manifolds that are prime but not irreducible are the trivial 2-sphere bundle over *S*<sup>1</sup> and the twisted 2-sphere bundle over *S*<sup>1</sup>. See, for example, [Prime decomposition (3-manifold)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29).
- A [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_space) if it is not the union of two proper closed subsets. This notion is used in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), where spaces are equipped with the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744); it is not of much significance for [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637). See also [irreducible component](wikipedia://en/2652476), [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808).
- In [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200), irreducible can refer to the inability to represent an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) as a composition of simpler structures using a product construction; for example [subdirectly irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdirectly_irreducible).
- A [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) is [P²-irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P²-irreducible) if it is irreducible and contains no [2-sided](wikipedia://en/4785316) $\mathbb RP^2$ ([real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325)).
- An [irreducible fraction](wikipedia://en/14822) (or fraction in lowest terms) is a [vulgar fraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulgar_fraction) in which the [numerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerator) and [denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominator) are smaller than those in any other equivalent fraction.
