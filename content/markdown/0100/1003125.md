# Bronisław Knaster

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bronisław Knaster"}

**Bronisław Knaster** (Polish: b; 22 May 1893 – 3 November 1980) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor at the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv) and the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław).[^o3] His main areas of research included [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology) and [continuum theory](wikipedia://en/24758520).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Knaster was born on 22 May 1893 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), then part of [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland) within the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), to physician Ludwik Knaster and Felicja (Wiernik).[^o5]

In 1911, after completing his secondary education, he enrolled at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) to study medicine. In 1914, when he obtained his doctor's qualifications, he returned to Warsaw. The following year, he started studying mathematics at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) where his teachers included [Stefan Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203), [Zygmunt Janiszewski](wikipedia://en/362471) and [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122), the founders of the [Warsaw School of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_School_of_Mathematics).[^o5]

In 1922, Knaster earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) at the University of Warsaw under Mazurkiewicz on the basis of his thesis *Un continu dont tout sous-continu est indécomposable*.[^o6] In 1933, he co-authored with [Witold Hurewicz](wikipedia://en/362385) *Ein Einbettungessatz über henkelfreie Kontinua*.[^o7]

Since 1939, Knaster worked as professor at the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv). During the Nazi occupation of Lwów (1941–1944), he was employed at Professor [Rudolf Weigl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Weigl)'s Typhus Research Institute as a [lice feeder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lice_feeder), alongside [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646).[^o5]

In 1945, he completed a project in collaboration with [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk) and [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) concerning the establishment of the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/67042302).[^o8]

He is known for his work in [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology) and in particular for his discoveries in 1922 of the hereditarily indecomposable continuum or [pseudo-arc](wikipedia://en/3264412) and of the [Knaster continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knaster_continuum), or buckethandle continuum.[^o9] Together with his teacher [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735) and his colleague [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), he also developed the [last diminisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_diminisher) procedure for [fair cake cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_cake_cutting). He also introduced the term [dendroid](wikipedia://en/8248350), a type of [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), when working at the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław).[^o11]

In 1950, Knaster received the [Golden Cross of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Merit_%28Poland%29).[^o12] In 1955, he was awarded the Officer's Cross of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) for "his contributions to professional work in the field of higher education".[^o13] Knaster's notable doctoral students included J. J. Charatonik, Roman Duda, Jerzy Mioduszewski, and Meir Reichaw.[^o6]
