# Class function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Class function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the fields of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [representation theory of groups](wikipedia://en/12695), a **class function** is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) on a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* that is constant on the [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176) of *G*. In other words, it is invariant under the [conjugation map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_map) on *G*. Such functions play a basic role in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

:::

## Characters {#Characters}

The [character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_%28group_theory%29) of a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of *G* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is always a class function with values in *K*. The class functions form the [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) of the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) *K*\[*G*\]. Here a class function *f* is identified with the element $\sum_{g \in G} f(g) g$.

## Inner products {#Inner_products}

The set of class functions of a group $G$ with values in a field $K$ form a $K$-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). If $G$ is finite and the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of the field does not divide the order of $G$, then there is an [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) defined on this space defined by $\langle \phi , \psi \rangle = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \phi(g) \overline{\psi(g)},$ where $|G|$ denotes the order of $G$ and the overbar denotes conjugation in the field $K$. The set of [irreducible characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_character) of $G$ forms an [orthogonal basis](wikipedia://en/755400). Further, if $K$ is a [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440) for $G$for instance, if $K$ is [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed), then the irreducible characters form an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063).

When $G$ is a [compact group](wikipedia://en/750326) and $K = \mathbf{C}$ is the field of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) can be applied to replace the finite sum above with an integral: $\langle \phi, \psi \rangle = \int_G \phi(t) \overline{\psi(t)}\, dt.$ In this setting, the irreducible characters form a Hilbert basis of the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of square-integrable class functions, by the [Peter–Weyl theorem](wikipedia://en/294474).

When $K$ is the real numbers or the complex numbers, the inner product is a [non-degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_form) [Hermitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form) [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477).
