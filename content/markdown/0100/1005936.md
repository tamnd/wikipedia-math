# List of theorems called fundamental

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of theorems called fundamental"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **fundamental theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) which is considered to be central and conceptually important for some topic. For example, the [fundamental theorem of calculus](wikipedia://en/19468696) gives the relationship between [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416) and [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus).[^o2] The names are mostly traditional, so that for example the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556) is basic to what would now be called [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Some of these are [classification theorems](wikipedia://en/4576710) of objects which are mainly dealt with in the field. For instance, the [fundamental theorem of curves](wikipedia://en/1056003) describes classification of [regular curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_curve) in space up to [translation](wikipedia://en/204682) and [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789).

:::

Likewise, the mathematical literature sometimes refers to the **fundamental lemma** of a field. The term [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) is conventionally used to denote a proven proposition which is used as a stepping stone to a larger result, rather than as a useful statement in-and-of itself.
