# Cyclic cover

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cyclic cover"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **cyclic cover** or **cyclic covering** is a [covering space](wikipedia://en/250323) for which the set of covering transformations forms a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327).[^o2][^o3] As with cyclic groups, there may be both finite and infinite cyclic covers.[^o4]

:::

Cyclic covers have proven useful in the descriptions of [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) topology[^o2][^o4] and the algebraic geometry of [Calabi–Yau manifolds](wikipedia://en/188932).[^o3]

In classical algebraic geometry, cyclic covers are a tool used to create new objects from existing ones through, for example, a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) by a root element.[^o5] The powers of the root element form a cyclic group and provide the basis for a cyclic cover. A [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) over a [complex projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) with [torsion](wikipedia://en/4342484) index $r$ may induce a cyclic Galois covering with cyclic group of order $r$.
