# Polychromatic symmetry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polychromatic symmetry"}

*Image: Threecolouranimation.gif.* Three-colour symmetry operation of colour group *p3*\[3\]<sub>1</sub>

**Polychromatic symmetry** is a colour symmetry which interchanges three or more colours in a symmetrical pattern. It is a natural extension of [dichromatic symmetry](wikipedia://en/73498718). The coloured symmetry groups are derived by adding to the position coordinates (*x* and *y* in two dimensions, *x*,*y* and *z* in three dimensions) an extra coordinate, *k*, which takes three or more possible values (colours).[^o2]

:::

An example of an application of polychromatic symmetry is crystals of substances containing molecules or ions in triplet states, that is with an electronic spin of magnitude 1, should sometimes have structures in which the spins of these groups have projections of + 1, 0 and -1 onto local magnetic fields. If these three cases are present with equal frequency in an orderly array, then the [magnetic space group](wikipedia://en/60491326) of such a crystal should be three-coloured.[^o3][^o4]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Group theory {#Group_theory}

Initial research by Wittke and Garrido (1959)[^o13] and by Niggli and Wondratschek (1960)[^o14] identified the relation between the colour groups of an object and the [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of the object's geometric [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186). In 1961 [van der Waerden](wikipedia://en/91390) and [Burckhardt](wikipedia://en/64426633)[^o15] built on the earlier work by showing that colour groups can be defined as follows: in a colour group of a pattern (or object) each of its geometric symmetry operations *s* is associated with a permutation *σ* of the *k* colours in such a way that all the pairs (*s*,*σ*) form a group. [Senechal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Senechal) showed that the permutations are determined by the subgroups of the geometric symmetry group *G* of the uncoloured pattern.[^o16] When each symmetry operation in *G* is associated with a unique colour permutation the pattern is said to be perfectly coloured.[^o17][^o18]

The Waerden-Burckhardt theory defines a *k*-colour group *G*(*H*) as being determined by a subgroup *H* of index *k* in the symmetry group *G*.[^o19] If the subgroup *H* is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) then the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) *G*/*H* permutes all the colours.[^o20]

## History {#History}

- 1956 First papers on polychromatic, as opposed to dichromatic, symmetry groups are published by [Belov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Belov_%28geochemist%29) and his co-workers.[^o22][^o23][^o24][^o25][^o26][^o27] [Vainshtein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Vainshtein) and [Koptsik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Alexandrovich_Koptsik) (1994) summarise the Russian work.[^o28]
- 1957 [Mackay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Lindsay_Mackay) publishes the first review of the Russian work in English.[^o29] Subsequent reviews were published by Koptsik (1968),[^o30] [Schwarzenberger](wikipedia://en/37553568) (1984),[^o31] in [Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) and [Shephard](wikipedia://en/33354532)'s *[Tilings and patterns](wikipedia://en/76301624)* (1987),[^o8] by [Senechal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Senechal) (1990)[^o16] and by Thomas (2012).[^o32]
- Late 1950s [M.C. Escher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.C._Escher)'s artworks based on dichromatic and polychromatic patterns popularise colour symmetry amongst scientists.[^o33][^o34]
- 1961 Clear definition by [van der Waerden](wikipedia://en/91390) and [Burckhardt](wikipedia://en/64426633) of colour symmetry in terms of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), regardless of the number of colours or dimensions involved.[^o15]
- 1964 First publication of [Shubnikov](wikipedia://en/70215429) and Belov's *[Colored Symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colored_Symmetry_%28book%29)* in English translation[^o35]
- 1971 Derivation by [Loeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Lee_Loeb) in *[Color and Symmetry](wikipedia://en/76540553)* of 2D colour symmetry configurations using rotocenters.[^o36]
- 1974 Publication of *[Symmetry in Science and Art](wikipedia://en/76514470)* by Shubnikov and Koptsik with extensive coverage of polychromatic symmetry.[^o37]
- 1983 Senechal examines the problem of colouring polyhedra symmetrically using group theory.[^o19][^o38] Cromwell later uses an algorithmic counting approach (1997).[^o39]
- 1988 Washburn and Crowe apply colour symmetry analysis to cultural patterns and objects.[^o40] Washburn and Crowe inspired further work, for example by Makovicky.[^o41]
- 1997 Lifshitz extends the theory of color symmetry from periodic to quasiperiodic crystals.[^o42]
- 2008 [Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), Burgiel and [Goodman-Strauss](wikipedia://en/56181508) publish *[The Symmetries of Things](wikipedia://en/69062204)* which describes the colour-preserving symmetries of coloured objects using a new notation based on [Orbifolds](wikipedia://en/293504).[^o43]
