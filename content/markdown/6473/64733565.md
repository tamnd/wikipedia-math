# Philip Marcus Levy

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Philip Marcus Levy"}

**Philip Marcus Levy** (1934 – 2011) was a British psychologist who specialised in the field of [mathematical psychology](wikipedia://en/2081516).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Phil Levy was born in 1934 in [Redcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar), [North Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Riding_of_Yorkshire) but grew up in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds) where he was a pupil at [Leeds Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Grammar_School). He took his first degree at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) followed by a PhD on discriminant analysis at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham) (1960). He stayed at Birmingham, initially as a research fellow and then as lecturer and senior lecturer. He was appointed the first chair in psychology at the [University of Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lancaster) in 1972.[^o4][^o5]

He was active in the [British Psychological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Psychological_Society) and edited the [British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology](wikipedia://en/23825185). He was elected president of the society in 1978. In his presidential address he called on psychologists to reflect on the assumptions underlying the science. As he said: *I feel that we have a simplified view of science, perhaps due - for understandable sociohistorical reasons - for our anxious desire to receive the accolade of being 'scientific' \[...\] I believe that science is much more a matter of social exchange than many of us are prepared to admit.*[^o6]

## Work {#Work}

His expertise was in mathematical and statistical psychology. He was an expert in the construction of tests in education and training.[^o8]

## Awards {#Awards}

- 1977-1978 - President, [British Psychological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Psychological_Society)
