## Head coaches {#Head_coaches}

In July 1935, Fritz Thelen became the club's first full-time head coach, but was not available in the first months of the season, forcing Dortmund player and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_national_football_team) international [Ernst Kuzorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kuzorra) to take over instead.[^o3][^o4] In 1966, [Willi Multhaup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willi_Multhaup) led his side to the [European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965–66_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup), the first German team to win a European trophy. [Horst Köppel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_Köppel) was the coach to bring major silverware to the club for the first time in over 20 years, winning the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988–89_DFB-Pokal) in 1989.

[Ottmar Hitzfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottmar_Hitzfeld) is the club's most successful coach, having won both the Bundesliga and [Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFL-Supercup) twice. In [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996–97_UEFA_Champions_League), Dortmund had waited for continental success for over 30 years; Hitzfeld crowned his period with an unexpected triumph and won the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League). Dortmund won the [Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Cup_%281960–2004%29) in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Intercontinental_Cup) and head coach [Nevio Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevio_Scala) became the first and so far the only non-native speaker who won a major title. In [2001–02](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001–02_Bundesliga), [Matthias Sammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Sammer), a former BVB player, brought the league title back to Dortmund. In [2008–09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008–09_Bundesliga), the club approached [Mainz 05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FSV_Mainz_05) head coach [Jürgen Klopp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jürgen_Klopp). He won the club's seventh championship title in [2010–11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010–11_Bundesliga). In his [fourth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011–12_Borussia_Dortmund_season), Dortmund won the [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011–12_Bundesliga) and the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011–12_DFB-Pokal) to complete the first league and cup [double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_%28association_football%29) in the club's history.[^o5]

The current coach is [Niko Kovač](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niko_Kovač).[^o6]

## Club captains {#Club_captains}

*Image: 2023-08-12 TSV Schott Mainz gegen Borussia Dortmund (DFB-Pokal 2023-24) by Sandro Halank–148 (cropped).jpg.* [Emre Can](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emre_Can), the current club captain

Since 1963, 19 players have held the position of club captain for Borussia Dortmund.[^o36][^o37] The first club captain after the introduction of the Bundesliga was [Alfred Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Schmidt_%28footballer%29), who was captain from 1963 to 1965.[^o36] The longest-serving captain [Michael Zorc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Zorc), who was club captain from 1988 to 1998, has the distinction of having won the most trophies as captain; he won two [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) titles, one [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFB-Pokal), three [DFL-Supercups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFL-Supercup) and one [UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League).[^o36] The current club captain is [Emre Can](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emre_Can), who took over after [Marco Reus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Reus) stepped down from his role as the club's captain for the [2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023–24_Borussia_Dortmund_season).[^o38][^o39]
