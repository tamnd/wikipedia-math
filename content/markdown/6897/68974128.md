# Harriet Redfield Cobb

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Harriet Redfield Cobb"}

**Harriet Redfield Cobb** (September 10, 1866 – February 13, 1958) was an American mathematics educator, a professor at [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) from 1895 to 1931.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Cobb was born in [Peekskill, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peekskill,_New_York), the daughter of Elisha G. Cobb and Esther Meroa Redfield Cobb. Her father was a Congregational clergyman. She attended Northampton High School and earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics at Smith College in 1889, and an honorary master's degree, also from Smith College, in 1891.[^o4] She pursued further studies at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Cobb taught mathematics at schools in Louisiana and Ohio before returning to Smith as a mathematics instructor in 1895.[^o5] She taught geometry[^o7] at Smith College[^o8] until she retired as professor emerita in 1931. Her colleagues at Smith included fellow mathematics professors [Eleanor P. Cushing](wikipedia://en/68940410). [Ruth Goulding Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Goulding_Wood), and [Suzan Rose Benedict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzan_Rose_Benedict).[^o9][^o10] She was a member of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) and a president of the Western Massachusetts Mathematics Association. She also taught Bible study classes at a Congregational church.[^o5]

Cobb was a busy traveler. She made an eight-month trip around the world with her friend, Pennsylvania math educator Sarah Gilbert, in 1907 and 1908.[^o11] She traveled to China several times in the 1920s,[^o12] and consulted there on math education for Chinese women.[^o7]

Cobb spent much of her retirement in Florida.[^o13] She was active in Smith alumnae activities there.[^o14] In 1936 she spoke to an audience of retired teachers in Florida about "The Pioneer Spirit of Smith College".[^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Cobb died in 1958, aged 91 years, in [Northampton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton,_Massachusetts).[^o17]
