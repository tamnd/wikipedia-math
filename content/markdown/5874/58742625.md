# Ferdinand Karl Schweikart

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ferdinand Karl Schweikart"}

**Ferdinand Karl Schweikart** (1780–1857) was a German [jurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurist) and amateur [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who developed an *astral geometry* before the discovery of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Schweikart, son of an attorney in Hesse, was educated in the school of his town. He went to the high school in [Hanau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanau) and [Waldeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldeck,_Hesse) before entering in 1796 to study law in the [university of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg), where he attended lectures of the mathematics professor J.K.F. Hauff.[^n1] He was awarded a doctorate in law at the [university of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) in 1798.

After practicing as a lawyer for a few years in Erbach, he was, from 1803 to 1807, instructor of the youngest prince of [Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen).[^n2] From 1809, he was university professor of jurisprudence successively at the universities of [Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen) (1809-1812), [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Kharkiv) (1812-1816), Marburg (1816-1821) and [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) (1821 afterwards).[^o6]

But Schweikart is best remembered for his works on mathematics: in 1807 he published *[Die Theorie der Parallellinien, nebst dem Vorschlage ihrer Verbannung aus der Geometrie (The theory of parallel lines, along with the suggestions of their banishment from geometry)](https://books.google.com/books?id=W6E6AQAAMAAJ)*.[^n3] Then, in 1818 he wrote to [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss), through his student [Christian Ludwig Gerling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Ludwig_Gerling), about a new geometry, called by him *astral geometry*, where the sum of the angles of a triangle was less than 180º (as in [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291)).[^n4] He influenced the work of his nephew, the mathematician [Franz Taurinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Taurinus).
