# Giuseppina Masotti Biggiogero

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Giuseppina Masotti Biggiogero"}

**Giuseppina Masotti Biggiogero** (8 August 1894 – 24 October 1977) was an Italian mathematician and historian. Known for her work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), she also wrote noted histories of mathematicians, like [Maria Gaetana Agnesi](wikipedia://en/42842830) and [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473). She was a member of the Lombard Institute Academy of Science and Letters and won both the Bordoni Prize and Torelli Prize for her work.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Giuseppina Biggiogero was born on 8 August 1894 in [Melegnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melegnano), Italy to Marta (née Massironi) and Biagio Biggiogero.[^n1][^n2] She completed her primary and secondary studies in [Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodi,_Lombardy), earning a degree as a teacher in 1912. While continuing her studies at the Carlo Cattaneo Technical Institute, she began teaching elementary school, first in [Carpiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpiano) and later in Melegnano.[^n3] [^n4] At the time that she was studying, the only paths available to enter university were to obtain a high school diploma, which was not typically available to women, or to obtain a degree from a [technical institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liceo_scientifico).[^n3][^n5] In 1916, Biggiogero earned her certificate with a specialty in physics and mathematics.[^n3]

Receiving a scholarship to attend the *[Politecnico di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Milan)* in 1917, she quit her teaching post.[^n2] She initially began her studies in engineering, but in 1918 moved to the mathematics courses.[^n3] Because the *Politecnico* did not offer a specific curriculum for math, she transferred in 1919 to the [University of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia), where she studied under the instruction of Luigi Brusotti.[^n2][^n6] She graduated in 1921, obtaining her diploma for teaching in pure mathematics. Working as an assistant to the professors [Luigi Berzolari](wikipedia://en/59222333) and [Francesco Gerbaldi](wikipedia://en/31737063),[^n3][^n2] Biggiogero published two works on [real algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_algebraic_curve), which were recognized with the Bordoni and Torelli prizes.[^n2][^n7] Her 1922 book, was titled *Sulle curve piane, algebriche, reali che presentano massimi d'inclusione*[^n8] and she published *Gruppi di massimi d'inclusione per curve piane, algebriche, reali, d'ordine n* in 1923.[^n9]

## Career {#Career}

In 1924, eager to work with [Oscar Chisini](wikipedia://en/1081345), Biggiogero returned to Milan and was appointed as his assistant and the professor for the descriptive and projective geometry courses at the *Politecnico di Milano*.[^n3][^n7] From 1927, she also gave lectures at the Mathematical and Physical Seminary of Milan, which was founded in that year, and taught higher and projective geometry courses at the [University of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Milan).[^n2][^n7] She was assigned as the editor of the mathematical entries in the *Enciclopedia Italiana* (Italian Encyclopedia, 1933) and reviewed the first sixteen volumes of the work, focusing on the compilations of [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661).[^n2] In 1939, Biggiogero married [Arnaldo Masotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaldo_Masotti), a fellow academic, who at the time was the professor of Rational Mechanics in the Faculty of Architecture. She was made chair of Geometry at the *Politecnico* in 1948 and retained that post until her retirement in 1969.[^n3] In addition to lecturing on [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891) in the mathematics department, she taught [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) to the students in the architectural and engineering departments.[^n3]

Biggiogero's research produced a large body of work on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), including research on the shapes and bundles of algebraic curves, [tensorial calculations](wikipedia://en/29965), Hessian singularities of curves and the construction of the triple and quadruple planes.[^n10] She and Chisini co-published several works together, including two textbooks *Lezioni di geometria descrittiva* (Lessons of Descriptive Geometry) published in 1941 and *Esercizi di geometria descrittiva* (Exercises of Descriptive Geometry), produced in 1946.[^n2] As a secondary path, she researched algebraic differentials, studying [transversals](wikipedia://en/897733), including [Liouville's](wikipedia://en/312301) and [Reiss' theorems](wikipedia://en/34651681), as well as the [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) of [Enrico Bompiani](wikipedia://en/48385220). She was also one of the first scholars in Italy to study [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336). She published works which summarized the results of [Morgan Crofton](wikipedia://en/4663836), [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514), and [Luis Santaló](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Santaló), presenting new formulas for determining ovals and [ellipses](wikipedia://en/9277).[^n11]

Also concerned with the history of mathematics, Biggiogero published studies on the geometry of the triangle and the tetrahedron, in conjunction with [Virginio Retali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginio_Retali) for the *Encyclopedia of Elementary Mathematics*. She wrote a biography of [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473) and with her husband produced a study on [Maria Gaetana Agnesi](wikipedia://en/42842830) and her works.[^n11] In 1949, she was made a member of the Lombard Institute Academy of Science and Letters and was also a member of Mathesis (organization), the Italian Society of Mathematical and Physical Sciences. In 1974,[^n2] [Clifford Truesdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Truesdell), editor-in-chief of the *Archive for History of Exact Sciences*, dedicated volume 14 to Biggiogero and her husband in recognition of their scholarship.[^n12]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Biggiogero died after a lengthy illness in Milan on 24 October 1977.[^n3] Posthumously, a street in her home town of Melegnano was named in her honor.[^n2]
