# Elizabeth McHarg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elizabeth McHarg"}

**Elizabeth Adam McHarg** (22 April 1923 – 29 April 1999) was a Scottish mathematician who, in 1965, became the first female president of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940).

:::

## Education {#Education}

McHarg studied at the [Glasgow High School for Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_School_of_Glasgow) and then at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), earning a master's degree with first-class honours in mathematics and natural philosophy in 1943. The university awarded her the Thomas Logan Medal and a George A. Clark scholarship, funding her as a researcher at [Girton College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College,_Cambridge). At Girton, she studied [nonlinear partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) with [Mary Cartwright](wikipedia://en/1529094) and completed her Ph.D. in 1948.

## Career and contributions {#Career_and_contributions}

McHarg returned to the University of Glasgow as a lecturer in 1948. There, she became an expert in [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110). She also translated the text *Differential Equations* by [Francesco Tricomi](wikipedia://en/19830487) from [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language) into English; her translation was published in 1961 by Hafner and republished in 2012 by Dover Publications.
