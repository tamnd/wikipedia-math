# Grace Alele-Williams

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Grace Alele-Williams"}

**Grace Awani Alele-Williams** [OON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Niger), FMAN, FNAE (16 December 1932 – 25 March 2022) was a Nigerian professor of [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471),[^o3][^o4] who made history as the first Nigerian woman to receive a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate),[^o5][^o6] and the first female [vice-chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_%28education%29) at any [Nigerian university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_universities),[^o7][^o8] at the [University of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Benin_%28Nigeria%29).[^o9][^o10][^o11]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Grace Awani Alele was born to [Itsekiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itsekiri_people) parents in [Warri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warri), Western Region (present-day [Delta State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_State)), [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_and_Protectorate_of_Nigeria) on 16 December 1932.[^o38][^o13][^o39][^o34] She attended [Government School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_School), Warri, [Queen's College, Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_College,_Lagos) and the University College of Ibadan[^o14] (now [University of Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ibadan)). She obtained a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in mathematics while teaching at [Queen's School, Ede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_School,_Ede) in [Osun State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osun_State) in 1957 and her [PhD degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics education at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) ([U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States)) in 1963,[^o14] thereby making her the first Nigerian woman to be awarded a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate).[^o15] Grace Alele was married later that year and became known as Grace Alele-Williams.[^o34] She returned to Nigeria for a couple of years' postdoctoral work at the University of Ibadan before joining the [University of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lagos) in 1965.[^o16]

## Career {#Career}

Alele-Williams's teaching career started at [Queen's School, Ede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_School,_Ibadan), [Osun State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osun_State), where she was a mathematics teacher from 1954 to 1957.[^o5] She left for the [University of Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vermont) to become a graduate assistant and later assistant professor.[^o18] From 1963 to 1965, Alele-Williams was a [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) research fellow, department (and institute) of education, [University of Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ibadan) from where she was appointed a professor of mathematics at the [University of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lagos) in 1976.[^o10]

She had a special interest in women's education.[^o19] While spending a decade directing the institute of education, she introduced innovative non-degree programmes, allowing older women working as elementary school teachers to receive certificates.[^o20] Alele-Williams always demonstrated concern for the access of female African students to scientific and technological subjects.[^o3] Her interest in mathematics education was originally sparked by her stay in the US, which coincided with the Sputnik phenomenon.[^o21] Working with the African Mathematics Program in [Newton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Massachusetts), under the leadership of MIT professor Ted Martins, she participated in mathematics workshops held in various African cities from 1963 to 1975.[^o16] Highlights included writing texts and correspondence courses covering basic concepts in mathematics working in concert with leading mathematicians and educators,[^o22] such as the book *Modern Mathematics Handbook for Teachers* published in 1974.[^o23] She taught at the University of Lagos from 1965 to 1985, and spent a decade directing the institute of education, which introduced innovative non-degree programmes, with many of the certificate recipients older women working as elementary school teachers.[^o10] By serving in various committees and boards, Alele-Williams had made useful contributions in the development of [education in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Nigeria).[^o24] She was chairman of the [curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum) review committee, former [Bendel State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendel_State) 1973–1979.[^o25][^o26] From 1979 to 1985, she served as chairman of the [Lagos State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State) curriculum review committee and Lagos State examinations boards.[^o16]

Alele-Williams was appointed vice-chancellor of the University of Benin in 1985, becoming the first female vice-chancellor of a Nigerian university[^o7][^o8] and she believes her appointment, which ended in 1992, was "a test case to demonstrate a woman's executive capability".[^o10]

Alele-Williams was a force for reform in the Dark Age for Nigeria's higher education in the 1980s.[^o27] Then, the activities of secret cults, confraternities and societies had spread within the Nigerian universities, especially in University of Benin.[^o21] She was able to make notable contributions.[^o3]

After serving as the vice-chancellor of the University of Benin, she joined the board of directors of [Chevron-Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron-Texaco) Nigeria.[^o28] She was also on the board of HIP asset management company limited, an [asset management company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_management_company) in Lagos, Nigeria.[^o34]

Alele-Williams was a member of governing council, [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO) Institute of Education.[^o3] She was also a consultant to the UNESCO and Institute of International Education Planning.[^o29][^o16] For a decade (1963–73), she was a member of the [African Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1575286) Programme, located in [Newton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Massachusetts), United States.[^o10] She was vice-president of the World Organisation for Early Childhood Education and later president of the Nigeria chapter,[^o26] and the first president of the [African Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/1764260) Commission on Women in Mathematics.[^o30]

She also served ten years (1993–2004) as regional vice-president for Africa of the [Third World Organization for Women in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_Women_in_Science_for_the_Developing_World).[^o31]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Alele-Williams married Babatunde Abraham Williams (1932–2010) in December 1963, not long after returning to Nigeria from the United States.[^o33] Williams was a political scientist who, at the time of their marriage, was a senior lecturer at the University of Ife, Osun State.[^o34]

Alele-Williams had five children, and, as of 2017, ten grandchildren.[^o4] She died on 25 March 2022 at the age of 89.[^o35][^o36][^o37] The next day, the University of Benin flew the institution's flag at [half-mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-mast) to mourn her death.[^o38][^o39]

## Awards {#Awards}

Alele-Williams received several awards and honours. She received the [Order of the Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Niger) in 1987, and was elected a Fellow of the Mathematical Association of Nigeria and a Fellow of the Nigerian Academy of Education.[^o41]

On 28 February 2014, she was one of 100 people to receive the Centenary Award, "a special recognition of unique contributions of Nigerians to the socio-cultural, economic and political development of the country in the last 100 years".[^o42][^o43][^o44]

She is included in a deck of playing cards featuring notable women mathematicians published by the Association of Women in Mathematics.[^o45]
