# Kronecker coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kronecker coefficient"}

In mathematics, **Kronecker coefficients** *g*${}^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}$ describe the decomposition of the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) (= [Kronecker product](wikipedia://en/712430)) of two [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) of a [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) into irreducible representations. They play an important role in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582) and [geometric complexity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_complexity_theory). They were introduced by [Murnaghan](wikipedia://en/29565672) in 1938.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a partition λ of *n*, write *V*<sub>λ</sub> for the [Specht module](wikipedia://en/5122533) associated to λ. Then the Kronecker coefficients *g*<sup>λ</sup><sub>*μν*</sub> are given by the rule

$$V_\mu \otimes V_\nu = \bigoplus_\lambda g_{\mu \nu}^{\lambda} V_\lambda.$$

One can interpret this on the level of [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359), giving a formula for the Kronecker product of two [Schur polynomials](wikipedia://en/3147062):

$$s_\mu \star s_\nu = \sum_{\lambda} g_{\mu \nu}^{\lambda} s_\lambda.$$

This is to be compared with [Littlewood–Richardson coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littlewood–Richardson_coefficient), where one instead considers the induced representation

$$\uparrow_{S_{|\mu|} \times S_{|\nu|}}^{S_{|\lambda|}} \left ( V_\mu \otimes V_\nu \right ) = \bigoplus_\lambda c_{\mu \nu}^{\lambda} V_\lambda,$$

and the corresponding operation of symmetric functions is the usual product. Also note that the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients are the analogue of the Kronecker coefficients for representations of GL<sub>*n*</sub>, i.e. if we write *W*<sub>λ</sub> for the irreducible representation corresponding to λ (where λ has at most *n* parts), one gets that

$$W_\mu \otimes W_\nu = \bigoplus_\lambda c_{\mu \nu}^{\lambda} W_\lambda.$$

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

(Bürgisser Ikenmeyer 2008) showed that computing Kronecker coefficients is [\#P-hard](wikipedia://en/27924) and contained in [GapP](wikipedia://en/4788155). A later work by (Ikenmeyer Mulmuley Walter 2017) shows that deciding whether a given Kronecker coefficient is non-zero is [NP-hard](wikipedia://en/54681).[^o8] This interest in computational complexity of these coefficients arises from its relevance in the [Geometric Complexity Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_complexity_theory) program.

A major unsolved problem in representation theory and combinatorics is to give a combinatorial description of the Kronecker coefficients. It has been open since 1938, when [Murnaghan](wikipedia://en/29565672) asked for such a combinatorial description.[^o9] A combinatorial description would also imply that the problem is [\#P-complete](wikipedia://en/27925) in light of the above result.

The Kronecker coefficients can be computed as

$$g(\lambda, \mu, \nu) = \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \chi^{\lambda}(\sigma)\chi^{\mu}(\sigma)\chi^{\nu}(\sigma),$$

where $\chi^{\lambda}(\sigma)$ is the [character value](wikipedia://en/691741) of the irreducible representation corresponding to [integer partition](wikipedia://en/104790) $\lambda$ on a permutation $\sigma \in S_n$.

The Kronecker coefficients also appear in the generalized Cauchy identity

$$\sum_{\lambda, \mu, \nu} g(\lambda, \mu , \nu) s_\lambda(x)s_\mu(y)s_\nu(z) = \prod_{i,j,k} \frac{1}{1-x_iy_jz_k}.$$

:::
