# Lectures in Geometric Combinatorics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lectures in Geometric Combinatorics"}

***Lectures in Geometric Combinatorics*** is a textbook on [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519). It was written by [Rekha R. Thomas](wikipedia://en/44907501), based on a course given by Thomas at the 2004 Park City Mathematics Institute, and published by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 2006, as volume 33 of their Student Mathematical Library book series.

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

The 14 chapters of the book can be grouped into two parts, with the first 2/3 of the book concerning the combinatorial properties of [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885) and the remainder connecting these topics to [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384).

The topics covered include [Schlegel diagrams](wikipedia://en/7201595) and [Gale diagrams](wikipedia://en/64979699), [irrational polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_polytope), [point set triangulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_set_triangulation), [regular triangulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_triangulation) and their polyhedral representation by [secondary polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_polytope), the [permutohedron](wikipedia://en/12745947) as an example of a secondary polytope, [Gröbner bases](wikipedia://en/357328), [toric ideals](wikipedia://en/45258510), and [toric varieties](wikipedia://en/737087), and the connections between Gröbner bases of toric ideals and regular triangulations of points.

## Audience and reception {#Audience_and_reception}

Although originally presented as an advanced undergraduate course, the book is also suitable for graduate students and for researchers interested in beginning work in this area. It requires only an undergraduate level of background material in mathematics (particularly linear algebra), and includes exercises making it suitable as a textbook. Reviewers [Miklós Bóna](wikipedia://en/39536649) and Alexander Zvonkin suggest it as a "quick introduction" to its topics, after which other books on the same topics can provide greater depth.
