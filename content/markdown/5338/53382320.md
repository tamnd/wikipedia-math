# Andrew Browder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrew Browder"}

**Andrew Browder** (January 8, 1931 – March 24, 2019) was an American mathematician at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Andrew Browder was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), Russia, where his father [Earl Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Browder), an American communist from Kansas, United States, was living and working for a period. His mother was Raisa Berkman, a Russian Jewish woman from St. Petersburg. His brothers were [Felix Browder](wikipedia://en/10201445) (older), also born in Moscow, and [William Browder](wikipedia://en/6646431) (younger). All three brothers had careers in mathematics. Their father returned to the United States in the early 1930s, bringing his family with him. The senior Browder became head of the [Communist Party USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA). He ran for US president in 1936 and 1940.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Browder traced his interest in mathematics to 1955 when he was a private at [Fort Dix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Dix) and [Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenhower) offered early release to servicemen entering [graduate school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_school). He studied at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). For two years he was a Miller Fellow at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and also studied at [Aarhus, Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus,_Denmark). As for teaching, "I taught well over one hundred courses, some of which I found interesting and enjoyable, some pretty depressing, most somewhere in between. The students had parallel experiences."[^o5][^o8]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Browder married and had three children including Laura Browder, professor of American Studies at the [University of Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Richmond).[^o5][^o10][^o11]

The analytic nature of the game of [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) enthralled Browder early on: "My own main sport was always chess. My father taught me the game when I was six, a friend of the family gave me a chess book when I was eleven or twelve, and after that I was hooked."

## Death {#Death}

Andrew Browder died age 88 on March 24, 2019.[^o5]

## Works {#Works}

In 1969 Browder published *Introduction to Function Algebras*. "The author develops much of the general theory of [function algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_algebra) and then applies it in the last two chapters to the theory of [rational approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padé_approximation) in the plane, and to finding analytic structure in the spectrum of a [Dirichlet algebra](wikipedia://en/8984724)."[^o14]

In 1996 he published an upper-level [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) *Mathematical Analysis* for well-motivated students having a background of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). Four topics are encompassed by the text: single-variable theory, [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630), [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration), and [functions of several variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_of_several_variables).[^o15]

In 2000 Browder published his article "Topology in the Complex Plane", which described the [Brouwer fixed point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brouwer_fixed_point_theorem), the [Jordan curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444), and [Alexander duality](wikipedia://en/3108888).[^o16]

With Hiroshi Yamaguchi, Browder wrote "A variation formula for harmonic modules and its application to several complex variables".[^o17]
