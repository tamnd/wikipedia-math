# Grothendieck trace formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grothendieck trace formula"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Grothendieck trace formula** expresses the number of points of a [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) in terms of the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of the [Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270) on its [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group). There are several generalizations: the Frobenius endomorphism can be replaced by a more general endomorphism, in which case the points over a finite field are replaced by its fixed points, and there is also a more general version for a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) over the variety, where the cohomology groups are replaced by cohomology with coefficients in the sheaf.

:::

The Grothendieck trace formula is an analogue in algebraic geometry of the [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/582530) in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

One application of the Grothendieck trace formula is to express the [zeta function](wikipedia://en/1157842) of a variety over a finite field, or more generally the [L-series](wikipedia://en/245283) of a sheaf, as a sum over traces of Frobenius on cohomology groups. This is one of the steps used in the proof of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705).

[Behrend's trace formula](wikipedia://en/41791807) generalizes the formula to [algebraic stacks](wikipedia://en/717377).

## Formal statement for *L*-functions {#Formal_statement_for_L-functions}

Let *k* be a finite field, *l* a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) invertible in *k*, *X* a [smooth *k*-scheme](wikipedia://en/35033819) of dimension *n*, and $\mathcal{F}$ a [constructible $\mathbb{Q}_l$-sheaf](wikipedia://en/26131119) on *X*. Then the following cohomological expression for the [*L*-function](wikipedia://en/245283) of $\mathcal{F}$ holds:

$$L(X, \mathcal{F}, t) = \prod_{i=0}^{2n} \det(1 - t\cdot F \,\, | \,\, H^i_c(X_{\bar{k}}, \mathcal{F}))^{(-1)^{i+1}} = \frac{\det(1 - t \cdot F \,\, | \,\, H^1_c(X_{\bar{k}}, \mathcal{F})) \cdots \det(1 - t \cdot F \,\, | \,\, H^{2n-1}_c(X_{\bar{k}}, \mathcal{F}))}{\det(1 - t \cdot F \,\, | \,\, H^0_c(X_{\bar{k}}, \mathcal{F})) \cdots \det(1 - t \cdot F \,\, | \,\, H^{2n}_c(X_{\bar{k}}, \mathcal{F}))}$$

where *F* is everywhere a [geometric Frobenius](wikipedia://en/6988121) action on [*l*-adic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etale_cohomology#ℓ-adic_cohomology_groups) with compact supports of the sheaf $\mathcal{F}$. Taking [logarithmic derivatives](wikipedia://en/470327) of both [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) produces a statement on sums of traces for each finite [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *E* of the base field *k*:

$$\sum_{x \in X(E)} \operatorname{tr}(F_E \,\, | \,\, \mathcal{F}_x) = \sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{tr}(F_E \,\, | \,\, H^i_c(X_{\bar{k}}, \mathcal{F}))$$

For a [constant sheaf](wikipedia://en/2783581) $\mathbb{Q}_l$ (viewed as $(\varprojlim \mathbb{Z}/l^n\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ to qualify as an *l*-adic sheaf) the left hand side of this formula is the number of *E*-points of *X*.
