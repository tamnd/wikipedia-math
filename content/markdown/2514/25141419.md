# Love number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Love number"}

The **Love numbers** (***h***, ***k***, and ***l***) are [dimensionless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity) parameters that measure the [rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiffness) of a [planetary body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) or other gravitating object, and the susceptibility of its shape to change in response to an external [tidal potential](wikipedia://en/30719).

:::

In 1909, [Augustus Edward Hough Love](wikipedia://en/1115798) introduced the values *h* and *k* which characterize the overall [elastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29) response of the Earth to the tides—*[Earth tides](wikipedia://en/7791685)* or *body tides*.[^o2] Later, in 1912, Toshi Shida added a third Love number, *l*, which was needed to obtain a complete overall description of the solid Earth's response to the [tides](wikipedia://en/30718).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The Love number ***h*** is defined as the ratio of the body tide to the height of the static [equilibrium tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_tide);[^o6] also defined as the vertical (radial) displacement or variation of the planet's elastic properties. In terms of the tide generating potential $V(\theta, \phi )/g$, the displacement is $h V(\theta, \phi)/g$ where $\theta$ is latitude, $\phi$ is east longitude and $g$ is acceleration due to gravity.[^o7] For a hypothetical solid Earth $h = 0$. For a liquid Earth, one would expect $h = 1$. However, the deformation of the sphere causes the potential field to change, and thereby deform the sphere even more. The theoretical maximum is $h = 2.5$. For the real Earth, $h$ lies between 0 and 1.

The Love number ***k*** is defined as the cubical dilation or the ratio of the additional potential (self-reactive force) produced by the deformation of the deforming potential. It can be represented as $k V(\theta, \phi)/g$, where $k = 0$ for a rigid body.[^o7]

The Love number ***l*** represents the ratio of the horizontal (transverse) displacement of an element of mass of the planet's crust to that of the corresponding static ocean tide.[^o6] In potential notation the transverse displacement is $l \nabla (V(\theta, \phi))/g$, where $\nabla$ is the horizontal [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) operator. As with *h* and *k*, $l = 0$ for a rigid body.[^o7]

:::

## Values {#Values}

According to Cartwright, "An elastic solid spheroid will yield to an external tide potential $U_2$ of [spherical harmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonic) degree 2 by a surface tide $h_2U_2/g$ and the self-attraction of this tide will increase the external potential by $k_2U_2$."[^o9] The magnitudes of the Love numbers depend on the rigidity and mass distribution of the spheroid. Love numbers $h_n$, $k_n$, and $l_n$ can also be calculated for higher orders of spherical harmonics.

For elastic Earth the Love numbers lie in the range: $0.616 \leq h_2 \leq 0.624$, $0.304 \leq k_2 \leq 0.312$ and $0.084 \leq l_2 \leq 0.088$.[^o6]

For Earth's tides one can calculate the tilt factor as $1 + k - h$ and the gravimetric factor as $1 + h - (3/2)k$, where subscript two is assumed.[^o9]

[Neutron stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star) are thought to have high rigidity in the crust, and thus a low Love number: $0.05 \leq k_2 \leq 0.17$.[^o10][^o11][^o12] In $D$ spacetime dimensions, Schwarzschild [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650) have vanishing static tidal Love numbers $k_\ell = 0$ at multipoles $\ell=D-3$.[^o13][^o14] Measuring the Love numbers of compact objects in binary mergers is a key goal of [gravitational-wave astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational-wave_astronomy), and requires relativistic calculations for neutron stars.[^o15][^o16]
