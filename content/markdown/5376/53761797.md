# Donatella Danielli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donatella Danielli"}

**Donatella Danielli** (born 1966) is a professor of mathematics at [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) and is known for her contributions to [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694), with specific emphasis on [free boundary problems](wikipedia://en/32070938).

:::

## Career {#Career}

She received a [Laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) [cum Laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_Laude) in Mathematics from the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), Italy in 1989. She completed her doctorate in 1999 at Purdue, under the supervision of [Carlos Kenig](wikipedia://en/54764530). Before joining the Purdue University faculty in 2001, she held positions at The [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) and at the [Institut Mittag-Leffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Mittag-Leffler) in Sweden. She was also a visiting fellow at the [Isaac Newton Institute for Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton_Institute_for_Mathematical_Sciences) in 2014. She serves as member-at-large in the executive committee of the Association for Women in Mathematics.[^o4]

## Selected awards {#Selected_awards}

- [National Science Foundation CAREER Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation_CAREER_Awards) (2003)
- Simons Fellow in Mathematics (2014)
- [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) since 2017 "for contributions to partial differential equations and geometric measure theory, and for service to the mathematical community".
- Fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) since 2020 for "her generous and consistent involvement in, and remarkable impact on, a large number of excellent local, national, and international initiatives to support interest and involvement of women in mathematics at all levels; and for remarkable, pioneering contributions positioning her as a role model for more junior mathematicians, particularly women".[^o6]
