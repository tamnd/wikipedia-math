# Fusion category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fusion category"}

In mathematics, a **fusion category** is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) that is [abelian](wikipedia://en/45063), [$k$-linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_category), [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_category), [monoidal](wikipedia://en/330604), and [rigid](wikipedia://en/25383437), and has only finitely many isomorphism classes of [simple objects](wikipedia://en/2390225#simple_object), such that the monoidal unit is simple. If the [ground field](wikipedia://en/27161748) $k$ is [algebraically closed](wikipedia://en/1018), then the latter is equivalent to $\mathrm{Hom}(1,1)\cong k$ by [Schur's lemma](wikipedia://en/521696).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [Representation Category of a finite group](wikipedia://en/874400) $G$ of cardinality $n$ over a field $\mathbb K$ is a fusion category if and only if $n$ and the characteristic of $\mathbb K$ are coprime. This is because of the condition of semisimplicity which needs to be checked by the [Maschke's theorem](wikipedia://en/30876007).

:::

## Reconstruction {#Reconstruction}

- Under [Tannaka–Krein duality](wikipedia://en/2588624), every fusion category arises as the representations of a [weak Hopf algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_Hopf_algebra).
- Every fusion category admits a [skeletonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletonization_of_fusion_categories), and so a fusion category can be specified simply by specifying the fusion rules of the underlying fusion ring (note that due to [Ocneanu Rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanu_Rigidity), this is not a unique specification in general).
