# Permutoassociahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Permutoassociahedron"}

*Image: Permutoassociahedron.svg.* The permutoassociahedron of dimension $2$ and the correspondence between its vertices and the bracketed permutations of three terms $a$, $b$, and $c$.

*Image: Permutoassociahedron3d.svg.* The four facets of the permutoassociahedron of dimension $3$ that share vertex $(ab)(cd)$. Three of these facets are quadrilaterals and the fourth is a pentagon.

In mathematics, the **permutoassociahedron** is an $n$-dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) whose vertices correspond to the bracketings of the [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of $n+1$ terms and whose edges connect two bracketings that can be obtained from one another either by moving a pair of brackets using [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity) or by [transposing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28mathematics%29) two consecutive terms that are not separated by a bracket.

:::

The permutoassociahedron was first defined as a [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) by [Mikhail Kapranov](wikipedia://en/62272076) who noted that this structure appears implicitly in [Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574)'s [coherence](wikipedia://en/49107439) theorem for symmetric and braided [categories](wikipedia://en/53759) as well as in [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747)'s work on the [Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations](wikipedia://en/16119106).[^o2] It was constructed as a convex polytope by Victor Reiner and [Günter M. Ziegler](wikipedia://en/14628647).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

When $n = 2$, the vertices of the permutoassociahedron can be represented by bracketing all the permutations of three terms $a$, $b$, and $c$. There are six such permutations, $abc$, $acb$, $bac$, $bca$, $cab$, and $cba$, and each of them admits two bracketings (obtained from one another by associativity). For instance, $abc$ can be bracketed as $(ab)c$ or as $a(bc)$. Hence, the $2$-dimensional permutoassociahedron is the [dodecagon](wikipedia://en/508486) with vertices $a(bc)$, $a(cb)$, $(ac)b$, $(ca)b$, $c(ab)$, $c(ba)$, $(cb)a$, $(bc)a$, $b(ca)$, $b(ac)$, $(ba)c$, and $(ab)c$.

When $n = 3$, the vertex $((ab)c)d$ is adjacent to exactly three other vertices of the permutoassociahedron: $(ab)(cd)$, $(a(bc))d$, and $((ba)c)d$. The first two vertices are reached from $((ab)c)d$ via associativity and the third via a transposition. The vertex $(ab)(cd)$ is adjacent to four vertices. Two of them, $((ab)c)d$ and $a(b(cd))$, are reached via associativity, and the other two, $(ba)(cd)$ and $(ab)(dc)$, via a transposition. This illustrates that, in dimension $3$ and above, the permutoassociahedron is not a [simple polytope](wikipedia://en/8853286).[^o6]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The $n$-dimensional permutoassociahedron has

$$n!{2n\choose n}$$

vertices. This is the product between the number of permutations of $n+1$ terms and the number of all possible bracketings of any such permutation. The former number is equal to the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) $(n+1)!$ and the latter is the $n$th [Catalan number](wikipedia://en/216488).

By its description in terms of bracketed permutations, the [1-skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910) of the permutoassociahedron is a [flip graph](wikipedia://en/47526601) with two different kinds of flips (associativity and transpositions).
