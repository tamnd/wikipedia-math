# Delzant's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Delzant's theorem"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a field of mathematics, **Delzant's theorem** provides a complete classification of symplectic [toric manifolds](wikipedia://en/12885890) in term of a special class of [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471).[^o2] It was proved by Thomas Delzant in 1988.[^o3]

:::

## Preliminaries {#Preliminaries}

A symplectic **toric manifold** is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) $(M^{2n},\omega)$ together with an [effective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_group_action) [Hamiltonian action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_action) of the $n$-dimensional [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) $\mathbb{T}^n = \mathbb{S}^1 \times \ldots \times \mathbb{S}^1$ on $M$, with moment map denoted by $\mu: M \to \mathbb{R}^n$.

The **convexity theorem**, proved independently in 1982 by [Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and by [Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636) and [Sternberg](wikipedia://en/5375996) using [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560), states that the image $\mu(M) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the images of the fixed points of the $\mathbb{T}^n$-action. It is therefore a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885), also called the **moment polytope**.[^o2][^o5][^o6]

A **Delzant polytope** is a special kind of convex polytope in $\mathbb{R}^n$, satisfying the following three properties

- it is a [simple polytope](wikipedia://en/8853286), i.e. for each vertex $x$, exactly $n$ edges meet at $v$;
- it is a rational polytope, i.e. for each vertex $x$, the edges at $x$ are of the form $x+tu_i^x$, with $t \geq 0$ and $u_i^x \in \mathbb{Q}$;
- it is smooth polytope, i.e. for each vertex $x$, the corresponding vectors $u_i^x$ form a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\mathbb{Z}^n$.

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

**Delzant's theorem** states that there is a bijective correspondence between $2n$-dimensional symplectic toric manifolds (up to torus-equivariant symplectomorphism) and Delzant polytopes in $\mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\{\text{Toric manifolds}\} \leftrightarrow \{\text{Delzant polytopes}\}, \quad (M^{2n},\omega,\mathbb{T}^n,\mu) \mapsto \mu(M).$$

More precisely, the moment polytope of every symplectic toric manifold is a Delzant polytope, every Delzant polytope is the moment polytope of such a manifold, and any two such manifolds with equivalent moment polytopes (up to translations and $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$-transformations) admit a torus-equivariant symplectomorphism between them. The proof of this result is based on [symplectic reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_reduction).[^o2]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### Projective spaces {#Projective_spaces}

The [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is a $2n$-dimensional toric manifold together with the Fubini-Study symplectic form, the action by $\mathbb{T}^n = \mathbb{S}^1 \times \ldots \times \mathbb{S}^1$ given by

$$(e^{i \theta_1}, \ldots, e^{i \theta_n}) \cdot [z_0 : z_1 : \ldots : z_n] := [z_0 : e^{i \theta_1} z_1 : \ldots : e^{i \theta_n} z_n ],$$

and the moment map

$$\mu: \mathbb{CP}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n, \quad [z_0: z_1 : \ldots : z_n] \mapsto \frac{(|z_1|^2, \ldots, |z_n|^2)}{|z_0|^2 + |z_1|^2 + \ldots + |z_n|^2} .$$

Its associated Delzant polytope is the standard $n$-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

In particular, the case $n=1$ can be identified with the $\mathbb{S}^1$-action by rotations on $\mathbb{CP}^1 \cong \mathbb{S}^2$, with moment map given by the height function; its associated Delzant polytope is a segment.

### Products {#Products}

The Delzant correspondence is compatible with products. For instance, the product $\mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2$ is a 4-dimensional toric manifold, being the product of two copies of the toric manifold $\mathbb{CP}^1 \cong \mathbb{S}^2$ from above. Its associated Delzant polytope is the product of two segments, i.e. a rectangle in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

### Other examples {#Other_examples}

Any [Hirzebruch surface](wikipedia://en/3212617) is a 4-dimensional toric manifold; its associated Delzant polytope is a [right-angled trapezium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-angled_trapezium).
