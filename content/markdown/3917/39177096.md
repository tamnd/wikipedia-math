# Mahler's 3/2 problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mahler's 3/2 problem"}

In mathematics, **Mahler's 3/2 problem** concerns the existence of "$Z$-numbers".

:::

A **$Z$-number** is a positive real number $x$ such that the [fractional parts](wikipedia://en/501532) of

$$x \left(\frac 3 2\right)^ n$$

are less than $1/2$ for all positive integers $n$. [Kurt Mahler](wikipedia://en/4421496) conjectured in 1968 that there are no $Z$-numbers [^o2].

More generally, for a real number $\alpha$, define $\Omega(\alpha)$ as

$$\Omega(\alpha) = \inf_{\theta>0}\left({ \limsup_{n \rightarrow \infty} \left\lbrace{\theta\alpha^n}\right\rbrace - \liminf_{n \rightarrow \infty} \left\lbrace{\theta\alpha^n}\right\rbrace }\right).$$

Mahler's conjecture would follow if $\Omega(3/2)$ exceeds $1/2$. Flatto, [Lagarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_C._Lagarias), and Pollington showed[^o3] that

$$\Omega\left(\frac p q\right) > \frac 1 p$$

for rational $p/q > 1$ in lowest terms.
