# One-dimensional space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="One-dimensional space"}

A **one-dimensional (1D) space** is a [mathematical space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_space) in which location can be specified with a single [coordinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate). An example is the [number line](wikipedia://en/287188), each [point](wikipedia://en/593693) of which is described by a single [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438).[^o2] Any [straight line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) or smooth [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) is a one-dimensional space, regardless of the dimension of the [ambient space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_space) in which the line or curve is embedded. Examples include the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) on a plane, or a [parametric space curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_space_curve). In [physical space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_space), a 1D [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28mathematics%29) is called a "linear [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28physics%29)" ([rectilinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectilinear_motion) or [curvilinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvilinear_motion)), with [units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_%28physics%29) of [length](wikipedia://en/18542) (e.g., [metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre)).

:::

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) there are several structures that are one-dimensional spaces but are usually referred to by more specific terms. Any [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ is a one-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over itself. The [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578) over $K,$ denoted $\mathbf P^1(K),$ is a one-dimensional space. In particular, if the field is the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) $\mathbb{C},$ then the [complex projective line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_line) $\mathbf P^1(\mathbb{C})$ is one-dimensional with respect to $\mathbb{C}$ (but is sometimes called the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799), as it is a model of the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), [two-dimensional](wikipedia://en/70691232) with respect to real-number coordinates).

For every [eigenvector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) of a [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) *T* on a vector space *V*, there is a one-dimensional space *A* ⊂ *V* generated by the eigenvector such that *T*(*A*) = *A*, that is, *A* is an [invariant set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_set) under the action of *T*.[^o3]

In [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681), a one-dimensional subspace of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is mapped to a [one-parameter group](wikipedia://en/754851) under the [Lie group–Lie algebra correspondence](wikipedia://en/43302095).[^o4]

More generally, a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) is a [length-one](wikipedia://en/816070) [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over itself. Similarly, the [projective line over a ring](wikipedia://en/1271901) is a one-dimensional space over the ring. In case the ring is an [algebra over a field](wikipedia://en/191788), these spaces are one-dimensional with respect to the algebra, even if the algebra is of higher dimensionality. Number-line.svg
