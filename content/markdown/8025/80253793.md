# Joshua Evan Greene

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joshua Evan Greene"}

**Joshua Evan Greene** (born in 1981) is a mathematician whose primary area of research is [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). He is a professor at [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College). Greene solved the lens space realization problem, while he and a co-author have made advances in the understanding of the [inscribed square problem](wikipedia://en/27631070).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Greene completed his undergraduate studies at [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College)[^o5] and at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). He received his PhD in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2009,[^o6] with [Zoltán Szabó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoltán_Szabó_%28mathematician%29) as advisor (thesis: *Donaldson's Theorem, Heegaard Floer Homology, and Results on Knots*).[^o7]

After completing his doctoral degree, Greene worked as a [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Postdoctoral Fellow from 2009 to 2011 at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). Greene is currently a professor at [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College).[^o8]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

In early work, Greene solved the lens space realization problem for knot surgery.[^o10]

In 2020, together with Andrew Lobb, Greene [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) the rectangular peg [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138).[^o8][^o11][^o12] Later they resolved the cyclic quadrilateral peg problem[^o11][^o13][^o14]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Greene was awarded the 2002 [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157) for his simple topological proof of [Kneser's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2724154#Chromatic_number). He received the [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize) in 2023 for his article in the [Notices of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4977512). His work was featured in the 2021 Current Events Bulletin of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16] He was a 2024 Simons Fellow in mathematics.[^o17] Together with Andrew Lobb, in 2025 Greene won a Frontiers of Science Award[^o18] for their work resolving the rectangular peg problem.
