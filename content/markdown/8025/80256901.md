# Lloyd S. Nelson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lloyd S. Nelson"}

**Lloyd Steadman Nelson** (March 29, 1922 – August 28, 2013) was an American statistician. He is known for developing the [Nelson rules](wikipedia://en/2015211), a [statistical process control](wikipedia://en/593680) methodology to determine when a process is out of control.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Lloyd Steadman Nelson was born on March 29, 1922, in [Norwich, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich,_Connecticut), to Marion (née Rogers) and Ronald R. Nelson.[^o4][^o5][^o6] He graduated from the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in chemistry in 1943.[^o4][^o7] He served in the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy) from 1944 to 1946. He then graduated with a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in inorganic chemistry from the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) in 1950.[^o4][^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Nelson taught for a few years.[^o4] He was a consulting statistician with [General Electric Lamp Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Lighting) in [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland). He was then manager at the Applied Mathematics Laboratory at [GE Appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Appliances) in [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky).[^o8] In 1980, he began working for [Nashua Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashua_Corporation) as director of statistical methods. In 1992, he retired from Nashua Corporation.[^o4][^o8]

Nelson was editor of the [American Society for Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality) (ASQ) publication *Industrial Quality Control* in the 1960s and helped direct the publication to split into two, a general interest magazine *Quality Progress* and a technical quarterly publication the *Journal of Quality Technology*. He was the first editor of the *Journal of Quality Technology*.[^o8] He served on ASQ's awards board and the Shewhart and Deming Medal committees. He was a member of the *Journal of Quality Technology**s editorial review board. He wrote the "Technical Aids" feature of the*****Journal of Quality Technology*for more than 20 years.[^o8]***

In the October 1984 issue of the *Journal of Quality Technology*, Nelson first published the [Nelson rules](wikipedia://en/2015211), a [statistical process control](wikipedia://en/593680) methodology to indicate when a process is out of control.[^o10][^o11]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Nelson married Frances "Fran" Betty Pallant, daughter of Margaret (née Roth) and Frank W. Pallant, in 1982.[^o4][^o13] They had one son and two daughters, Peter R., Fay and Barbara.[^o4] His wife died in 2007.[^o13] He lived most of his life in [Londonderry, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Londonderry,_New_Hampshire).[^o4]

Nelson died on August 28, 2013, in [Clemson, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemson,_South_Carolina).[^o8]

## Awards and legacy {#Awards_and_legacy}

In 1964, Nelson was made a fellow of ASQ and was awarded the [Shewhart Medal](wikipedia://en/831964) in 1978. In 2001, he received ASQ's Distinguished Service Medal.[^o8] On February 8, 2003, he was made an honorary member of ASQ.[^o8] Subsequently, to commemorate his many contributions, the [Lloyd S. Nelson Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_S._Nelson_Award) was established in his honor.[^o15]

In [W. Edwards Deming](wikipedia://en/70534)'s *[Out of the Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_the_Crisis)*, Deming highlights Nelson's observation that "the most important figures that one needs for management are unknown or unknowable, but successful management must nevertheless take them into account".[^o8]
