# Borel graph theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Borel graph theorem"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Borel graph theorem** is generalization of the [closed graph theorem](wikipedia://en/497834) that was proven by L. Schwartz.[^n1]

:::

The Borel graph theorem shows that the [closed graph theorem](wikipedia://en/64620077) is valid for [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) defined on and valued in most spaces encountered in analysis.[^n1]

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is called a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622) if it is a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [complete metrizable space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_metrizable_space) and that a [Souslin space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souslin_space) is the continuous image of a Polish space. The weak [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) of a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) and the [strong dual](wikipedia://en/63951372) of a separable [Fréchet–Montel space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fréchet–Montel_space) are Souslin spaces. Also, the space of distributions and all [Lp-spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lp-space) over open subsets of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) as well as many other spaces that occur in analysis are Souslin spaces. The Borel graph theorem states:[^n1]

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) spaces and let $u : X \to Y$ be linear. If $X$ is the [inductive limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_limit) of an arbitrary family of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), if $Y$ is a Souslin space, and if the graph of $u$ is a Borel set in $X \times Y,$ then $u$ is continuous.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

An improvement upon this theorem, proved by A. Martineau, uses K-analytic spaces. A topological space $X$ is called a $K_{\sigma \delta}$ if it is the countable intersection of countable unions of [compact sets](wikipedia://en/6042). A Hausdorff topological space $Y$ is called **K-analytic** if it is the continuous image of a $K_{\sigma \delta}$ space (that is, if there is a $K_{\sigma \delta}$ space $X$ and a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) of $X$ onto $Y$). Every compact set is K-analytic so that there are non-separable K-analytic spaces. Also, every Polish, Souslin, and [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) is K-analytic as is the weak dual of a Fréchet space. The generalized theorem states:[^n2]

Let $X$ and $Y$ be locally convex Hausdorff spaces and let $u : X \to Y$ be linear. If $X$ is the inductive limit of an arbitrary family of Banach spaces, if $Y$ is a K-analytic space, and if the graph of $u$ is closed in $X \times Y,$ then $u$ is continuous.

:::
