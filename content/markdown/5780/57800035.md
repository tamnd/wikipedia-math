# Eileen Brooke

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eileen Brooke"}

**Eileen Minnie Brooke** (1905 – 1989) was a British [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and health policy professional.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Eileen Minnie Brooke attended [East London College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Queen_Mary_University_of_London), earning a B.Sc. in mathematics in 1926, and an M.Sc. in mathematics in 1929. She completed doctoral studies in 1952.[^o4]

## In Great Britain, 1940s–1960s {#In_Great_Britain,_1940s–1960s}

In the 1940s, Brooke was based at the [Emergency Medical Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Hospital_Service) Statistical Branch in Norcross, and studied wartime health issues, including battle exhaustion,[^o31] burns,[^o6] and [gastric ulcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptic_ulcer_disease).[^o7] She was elected a fellow of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) in 1943.[^o8]

In the 1950s, Brooke was a statistician in the [Medical Statistics](wikipedia://en/11082896) branch of the [General Register Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Register_Office_for_England_and_Wales).[^o9][^o10] She attended the Second [World Congress of Psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Psychiatric_Association) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) in 1957, and presented a paper on [schizophrenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophrenia).[^o11] She also attended the [International Congress on Mental Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federation_for_Mental_Health) in Paris in 1961.[^o12]

Brooke was co-author of *The survey of sickness, 1943 to 1952* (1957, with W. P. D. Logan),[^o13] and author of *A cohort study of patients first admitted to mental hospitals in 1954 and 1955* (1963)[^o14][^o15] and *A census of patients in psychiatric beds, 1963* (1967).[^o16]

## International work, 1960s–1970s {#International_work,_1960s–1970s}

Brooke spoke at a mental health conference in Pennsylvania in 1964.[^o18] She was chief of the Department of Medical Information and Statistics at the University Institute of Social and Preventive Medicine in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne).[^o19][^o20] She was a collaborating investigator on the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization)'s International Pilot Study of Schizophrenia in the late 1960s.[^o21][^o22] In 1977 she spoke at an WHO workshop on "the medico-social risks of alcohol consumption" in Luxembourg.[^o20]

Brooke wrote and edited policy reports for the World Health Organization and other international bodies, including *The methodology of psychiatric out-patient data collection* (1973),[^o23] *The current and future use of registers in health information systems* (1974),[^o24][^o25] *Suicide and attempted suicide* (1974),[^o26] and *Activities in the field of drug dependence (European region)* (1975).[^o27]

## Death {#Death}

Brooke died in 1989. A colleague wrote in an obituary that "Miss Brooke was precious to WHO's programmes because she was a statistician who liked to assemble data, enjoyed handling them and had the ability to present them without ever losing sight of the broader context in which these data were gathered."[^o29] Her papers are held in the Mile End Library, [Queen Mary University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_University_of_London).[^o4]

- "More and More Barbiturates" *Medicine, Science and the Law*, 1964. (with M. M. Glatt)[^o33]
