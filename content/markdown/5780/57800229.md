# Cramér's theorem (large deviations)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cramér's theorem"}

**Cramér's theorem** is a fundamental result in the [theory of large deviations](wikipedia://en/3772909), a subdiscipline of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). It determines the [rate function](wikipedia://en/3772816) of a series of [iid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iid) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685). A weak version of this result was first shown by [Harald Cramér](wikipedia://en/223129) in 1938.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The logarithmic [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) (which is the [cumulant](wikipedia://en/359684)-generating function) of a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) is defined as:

$$\Lambda(t)=\log \operatorname E [\exp(tX_1)].$$

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be a sequence of [iid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iid) real [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) with finite logarithmic moment generating function, i.e. $\Lambda(t) < \infty$ for all $t \in \mathbb R$.

Then the [Legendre transform](wikipedia://en/390273) of $\Lambda$:

$$\Lambda^*(x):= \sup_{t \in \mathbb R} \left(tx-\Lambda(t) \right)$$

satisfies,

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac 1n \log \left(P\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \geq nx \right)\right) = -\Lambda^*(x)$$

for all $x > \operatorname E[X_1].$[^o4]p. 508

In the terminology of the theory of large deviations the result can be reformulated as follows:

If $X_1, X_2, \dots$ is a series of iid random variables, then the distributions $\left(\mathcal L ( \tfrac 1n \sum_{i=1}^n X_i) \right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ satisfy a [large deviation principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_deviation_principle) with [rate function](wikipedia://en/3772816) $\Lambda^*$, where $\mathcal{L}(X)$ denotes the distribution of the random variable $X$.[^o4]p. 514

:::
