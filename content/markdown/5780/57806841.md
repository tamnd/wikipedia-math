# Karl W. Gruenberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Karl W. Gruenberg"}

**Karl W. Gruenberg** (3 June 1928 – 10 October 2007) was a British mathematician who specialised in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), in particular with the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) theory of groups.[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

At the age of eleven, Gruenberg was one of the many Jewish children sent from Austria to Great Britain as part of the [Kindertransport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindertransport) in 1939.[^o5] Most of the Kindertransport children never saw their parents again but Karl was lucky and his mother soon joined him, and they moved to London in 1943 where he entered [Kilburn Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilburn_Grammar_School). In 1946 he won a scholarship to study mathematics at [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge), where he received a BA degree in 1950 (duly promoted to [MA (Cantab.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxford,_Cambridge,_and_Dublin%29) in 1954).

He was appointed as an Assistant Lecturer in Mathematics at [Queen Mary College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_College), London University from 1953 to 1955. He got his PhD in 1954 under [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) at Cambridge with his treatise "A Contribution to the Theory of Commutators in Groups and Associative Rings".[^o8] He was awarded a [Commonwealth Fund Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Fund_Fellowship) which made it possible for him to spend 1955–56 at Harvard[^o6] and then 1956–57 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton, New Jersey.[^o9] In 1948 he became a British citizen.[^o8]

In 1967 he moved back to Queen Mary College where he became a leading figure in the algebra research community[^o6] and where he remained for the rest of his career.[^o10] He became a professor in the Department of Pure Mathematics where he worked with [Bertram Huppert](wikipedia://en/17639456) and [Wolfgang Gaschütz](wikipedia://en/75172510) organising the group theory conferences at the [Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) in Germany.[^o6]

He had a son Mark and a daughter Anne by his first wife Katherine. For thirty years he was married to his second wife Margaret.[^o10]
