# Split Lie algebra

Semi-simple

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Split Lie algebra"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681), a **split Lie algebra** is a pair $(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{h})$ where $\mathfrak{g}$ is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) and $\mathfrak{h} < \mathfrak{g}$ is a **splitting [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000)**, where "splitting" means that for all $x \in \mathfrak{h}$, $\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}} x$ is [triangularizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangularizable). If a Lie algebra admits a splitting, it is called a **splittable Lie algebra**.[^o2] Note that for reductive Lie algebras, the Cartan subalgebra is required to contain the center.

:::

Over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) such as the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers), all [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587) are splittable (indeed, not only does the Cartan subalgebra act by triangularizable matrices, but even stronger, it acts by diagonalizable ones) and all splittings are conjugate; thus split Lie algebras are of most interest for non-algebraically closed fields.

Split Lie algebras are of interest both because they formalize the [split real form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_real_form) of a complex Lie algebra, and because split semisimple Lie algebras (more generally, split reductive Lie algebras) over any field share many properties with semisimple Lie algebras over algebraically closed fields – having essentially the same representation theory, for instance – the splitting Cartan subalgebra playing the same role as the Cartan subalgebra plays over algebraically closed fields. This is the approach followed in (Bourbaki 2005), for instance.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Over an algebraically closed field, all Cartan subalgebras are conjugate. Over a non-algebraically closed field, not all Cartan subalgebras are conjugate in general; however, in a splittable semisimple Lie algebra all *splitting* Cartan algebras are conjugate.
- Over an algebraically closed field, all semisimple Lie algebras are splittable.
- Over a non-algebraically closed field, there exist non-splittable semisimple Lie algebras.[^o6]
- In a splittable Lie algebra, there *may* exist Cartan subalgebras that are not splitting.[^o7]
- Direct sums of splittable Lie algebras and ideals in splittable Lie algebras are splittable.

### Split real Lie algebras {#Split_real_Lie_algebras}

For a real Lie algebra, splittable is equivalent to either of these conditions:[^o9]

- The real rank equals the complex rank.
- The [Satake diagram](wikipedia://en/20514575) has neither black vertices nor arrows.

Every complex semisimple Lie algebra has a unique (up to isomorphism) split real Lie algebra, which is also semisimple, and is simple if and only if the complex Lie algebra is.[^o10]

For real semisimple Lie algebras, split Lie algebras are opposite to [compact Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/25262324) – the corresponding [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) is "as far as possible" from being compact.

:::

::: {#o12 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The split real forms for the complex semisimple Lie algebras are:[^o13]

- $$A_n, \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbf{C}): \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbf{R})$$
- $$B_n, \mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}(\mathbf{C}): \mathfrak{so}_{n,n+1}(\mathbf{R})$$
- $$C_n, \mathfrak{sp}_n(\mathbf{C}): \mathfrak{sp}_n(\mathbf{R})$$
- $$D_n, \mathfrak{so}_{2n}(\mathbf{C}): \mathfrak{so}_{n,n}(\mathbf{R})$$
- Exceptional Lie algebras: $E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2$ have split real forms *E*I, *E*V, *E*VIII, *F*I, *G*.

These are the Lie algebras of the split real groups of the complex Lie groups.

Note that for $\mathfrak{sl}$ and $\mathfrak{sp}$, the real form is the real points of (the Lie algebra of) the same [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924), while for $\mathfrak{so}$ one must use the split forms (of maximally indefinite index), as the group SO is compact.

:::
