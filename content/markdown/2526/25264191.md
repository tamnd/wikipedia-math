# Hardy hierarchy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hardy hierarchy"}

In [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) and [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), the **Hardy hierarchy**, named after [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599), is a hierarchy of sets of numerical functions generated from an ordinal-indexed family of functions *h*<sub>α</sub>: **N** → **N** (where **N** is the set of [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers), {0, 1, ...}) called **Hardy functions**. It is related to the [fast-growing hierarchy](wikipedia://en/24947285) and [slow-growing hierarchy](wikipedia://en/25264092).

:::

The Hardy hierarchy was introduced by Stanley S. Wainer in 1972,[^o2][^o3] but the idea of its definition comes from Hardy's 1904 paper,[^o3][^o4] in which Hardy exhibits a set of reals with cardinality $\aleph_1$.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *μ* be a [large countable ordinal](wikipedia://en/4376814) such that a [fundamental sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_sequence_%28ordinals%29) is assigned to every [limit ordinal](wikipedia://en/373137) less than *μ*. The **Hardy functions** *h*<sub>*α*</sub>: **N** → **N**, for *α* \< *μ*, is then defined as follows:

- $$H_0(n) = n,$$
- $$H_{\alpha+1}(n) = H_\alpha(n + 1),$$
- $H_{\alpha}(n) = H_{\alpha[n]}(n)$ if *α* is a limit ordinal.

Here *α*\[*n*\] denotes the *n*<sup>th</sup> element of the fundamental sequence assigned to the limit ordinal *α*. A standardized choice of fundamental sequence for all *α* ≤ *ε*<sub>0</sub> is described in the article on the [fast-growing hierarchy](wikipedia://en/24947285#The_Wainer_hierarchy).

The **Hardy hierarchy** $\{\mathcal{H}_\alpha\}_{\alpha<\mu}$ is a family of numerical functions. For each ordinal $\alpha$, a set $\mathcal{H}_\alpha$ is defined as the smallest class of functions containing $H_{\alpha}$, zero, successor and projection functions, and closed under limited primitive recursion and limited substitution[^o3] (similar to [Grzegorczyk hierarchy](wikipedia://en/14334415)).

(Caicedo 2007) defines a modified Hardy hierarchy of functions $H_\alpha$ by using the standard fundamental sequences, but with *α*\[*n*+1\] (instead of *α*\[*n*\]) in the third line of the above definition.

:::

## Relation to fast-growing hierarchy {#Relation_to_fast-growing_hierarchy}

The [Wainer hierarchy](wikipedia://en/24947285) of functions *f*<sub>α</sub> and the Hardy hierarchy of functions *H*<sub>α</sub> are related by *f*<sub>α</sub> = *H*<sub>ω<sup>α</sup></sub> for all α \< ε<sub>0</sub>. Thus, for any α \< ε<sub>0</sub>, *H*<sub>α</sub> grows much more slowly than does *f*<sub>α</sub>. However, the Hardy hierarchy "catches up" to the Wainer hierarchy at α = ε<sub>0</sub>, such that *f*<sub>ε<sub>0</sub></sub> and *H*<sub>ε<sub>0</sub></sub> have the same growth rate, in the sense that *f*<sub>ε<sub>0</sub></sub>(*n*-1) ≤ *H*<sub>ε<sub>0</sub></sub>(*n*) ≤ *f*<sub>ε<sub>0</sub></sub>(*n*+1) for all *n* ≥ 1. (Gallier 1991)
