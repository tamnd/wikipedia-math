# Exceptional isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exceptional isomorphism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **exceptional isomorphism**, also called an **accidental isomorphism**, is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between members *a*<sub>*i*</sub> and *b*<sub>*j*</sub> of two families, usually infinite, of mathematical objects, which is incidental, in that it is not an instance of a general pattern of such isomorphisms.[^o2] These coincidences are at times considered a matter of trivia,[^n1] but in other respects they can give rise to consequential phenomena, such as [exceptional objects](wikipedia://en/5280515).[^n1] In the following, coincidences are organized according to the structures where they occur.

:::

## Finite groups {#Finite_groups}

The exceptional isomorphisms between the series of [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) mostly involve [projective special linear groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) and [alternating groups](wikipedia://en/49726), and are:[^n1]

- PSL${}_{2}$(4) ≅ PSL${}_{2}$(5) ≅ A${}_{5}$, the smallest non-abelian simple group (order 60);
- PSL${}_{2}$(7) ≅ PSL${}_{3}$(2), the second-smallest non-abelian simple group (order 168) – [PSL(2,7)](wikipedia://en/390426);
- PSL${}_{2}$(9) ≅ A${}_{6}$;
- PSL${}_{4}$(2) ≅ A${}_{8}$;
- PSU${}_{4}$(2) ≅ PSp${}_{4}$(3), between a [projective special unitary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_unitary_group) and a [projective symplectic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_symplectic_group).

### Alternating groups and symmetric groups {#Alternating_groups_and_symmetric_groups}

*Image: Compound of five tetrahedra.png.* The [compound of five tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/3645795) expresses the exceptional isomorphism between the chiral icosahedral group and the alternating group on five letters.

There are coincidences between symmetric/alternating groups and small [groups of Lie type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_Lie_type)/[polyhedral groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_groups):[^n2]

- S${}_{3}$ ≅ PSL${}_{2}$(2) ≅ [dihedral group of order 6](wikipedia://en/2375824),
- A${}_{4}$ ≅ PSL${}_{2}$(3),
- S${}_{4}$ ≅ PGL${}_{2}$(3) ≅ PSL${}_{2}$(**Z**/4),
- A${}_{5}$ ≅ PSL${}_{2}$(4) ≅ PSL${}_{2}$(5),
- S${}_{5}$ ≅ PΓL${}_{2}$(4) ≅ PGL${}_{2}$(5),
- A${}_{6}$ ≅ PSL${}_{2}$(9) ≅ Sp${}_{4}$(2)′,
- S${}_{6}$ ≅ Sp${}_{4}$(2),
- A${}_{8}$ ≅ PSL${}_{4}$(2) ≅ O${}^{+}_{6}$(2)′,
- S${}_{8}$ ≅ O${}^{+}_{6}$(2).

These can all be explained in a systematic way by using linear algebra (and the action of S${}_{n}$ on affine *n*space) to define the isomorphism going from the right side to the left side. (The above isomorphisms for A${}_{8}$ and S${}_{8}$ are linked via the exceptional isomorphism SL${}_{4}$/*μ*${}_{2}$ ≅ SO${}_{6}$.)

There are also some coincidences with symmetries of [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra): the alternating group A<sub>5</sub> agrees with the chiral [icosahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_group) (itself an exceptional object), and the [double cover](wikipedia://en/25118486) of the alternating group A<sub>5</sub> is the [binary icosahedral group](wikipedia://en/3195145).

### Trivial group {#Trivial_group}

The [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) arises in numerous ways. The trivial group is often omitted from the beginning of a classical family. For instance:

- C${}_{1}$, the cyclic group of order 1;
- A${}_{0}$ ≅ A${}_{1}$ ≅ A${}_{2}$, the alternating group on 0, 1, or 2 letters;
- S${}_{0}$ ≅ S${}_{1}$, the symmetric group on 0 or 1 letters;
- GL(0, *K*) ≅ SL(0, *K*) ≅ PGL(0, *K*) ≅ PSL(0, *K*), linear groups of a 0-dimensional vector space;
- SL(1, *K*) ≅ PGL(1, *K*) ≅ PSL(1, *K*), linear groups of a 1-dimensional vector space
- and many others.

## Spheres {#Spheres}

The spheres *S*<sup>0</sup>, *S*<sup>1</sup>, and *S*<sup>3</sup> admit group structures, which can be described in many ways:

- *S*${}^{0}$ ≅ Spin(1) ≅ O(1) ≅ (**Z**/2**Z**)${}^{+}$ ≅ **Z**${}^{\times}$, the last being the group of units of the integers;
- *S*${}^{1}$ ≅ Spin(2) ≅ SO(2) ≅ U(1) ≅ **R**/**Z** ≅ [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177);
- *S*${}^{3}$ ≅ Spin(3) ≅ SU(2) ≅ Sp(1) ≅ [unit quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versors).

## Classical groups {#Classical_groups}

In addition to Spin(1), Spin(2) and Spin(3) above, there are isomorphisms for higher dimensional [spin groups](wikipedia://en/411231):

- Spin(4) ≅ Sp(1) × Sp(1) ≅ SU(2) × SU(2)
- Spin(5) ≅ Sp(2)
- Spin(6) ≅ SU(4)

Also, [Spin(8)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin%288%29) has an exceptional order 3 [triality](wikipedia://en/683109) automorphism.

In non-Euclidean signature, there are also the isomorphisms

- $$\mathrm{Spin}^+(1,2) \cong \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$$
- $$\mathrm{Spin}^+(1,3) \cong \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$$
- $$\mathrm{Spin}^+(2,2) \cong \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$$
- $$\mathrm{Spin}^+(1,4) \cong \mathrm{Sp}(1,1)$$
- $$\mathrm{Spin}^+(2,3) \cong \mathrm{Sp}(4,\mathbb R)$$
- $$\mathrm{Spin}^+(2,4) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2,2)$$
- $$\mathrm{Spin}^+(3,3) \cong \mathrm{SL}(4,\mathbb R)$$
- $\mathrm{Spin}^+(1,5) \cong \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb H)$ (i.e., $\mathrm{SU}^*(4)$)

where $\mathrm{Spin}^+$ refers to the connected component of the identity.

(These group isomorphisms are sometimes presented as central [isogenies](wikipedia://en/1085176) or isomorphisms of the associated [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) instead.)

## Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams {#Coxeter–Dynkin_diagrams}

There are some exceptional isomorphisms of [Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/277109#Isomorphisms), yielding isomorphisms of the corresponding [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) and of polytopes realizing the symmetries, as well as isomorphisms of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) whose root systems are described by the same diagrams. These are:

| Diagram | Dynkin classification | Lie algebra | Polytope |
|---|---|---|---|
|  | A<sub>1</sub> = B<sub>1</sub> = C<sub>1</sub> | $\mathfrak{sl}_2 \cong \mathfrak{so}_3 \cong \mathfrak{sp}_2$ | — |
| $\cong$ | A<sub>2</sub> = I<sub>2</sub>(3) | — | [2-simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) is [regular 3-gon](wikipedia://en/333306) ([equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285)) |
|  | BC<sub>2</sub> = I<sub>2</sub>(4) | $\mathfrak{so}_5 \cong \mathfrak{sp}_4$ | [2-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-cube) is [2-cross polytope](wikipedia://en/716401) is [regular 4-gon](wikipedia://en/333306) ([square](wikipedia://en/659939)) |
| $\cong$ | A<sub>1</sub> × A<sub>1</sub> = D<sub>2</sub> | $\mathfrak{sl}_2 \oplus \mathfrak{sl}_2 \cong \mathfrak{so}_{4}$ | — |
