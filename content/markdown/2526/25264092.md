# Slow-growing hierarchy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Slow-growing hierarchy"}

In [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) and [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), the **slow-growing hierarchy** is an [ordinal](wikipedia://en/26547932)-indexed family of slowly increasing functions *g*<sub>*α*</sub>: **N** → **N** (where **N** is the set of [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers), {0, 1, ...}). It contrasts with the [fast-growing hierarchy](wikipedia://en/24947285).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *μ* be a [large countable ordinal](wikipedia://en/4376814) such that a [fundamental sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_sequence_%28ordinals%29) is assigned to every [limit ordinal](wikipedia://en/373137) less than *μ*. The **slow-growing hierarchy** of functions *g*<sub>*α*</sub>: **N** → **N**, for *α* \< *μ*, is then defined as follows:[^o4]

- $$g_0(n) = 0$$
- $$g_{\alpha+1}(n) = g_\alpha(n) + 1$$
- $g_\alpha(n) = g_{\alpha[n]}(n)$ for limit ordinal *α*.

Here *α*\[*n*\] denotes the *n*<sup>th</sup> element of the fundamental sequence assigned to the limit ordinal *α*.

The article on the [fast-growing hierarchy](wikipedia://en/24947285#Definition) describes a standardized choice for fundamental sequence for all *α* \< ε<sub>0</sub>.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

- $$g_1(n) = 1$$
- $$g_m(n) = m$$
- $$g_\omega(n) = n$$
- $$g_{\omega+m}(n) = n+m$$
- $$g_{\omega m}(n) = mn$$
- $$g_{\omega^m}(n) = n^m$$
- $$g_{\omega^\omega}(n) = n^n$$
- $$g_{\varepsilon _0}(n) = n\uparrow\uparrow n$$

:::

## Relation to fast-growing hierarchy {#Relation_to_fast-growing_hierarchy}

The slow-growing hierarchy grows much more slowly than the fast-growing hierarchy. Even *g*<sub>[ε<sub>0</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon_zero_%28mathematics%29)</sub> is only equivalent to *f*<sub>3</sub> and *g*<sub>*α*</sub> only attains the growth of *f*<sub>ε<sub>0</sub></sub> (the first function that [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) cannot prove [total](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_function) in the hierarchy) when *α* is the [Bachmann–Howard ordinal](wikipedia://en/12732453).[^o8][^o9][^o10]

However, [Girard](wikipedia://en/579673) proved that the slow-growing hierarchy eventually *catches up* with the fast-growing one.[^o8] Specifically, that there exists an ordinal *α* such that for all integers *n*

*g*<sub>*α*</sub>(*n*) \< *f*<sub>*α*</sub>(*n*) \< *g*<sub>*α*</sub>(*n* + 1)

where *f*<sub>*α*</sub> are the functions in the fast-growing hierarchy. He further showed that the first *α* this holds for is the ordinal of the theory ID<sub>\<ω</sub> of [arbitrary finite iterations of an inductive definition](wikipedia://en/68497153).[^o11] However, for the assignment of fundamental sequences found in [^o9] the first match up occurs at the level ε<sub>0</sub>.[^o12] For Buchholz style tree ordinals it could be shown that the first match up even occurs at $\omega^2$.

Extensions of the result proved[^o11] to considerably larger ordinals show that there are very few ordinals below the ordinal of transfinitely iterated $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension where the slow- and fast-growing hierarchy match up.[^o13]

The slow-growing hierarchy depends extremely sensitively on the choice of the underlying fundamental sequences.[^o12][^o14][^o15]
