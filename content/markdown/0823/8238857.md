# Schoen–Yau conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schoen–Yau conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Schoen–Yau conjecture** is a disproved conjecture in [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), named after the [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137).

:::

It was inspired by a theorem of [Erhard Heinz](wikipedia://en/31696540) (1952). One method of disproof is the use of [Scherk surfaces](wikipedia://en/8235776), as used by [Harold Rosenberg](wikipedia://en/46753021) and Pascal Collin (2006).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Setting and statement of the conjecture"}

## Setting and statement of the conjecture {#Setting_and_statement_of_the_conjecture}

Let $\mathbb{C}$ be the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) considered as a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with its usual (flat) Riemannian metric. Let $\mathbb{H}$ denote the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/318742), i.e. the [unit disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_disc)

$$\mathbb{H} := \{ (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 | x^2 + y^2 < 1 \}$$

endowed with the hyperbolic metric

$$\mathrm{d}s^2 = 4 \frac{\mathrm{d} x^2 + \mathrm{d} y^2}{(1 - (x^2 + y^2))^2}.$$

E. Heinz proved in 1952 that there can exist no [harmonic](wikipedia://en/4577484) [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564)

$$f : \mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{C}. \,$$

In light of this theorem, Schoen conjectured that there exists no harmonic diffeomorphism

$$g : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{H}. \,$$

(It is not clear how Yau's name became associated with the conjecture: in unpublished correspondence with Harold Rosenberg, both Schoen and Yau identify Schoen as having postulated the conjecture). The Schoen(-Yau) conjecture has since been disproved.

:::

## Comments {#Comments}

The emphasis is on the existence or non-existence of an *harmonic* diffeomorphism, and that this property is a "one-way" property. In more detail: suppose that we consider two Riemannian manifolds *M* and *N* (with their respective metrics), and write

$$M \sim N\,$$

if there exists a diffeomorphism from *M* onto *N* (in the usual terminology, *M* and *N* are diffeomorphic). Write

$$M \propto N$$

if there exists an harmonic diffeomorphism from *M* onto *N*. It is not difficult to show that $\sim$ (being diffeomorphic) is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on the [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) of the [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) of Riemannian manifolds. In particular, $\sim$ is a [symmetric relation](wikipedia://en/200459):

$$M \sim N \iff N \sim M.$$

It can be shown that the hyperbolic plane and (flat) complex plane are indeed diffeomorphic:

$$\mathbb{H} \sim \mathbb{C},$$

so the question is whether or not they are "harmonically diffeomorphic". However, as the truth of Heinz's theorem and the falsity of the Schoen–Yau conjecture demonstrate, $\propto$ is not a symmetric relation:

$$\mathbb{C} \propto \mathbb{H} \text{ but } \mathbb{H} \not \propto \mathbb{C}.$$

Thus, being "harmonically diffeomorphic" is a much stronger property than simply being diffeomorphic, and can be a "one-way" relation.
