# Scherk surface

*Image: Scherkassociatefamily.gif.* Animation of Scherk's first and second surface transforming into each other: they are members of the same [associate family](wikipedia://en/37016612) of minimal surfaces.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scherk surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Scherk surface** (named after [Heinrich Scherk](wikipedia://en/7993138)) is an example of a [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734). Scherk described two complete embedded minimal surfaces in 1834;[^o2] his first surface is a doubly periodic surface, his second surface is singly periodic. They were the third non-trivial examples of minimal surfaces (the first two were the [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329) and [helicoid](wikipedia://en/684207)).[^o3] The two surfaces are [conjugates](wikipedia://en/37016612) of each other.

:::

Scherk surfaces arise in the study of certain limiting minimal surface problems and in the study of harmonic [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) of [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742).

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Scherk's First Surface"}

## Scherk's first surface {#Scherk's_first_surface}

Scherk's first surface is asymptotic to two infinite families of parallel planes, orthogonal to each other, that meet near *z* = 0 in a checkerboard pattern of bridging arches. It contains an infinite number of straight vertical lines.

### Construction of a simple Scherk surface {#Construction_of_a_simple_Scherk_surface}

*Image: Scherk-1 surface unit cell.stl.* [STL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STL_%28file_format%29) unit cell of the first Scherk surface

*Image: Superficie di scherk.jpg.* Five unit cells placed together

Consider the following minimal surface problem on a square in the Euclidean plane: for a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n*, find a minimal surface Σ<sub>*n*</sub> as the graph of some function

$$u_{n} : \left( - \frac{\pi}{2}, + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) \times \left( - \frac{\pi}{2}, + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) \to \mathbb{R}$$

such that

$$\lim_{y \to \pm \pi / 2} u_{n} \left( x, y \right) = + n \text{ for } - \frac{\pi}{2} < x < + \frac{\pi}{2},$$

$$\lim_{x \to \pm \pi / 2} u_{n} \left( x, y \right) = - n \text{ for } - \frac{\pi}{2} < y < + \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

That is, *u*<sub>*n*</sub> satisfies the [minimal surface equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surface_equation)

$$\mathrm{div} \left( \frac{\nabla u_{n} (x, y)}{\sqrt{1 + | \nabla u_{n} (x, y) |^{2}}} \right) \equiv 0$$

and

$$\Sigma_{n} = \left\{ (x, y, u_{n}(x, y)) \in \mathbb{R}^{3} \left| - \frac{\pi}{2} < x, y < + \frac{\pi}{2} \right. \right\}.$$

What, if anything, is the limiting surface as *n* tends to infinity? The answer was given by H. Scherk in 1834: the limiting surface Σ is the graph of

$$u : \left( - \frac{\pi}{2}, + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) \times \left( - \frac{\pi}{2}, + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) \to \mathbb{R},$$

$$u(x, y) = \log \left( \frac{\cos (x)}{\cos (y)} \right).$$

That is, the **Scherk surface** over the square is

$$\Sigma = \left\{ \left. \left(x, y, \log \left( \frac{\cos (x)}{\cos (y)} \right) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{3} \right| - \frac{\pi}{2} < x, y < + \frac{\pi}{2} \right\}.$$

### More general Scherk surfaces {#More_general_Scherk_surfaces}

One can consider similar minimal surface problems on other [quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/25278) in the Euclidean plane. One can also consider the same problem on quadrilaterals in the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/318742). In 2006, Harold Rosenberg and Pascal Collin used hyperbolic Scherk surfaces to construct a harmonic diffeomorphism from the complex plane onto the hyperbolic plane (the unit disc with the hyperbolic metric), thereby disproving the [Schoen–Yau conjecture](wikipedia://en/8238857).

## Scherk's second surface {#Scherk's_second_surface}

*Image: Scherk's second surface.png.* Scherk's second surface

*Image: Scherk-2 surface unit cell.stl.* STL unit cell of the second Scherk surface

Scherk's second surface looks globally like two orthogonal planes whose intersection consists of a sequence of tunnels in alternating directions. Its intersections with horizontal planes consists of alternating hyperbolas.

It has implicit equation:

$$\sin(z) - \sinh(x)\sinh(y)=0$$

It has the [Weierstrass–Enneper parameterization](wikipedia://en/2996193) $f(z) = \frac{4}{1-z^4}$, $g(z) = iz$ and can be parametrized as:[^o10]

$$x(r,\theta) = 2 \Re ( \ln(1+re^{i \theta}) - \ln(1-re^{i \theta}) ) = \ln \left( \frac{1+r^2+2r \cos \theta}{1+r^2-2r \cos \theta} \right)$$

$$y(r,\theta) = \Re ( 4i \tan^{-1}(re^{i \theta})) = \ln \left( \frac{1+r^2-2r \sin\theta}{1+r^2+2r \sin \theta} \right)$$

$$z(r,\theta) = \Re ( 2i(-\ln(1-r^2e^{2i \theta}) + \ln(1+r^2e^{2i \theta}) ) = 2 \tan^{-1}\left( \frac{2 r^2 \sin 2\theta}{r^4-1} \right)$$

for $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$ and $r \in (0,1)$. This gives one period of the surface, which can then be extended in the z-direction by symmetry.

The surface has been generalised by H. Karcher into the [saddle tower](wikipedia://en/36763480) family of periodic minimal surfaces.

Somewhat confusingly, this surface is occasionally called Scherk's fifth surface in the literature.[^o11][^o12] To minimize confusion it is useful to refer to it as Scherk's singly periodic surface or the Scherk-tower.
