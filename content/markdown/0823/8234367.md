# Tree-graded space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tree-graded space"}

A [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) $X$ is called a ***tree-graded space*** with respect to a collection of [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) proper subsets called *pieces*, if any two distinct pieces [intersect](wikipedia://en/23476429) in at most one point, and every non-trivial simple [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) of $X$ is contained in one of the pieces.

:::

Tree-graded spaces behave like [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914) "up to what can happen within the pieces", while allowing non-tree-like behavior within the pieces. For example, any [topologically embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_embedding) circle is contained in a piece; there is a well-defined [projection](wikipedia://en/37259262) on every piece, such that every [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected) subset meeting a piece in at most one point projects to a unique point on that piece; the space is naturally [fibered](wikipedia://en/316826) into real trees that are [transverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversality_%28mathematics%29) to pieces; and pieces can be "merged along embedded paths" in a way that preserves a tree-graded structure.

Tree-graded spaces were introduced by (txt) in their study of the [asymptotic cones](wikipedia://en/18609360#Asymptotic_cones) of [relatively hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817). This point of view allows for a notion of relative hyperbolicity that makes sense for [geodesic metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space) and which is invariant under [quasi-isometries](wikipedia://en/12747259).

For instance, a [CAT(0) group](wikipedia://en/12942017) has [isolated flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_group_with_isolated_flats), if and only if all its asymptotic cones are tree-graded metric spaces all of whose pieces are isometric to [euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697).[^o3]
