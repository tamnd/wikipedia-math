# Paco Lagerstrom

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Paco Lagerstrom"}

**Paco Axel Lagerstrom** (February 24, 1914 – February 16, 1989) was a Swedish-American applied mathematician and aeronautical engineer. He was trained formally in mathematics, but worked for much of his career in aeronautical applications. He was known for work in applying the method of [asymptotic expansion](wikipedia://en/642090) to fluid mechanics problems.[^o5] Several of his works have become classics, including "Matched Asymptotic Expansions: Ideas And Techniques".[^o6]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born on February 24, 1914, in [Oskarshamn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskarshamn), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden).

Lagerstrom earned bachelor's and master's degrees, in 1935 and 1939 respectively, at the [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University).[^o2] He then came to America as a graduate student at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), earning a PhD in 1942 in mathematics under [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847)[^o3] with a dissertation entitled "Measure and Integral in Partially Ordered Spaces". During this time, Lagerstrom was also a mathematics instructor.

He left Princeton in 1944 to work briefly at [Bell Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Aircraft) in Niagara Falls, New York until 1945,[^o2] after which he worked for a similarly brief period at [Douglas Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Aircraft_Company) in Santa Monica.[^o8] While he had already published significant results in pure mathematics,[^o9][^o10] he was, by this time, firmly interested in its applications to fluid dynamic and aerodynamic problems. In 1946, Lagerstrom was recruited by [Hans Liepmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_W._Liepmann) to the [Guggenheim Aeronautical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Aeronautical_Laboratory) at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology).[^o8] He was later promoted to Professor of Aeronautics in 1952 and Professor of Applied Mathematics in 1967,[^o8] having departed only briefly to the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in 1960–1961 as visiting professor on a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Guggenheim_Fellowships_awarded_in_1960).[^o2]

He died on February 16, 1989.[^o11]

## Publications {#Publications}

His book "Laminar flow theory", initially published in 1964 in the Theory of Laminar flows, edited by F.K.Moore, is still considered as the standard textbook for fluid mechanics.
