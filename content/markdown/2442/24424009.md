# Herbert Keller

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herbert Keller"}

**Herbert Bishop Keller** (19 June 1925 in [Paterson, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paterson,_New_Jersey) – 26 January 2008 in [Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California))[^o3] was an American [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [numerical analyst](wikipedia://en/21506). He was professor of applied mathematics, emeritus, at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Keller graduated from the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology) with a bachelor's in electrical engineering in 1945; and from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), later known as the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute), with a Ph.D. in 1954.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

A Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), Keller was known for his contributions to the field of applied mathematics and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) including [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042), [computational fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/305924) (CFD), and [scientific computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computing). He is the co-author, with [Eugene Isaacson](wikipedia://en/57110772), of the classic textbook *Analysis of Numerical Methods*, which is still in use in leading graduate programs in applied mathematics.[^o8] It is interesting to observe that the first letters of each sentence in the Preface spell "Down With Computers And Their Lackeys." He served as president of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (1974–1976).[^o9]

Keller was a recipient of the [Theodore von Kármán Prize](wikipedia://en/24419775) of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) in 1994.[^o10] Herbert was the younger brother of applied mathematician [Joseph B. Keller](wikipedia://en/4454126).[^o11] Joseph Keller was also a co-advisor for Herbert's PhD thesis.

Keller was an avid bicyclist even at 82 years old. He died after his regular Saturday morning bike ride when he took a dip in a hot tub to relax his muscles, lost consciousness due to hyperthermia, and drowned.
