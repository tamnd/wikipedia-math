# Jacques Hurtubise (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacques Hurtubise"}

**Jacques Claude Hurtubise** [FRSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) (born March 12, 1957) is a Canadian mathematician who works as a professor of mathematics and chair of the mathematics department at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University). His research interests include [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609), [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213), and [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181).[^o2] Among other contributions, he is known for proving the [Atiyah–Jones conjecture](wikipedia://en/34995804) (in collaboration with Boyer, Mann, and Milgram).[^o6][^o3]

:::

After undergraduate studies at the [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal), Hurtubise became a [Rhodes Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholarship) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) for 1978–1981,[^o2] and earned a DPhil from Oxford in 1982, supervised by [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164), with a dissertation concerning links between [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). Following his DPhil, he taught at the [Université du Québec à Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_du_Québec_à_Montréal) until 1988, when he moved to McGill. He has also been director of the [Centre de Recherches Mathématiques](wikipedia://en/3505436).[^o5]

Hurtubise won the Coxeter–James Prize of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) in 1993, and was an [AMS Centennial Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMS_Centennial_Fellowship) for 1993–1994. In 2004 he became a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada),[^o6] and in 2012, he became one of the inaugural fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7] In 2018 the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) listed him in their inaugural class of fellows.[^o8]

In 2022 was the recipient of the 2022 David Borwein Distinguished Career Award by the Canadian Mathematical Society (CMS), "for his exceptional, continued, and broad contributions to mathematics".[^o9]
