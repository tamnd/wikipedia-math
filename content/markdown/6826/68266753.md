# Jin-Yi Cai

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jin-Yi Cai"}

**Jin-Yi Cai** (; born 1961) is a Chinese American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). He is a professor of computer science, and also the [Steenbock Professor of Mathematical Sciences](https://research.wisc.edu/professorships-and-faculty-fellowships/steenbock-professorships/)[^o2] at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). His research is in [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392), especially [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543). In recent years he has concentrated on the classification of computational [counting problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counting_problem_%28complexity%29), especially counting [graph homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/676328), counting [constraint satisfaction problems](wikipedia://en/211652), and Holant problems as related to [holographic algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_algorithm).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Cai was born in [Shanghai, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai,_China). He studied mathematics at [Fudan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fudan_University), graduating in 1981. He earned a master's degree at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) in 1983, a second master's degree at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1985,[^o6] and his Ph.D. from Cornell in 1986, with [Juris Hartmanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Hartmanis) as his [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

He became a faculty member at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) (1986-1989), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) (1989-1993), and [SUNY Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY-Buffalo) (1993-2000), rising from [Assistant Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) to [Full Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Professor) in 1996. He became a Professor of Computer Science at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 2000.[^o6]

## Awards {#Awards}

Cai was a [Presidential Young Investigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Young_Investigator_Award), [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship),[^o8] and a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o9] He received a [Morningside Silver Medal](wikipedia://en/39615068), and a [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award) for Senior U.S. Scientists.[^o6] He was jointly awarded the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) in 2021, an award in theoretical computer science for his work in the paper titled: *Complexity of Counting CSP with Complex Weights.*[^o10] He was also awarded the [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) in Discrete Mathematics awarded by the American Mathematical Society and the Mathematical Programming Society.[^o11]

He was elected a Fellow of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) (2001), [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (2007), and a foreign member of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) (2017).[^o6] He was named to the 2023 class of Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), "for contributions to computational complexity theory, especially in the areas of complexity dichotomy".[^o12]
