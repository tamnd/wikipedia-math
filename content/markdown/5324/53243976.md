# Gustav Conrad Bauer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gustav Conrad Bauer"}

**Gustav Conrad Bauer** (18 November 1820, [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg) – 3 April 1906, [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich)) was a German mathematician,[^o2] known for the Bauer-Muir transformation[^o3][^o4] and Bauer's conic sections. He earned a footnote in the history of science as the doctoral advisor (*Doktorvater*) of [Heinrich Burkhardt](wikipedia://en/19931824), who became one of the two referees of [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s doctoral dissertation.

:::

## Education and family {#Education_and_family}

Gustav Bauer passed in 1837 his *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur)* at Augsburg's [Gymnasium bei St. Anna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_bei_St._Anna). He continued his studies of mathematics at the Polytechnischen Schule Augsburg and also the universities of [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen), [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). At Humboldt University in Berlin, Bauer received in 1842 his [Promotierung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_%28Germany%29) under [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002). From 1842 Gustav Bauer continued his studies in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) under [Joseph Liouville](wikipedia://en/341810), as well as other mathematicians.

In 1862 Gustav Bauer married Amalie, daughter of the *Archivrat* and Professor Honorarius Nathanael von Schlichtegroll. The marriage produced two daughters and a son Gustav junior, who became a well-known engineer.

## Professional career {#Professional_career}

At the beginning of his professional employment, Bauer applied for a civil service position as a schoolteacher but became a private tutor from 1845 to 1853 in the royal house of Prince [Mihail Sturdza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihail_Sturdza) and his successor Prince [Grigore Alexandru Ghica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigore_Alexandru_Ghica) in what is now Rumania. In 1857 Bauer spent three months in England and upon his return to Germany became a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) for the Mathematics Faculty of the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München). There he received his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) and became in 1865 professor extraordinarius, in 1869 professor ordinarius, and in 1900 professor emeritus.

Bauer's mathematical research dealt with algebra, geometric problems, [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056), the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), and [generalized continued fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continued_fraction). In 1871 Bauer was elected a full member of the [Bayerische Akademie der Wissenschaften](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayerische_Akademie_der_Wissenschaften). In 1884 he was elected a member of the [Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina). His doctoral students include [Heinrich Burkhardt](wikipedia://en/19931824), [Eduard Ritter von Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Ritter_von_Weber), and Christian August Vogler.

## Footnotes in the history of mathematics {#Footnotes_in_the_history_of_mathematics}

In [Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717)'s first letter to [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599), one of the theorems that impressed Hardy was:

$$1 - 5\left(\frac12\right)^3 + 9\left(\frac{1\times3}{2\times4}\right)^3 - 13\left(\frac{1\times3\times5}{2\times4\times6}\right)^3 + \cdots = \frac{2}{\pi}$$

However, Bauer proved the theorem in 1859.[^o9][^o10] Using a result of Bauer on generalized continued fractions, [Oskar Perron](wikipedia://en/1485469) published in 1952 the first proof of another formula of Ramanujan.[^o2][^o11]
