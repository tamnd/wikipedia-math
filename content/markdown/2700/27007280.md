# Paul Finsler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Finsler"}

**Paul Finsler** (11 April 1894 – 29 April 1970) was a German and Swiss [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Born in [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), Finsler did his undergraduate studies at the [Technische Hochschule Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Stuttgart),[^o3] and his graduate studies at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he received his Ph.D. in 1919 under the supervision of [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625).[^o4] He studied for his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne), receiving it in 1922.[^o3] He joined the faculty of the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) in 1927, and was promoted to [ordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_professor) there in 1944.[^o3] He died on 29 April 1970.

Finsler's thesis work concerned [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and [Finsler spaces](wikipedia://en/385510) were named after him by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) in 1934.[^o3] The [Hadwiger–Finsler inequality](wikipedia://en/12802352), a relation between the side lengths and area of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), is named after Finsler and his co-author [Hugo Hadwiger](wikipedia://en/630451), as is the [Finsler–Hadwiger theorem](wikipedia://en/27007324) on a square derived from two other squares that share a vertex.[^o5] Finsler is also known for his work on the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358), developing a [non-well-founded set theory](wikipedia://en/1091767) with which he hoped to resolve the contradictions implied by [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095).[^o3][^o6]
