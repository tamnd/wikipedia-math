# Giulio Ascoli

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Giulio Ascoli"}

**Giulio Ascoli** (20 January 1843, [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) – 12 July 1896, [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan)) was a [Jewish-Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Jews) mathematician. He was a student of the [Scuola Normale di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa), where he graduated in 1868.

:::

In 1872 he became Professor of Algebra and Calculus of the [Politecnico di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politecnico_di_Milano) University. From 1879 he was professor of mathematics at the Reale Istituto Tecnico Superiore, where, in 1901, was affixed a plaque that remembers him.

He was also a corresponding member of Istituto Lombardo.

He made contributions to the [theory of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_%28mathematics%29) of a real variable and to [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038). For example, Ascoli introduced [equicontinuity](wikipedia://en/460642) in 1884, a topic regarded as one of the fundamental concepts in the theory of real functions.[^o4] In 1889, Italian mathematician [Cesare Arzelà](wikipedia://en/1683941) generalized Ascoli's Theorem into the [Arzelà–Ascoli theorem](wikipedia://en/596622), a practical sequential compactness criterion of functions.[^o5]
