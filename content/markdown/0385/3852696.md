# Compact quantum group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compact quantum group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **compact quantum groups** are generalisations of [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326), where the commutative [$\mathrm{C}^*$-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) of continuous complex-valued functions on a compact group is generalised to an [abstract structure](wikipedia://en/359377) on a not-necessarily commutative unital $\mathrm{C}^*$-algebra, which plays the role of the "algebra of continuous complex-valued functions on the compact quantum group".[^o2]

:::

The basic motivation for this theory comes from the following analogy. The space of complex-valued functions on a compact Hausdorff [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) forms a *commutative* C\*-algebra. On the other hand, by the [Gelfand Theorem](wikipedia://en/364372), a commutative C\*-algebra is isomorphic to the C\*-algebra of continuous complex-valued functions on a compact Hausdorff topological space, and the topological space is uniquely determined by the C\*-algebra up to [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660).

[S. L. Woronowicz](wikipedia://en/7242261)[^o3] introduced the important concept of **compact matrix quantum groups**, which he initially called **compact pseudogroups**. Compact matrix quantum groups are abstract structures on which the "continuous functions" on the structure are given by elements of a C\*-algebra. The geometry of a compact matrix quantum group is a special case of a [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

For a compact [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315), $G$, there exists a C\*-algebra homomorphism

$$\Delta : C(G) \to C(G) \otimes C(G)$$

where $C(G) \otimes C(G)$ is the minimal C\*-algebra tensor product — the completion of the algebraic [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of $C(G)$ and $C(G)$ — such that

$$\Delta(f)(x,y) = f(xy)$$

for all $f \in C(G)$, and for all $x, y \in G$, where

$$(f \otimes g)(x,y) = f(x) g(y)$$

for all $f, g \in C(G)$ and all $x, y \in G$. There also exists a linear multiplicative mapping

$$\kappa : C(G) \to C(G),$$

such that

$$\kappa(f)(x) = f(x^{-1})$$

for all $f \in C(G)$ and all $x \in G$. Strictly speaking, this does not make $C(G)$ into a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714), unless $G$ is finite.

On the other hand, a finite-dimensional [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of $G$ can be used to generate a [\*-subalgebra](wikipedia://en/293426) of $C(G)$ which is also a Hopf \*-algebra. Specifically, if

$$g \mapsto (u_{ij}(g))_{i,j}$$

is an $n$-dimensional representation of $G$, then

$$u_{ij} \in C(G)$$

for all $i, j$, and

$$\Delta(u_{ij}) = \sum_k u_{ik} \otimes u_{kj}$$

for all $i, j$. It follows that the [\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/293426) generated by $u_{ij}$ for all $i, j$ and $\kappa(u_{ij})$ for all $i, j$ is a Hopf \*-algebra: the counit is determined by

$$\epsilon(u_{ij}) = \delta_{ij}$$

for all $i, j$ (where $\delta_{ij}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890)), the antipode is $\kappa$, and the unit is given by

$$1 = \sum_k u_{1k} \kappa(u_{k1}) = \sum_k \kappa(u_{1k}) u_{k1}.$$

:::

## Compact matrix quantum groups {#Compact_matrix_quantum_groups}

As a generalization, a **compact matrix quantum group** is defined as a pair $(C, u)$, where $C$ is a C\*-algebra and

$$u = (u_{ij})_{i,j = 1,\dots,n}$$

is a matrix with entries in $C$ such that

- The \*-subalgebra, $C_{0}$, of $C$, which is generated by the matrix elements of $u$, is dense in $C$;
- There exists a C\*-algebra homomorphism, called the comultiplication, $\Delta : C \to C \otimes C$ (here $C \otimes C$ is the C\*-algebra tensor product - the completion of the algebraic tensor product of $C$ and $C$) such that

$$\forall i, j: \qquad \Delta(u_{ij}) = \sum_k u_{ik} \otimes u_{kj};$$

- There exists a linear antimultiplicative map, called the coinverse, $\kappa : C_{0} \to C_{0}$ such that $\kappa(\kappa(v*)*) = v$ for all $v \in C_0$ and $\sum_k \kappa(u_{ik}) u_{kj} = \sum_k u_{ik} \kappa(u_{kj}) = \delta_{ij} I,$ where $I$ is the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of $C$. Since $\kappa$ is antimultiplicative, $\kappa(vw) = \kappa(w)\kappa(v)$ for all $v, w \in C_0$.

As a consequence of continuity, the comultiplication on $C$ is coassociative.

In general, $C$ is a bialgebra, and $C_{0}$ is a Hopf \*-algebra.

Informally, $C$ can be regarded as the \*-algebra of continuous complex-valued functions over the compact matrix quantum group, and $u$ can be regarded as a finite-dimensional representation of the compact matrix quantum group.

## Compact quantum groups {#Compact_quantum_groups}

For C\*-algebras $A$ and $B$ acting on the Hilbert spaces $H$ and $K$ respectively, their minimal tensor product is defined to be the norm completion of the algebraic tensor product $A \otimes B$ in $B(H \otimes K)$; the norm completion is also denoted by $A \otimes B$.

A compact quantum group[^o8][^o9] is defined as a pair $(C, \Delta)$, where $C$ is a unital C\*-algebra and

- $\Delta : C \to C \otimes C$ is a unital \*-homomorphism satisfying $(\Delta \otimes \operatorname{id}) \Delta = (\operatorname{id} \otimes \Delta) \Delta$;
- the sets $\{(C \otimes 1) \Delta(C)\}$ and $\{(1 \otimes C) \Delta(C)\}$ are dense in $C \otimes C$.

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Representations"}

## Representations {#Representations}

A representation of the compact matrix quantum group is given by a [corepresentation](wikipedia://en/310886) of the Hopf \*-algebra[^o12] Furthermore, a representation, *v*, is called unitary if the matrix for *v* is unitary, or equivalently, if

$$\forall i, j: \qquad \kappa(v_{ij}) = v^*_{ji}.$$

:::

## Example {#Example}

An example of a compact matrix quantum group is $\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2)$,[^o15] where the parameter $\mu$ is a positive real number.

### First definition {#First_definition}

$\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2) = (C(\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2)), u)$, where $C(\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2))$ is the C\*-algebra generated by $\alpha$ and $\gamma$, subject to

$$\gamma \gamma^* = \gamma^* \gamma, \ \alpha \gamma = \mu \gamma \alpha, \ \alpha \gamma^* = \mu \gamma^* \alpha, \ \alpha \alpha^* + \mu \gamma^* \gamma = \alpha^* \alpha + \mu^{-1} \gamma^* \gamma = I,$$

and

$$u = \left( \begin{matrix} \alpha & \gamma \\ - \gamma^* & \alpha^* \end{matrix} \right),$$

so that the comultiplication is determined by $\Delta(\alpha) = \alpha \otimes \alpha - \gamma \otimes \gamma^*, \Delta(\gamma) = \alpha \otimes \gamma + \gamma \otimes \alpha^*$, and the coinverse is determined by $\kappa(\alpha) = \alpha^*, \kappa(\gamma) = - \mu^{-1} \gamma, \kappa(\gamma^*) = - \mu \gamma^*, \kappa(\alpha^*) = \alpha$. Note that $u$ is a representation, but not a [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788). $u$ is equivalent to the unitary representation

$$v = \left( \begin{matrix} \alpha & \sqrt{\mu} \gamma \\ - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}} \gamma^* & \alpha^* \end{matrix} \right).$$

### Second definition {#Second_definition}

$\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2) = (C(\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2)), w)$, where $C(\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2))$ is the C\*-algebra generated by $\alpha$ and $\beta$, subject to

$$\beta \beta^* = \beta^* \beta, \ \alpha \beta = \mu \beta \alpha, \ \alpha \beta^* = \mu \beta^* \alpha, \ \alpha \alpha^* + \mu^2 \beta^* \beta = \alpha^* \alpha + \beta^* \beta = I,$$

and

$$w = \left( \begin{matrix} \alpha & \mu \beta \\ - \beta^* & \alpha^* \end{matrix} \right),$$

so that the comultiplication is determined by $\Delta(\alpha) = \alpha \otimes \alpha - \mu \beta \otimes \beta^*, \Delta(\beta) = \alpha \otimes \beta + \beta \otimes \alpha^*$, and the coinverse is determined by $\kappa(\alpha) = \alpha^*, \kappa(\beta) = - \mu^{-1} \beta, \kappa(\beta^*) = - \mu \beta^*$, $\kappa(\alpha^*) = \alpha$. Note that $w$ is a unitary representation. The realizations can be identified by equating $\gamma = \sqrt{\mu} \beta$.

### Limit case {#Limit_case}

If $\mu = 1$, then $\operatorname{SU}_{\mu}(2)$ is equal to the concrete compact group $\operatorname{SU}(2)$.

:::
