# Statistical epidemiology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical epidemiology"}

**Statistical epidemiology** is an emerging branch of the disciplines of [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) and [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) that aims to:

:::

- Bring more statistical rigour to bear in the field of [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997)
- Recognise the importance of applied [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), especially with respect to the context in which statistical methods are appropriate and inappropriate
- Aid and improve our interpretation of observations

## Introduction {#Introduction}

The science of [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) has had enormous growth, particularly with charity and government funding. Many researchers have been trained to conduct studies, requiring multiple skills ranging from liaising with clinical staff to the [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_analysis) of complex data, such as using [Bayesian methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_method). The role of a Statistical Epidemiologist is to bring the most appropriate methods available to bear on [observational study](wikipedia://en/6326483) from medical research, requiring a broad appreciation of the underpinning methods and their context of applicability and interpretation.

The earliest mention of this phrase was in an article by EB Wilson,[^o7] taking a critical look at the way in which statistical methods were developing and being applied in the science of epidemiology.

## Academic recognition {#Academic_recognition}

There are two Professors of Statistical Epidemiology in the United Kingdom ([University of Leeds](http://www.leeds.ac.uk/light/staff/Mark-S-Gilthorpe) and [Imperial College](http://www1.ic.ac.uk/medicine/research/researchthemes/publicandint/ide/research_groups/stats/), London) and a Statistical Epidemiology group ([Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford)).

## Related fields {#Related_fields}

Statistical epidemiology draws upon [quantitative methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_methods) from fields such as: [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).
