# Martin Kreuzer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Martin Kreuzer"}

**Martin Kreuzer** (born 15 July 1962 in [Ihrlerstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihrlerstein)) is a German mathematics professor and [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) player who holds the [chess titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_title) of [International Correspondence Chess Grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Correspondence_Chess_Grandmaster) and [FIDE Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Master).

:::

Kreuzer did his graduate studies in mathematics at the [University of Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Regensburg), located on the [Danube River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_River) in Bavaria. After spending one year in the United States as a foreign exchange student at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston), he finished his diploma in Mathematics in Regensburg in 1986. He received his doctorate of natural sciences with dissertation Vektorbündel und der Satz von Cayley-Bacharach from University of Regensburg in 1989, working under advisor [Ernst Kunz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kunz). Next came a post-doctoral fellowship at [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) in [Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario), Ontario, Canada, from 1989 to 1991, working in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) with Professor Anthony Geramita. He then returned to Germany, worked as a scientific assistant at the [University of Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Regensburg) and gained his habilitation in Mathematics in 1997. After substituting for the chair of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) at the [University of Bayreuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bayreuth) in 2000–2001 and for the chair of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) at [Technical University of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Dortmund) from 2002 to 2007, he moved to [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau) where he holds the chair of [symbolic computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_computation) at the [University of Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Passau). His main research interests are [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805), [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), computational commutative algebra, algebraic geometry, and their industrial applications.

Kreuzer's chess skills have earned him the [FIDE Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Master) title for over-the-board play. During his short stay in Canada, he finished fourth at the 1990 Open [Canadian Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Chess_Championship) at [Edmundston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmundston). Further notable over-the-board results include the prize for the best player without Elo number in the [Elo rating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system) at the 1987 Open "Chess for Peace" in London and a fourth place at the first Novotel Open in [Genova, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genova,_Italy) in 1997.

He gained the title of [International Correspondence Chess Grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Correspondence_Chess_Grandmaster)[^o4] (GMC) 1994, from his result in the 1988–95 Von Massow Memorial tournament. Kreuzer played board six in the finals, on the German team which shared the gold medal at the 11th Correspondence Chess Olympiad, 1992–1999. He was a member the German team which won the 12th Correspondence Chess Olympiad, 1998–2004, and a member of the German team which won the 13th Correspondence Chess Olympiad, 2004–2009.

A selection of his games can be found at [chessbase.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chessbase.com).
