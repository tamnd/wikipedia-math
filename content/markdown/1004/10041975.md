# Bridge number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bridge number"}

a mathematical concept

*Image: 2-bridge trefoil.svg.* A [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773), drawn with bridge number 2

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **bridge number**, also called the **bridge index**, is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) of a knot defined as the minimal number of bridges required in all the possible bridge representations of a knot.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a knot or link, draw a diagram of the link using the convention that a gap in the line denotes an undercrossing. Call an unbroken arc in this diagram a bridge if it includes at least one overcrossing. Then the bridge number of a knot can be found as the minimum number of bridges required for any diagram of the knot.[^o4] Bridge numbers were first studied in the 1950s by [Horst Schubert](wikipedia://en/38487829).[^o5] [^o6]

The bridge number can equivalently be defined geometrically instead of [topologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological). In bridge representation, a knot lies entirely in the plane apart for a finite number of bridges whose projections onto the plane are straight lines. Equivalently, the bridge number is the minimal number of local maxima of the projection of the knot onto a vector, where we minimize over all projections and over all conformations of the knot. In this context, the bridge number is often called the **crookedness***.*

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every non-trivial knot has bridge number at least two,[^o4] so the knots that minimize the bridge number (other than the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075)) are the [2-bridge knots](wikipedia://en/3396136). It can be shown that every n-bridge knot can be decomposed into two trivial n-[tangles](wikipedia://en/30858815) and hence 2-bridge knots are [rational knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_knot).

If K is the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of K<sub>1</sub> and K<sub>2</sub>, then the bridge number of K is one less than the sum of the bridge numbers of K<sub>1</sub> and K<sub>2</sub>.[^o9]

:::
