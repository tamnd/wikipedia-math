# Map projection of the triaxial ellipsoid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Map projection of the triaxial ellipsoid"}

In [geodesy](wikipedia://en/12608), a **map projection of the triaxial ellipsoid** maps Earth or some other [astronomical body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_body) modeled as a [triaxial ellipsoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triaxial_ellipsoid) to the plane. Such a model is called the [reference ellipsoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_ellipsoid). In most cases, reference ellipsoids are [spheroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spheroids), and sometimes [spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spheres). Massive objects have sufficient gravity to overcome their own rigidity and usually have an oblate ellipsoid shape. However, minor moons or [small solar system bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Solar_System_body) are not under [hydrostatic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/51776). Usually such bodies have irregular shapes. Furthermore, some of [gravitationally rounded objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_gravitationally_rounded_objects_of_the_Solar_System) may have a tri-axial ellipsoid shape due to rapid rotation (such as [Haumea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haumea)) or unidirectional strong tidal forces (such as [Io](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Io_%28moon%29)).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

A triaxial equivalent of the [Mercator projection](wikipedia://en/20771) was developed by [John P. Snyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_P._Snyder).[^o4]

Equidistant map projections of a triaxial ellipsoid were developed by Paweł Pędzich.[^o5]

Conic Projections of a triaxial ellipsoid were developed by Maxim Nyrtsov.[^o6]

Equal-area cylindrical and azimuthal projections of the triaxial ellipsoid were developed by Maxim Nyrtsov.[^o7]

Jacobi conformal projections were described by [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^o8]
