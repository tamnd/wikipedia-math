# Patrick X. Gallagher

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Patrick X. Gallagher"}

**Patrick Ximenes Gallagher** (January 2, 1935[^o3][^o4] – March 30, 2019)[^o4][^o5] was an American mathematician who pioneered [large sieve theory](wikipedia://en/1967859) and invented the [larger sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larger_sieve).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Patrick Ximenes Gallagher was born on January 2, 1935, in [Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey) to school superintendent Ralph P. Gallagher and elementary school teacher Natalie Forcheimer Gallagher.[^o3][^o8][^o9] Gallagher graduated from [Bound Brook High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_Brook_High_School) and received a scholarship from the Harvard Club of New Jersey to attend [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o9][^o12]

### Education {#Education}

In 1956, Gallagher received a B.A. degree *magna cum laude* from Harvard University.[^o11][^o9] At Harvard, he was a member of the Harvard Mathematics Club and [Eliot House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliot_House_%28Harvard_College%29) Mathematics-Physics Club and completed an undergraduate honors thesis entitled *On a property of some entire functions*.[^o12] In 1959, Gallagher received a PhD from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with a doctoral dissertation entitled *Metric Diophantine Approximation in One and Several Dimensions* completed under the supervision of [Donald C. Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer).

### Career {#Career}

After receiving his doctoral degree, Gallagher served as an instructor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1959 to 1961.[^o3] He spent one year living in the [Latin Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Quarter,_Paris) of Paris before becoming an assistant professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1962.[^o3][^o15] He moved from Columbia to become a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) for the 1964-1965 academic year.[^o3] From 1965 to 1972, he was an associate professor and then full professor at [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College).[^o3][^o11]

In 1972, Gallagher moved back to Columbia University as a professor of mathematics.[^o16][^o17][^o3] Gallagher received the Columbia University Presidential Teaching Award in 2005[^o11] and became director of undergraduate studies in the department of mathematics in 2013.[^o16][^o17] He retired from Columbia in 2017 and was professor emeritus until his death in 2019.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

In the 1960s and 1970s, Gallagher proved several results in [large sieve methods](wikipedia://en/1967859) in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and simplified key ingredients used in the proof of the [Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem](wikipedia://en/4632596).[^o19][^o20] He also applied the large sieve to study the asymptotics of [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673) of monic integral polynomials of bounded [height](wikipedia://en/3141761#N), improving on results by [van der Waerden](wikipedia://en/91390).[^o21][^o22]

In 1971, he invented the [larger sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larger_sieve).[^o23]

## Family {#Family}

Gallagher met his wife, Minh Chau Gallagher, while he was an instructor at MIT in 1960.[^o15] Minh Chau was born in [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi) to Roman Catholic parents.[^o25] They had two sons together.[^o15]
