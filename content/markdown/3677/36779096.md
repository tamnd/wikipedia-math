# Hemiperfect number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hemiperfect number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **hemiperfect number** is a [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) with a half-integer [abundancy index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abundancy_index). In other words, *σ*(*n*)/*n* = *k*/2 for an odd integer *k*, where *σ*(*n*) is the [sum-of-divisors function](wikipedia://en/407249), the sum of all positive [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) of *n*.

:::

The first few hemiperfect numbers are:

2, 24, 4320, 4680, 26208, 8910720, 17428320, 20427264, 91963648, 197064960, ...

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

24 is a hemiperfect number because the sum of the divisors of 24 is

1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 12 + 24 = 60 = $\tfrac{5}{2}$ × 24.

The abundancy index is 5/2 which is a half-integer.

:::

## Smallest hemiperfect numbers of abundancy *k*/2 {#Smallest_hemiperfect_numbers_of_abundancy_k/2}

The following table gives an overview of the smallest hemiperfect numbers of abundancy *k*/2 for *k* ≤ 13 :

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| *k* | Smallest number of abundancy *k*/2 | Number of digits |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29) | 1 |
| 5 | [24](wikipedia://en/362201) | 2 |
| 7 | 4320 | 4 |
| 9 | 8910720 | 7 |
| 11 | 17116004505600 | 14 |
| 13 | 170974031122008628879954060917200710847692800 | 45 |

:::

The current best known upper bounds for the smallest numbers of abundancy 15/2 and 17/2 were found by Michel Marcus.[^o6]

The smallest known number of abundancy 15/2 is ≈ \1.274947 × 10<sup>88</sup>, and the smallest known number of abundancy 17/2 is ≈ \2.717290 × 10<sup>190</sup>.[^o6]

There are no known numbers of abundancy 19/2.[^o6]
