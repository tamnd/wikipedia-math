# Kathryn Roeder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kathryn Roeder"}

**Kathryn M. Roeder** is an American statistician known for her development of statistical methods to uncover the genetic basis of complex disease and her contributions to mixture models, semiparametric inference, and multiple testing. Roeder holds positions as professor of statistics and professor of [computational biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology) at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), where she leads a project focused on discovering genes associated with [autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autism).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Roeder did her undergraduate studies at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho), where she graduated in 1982 with a bachelor's degree in wildlife resources. Roeder worked as a biologist for a year in the Pacific Northwest before returning to academia for graduate studies in statistics. She completed her Ph.D. in 1988 at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University); her dissertation, supervised by [Bruce G. Lindsay](wikipedia://en/46804348), was *Method of Spacings for Semiparametric Inference*.

Roeder joined the faculty of [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1988 and earned tenure there. She remained at Yale until 1994, when she moved to the statistics department at Carnegie Mellon. She added a second appointment in [computational biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology) in 1998, and served a term as Vice Provost for Faculty from 2015 to 2019.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1995 Roeder became an elected member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute). She was elected a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1996. In 1997 she received two major awards from the [Committee of Presidents of Statistical Societies](wikipedia://en/44155344): the Presidents' Award "in recognition of outstanding contributions to the profession of statistics", and the [George W. Snedecor Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Snedecor_Award), for her work in [biometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometry) with Bruce Lindsay and [Raymond J. Carroll](wikipedia://en/39341779). In the same year she was elected as a fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), and in 1999 gave the Medallion Lecture of the Institute of Mathematical Statistics. She won the Janet L Norwood Award for outstanding achievement by a woman in the statistical sciences in 2013. Roeder was elected to the National Academy of Sciences and as a fellow [^o5] of the American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS) in 2019. She was awarded the 2020 [R. A. Fisher Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher_Lectureship).

## Personal {#Personal}

Roeder is married to [Bernard J. Devlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_J._Devlin), a psychiatrist at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), and has worked with him on research involving genetics and autism.
