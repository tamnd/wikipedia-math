# Acylindrically hyperbolic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Acylindrically hyperbolic group"}

In the mathematical subject of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), an **acylindrically hyperbolic group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) admitting a non-elementary 'acylindrical' isometric [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on some geodesic [hyperbolic metric space](wikipedia://en/1285375).[^o2] This notion generalizes the notions of a [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817) and of a [relatively hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/21993809) and includes a significantly wider class of examples, such as [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295) and [Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*)](wikipedia://en/12747184).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

### Acylindrical action {#Acylindrical_action}

Let *G* be a group with an isometric action on some geodesic [hyperbolic metric space](wikipedia://en/1285375) *X*. This action is called **acylindrical**[^o2] if for every $R\ge 0$ there exist $N>0, L>0$ such that for every $x,y\in X$ with $d(x,y)\ge L$ one has

$$\# \{g\in G\mid d(x,gx)\le R, d(y,gy)\le R\} \le N.$$

If the above property holds for a specific $R\ge 0$, the action of *G* on *X* is called *R*-**acylindrical**. The notion of acylindricity provides a suitable substitute for being a [proper action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_action) in the more general context where non-proper actions are allowed.

An acylindrical isometric action of a group *G* on a geodesic [hyperbolic metric space](wikipedia://en/1285375) *X* is **non-elementary** if **G** admits two independent [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1285375#The_action_of_isometries_on_the_boundary_and_their_classification) isometries of *X*, that is, two loxodromic elements $g,h\in G$ such that their fixed point sets $\{g^+,g^-\}\subseteq \partial X$ and $\{h^+,h^-\}\subseteq \partial X$ are disjoint.

It is known (Theorem 1.1 in [^o2]) that an acylindrical action of a group *G* on a geodesic hyperbolic metric space *X* is non-elementary if and only if this action has unbounded orbits in *X* and the group *G* is not a finite extension of a cyclic group generated by loxodromic isometry of *X*.

### Acylindrically hyperbolic group {#Acylindrically_hyperbolic_group}

A group *G* is called **acylindrically hyperbolic** if *G* admits a non-elementary acylindrical isometric action on some geodesic hyperbolic metric space *X*.

:::

## Equivalent characterizations {#Equivalent_characterizations}

It is known (Theorem 1.2 in [^o2]) that for a group *G* the following conditions are equivalent:

- The group *G* is acylindrically hyperbolic.
- There exists a (possibly infinite) generating set *S* for *G*, such that the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) $\Gamma(G,S)$ is hyperbolic, and the natural translation action of *G* on $\Gamma(G,S)$ is a non-elementary acylindrical action.
- The group *G* is not [virtually cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtually_cyclic_group), and there exists an isometric action of *G* on a geodesic hyperbolic metric space *X* such that at least one element of *G* acts on *X* with the WPD ('Weakly Properly Discontinuous') property.
- The group *G* contains a proper infinite 'hyperbolically embedded' [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011).[^o8]

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every acylindrically hyperbolic group *G* is [SQ-universal](wikipedia://en/5725210), that is, every countable group embeds as a subgroup in some [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) of *G*.
- The class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups is closed under taking infinite [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918), and, more generally, under taking 's-normal' subgroups.[^o2] Here a subgroup $H\le G$ is called *s-normal* in $G$ if for every $g\in G$ one has $|H\cap g^{-1}Hg|=\infty$.
- If *G* is an acylindrically hyperbolic group and $V=\mathbb R$ or $V=\ell^p(G)$ with $p\in [1,\infty)$ then the [bounded cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_cohomology) $H_b(G,V)$ is infinite-dimensional.[^o12][^o13][^o2]
- Every acylindrically hyperbolic group *G* admits a unique maximal normal finite subgroup denoted *K(G)*.[^o8]
- If *G* is an acylindrically hyperbolic group with *K(G)={1}* then *G* has infinite conjugacy classes of nontrivial elements, *G* is not inner amenable, and the reduced [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) of *G* is simple with unique trace.[^o8]
- There is a version of [small cancellation theory](wikipedia://en/19882883) over acylindrically hyperbolic groups, allowing one to produce many quotients of such groups with prescribed properties.[^o14]
- Every finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group has cut points in all of its [asymptotic cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_cone).[^o15]
- For a finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group *G*, the probability that the [simple random walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_random_walk) on *G* of length *n* produces a 'generalized loxodromic element' in *G* converges to 1 exponentially fast as $n\to\infty$. [^o16]
- Every finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group *G* has exponential conjugacy growth, meaning that the number of distinct [conjugacy classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugacy_classes) of elements of *G* coming from the ball of radius *n* in the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of *G* grows exponentially in *n*. [^o17]

:::

## Examples and non-examples {#Examples_and_non-examples}

- Finite groups, virtually nilpotent groups and virtually solvable groups are not acylindrically hyperbolic.
- Every non-elementary subgroup of a [word-hyperbolic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group) is acylindrically hyperbolic.
- Every non-elementary [relatively hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/21993809) is acylindrically hyperbolic.
- The [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) $MCG(S_{g,p})$ of a connected oriented surface of genus $g\ge 0$ with $p\ge 0$ punctures is acylindrically hyperbolic, except for the cases where $g=0, p\le 3$ (in those exceptional cases the mapping class group is finite).[^o2]
- For $n\ge 2$ the group [Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*)](wikipedia://en/12747184) is acylindrically hyperbolic.[^o2]
- By a result of [Osin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Osin), every non virtually cyclic group *G*, that admits a proper isometric action on a proper [CAT(0) space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) with *G* having at least one rank-1 element, is acylindrically hyperbolic.[^o2] Caprace and Sageev proved that if *G* is a finitely generated group acting isometrically properly discontinuously and cocompactly on a geodetically complete [CAT(0) cubical complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) *X*, then either *X* splits as a direct product of two unbounded convex subcomplexes, or *G* contains a rank-1 element. [^o19]
- Every right-angled Artin group *G*, which is not cyclic and which is directly indecomposable, is acylindrically hyperbolic.
- For $n\ge 3$ the [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) $SL(n,\mathbb Z)$ is not acylindrically hyperbolic (Example 7.5 in [^o2]).
- For $m\ne 0, n\ne 0$ the [Baumslag–Solitar group](wikipedia://en/11485892) $BS(m,n)=\langle a,t\mid t^{-1}a^mt=a^n\rangle$ is not acylindrically hyperbolic. (Example 7.4 in [^o2])
- Many groups admitting nontrivial actions on simplicial trees (that is, admitting nontrivial splittings as fundamental groups of graphs of groups in the sense of [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708)) are acylindrically hyperbolic. For example, all one-relator groups on at least three generators are acylindrically hyperbolic.[^o20]
- Most 3-manifold groups are acylindrically hyperbolic.[^o20]
