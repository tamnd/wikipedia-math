# De Rham invariant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De Rham invariant"}

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the **de Rham invariant** is a mod 2 invariant of a (4*k*+1)-dimensional manifold, that is, an element of $\mathbf{Z}/2$ – either 0 or 1. It can be thought of as the simply-connected *symmetric* [L-group](wikipedia://en/696568) $L^{4k+1},$ and thus analogous to the other invariants from L-theory: the [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311), a 4*k*-dimensional invariant (either symmetric or quadratic, $L^{4k} \cong L_{4k}$), and the [Kervaire invariant](wikipedia://en/13816037), a (4*k*+2)-dimensional *quadratic* invariant $L_{4k+2}.$

:::

It is named for Swiss mathematician [Georges de Rham](wikipedia://en/398699), and used in [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115).[^o2][^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The de Rham invariant of a (4*k*+1)-dimensional manifold can be defined in various equivalent ways:[^o6]

- the rank of the 2-torsion in $H_{2k}(M),$ as an integer mod 2;
- the [Stiefel–Whitney number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiefel–Whitney_number) $w_2w_{4k-1}$;
- the (squared) Wu number, $v_{2k}Sq^1v_{2k},$ where $v_{2k} \in H^{2k}(M;Z_2)$ is the [Wu class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_class) of the normal bundle of $M$ and $Sq^1$ is the [Steenrod square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steenrod_square); formally, as with all [characteristic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_number), this is evaluated on the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440): $(v_{2k}Sq^1v_{2k},[M])$;
- in terms of a [semicharacteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semicharacteristic).

:::
