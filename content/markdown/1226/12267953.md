# 6-sphere coordinates

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="6-sphere coordinates"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **6-sphere coordinates** are a [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931) for three-dimensional space obtained by [inverting](wikipedia://en/295844) the 3D [Cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) across the [unit 2-sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) $x^2+y^2+z^2=1$. They are so named because the [loci](wikipedia://en/542465) where one coordinate is constant form [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) tangent to the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) from one of six sides (depending on which coordinate is held constant and whether its value is positive or negative). This coordinate system exists independently from and has no relation to the [6-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). The three coordinates are

:::

$$u = \frac{x}{x^2+y^2+z^2},\quad v = \frac{y}{x^2+y^2+z^2},\quad w = \frac{z}{x^2+y^2+z^2}.$$

Since inversion is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450), the equations for *x*, *y*, and *z* in terms of *u*, *v*, and *w* are similar:

$$x = \frac{u}{u^2+v^2+w^2},\quad y = \frac{v}{u^2+v^2+w^2},\quad z = \frac{w}{u^2+v^2+w^2}.$$

This coordinate system is [$R$-separable](wikipedia://en/6885778) for the 3-variable Laplace equation.
