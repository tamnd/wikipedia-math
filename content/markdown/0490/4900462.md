# Segal's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Segal's conjecture"}

**Segal's Burnside ring conjecture**, or, more briefly, the **Segal conjecture**, is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). The theorem relates the [Burnside ring](wikipedia://en/4933121) of a finite [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* to the [stable cohomotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomotopy_group) of the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) *BG*. The conjecture was made in the mid 1970s by [Graeme Segal](wikipedia://en/6713316) and proved in 1984 by [Gunnar Carlsson](wikipedia://en/35054518). This statement is still commonly referred to as the Segal conjecture, even though it now has the status of a theorem.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

The Segal conjecture has several different formulations, not all of which are equivalent. Here is a weak form: there exists, for every finite group *G*, an isomorphism

$$\varprojlim \pi_S^0 \left( BG^{(k)}_+ \right) \to \widehat{A}(G).$$

Here, lim denotes the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109), $\pi$<sub>S</sub>\* denotes the stable cohomotopy ring, *B* denotes the classifying space, the superscript *k* denotes the *k*-[skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex), and the subscript + denotes the addition of a disjoint basepoint. On the right-hand side, the hat denotes the [completion](wikipedia://en/60097#Completion) of the Burnside ring with respect to its [augmentation ideal](wikipedia://en/2224774).

:::

## The Burnside ring {#The_Burnside_ring}

The Burnside ring of a finite group *G* is constructed from the category of finite [*G*-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) as a [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790). More precisely, let *M*(*G*) be the commutative [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) of isomorphism classes of finite *G*-sets, with addition the disjoint union of *G*-sets and identity element the empty set (which is a *G*-set in a unique way). Then *A*(*G*), the Grothendieck group of *M*(*G*), is an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974). It is in fact a [free](wikipedia://en/247296) abelian group with basis elements represented by the *G*-sets *G*/*H*, where *H* varies over the subgroups of *G*. (Note that *H* is not assumed here to be a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*, for while *G*/*H* is not a group in this case, it is still a *G*-set.) The [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) structure on *A*(*G*) is induced by the direct product of *G*-sets; the multiplicative identity is the (isomorphism class of any) one-point set, which becomes a *G*-set in a unique way.

The Burnside ring is the analogue of the [representation ring](wikipedia://en/16673816) in the category of finite sets, as opposed to the category of finite-dimensional [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) (see motivation below). It has proven to be an important tool in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/12695) of finite groups.

## The classifying space {#The_classifying_space}

For any [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) *G* admitting the structure of a [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex), one may consider the category of [principal *G*-bundles](wikipedia://en/310950). One can define a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from the category of CW-complexes to the category of sets by assigning to each CW-complex *X* the set of principal *G*-bundles on *X*. This functor descends to a functor on the homotopy category of CW-complexes, and it is natural to ask whether the functor so obtained is [representable](wikipedia://en/844461). The answer is affirmative, and the representing object is called the classifying space of the group *G* and typically denoted *BG*. If we restrict our attention to the homotopy category of CW-complexes, then *BG* is unique. Any CW-complex that is homotopy equivalent to *BG* is called a *model* for *BG*.

For example, if *G* is the group of order 2, then a model for *BG* is infinite-dimensional [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694). It can be shown that if *G* is finite, then any CW-complex modelling *BG* has cells of arbitrarily large dimension. On the other hand, if *G* = **Z**, the integers, then the classifying space *BG* is homotopy equivalent to the circle *S*<sup>1</sup>.

## Motivation and interpretation {#Motivation_and_interpretation}

The content of the theorem becomes somewhat clearer if it is placed in its historical context. In the theory of representations of finite groups, one can form an object $R[G]$ called the representation ring of $G$ in a way entirely analogous to the construction of the Burnside ring outlined above. The stable [cohomotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomotopy_group) is in a sense the natural analog to complex [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748), which is denoted $KU^*$. Segal was inspired to make his conjecture after [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) proved the existence of an isomorphism

$$KU^0(BG) \to \widehat{R}[G]$$

which is a special case of the [Atiyah–Segal completion theorem](wikipedia://en/18017949).
