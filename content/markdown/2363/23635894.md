# Non-autonomous mechanics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-autonomous mechanics"}

**Non-autonomous mechanics** describe non-[relativistic](wikipedia://en/30001) mechanical systems subject to time-dependent transformations. In particular, this is the case of mechanical systems whose [Lagrangians](wikipedia://en/23371726) and [Hamiltonians](wikipedia://en/198319) depend on the time. The configuration space of non-autonomous mechanics is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) $Q\to \mathbb R$ over the time axis $\mathbb R$ coordinated by $(t,q^i)$.

:::

This bundle is trivial, but its different trivializations $Q=\mathbb R\times M$ correspond to the choice of different non-relativistic reference frames. Such a reference frame also is represented by a [connection](wikipedia://en/308815) $\Gamma$ on $Q\to\mathbb R$ which takes a form $\Gamma^i =0$ with respect to this trivialization. The corresponding covariant differential $(q^i_t-\Gamma^i)\partial_i$ determines the relative velocity with respect to a reference frame $\Gamma$.

As a consequence, non-autonomous mechanics (in particular, non-autonomous Hamiltonian mechanics) can be formulated as a [covariant classical field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_classical_field_theory) (in particular [covariant Hamiltonian field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_Hamiltonian_field_theory)) on $X=\mathbb R$. Accordingly, the velocity [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) of non-autonomous mechanics is the [jet manifold](wikipedia://en/928060) $J^1Q$ of $Q\to \mathbb R$ provided with the coordinates $(t,q^i,q^i_t)$. Its momentum phase space is the vertical cotangent bundle $VQ$ of $Q\to \mathbb R$ coordinated by $(t,q^i,p_i)$ and endowed with the canonical [Poisson structure](wikipedia://en/882902). The dynamics of Hamiltonian non-autonomous mechanics is defined by a Hamiltonian form $p_idq^i-H(t,q^i,p_i)dt$.

One can associate to any Hamiltonian non-autonomous system an equivalent Hamiltonian autonomous system on the cotangent bundle $TQ$ of $Q$ coordinated by $(t,q^i,p,p_i)$ and provided with the canonical [symplectic form](wikipedia://en/28356); its [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/198319) is $p-H$.
