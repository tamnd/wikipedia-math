# Cheng's eigenvalue comparison theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cheng's eigenvalue comparison theorem"}

In [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), **Cheng's eigenvalue comparison theorem** states in general terms that when a domain is large, the first [Dirichlet eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/22860539) of its [Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356) is small. This general characterization is not precise, in part because the notion of "size" of the domain must also account for its [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770).[^o2] The theorem is due to (Cheng 1975b) by [Shiu-Yuen Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiu-Yuen_Cheng). Using [geodesic balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_ball), it can be generalized to certain tubular domains (Lee 1990).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Let *M* be a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with dimension *n*, and let *B*<sub>*M*</sub>(*p*, *r*) be a geodesic ball centered at *p* with radius *r* less than the [injectivity radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injectivity_radius) of *p* ∈ *M*. For each real number *k*, let *N*(*k*) denote the [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) [space form](wikipedia://en/1087009) of dimension *n* and constant [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) *k*. Cheng's eigenvalue comparison theorem compares the first eigenvalue λ<sub>1</sub>(*B*<sub>*M*</sub>(*p*, *r*)) of the Dirichlet problem in *B*<sub>*M*</sub>(*p*, *r*) with the first eigenvalue in *B*<sub>*N*(*k*)</sub>(*r*) for suitable values of *k*. There are two parts to the theorem:

- Suppose that *K*<sub>*M*</sub>, the [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) of *M*, satisfies

$$K_M\le k.$$

Then

$$\lambda_1\left(B_{N(k)}(r)\right) \le \lambda_1\left(B_M(p,r)\right).$$

The second part is a comparison theorem for the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) of *M*:

- Suppose that the Ricci curvature of *M* satisfies, for every vector field *X*,

$$\operatorname{Ric}(X,X) \ge k(n-1)|X|^2.$$

Then, with the same notation as above,

$$\lambda_1\left(B_{N(k)}(r)\right) \ge \lambda_1\left(B_M(p,r)\right).$$

S.Y. Cheng used [Barta's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barta's_theorem) to derive the eigenvalue comparison theorem. As a special case, if *k* = −1 and inj(*p*) = ∞, Cheng’s inequality becomes *λ*<sup>\*</sup>(*N*) ≥ *λ*<sup>\*</sup>(*H*<sup>*n*</sup>(−1)) which is [McKean’s inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKean’s_inequality).[^o7]
