# Pappus configuration

*Image: Pappusconfig.svg.* Pappus configuration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pappus configuration"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Pappus configuration** is a [configuration](wikipedia://en/7200539) of nine points and nine lines in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), with three points per line and three lines through each point.[^o2]

:::

## History and construction {#History_and_construction}

This configuration is named after [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300). [Pappus's hexagon theorem](wikipedia://en/1493395) states that every two triples of collinear points $ABC$ and $abc$ (none of which lie on the intersection of the two lines) can be completed to form a Pappus configuration, by adding the six lines $\overline{Ab}$, $\overline{aB}$, $\overline{Ac}$, $\overline{aC}$, $\overline{Bc}$, and $\overline{bC}$, and their three intersection points $X = \overline{Ab} \cdot \overline{aB}$, $Y = \overline{Ac} \cdot \overline{aC}$, and $Z = \overline{Bc} \cdot \overline{bC}$. These three points are the intersection points of the "opposite" sides of the hexagon $AbCaBc$. According to Pappus' theorem, the resulting system of nine points and eight lines always has a ninth line containing the three intersection points $X$, $Y$, and $Z$, called the *Pappus line*.[^o4]

*Image: Pappus hexagon.svg.* The Pappus configuration from [perspective triangles](wikipedia://en/14909067) $\triangle XcC$ and $\triangle YbB$

The Pappus configuration can also be derived from two triangles $\triangle XcC$ and $\triangle YbB$ that are in perspective with each other (the three lines through corresponding pairs of points meet at a single crossing point) in three different ways, together with their three centers of [perspectivity](wikipedia://en/23912960) $Z$, $a$, and $A$. The points of the configuration are the points of the triangles and centers of perspectivity, and the lines of the configuration are the lines through corresponding pairs of points.

## Related constructions {#Related_constructions}

*Image: Pappus graph 2COL.svg.* The Pappus graph with bipartite coloring

The [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of the Pappus configuration is known as the [Pappus graph](wikipedia://en/7250518). It is a [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431) [symmetric](wikipedia://en/1577896) [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) with 18 vertices and 27 edges.[^o6]

Adding three more parallel lines to the Pappus configuration, through each triple of points that are not already connected by lines of the configuration, produces the [Hesse configuration](wikipedia://en/35282956).[^o7]

Like the Pappus configuration, the [Desargues configuration](wikipedia://en/7209841) can be defined in terms of perspective triangles, and the [Reye configuration](wikipedia://en/35301443) can be defined analogously from two tetrahedra that are in perspective with each other in four different ways, forming a [desmic system](wikipedia://en/23457340) of tetrahedra.

For any nonsingular [cubic plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_plane_curve) in the Euclidean plane, three real [inflection points](wikipedia://en/379845) of the curve, and a fourth point on the curve, there is a unique way of completing these four points to form a Pappus configuration in such a way that all nine points lie on the curve.[^o8]

## Applications {#Applications}

*Image: Orchard-planting problem.svg.* The Pappus configuration, augmented with an additional line (the vertical one in the center of the figure), solves the [orchard-planting problem](wikipedia://en/29600081) for 9 points, with 3 points per line.

A variant of the Pappus configuration provides a solution to the [orchard-planting problem](wikipedia://en/29600081), the problem of finding sets of points that have the largest possible number of lines through three points. The nine points of the Pappus configuration form only nine three-point lines. However, they can be arranged so that there is another three-point line, making a total of ten. This is the maximum possible number of three-point lines through nine points.
