# Uniform theory of diffraction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform theory of diffraction"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), the **uniform theory of diffraction** (**UTD**) is a [high-frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_frequency) method for solving [electromagnetic](wikipedia://en/9426) [scattering](wikipedia://en/164483) problems from electrically small discontinuities or discontinuities in more than one dimension at the same point.[^o2] UTD is an extension of [Joseph Keller](wikipedia://en/4454126)'s **geometrical theory of diffraction** (GTD)[^o3] and was introduced by [Robert Kouyoumjian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kouyoumjian) and Prabhakar Pathak in 1974.[^o2][^o4]

:::

The uniform theory of diffraction approximates [near field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-field_optics) electromagnetic fields as [quasi optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi_optical) and uses [knife-edge diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knife-edge_diffraction) to determine diffraction coefficients for each diffracting object-source combination. These coefficients are then used to calculate the field strength and [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) for each direction away from the diffracting point. These fields are then added to the incident fields and reflected fields to obtain a total solution.
