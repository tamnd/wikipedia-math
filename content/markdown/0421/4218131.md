# Ernst Fetterlein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Fetterlein"}

**Ernst Constantin Fetterlein** (3 April 1873[^o2] – June 1944[^o3][^o4]) was a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_%28citizen%29) [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) who later defected to [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom).

:::

Fetterlein was born in [St Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Petersburg), the son of Karl Fedorovich Fetterlein, a German-language tutor, and Olga Fetterlein, née Meier.[^o2] He studied a variety of eastern languages at the [University of St Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Petersburg), graduating in 1894. On 25 November 1896 he joined the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.[^o2] He eventually became the chief cryptologist for the [Tsar of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar_of_Russia),[^o5] holding the rank of "General-Admiral," an honorary title in Tsarist Russia.[^o6] During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), he was known for a time as **Ernst Popov** as his German-derived name could have drawn unwanted attention.[^o7] Amongst others, he solved German, Austrian and British codes.[^o6]

Upon the [Russian Revolution of 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917), he fled to Western Europe with his wife on board a Swedish ship, narrowly evading capture.[^o6] He contacted the British and French intelligence organisations, offering to work for whoever would pay him the most, which was apparently the British, as he was recruited to [Room 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_40) in June 1918[^o6] to work on [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Georgia), Austrian and Bolshevik codes. After the end of World War I, he worked for the successor to Room 40, the [Government Code and Cypher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Code_and_Cypher_School) (GC&CS), becoming a senior assistant on 17 December 1919.[^o2] During this time he worked on Soviet Communist traffic.[^o8] He was thought well of by his colleagues, one of whom wrote, "He was a brilliant cryptographer. On book cipher and anything else where insight was vital he was quite the best. He was a fine linguist and would usually get an answer no matter the language."[^o6] He retired in 1938.[^o4] His brother, P. K. Fetterlein, also worked for GC&CS.[^o6]

Fetterlein came out of retirement during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) to assist GC&CS's diplomatic section at Berkeley Street. He worked on "[Floradora](wikipedia://en/225611)", a German diplomatic code.[^o9]
