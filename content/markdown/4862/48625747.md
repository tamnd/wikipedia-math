# Jeremy Kahn

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jeremy Kahn"}

**Jeremy Adam Kahn** (born October 26, 1969) is an American mathematician. He works on [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) and [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Kahn grew up in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) and attended [Hunter College High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College_High_School). He was a [child prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy) who mastered [quadratic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_equations) at the age of 7, at the age of 8 designed an equation identical to the one [Carl Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gauss) designed at the age of 9, and proved the Pythagorean Theorem at the age of 10.[^o4] Kahn was part of the [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) [Study of Mathematically Precocious Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Study_of_Mathematically_Precocious_Youth) longitudinal cohort. At the age of 11, he scored a 780 out of a possible 800 on the math portion of the [SAT-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT-I) exam.[^o4] At the age of 13, he became the youngest person ever to make the United States [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) team [^o5] He participated in the Olympiad four times, winning silver medals in 1983 and 1984, and gold medals in 1985 and 1986.[^o6]

On the basis of his success in the [Putnam competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_competition), he became a Putnam Fellow in 1988.[^o7] He received a bachelor's degree in mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), then received his Ph.D. from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1995 under [Curtis McMullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_McMullen) with the thesis *Holomorphic removability of quadratic polynomial Julia sets*.

## Career {#Career}

Kahn was assistant professor at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) from 1995 to 1998 and was later a postdoc at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto). After that, he worked for the investment firm [Highbridge Capital Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highbridge_Capital_Management) as an analyst in [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics). He was a postdoc and later assistant professor at the [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University). He is currently a professor of mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University).[^o10]

In 2012, Kahn and [Vladimir Markovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Markovic) received the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) for their research on [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), specifically, for their result on immersions into a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold (proof of the [surface subgroup conjecture](wikipedia://en/4565128))[^o11] and for their proof of the [Ehrenpreis conjecture](wikipedia://en/36201091).[^o12]

In 2014 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul) and gave a talk called "The Surface Subgroup and the Ehrenpreis Conjectures".
