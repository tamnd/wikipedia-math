# Mireille Bousquet-Mélou

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mireille Bousquet-Mélou"}

*Image: Mireille Bousquet-Mélou à Oberwolfach 2014.jpg.* Mireille Bousquet-Mélou at [Oberwolfach](wikipedia://en/1368669) in 2014.

**Mireille Bousquet-Mélou** (born 12 May 1967) is a French mathematician who specializes in [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533) and who works as a senior researcher for the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (CNRS) at the computer science department (LaBRI) of the [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux).[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bousquet-Mélou was born in [Albi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albi), the second daughter of two high school teachers, and grew up in [Pau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pau,_Pyrénées-Atlantiques) where her family moved when she was three.[^o4] She studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in Paris from 1986 to 1990,[^o2] as the only woman in her entering class of mathematicians,[^o4] and earned an [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in mathematics in 1989,[^o2] with [Xavier Gérard Viennot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_Gérard_Viennot) as her mentor in combinatorics.[^o4] She completed her Ph.D. at the University of Bordeaux in 1991, with a dissertation on the enumeration of [orthogonally convex](wikipedia://en/7847685) [polyominos](wikipedia://en/72634) supervised by Viennot. She joined CNRS as a junior researcher in 1990, and completed a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at Bordeaux in 1996.[^o2]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Bousquet-Mélou won the bronze medal of the CNRS in 1993, and the silver medal in 2014. [Linköping University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linköping_University) gave her an honorary doctorate in 2005, and the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) gave her their Charles-Louis de Saulces de Freycinet Prize in 2009.[^o2] In 2006, she was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in the section on combinatorics.[^o7] Her presentation at the congress concerned connections between enumerative combinatorics, [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939) theory, and the algebraic structure of [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993), according to which enumeration problems whose generating functions are [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) are often isomorphic to [regular languages](wikipedia://en/25723), and problems whose generating functions are algebraic are often isomorphic to unambiguous [context-free languages](wikipedia://en/6867).
