# Spherically complete field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherically complete field"}

In mathematics, a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* with an [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991#Fields) is called **spherically complete** if the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of every [decreasing sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decreasing_sequence) of [balls](wikipedia://en/160556) (in the sense of the metric induced by the absolute value) is nonempty:[^o2]

:::

$$B_1\supseteq B_2\supseteq \cdots \Rightarrow\bigcap_{n\in {\mathbf N}} B_n\neq \emptyset.$$

The definition can be adapted also to a field *K* with a [valuation](wikipedia://en/347049) *v* taking values in an arbitrary ordered abelian group: (*K*,*v*) is spherically complete if every collection of balls that is totally ordered by inclusion has a nonempty intersection.

Spherically complete fields are important in [nonarchimedean](wikipedia://en/264158) [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), since many results analogous to theorems of classical functional analysis require the base field to be spherically complete.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) field is spherically complete. This includes, in particular, the fields **Q**<sub>*p*</sub> of [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423), and any of their finite extensions.
- Every spherically complete field is [complete](wikipedia://en/7832). On the other hand, **C**<sub>*p*</sub>, the [completion](wikipedia://en/7832) of the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of **Q**<sub>*p*</sub>, is not spherically complete.[^o6]
- Any field of [Hahn series](wikipedia://en/21368973) is spherically complete.
