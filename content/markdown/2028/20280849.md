# André Haefliger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="André Haefliger"}

**André Haefliger** (; 22 May 19297 March 2023) was a Swiss mathematician who worked primarily on [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Haefliger went to school in [Nyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyon) and then attended his final years at [Collège de Genève](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_Calvin) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva). He studied mathematics at the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne) from 1948 to 1952. He worked for two years as a teaching assistant at [École Polytechnique de l'Université de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne). He then moved to [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), then he followed [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108) in Paris, where he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) degree in 1958.[^o4] His thesis was entitled "*Structures feuilletées et cohomologie à valeurs dans un faisceau de groupoïdes*" and was written under the supervision of [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108).

Haefliger got a [research fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_fellowship) for one year at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), where he participated in the [seminar](wikipedia://en/3862616) of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536), and then from 1959 to 1961 he worked at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). Since 1962 he has been a full professor at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) until his retirement in 1996.[^o6]

In 1966 Haefliger was [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065#1966,_Moscow) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Moscow.[^o7] In 1974–75, he was president of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society).[^o8]

Haefliger obtained a Doctorate *[honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa)* from the [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Institute_of_Technology_Zürich) in 1992 and from the [University of Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dijon) in 1997. In 2020 Haefliger and [Martin Bridson](wikipedia://en/36378173) were awarded the American Mathematical Society's [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Mathematical Exposition, for their book *Metric Spaces of Non-Positive Curvature* (Springer Verlag, 1999).[^o9]

Haefliger died on 7 March 2023, at the age of 93.[^o10]

## Research {#Research}

Haefliger's main research interests were [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

Haefliger found the topological obstruction to the existence of a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) on an [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652).[^o12] In two papers in the [Annals](wikipedia://en/2785) he studied various [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) of [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) in relations to [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008).[^o13][^o14] He has also made important contributions in the theory of [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227), introducing the notion of [Haefliger structures](wikipedia://en/21045118).[^o15]

*Image: Eckmann Hilton Serre Haefliger.jpg.* From left to right: [Beno Eckmann](wikipedia://en/19613365), [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286), [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), and André Haefliger in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) in 2007

He wrote more than 80 papers in peer review journals[^o16] and had 20 Ph.D. students, including [Augustin Banyaga](wikipedia://en/8994104) and the future [Field Medalist](wikipedia://en/10859) [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628).[^o5]
