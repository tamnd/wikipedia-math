# Ivan Privalov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ivan Privalov"}

**Ivan Ivanovich Privalov** (Ива́н Ива́нович Привáлов; 11 February 1891 – 13 July 1941) was a Soviet and Russian mathematician best known for his work on [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Privalov graduated from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) (MSU) in 1913 studying under [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov) and [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228). He obtained his master's degree from MSU in 1916 and became professor at [Imperial Saratov University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov_State_University) (1917—1922). In 1922, he was appointed professor at MSU and worked there for the rest of his life.

He was elected a corresponding member of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1939. He was also a member of the French Mathematical Society ([Société mathématique de France](wikipedia://en/1691205)) and the Mathematical Circle of Palermo ([Circolo Matematico di Palermo](wikipedia://en/32146789)).

## Research work {#Research_work}

Privalov wrote *Cauchy Integral* (1918) which built on work by [Fatou](wikipedia://en/629100). He also worked on many problems jointly with [Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228). In 1934, he studied subharmonic functions, building on the work of [Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz).
