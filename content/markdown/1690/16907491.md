# Archimedes' quadruplets

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Archimedes' quadruplets"}

*Image: Archimedes' Quadruplets.svg.* Each of the Archimedes' quadruplets (green) have equal area to each other and to Archimedes' twin circles

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Archimedes' quadruplets** are four [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) associated with an [arbelos](wikipedia://en/887197). Introduced by Frank Power in the summer of 1998, each have the same [area](wikipedia://en/1209) as [Archimedes' twin circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes'_twin_circles), making them [Archimedean circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_circle).[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

An arbelos is formed from three collinear points *A*, *B*, and *C*, by the three [semicircles](wikipedia://en/617831) with [diameters](wikipedia://en/8007) *AB*, *AC*, and *BC*. Let the two smaller circles have [radii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radii) *r*<sub>1</sub> and *r*<sub>2</sub>, from which it follows that the larger semicircle has radius *r* = *r*<sub>1</sub>+*r*<sub>2</sub>. Let the points *D* and *E* be the [center](wikipedia://en/4850775) and [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979), respectively, of the semicircle with the radius *r*<sub>1</sub>. Let *H* be the midpoint of line *AC*. Then two of the four quadruplet circles are tangent to line *HE* at the point *E*, and are also tangent to the outer semicircle. The other two quadruplet circles are formed in a symmetric way from the semicircle with radius *r*<sub>2</sub>.

:::

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of congruency"}

## Proof of congruency {#Proof_of_congruency}

According to Proposition 5 of [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844)' *[Book of Lemmas](wikipedia://en/17941791)*, the common [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of Archimedes' twin circles is:

$$\frac{r_1\cdot r_2}{r}.$$

By the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034):

$$\left(HE\right)^2=\left(r_1\right)^2+\left(r_2\right)^2.$$

Then, create two circles with centers *J<sub>i</sub>* [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to *HE*, [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the large semicircle at point *L<sub>i</sub>*, tangent to point *E*, and with equal radii *x*. Using the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034):

$$\left(HJ_i\right)^2=\left(HE\right)^2+x^2=\left(r_1\right)^2+\left(r_2\right)^2+x^2$$

Also:

$$HJ_i=HL_i-x=r-x=r_1+r_2-x~$$

Combining these gives:

$$\left(r_1\right)^2+\left(r_2\right)^2+x^2=\left(r_1+r_2-x\right)^2$$

Expanding, collecting to one side, and factoring:

$$2r_1r_2-2x\left(r_1+r_2\right)=0$$

Solving for *x*:

$$x=\frac{r_1\cdot r_2}{r_1+r_2}=\frac{r_1\cdot r_2}{r}$$

Proving that each of the Archimedes' quadruplets' areas is equal to each of Archimedes' twin circles' areas.[^o9]
