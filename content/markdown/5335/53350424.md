# Jean-Pierre Demailly

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Pierre Demailly"}

**Jean-Pierre Demailly** (25 September 1957 – 17 March 2022) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101). He was a professor at [Université Grenoble Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Grenoble_Alpes) and a permanent member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Demailly was born on 25 September 1957 in [Péronne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Péronne,_Somme), France.[^o4][^o5] He attended the Lycée de Péronne from 1966 to 1973 and the [Lycée Faidherbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Faidherbe) from 1973 to 1975.[^o4] He entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1975, where he received his [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in 1977 and graduated in 1979.[^o5] During this time, he received an undergraduate *[licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licence_%28France%29)* degree from [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) in 1976 and a *[diplôme d'études approfondies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplôme_d'études_approfondies)* under [Henri Skoda](wikipedia://en/48394269) at the [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) in 1979.[^o4] He received his *[Doctorat d'État](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorat_d'État)* in 1982 under the direction of Skoda at the Pierre and Marie Curie University, with thesis "Sur différents aspects de la positivité en analyse complexe".[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Demailly became a professor at [Université Grenoble Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Grenoble_Alpes) in 1983.[^o5] He served as the editor-in-chief of the *[Annales de l'Institut Fourier](wikipedia://en/1424210)* from 1998 to 2006 and the editor-in-chief of *[Comptes Rendus Mathématique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptes_Rendus_Mathématique)* from 2010 to 2015.[^o5][^o20] He was also an editor for *[Inventiones Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventiones_Mathematicae)* from 1997 to 2002.[^o5]

He was the director of the Institut Fourier from 2003 to 2006.[^o5] From June 2003 onwards, he led the *Groupe de réflexion interdisciplinaire sur les programmes* (GRIP), which ran experimental classes in primary schools.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Demailly's mathematical works primarily concerned [complex analytic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analytic_geometry), using techniques from [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101) with applications to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o5] He also wrote and co-authored several [Unix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix) and [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux) programs and libraries starting in the 1990s, including *[XPaint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XPaint)*, *sunclock*, and *[dmg2img](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmg2img)*.[^o5]

### Kählerian geometry {#Kählerian_geometry}

One main topic of Demailly's research is [Pierre Lelong](wikipedia://en/34789789)'s generalization of the notion of a [Kähler form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_form) to allow forms with singularities, known as [currents](wikipedia://en/3021207). In particular, for a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) $X$, an element of the [Dolbeault cohomology](wikipedia://en/3189601) group $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})$ is called pseudo-effective if it is represented by a closed positive (1,1)-[current](wikipedia://en/3021207) (where "positive" means "nonnegative" in this phrase), or [big](wikipedia://en/1186896#Big_line_bundles) if it is represented by a strictly positive (1,1)-current; these definitions generalize the corresponding notions for holomorphic [line bundles](wikipedia://en/581859) on [projective varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_varieties). Demailly's regularization theorem says, in particular, that any big class can be represented by a Kähler current with analytic singularities.[^o10]

Such analytic results have had many applications to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). In particular, Boucksom, Demailly, Păun, and Peternell showed that a [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) complex projective variety $X$ is [uniruled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniruled_variety) if and only if its [canonical bundle](wikipedia://en/734893) $K_X$ is not pseudo-effective.[^o11]

### Multiplier ideals {#Multiplier_ideals}

For a singular metric on a line bundle, Nadel, Demailly, and [Yum-Tong Siu](wikipedia://en/6085925) developed the concept of the [multiplier ideal](wikipedia://en/28300822), which describes where the metric is most singular. There is an analog of the [Kodaira vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/2988093) for such a metric, on compact or noncompact complex manifolds.[^o13] This led to the first effective criteria for a line bundle on a complex projective variety $X$ of any dimension $n$ to be [very ample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_ample), that is, to have enough global sections to give an embedding of $X$ into [projective space](wikipedia://en/762977). For example, Demailly showed in 1993 that $2K_X+ 12n^nL$ is very ample for any [ample line bundle](wikipedia://en/1186896) *L*, where addition denotes the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of line bundles. The method has inspired later improvements in the direction of the [Fujita conjecture](wikipedia://en/3086309).[^o14]

### Kobayashi hyperbolicity {#Kobayashi_hyperbolicity}

Demailly used the technique of [jet](wikipedia://en/1521924) differentials introduced by Green and [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414) to prove [Kobayashi hyperbolicity](wikipedia://en/31025902) for various projective varieties. For example, Demailly and El Goul showed that a very general complex surface $X$ of [degree](wikipedia://en/2776558) at least 21 in projective space $\mathbb{CP}^3$ is hyperbolic; equivalently, every [holomorphic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_map) $\mathbb{C} \to X$ is constant.[^o16] For any variety $X$ of [general type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_type), Demailly showed that every holomorphic map $\mathbb{C} \to X$ satisfies some (in fact, many) [algebraic differential equations](wikipedia://en/9501827).[^o17]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Demailly received the [CNRS Bronze Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Bronze_Medal) in 1981,[^o5] the Prix Mergier-Bourdeix from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1994,[^o5][^o19] the [Humboldt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize) in 1996,[^o5] the [Simion Stoilow Prize](wikipedia://en/20268944) from the [Romanian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2006,[^o5] the [Stefan Bergman Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bergman_Prize) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2015,[^o5][^o20] and the [Heinz Hopf Prize](wikipedia://en/24560211) from [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH) in 2021.[^o21]

Demailly was elected a correspondent of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1994 and then became a permanent member in 2007.[^o5][^o22] He was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1994 and a plenary speaker in 2006.[^o23]

In 2024, the [Jean-Pierre Demailly prize for open science in mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Demailly_prize_for_open_science_in_mathematics) is created in his honor. It is awarded every two years.[^o24]

In 2025, the [Société mathématique de France](wikipedia://en/1691205) published a special issue of its gazette in his memory.[^o25]

## Death {#Death}

Demailly died on 17 March 2022.[^o27]
