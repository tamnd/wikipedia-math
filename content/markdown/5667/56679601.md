# Colin Lingwood Mallows

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Colin Lingwood Mallows"}

**Colin Lingwood Mallows** (10 September 1930 – 4 November 2023) was an English statistician who worked in the United States from 1960.[^o4] He was known for [Mallows's *C<sub>p</sub>*](wikipedia://en/8284591), a regression model diagnostic procedure, widely used in [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997) and the [Fowlkes–Mallows index](wikipedia://en/33830471), a popular [clustering](wikipedia://en/669675) validation criterion.[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Mallows began studying at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) (UCL) in 1948 and received in 1951 his bachelor's degree and in 1953 his PhD (at the age of 22) from under [Florence Nightingale David](wikipedia://en/15434066) and [Norman Lloyd Johnson](wikipedia://en/2590640) with thesis *Some problems connected with distribution problems.* Mallows joined the UCL faculty and taught there from 1955 to 1959 with a sabbatical year at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in the academic year 1957–1958. He worked for [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) in [Murray Hill, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill,_New_Jersey) from 1960 to 1995 and then for [AT&T Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Labs) in [Florham Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florham_Park,_New_Jersey) from 1995 to 2000, when he retired. From 2000, Mallows was a consultant for [Avaya Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avaya). He was the author or coauthor of about 140 research publications.[^o5]

## Death {#Death}

Mallows died on 4 November 2023, at the age of 93.[^o10][^o2]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Mallows was awarded the [R. A. Fisher Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher_Lectureship) in 1997, the [Deming Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deming_Lectureship) in 2004, and the [Wilks Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/15966180) in 2007. Mallows and [George Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Box) are the only two statisticians to have received all three of those honours.[^o5] He was a Fellow of the American Statistical Association, the Institute of Mathematical Statistics, and the Royal Statistical Society.[^o10]

Mallows solved a \$10,000 mathematical problem posed by [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807),[^o12] but declined the prize money on the grounds that the problem was too easy.[^o5]
