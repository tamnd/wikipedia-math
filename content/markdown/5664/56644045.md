# Lillian K. Bradley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lillian K. Bradley"}

**Lillian Katie Bradley** (October 15, 1921 – February 11, 1995) was an American mathematician and mathematics educator who in 1960 became the first [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American) woman to earn a doctorate in any subject at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin). She accomplished this ten years after African-Americans were first admitted to the school, despite the discriminatory views of [R. L. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._L._Moore) an influential professor in the mathematics department at University of Texas at Austin.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Bradley was born in [Tyler, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler,_Texas). She earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1938 from [Texas College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_College), and a master's degree in mathematics education in 1946 from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). She completed her doctorate at the University of Texas in July 1960. Her dissertation, in [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471), was *An Evaluation of the Effectiveness of a Collegiate General Mathematics Course*.

## Career {#Career}

Dr. Bradley held many teaching positions throughout her career. She was a teacher at a segregated Black high school in [Hawkins, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawkins,_Texas). She also taught at [Paul Quinn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Quinn_College), and at Texas College, before becoming an assistant professor of mathematics at [Prairie View A&M College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_View_A&M_University). There, in 1957–1958, she was awarded a National Science Faculty Fellowship, one of only 100 awarded in the inaugural year of the program. In 1962 she moved from Prairie View to [Texas Southern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Southern_University), as an associate professor.

Bradley died in February 1995 at the age of 76.
