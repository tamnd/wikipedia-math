# David Shanno

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Shanno"}

**David F. Shanno** (born April 19, 1938 – 2019[^o2]) was an American mathematician, who specialized in [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033) and [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476). He was professor emeritus at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) (Rutgers Center for Operations Research, Rutcor).[^o3]

:::

Shanno obtained his B.Sc. in mathematics from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1959, his M.Sc. in mathematics from [Carnegie-Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie-Mellon_University) 1962, and his Ph.D. in 1967. He held positions at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) and the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis) before becoming professor at Rutgers University.

In 1970 he was one of the developers of the [BFGS algorithm](wikipedia://en/1926409), a [Quasi-Newton method](wikipedia://en/7883127).[^o4]

In 2005 he became a fellow at [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences).[^o5] He received the E.M.L. Beale – W.B. Orchard-Hayes Prize for Excellence in Computational Mathematical Programming of the [Mathematical Programming Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Programming_Society) in 1991 together with I. J. Lustig and R. E. Marsten. Shanno was associate editor of the journal *Mathematical Programming* from 1980 to 1989 and of the *Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications* from 1982 to 1990.
