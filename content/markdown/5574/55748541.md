# Marion Elizabeth Stark

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Marion Elizabeth Stark"}

**Marion Elizabeth Stark** (23 Aug 1894[^o3] – 15 April 1982)[^o4] was an American mathematician. She was one of the first women to receive a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics.[^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

She got her [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.B.) in 1916, and her [A.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1917, both from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University).[^o9] In 1917, she became the professor of mathematics [Meredith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_College) in [Raleigh, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh,_North_Carolina). In autumn 1919, she started teaching in [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) as a part-time instructor, while attending courses of [Helen Abbot Merrill](wikipedia://en/17201962) and [Mabel M. Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabel_M._Young).[^o7][^o8] In the 1923 summer quarter, and, supported by a fellowship, in autumn 1924 through summer 1925, she studied at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago)[^o9] where she received her Ph.D. in 1926.[^o5][^o10]

In 1927, she was appointed assistant professor of mathematics at Wellesley,[^o12][^o13] in 1936, she was promoted to an associate professor there.[^o14] In 1945, she was promoted to a professorship;[^o15] in 1946, she became Chairman of the Department.[^o16] In 1960, she retired from Wellesley after 40 years, her last rank being a Lewis Atterbury Stimson Professor of Mathematics.[^o5][^o17]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Stark was named a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1938.[^o19]
