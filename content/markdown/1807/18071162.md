# Monogenic semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monogenic semigroup"}

*Image: Monogenic semigroup order 9 period 6.gif.* Monogenic semigroup of order 9 and period 6. Numbers are exponents of the generator *a*; arrows indicate multiplication by *a*.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **monogenic semigroup** is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) generated by a single element.[^o2] Monogenic semigroups are also called **cyclic semigroups**.[^o3]

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

The monogenic semigroup generated by the [singleton set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton_set) {*a*} is denoted by $\langle a \rangle$. The set of elements of $\langle a \rangle$ is {*a*, *a*<sup>2</sup>, *a*<sup>3</sup>, ...}. There are two possibilities for the monogenic semigroup $\langle a \rangle$:

- *a<sup>m</sup>* = *a<sup>n</sup>* ⇒ *m* = *n*.
- There exist *m* ≠ *n* such that *a<sup>m</sup>* = *a<sup>n</sup>*.

In the former case $\langle a \rangle$ is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the semigroup ({1, 2, ...}, +) of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) under [addition](wikipedia://en/61338). In such a case, $\langle a \rangle$ is an *infinite monogenic semigroup* and the element *a* is said to have *infinite order*. It is sometimes called the *free monogenic semigroup* because it is also a [free semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_semigroup) with one generator.

In the latter case let *m* be the smallest positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) such that *a<sup>m</sup>* = *a<sup>x</sup>* for some positive integer *x* ≠ *m*, and let *r* be smallest positive integer such that *a<sup>m</sup>* = *a*<sup>*m*+*r*</sup>. The positive integer *m* is referred to as the **index** and the positive integer *r* as the **period** of the monogenic semigroup $\langle a \rangle$. The **order** of *a* is defined as *m*+*r*−1. The period and the index satisfy the following properties:

- *a<sup>m</sup>* = *a*<sup>*m*+*r*</sup>
- *a*<sup>*m*+*x*</sup> = *a*<sup>*m*+*y*</sup> [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *m* + *x* ≡ *m* + *y* (mod *r*)
- $\langle a \rangle$ = {*a*, *a*<sup>2</sup>, ... , *a*<sup>*m*+*r*−1</sup>}
- *K*<sub>*a*</sub> = {*a<sup>m</sup>*, *a*<sup>*m*+1</sup>, ... , *a*<sup>*m*+*r*−1</sup>} is a [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) and also an [ideal](wikipedia://en/27799#Subsemigroups_and_ideals) of $\langle a \rangle$. It is called the *[kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_of_a_semigroup)* of *a* and it is the [minimal ideal](wikipedia://en/34233472) of the monogenic semigroup $\langle a \rangle$.[^o5][^o6]

The pair (*m*, *r*) of positive integers determine the structure of monogenic semigroups. For every pair (*m*, *r*) of positive integers, there exists a monogenic semigroup having index *m* and period *r*. The monogenic semigroup having index *m* and period *r* is denoted by *M*(*m*, *r*). The monogenic semigroup *M*(1, *r*) is the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *r*.

The results in this section [actually hold](wikipedia://en/27799#Structure_of_semigroups) for any element *a* of an arbitrary semigroup and the monogenic subsemigroup $\langle a \rangle$ it generates.

## Related notions {#Related_notions}

A related notion is that of **periodic semigroup** (also called **torsion semigroup**), in which every element has finite order (or, equivalently, in which every monogenic subsemigroup is finite). A more general class is that of quasi-periodic semigroups (aka group-bound semigroups or [epigroups](wikipedia://en/37300268)) in which every element of the semigroup has a power that lies in a subgroup.[^o8][^o9]

An [aperiodic semigroup](wikipedia://en/669994) is one in which every monogenic subsemigroup has a period of 1.
