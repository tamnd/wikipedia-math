# Trifolium curve

*Image: Trifolium curve chart.png.* This image shows a trifolium curve using its Cartesian equation.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trifolium curve"}

The **trifolium curve** (also **three-leafed clover curve**, **3-petaled rose curve**, and **pâquerette de [mélibée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tale_of_Melibee)**) is a type of [quartic plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve). The name comes from the Latin terms for 3-leaved, defining itself as a [folium](wikipedia://en/2737940) shape with 3 equally sized leaves.[^o2]

:::

It is described as

$$x^4+2x^2y^2+y^4-x^3+3xy^2=0. \,$$

By solving for *y* by substituting *y*<sup>2</sup> and its square, the curve can be described by the following function(s):

$$y=\pm\sqrt{\frac{-2x^2-3x\pm\sqrt{16x^3+9x^2}}{2}},\,\,y^2=\frac{-x(2x+3)\pm x\sqrt{16x+9}}{2}$$

Due to the separate ± symbols, it is possible to solve for 4 different answers at a given (positive) x-coordinate; 2 y-values per negative x-coordinate. One sees 2 resp. 1 pair(s) of solutions, mirroring points on the curve.

It has a [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) of

*Image: Three-leaved clover polar.svg.* This image shows the trifolium curve using its polar equation. Its area is equivalent to one quarter the area of the [inscribed circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed_circle).

$$r=-a\cos(3\theta)$$

and a [Cartesian equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_equation) of

$$(x^2+y^2)[y^2+x(x+a)] = 4axy^2.$$

The area of the trifolium shape is defined by the following equation:

$A=\frac{1}{2} a^2 \int_{0}^{\pi}\cos^2(3\theta) \, d\theta\,$[^o3] $=\frac{\pi a^2}{4}$

And it has a length of

$6a\int_{0}^{\tfrac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{1-\frac{8}{9}\sin^2t}\, dt\thickapprox6.7 a$[^o4]

*Image: Trifolium equations.png.* This image shows two equations for the trifolium defined as $x^4+2x^2y^2+y^4-x^3+3xy^2=0$ (blue) and $(x^2+y^2)^3-x(x^2-3y^2)=0$ (red).

The trifolium was described by J.D. Lawrence as a form of [Kepler's folium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler's_folium) when

$b \in (0, 4, a)$[^o5]

A more present definition is when $a=b.$

The trifolium was described by Dana-Picard as

$$(x^2+y^2)^3-x(x^2-3y^2)=0$$

He defines the trifolium as having three leaves and having a triple point at the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) made up of 4 [arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometric%29). The trifolium is a [sextic](wikipedia://en/253260) curve meaning that any line through the origin will have it pass through the curve again and through its [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) twice.[^o6]

The trifolium is a type of [rose curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_curve) when $k = 3$[^o7]

[Gaston Albert Gohierre de Longchamps](wikipedia://en/36941284) was the first to study the trifolium, and it was given the name *Torpille* because of its resemblance to fish.[^o8]

The trifolium was later studied and given its name by [Henry Cundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyn_Cundy) and Arthur Rollett.[^o9]
