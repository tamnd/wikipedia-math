# Banach lattice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Banach lattice"}

In the [mathematical disciplines](wikipedia://en/18831) of in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924), a **Banach lattice** $(X,\|\cdot\|)$ is a [complete normed vector space](wikipedia://en/3989) with a [lattice order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_order), $\leq$, such that for all $x, y \in X$, the implication

$${|x|\leq|y|}\Rightarrow{\|x\|\leq\|y\|}$$

holds, where the absolute value $|\cdot|$ is defined as

$$|x| = x \vee -x := \sup\{x, -x\}\text{.}$$

:::

## Examples and constructions {#Examples_and_constructions}

Banach lattices are extremely common in functional analysis, and "every known example \[in 1948\] of a Banach space \[was\] also a [vector lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_lattice)."[^n1] In particular:

- $R$, together with its absolute value as a norm, is a Banach lattice.
- Let $X$ be a topological space, $Y$ a Banach lattice and $\text{𝒞}(X,Y)$ the [space of continuous bounded functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_of_continuous_bounded_functions) from $X$ to $Y$ with norm $$\|f\|_{\infty} = \sup_{x \in X} \|f(x)\|_Y\text{.}$$ Then $\text{𝒞}(X,Y)$ is a Banach lattice under the pointwise partial order: $${f \leq g}\Leftrightarrow(\forall x\in X)(f(x)\leq g(x))\text{.}$$

Examples of non-lattice Banach spaces are now known; [James' space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_space) is one such.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The continuous dual space of a Banach lattice is equal to its [order dual](wikipedia://en/12259232).[^n2]

Every Banach lattice admits a [continuous approximation to the identity](wikipedia://en/949628).[^n3]

:::

## Abstract (L)-spaces {#Abstract_(L)-spaces}

A Banach lattice satisfying the additional condition

$${f,g\geq0}\Rightarrow\|f+g\|=\|f\|+\|g\|$$

is called an abstract (L)-space. Such spaces, under the assumption of separability, are isomorphic to closed sublattices of $L^{1}([0,1])$.[^n4] The classical [mean ergodic theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_ergodic_theorem) and [Poincaré recurrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_recurrence) generalize to abstract (L)-spaces.[^n5]
