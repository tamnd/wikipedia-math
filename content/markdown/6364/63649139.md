# Sum of residues formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sum of residues formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **residue formula** says that the sum of the residues of a meromorphic differential form on a [smooth](wikipedia://en/7837041) [proper](wikipedia://en/603782) [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) vanishes.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

In this article, *X* denotes a proper smooth algebraic curve over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k*. A meromorphic [(algebraic) differential form](wikipedia://en/603916) $\omega$ has, at each [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408) *x* in *X*, a [residue](wikipedia://en/73102) which is denoted $\operatorname{res}_x \omega$. Since $\omega$ has poles only at finitely many points, in particular the residue vanishes for all but finitely many points. The residue formula states:

$$\sum_{x} \operatorname{res}_x \omega=0.$$

:::

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

A geometric way of proving the theorem is by reducing the theorem to the case when *X* is the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578), and proving it by explicit computations in this case, for example in (Altman Kleiman 1970, Ch. VIII, p. 177).

(Tate 1968) proves the theorem using a notion of [traces](wikipedia://en/43270) for certain [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of [infinite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370). The residue of a differential form $f dg$ can be expressed in terms of traces of endomorphisms on the [fraction field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_field) $K_x$ of the completed local rings $\hat \mathcal O_{X, x}$ which leads to a conceptual proof of the formula. A more recent exposition along similar lines, using more explicitly the notion of [Tate vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_vector_space), is given by (Clausen 2009).

:::
