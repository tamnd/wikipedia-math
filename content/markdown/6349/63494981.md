# Brouwer's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brouwer's conjecture"}

In the mathematical field of [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914), **Brouwer's conjecture** is a conjecture by [Andries Brouwer](wikipedia://en/22709916) on upper bounds for the intermediate sums of the [eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/2161429) of the [Laplacian](wikipedia://en/1448472) of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) in term of its number of edges.[^o2]

:::

The conjecture states that if *G* is a simple undirected graph and *L*(*G*) its Laplacian matrix, then its eigenvalues *λ*<sub>*n*</sub>(*L*(*G*)) ≤ *λ*<sub>*n*−1</sub>(*L*(*G*)) ≤ ... ≤ *λ*<sub>1</sub>(*L*(*G*)) satisfy

$$\sum_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}(L(G))\leq m(G)+\left(\begin{array}{c} t+1\\ 2 \end{array}\right),\quad t=1,\ldots,n$$

where *m*(*G*) is the number of edges of *G*.

## State of the art {#State_of_the_art}

Brouwer has confirmed by computation that the conjecture is valid for all graphs with at most 10 vertices.[^o2] It was later shown that the conjecture also holds for all graphs with up to 11 vertices.[^o4]

It is also known that the conjecture is valid for any number of vertices if *t* = 1, 2, 3, *n* − 4, *n* − 3, *n* − 2, *n* − 1, and *n*.[^o5]

For certain types of graphs, Brouwer's conjecture is known to be valid for all *t* and for any number of vertices. In particular, it is known that is valid for trees,[^o6] and for unicyclic and bicyclic graphs.[^o7] It was also proved that Brouwer’s conjecture holds for two large families of graphs; the first family of graphs is obtained from a clique by identifying each of its vertices to a vertex of an arbitrary c-cyclic graph, and the second family is composed of the graphs in which the removal of the edges of the maximal complete bipartite subgraph gives a graph each of whose non-trivial components is a c-cyclic graph.[^o8] For certain sequences of random graphs, Brouwer's conjecture holds true with probability tending to one as the number of vertices tends to infinity.[^o9]
