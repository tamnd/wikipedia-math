# Quasi-polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-polynomial"}

quasi-polynomial bounds in the analysis of algorithms

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quasi-polynomial** (sometimes called **pseudo-polynomial**) is a generalization of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000). While the coefficients of a polynomial come from a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), the coefficients of quasi-polynomials are instead [periodic functions](wikipedia://en/76848) with integral period. Quasi-polynomials appear throughout much of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) as the enumerators for various objects.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **quasi-polynomial** is a function $q$ defined on $\mathbb{Z}$ of the form $q(n) = c_d(n) n^d + c_{d-1}(n) n^{d-1} + \cdots + c_0(n)$, where each $c_i(n)$ is a [periodic function](wikipedia://en/76848) with integral period. If $c_d(n)$ is not identically zero, then the **degree** of $q$ is $d$, and any common period of $c_0(n), c_1(n), \dots, c_d(n)$ is a **period** of $q$. The minimal such period (sometimes simply called the period or the **quasi-period** of $q$) is the [least common multiple](wikipedia://en/17961) of the periods of $c_0(n), c_1(n), \dots, c_d(n)$.

Equivalently, a function $q$ defined on $\mathbb{Z}$ is a quasi-polynomial if there exist a positive integer $s$ and polynomials $p_0, \dots, p_{s-1}$ such that $q(n) = p_i(n)$ when $i \equiv n \bmod s$. The minimal such $s$ coincides with the minimal period of $q$. The polynomials $p_i$ are called the **constituents** of $q$.

:::

## Generating functions {#Generating_functions}

A function $q$ defined on $\mathbb{Z}$ is a quasi-polynomial of degree $\le d$ and period dividing $r$ if and only its [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993)

$$Q(x) := \sum_{ n \ge 0 } q(n) x^n$$

evaluates to a rational function of the form $Q(x) = \frac{ h(x) }{ (1-x^r)^{ d+1 } }$ where $h(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $< r(d+1)$.[^o5][^o6] Thus quasi-polynomials are characterized through generating functions that are rational and whose poles are rational roots of unity.

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Given a $d$-dimensional [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) $P$ with [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) vertices $v_1,\dots,v_n$, define $tP$ to be the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of $tv_1,\dots,tv_n$. The function $L(P,t) = \#(tP \cap \mathbb{Z}^d)$ is a quasi-polynomial in $t$ (viewed as a positive integer variable) of degree $d$; the minimal positive integer $r$ such that $r P$ has integer vertices is a period of $L(P,t)$. This is known as the [Ehrhart quasi-polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehrhart_quasi-polynomial), named after [Eugène Ehrhart](wikipedia://en/33824722).
- Given two quasi-polynomials $F$ and $G$, the [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) of $F$ and $G$ is

$$(F*G)(k) = \sum_{m=0}^k F(m)G(k-m)$$

which is a quasi-polynomial with degree $\le \deg F + \deg G + 1.$

:::
