# Georg Frederik Ursin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Georg Frederik Ursin"}

**Georg Frederik Krüger Ursin** (22 June 1797 – 4 December 1849) was a Danish mathematician and astronomer.[^o3]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

His father, Georg Jacob Krüger, was a first lieutenant in the [Royal Danish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Navy), however, in 1798, his was stripped of his functions where was taken to [Munkholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munkholmen), an islet north of [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim), [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway).

In the same year, Ursin was given royal license to carry the maternal family name from his mother, Jacobine Ursin (1772–1819), daughter of rear admiral Ursin, married to a wealthy shipbuilder and shipyard owner Lars Larsen.

## Education {#Education}

In 1814, Ursin passed an exam in [land surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_surveying) before graduating [cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_laude) from [Metropolitanskolen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitanskolen) in 1815. Having won a prize assignment involving [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299), he passed a second exam cum laude.

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Ursin died on December 4, 1849, in Copenhagen.

A [DSB Class EA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSB_Class_EA) locomotive no. 3020 is named after Ursin.
