# Schwarz's list

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schwarz's list"}

*Image: Hermann Amand Schwarz (1843-1921) by Louis Zipfel.jpg.* [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834), c. 1890

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) theory of [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function), **Schwarz's list** or the **Schwarz table** is the list of 15 cases found by (txt, p. 323) when [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223) can be expressed algebraically. More precisely, it is a listing of parameters determining the cases in which the [hypergeometric equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_equation) has a finite [monodromy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monodromy_group), or equivalently has two independent solutions that are [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169). It lists 15 cases, divided up by the [isomorphism class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of the monodromy group (excluding the case of a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327)), and was first derived by Schwarz by methods of complex analytic geometry. Correspondingly the statement is not directly in terms of the parameters specifying the hypergeometric equation, but in terms of quantities used to describe certain [spherical triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle).

:::

The wider importance of the table, for general second-order differential equations in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), was shown by [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), who proved a result to the effect that cases of finite monodromy for such equations and [regular singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_singularities) could be attributed to changes of variable (complex analytic mappings of the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) to itself) that reduce the equation to hypergeometric form. In fact more is true: Schwarz's list underlies all second-order equations with regular singularities on compact [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) having finite monodromy, by a pullback from the hypergeometric equation on the Riemann sphere by a complex analytic mapping, of degree computable from the equation's data.[^o3][^o4]

The numbers $\lambda, \mu, \nu$ are (up to permutations, sign changes and addition of $(\ell,m,n) \in \mathbb{Z}^3$ with $\ell+m+n$ even) the differences $1-c, c-a-b,b-a$ of the exponents of the [hypergeometric differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_differential_equation) at the three singular points $0,1,\infty$. They are rational numbers if and only if $a,b$ and $c$ are, a point that matters in arithmetic rather than geometric approaches to the theory.

## Further work {#Further_work}

An extension of Schwarz's results was given by T. Kimura, who dealt with cases where the [identity component](wikipedia://en/1088787) of the [differential Galois group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_Galois_group) of the hypergeometric equation is a [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012).[^o7][^o8] A general result connecting the differential Galois group *G* and the monodromy group Γ states that *G* is the [Zariski closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_closure) of Γ — this theorem is attributed in the book of Matsuda to [Michio Kuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michio_Kuga). By general differential [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), the resulting Kimura-Schwarz table classifies cases of integrability of the equation by algebraic functions and [quadratures](wikipedia://en/170089).

Another [relevant list is that of K. Takeuchi](wikipedia://en/5358554#Arithmetic_triangle_group), who classified the (hyperbolic) [triangle groups](wikipedia://en/2069342) that are [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741) (85 examples).[^o9]

[Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000) sought to extend the work of Schwarz in complex geometry, by means of a [generalized hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/361184), to construct cases of equations where the monodromy was a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) in the [projective unitary group](wikipedia://en/4402359) *PU*(1, *n*). [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) and [George Mostow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mostow) used his ideas to construct [lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_%28group_theory%29) in the projective unitary group. This work recovers in the classical case the finiteness of Takeuchi's list, and by means of a characterisation of the lattices they construct that are arithmetic groups, provided new examples of non-arithmetic lattices in *PU*(1, *n*).[^o10]

Baldassari applied the Klein universality, to discuss algebraic solutions of the [Lamé equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamé_equation) by means of the Schwarz list.[^o11]

Other hypergeometric functions which can be expressed algebraically, like those on Schwarz's list, arise in theoretical physics in the context of $T \overline{T}$ deformations of two-dimensional gauge theories.[^o12]
