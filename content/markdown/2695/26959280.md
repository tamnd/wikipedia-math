# Blanche Descartes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Blanche Descartes"}

**Blanche Descartes** was a collaborative [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym) used by the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people) [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [R. Leonard Brooks](wikipedia://en/28209555), [Arthur Harold Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Harold_Stone), [Cedric Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Smith_%28statistician%29), and [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021). The four mathematicians met in 1935 as undergraduate students at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where they joined the [Trinity Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/21274363) and began meeting together to work on mathematical problems.

:::

## Pseudonym {#Pseudonym}

The pseudonym originated by combining the initials of the mathematicians' given names (Bill, Leonard, Arthur, and Cedric) to form *BLAC*. This was extended to *BLAnChe*. The surname *Descartes* was chosen as a play on the common phrase *carte blanche*.[^o3]

## Publication {#Publication}

Over 30 works were published under the name, including whimsical poetry and [mathematical humour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_humour), but some serious mathematical results as well. Many of these publications appeared in *[Eureka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_%28University_of_Cambridge_magazine%29)*, a mathematical student magazine in Cambridge. Notably, the foursome [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) several [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) in mathematical [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671). In particular, they solved the problem of [squaring the square](wikipedia://en/67903), showing that a square can be divided into smaller squares, no two of which are the same. They also discovered "Blanche's Dissection", a method of dividing a square into rectangles of equal area but different dimensions. They modelled these using abstract [electrical networks](wikipedia://en/9559), an approach that yielded not only solutions to the original problem, but techniques with wider applications to the field of electrical networks.[^o6] They published their results—under their own names—in 1940.[^o7] Tutte, who is believed to have contributed the most work under Descartes's name, kept up the pretence for years, refusing to acknowledge even in private that she was fictitious.[^o8]

"Descartes" also published on [graph colouring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_colouring), and Tutte used the pseudonym to publish the fourth known [snark](wikipedia://en/727811), now called the [Descartes snark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes_snark).[^o9] She also published the poem "Hymne to Hymen"[^o10] as a gift to [Hector Pétard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Pétard) (another fictitious mathematical personage) on the day of his wedding to Betti Bourbaki (daughter of [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394), yet another fictitious mathematical personage).[^o3]
