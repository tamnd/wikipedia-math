# McCay cubic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="McCay cubic"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **McCay cubic** (also called **M'Cay cubic**[^o2] or **Griffiths cubic**[^o3]) is a [cubic plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_plane_curve) in the plane of a reference [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) and associated with it. It is the third cubic curve in Bernard Gilbert's [Catalogue of Triangle Cubics](wikipedia://en/69374512) and it is assigned the identification number K003.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: McCayCubicLocus.png.* solid blue solid magenta solid brown solid #03a9fc solid red

The McCay cubic can be defined by [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) properties in several ways.[^o3] For example, the McCay cubic is the locus of a point $P$ such that the pedal circle of $P$ is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the nine-point circle of the reference triangle $\triangle ABC$.[^o6] The McCay cubic can also be defined as the locus of point $P$ such that the [circumcevian triangle](wikipedia://en/69583443) of $P$ and $\triangle ABC$ are [orthologic](wikipedia://en/69531104).

:::

## Equation of the McCay cubic {#Equation_of_the_McCay_cubic}

The equation of the McCay cubic in [barycentric coordinates](wikipedia://en/762954) $x:y:z$ is

$$\sum_{\text{cyclic}}(a^2(b^2+c^2-a^2)x(c^2y^2-b^2z^2))=0.$$

The equation in [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) $\alpha : \beta : \gamma$ is

$$\alpha (\beta^2 - \gamma^2)\cos A + \beta (\gamma^2 - \alpha^2)\cos B + \gamma (\alpha^2 - \beta^2)\cos C = 0$$

## McCay cubic as a stelloid {#McCay_cubic_as_a_stelloid}

*Image: McCayStelloid.png.* McCay cubic with its three concurring asymptotes

A stelloid is a cubic that has three real concurring [asymptotes](wikipedia://en/3107) making 60° angles with one another. McCay cubic is a stelloid in which the three asymptotes concur at the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) of triangle ABC.[^o3] A circum-stelloid having the same asymptotic directions as those of McCay cubic and concurring at a certain (finite) is called **McCay stelloid**. The point where the asymptoptes concur is called the "radial center" of the stelloid.[^o9] Given a finite point X there is one and only one McCay stelloid with X as the radial center.
