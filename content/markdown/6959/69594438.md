# Kruskal count

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kruskal count"}

Kruskal's principle Sum Total Dynkin's card trick

The **Kruskal count**[^o19][^o20] (also known as **Kruskal's principle**,[^o13][^o22][^o23][^o24][^o25] **Dynkin–Kruskal count**,[^o32] **Dynkin's counting trick**,[^o36] **Dynkin's card trick**,[^o28][^o29][^o30][^o31] **coupling card trick**[^o33][^o34][^o35] or **shift coupling**[^o28][^o29][^o30][^o31]) is a [probabilistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic) concept originally demonstrated by the Russian mathematician [Evgenii Borisovich Dynkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgenii_Borisovich_Dynkin) in the 1950s or 1960s discussing [coupling](wikipedia://en/887545) effects[^o33][^o34][^o36][^o35] and rediscovered as a [card trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Card_trick) by the American mathematician [Martin David Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_David_Kruskal) in the early 1970s[^o37][^o6] as a side-product while working on another problem.[^o27] It was published by Kruskal's friend[^o26] [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435)[^o17][^o19] and magician [Karl Fulves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Fulves) in 1975.[^o18] This is related to a similar trick published by magician Alexander F. Kraus in 1957 as *Sum total*[^o14][^o15][^o16][^o38] and later called *Kraus principle*.[^o20][^o25][^o38][^o27]

:::

Besides uses as a card trick, the underlying phenomenon has applications in [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), [code breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_breaking), [software tamper protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_tamper_protection), [code self-synchronization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_self-synchronization), [control-flow resynchronization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control-flow_resynchronization), design of [variable-length codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable-length_code) and [variable-length instruction sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable-length_instruction_set), [web navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_navigation), object alignment, and others.

## Card trick {#Card_trick}

*Image: Kruskal\_count\_principle.svg.* Explanation of *Kruskal count*

The trick is performed with cards, but is more a magical-looking effect than a conventional magic trick. The magician has no access to the cards, which are manipulated by members of the audience. Thus [sleight of hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleight_of_hand) is not possible. Rather the effect is based on the mathematical fact that the output of a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876), under certain conditions, is typically independent of the input.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o21][^o24] A simplified version using the hands of a clock performed by [David Copperfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Copperfield_%28illusionist%29) is as follows.[^o3][^o8] A volunteer picks a number from one to twelve and does not reveal it to the magician. The volunteer is instructed to start from 12 on the clock and move clockwise by a number of spaces equal to the number of letters that the chosen number has when spelled out. This is then repeated, moving by the number of letters in the new number. The output after three or more moves does not depend on the initially chosen number and therefore the magician can predict it.
