# Valentine Joseph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Valentine Joseph"}

**Valentine Joseph** (27 January 1929 – 15 March 2017) was a [Sri Lankan Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils) mathematician, noted for his contributions to education.[^o7][^o5][^o22]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Joseph was born in [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang), [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya) to [Sri Lankan Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils) parents. His extended family were [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholics), with his cousin, [Dominic Vendargon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Vendargon), becoming the first [Archbishop of Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Kuala_Lumpur). Joseph commenced school at [St. Xavier's Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Xavier's_Institution) in Penang in 1935.[^o5]

Joseph prepared for the [University Entrance Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Entrance_Examination) as a science student studying pure mathematics, applied mathematics, physics and geography at [St. Patrick's College, Jaffna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_College,_Jaffna). In 1949, Joseph entered [University of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ceylon) (Colombo campus) to study for a four-year mathematics degree, obtaining a [first class degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_degree) in three years.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Joseph joined the University of Ceylon (Colombo campus) as an assistant lecturer in 1952 where he stayed for the next 42 years.[^o5] As one of the prominent mathematicians in [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka) at the time, he represented the country at many international gatherings and organisations such as the [International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in Trieste, Italy.[^o7][^o5] He was sent by the university to [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) in 1956 to commence a doctoral thesis in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), joining a renowned group of General Relativity specialists, under the guidance of Professor [Felix Pirani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Pirani), to work on developing Einstein's [Theory of General Relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_general_relativity).[^o5][^o8] Joseph went on to publish a number of academic papers pertaining to this subject.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12]

His output of articles and lectures covering these themes, spanned a period of over 60 years, from 1955 to 2016.[^o13]

During his university career, which spanned over four decades, Joseph taught several generations of students, first as a lecturer and later as a professor.[^o5][^o14][^o15][^o16] In addition to his university duties, Joseph was Chief Examiner for the GCE Advanced level examinations in mathematics for over twenty years, setting exam questions which were regarded to have been creative.[^o17] He also authored an A-level mathematics textbook in [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_language) entitled Gathikaya in 1994.[^o5][^o18][^o19] He maintained an interest in teaching throughout his later retirement, and in 2016 published an updated English edition of his book, *Dynamics: Newtonian Relativity*.[^o20][^o21]

Joseph gave his final lecture in 2016 entitled "Einstein: The Wunderkind".[^o22]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Joseph married Antonia (née Arulanandam) in 1965 and they had two children, Umesh and Arulesh.[^o7][^o24]

Joseph and his family were badly affected during the [ethnic riots of 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_July).[^o7] According to witnesses, before a mob ransacked his house in [Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo), Joseph was asked whether he was a [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils) or a [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people).[^o25] He replied in [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_language): "I am a human being". Following this statement, he was physically assaulted.[^o26]
