# Gyroelongated triangular cupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyroelongated triangular cupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyroelongated triangular cupola** is one of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) (*J*<sub>22</sub>). It can be constructed by attaching a [hexagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860125) to the base of a [triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1128465) (*J*<sub>3</sub>). This is called "gyroelongation", which means that an [antiprism](wikipedia://en/1851) is joined to the base of a solid, or between the bases of more than one solid.

:::

The gyroelongated triangular cupola can also be seen as a [gyroelongated triangular bicupola](wikipedia://en/1196315) (*J*<sub>44</sub>) with one triangular cupola removed. Like all [cupolae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupola), the [base](wikipedia://en/9579379) [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) has twice as many sides as the top (in this case, the bottom polygon is a [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) because the top is a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654)).

## Formulae {#Formulae}

The following [formulae](wikipedia://en/164040) for [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) and [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) can be used if all [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) are [regular](wikipedia://en/333306), with edge length *a*:[^o4]

$$V=\left(\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{61}{2}+18\sqrt{3}+30\sqrt{1+\sqrt{3}}}\right)a^3\approx3.51605...a^3$$

$$A=\left(3+\frac{11\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)a^2\approx12.5263...a^2$$

## Dual polyhedron {#Dual_polyhedron}

The dual of the gyroelongated triangular cupola has 15 faces: 6 kites, 3 rhombi, and 6 pentagons.
