# Schnorr group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schnorr group"}

A **Schnorr group**, proposed by [Claus P. Schnorr](wikipedia://en/9439931), is a large prime-order [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, the [multiplicative group of integers modulo $p$](wikipedia://en/1195234) for some [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$. [^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

To generate such a group, generate $p$, $q$, $r$ such that

$$p = qr + 1$$

with $p$, $q$ prime. Then choose any $h$ in the range $1 < h < p$ until you find one such that

$$h^r \not\equiv 1\;(\text{mod}\;p).$$

This value

$$g = h^r\text{ mod }p$$

is a generator of a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ of order $q$.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Schnorr groups are useful in [discrete log](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_log) based [cryptosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptosystems) including [Schnorr signatures](wikipedia://en/450536) and [DSA](wikipedia://en/59470). In such applications, typically $p$ is chosen to be large enough to resist [index calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_calculus) and related methods of solving the discrete-log problem (perhaps 1024 to 3072 bits), while $q$ is large enough to resist the [birthday attack](wikipedia://en/189369) on discrete log problems, which works in any group (perhaps 160 to 256 bits). Because the Schnorr group is of prime order, it has no non-trivial proper subgroups, thwarting [confinement attacks](wikipedia://en/2173873) due to small subgroups. Implementations of protocols that use Schnorr groups must verify where appropriate that integers supplied by other parties are in fact members of the Schnorr group; $x$ is a member of the group if $0 < x < p$ and $x^q \equiv 1\;(\text{mod }p)$. Any member of the group except the element $1$ is also a generator of the group.

:::
