# Elongated triangular cupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Elongated triangular cupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **elongated triangular cupola** is a polyhedron constructed from a [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668) by attaching a [triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1128465). It is an example of a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The elongated triangular cupola is constructed from a [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668) by attaching a [triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1128465) onto one of its bases, a process known as the [elongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29). This cupola covers the hexagonal face so that the resulting polyhedron has four [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), nine [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), and one [regular hexagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon). A [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) polyhedron in which all of the faces are [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) is the [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). The elongated triangular cupola is one of them, enumerated as the eighteenth Johnson solid $J_{18}$.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The surface area of an elongated triangular cupola $A$ is the sum of all polygonal face's area. The volume of an elongated triangular cupola can be ascertained by dissecting it into a cupola and a hexagonal prism, after which summing their volume. Given the edge length $a$, its surface and volume can be formulated as:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac{18 + 5\sqrt{3}}{2}a^2 &\approx 13.330a^2, \\ V &= \frac{5\sqrt{2} + 9\sqrt{3}}{6}a^3 &\approx 3.777a^3. \end{aligned}$$

*Image: J18 elongated triangular cupola.stl.* 3D model of an elongated triangular cupola

It has the same [three-dimensional same symmetry](wikipedia://en/2694525) as the triangular cupola, the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $C_{3\mathrm{v}}$ of order 6. Its [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) can be calculated by adding the angle of a triangular cupola and a hexagonal prism:

- the dihedral angle of an elongated triangular cupola between square-to-triangle is that of a triangular cupola between those: 125.3°;
- the dihedral angle of an elongated triangular cupola between two adjacent squares is that of a hexagonal prism, the internal angle of its base 120°;
- the dihedral angle of a hexagonal prism between square-to-hexagon is 90°, that of a triangular cupola between square-to-hexagon is 54.7°, and that of a triangular cupola between triangle-to-hexagonal is an 70.5°. Therefore, the elongated triangular cupola between square-to-square and triangle-to-square, on the edge where a triangular cupola is attached to a hexagonal prism, is 90° + 54.7° = 144.7° and 90° + 70.5° = 166.5° respectively.
