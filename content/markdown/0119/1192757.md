# Skellam distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Skellam distribution"}

The **Skellam distribution** is the [discrete probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution) of the difference $N_1-N_2$ of two [statistically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistically_independent) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) $N_1$ and $N_2,$ each [Poisson-distributed](wikipedia://en/23009144) with respective [expected values](wikipedia://en/9653) $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$. It is useful in describing the statistics of the difference of two images with simple [photon noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon_noise), as well as describing the [point spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_betting) distribution in sports where all scored points are equal, such as [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball), [hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey) and [soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer).

:::

The distribution is also applicable to a special case of the difference of dependent Poisson random variables, but just the obvious case where the two variables have a common additive random contribution which is cancelled by the differencing: see Karlis & Ntzoufras (2003) for details and an application.

The [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) for the Skellam distribution for a difference $K=N_1-N_2$ between two independent Poisson-distributed random variables with means $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ is given by:

$$p(k;\mu_1,\mu_2) = \Pr\{K=k\} = e^{-(\mu_1+\mu_2)} \left({\mu_1\over\mu_2}\right)^{k/2} I_k(2\sqrt{\mu_1\mu_2})$$

where *I*<sub>*k*</sub>(*z*) is the [modified Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700#Modified_Bessel_functions) of the first kind. Since *k* is an integer we have that *I*<sub>*k*</sub>(*z*) = *I*<sub>\|*k*|</sub>(*z*).

## Derivation {#Derivation}

The [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) of a [Poisson-distributed](wikipedia://en/23009144) random variable with mean μ is given by

$$p(k;\mu) = {\mu^k\over k!} e^{-\mu}.\,$$

for $k \ge 0$ (and zero otherwise). The Skellam probability mass function for the difference of two independent counts $K = N_1 - N_2$ is the [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) of two Poisson distributions: ([Skellam](wikipedia://en/31693107), 1946)

$$\begin{aligned} p(k;\mu_1,\mu_2) & =\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty p(k+n;\mu_1)p(n;\mu_2) \\ & =e^{-(\mu_1+\mu_2)}\sum_{n=\max(0,-k)}^\infty {{\mu_1^{k+n}\mu_2^n}\over{n!(k+n)!}} \end{aligned}$$

Since the Poisson distribution is zero for negative values of the count $(p(N<0;\mu)=0)$, the second sum is only taken for those terms where $n \ge 0$ and $n+k \ge 0$. It can be shown that the above sum implies that

$$\frac{p(k;\mu_1,\mu_2)}{p(-k;\mu_1,\mu_2)} = \left(\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}\right)^k$$

so that:

$$p(k;\mu_1,\mu_2)= e^{-(\mu_1+\mu_2)} \left({\mu_1\over\mu_2}\right)^{k/2} I_{|k|}(2\sqrt{\mu_1\mu_2})$$

where *I* <sub>k</sub>(z) is the [modified Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700#Modified_Bessel_functions) of the first kind. The special case for $\mu_1 = \mu_2 (= \mu)$ is given by Irwin (1937):

$$p{\left(k;\mu,\mu\right)} = e^{-2\mu}I_{|k|}(2\mu).$$

Using the limiting values of the modified Bessel function for small arguments, we can recover the Poisson distribution as a special case of the Skellam distribution for $\mu_2 = 0$.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

As it is a discrete probability function, the Skellam probability mass function is normalized:

$$\sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty p(k;\mu_1,\mu_2) = 1.$$

We know that the [probability generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability-generating_function) (pgf) for a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) is:

$$G\left(t;\mu\right)= e^{\mu(t-1)}.$$

It follows that the pgf, $G(t;\mu_1,\mu_2)$, for a Skellam probability mass function will be:

$$\begin{aligned} G(t;\mu_1,\mu_2) & = \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty p(k;\mu_1,\mu_2)t^k \\[4pt] & = G\left(t;\mu_1\right)G\left(1/t;\mu_2\right) \\[4pt] & = e^{-(\mu_1+\mu_2)+\mu_1 t+\mu_2/t}. \end{aligned}$$

Notice that the form of the [probability-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability-generating_function) implies that the distribution of the sums or the differences of any number of independent Skellam-distributed variables are again Skellam-distributed. It is sometimes claimed that any linear combination of two Skellam distributed variables are again Skellam-distributed, but this is clearly not true since any multiplier other than $\pm 1$ would change the [support](wikipedia://en/381013) of the distribution and alter the pattern of [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) in a way that no Skellam distribution can satisfy.

The [moment-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function) is given by:

$$M\left(t;\mu_1,\mu_2\right) = G(e^t;\mu_1,\mu_2) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty { t^k \over k!}\,m_k$$

which yields the raw moments *m*<sub>*k*</sub> . Define:

$$\Delta\ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \mu_1-\mu_2$$

$$\mu\ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \tfrac{1}{2}(\mu_1+\mu_2).$$

Then the raw moments *m*<sub>*k*</sub> are

$$\begin{aligned} m_1 &= \Delta \\ m_2 &= 2\mu + \Delta^2 \\ m_3 &= \Delta \left(1 + 6\mu + \Delta^2\right) \end{aligned}$$

The [central moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_about_the_mean) *M*<sub>*k*</sub> are

$$\begin{aligned} M_2 &= 2\mu, \\ M_3 &= \Delta, \\ M_4 &= 2\mu + 12\mu^2.\, \end{aligned}$$

The [mean](wikipedia://en/9653), [variance](wikipedia://en/32344), [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212), and [kurtosis excess](wikipedia://en/16848) are respectively:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname E(n) & = \Delta, \\[4pt] \sigma^2 & = 2\mu, \\[4pt] \gamma_1 & = \Delta/(2\mu)^{3/2}, \\[4pt] \gamma_2 & = 1/2. \end{aligned}$$

The [cumulant-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulant-generating_function) is given by:

$$K(t;\mu_1,\mu_2)\ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \ln(M(t;\mu_1,\mu_2)) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{ t^k }{ k!}\,\kappa_k$$

which yields the [cumulants](wikipedia://en/359684):

$$\begin{aligned} \kappa_{2k} &= 2\mu, \\ \kappa_{2k+1} &= \Delta . \end{aligned}$$

For the special case when *μ*<sub>1</sub> = *μ*<sub>2</sub>, an [asymptotic expansion](wikipedia://en/642090) of the [modified Bessel function of the first kind](wikipedia://en/4700) yields for large *μ*:

$$p(k;\mu,\mu)\sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\mu}} \left[1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(-1\right)^n \frac{ \left\{4k^2-1^2\right\} \left\{4k^2-3^2\right\} \cdots \left\{4k^2-(2n-1)^2\right\} }{ n!\,2^{3n}\,(2\mu)^n }\right].$$

(Abramowitz & Stegun 1972, p. 377). Also, for this special case, when *k* is also large, and of [order](wikipedia://en/44578) of the square root of 2*μ*, the distribution tends to a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462):

$$p(k;\mu,\mu) \sim \frac{e^{-k^2/4\mu}}{\sqrt{4\pi\mu}}.$$

These special results can easily be extended to the more general case of different means.

### Bounds on weight above zero {#Bounds_on_weight_above_zero}

If $X \sim \operatorname{Skellam} (\mu_1, \mu_2)$, with $\mu_1 < \mu_2$, then

$$\frac{\exp\left[-\left(\sqrt{\mu_1} -\sqrt{\mu_2}\right)^2 \right]}{\left(\mu_1 + \mu_2\right)^2} - \frac{e^{-(\mu_1 + \mu_2)}}{2\sqrt{\mu_1 \mu_2}} - \frac{e^{-(\mu_1 + \mu_2)}}{4\mu_1 \mu_2} \leq \Pr\{X \geq 0\} \leq \exp \left[- \left(\sqrt{\mu_1} -\sqrt{\mu_2}\right)^2\right]$$

Details can be found in Poisson distribution#Poisson races
