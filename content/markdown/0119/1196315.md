# Gyroelongated triangular bicupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyroelongated triangular bicupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyroelongated triangular bicupola** is one of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) ($J_{44}$). As the name suggests, it can be constructed by gyroelongating a triangular bicupola (either [triangular orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1196250), $J_{27}$, or the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280)) by inserting a [hexagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860125) between its congruent halves.

:::

The gyroelongated triangular bicupola is one of five Johnson solids which are [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160), meaning that they have a "left-handed" and a "right-handed" form. In the illustration to the right, each square face on the bottom half of the figure is connected by a path of two triangular faces to a square face above it and to the right. In the figure of opposite chirality (the mirror image of the illustrated figure), each bottom square would be connected to a square face above it and to the left. The two chiral forms of $J_{44}$ are not considered different Johnson solids.

## Formulae {#Formulae}

The following [formulae](wikipedia://en/164040) for [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) and [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) can be used if all [faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faces_%28geometry%29) are [regular](wikipedia://en/333306), with edge length *a*:[^o4]

$$V= \sqrt{2} \left(\frac{5}{3}+\sqrt{1+\sqrt{3}}\right) a^3 \approx 4.69456...a^3$$

$$A=\left(6+5\sqrt{3}\right)a^2 \approx 14.6603...a^2$$
