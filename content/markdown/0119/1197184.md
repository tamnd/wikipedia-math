# Formal proof

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formal proof"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **formal proof** or **derivation** is a finite [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [sentences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposition_%28philosophy%29) (known as [well-formed formulas](wikipedia://en/404582) when relating to [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939)), each of which is an [axiom](wikipedia://en/928), is an assumption, or [follows from](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follows_from) the preceding sentences in the sequence, according to the [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311). It differs from a natural language argument in that it is rigorous, unambiguous and mechanically verifiable.[^o2] If the set of assumptions is empty, then the last sentence in a formal proof is called a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of the [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102). The notion of theorem is generally [effective](wikipedia://en/12979890), but there may be no method by which we can reliably find proof of a given sentence or determine that none exists. The concepts of [Fitch-style proof](wikipedia://en/7316682), [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329) and [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072) are [generalizations](wikipedia://en/12746) of the concept of proof.[^o3][^o4]

:::

The theorem is a syntactic consequence of all the well-formed formulas preceding it in the proof. For a well-formed formula to qualify as part of a proof, it must be the result of applying a rule of the [deductive apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive_apparatus) (of some formal system) to the previous well-formed formulas in the proof sequence.

Formal proofs often are constructed with the help of computers in [interactive theorem proving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_theorem_proving) (e.g., through the use of proof checker and [automated theorem prover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_theorem_prover)).[^o5] Significantly, these proofs can be checked automatically, also by computer. Checking formal proofs is usually simple, while the problem of *finding* proofs (automated theorem proving) is usually [computationally intractable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computationally_intractable#Intractability) and/or only [semi-decidable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-decidable), depending upon the formal system in use.

## Background {#Background}

### Formal language {#Formal_language}

A *formal language* is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of finite [sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_%28mathematics%29) of [symbols](wikipedia://en/37673). Such a language can be defined without [reference](wikipedia://en/20110874) to any [meanings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meaning_%28linguistics%29) of any of its expressions; it can exist before any [interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) is assigned to it – that is, before it has any meaning. Formal proofs are expressed in some formal languages.

### Formal grammar {#Formal_grammar}

A *formal grammar* (also called *formation rules*) is a precise description of the [well-formed formulas](wikipedia://en/404582) of a formal language. It is synonymous with the set of [strings](wikipedia://en/27701) over the [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet) of the formal language which constitute well formed formulas. However, it does not describe their [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107) (i.e. what they mean).

### Formal systems {#Formal_systems}

A *formal system* (also called a *logical calculus*, or a *logical system*) consists of a formal language together with a deductive apparatus (also called a *deductive system*). The deductive apparatus may consist of a set of [transformation rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_rule) (also called *inference rules*) or a set of [axioms](wikipedia://en/928), or have both. A formal system is used to [derive](wikipedia://en/183478) one expression from one or more other expressions.

### Interpretations {#Interpretations}

An *interpretation* of a formal system is the assignment of meanings to the symbols, and truth values to the sentences of a formal system. The study of interpretations is called [formal semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_semantics_%28logic%29). *Giving an interpretation* is synonymous with *constructing a [model](wikipedia://en/4055928)*.
