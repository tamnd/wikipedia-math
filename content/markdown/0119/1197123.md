# Initial topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Initial topology"}

In [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **initial topology** (or **induced topology**[^o3] or **weak topology**[^o4] or **limit topology** or **projective topology**) on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $X,$ with respect to a family of functions on $X,$ is the [coarsest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarsest_topology) on $X$ that makes those functions [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29).

:::

The [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) and [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910) constructions are both special cases of initial topologies. Indeed, the initial topology construction can be viewed as a generalization of these.

The [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) notion is the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736), which for a given family of functions mapping to a set $Y$ is the [finest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finest_topology) on $Y$ that makes those functions continuous.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a set $X$ and an [indexed family](wikipedia://en/23624339) $\left(Y_i\right)_{i \in I}$ of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) with functions

$$f_i : X \to Y_i,$$

the initial topology $\tau$ on $X$ is the [coarsest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarsest_topology) on $X$ such that each

$$f_i : (X, \tau) \to Y_i$$

is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29).

::: {#o7 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition in terms of open sets"}

**Definition in terms of open sets**

If $\left(\tau_i\right)_{i \in I}$ is a family of topologies $X$ indexed by $I \neq \varnothing,$ then the [least upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_upper_bound) topology of these topologies is the coarsest topology on $X$ that is finer than each $\tau_i.$ This topology always exists and it is equal to the [topology generated by](wikipedia://en/395395) $\bigcup_{i \in I} \tau_i.$[^n1]

If for every $i \in I,$ $\sigma_i$ denotes the topology on $Y_i,$ then $f_i^{-1}\left(\sigma_i\right) = \left\{f_i^{-1}(V) : V \in \sigma_i\right\}$ is a topology on $X$, and the initial topology of the $Y_i$ by the mappings $f_i$ is the least upper bound topology of the $I$-indexed family of topologies $f_i^{-1}\left(\sigma_i\right)$ (for $i \in I$).[^n1] Explicitly, the initial topology is the collection of open sets [generated](wikipedia://en/395395) by all sets of the form $f_i^{-1}(U),$ where $U$ is an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in $Y_i$ for some $i \in I,$ under finite intersections and arbitrary unions.

Sets of the form $f_i^{-1}(V)$ are often called [cylinder sets](wikipedia://en/2613658). If $I$ contains [exactly one element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton_set), then all the open sets of the initial topology $(X, \tau)$ are cylinder sets.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Several topological constructions can be regarded as special cases of the initial topology.

- The [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) is the initial topology on the subspace with respect to the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516).
- The [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910) is the initial topology with respect to the family of [projection maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projection_map).
- The [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) (also called projective limit) of any [inverse system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_system) of spaces and continuous maps is the set-theoretic inverse limit together with the initial topology determined by the canonical morphisms.
- The [weak topology](wikipedia://en/33662) on a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space) is the initial topology with respect to the [continuous linear forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_form) of its [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988).
- Given a [family](wikipedia://en/23624339) of topologies $\left\{\tau_i\right\}$ on a fixed set $X$ the initial topology on $X$ with respect to the functions $\operatorname{id}_i : X \to \left(X, \tau_i\right)$ is the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) (or join) of the topologies $\left\{\tau_i\right\}$ in the [lattice of topologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_of_topologies) on $X.$ That is, the initial topology $\tau$ is the topology generated by the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of the topologies $\left\{\tau_i\right\}.$
- A topological space is [completely regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_regular) if and only if it has the initial topology with respect to its family of ([bounded](wikipedia://en/311509)) real-valued continuous functions.
- Every topological space $X$ has the initial topology with respect to the family of continuous functions from $X$ to the [Sierpiński space](wikipedia://en/387651).

## Properties {#Properties}

### Characteristic property {#Characteristic_property}

The initial topology on $X$ can be characterized by the following characteristic property: A function $g$ from some space $Z$ to $X$ is continuous if and only if $f_i \circ g$ is continuous for each $i \in I.$[^n2]

*Image: InitialTopology-01.png.* Characteristic property of the initial topology

Note that, despite looking quite similar, this is not a [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245). A categorical description is given below.

A [filter](wikipedia://en/12646549) $\mathcal{B}$ on $X$ [converges to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_filter) a point $x \in X$ if and only if the [prefilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefilter) $f_i(\mathcal{B})$ [converges to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_prefilter) $f_i(x)$ for every $i \in I.$[^n2]

::: {#o18 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Evaluation map (topology)"}

### Evaluation {#Evaluation}

By the universal property of the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910), we know that any family of continuous maps $f_i : X \to Y_i$ determines a unique continuous map

$$\begin{alignedat}{4} f :\;&& X &&\;\to \;& \prod_i Y_i \\[0.3ex] && x &&\;\mapsto\;& \left(f_i(x)\right)_{i \in I} \\ \end{alignedat}$$

This map is known as the **evaluation map**.

A family of maps $\{f_i : X \to Y_i\}$ is said to *[separate points](wikipedia://en/13972359)* in $X$ if for all $x \neq y$ in $X$ there exists some $i$ such that $f_i(x) \neq f_i(y).$ The family $\{f_i\}$ separates points if and only if the associated evaluation map $f$ is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective).

The evaluation map $f$ will be a [topological embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_embedding) if and only if $X$ has the initial topology determined by the maps $\{f_i\}$ and this family of maps separates points in $X.$

:::

### Hausdorffness {#Hausdorffness}

If $X$ has the initial topology induced by $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ and if every $Y_i$ is Hausdorff, then $X$ is a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) if and only if these maps separate points on $X.$[^n1]

### Transitivity of the initial topology {#Transitivity_of_the_initial_topology}

If $X$ has the initial topology induced by the $I$-indexed family of mappings $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ and if for every $i \in I,$ the topology on $Y_i$ is the initial topology induced by some $J_i$-indexed family of mappings $\left\{g_j : Y_i \to Z_j\right\}$ (as $j$ ranges over $J_i$), then the initial topology on $X$ induced by $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ is equal to the initial topology induced by the ${\textstyle \bigcup\limits_{i \in I} J_i}$-indexed family of mappings $\left\{g_j \circ f_i : X \to Z_j\right\}$ as $i$ ranges over $I$ and $j$ ranges over $J_i.$[^n3] Several important corollaries of this fact are now given.

In particular, if $S \subseteq X$ then the subspace topology that $S$ inherits from $X$ is equal to the initial topology induced by the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) $S \to X$ (defined by $s \mapsto s$). Consequently, if $X$ has the initial topology induced by $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ then the subspace topology that $S$ inherits from $X$ is equal to the initial topology induced on $S$ by the restrictions $\left\{\left.f_i\right|_S : S \to Y_i\right\}$ of the $f_i$ to $S.$[^n2]

The [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910) on $\prod_i Y_i$ is equal to the initial topology induced by the canonical projections $\operatorname{pr}_i : \left(x_k\right)_{k \in I} \mapsto x_i$ as $i$ ranges over $I.$[^n2] Consequently, the initial topology on $X$ induced by $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ is equal to the inverse image of the product topology on $\prod_i Y_i$ by the evaluation map $f : X \to \prod_i Y_i\,.$[^n2] Furthermore, if the maps $\left\{f_i\right\}_{i \in I}$ separate points on $X$ then the evaluation map is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) onto the subspace $f(X)$ of the product space $\prod_i Y_i.$[^n2]

### Separating points from closed sets {#Separating_points_from_closed_sets}

If a space $X$ comes equipped with a topology, it is often useful to know whether or not the topology on $X$ is the initial topology induced by some family of maps on $X.$ This section gives a sufficient (but not necessary) condition.

A family of maps $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ *separates points from closed sets* in $X$ if for all [closed sets](wikipedia://en/47279) $A$ in $X$ and all $x \not\in A,$ there exists some $i$ such that

$$f_i(x) \notin \operatorname{cl}(f_i(A))$$

where $\operatorname{cl}$ denotes the [closure operator](wikipedia://en/42309).

**Theorem**. A family of continuous maps $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ separates points from closed sets if and only if the cylinder sets $f_i^{-1}(V),$ for $V$ open in $Y_i,$ form a [base for the topology](wikipedia://en/59220) on $X.$

It follows that whenever $\left\{f_i\right\}$ separates points from closed sets, the space $X$ has the initial topology induced by the maps $\left\{f_i\right\}.$ The converse fails, since generally the cylinder sets will only form a subbase (and not a base) for the initial topology.

If the space $X$ is a [T<sub>0</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space), then any collection of maps $\left\{f_i\right\}$ that separates points from closed sets in $X$ must also separate points. In this case, the evaluation map will be an embedding.

::: {#o29 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Initial uniform structure"}

### Initial uniform structure {#Initial_uniform_structure}

If $\left(\mathcal{U}_i\right)_{i \in I}$ is a family of [uniform structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_structure) on $X$ indexed by $I \neq \varnothing,$ then the [least upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_upper_bound) uniform structure of $\left(\mathcal{U}_i\right)_{i \in I}$ is the coarsest uniform structure on $X$ that is finer than each $\mathcal{U}_i.$ This uniform always exists and it is equal to the [filter](wikipedia://en/12646549) on $X \times X$ generated by the [filter subbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_subbase) ${\textstyle \bigcup\limits_{i \in I} \mathcal{U}_i}.$[^n4] If $\tau_i$ is the topology on $X$ induced by the uniform structure $\mathcal{U}_i$ then the topology on $X$ associated with least upper bound uniform structure is equal to the least upper bound topology of $\left(\tau_i\right)_{i \in I}.$[^n4]

Now suppose that $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ is a family of maps and for every $i \in I,$ let $\mathcal{U}_i$ be a uniform structure on $Y_i.$ Then the initial uniform structure of the $Y_i$ by the mappings $f_i$ is the unique coarsest uniform structure $\mathcal{U}$ on $X$ making all $f_i : \left(X, \mathcal{U}\right) \to \left(Y_i, \mathcal{U}_i\right)$ [uniformly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_continuous).[^n4] It is equal to the least upper bound uniform structure of the $I$-indexed family of uniform structures $f_i^{-1}\left(\mathcal{U}_i\right)$ (for $i \in I$).[^n4] The topology on $X$ induced by $\mathcal{U}$ is the coarsest topology on $X$ such that every $f_i : X \to Y_i$ is continuous.[^n4] The initial uniform structure $\mathcal{U}$ is also equal to the coarsest uniform structure such that the identity mappings $\operatorname{id} : \left(X, \mathcal{U}\right) \to \left(X, f_i^{-1}\left(\mathcal{U}_i\right)\right)$ are uniformly continuous.[^n4]

**Hausdorffness**: The topology on $X$ induced by the initial uniform structure $\mathcal{U}$ is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) if and only if for whenever $x, y \in X$ are distinct ($x \neq y$) then there exists some $i \in I$ and some entourage $V_i \in \mathcal{U}_i$ of $Y_i$ such that $\left(f_i(x), f_i(y)\right) \not\in V_i.$[^n4] Furthermore, if for every index $i \in I,$ the topology on $Y_i$ induced by $\mathcal{U}_i$ is Hausdorff then the topology on $X$ induced by the initial uniform structure $\mathcal{U}$ is Hausdorff if and only if the maps $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\}$ separate points on $X$[^n4] (or equivalently, if and only if the evaluation map $f : X \to \prod_i Y_i$ is injective)

**Uniform continuity**: If $\mathcal{U}$ is the initial uniform structure induced by the mappings $\left\{f_i : X \to Y_i\right\},$ then a function $g$ from some uniform space $Z$ into $(X, \mathcal{U})$ is [uniformly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_continuous) if and only if $f_i \circ g : Z \to Y_i$ is uniformly continuous for each $i \in I.$[^n4]

**Cauchy filter**: A [filter](wikipedia://en/12646549) $\mathcal{B}$ on $X$ is a [Cauchy filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_filter) on $(X, \mathcal{U})$ if and only if $f_i\left(\mathcal{B}\right)$ is a Cauchy prefilter on $Y_i$ for every $i \in I.$[^n4]

**Transitivity of the initial uniform structure**: If the word "topology" is replaced with "uniform structure" in the statement of "transitivity of the initial topology" given above, then the resulting statement will also be true.

## Categorical description {#Categorical_description}

In the language of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the initial topology construction can be described as follows. Let $Y$ be the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from a [discrete category](wikipedia://en/808519) $J$ to the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) $\mathrm{Top}$ which maps $j\mapsto Y_j$. Let $U$ be the usual [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) from $\mathrm{Top}$ to $\mathrm{Set}$. The maps $f_j : X \to Y_j$ can then be thought of as a [cone](wikipedia://en/7533645) from $X$ to $UY.$ That is, $(X,f)$ is an object of $\mathrm{Cone}(UY) := (\Delta\downarrow{UY})$—the [category of cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_cones) to $UY.$ More precisely, this cone $(X,f)$ defines a $U$-structured cosink in $\mathrm{Set}.$

The forgetful functor $U : \mathrm{Top} \to \mathrm{Set}$ induces a functor $\bar{U} : \mathrm{Cone}(Y) \to \mathrm{Cone}(UY)$. The characteristic property of the initial topology is equivalent to the statement that there exists a [universal morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_morphism) from $\bar{U}$ to $(X,f);$ that is, a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) in the category $\left(\bar{U}\downarrow(X,f)\right).$ Explicitly, this consists of an object $I(X,f)$ in $\mathrm{Cone}(Y)$ together with a morphism $\varepsilon : \bar{U} I(X,f) \to (X,f)$ such that for any object $(Z,g)$ in $\mathrm{Cone}(Y)$ and morphism $\varphi : \bar{U}(Z,g) \to (X,f)$ there exists a unique morphism $\zeta : (Z,g) \to I(X,f)$ such that the following diagram commutes:

*Image: UniversalPropInitialTop.jpg.*

The assignment $(X,f) \mapsto I(X,f)$ placing the initial topology on $X$ extends to a functor $I : \mathrm{Cone}(UY) \to \mathrm{Cone}(Y)$ which is [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) to the forgetful functor $\bar{U}.$ In fact, $I$ is a right-inverse to $\bar{U}$; since $\bar{U}I$ is the identity functor on $\mathrm{Cone}(UY).$
