# Ott-Heinrich Keller

*Image: Ott-Heinrich Keller (cropped).jpg.* Ott-Heinrich Keller

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ott-Heinrich Keller"}

**Eduard Ott-Heinrich Keller** (22 June 1906 – 5 December 1990) was a German mathematician who worked in the fields of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He formulated the celebrated problem which is now called the [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823) in 1939.

:::

He was born in [Frankfurt–am-Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt–am-Main), and studied at the universities of Frankfurt, [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), Berlin and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). As a student of [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602) he wrote a dissertation on the [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) of space with [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285). This led to another 'Keller conjecture': the [Keller cube-tiling conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keller_cube-tiling_conjecture) from 1930.

Subsequently, he worked with [Georg Hamel](wikipedia://en/1509021) in Berlin, [habilitating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1933 with a thesis on [Cremona transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona_transformation). The Jacobian conjecture is quite naturally posed in that setting. The motivation for looking at rather general [polynomial transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_transformations), say of the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350), came from the [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763) for [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260).

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he taught in a naval college in [Flensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flensburg). After the war he had several positions, and was appointed a professor at [Martin Luther University of Halle-Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_of_Halle-Wittenberg) in 1952, as successor of [H. W. E. Jung](wikipedia://en/37050659).
