# Covariance intersection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Covariance intersection"}

**Covariance intersection** (**CI**) is an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) for combining two or more estimates of [state variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_variable) in a [Kalman filter](wikipedia://en/180855) when the correlation between them is unknown.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Items of information **a** and **b** are known and are to be fused into information item **c**. We know **a** and **b** have [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_%28mathematics%29)/covariance $\hat a$, $A$ and $\hat b$, $B$, but the cross [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) is not known. The covariance intersection update gives mean and covariance for **c** as

$$C^{-1} = \omega A^{-1} + (1-\omega) B^{-1} \, ,$$

$$\hat c = C(\omega A^{-1} \hat a + (1-\omega)B^{-1} \hat b) \, .$$

where *ω* is computed to minimize a selected norm, e.g., the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270), or the logarithm of the determinant. While it is necessary to solve an [optimization problem](wikipedia://en/1126536) for higher [dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics_and_physics%29), [closed-form](wikipedia://en/585143) solutions exist for lower dimensions.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

CI can be used in place of the conventional Kalman update equations to ensure that the resulting estimate is conservative, regardless of the correlation between the two estimates, with covariance strictly non-increasing according to the chosen measure. The use of a fixed measure is necessary for rigor to ensure that a sequence of updates does not cause the filtered [covariance](wikipedia://en/157059) to increase.[^o2][^o11]

:::

## Advantages {#Advantages}

According to a recent survey paper [^o13] and,[^o14] the covariance intersection has the following advantages:

1. The identification and computation of the cross covariances are completely avoided.
1. It yields a consistent fused estimate, and thus a non-divergent filter is obtained.
1. The accuracy of the fused estimate outperforms each local one.
1. It gives a common upper bound of actual estimation [error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_variable) variances, which has robustness with respect to unknown correlations.

These advantages have been demonstrated in the case of [simultaneous localization and mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_localization_and_mapping) (SLAM) involving over a million map features/beacons.[^o15]

## Motivation {#Motivation}

It is widely believed that unknown [correlations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlations) exist in a diverse range of [multi-sensor fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_fusion) problems. Neglecting the effects of unknown correlations can result in severe performance degradation, and even divergence. As such, it has attracted and sustained the attention of researchers for decades. However, owing to its intricate, unknown nature, it is not easy to come up with a satisfying scheme to address fusion problems with unknown correlations. If we ignore the correlations, which is the so-called "naive fusion",[^o17] it may lead to filter divergence. To compensate this kind of divergence, a common sub-optimal approach is to artificially increase the system noise. However, this [heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristics) requires considerable expertise and compromises the integrity of the Kalman filter framework.[^o18]
