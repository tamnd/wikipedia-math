# Kemnitz's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kemnitz's conjecture"}

In [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333), **Kemnitz's conjecture** states that every set of [integer lattice points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_lattice_point) in the plane has a large [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) whose [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) is also a lattice point. It was proved independently in the autumn of 2003 by [Christian Reiher](wikipedia://en/20113579), then an undergraduate student, and Carlos di Fiore, then a high school student.

:::

The exact formulation of this conjecture is as follows:

Let $n$ be a natural number and $S$ a set of $4n-3$ lattice points in plane. Then there exists a subset $S_1 \subseteq S$ with $n$ points such that the centroid of all points from $S_1$ is also a lattice point.

Kemnitz's conjecture was formulated in 1983 by Arnfried Kemnitz as a generalization of the [Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem](wikipedia://en/2546047), an analogous one-dimensional result stating that every $2n-1$ integers have a subset of size $n$ whose average is an integer. In 2000, Lajos Rónyai proved a weakened form of Kemnitz's conjecture for sets with $4n-2$ lattice points. Then, in 2003, Christian Reiher proved the full conjecture using the [Chevalley–Warning theorem](wikipedia://en/2934314).
