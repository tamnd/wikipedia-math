# Wanda Szmielew

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wanda Szmielew"}

**Wanda Szmielew** [née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Née) **Montlak** (5 April 1918 – 27 August 1976) was a Polish [mathematical logician](wikipedia://en/19636) who first proved the [decidability](wikipedia://en/913118) of the [first-order theory](wikipedia://en/10983) of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974).[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Wanda Montlak was born on 5 April 1918 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw). She completed high school in 1935 and married, taking the name Szmielew. In the same year she entered the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), where she studied logic under [Adolf Lindenbaum](wikipedia://en/1029330), [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064), [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075), and [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920). Her research at this time included work on the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), but it was interrupted by the 1939 [Invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland).[^o3]

Szmielew became a [surveyor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), during which time she continued her research on her own, developing a [decision procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_procedure) based on [quantifier elimination](wikipedia://en/2150441) for the theory of abelian groups. She also taught for the Polish underground. After the liberation of Poland, Szmielew took a position at the [University of Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Łódź), which was founded in May 1945. In 1947, she published her paper on the axiom of choice, earned a master's degree from the University of Warsaw, and moved to Warsaw as a senior assistant.[^o3][^o5]

In 1949 and 1950, Szmielew visited the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where Tarski had found a permanent position after being exiled from Poland for the war. She lived in the home of Tarski and his wife as Tarski's mistress, leaving her husband behind in Poland,[^o6] and completed a Ph.D. at Berkeley in 1950 under Tarski's supervision, with her dissertation consisting of her work on abelian groups.[^o3][^o5] For the 1955 journal publication of these results, Tarski convinced Szmielew to rephrase her work in terms of his theory of arithmetical functions, a decision that caused this work to be described by [Solomon Feferman](wikipedia://en/355401) as "unreadable".[^o8] Later work by (Eklof Fischer 1972) re-proved Szmielew's result using more standard [model-theoretic](wikipedia://en/19858) techniques.[^o8][^o10]

Returning to Warsaw as an assistant professor, her interests shifted to the [foundations of geometry](wikipedia://en/21317755). With [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk), she published a text on the subject in 1955 (translated into English in 1960), and another monograph, published posthumously in 1981 and (in English translation) 1983.[^o3][^o5]

She died of cancer on 27 August 1976 in Warsaw.[^o3]
