# Regular p-group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regular p-group"}

In [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) finite [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the concept of **regular *p*-group** captures some of the more important properties of [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664), but is general enough to include most "small" *p*-groups. Regular *p*-groups were introduced by (txt).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A finite *p*-group *G* is said to be **regular** if any of the following equivalent (Hall 1959, Ch. 12.4), (Huppert 1967, Kap. III §10) conditions are satisfied:

- For every *a*, *b* in *G*, there is a *c* in the [derived subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_subgroup) *H* of the subgroup *H* of *G* generated by *a* and *b*, such that *a*<sup>*p*</sup> · *b*<sup>*p*</sup> = (*ab*)<sup>*p*</sup> · *c*<sup>*p*</sup>.
- For every *a*, *b* in *G*, there are elements *c*<sub>*i*</sub> in the derived subgroup of the subgroup generated by *a* and *b*, such that *a*<sup>*p*</sup> · *b*<sup>*p*</sup> = (*ab*)<sup>*p*</sup> · *c*<sub>1</sub><sup>*p*</sup> ⋯ *c*<sub>k</sub><sup>*p*</sup>.
- For every *a*, *b* in *G* and every positive integer *n*, there are elements *c*<sub>*i*</sub> in the derived subgroup of the subgroup generated by *a* and *b* such that *a*<sup>*q*</sup> · *b*<sup>*q*</sup> = (*ab*)<sup>*q*</sup> · *c*<sub>1</sub><sup>*q*</sup> ⋯ *c*<sub>k</sub><sup>*q*</sup>, where *q* = *p*<sup>*n*</sup>.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Many familiar *p*-groups are regular:

- Every [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) *p*-group is regular.
- Every *p*-group of [nilpotency class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilpotency_class) strictly less than *p* is regular. This follows from the [Hall–Petresco identity](wikipedia://en/46237138).
- Every *p*-group of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) at most *p*<sup>*p*</sup> is regular.
- Every finite group of [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent_%28group_theory%29) *p* is regular.

However, many familiar *p*-groups are not regular:

- Every nonabelian 2-group is irregular.
- The [Sylow *p*-subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_subgroup) of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on *p*<sup>2</sup> points is irregular and of order *p*<sup>*p*+1</sup>.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A *p*-group is regular [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) every [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) generated by two elements is regular.

Every subgroup and [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) of a regular group is regular, but the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of regular groups need not be regular.

A 2-group is regular if and only if it is abelian. A 3-group with two generators is regular if and only if its derived subgroup is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327). Every *p*-group of odd order with cyclic derived subgroup is regular.

The subgroup of a *p*-group *G* generated by the elements of order dividing *p*<sup>*k*</sup> is denoted [Ω<sub>*k*</sub>(*G*)](wikipedia://en/23046974) and regular groups are well-behaved in that Ω<sub>*k*</sub>(*G*) is precisely the set of elements of order dividing *p*<sup>*k*</sup>. The subgroup generated by all *p*<sup>*k*</sup>-th powers of elements in *G* is denoted [℧<sub>*k*</sub>(*G*)](wikipedia://en/23046974). In a regular group, the [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_%28group_theory%29) \[G:℧<sub>*k*</sub>(*G*)\] is equal to the order of Ω<sub>*k*</sub>(*G*). In fact, commutators and powers interact in particularly simple ways (Huppert 1967, Kap III §10, Satz 10.8). For example, given normal subgroups *M* and *N* of a regular *p*-group *G* and nonnegative integers *m* and *n*, one has \[℧<sub>*m*</sub>(*M*),℧<sub>*n*</sub>(*N*)\] = ℧<sub>*m*+*n*</sub>(\[*M*,*N*\]).

- [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033)'s criteria of regularity of a *p*-group *G*: *G* is regular, if one of the following hold:
  1. \[*G*:℧<sub>1</sub>(*G*)\] \< *p*<sup>*p*</sup>
  1. \[*G*:℧<sub>1</sub>(*G*)| \< *p*<sup>*p*−1</sup>
  1. \|Ω<sub>1</sub>(*G*)| \< *p*<sup>*p*−1</sup>
