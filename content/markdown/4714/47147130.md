# Liouvillian function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Liouvillian function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Liouvillian functions** comprise a set of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) including the [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412) and their repeated [integrals](wikipedia://en/2823). Liouvillian functions can be [recursively defined](wikipedia://en/1047605) as integrals of other Liouvillian functions.

:::

More explicitly, a Liouvillian function is a function of one [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109) which is the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of a finite number of algebraic operations ( addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, exponentiation, and root extraction) and [antiderivatives](wikipedia://en/2823). The [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/17860) function does not need to be explicitly included since it is the integral of $1/x$.

It follows directly from the definition that the set of Liouvillian functions is [closed](wikipedia://en/293802) under arithmetic operations, composition, and integration. It is also closed under [differentiation](wikipedia://en/7921). It is not closed under [limits](wikipedia://en/285773) and [infinite sums](wikipedia://en/15287).

Liouvillian functions were introduced by [Joseph Liouville](wikipedia://en/341810) in a series of papers from 1833 to 1841.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

All [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412) are Liouvillian.

Examples of well-known functions which are Liouvillian but not elementary are the [nonelementary antiderivatives](wikipedia://en/1520379), for example:

- The [error function](wikipedia://en/177111), $\mathrm{erf}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,dt,$
- The [exponential](wikipedia://en/648614) (*Ei*), [logarithmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_integral) (*Li* or *li*) and [Fresnel](wikipedia://en/271143) (*S* and *C*) integrals.

All Liouvillian functions are solutions of [algebraic differential equations](wikipedia://en/9501827), but not conversely. Examples of functions which are solutions of algebraic differential equations but not Liouvillian include:[^o4]

- the [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700) (except special cases);
- the [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223) (except special cases).

Examples of functions which are *not* solutions of algebraic differential equations and thus not Liouvillian include all [transcendentally transcendental functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendentally_transcendental_function), such as:

- the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316);
- the [zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_function).

:::
