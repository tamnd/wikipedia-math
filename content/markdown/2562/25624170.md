# Carolyn S. Gordon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carolyn S. Gordon"}

**Carolyn S. Gordon** (born 1950)[^o3] is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is the Benjamin Cheney Professor of Mathematics at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College). She is most well known for giving a negative answer to the question "Can you hear the shape of a drum?" in her work with [David Webb](wikipedia://en/41665960) and [Scott A. Wolpert](wikipedia://en/47567558). She is a [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) winner and a 2010 [Noether Lecturer](wikipedia://en/11566873).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gordon received her [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree from [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University). She entered graduate studies at the [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis), earning her [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics in 1979. Her doctoral advisor was Edward Nathan Wilson and her thesis was on [isometry groups](wikipedia://en/1101694) of [homogeneous manifolds](wikipedia://en/363325). She completed a postdoc at [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology) and held positions at [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University) and in the [Arts and Sciences at Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_and_Sciences_at_Washington_University_in_St._Louis).

## Career {#Career}

*Image: Isospectral drums.svg.* This is the Gordon–Webb–Wolpert example of two flat surfaces with the same spectrum. Notice that both polygons have the same area and perimeter.

Gordon is most well known for her work in [isospectral geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isospectral_geometry), for which [hearing the shape of a drum](wikipedia://en/1072325) is the prototypical example. In 1966 [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425) asked whether the shape of a drum could be determined by the sound it makes (whether a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) is determined by the spectrum of its [Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356)). [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) observed that a theorem due to Witt implied the existence of a pair of 16-dimensional [tori](wikipedia://en/74800) that have the same spectrum but different shapes. However, the problem in two dimensions remained open until 1992, when Gordon, with coauthors Webb and Wolpert, constructed a pair of regions in the Euclidean plane that have different shapes but identical eigenvalues (see figure on right). In further work, Gordon and Webb produced convex isospectral domains in the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane)[^o6] and in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697).[^o7]

Gordon has written or coauthored over 30 articles on isospectral geometry including work on isospectral closed [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) with a common Riemannian covering. These isospectral Riemannian manifolds have the same local geometry but different topology. They can be found using the "Sunada method," due to [Toshikazu Sunada](wikipedia://en/22019166). In 1993 she found isospectral Riemannian manifolds which are not locally isometric and, since that time, has worked with coauthors to produce a number of other such examples.[^o8]

Gordon has also worked on projects concerning the [homology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_class), length spectrum (the collection of lengths of all [closed geodesics](wikipedia://en/19903176), together with multiplicities) and [geodesic flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_flow) on isospectral Riemannian manifolds.[^o7][^o9]

## Selected awards and honors {#Selected_awards_and_honors}

In 2001 Gordon and Webb were awarded the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for their 1996 *[American Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Scientist)* paper, "You can't hear the shape of a drum". In 1990 she was awarded an [AMS Centennial Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMS_Centennial_Fellowship) by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for outstanding early career research. In 1999 Gordon presented an AMS-MAA joint invited address. In 2010 she was selected as a [Noether Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noether_Lecturer).[^o11] In 2012 she became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o12] and of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o13] She was also an [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) Council member at large from 2005 to 2007.[^o14] In 2017 she was selected as a fellow of the Association for Women in Mathematics in the inaugural class.[^o15] Gordon was featured in the Women's History Month tribute in the March 2018 edition of the AMS Notices.[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Gordon is married to David Webb. She cites raising her daughter, Annalisa, as her greatest joy in life.[^o16]
