# Group stack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Group stack"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **group stack** is an [algebraic stack](wikipedia://en/717377) whose categories of points have group structures or even [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) structures in a compatible way.[^o2] It generalizes a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523), which is a scheme whose sets of points have group structures in a compatible way.

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A group scheme is a group-\\ stack. More generally, a **group algebraic-space**, an [algebraic-space](wikipedia://en/2394027) analog of a group scheme, is a group-stack.
- Over a field *k*, a **vector bundle stack** $\mathcal{V}$ on a [Deligne–Mumford stack](wikipedia://en/40639179) *X* is a group-stack such that there is a vector bundle *V* over *k* on *X* and a presentation $V \to \mathcal{V}$. It has an action by the affine line $\mathbb{A}^1$ corresponding to [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373).
- A [Picard stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_stack) is an example of a group-stack (or groupoid-stack).

:::

## Actions of group stacks {#Actions_of_group_stacks}

The definition of a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of a group stack is a bit tricky. First, given an algebraic stack *X* and a group scheme *G* on a base scheme *S*, a right action of *G* on *X* consists of

1. a [morphism](wikipedia://en/54323368) $\sigma: X \times G \to X$,
1. (associativity) a natural isomorphism $\sigma \circ (m \times 1_X) \overset{\sim}\to \sigma \circ (1_X \times \sigma)$, where *m* is the multiplication on *G*,
1. (identity) a natural isomorphism $1_X \overset{\sim}\to \sigma \circ (1_X \times e)$, where $e: S \to G$ is the identity section of *G*,

that satisfy the typical compatibility conditions.

If, more generally, *G* is a group stack, one then extends the above using local presentations.
