# Lindenbaum's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lindenbaum's lemma"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **Lindenbaum's lemma**, named after [Adolf Lindenbaum](wikipedia://en/1029330), states that any [consistent theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistent_theory) of [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic) can be extended to a [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335) consistent theory. The lemma is a special case of the [ultrafilter lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_lemma) for [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/54476844), applied to the [Lindenbaum algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindenbaum_algebra) of a theory.

:::

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

It is used in the proof of [Gödel's completeness theorem](wikipedia://en/12450), among other places.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

## Extensions {#Extensions}

The effective version of the lemma's statement, "every consistent [computably enumerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computably_enumerable) theory can be extended to a complete consistent computably enumerable theory," fails (provided [Peano arithmetic](wikipedia://en/25005#Peano_arithmetic_as_first-order_theory) is consistent) by [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem).

:::

## History {#History}

The lemma was not published by [Adolf Lindenbaum](wikipedia://en/1029330); it is originally attributed to him by [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920).[^o7]
