# Rank of a group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rank of a group"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) subject of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **rank of a group** *G*, denoted rank(*G*), can refer to the smallest [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of a [generating](wikipedia://en/99945) set for *G*, that is

:::

$$\operatorname{rank}(G)=\min\{ |X|: X\subseteq G, \langle X\rangle =G\}.$$

If *G* is a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042), then the rank of *G* is a non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). The notion of rank of a group is a group-theoretic analog of the notion of [dimension of a vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_of_a_vector_space). Indeed, for [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664), the rank of the group *P* is the dimension of the vector space *P*/Φ(*P*), where Φ(*P*) is the [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707).

The rank of a group is also often defined in such a way as to ensure subgroups have rank less than or equal to the whole group, which is automatically the case for dimensions of vector spaces, but not for groups such as [affine groups](wikipedia://en/298828). To distinguish these different definitions, one sometimes calls this rank the **subgroup rank**. Explicitly, the subgroup rank of a group *G* is the maximum of the ranks of its subgroups:

$$\operatorname{sr}(G)=\max_{H \leq G} \min\{ |X|: X \subseteq H, \langle X \rangle = H \}.$$

Sometimes the subgroup rank is restricted to abelian subgroups.

## Known facts and examples {#Known_facts_and_examples}

- For a nontrivial group *G*, we have rank(*G*) = 1 if and only if *G* is a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327). The trivial group *T* has rank(*T*) = 0, since the minimal generating set of *T* is the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566).
- For the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) $\mathbb Z^n$, we have ${\rm rank}(\mathbb Z^n)=n.$
- If *X* is a set and *G* = *F*(*X*) is the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) with free basis *X* then rank(*G*) = |*X*|.
- If a group *H* is a [homomorphic image](wikipedia://en/12396) (or a [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526)) of a group *G* then rank(*H*) ≤ rank(*G*).
- If *G* is a finite non-abelian [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) (e.g. *G = A<sub>n</sub>*, the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726), for *n* > 4) then rank(*G*) = 2. This fact is a consequence of the [Classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).
- If *G* is a finitely generated group and Φ(*G*) ≤ *G* is the [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707) of *G* (which is always normal in *G* so that the quotient group *G*/Φ(*G*) is defined) then rank(*G*) = rank(*G*/Φ(*G*)).[^o3]
- If *G* is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a closed (that is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and without boundary) connected [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) *M* then rank(*G*)≤*g*(*M*), where *g*(*M*) is the [Heegaard genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heegaard_genus) of *M*.[^o4]
- If *H*,*K* ≤ *F*(*X*) are [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) subgroups of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) *F*(*X*) such that the intersection $L=H\cap K$ is nontrivial, then *L* is finitely generated and

rank(*L*) − 1 ≤ 2(rank(*K*) − 1)(rank(*H*) − 1).

This result is due to [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308).[^o5][^o6] The [Hanna Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/19296207) states that in fact one always has rank(*L*) − 1 ≤ (rank(*K*) − 1)(rank(*H*) − 1). The [Hanna Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/19296207) has recently been solved by Igor Mineyev[^o7] and announced independently by Joel Friedman.[^o8]

- According to the classic [Grushko theorem](wikipedia://en/18715724), rank behaves additively with respect to taking [free products](wikipedia://en/360036), that is, for any groups *A* and *B* we have

rank(*A*$\ast$*B*) = rank(*A*) + rank(*B*).

- If $G=\langle x_1,\dots, x_n| r=1\rangle$ is a [one-relator group](wikipedia://en/51159172) such that *r* is not a [primitive element](wikipedia://en/43337163) in the free group *F*(*x*<sub>1</sub>,..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>), that is, *r* does not belong to a free basis of *F*(*x*<sub>1</sub>,..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>), then rank(*G*) = *n*.[^o9][^o10]
- The rank of a symmetry group is closely related to the complexity of the object (a molecule, a crystal structure) being under the action of the group. If *G* is a [crystallographic point group](wikipedia://en/663786), then rank(*G*) is up to 3.[^o11] If *G* is a [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214), then rank(*G*) = 2 to 4. The only wallpaper-group type of rank 4 is [*p*2*mm*](wikipedia://en/386214#Group_p2mm).[^o12] If *G* is a 3-dimensional [space group](wikipedia://en/463721), then rank(*G*) = 2 to 6. The only space-group type of rank 6 is *Pmmm*.[^o13]

## The rank problem {#The_rank_problem}

There is an algorithmic problem studied in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), known as the **rank problem**. The problem asks, for a particular class of [finitely presented groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) if there exists an algorithm that, given a finite presentation of a group from the class, computes the rank of that group. The rank problem is one of the harder algorithmic problems studied in group theory and relatively little is known about it. Known results include:

- The rank problem is algorithmically undecidable for the class of all [finitely presented groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group). Indeed, by a classical [result of Adian–Rabin](wikipedia://en/51425733), there is no algorithm to decide if a finitely presented group is trivial, so even the question of whether rank(*G*)=0 is undecidable for finitely presented groups.[^o15][^o16]
- The rank problem is decidable for finite groups and for finitely generated [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974).
- The rank problem is decidable for finitely generated [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569). The reason is that for such a group *G*, the [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707) of *G* contains the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) of *G* and hence the rank of *G* is equal to the rank of the [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) of *G*.[^o17]
- The rank problem is undecidable for [word hyperbolic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_hyperbolic_group).[^o18]
- The rank problem is decidable for torsion-free [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652).[^o19]
- The rank problem is open for finitely generated virtually abelian groups (that is containing an abelian subgroup of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581)), for virtually free groups, and for [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) groups.

## Generalizations and related notions {#Generalizations_and_related_notions}

The rank of a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) *G* can be equivalently defined as the smallest cardinality of a set *X* such that there exists an onto [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) *F*(*X*) → *G*, where *F*(*X*) is the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) with free basis *X*. There is a dual notion of **co-rank** of a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) *G* defined as the *largest* [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of *X* such that there exists an onto [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) *G* → *F*(*X*). Unlike rank, co-rank is always algorithmically computable for [finitely presented groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group),[^o21] using the algorithm of [Makanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennady_Semenovich_Makanin) and [Razborov](wikipedia://en/3533454) for solving systems of equations in free groups.[^o22][^o23] The notion of co-rank is related to the notion of a *cut number* for [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifolds).[^o24]

If *p* is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), then the *p*-**rank** of *G* is the largest rank of an [elementary abelian](wikipedia://en/4843202) *p*-subgroup.[^o25] The **sectional** *p*-**rank** is the largest rank of an elementary abelian *p*-section (quotient of a subgroup).
