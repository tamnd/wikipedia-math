# Scott Sheffield

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scott Sheffield"}

**Scott Sheffield** is a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o3] His primary research field is theoretical probability.

:::

## Research {#Research}

Much of Sheffield's work examines conformal invariant objects which arise in the study of two-dimensional statistical physics models. He studies the [Schramm–Loewner evolution](wikipedia://en/6706108) SLE(κ) and its relations to a variety of other random objects. For example, he proved that SLE describes the interface between two Liouville quantum gravity surfaces that have been [conformally welded](wikipedia://en/34437104) together.[^o5] In joint work with Oded Schramm, he showed that contour lines of the [Gaussian free field](wikipedia://en/9200590) are related to SLE(4).[^o6][^o7] With [Jason Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_P._Miller), he developed the theory of Gaussian free field flow lines, which include SLE(κ) for all values of κ, as well as many variants of SLE.[^o8]

Sheffield and Bertrand Duplantier proved the Knizhnik–Polyakov–Zamolodchikov (KPZ) relation for fractal scaling dimensions in Liouville quantum gravity.[^o9] Sheffield also defined [conformal loop ensembles](wikipedia://en/36356195), which serve as scaling limits of the collections of all interfaces in various statistical physics models.[^o10] In joint work with [Wendelin Werner](wikipedia://en/6607797), he described the conformal loop ensembles as the outer boundaries of clusters of Brownian loops.[^o11]

In addition to these contributions, Sheffield has also proved results regarding internal [diffusion-limited aggregation](wikipedia://en/1312515), [dimers](wikipedia://en/10565476), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), and Lipschitz extension theory.[^o12]

## Teaching {#Teaching}

Since 2011, Sheffield has taught 18.600 (formerly 18.440), the introductory probability course at MIT.[^o14]

Sheffield was a visiting professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) for the 2022 to 2023 academic year.[^o15]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Sheffield graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1998 with an A.B. and A.M. in mathematics. In 2003, he received his Ph.D. in mathematics from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). Before becoming a professor at MIT, Sheffield held postdoctoral positions at [Microsoft Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research), the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley), and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). He was also an associate professor at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University).[^o17]

## Awards {#Awards}

Scott Sheffield received the [Loève Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loève_Prize), the [Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Early_Career_Award_for_Scientists_and_Engineers), the [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship), and the [Rollo Davidson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize). He was also an invited speaker at the 2010 meeting of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) and a plenary speaker in 2022. In 2017 he received the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) jointly with [Jason Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_P._Miller).[^o19] He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2021.[^o20] In 2023 he received the Leonard Eisenbud Prize for Mathematics and Physics of the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) jointly with [Jason Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_P._Miller). In 2024, he received the [Henri Poincaré Prize](wikipedia://en/4890712) from the [International Association of Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/23897340).[^o21]
