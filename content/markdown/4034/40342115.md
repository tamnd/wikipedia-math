# Rhonda Hughes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rhonda Hughes"}

**Rhonda Jo Hughes** (born Rhonda Weisberg September 28, 1947)[^o3] is an American mathematician, the Helen Herrmann Professor Emeritus of Mathematics at [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Hughes grew up on the South Side of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago). She attended [Gage Park High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gage_Park_High_School), where she was a cheerleader and valedictorian of her class. She studied engineering at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign) for one and a half years, then left school and worked for six months before resuming her education at the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago) on an Illinois State Scholarship studying mathematics. There, she came under the mentorship of Yoram Sagher, who encouraged her to pursue graduate studies in mathematics.[^o3] She earned a Ph.D. from the same university in 1975, under the supervision of Shmuel Kantorovitz, with a dissertation entitled *Semi-Groups of Unbounded Linear Operators in Banach Space*.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

She began her teaching career at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University) then spent a year as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) at the [Bunting Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunting_Institute) of [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College). She moved to [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College) in 1980,[^o3] where she served as department chair for six years. She is the Helen Herrmann professor emeritus of mathematics at Bryn Mawr, and retired in 2011.[^o8]

She was president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM) 1987–1988.[^o3][^o9] She has served on the Commission on Physical Science, Mathematics, and Applications of the [United States National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council).[^o3] She served as an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) Council member at large from 1988 to 1990.[^o10]

She and [Sylvia Bozeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Bozeman) organized the Spelman-Bryn Mawr Summer Mathematics Program for female undergraduate students from 1992 to 1994.[^o3] In 1998, they founded the [EDGE Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_Program) (Enhancing Diversity in Graduate Education), a transition program for women entering graduate programs in the mathematical sciences. The program is now in its twentieth year.

Her most recent research involves [ill-posed problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ill-posed_problems).[^o4]

## Honors {#Honors}

In 1998, Hughes received the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o12]

In 2004 she received the AAAS Mentor Award for Lifetime Achievement, in 2010 the [Gweneth Humphreys Award](wikipedia://en/51370132) for Mentorship of Undergraduate Women in Mathematics of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698),[^o13] and in 2013 she received the Elizabeth Bingham Award of the Philadelphia Chapter of the [Association for Women in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Women_in_Science).[^o8]

In 2017, she was selected as a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the inaugural class.[^o14]
