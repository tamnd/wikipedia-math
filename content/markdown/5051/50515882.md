# Rolin Wavre

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rolin Wavre"}

**Rolin-Louis Wavre** (25 March 1896 in [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Neuchâtel) – 9 December 1949 in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva)) was a Swiss mathematician.[^o2]

:::

Wavre studied at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) and received his Ph.D. in 1921 from the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva), where he became a professor extraordinarius in 1922, and a professor ordinarius in 1934 (as successor to Charles Cailler). Wavre did research on, among other subjects, logic and the philosophy of mathematics, in which he was an adherent of [Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitzen_Egbertus_Jan_Brouwer)'s intuitionism. Independently of, and almost simultaneously with, [Leon Lichtenstein](wikipedia://en/31433458), he dealt with equilibrium figures of a heterogeneous fluid mass, with a view to applications to planetary systems in astrophysics.

In 1932, in Zürich, he was a plenary speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) with talk *L'aspect analytique du problème des figures planétaires*. He was an invited speaker at the ICM in 1920 in Strasbourg,[^o3] in 1928 in Bologna,[^o4] and in 1936 in Oslo. For the two years 1936 and 1937, he was the president of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society).

As an adolescent, Wavre attended Neuchâtel's gymnasium and was a close friend of [Jean Piaget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget) (who became a famous psychologist) and [Gustave Juvet](wikipedia://en/50557405) (who became a prominent mathematician).[^o5]
