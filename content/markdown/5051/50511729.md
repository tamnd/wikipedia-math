# Mark Stern

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mark Stern"}

**Mark A. Stern** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose focus has been on [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474), [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202), [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), and [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

:::

One of Stern's foremost accomplishments is his proof (joint with Leslie D. Saper) of the [Zucker conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucker_conjecture) concerning locally symmetric spaces.[^o2] Since about 2000, Stern has focused on geometric problems arising in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), ranging from [harmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic) theory to string theory and [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636).

Stern has taught at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) since 1985, and was promoted to [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 1992. He has been the mathematics department [chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman) but has focused primarily on research and teaching, with major grant support from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation). At Duke, he teaches such courses as [multivariable calculus](wikipedia://en/615222).[^o3]

Since 2010, Stern has spoken to advanced math audiences at the [Newton Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_Institute), [CUNY Graduate Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUNY_Graduate_Center), [U.C. Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.C._Irvine), [Johns Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland), and multiple academic groups.[^o4]

## Academic background {#Academic_background}

Prior to Duke, Stern was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), where he received his Ph.D. in 1985. His thesis advisor was [S.T. Yau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.T._Yau). Stern studied math at [Texas A&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M), where he received his B.S. degree in 1980, before moving to Princeton. Stern grew up in Dallas, where he graduated from [St. Mark's School of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_School_of_Texas).

Stern is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and has won an [Alfred P. Sloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan) Fellowship and a Presidential Young Investigator Award.[^o6][^o7][^o2] In 2014, Stern was inducted into the Academy of Distinguished Former Students at Texas A&M.[^o2]
