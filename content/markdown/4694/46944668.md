# Friedman's SSCG function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Friedman's SSCG function"}

**Friedman's SSCG function** is a [mathematical function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_function) defined by [Harvey Friedman](wikipedia://en/3038108). It is defined by $\text{SSCG}(k)$ as the largest integer $n$ satisfying the following:

:::

There is a sequence $G_1,\ldots,G_n$ of simple subcubic graphs such that each $G_i$ has at most $i+k$ vertices and for no $i < j$ is $G_i$ [homeomorphically](wikipedia://en/353021) embeddable into $G_j$.

Later, Friedman defined the more general subcubic graphs $\text{SCG}(k)$.

## Background {#Background}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **simple subcubic graph** (**SSCG**) is a finite simple [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) in which each [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) has a [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_degree) of at most three. Suppose we have a sequence of simple subcubic graphs $G_1$, $G_2$, ... such that each graph $G_i$ has at most $i+k$ vertices (for some integer $k$) and for no $i<j$ is $G_i$ [homeomorphically embeddable](wikipedia://en/353021) into (i.e. is a [graph minor](wikipedia://en/353042) of) $G_j$.

The [Robertson–Seymour theorem](wikipedia://en/351769) proves that subcubic graphs (simple or not) are well-quasi-ordered by homeomorphic embeddability, implying such a sequence cannot be infinite. Then, by applying [Kőnig's lemma](wikipedia://en/153788) on the tree of such sequences under extension, for each value of $k$ there is a sequence with maximal length. The function $\text{SSCG}(k)$ denotes that length for simple subcubic graphs. The function $\text{SCG}(k)$ denotes that length for (general) subcubic graphs.

[Harvey Friedman](wikipedia://en/3038108) defined two functions: SSCG and SCG.

## SSCG function {#SSCG_function}

*Image: SSCG(3) sequence.png.* A sequence of subcubic graphs. The $n$-th graph in the sequence contains at most $n+3$ vertices, and no graph is homeomorphically embeddable within any later graph in the sequence. $\operatorname{SSCG}(3)$ is defined to be the longest possible length of such a sequence.

Friedman defined $\text{SSCG}(k)$ as the largest integer $n$ satisfying the following:[^o4]

There is a sequence $G_1,\ldots,G_n$ of simple subcubic graphs such that each $G_i$ has at most $i+k$ vertices and for no $i < j$ is $G_i$ [homeomorphically](wikipedia://en/353021) embeddable into $G_j$.

The first few terms of the sequence are

$$\operatorname{SSCG}(0) = 2,$$

$\operatorname{SSCG}(1) = 5,$ and

$$\operatorname{SSCG}(2) = 3\cdot2^{3\cdot2^{95}}\!\!-8 = 3\cdot2^{118\,842\,243\,771\,396\,506\,390\,315\,925\,504}\! - 8$$

$$\qquad \qquad \; \approx\, 3.241\,704\,229 \cdot 10^{35\,775\,080\,127\,201\,286\,522\,908\,640\,065}$$

$$\qquad \qquad \; \approx\, 10^{3.5775 \,\cdot\, 10^{28}} .$$

It has been shown that the next term, $\text{SSCG}(3)$, is greater than [TREE(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TREE%283%29). Friedman showed that $\text{SSCG}(13)$ is greater than the halting time of any [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) that can be proved to halt in [Π${}^{1}_{1}$-CA<sub>0</sub>](wikipedia://en/326365#Π11_comprehension_Π11-CA0) with at most $2\uparrow\uparrow2000$ symbols, and that it cannot be proved to exist in that theory with less than $2\uparrow\uparrow1000$ symbols, where $\uparrow\uparrow$ denotes [tetration](wikipedia://en/482379). He does this using a similar idea as with a similar statement he proved about $\text{TREE}(3)$.[^o4]

## SCG function {#SCG_function}

Later, Friedman realized there was no good reason for imposing "simple" on subcubic graphs. He relaxes the condition and defines $\text{SCG}(k)$ as the largest $n$ satisfying:[^o9]

There is a sequence $G_1,\ldots,G_n$ of subcubic graphs such that each $G_i$ has at most $i+k$ vertices and for no $i < j$ is $G_i$ homeomorphically embeddable into $G_j$.

The first term of the sequence is $\text{SCG}(0)=6$, while the next term $\text{SCG}(1)$ is bigger than [Graham's number](wikipedia://en/234232). Furthermore, $\text{SCG}(3)$ is bigger than $\text{TREE}^{\text{TREE}(3)}(3)$.[^o6]

Adam P. Goucher claims there is no qualitative difference between the asymptotic growth rates of SSCG and SCG. He writes "It's clear that $\text{SCG}(n) \ge \text{SSCG}(n)$, but I can also prove $\text{SSCG}(4n+3) \ge \text{SCG}(n)$".[^o10]
