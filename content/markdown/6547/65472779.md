# Kovner–Besicovitch measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kovner–Besicovitch measure"}

*Image: Symmetry measure of Reuleaux triangle.svg.* The largest centrally symmetric subset (central shaded region) of a [Reuleaux triangle](wikipedia://en/580252) and its reflection across the center of symmetry of the subset

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry) the **Kovner–Besicovitch measure** is a number defined for any bounded [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) describing how close to being [centrally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_symmetry) it is. It is the fraction of the area of the set that can be covered by its largest centrally symmetric subset.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

This measure is one for a set that is centrally symmetric, and less than one for sets whose closure is not centrally symmetric. It is invariant under [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449) of the plane. If $c$ is the center of symmetry of the largest centrally-symmetric set within a given convex body $K$, then the centrally-symmetric set itself is the intersection of $K$ with its reflection across $c$.

:::

## Minimizers {#Minimizers}

The convex sets with the smallest possible Kovner–Besicovitch measure are the triangles, for which the measure is 2/3. The result that triangles are the minimizers of this measure is known as **Kovner's theorem** or the **Kovner–Besicovitch theorem**, and the inequality bounding the measure above 2/3 for all convex sets is the **Kovner–Besicovitch inequality**. The [curve of constant width](wikipedia://en/580238) with the smallest possible Kovner–Besicovitch measure is the [Reuleaux triangle](wikipedia://en/580252).

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

The Kovner–Besicovitch measure of any given convex polygon with $n$ vertices can be found in time $O(n\log n)$ by determining a translation of the reflection of the polygon that has the largest possible overlap with the unreflected polygon.

## History {#History}

[Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) writes that the Kovner–Besicovitch theorem was first published in Russian, in a 1935 textbook on the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) by [Mikhail Lavrentyev](wikipedia://en/63358) and [Lazar Lyusternik](wikipedia://en/4201372), where it was credited to Soviet mathematician and geophysicist S. S. Kovner. Additional proofs were given by [Abram Samoilovitch Besicovitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abram_Samoilovitch_Besicovitch) and by [István Fáry](wikipedia://en/13183981), who also proved that every minimizer of the Kovner–Besicovitch measure is a triangle.
