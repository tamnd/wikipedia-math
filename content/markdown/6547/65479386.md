# Adam Harper

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adam Harper"}

**Adam Harper** is a mathematician specialising in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), particularly in [analytic](wikipedia://en/251513), combinatorial and [probabilistic](wikipedia://en/3644492) number theory. He is currently a professor at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), England. Harper was awarded the [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize) in 2019 "for several outstanding contributions to analytic and probabilistic number theory."[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

"Harper's research, both individually and in collaboration, covers the theory of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), random multiplicative functions, S-unit equations, [smooth numbers](wikipedia://en/1035039), the [large sieve](wikipedia://en/1967859), and the recent highly innovative "pretentious" approach to number theory. In establishing these results, he has shown mastery over probabilistic methods which he has used with remarkable effect in analytic number theory."

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Adam Harper was born in [Lowestoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft) in the United Kingdom. He did a four-year [MMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMath) course at [Exeter College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_College,_Oxford) and won the Oxford Junior Mathematics Prize. He completed his PhD in 2012 at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) under the guidance of Professor [Ben Green](wikipedia://en/1927952), and as a PhD student won the [Smith Essay Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize). He was a postdoctoral fellow with Professor [Andrew Granville](wikipedia://en/2910434) at [CRM Montréal](wikipedia://en/3505436) during 2012–2013, following which he was a research fellow at [Jesus College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge) during 2013–16. He returned to Montréal in 2018 as Simons CRM Visiting Professor. He is currently a professor at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick).[^o7]

## Other awards {#Other_awards}

Adam Harper was awarded a [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) in 2020 for "his deep and important contributions to [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), and in particular for his work on the value distribution of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) and random multiplicative functions using sophisticated ideas and techniques from [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542)."[^o9][^o10]
