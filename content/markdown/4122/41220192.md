# Practical syllogism (theology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Practical syllogism"}

In [Reformed theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_theology), the **practical syllogism** (syllogismus practicus)[^o2] is a concept relating [assurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assurance_%28theology%29) of salvation to evidence in a person's life of such, such as [good works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_works) and [sanctification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctification). The major premise of the [syllogism](wikipedia://en/48046) is that some principle is characteristic of being a Christian. The minor premise is that the characteristic is present in oneself. The conclusion is that one is a Christian.[^o3] Works are said to be the [epistemological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemological) ground of faith and [justification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justification_%28theology%29), rather than the [ontological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontological) ground, because the evidences of faith only make faith apparent; they do not create it.[^o2] [John Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Calvin) recognizes that outward signs of God's favor may confirm faith, but warns against inferring election from such signs. Faith is always to be placed in the preaching of the gospel rather than any outward action of the believer, but the outward actions may be a final confirmation of election.[^o4] The concept is present in several [Reformed confessions of faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_confessions_of_faith). The [Heidelberg Catechism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Catechism) includes assurance as one of the reasons Christians should perform good works. The [Westminster Confession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Confession) calls good works "the Fruits and Evidences of a true and lively faith."[^o2]
