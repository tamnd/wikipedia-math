# Thomas Hakon Grönwall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Hakon Grönwall"}

**Thomas Hakon Grönwall** or **Thomas Hakon Gronwall** (born **Hakon Tomi Grönwall**;[^o2] January 16, 1877 in Dylta bruk, [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) – May 9, 1932 in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29)) was a . He studied at the [University College of Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_of_Stockholm) and [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) and completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at Uppsala in 1898. Grönwall worked for about a year as a civil engineer in Germany before he emigrated to the United States in 1904. He later taught mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and from 1925 he was a member of the physics department at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o2][^o4]

:::

In 1925 he started to collaborate with [Victor LaMer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_LaMer), which led to his joining the Department of Physics at Columbia University as an associate in 1927. This connection was a great opportunity. There were no teaching obligations; he had complete control of his own time and an abundance of new intriguing problems to address in physical chemistry and in atomic physics. He developed an analytical solution to the [Poisson-Boltzmann equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson-Boltzmann_equation) as it appears in the [Debye–Hückel theory](wikipedia://en/7729301)[^o2]
