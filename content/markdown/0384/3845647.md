# Peter B. Kronheimer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter B. Kronheimer"}

**Peter Benedict Kronheimer** (born 1963) is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work on [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) and its applications to [3-](wikipedia://en/1018257) and [4-dimensional](wikipedia://en/1362795) topology. He is William Caspar Graustein Professor of Mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and former chair of the mathematics department.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Kronheimer attended the [City of London School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London_School). He completed his [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) under the direction of [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698). He has had a long association with [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College), the oldest of the constituent colleges of Oxford University, being an undergraduate, graduate, and full fellow of the college.

## Career {#Career}

Kronheimer's early work was on [gravitational instantons](wikipedia://en/2618270), in particular the classification of [hyperkähler](wikipedia://en/646120) [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795) with asymptotical locally [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) (ALE spaces), leading to the papers "The construction of ALE spaces as hyper-Kähler quotients" and "A Torelli-type theorem for gravitational instantons." He and [Hiraku Nakajima](wikipedia://en/4383763) gave a construction of [instantons](wikipedia://en/296077) on ALE spaces generalizing the [Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698)–[Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164)–[Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747)–[Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086) construction. This constructions identified these [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) as moduli spaces for certain [quivers](wikipedia://en/355100) (see "Yang-Mills instantons on ALE gravitational instantons.") He was the initial recipient of the [Oberwolfach prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach#Oberwolfach_Prize) in 1998 on the basis of some of this work.

Kronheimer has frequently collaborated with [Tomasz Mrowka](wikipedia://en/20652006) from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). Their collaboration began at the [Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach), and their first work developed analogues of [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305)'s invariants for 4-manifolds with a distinguished surface. They used the tools developed to prove a conjecture of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), that the four-ball genus of a $(p,q)$-torus knot is $(p-1)(q-1)/2$. They then went on to develop these tools further and established a structure theorem for [Donaldson's polynomial invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson's_polynomial_invariants) using [Kronheimer–Mrowka basic classes](wikipedia://en/35131387). After the arrival of [Seiberg–Witten theory](wikipedia://en/3526032) their work on embedded surfaces culminated in a proof of the [Thom conjecture](wikipedia://en/3846986)—which had been outstanding for several decades. Another of Kronheimer and Mrowka's results was a proof of the [Property P conjecture](wikipedia://en/1294354) for knots. They developed an instanton Floer invariant for knots which was used in their proof that Khovanov homology detects the unknot.

Besides his research articles, his writings include a book, with [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305), on 4-manifolds, and a book with Mrowka on [Seiberg–Witten–Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856#Seiberg–Witten_Floer_homology), entitled "Monopoles and Three-Manifolds".[^o7] This book won the 2011 [Doob Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doob_Prize) of the AMS.

In 1990 he was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto). In 2018 he gave a plenary lecture at the ICM in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro), together with Tomasz Mrowka. In 2023 he was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research.[^o8]

Kronheimer's PhD students have included Ian Dowker, Jacob Rasmussen, [Ciprian Manolescu](wikipedia://en/3034280), Olga Plamenevskaya and Aliakbar Daemi.
