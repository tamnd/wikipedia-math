# Thom conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thom conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a smooth [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) $C$ in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155), of degree $d$, has [genus](wikipedia://en/61891#Topology) given by the [genus–degree formula](wikipedia://en/23283463)

:::

$$g = (d-1)(d-2)/2.$$

The **Thom conjecture**, named after French mathematician [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633), states that if $\Sigma$ is any smoothly embedded connected curve representing the same class in [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) as $C$, then the genus $g$ of $\Sigma$ satisfies the inequality

$$g \geq (d-1)(d-2)/2.$$

In particular, *C* is known as a *genus minimizing representative* of its homology class. It was first proved by [Peter Kronheimer](wikipedia://en/3845647) and [Tomasz Mrowka](wikipedia://en/20652006) in October 1994,[^o2] using the then-new [Seiberg–Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_invariant).

Assuming that $\Sigma$ has nonnegative self [intersection number](wikipedia://en/384327) this was generalized to [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) (an example being the complex projective plane) by [John Morgan](wikipedia://en/8487966), [Zoltán Szabó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoltán_Szabó_%28mathematician%29), and [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920),[^o3] also using the Seiberg–Witten invariants.

There is at least one generalization of this conjecture, known as the [symplectic](wikipedia://en/294298) Thom conjecture (which is now a theorem, as proved for example by [Peter Ozsváth](wikipedia://en/5027843) and Szabó in 2000[^o4]). It states that a symplectic surface of a symplectic 4-manifold is genus minimizing within its homology class. This would imply the previous result because algebraic curves (complex dimension 1, real dimension 2) are symplectic surfaces within the complex projective plane, which is a symplectic 4-manifold.
