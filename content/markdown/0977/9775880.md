# Fraňková–Helly selection theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fraňková–Helly selection theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Fraňková–Helly selection theorem** is a generalisation of [Helly's selection theorem](wikipedia://en/6740565) for functions of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) to the case of [regulated functions](wikipedia://en/9776617). It was proved in 1991 by the [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Dana Fraňková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Fraňková).

:::

## Background {#Background}

Let *X* be a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), and let BV(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) denote the [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538) of all functions *f* : \[0, *T*\] → *X* with finite total variation over the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) \[0, *T*\], equipped with the total variation norm. It is well known that BV(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) satisfies the [compactness theorem](wikipedia://en/6042) known as **Helly's selection theorem**: given any sequence of functions (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>)<sub>*n*∈**N**</sub> in BV(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) that is uniformly bounded in the total variation norm, there exists a subsequence

$$\left( f_{n(k)} \right) \subseteq (f_{n}) \subset \mathrm{BV}([0, T]; X)$$

and a limit function *f* ∈ BV(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) such that *f*<sub>*n*(*k*)</sub>(*t*) [converges weakly](wikipedia://en/2119266) in *X* to *f*(*t*) for every *t* ∈ \[0, *T*\]. That is, for every [continuous linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_functional) *λ* ∈ *X*\*,

$$\lambda \left( f_{n(k)}(t) \right) \to \lambda(f(t)) \mbox{ in } X \mbox{ as } k \to \infty.$$

Consider now the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) Reg(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) of all regulated functions *f* : \[0, *T*\] → *X*, equipped with the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm). Helly's theorem does not hold for the space Reg(\[0, *T*\]; *X*): a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) is given by the sequence

$$f_{n} (t) = \sin (n t).$$

One may ask, however, if a weaker selection theorem is true, and the **Fraňková–Helly selection theorem** is such a result.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the Fraňková–Helly selection theorem"}

## Statement of the Fraňková–Helly selection theorem {#Statement_of_the_Fraňková–Helly_selection_theorem}

As before, let *X* be a separable Hilbert space and let Reg(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) denote the space of regulated functions *f* : \[0, *T*\] → *X*, equipped with the supremum norm. Let (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>)<sub>*n*∈**N**</sub> be a sequence in Reg(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) satisfying the following condition: for every *ε* > 0, there exists some *L*<sub>ε</sub> > 0 so that each *f*<sub>*n*</sub> may be approximated by a *u*<sub>*n*</sub> ∈ BV(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) satisfying

$$\| f_{n} - u_{n} \|_{\infty} < \varepsilon$$

and

$$| u_{n}(0) | + \mathrm{Var}(u_{n}) \leq L_{\varepsilon},$$

where |-| denotes the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) in *X* and Var(*u*) denotes the variation of *u*, which is defined to be the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum)

$$\sup_{\Pi} \sum_{j=1}^{m} | u(t_{j}) - u(t_{j-1}) |$$

over all [partitions](wikipedia://en/471332)

$$\Pi = \{ 0 = t_{0} < t_{1} < \dots < t_{m} = T , m \in \mathbf{N} \}$$

of \[0, *T*\]. Then there exists a subsequence

$$\left( f_{n(k)} \right) \subseteq (f_{n}) \subset \mathrm{Reg}([0, T]; X)$$

and a limit function *f* ∈ Reg(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) such that *f*<sub>*n*(*k*)</sub>(*t*) converges weakly in *X* to *f*(*t*) for every *t* ∈ \[0, *T*\]. That is, for every continuous linear functional *λ* ∈ *X*\*,

$$\lambda \left( f_{n(k)}(t) \right) \to \lambda(f(t)) \mbox{ in } \mathbb{R} \mbox{ as } k \to \infty.$$

:::
