# Trigonometric functions of matrices

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trigonometric functions of matrices"}

The **trigonometric functions** (especially [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) and [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine)) for complex [square matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_matrices) occur in solutions of second-order systems of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309).[^o2] They are defined by the same [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) that hold for the trigonometric functions of [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers):[^o3]

:::

$$\begin{aligned} \sin X & = X - \frac{X^3}{3!} + \frac{X^5}{5!} - \frac{X^7}{7!} + \cdots & = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)!}X^{2n+1} \\ \cos X & = I - \frac{X^2}{2!} + \frac{X^4}{4!} - \frac{X^6}{6!} + \cdots & = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n)!}X^{2n} \end{aligned}$$

with $X^{n}$ being the $n$th [power](wikipedia://en/125280#Powers_of_a_matrix) of the matrix $X$, and $I$ being the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) of appropriate dimensions.

Equivalently, they can be defined using the [matrix exponential](wikipedia://en/672731) along with the matrix equivalent of [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/9613), $e^{iX} = \cos X + i \sin X$, yielding

$$\begin{aligned} \sin X & = {e^{iX} - e^{-iX} \over 2i} \\ \cos X & = {e^{iX} + e^{-iX} \over 2}. \end{aligned}$$

For example, taking $X$ to be a standard [Pauli matrix](wikipedia://en/24868),

$$\sigma_1 = \sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix} 0&1\\ 1&0 \end{pmatrix} ~,$$

one has

$$\sin(\theta \sigma_1) = \sin(\theta)~ \sigma_1 , \qquad \cos (\theta \sigma_1) = \cos (\theta)~I~,$$

as well as, for the [cardinal sine function](wikipedia://en/610583),

$$\operatorname{sinc}( \theta \sigma_1) =\operatorname{sinc}( \theta) ~I.$$

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The analog of the [Pythagorean trigonometric identity](wikipedia://en/1050741) holds:[^o3]

$$\sin^2 X + \cos^2 X = I$$

If $X$ is a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080), $\sin X$ and $\cos X$ are also diagonal matrices with $(\sin X)_{nn} = \sin(X_{nn})$ and $(\cos X)_{nn} = \cos(X_{nn})$, that is, they can be calculated by simply taking the sines or cosines of the matrices's diagonal components.

The analogs of the [trigonometric addition formulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_addition_formulas) are true [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $XY = YX$:[^o3]

$$\begin{aligned} \sin (X \pm Y) = \sin X \cos Y \pm \cos X \sin Y \\ \cos (X \pm Y) = \cos X \cos Y \mp \sin X \sin Y \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Other functions {#Other_functions}

The tangent, as well as [inverse trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/374220), [hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function) and [inverse hyperbolic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_hyperbolic_function) have also been defined for matrices:[^o7]

$\arcsin X = -i \ln \left( iX + \sqrt{I-X^2} \right)$ (see [Inverse trigonometric functions#Logarithmic forms](wikipedia://en/374220#Logarithmic_forms), [Matrix logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_logarithm), [Square root of a matrix](wikipedia://en/2929701))

$$\begin{aligned} \sinh X & = {e^X - e^{-X} \over 2} \\ \cosh X & = {e^X + e^{-X} \over 2} \end{aligned}$$

and so on.
