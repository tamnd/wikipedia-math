# Desmic system

*Image: Compound of two tetrahedra.png.* Two desmic tetrahedra. The third tetrahedron of this system is not shown, but has one vertex at the center and the other three on the plane at infinity.

*Image: Reye configuration.svg.* The [Reye configuration](wikipedia://en/35301443) with the same 12 vertices as a desmic system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Desmic system"}

In [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), a **desmic system** (el) is a set of three [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) in 3-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135), such that any two are **desmic** (related such that each [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) of one cuts a pair of opposite edges of the other). It was introduced by (txt). The three tetrahedra of a desmic system are contained in a [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_%28mathematics%29) of [quartic surfaces](wikipedia://en/20133775).

:::

Every line that passes through two vertices of two tetrahedra in the system also passes through a vertex of the third tetrahedron. The 12 vertices of the desmic system and the 16 lines formed in this way are the points and lines of a [Reye configuration](wikipedia://en/35301443).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The three tetrahedra given by the equations

- $$\displaystyle (w^2-x^2)(y^2-z^2) = 0$$
- $$\displaystyle (w^2-y^2)(x^2-z^2) = 0$$
- $$\displaystyle (w^2-z^2)(y^2-x^2) = 0$$

form a desmic system, contained in the pencil of quartics

- $$\displaystyle a(w^2x^2+y^2z^2) + b(w^2y^2+x^2z^2) + c (w^2z^2+x^2y^2) = 0$$

for *a* + *b* + *c* = 0.

:::
