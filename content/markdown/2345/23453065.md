# Dürer graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dürer graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Dürer graph** is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with 12 vertices and 18 edges. It is named after [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_Dürer), whose 1514 [engraving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engraving) *[Melencolia I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melencolia_I)* includes a depiction of [Dürer's solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dürer's_solid), a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) having the Dürer graph as its [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_%28topology%29). Dürer's solid is one of only four [well-covered](wikipedia://en/32696387) [simple](wikipedia://en/8853286) convex polyhedra.

:::

## Dürer's solid {#Dürer's_solid}

Dürer's solid is combinatorially equivalent to a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) with two opposite vertices [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429), although Dürer's depiction of it is not in this form but rather as a truncated [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406) or [truncated triangular trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/41031846).[^n1] The exact geometry of the solid depicted by Dürer is a subject of some academic debate, with different hypothetical values for its [acute angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_angle) ranging from 72° to 82°.[^o6]

## Graph-theoretic properties {#Graph-theoretic_properties}

The Dürer graph is the graph formed by the vertices and edges of the Dürer solid. It is a [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) of [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 3 and [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) 4. As well as its construction as the skeleton of Dürer's solid, it can be obtained by applying a [Y-Δ transform](wikipedia://en/268145) to the opposite vertices of a [cube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815), or as the [generalized Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/23716451) *G*(6,2).[^n2] As with any [graph of a convex polyhedron](wikipedia://en/24656270), the Dürer graph is a [3-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) simple [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314).

The Dürer graph is a [well-covered graph](wikipedia://en/32696387), meaning that all of its [maximal independent sets](wikipedia://en/1793590) have the same number of vertices, four. It is one of four well-covered cubic polyhedral graphs and one of seven well-covered 3-connected cubic graphs. The only other three well-covered [simple](wikipedia://en/8853286) convex polyhedra are the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584), and [pentagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859659).[^o9]

The Dürer graph is [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/244437), with [LCF notation](wikipedia://en/24186720) \[−4, 5, 2, −4, −2, 5; −\].[^o10] More precisely, it has exactly six Hamiltonian cycles, each pair of which may be mapped into each other by a [symmetry](wikipedia://en/15094186) of the graph.[^n3]

The Dürer graph is a [unit distance graph](wikipedia://en/7158665). However, although the Dürer graph is planar, its unit-distance embedding has crossings. Thus, it is a planar unit-distance graph that is not a [matchstick graph](wikipedia://en/25730128).[^n4]

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetries"}

## Symmetries {#Symmetries}

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) both of the Dürer graph and of the Dürer solid (in either the truncated cube form or the form shown by Dürer) is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) 12, denoted D<sub>6</sub>.

:::
