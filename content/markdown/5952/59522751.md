# Bauer maximum principle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bauer maximum principle"}

**Bauer's maximum principle** is the following theorem in [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033):

:::

Any function that is [convex](wikipedia://en/245568) and [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122), and defined on a set that is [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) and [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), attains its maximum at some extreme point of that set.

It is attributed to the German mathematician [Heinz Bauer](wikipedia://en/25512518).[^o2]

Bauer's maximum principle immediately implies the analogue *minimum principle*:

Any function that is [**concave**](wikipedia://en/263321) and [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122), and defined on a set that is [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) and [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), attains its **minimum** at some extreme point of that set.

Since a [linear function](wikipedia://en/152111) is simultaneously convex and concave, it satisfies both principles, i.e., it attains both its maximum and its minimum at extreme points.

Bauer's maximization principle has applications in various fields, for example, differential equations[^o3] and economics.[^o4]
