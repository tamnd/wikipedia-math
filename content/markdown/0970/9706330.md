# David Luenberger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Luenberger"}

**David Gilbert Luenberger** (September 16, 1937 – May 19, 2026)[^o3] was an American mathematical scientist known for his research and his textbooks, which center on [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033). He was a professor in the department of Management Science and Engineering at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Luenberger was one of the original founders of the Department of Engineering-Economic Systems in 1967. He served as chairman of the department for eleven years. He worked as a professor at the University for 50 years, retiring as of September 2013.

He has over 70 technical publications on [systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_system) and [control](wikipedia://en/362565), in [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29), in [microeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microeconomics), and in [financial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_engineering).[^o5] His *Investment Science* is widely prescribed and referenced by finance academics and practitioners.

He received his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) in [Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Engineering) from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1959, and he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Electrical Engineering from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1963. His PhD thesis was titled, "Determining the State of a Linear System with Observers of Low Dynamic Order".[^o6][^o7] In his dissertation Luenberger introduced new methods for construction of [state observers](wikipedia://en/1261170). The celebrated [Luenberger observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luenberger_observer) is named after him.

Luenberger died on May 19, 2026, at the age of 88.[^o8]
