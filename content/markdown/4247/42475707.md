# Exp algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exp algebra"}

In mathematics, an **exp algebra** is a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) Exp(*G*) constructed from an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *G*, and is the [universal](wikipedia://en/32245) [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* such that there is an exponential map from *G* to the group of the [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) in *R*\[\[*t*\]\] with constant term 1. In other words the functor Exp from abelian groups to [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) is [adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) to the functor from commutative rings to abelian groups taking a ring to the group of formal power series with constant term 1.

:::

The definition of the exp ring of *G* is similar to that of the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) **Z**\[*G*\] of *G*, which is the universal ring such that there is an exponential homomorphism from the group to its units. In particular there is a natural homomorphism from the group ring to a completion of the exp ring. However in general the Exp ring can be much larger than the group ring: for example, the group ring of the integers is the ring of [Laurent polynomials](wikipedia://en/61351) in 1 variable, while the exp ring is a polynomial ring in countably many generators.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

For each element *g* of *G* introduce a countable set of variables *g*<sub>*i*</sub> for *i*>0. Define exp(*gt*) to be the formal power series in *t*

$$\exp(gt) = 1+g_1t+g_2t^2+g_3t^3+\cdots.$$

The exp ring of *G* is the commutative ring generated by all the elements *g*<sub>*i*</sub> with the relations

$$\exp((g+h)t) = \exp(gt)\exp(ht)$$

for all *g*, *h* in *G*; in other words the coefficients of any power of *t* on both sides are identified.

The ring Exp(*G*) can be made into a commutative and cocommutative [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) as follows. The coproduct of Exp(*G*) is defined so that all the elements exp(*gt*) are group-like. The antipode is defined by making exp(–*gt*) the antipode of exp(*gt*). The counit takes all the generators *g*<sub>*i*</sub> to 0.

(Hoffman 1983) showed that Exp(*G*) has the structure of a [λ-ring](wikipedia://en/32055176).

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The exp ring of an infinite cyclic group such as the integers is a polynomial ring in a countable number of generators *g*<sub>*i*</sub> where *g* is a generator of the cyclic group. This ring (or Hopf algebra) is naturally isomorphic to the [ring of symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/364460) (or the [Hopf algebra of symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_algebra_of_symmetric_functions)).
- (txt) suggest that it might be interesting to extend the theory to non-commutative groups *G*.

:::
