# Roland Bulirsch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roland Bulirsch"}

**Roland Zdeněk Bulirsch** (10 November 1932 – 21 September 2022) was a German mathematician specialising in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). He studied and taught at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), and taught internationally as visiting professor. He was co-author of the reference book *Introduction to Numerical Analysis*, and president of the edition of the works by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736). He received honorary doctorates from international universities, and several awards.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Bulirsch was born in [Liberec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberec) (Reichenberg) on 10 November 1932.[^o4] He [had to leave Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Germans_from_Czechoslovakia) in 1946. In 1947, he became an apprentice as a machinist with [Siemens-Schuckert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens-Schuckert) in Nuremberg, completing in 1951. He achieved the [Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur) in [Nördlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nördlingen) in 1954,[^o4] and then studied mathematics and physics at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) to 1959,[^o5] earning his Ph.D. there in 1961, supervised by [Klaus Samelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Samelson), and his habilitation in mathematics in 1965.[^o4] He taught as associated professor at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), from 1967 to 1969, and as professor of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) from 1969. He joined the faculty in Munich in 1973.[^o5]

He was elected a member of the [Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) in 1991. In 1998 he became president of the edition of the works by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736). He was emerited in 2001.[^o4]

Bulirsch specialised in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o6] He is the author (with [Josef Stoer](wikipedia://en/39317718)) of *Introduction to Numerical Analysis*, a standard reference for the theory of [numerical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_methods), and has also authored numerous other books and articles. The book *From Nano to Space: Applied Mathematics Inspired by Roland Bulirsch* is a tribute to his work.[^o6] The [Bulirsch–Stoer algorithm](wikipedia://en/20221609) is named after him and Stoer.

Bulirsch received honorary doctorates from the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg), the Technical University of Liberec, the National Technical University of Athens and the [University of Viên Toán Hoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Viên_Toán_Hoc) in Hanoi. He was awarded a medal from the Union of Czech Mathematicians and Physicists, and a medal from the [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in Prague. In 1998, he received the [Bavarian Maximilian Order for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Maximilian_Order_for_Science_and_Art), and the following year the Liebig-Denkmünze, the highest award of the Heimatkreis Reichenberg. He was elected to the Sudetendeutsche Akademie der Wissenschaften und Künste the same year. In 2012, he received the Großer Sudetendeutscher Kulturpreis.[^o7]

### Personal life {#Personal_life}

Bulirsch and his wife Waltraut had two daughters. She died in 2020.[^o9]

Bulirsch died in [Gauting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauting) on 21 September 2022 at age 89.[^o10]
