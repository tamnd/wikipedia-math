# Almost Mathieu operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Almost Mathieu operator"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the **almost Mathieu operator**, named for its similarity to the [Mathieu operator](wikipedia://en/1724802)[^o2] introduced by [Émile Léonard Mathieu](wikipedia://en/1724751),[^o3] arises in the study of the [quantum Hall effect](wikipedia://en/50425). It is given by

:::

$$[H^{\lambda,\alpha}_\omega u](n) = u(n+1) + u(n-1) + 2 \lambda \cos(2\pi (\omega + n\alpha)) u(n), \,$$

acting as a [self-adjoint operator](wikipedia://en/187408) on the [Hilbert space $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$](wikipedia://en/20598932#Sequence_spaces). Here $\alpha,\omega \in\mathbb{T}, \lambda > 0$ are parameters. In [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210), its importance comes from the fact of being one of the best-understood examples of an [ergodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic) [Schrödinger operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_operator). For example, three problems (now all solved) of [Barry Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon)'s fifteen problems about Schrödinger operators "for the twenty-first century" featured the almost Mathieu operator.[^o4] In physics, the almost Mathieu operators can be used to study metal to insulator transitions like in the [Aubry–André model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubry–André_model).

For $\lambda = 1$, the almost Mathieu operator is sometimes called **Harper's equation**.

## Spectral type {#Spectral_type}

If $\alpha$ is a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024), then $H^{\lambda,\alpha}_\omega$ is a periodic operator and by [Floquet theory](wikipedia://en/2987843) its [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) is purely [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous).

Now to the case when $\alpha$ is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689). Since the transformation $\omega \mapsto \omega + \alpha$ is minimal, it follows that the spectrum of $H^{\lambda,\alpha}_\omega$ does not depend on $\omega$. On the other hand, by ergodicity, the supports of absolutely continuous, singular continuous, and pure point parts of the spectrum are almost surely independent of $\omega$. It is now known, that

- For $0 < \lambda < 1$, $H^{\lambda,\alpha}_\omega$ has surely purely absolutely continuous spectrum.[^o7] (This was one of Simon's problems.)
- For $\lambda= 1$, $H^{\lambda,\alpha}_\omega$ has surely purely singular continuous spectrum for any irrational $\alpha$.[^o8]
- For $\lambda > 1$, $H^{\lambda,\alpha}_\omega$ has almost surely pure point spectrum and exhibits [Anderson localization](wikipedia://en/2213942).[^o9] (It is known that almost surely can not be replaced by surely.)[^o10][^o11]

That the spectral measures are singular when $\lambda \geq 1$ follows (through the work of Yoram Last and Simon) [^o12] from the lower bound on the [Lyapunov exponent](wikipedia://en/166441) $\gamma(E)$ given by

$$\gamma(E) \geq \max \{0,\log(\lambda)\}. \,$$

This lower bound was proved independently by Joseph Avron, Simon and [Michael Herman](wikipedia://en/28451358), after an earlier almost rigorous argument of Serge Aubry and Gilles André. In fact, when $E$ belongs to the spectrum, the inequality becomes an equality (the Aubry–André formula), proved by [Jean Bourgain](wikipedia://en/1024131) and [Svetlana Jitomirskaya](wikipedia://en/46271961).[^o13]

## Structure of the spectrum {#Structure_of_the_spectrum}

*Image: Hofstadter's\_butterfly.png.* [Hofstadter's butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstadter's_butterfly)

Another striking characteristic of the almost Mathieu operator is that its spectrum is a [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172) for all irrational $\alpha$ and $\lambda > 0$. This was shown by [Avila](wikipedia://en/23579022) and [Jitomirskaya](wikipedia://en/46271961) solving the by-then famous 'Ten Martini Problem' [^o15] (also one of Simon's problems) after several earlier results (including generically[^o16] and almost surely[^o17] with respect to the parameters).

Furthermore, the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) of the spectrum of the almost Mathieu operator is known to be

$$\operatorname{Leb}(\sigma(H^{\lambda,\alpha}_\omega)) = |4 - 4 \lambda| \,$$

for all $\lambda > 0$. For $\lambda = 1$ this means that the spectrum has zero measure (this was first proposed by [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) and later became one of Simon's problems).[^o18] For $\lambda \neq 1$, the formula was discovered numerically by Aubry and André and proved by Jitomirskaya and Krasovsky. Earlier Last [^o19][^o20] had proven this formula for most values of the parameters.

The study of the spectrum for $\lambda =1$ leads to the [Hofstadter's butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstadter's_butterfly), where the spectrum is shown as a set.
