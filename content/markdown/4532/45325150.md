# Congruence-permutable algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Congruence-permutable algebra"}

In [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200), a **congruence-permutable algebra** is an algebra whose [congruences](wikipedia://en/48167) [commute](wikipedia://en/294390) under [composition](wikipedia://en/7472170). This symmetry has several equivalent characterizations, which lend to the analysis of such algebras. Many familiar [varieties of algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_of_algebras), such as the variety of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), consist of congruence-permutable algebras, but some, like the variety of [lattices](wikipedia://en/469304), have members that are not congruence-permutable.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given an algebra $\mathbf{A}$, a pair of [congruences](wikipedia://en/48167) $\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Con}(\mathbf{A})$ are said to **permute** when $\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha$.p. 121 An algebra $\mathbf{A}$ is called **congruence-permutable** when each pair of congruences of $\mathbf{A}$ permute.p. 122 A [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) of algebras $\mathcal{V}$ is referred to as **congruence-permutable** when every algebra in $\mathcal{V}$ is congruence-permutable.p. 122

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Most classical varieties in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), such as [groups](wikipedia://en/19447)p. 123, [rings](wikipedia://en/48404)p. 123, and [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) are congruence-permutable. Any variety that contains a group operation is congruence-permutable, and the Maltsev term is $xy^{-1}z$.

:::

## Nonexamples {#Nonexamples}

Viewed as a lattice the [chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_%28order_theory%29) with three elements is not congruence-permutable and hence neither is the variety of lattices.p. 123
