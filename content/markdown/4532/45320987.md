# Furstenberg boundary

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Furstenberg boundary"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Furstenberg boundary** is a notion of [boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_of_a_set) associated with a [group](wikipedia://en/19447). It is named for [Harry Furstenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Furstenberg), who introduced it in a series of papers beginning in 1963 (in the case of semisimple [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945)). The Furstenberg boundary can be characterized as a universal boundary space for harmonic analysis on the group, in the sense that bounded [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762) can be represented by their boundary values via a [Poisson-type integral](wikipedia://en/2214583).

:::

For example, when $G=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, the Furstenberg boundary is the real projective line $\mathbb{RP}^1$, which may be identified with the boundary circle of the hyperbolic plane, and the Poisson-like integral is the usual [Poisson kernel](wikipedia://en/2214583) for the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826).

## Semisimple Lie groups {#Semisimple_Lie_groups}

Let $G$ be a connected [semisimple Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group). The Furstenberg boundary of $G$ is the homogeneous space

$$G/P,$$

where $P$ is a minimal [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356#Parabolic_subgroups) of $G$.

This space is compact and homogeneous under the action of $G$. More generally, quotients $G/Q$ by parabolic subgroups $Q$ are generalized flag manifolds, and the Furstenberg boundary is the maximal one among these in the sense that every quotient by a parabolic subgroup is a factor of $G/P$.

For example, if $G=\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb R)$, then the Furstenberg boundary is the manifold of complete flags in $\mathbb R^n$. For $G=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$, it is $\mathbb{RP}^1$.

## Relation to Poisson boundaries {#Relation_to_Poisson_boundaries}

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $G$. A function $f$ on $G$ is called $\mu$-harmonic if

$$f(g)=\int_G f(gg')\,d\mu(g').$$

The [Poisson boundary](wikipedia://en/51465170) of the measured group $(G,\mu)$ is a measure space that represents bounded $\mu$-harmonic functions by boundary integrals. Unlike the Furstenberg boundary, the Poisson boundary depends on the choice of the measure $\mu$.

For semisimple Lie groups, Furstenberg showed that for broad classes of measures the Poisson boundary can be realized on a homogeneous boundary of the form $G/Q$, where $Q$ is a parabolic subgroup. In particular situations the maximal boundary $G/P$ plays the role of a universal homogeneous boundary from which the others are obtained as quotients.
