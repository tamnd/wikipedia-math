# Local asymptotic normality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local asymptotic normality"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **local asymptotic normality** is a property of a sequence of [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576), which allows this sequence to be [asymptotically approximated](wikipedia://en/3001287) by a [normal location model](wikipedia://en/21462), after an appropriate rescaling of the parameter. An important example when the local asymptotic normality holds is in the case of [i.i.d](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.i.d) sampling from a [regular parametric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_parametric_model).

:::

The notion of local asymptotic normality was introduced by (Le Cam 1960) and is fundamental in the treatment of [estimator and test efficiency](wikipedia://en/27250866).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A sequence of [parametric statistical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_statistical_model) { *P<sub>n,θ</sub>*: *θ* ∈ Θ } is said to be **locally asymptotically normal (LAN)** at *θ* if there exist [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) *r<sub>n</sub>* and *I<sub>θ</sub>* and a [random vector](wikipedia://en/49821) Δ<sub>*n,θ*</sub> ~ *N*(0, *I<sub>θ</sub>*) such that, for every converging sequence $h_{n} \to h$,[^o6]

$$\ln \frac{dP_{\!n,\theta+r_n^{-1}h_n}}{dP_{n,\theta}} = h'\Delta_{n,\theta} - \frac12 h'I_\theta\,h + o_{P_{n,\theta}}(1),$$

where the derivative here is a [Radon–Nikodym derivative](wikipedia://en/338746#Radon–Nikodym_derivative), which is a formalised version of the [likelihood ratio](wikipedia://en/44968), and where *o* is a type of [little O in probability notation](wikipedia://en/22284600). In other words, the local likelihood ratio must [converge in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) to a normal random variable whose mean is equal to minus one half the variance:

$$\ln \frac{dP_{\!n,\theta+r_n^{-1}h_n}}{dP_{n,\theta}}\ \ \xrightarrow{d}\ \ \mathcal{N}\Big( {-\tfrac12} h'I_\theta\,h,\ h'I_\theta\,h\Big).$$

The sequences of distributions $P_{\!n,\theta+r_n^{-1}h_n}$ and $P_{n,\theta}$ are [contiguous](wikipedia://en/25056375).[^o6]

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

The most straightforward example of a LAN model is an iid model whose likelihood is twice continuously differentiable. Suppose { *X*<sub>1</sub>, *X*<sub>2</sub>, …, *X<sub>n</sub>* } is an iid sample, where each *X<sub>i</sub>* has density function *f*(*x*, *θ*). The likelihood function of the model is equal to

$$p_{n,\theta}(x_1,\ldots,x_n;\,\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i,\theta).$$

If *f* is twice continuously differentiable in *θ*, then

$$\begin{aligned} \ln p_{n,\theta+\delta\theta} &\approx \ln p_{n,\theta} + \delta\theta'\frac{\partial \ln p_{n,\theta}}{\partial\theta} + \frac12 \delta\theta' \frac{\partial^2 \ln p_{n,\theta}}{\partial\theta\,\partial\theta'} \delta\theta \\ &= \ln p_{n,\theta} + \delta\theta' \sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\partial \ln f(x_i,\theta)}{\partial\theta} + \frac12 \delta\theta' \bigg[\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\partial^2 \ln f(x_i,\theta)}{\partial\theta\,\partial\theta'} \bigg]\delta\theta . \end{aligned}$$

Plugging in $\delta\theta=h/\sqrt{n}$, gives

$$\ln \frac{p_{n,\theta+h/\sqrt{n}}}{p_{n,\theta}} = h' \Bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\partial \ln f(x_i,\theta)}{\partial\theta}\Bigg) \;-\; \frac12 h' \Bigg( \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n - \frac{\partial^2 \ln f(x_i,\theta)}{\partial\theta\,\partial\theta'} \Bigg) h \;+\; o_p(1).$$

By the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406), the first term (in parentheses) converges in distribution to a normal random variable Δ<sub>*θ*</sub> ~ *N*(0, *I<sub>θ</sub>*), whereas by the [law of large numbers](wikipedia://en/157055) the expression in second parentheses converges in probability to *I<sub>θ</sub>*, which is the [Fisher information matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_information_matrix):

$$I_\theta = \mathrm{E}\bigg[{- \frac{\partial^2 \ln f(X_i,\theta)}{\partial\theta\,\partial\theta'}}\bigg] = \mathrm{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{\partial \ln f(X_i,\theta)}{\partial\theta}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\partial \ln f(X_i,\theta)}{\partial\theta}\bigg)'\,\bigg].$$

Thus, the definition of the local asymptotic normality is satisfied, and we have confirmed that the parametric model with iid observations and twice continuously differentiable likelihood has the LAN property.
