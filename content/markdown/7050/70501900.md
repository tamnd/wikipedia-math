# H. G. Landau

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="H. G. Landau"}

**Hyman Garshin Landau** (December 18, 1909 – December 2, 1966), more often known as **H. G. Landau**, was an American [mathematical biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biologist), [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [sociologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) who is known for using mathematical methods such as [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) to understand [animal behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethology) and [social dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_dynamics). After receiving his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) from the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), he worked at the [Ballistic Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_Research_Laboratory) at [Aberdeen Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Proving_Ground), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland), while teaching part-time at the [University of Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Delaware). He carried out his seminal work on [graph tournaments](wikipedia://en/1427502) as a research associate at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o3] Later, he moved to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), again as a research associate, after being forced to leave [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) by the [House Un-American Activities Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Un-American_Activities_Committee).[^o4]
