# Ingebrigt Johansson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ingebrigt Johansson"}

**Ingebrigt Johansson** (24 October 1904 – 24 April 1987) was a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He developed the [symbolic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_logic) system known as [minimal logic](wikipedia://en/26267678).[^n1]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Johansson was born in [Narvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narvik_%28town%29), the son of bricklayer Isak Johansson (1849–1941) and Gjertrud Kletten (1865–1948). In 1941 he married Gidske Jacoba Schult (1908–1994). He died in Oslo, Norway on 24 April 1987.[^n2]

In 1923 Johansson entered the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) to study mathematics. After he received his [Candidatus realium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidatus_realium) degree in 1928 he continued his studies in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn) in 1929 and in [Frankfurt a.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt) in 1930 and 1931. In 1931 he received his doctorate ([Dr. philos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy#Scandinavia)) from the University of Oslo for his dissertation "Topologische Untersuchungen über unverzweigte Überlagerungsflächen".[^n3][^n2]

He spent his professional career at the University of Oslo, from 1931 to 1936 as a fellow in geometry, from 1936 to 1942 as a lecturer in [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891) and finally as a professor of mathematics from 1946 (his appointment dated from 1942). From 1935 to 1946 Johansson served as president of the [Norwegian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/15255049). He was elected to the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) in 1937.[^n2]

He put great effort into mathematical didactics, experimenting with new methods in teaching and introducing new exam forms such as critique and comment tasks. He also wrote several textbooks and worked on curriculum reforms for the Oslo mathematics faculty.[^n4][^n5] He died in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo).
