# Historical dynamics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Historical dynamics"}

**Historical dynamics** broadly includes the scientific modeling of [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History). This might also be termed computer modeling of history, historical simulation, or simulation of history - allowing for an extensive range of techniques in simulation and estimation. Historical dynamics does not exist as a separate science, but there are individual efforts such as long range planning, [population modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_modeling), [economic forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_forecasting), [demographics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics), [global modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_modeling), [country modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_modeling), [regional planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_planning), [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) and many others in the general categories of [computer modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_modeling), [planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning), [forecasting](wikipedia://en/246074), and [simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulations).

:::

Some examples of "large" history where historical dynamics simulations would be helpful include; [global history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_history_%28field%29), large structures, , long duration history, [philosophy of history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_history), [Eurasian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_history), [comparative history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_history), long-range [environmental history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_history), [world systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_systems_theory), non-Western political and economic development, and [historical demography](wikipedia://en/10817881).

## Information sources for simulations {#Information_sources_for_simulations}

With the rise of technologies like wikis, and internet-wide search engines, some historical and social data can be mined to constrain models of history and society. Data from social media sites, and busy sites, can be mined for human patterns of action. These can provide more and more realistic behavioral models for individuals and groups of any size. [Agent-based models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent-based_model) and [microsimulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsimulation) of [human behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_behavior) can be embedded in larger historical simulations. Related subfields are [behavioral economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_economics) and [human behavioral ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_behavioral_ecology).

- [Data mining](wikipedia://en/42253), [web mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_mining), [predictive analytics](wikipedia://en/4141563)
- [Social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media), [web analytics](wikipedia://en/2475872), [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network)
- [Automated translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_translation), [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing), [Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test)[^o3]
- [Crowd computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowd_computing) has been applied to [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowd_computing#History), and offers another tool for historical verification and validation.

See , , , and .

Some examples of database activity include [Asian Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Development_Bank) statistics,[^o5] [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank) data,[^o6] and the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund) data.[^o7]

[Time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis) and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) are well established fields for the analysis of trends and [forecasting](wikipedia://en/246074); but, survey data and microdatasets can also be used in forecasts and simulations.

## Global, country, and sectoral models in international development {#Global,_country,_and_sectoral_models_in_international_development}

The United Nations[^o9] and other organizations routinely project the population of individual countries and regions of the world decades into the future. These demographic models are used by other organizations for projecting demand for services in all sectors of each economy.

- [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund) - Finance, Government Accounts
- [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development)
- [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) - Health
- [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) - Food and Agriculture
- [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization) - Labour
- [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) - Trade
- [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank) - extensive modeling and data activities,[^o10][^o11]

Each country often has their corresponding modeling groups for each of these major sectors. These can be grouped in separate articles according to sector. Groups include government departments, [international aid agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_development_aid_agencies), as well as nonprofit and [non-governmental organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organization).

A broad class of models used for economic and social modeling of countries and sectors are the [Computable general equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computable_general_equilibrium) (CGE) model - also called [applied general equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_general_equilibrium) models. In the context of time based simulations and policy analysis, see [dynamic stochastic general equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_stochastic_general_equilibrium) models.

## Linked economic, social, and climate models {#Linked_economic,_social,_and_climate_models}

Partly because of the controversy over [global climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_climate_change), there is an extensive network of global climate models,[^o13][^o14] and related social and economic models. These seek to estimate, not only the change in climate and its physical effects, but also the impact on human society and the natural environment. See [global economic models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_economic_models), [social model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_model), [microsimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsimulation), [climate model](wikipedia://en/47513), [global climate models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_climate_models), and [general circulation model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_circulation_model).

The relationship between the environment and society is examined through [environmental social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_social_science). [human ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_ecology), [political ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_ecology), and [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology), in general, can be areas where computer and mathematical modeling over time can be relevant to historical simulation.

## Simulating society {#Simulating_society}

Computer models for simulating society fall under [artificial society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_society), [social simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_simulation), [computational sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_sociology), [computational social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_social_science), and [mathematical sociology](wikipedia://en/4771336). There is an interdisciplinary [Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Artificial_Societies_and_Social_Simulation) for computer simulation of social processes.[^o19] The European Social Simulation Association promotes social simulation research in Europe; it is the publisher of JASSS.[^o20] There is a corresponding [Computational Social Science Society of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_Social_Science_Society_of_the_Americas).,[^o21] and a Pan-Asian Association for Agent-based Approach in Social Systems Sciences.[^o22] PAAA lists some related Japanese associations.[^o23]

The SimSoc ([Simulated Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulated_Society) tool) is in its fifth edition.

## Cities {#Cities}

There has been extensive research in [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning), [environmental planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_planning) and related fields: [regional planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_planning), [land-use planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_planning), [transportation planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_planning), [urban studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_studies), and [regional science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_science). Journals for these fields are listed at [List of planning journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_planning_journals).

[SimCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimCity) is a game for simulations of artificial cities. It has spawned a range of "sim" games. The planning groups try to simulate changes in real cities. The game groups allow experiments with artificial cities. And the two are merging in such efforts as Vizicities[^o25]

## Industry {#Industry}

The profiling of industries is well developed, and most industries make forecasts and plans. See [industrial history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_history), [history of steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_steel), [history of mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mining), [history of construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_construction), [history of the petroleum industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_petroleum_industry), and many other histories of specific industries. See [cyclical industrial dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclical_industrial_dynamics) for modeling of industries in the sense of "historical dynamics of industries". Some related terms are [industrial planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_planning), [history of industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_industry), [industrial evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_evolution), [technology change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_change), and [technology forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_forecasting). An example of "history friendly" industrial models.[^o27] from the journal, Industrial and Corporate Change.[^o28]

Economy-wide models must take into account the interactions between industry and the rest of the economy. See [Input–output model](wikipedia://en/1036651), [economic planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_planning), and [social accounting matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_accounting_matrix) for some relevant techniques.

## Futures {#Futures}

Many of the techniques from [futures studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futures_studies) are applicable to historical dynamics. Whether projecting forward from a point in the past to the present for validation studies, or projecting backwards from the present into the past - many of the techniques are useful. Likewise, simulations of the past, or alternative pasts, provide a groundwork of techniques for futures studies.
