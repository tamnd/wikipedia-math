# Aubrey William Ingleton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aubrey William Ingleton"}

**Aubrey William Ingleton** (1920–2000) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Ingleton was born in [Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester), the son of an accountant. He joined the civil service at age 16, and during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) was seconded to a radar development project. After the war, he entered [Northern Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Polytechnic), and earned a B.Sc. in mathematics in 1949 as an external student at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), winning first class honours, the Lubbock Prize, and the Sherbrooke Prize.[^o3] He did his graduate studies in mathematics at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) under the supervision of Anthony Francis Ruston, on subjects related to the [Hahn–Banach theorem](wikipedia://en/13860).[^o3] He took a faculty position at [Birkbeck College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck_College) in 1951, and married in 1952. In 1961 he moved to [New College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford), as the Mathematics Tutor. In 1966 he took up a Chair in Pure Mathematics at [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University), but he returned to Oxford in 1967, becoming a fellow of [Balliol College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College), where he remained for the rest of his career.[^o3] Among his students at Oxford was [Paul Seymour](wikipedia://en/1575708).[^o2]

As a mathematician his works are related to many different topics in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). His work on [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321) culminated in the paper "Representation of matroids" published in 1969. In his work, Ingleton studied matroids as a generalization of the concept of [linear independence](wikipedia://en/101863). The paper is a survey about [representable matroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representable_matroid) as it exhibited matroids representable over **C** but not over **R** and similarly over **R** but not over **Q**. He included in his paper a single theorem giving a necessary condition for the representability of matroids. This condition is known in the literature as [Ingleton's inequality](wikipedia://en/39661138).[^o3]
