# Categories for the Working Mathematician

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Categories for the Working Mathematician"}

***Categories for the Working Mathematician*** is a textbook in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) written by American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574), who founded the subject together with [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501). It was first published in 1971, and is based on his lectures given at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University), [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College), and [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University). It is widely regarded[^n1][^n2] as the premier introduction to the subject.

:::

## Contents {#Contents}

The book has twelve chapters, which are:

Chapter I. [Categories](wikipedia://en/53759), [Functors](wikipedia://en/10987), and [Natural Transformations](wikipedia://en/45022).

Chapter II. Constructions on Categories.

Chapter III. Universals and [Limits](wikipedia://en/39248).

Chapter IV. [Adjoints](wikipedia://en/53991).

Chapter V. [Limits](wikipedia://en/39248).

Chapter VI. [Monads](wikipedia://en/355140) and Algebras.

Chapter VII. [Monoids](wikipedia://en/330604).

Chapter VIII. [Abelian Categories](wikipedia://en/45063).

Chapter IX. Special Limits.

Chapter X. [Kan Extensions](wikipedia://en/2502518).

Chapter XI. Symmetry and [Braiding in Monoidal Categories](wikipedia://en/1470466)

Chapter XII. Structures in Categories.

Chapters XI and XII were added in the 1998 second edition, the first in view of its importance in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), and the second to address higher-dimensional categories that have come into prominence.[^n3]

Although it is the classic reference for [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), some of the terminology is not standard. In particular, Mac Lane attempted to settle an ambiguity in usage for the terms [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) and [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) by introducing the terms *epic* and *monic,* but the distinction is not in common use.[^n4]
