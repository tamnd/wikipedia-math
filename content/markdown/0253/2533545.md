# Neumann series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neumann series"}

A **Neumann series** is a [mathematical series](wikipedia://en/15287) that sums *k*-times repeated applications of an [operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28physics%29) $T$. This has the generator form

:::

$$\sum_{k=0}^\infty T^k$$

where $T^k$ is the *k*-times repeated application of $T$; $T^0$ is the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator) $I$ and $T^k := {}T^{k-1}\circ{T}$ for $k > 0$. This is a special case of the generalization of a [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630) of real or complex numbers to a geometric series of operators. The generalized initial term of the series is the identity operator $T^0 = I$ and the generalized common ratio of the series is the operator $T.$

The series is named after the mathematician [Carl Neumann](wikipedia://en/915551), who used it in 1877 in the context of [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342). The Neumann series is used in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). It is closely connected to the [resolvent formalism](wikipedia://en/3378540) for studying the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of bounded operators and, applied from the left to a function, it forms the [Liouville-Neumann series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville-Neumann_series) that formally solves [Fredholm integral equations](wikipedia://en/363377).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Suppose that $T$ is a bounded linear operator on the [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538) $X$. If the Neumann series [converges](wikipedia://en/1743431) in the [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722), then $I-T$ is [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) and its inverse is the series:

$$(I - T)^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty T^k,$$

where $I$ is the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator) in $X$. To see why, consider the partial sums

$$S_n := \sum_{k=0}^n T^k.$$

Then we have

$$\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}(I-T)S_n = \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^n T^k - \sum_{k=0}^n T^{k+1}\right) = \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}\left(I - T^{n+1}\right) = I$$

This result on operators is analogous to [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630) in $\mathbb{R}$.

One case in which convergence is guaranteed is when $X$ is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) and $|T| <1$ in the operator norm; another compatible case is that $\sum_{k=0}^\infty |T^k|$ converges. However, there are also results which give weaker conditions under which the series converges.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let $C\in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ be given by:

$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{4} \\ \frac{5}{7} & 0 & \frac{1}{7} \\ \frac{3}{10} & \frac{3}{5} & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

For the Neumann series $\sum_{k=0}^n C^k$ to converge to $(I-C)^{-1}$ as $n$ goes to infinity, the [spectral norm](wikipedia://en/1543735#Spectral_norm_%28p_=_2%29) of $C$ must be smaller than unity. This norm is about $0.9056$ as can be checked numerically (e.g., by computing the singular values), confirming that the Neumann series converges.

:::

## Approximate matrix inversion {#Approximate_matrix_inversion}

A truncated Neumann series can be used for [approximate matrix inversion](wikipedia://en/217122#By_Neumann_series). To approximate the inverse of an invertible matrix $A$, consider that

$$\begin{aligned} A^{-1} &= (I - I + A)^{-1} \\ & = (I - (I - A))^{-1} \\ & = (I - T)^{-1} \end{aligned}$$

for $T =(I - A).$ Then, using the Neumann series identity that $\sum_{k=0}^\infty T^k = (I - T)^{-1}$ if the appropriate norm condition on $T = (I - A)$ is satisfied, $A^{-1} = (I - (I - A))^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty (I - A)^k.$ Since these terms shrink with increasing $k,$ given the conditions on the norm, then truncating the series at some finite $n$ may give a practical approximation to the inverse matrix:

$$A^{-1} \approx \sum_{k=0}^n (I- A)^k.$$

## The set of invertible operators is open {#The_set_of_invertible_operators_is_open}

A corollary is that the set of invertible operators between two Banach spaces $B$ and $B'$ is open in the topology induced by the operator norm. Indeed, let $S : B \to B'$ be an invertible operator and let $T: B \to B'$ be another operator. If $|S-T| < |S^{-1}|^{-1}$, then $T$ is also invertible. Since $|I - S^{-1}T| < 1$, the Neumann series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty (I-S^{-1}T)^k$ is convergent. Therefore, we have

$$T^{-1}S = (I - (I - S^{-1}T))^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty(I - S^{-1}T)^k$$

Taking the norms, we get

$$|T^{-1}S| \le \frac{1}{1 - | I - (S^{-1}T)|}$$

The norm of $T^{-1}$ can be bounded by

$$|T^{-1}| \le \tfrac{1}{1-q} |S^{-1}| \quad\text{where}\quad q = |S-T| \, |S^{-1}|.$$

## Applications {#Applications}

The Neumann series has been used for linear data detection in massive multiuser [multiple-input multiple-output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple-input_multiple-output) (MIMO) wireless systems. Using a truncated Neumann series avoids computation of an explicit matrix inverse, which reduces the complexity of linear data detection from cubic to square.[^o10]

Another application is the theory of [propagation graphs](wikipedia://en/67349962) which takes advantage of Neumann series to derive closed form expressions for transfer functions.
