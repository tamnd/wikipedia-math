# Layer cake representation

*Image: Volume-Alhazen.svg.* Layer cake representation.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Layer cake representation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **layer cake representation** of a non-[negative](wikipedia://en/154616), [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) $f$ defined on a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) $(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)$ is the formula

:::

$$f(x) = \int_0^\infty 1_{L(f, t)} (x) \, \mathrm{d}t,$$

for all $x \in \Omega$, where $1_E$ denotes the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of a subset $E\subseteq \Omega$ and $L(f,t)$ denotes the (strict) [super-level set](wikipedia://en/559622):

$$L(f, t) = \{ y \in \Omega \mid f(y) \geq t \}\;\;\;{\text{or}\; L(f, t) = \{ y \in \Omega \mid f(y) > t \}}.$$

The layer cake representation follows easily from observing that

$$1_{L(f, t)}(x) = 1_{[0, f(x)]}(t)\;\;\; {\text{or}\;1_{L(f, t)}(x) = 1_{[0, f(x))}(t)}$$

where either integrand gives the same integral:

$$f(x) = \int_0^{f(x)} \,\mathrm{d}t.$$

The [layer cake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layer_cake) representation takes its name from the representation of the value $f(x)$ as the sum of contributions from the "layers" $L(f,t)$: "layers"/values $t$ below $f(x)$ contribute to the integral, while values $t$ above $f(x)$ do not. It is a generalization of [Cavalieri's principle](wikipedia://en/21697672) and is also known under this name.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The layer cake representation can be used to rewrite the [Lebesgue integral as an improper Riemann integral](wikipedia://en/26064288#Via_improper_Riemann_integral). For the measure space, $(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)$, let $S\subseteq\Omega$, be a measureable subset ($S\in\mathcal{A})$ and $f$ a non-negative measureable function. By starting with the Lebesgue integral, then expanding $f(x)$, then exchanging integration order (see [Fubini-Tonelli theorem](wikipedia://en/255245#Tonelli's_theorem_for_non-negative_measurable_function)) and simplifying in terms of the [Lebesgue integral of an indicator function](wikipedia://en/26064288#Indicator_functions), we get the [Riemann integral](wikipedia://en/26390):

$$\begin{aligned} \int_S f(x)\,\text{d}\mu(x) &= \int_S \int_0^\infty 1_{\{x\in\Omega\mid f(x)>t\}}(x)\,\text{d}t\,\text{d}\mu(x) \\ &= \int_0^\infty\!\! \int_S 1_{\{x\in\Omega\mid f(x)>t\}}(x)\,\text{d}\mu(x)\,\text{d}t\\ &= \int_0^\infty\!\! \int_\Omega 1_{\{x\in S\mid f(x)>t\}}(x)\,\text{d}\mu(x)\,\text{d}t\\ &= \int_0^{\infty} \mu(\{x\in S \mid f(x)>t\})\,\text{d}t. \end{aligned}$$

This can be used in turn, to rewrite the integral for the [*L*<sup>*p*</sup>-space *p*-norm](wikipedia://en/45194#Lp_spaces_and_Lebesgue_integrals), for $1\leq p<+\infty$:

$$\int_\Omega |f(x)|^p \, \mathrm{d}\mu(x) = p\int_0^{\infty} s^{p-1}\mu(\{ x \in \Omega:|f(x)| > s \}) \mathrm{d}s,$$

which follows immediately from the change of variables $t=s^{p}$ in the layer cake representation of $|f(x)|^p$. This representation can be used to prove [Markov's inequality](wikipedia://en/238232) and [Chebyshev's inequality](wikipedia://en/156533).
