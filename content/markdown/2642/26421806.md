# David Schmeidler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Schmeidler"}

**David Schmeidler** (דוד שמידלר; 1939 – 17 March 2022) was an Israeli mathematician and economic theorist. He was a Professor Emeritus at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) and the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

David Schmeidler was born in 1939 in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków), Poland. He spent the war years in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) and moved back to Poland at the end of the war and to Israel in 1949. From 1960 to 1969 he studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) (BSc, MSc, and PhD), the advanced degrees under the supervision of [Robert Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aumann). He visited the [Catholic University of Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_catholique_de_Louvain) and [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) at Berkeley before joining [Tel-Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) in 1971, holding professorships in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), and [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management). He held a part-time position as professor of economics at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) since 1987. Schmeidler died on 17 March 2022.

## Main contributions {#Main_contributions}

Schmeidler's early contributions were in [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and [general equilibrium theory](wikipedia://en/45938). He suggested a new approach to solving [cooperative games](wikipedia://en/657958) – the [nucleolus](https://www.encyclopediaofmath.org/index.php/Core_in_the_theory_of_games) – based on equity as well as feasibility considerations. This concept, originating from Schmeidler's PhD dissertation, was used to resolve a 2000 years old problem. [Robert Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aumann) and [Michael Maschler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Maschler), in a paper published in 1985, showed that a conundrum from the [Babylonian Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud), which defied scholars’ attempts at comprehension over two millennia, was naturally resolved when applying the concept of the nucleolus.[^o5]

Schmeidler also pioneered the study of [non-atomic strategic games](https://www.encyclopediaofmath.org/index.php/Non-atomic_game),[^o6] in which each player has negligible impact on the play of the game, as well as the related concept of “congestion games”, where a player's payoff only depends on the distribution of the other players’ strategic choices (and not on individual choices).

Schmeidler has made many other contributions, ranging from conceptual issues in [implementation theory](wikipedia://en/11268855), to mathematical results in [measure theory](wikipedia://en/19873). But his most influential contribution is probably in [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216). Schmeidler was the first to propose a general-purpose, axiomatically-based decision theoretic model that deviated from the Bayesian dictum, according to which any uncertainty can and should be quantified by probabilities. He suggested and axiomatized [Choquet Expected Utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choquet_expected_utility),[^o7][^o8] according to which uncertainty is modeled by a [capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacity_utilization) (not-necessarily-additive set function) and expectation is computed by the [Choquet integral](wikipedia://en/10252066).

While this approach can be used to explain commonly observed behavior in [Ellsberg](wikipedia://en/1912480)'s experiments, Schmeidler's motivation was not to explain psychological findings. Rather, along the lines attributed to [Frank Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Knight) and [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes), the argument is normative, suggesting that *it is not necessarily more rational to be Bayesian than not*.[^o9] While in the experiments, drawing balls from urns, one may adopt a probabilistic belief, in real life one often couldn't find a natural candidate for one's beliefs.[^o10]

With [Elisha Pazner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Pazner), he introduced the notion of [egalitarian equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarian_equivalence) - a criterion for [fair division](wikipedia://en/669899) of homogeneous resources, that has advantages over the previously studied criterion of [envy-freeness](wikipedia://en/47735132).

With his student, [Itzhak Gilboa](wikipedia://en/38344871), David Schmeidler also developed the theory [maxmin expected utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxmin_expected_utility)[^o11] and [case-based decision theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case-based_decision_theory).[^o12][^o13] He has also served as the advisor of [Peter Wakker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Wakker), [Shiri Alon](https://shirialon.weebly.com/), and [Xiangyu Qu](https://xiangyuqu.weebly.com/).

- 1973: "Equilibrium points of non-atomic games", [Journal of Statistical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Statistical_Physics) 7: 295–301.

- 1989: (with [Itzhak Gilboa](wikipedia://en/38344871)) "Maximin expected utility with a non-unique prior", [Journal of Mathematical Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Mathematical_Economics) 18: 141–153.

## Honors {#Honors}

David Schmeidler was a Fellow of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society), Honorary Foreign Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and a Member of the [Israeli Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities). He served as the President of the [Game Theory Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Theory_Society) (2014–2016).
