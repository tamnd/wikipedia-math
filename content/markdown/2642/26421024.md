# Balanos Vasilopoulos

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Balanos Vasilopoulos"}

**Balanos Vasilopoulos** (Μπαλάνος Βασιλόπουλος; 1694–1760) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks) [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) cleric, author, mathematician, physicist, and philosopher. He is known for attempting to solve [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110). He was one of the most influential Greek mathematicians of the 18th century. His teacher was the notable scientist [Methodios Anthrakites](wikipedia://en/23159763). He made a significant contribution to the [Modern Greek Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Greek_Enlightenment) during the [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) occupation of the Greek world.[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Balanos was born in [Ioannina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioannina), a major center of the Greek Enlightenment movement during that time.[^o5] He was a student of [Methodios Anthrakites](wikipedia://en/23159763) director of the local Gouma (later Balaneios) school. As a teacher, he initially became director of the school Epifaneios Igoumenos (1719–1734) and then the Gouma. Both schools were the most prestigious in Ioannina.

Regarding the [Greek language question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language_question), he supported the conservative party and teaching classical Greek in education. [Eugenios Voulgaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenios_Voulgaris) accused Balanos of not supporting the progressive [Demotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demotic_Greek).[^o6] In ca. 1760 his son [Kosmas Balanos](wikipedia://en/32101480) succeeded him as schoolmaster of the Gouma School.[^o7]

## Work {#Work}

Because he experienced the [Methodios Affair](wikipedia://en/23159763) first hand he was a strict teacher of [Korydalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korydalism). On the other hand, he expanded and reprinted the work of his teacher Methodios Anthrakites *The Way of Mathematics* (Οδός Μαθηματική, 1749).[^o9] This was the first handbook in the field of mathematics published in Greek during the [Ottoman occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Greece).[^o10] He also wrote the following, and various other books of less significance:

- *Έκθεσις ακριβεστάτη της αριθμητικής (Precise Exposition of Arithmetic)*, Venice, 1803
- *Ερμηνεία εις τους αφορισμούς του Ιπποκράτους (Interpretation of the Aphorisms of [Hippocrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocrates))*

Balanos claimed to have solved the problem of [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110), i.e. finding the cube root of 2 using ruler and compasses. He published this in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) in 1756, and attempted to gain international recognition for his solution from the community of mathematicians, and in particular [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) and the members of the [Saint Petersburg Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences). His son [Kosmas Balanos](wikipedia://en/32101480) rejected the solution in a work published in 1816 after his own death, *Αντιπελάργησις* (*Antipelargisis, Against the Stork*). The problem was later proved to be impossible.[^o11][^o12]
