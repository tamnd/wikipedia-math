# Lévy's modulus of continuity theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lévy's modulus of continuity theorem"}

**Lévy's modulus of continuity theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) that gives a result about an [almost sure](wikipedia://en/351908) behaviour of an estimate of the [modulus of continuity](wikipedia://en/964161) for [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984), that is used to model what's known as [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436).

:::

Lévy's modulus of continuity theorem is named after the French mathematician [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462).

## Statement of the result {#Statement_of_the_result}

Let $B : [0, 1] \times \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ be a standard Wiener process. Then, [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908),

$$\lim_{h \to 0} \sup_{t, t'\leq 1; |t-t'|\leq h } \frac{| B_{t'} - B_{t} |}{\sqrt{2 h \log (1 / h)}} = 1.$$

In other words, the [sample paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_path) of Brownian motion have modulus of continuity

$$\omega_{B} (\delta) = c\sqrt{2 \delta \log (1 / \delta)}$$

with probability one, for $c > 1$ and sufficiently small $\delta > 0$.[^o3]
