# Skorokhod's representation theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Skorokhod's representation theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Skorokhod's representation theorem** is a result that shows that a [weakly convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures) [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) whose limit measure is sufficiently well-behaved can be represented as the distribution/law of a [pointwise convergent](wikipedia://en/340198) sequence of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) defined on a common [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325). It is named for the Ukrainian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [A. V. Skorokhod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Skorokhod).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $(\mu_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of probability measures on a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) $S$ such that $\mu_n$ [converges weakly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) to some probability measure $\mu_\infty$ on $S$ as $n \to \infty$. Suppose also that the [support](wikipedia://en/6591796) of $\mu_\infty$ is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855). Then there exist $S$-valued random variables $X_n$ defined on a common probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})$ such that the law of $X_n$ is $\mu_n$ for all $n$ (including $n=\infty$) and such that $(X_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ converges to $X_\infty$, $\mathbf{P}$-almost surely.

:::
