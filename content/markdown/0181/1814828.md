# Albanese variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Albanese variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Albanese variety** $A(V)$, named for [Giacomo Albanese](wikipedia://en/629009), is a generalization of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of a curve.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Precise statement"}

## Precise statement {#Precise_statement}

The Albanese variety of a smooth projective algebraic variety $V$ is an abelian variety $\operatorname{Alb}(V)$ together with a morphism $V\to \operatorname{Alb}(V)$ such that any morphism from $V$ to an abelian variety factors uniquely through this morphism. For complex manifolds, (txt) defined the Albanese variety in a similar way, as a morphism from $V$ to a complex torus $\operatorname{Alb}(V)$ such that any morphism to a complex torus factors uniquely through this map. (The complex torus $\operatorname{Alb}(V)$ need not be algebraic in this case.)

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

For [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) the dimension of the Albanese variety is the [Hodge number](wikipedia://en/501590) $h^{1,0}$, the dimension of the space of [differentials of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentials_of_the_first_kind) on $V$, which for surfaces is called the [irregularity of a surface](wikipedia://en/22857258). In terms of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519), any holomorphic 1-form on $V$ is a [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) of translation-invariant 1-form on the Albanese variety, coming from the holomorphic [cotangent space](wikipedia://en/6620) of $\operatorname{Alb}(V)$ at its identity element. Just as for the curve case, by choice of a [base point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_point) on $V$ (from which to 'integrate'), an **Albanese morphism**

$$V \to \operatorname{Alb}(V)$$

is defined, along which the 1-forms pull back. This morphism is unique up to a translation on the Albanese variety. For varieties over fields of positive characteristic, the dimension of the Albanese variety may be less than the Hodge numbers $h^{1,0}$ and $h^{0,1}$ (which need not be equal). To see the former note that the Albanese variety is dual to the [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety), whose tangent space at the identity is given by $H^1(X, O_X).$ That $\dim \operatorname{Alb}(X) \leq h^{1,0}$ is a result of [Jun-ichi Igusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun-ichi_Igusa) in the bibliography.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Roitman's theorem"}

## Roitman's theorem {#Roitman's_theorem}

If the ground field *k* is [algebraically closed](wikipedia://en/1018), the Albanese map $V \to \operatorname{Alb}(V)$ can be shown to factor over a group homomorphism (also called the *Albanese map*)

$$CH_0(V) \to \operatorname{Alb}(V)(k)$$

from the [Chow group](wikipedia://en/4282051) of 0-dimensional cycles on *V* to the group of [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) of $\operatorname{Alb}(V)$, which is an abelian group since $\operatorname{Alb}(V)$ is an abelian variety.

*Roitman's theorem*, introduced by (txt), asserts that, for *l* prime to char(*k*), the Albanese map induces an isomorphism on the *l*-torsion subgroups.[^o10][^o11] The constraint on the primality of the order of torsion to the characteristic of the base field has been removed by Milne[^o12] shortly thereafter: the torsion subgroup of $\operatorname{CH}_0(X)$ and the torsion subgroup of *k*-valued points of the Albanese variety of *X* coincide.

Replacing the Chow group by Suslin–Voevodsky algebraic singular homology after the introduction of [Motivic cohomology](wikipedia://en/809314) *Roitman's theorem* has been obtained and reformulated in the motivic framework. For example, a similar result holds for non-singular quasi-projective varieties.[^o13] Further versions of *Roitman's theorem* are available for normal schemes.[^o14] Actually, the most general formulations of *Roitman's theorem* (i.e. homological, cohomological, and [Borel–Moore](wikipedia://en/6495737)) involve the motivic Albanese complex $\operatorname{LAlb} (V)$ and have been proven by Luca Barbieri-Viale and Bruno Kahn (see the references III.13).

:::

## Connection to Picard variety {#Connection_to_Picard_variety}

The Albanese variety is [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_theory_of_abelian_varieties) to the [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety) (the [connected component](wikipedia://en/6233) of zero of the [Picard scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_scheme) classifying [invertible sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible_sheaves) on *V*):

$$\operatorname{Alb} V = (\operatorname{Pic}_0 V)^\vee.$$

For algebraic curves, the [Abel–Jacobi theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel–Jacobi_theorem) implies that the Albanese and Picard varieties are isomorphic.
