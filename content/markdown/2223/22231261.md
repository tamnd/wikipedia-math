# Sum of radicals

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sum of radicals"}

In mathematics, a **sum of radicals** is defined as a finite [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of [$n$th roots](wikipedia://en/235029):

:::

$$\sum_{i=1}^n k_i\sqrt[r_i]{x_i},$$

where $n, r_i$ are [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) and $k_i, x_i$ are [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438).

A particular special case arising in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) is the [square-root sum problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-root_sum_problem), asking whether it is possible to determine the sign of a sum of [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208), with integer coefficients, in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time). This is of importance for many problems in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), since the computation of the [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) between two points in the general case involves the computation of a [square root](wikipedia://en/29208), and therefore the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) or the length of a polygonal chain takes the form of a sum of radicals.[^o2]

In 1991, Blömer proposed a polynomial time [Monte Carlo algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_algorithm) for determining whether a sum of radicals is zero, or more generally whether it represents a rational number.[^o3] Blömer's result applies more generally than the square-root sum problem, to sums of radicals that are not necessarily square roots. However, his algorithm does not solve the problem, because it does not determine the sign of a non-zero sum of radicals.[^o3]
