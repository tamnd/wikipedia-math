# Akram Aldroubi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Akram Aldroubi"}

**Akram Aldroubi** is an American mathematician known for his work in [sampling theory](wikipedia://en/37864), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), and their applications to [signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processing) and [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_image_processing) as well as biomedical data analysis.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Aldroubi received a [diplôme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplôme_universitaire) in electrical engineering from [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne), in Switzerland, in 1982.[^o4][^o5] He studied mathematics at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), earning his master's in 1984 and his doctorate in 1987.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

After Carnegie, Aldroubi worked as a researcher at the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health).[^o6] He moved to the Department of Mathematics at Vanderbilt University in 1997 where he currently holds a position of professor.[^o4] He has worked on [sampling theory](wikipedia://en/37864), [wavelets](wikipedia://en/50903), [frame theory](wikipedia://en/4504771) and their applications to [signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processing) and [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_image_processing).[^o6][^o8]

In 2009, he was awarded the [Fulbright Foreign Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) to continue teaching and conduct research.[^o9] In 2014, Aldroubi was inducted as a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for "contributions to modern [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) and its applications, and for building bridges between mathematics and other areas of science and engineering."[^o10][^o11]
