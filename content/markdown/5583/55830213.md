# Fernando de Helguero

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fernando de Helguero"}

**Fernando de Helguero** (1 November 1880, [Pelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelago) – 28 December 1908, [Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messina)) was an Italian mathematician, statistician and pioneer of biostatistics.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

Fernando de Helguero was born near [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence). He studied mathematics at the University of Rome. After receiving his licentiate degree in 1903,[^o4] he taught mathematics while he studied natural sciences, biology, statistics, and biometry.[^o2] He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Rome in April 1908.[^o5] However, he died later the same year in the [1908 Messina earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Messina_earthquake).[^o2]

His collected papers were published in 1972.[^o6]

According to [Adelchi Azzalini](wikipedia://en/73686110) and Antonella Capitanio:[^o7] The tragic end of de Helguero's life prevented the development of his innovative ideas, and the whole formulation passed unnoticed for the rest of the 20th century. It appears that his contribution has re-emerged only in the discussion of Azzalini (2005), thanks to a personal communication of D. M. Cifarelli. ... The formulation is distinctly one step ahead of the mainstream approach of data fitting of those years: probability distributions must not simply be devised to provide a numerical fit to observed frequencies, but they must also help to understand how the non-normal distribution has been generalized. This goal can be achieved by a formulation which relates perturbation of normality to the effect of some external mechanism.
