# Hélène Frankowska

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hélène Frankowska"}

**Hélène Frankowska**, or **Halina Frankowska** is a Polish and French mathematician known for her research in [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) and [set-valued analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-valued_analysis). She is a director of research at the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique), and works in the Institut de Mathématiques de Jussieu of [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Frankowska completed her undergraduate studies in 1979 at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), in the Department of Mathematics, Informatics and Mechanics. After a year of studies at the [International School for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_for_Advanced_Studies) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), Italy, she completed her [doctorate of the third cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate#France) in 1983 and her [state doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_doctorate) in mathematics in 1984 at [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University). Her dissertation, jointly supervised by [Czesław Olech](wikipedia://en/31633829) and Jean-Pierre Aubin, was *Nonsmooth Analysis and its Applications to Viability and Control*.

## Contributions and recognition {#Contributions_and_recognition}

With Aubin, Frankowska wrote *Set-Valued Analysis* (Birkhäuser, 1990, reprinted 2009).

She was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the 2010 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), in the section on control theory and optimization.

She is a [SIAM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Fellow), in the 2024 class of fellows, elected "for fundamental and pioneering contributions to optimal control theory and differential inclusions, both deterministic, stochastic, and in Wasserstein spaces".
