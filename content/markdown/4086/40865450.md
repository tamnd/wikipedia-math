# Bell triangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bell triangle"}

*Image: BellNumberAnimated.gif.* Construction of the Bell triangle

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Bell triangle** is a triangle of numbers analogous to [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497), whose values count [partitions of a set](wikipedia://en/340240) in which a given element is the largest [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798). It is named for its close connection to the [Bell numbers](wikipedia://en/201022),[^o2] which may be found on both sides of the triangle, and which are in turn named after [Eric Temple Bell](wikipedia://en/255236). The Bell triangle has been discovered independently by multiple authors, beginning with (txt) and including also (txt) and (Cohn Even Menger Hooper 1962), and for that reason has also been called **Aitken's array** or the **Peirce triangle**.

:::

## Values {#Values}

Different sources give the same triangle in different orientations, some flipped from each other.[^o8] In a format similar to that of Pascal's triangle, and in the order listed in the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004) (OEIS), its first few rows are:[^o6]

1 1 2 2 3 5 5 7 10 15 15 20 27 37 52 52 67 87 114 151 203 203 255 322 409 523 674 877

::: {#o10 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The Bell triangle may be constructed by placing the number 1 in its first position. After that placement, the leftmost value in each row of the triangle is filled by copying the rightmost value in the previous row. The remaining positions in each row are filled by a rule very similar to that for [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497): they are the sum of the two values to the left and upper left of the position.

Thus, after the initial placement of the number 1 in the top row, it is the last position in its row and is copied to the leftmost position in the next row. The third value in the triangle, 2, is the sum of the two previous values above-left and left of it. As the last value in its row, the 2 is copied into the third row, and the process continues in the same way.

:::

## Combinatorial interpretation {#Combinatorial_interpretation}

The [Bell numbers](wikipedia://en/201022) themselves, on the left and right sides of the triangle, count the number of ways of [partitioning](wikipedia://en/340240) a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) into subsets, or equivalently the number of [equivalence relations](wikipedia://en/9259) on the set. (Sun Wu 2011) provide the following combinatorial interpretation of each value in the triangle. Following Sun and Wu, let *A<sub>n,k</sub>* denote the value that is *k* positions from the left in the *n*th row of the triangle, with the top of the triangle numbered as *A*<sub>1,1</sub>. Then *A<sub>n,k</sub>* counts the number of partitions of the set {1, 2, ..., *n* + 1} in which the element *k* + 1 is the only element of its set and each higher-numbered element is in a set of more than one element. That is, *k* + 1 must be the largest [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) of the partition.

For instance, the number 3 in the middle of the third row of the triangle would be labeled, in their notation, as *A*<sub>3,2</sub>, and counts the number of partitions of {1, 2, 3, 4} in which 3 is the largest singleton element. There are three such partitions:

{1}, {2, 4}, {3}

{1, 4}, {2}, {3}

{1, 2, 4}, {3}.

The remaining partitions of these four elements either do not have 3 in a set by itself, or they have a larger singleton set {4}, and in either case are not counted in *A*<sub>3,2</sub>.

In the same notation, (Sun Wu 2011) augment the triangle with another diagonal to the left of its other values, of the numbers

*A*<sub>*n*,0</sub> = 1, 0, 1, 1, 4, 11, 41, 162, ...

counting partitions of the same set of *n* + 1 items in which only the first item is a singleton. Their augmented triangle is

1 0 1 1 1 2 1 2 3 5 4 5 7 10 15 11 15 20 27 37 52 41 52 67 87 114 151 203 162 203 255 322 409 523 674 877

This triangle may be constructed similarly to the original version of Bell's triangle, but with a different rule for starting each row: the leftmost value in each row is the difference of the rightmost and leftmost values of the previous row.

An alternative but more technical interpretation of the numbers in the same augmented triangle is given by (Quaintance Kwong 2013).

## Diagonals and row sums {#Diagonals_and_row_sums}

The leftmost and rightmost diagonals of the Bell triangle both contain the sequence 1, 1, 2, 5, 15, 52, ... of the [Bell numbers](wikipedia://en/201022) (with the initial element missing in the case of the rightmost diagonal). The next diagonal parallel to the rightmost diagonal gives the sequence of [differences](wikipedia://en/39147) of two consecutive Bell numbers, 1, 3, 10, 37, ..., and each subsequent parallel diagonal gives the sequence of differences of previous diagonals.

In this way, as (Aitken 1933) observed, this triangle can be interpreted as implementing the [Gregory–Newton interpolation formula](wikipedia://en/216333), which finds the coefficients of a polynomial from the sequence of its values at consecutive integers by using successive differences. This formula closely resembles a [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) that can be used to define the Bell numbers.

The sums of each row of the triangle, 1, 3, 10, 37, ..., are the same sequence of first differences appearing in the second-from-right diagonal of the triangle.[^o18] The *n*th number in this sequence also counts the number of partitions of *n* elements into subsets, where one of the subsets is distinguished from the others; for instance, there are 10 ways of partitioning three items into subsets and then choosing one of the subsets.[^o19]

## Related constructions {#Related_constructions}

A different triangle of numbers, with the Bell numbers on only one side, and with each number determined as a weighted sum of nearby numbers in the previous row, was described by (Aigner 1999).
