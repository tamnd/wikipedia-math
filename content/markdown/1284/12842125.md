# Group Hopf algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Group Hopf algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **group Hopf algebra** of a given [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is a certain construct related to the symmetries of [group actions](wikipedia://en/12781). Deformations of group Hopf algebras are foundational in the theory of [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *G* be a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) and *k* a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). The *group Hopf algebra* of *G* over *k*, denoted *kG* (or *k*\[*G*\]), is as a set (and a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370)) the [free vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_vector_space) on *G* over *k*. As an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788), its product is defined by linear extension of the group composition in *G*, with multiplicative unit the identity in *G*; this product is also known as [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519).

Note that while the group algebra of a *[finite](wikipedia://en/323707)* group can be identified with the space of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) on the group, for an infinite group these are different. The group algebra, consisting of *finite* sums, corresponds to functions on the group that vanish for [cofinitely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinitely) many points; [topologically](wikipedia://en/29954) (using the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology)), these are the functions with [compact support](wikipedia://en/381013#Compact_support).

However, the group algebra $k[G]$ and $k^G$ – the [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) of functions of *G* into *k* – are dual: given an element of the group algebra $x = \sum_{g\in G} a_g g$ and a function on the group $f\colon G \to k,$ these pair to give an element of *k* via $(x,f) = \sum_{g\in G} a_g f(g),$ which is a well-defined sum because it is finite.

:::

## Hopf algebra structure {#Hopf_algebra_structure}

We give *kG* the structure of a cocommutative [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) by defining the coproduct, counit, and antipode to be the linear extensions of the following maps defined on *G*:[^o5]

$$\Delta(x) = x \otimes x;$$

$$\epsilon(x) = 1_{k};$$

$$S(x) = x^{-1}.$$

The required Hopf algebra compatibility axioms are easily checked. Notice that $\mathcal{G}(kG)$, the set of group-like elements of *kG* (i.e. elements $a \in kG$ such that $\Delta(a) = a \otimes a$ and $\epsilon(a)=1$), is precisely *G*.

## Symmetries of group actions {#Symmetries_of_group_actions}

Let *G* be a group and *X* a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). Any [action](wikipedia://en/12781) $\alpha\colon G \times X \to X$ of *G* on *X* gives a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $\phi_\alpha\colon G \to \mathrm{Aut}(F(X))$, where *F*(*X*) is an appropriate algebra of *k*-valued functions, such as the Gelfand–Naimark algebra $C_0(X)$ of [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) functions [vanishing at infinity](wikipedia://en/461986). The homomorphism $\phi_{\alpha}$ is defined by $\phi_\alpha(g) = \alpha^*_g$, with the adjoint $\alpha^*_{g}$ defined by

$$\alpha^*_g(f)x = f(\alpha(g,x))$$

for $g \in G, f \in F(X)$, and $x \in X$.

This may be described by a [linear mapping](wikipedia://en/18102)

$$\lambda\colon kG \otimes F(X) \to F(X)$$

$$\lambda((c_1 g_1 + c_2 g_2 + \cdots ) \otimes f)(x) = c_1 f(g_1 \cdot x) + c_2 f(g_2 \cdot x) + \cdots$$

where $c_1,c_2,\ldots \in k$, $g_1, g_2,\ldots$ are the elements of *G*, and $g_i \cdot x := \alpha(g_i,x)$, which has the property that group-like elements in $kG$ give rise to [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of *F*(*X*).

$\lambda$ endows *F*(*X*) with an important extra structure, described below.

## Hopf module algebras and the Hopf smash product {#Hopf_module_algebras_and_the_Hopf_smash_product}

Let *H* be a Hopf algebra. A (left) *Hopf H-module algebra* *A* is an algebra which is a (left) [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over the algebra *H* such that $h \cdot 1_A = \epsilon(h)1_A$ and

$$h \cdot (ab) = (h_{(1)} \cdot a)(h_{(2)} \cdot b)$$

whenever $a, b \in A$, $h \in H$ and $\Delta(h) = h_{(1)} \otimes h_{(2)}$ in sumless [Sweedler notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweedler_notation). When $\lambda$ has been defined as in the previous section, this turns *F*(*X*) into a left Hopf *kG*-module algebra, which allows the following construction.

Let *H* be a Hopf algebra and *A* a left Hopf *H*-module algebra. The *smash product* algebra $A\mathop{\#} H$ is the vector space $A \otimes H$ with the product

$$(a \otimes h)(b \otimes k) := a(h_{(1)} \cdot b) \otimes h_{(2)}k,$$

and we write $a\mathop{\#} h$ for $a \otimes h$ in this context.[^o9]

In our case, $A = F(X)$ and $H = kG$, and we have

$$(a\mathop{\#} g_1)(b\mathop{\#} g_2) = a(g_1 \cdot b)\mathop{\#} g_1 g_2.$$

In this case the smash product algebra $A\mathop{\#} kG$ is also denoted by $A\mathop{\#} G$.

The [cyclic homology](wikipedia://en/1460172) of Hopf smash products has been computed.[^o10] However, there the smash product is called a crossed product and denoted $A \rtimes H$- not to be confused with the [crossed product](wikipedia://en/2749495) derived from $C^{*}$-dynamical systems.[^o11]

:::
