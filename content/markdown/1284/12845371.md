# Borel fixed-point theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Borel fixed-point theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Borel fixed-point theorem** is a [fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/1011848) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) generalizing the [Lie–Kolchin theorem](wikipedia://en/1020661). The result was proved by (txt).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

If *G* is a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012), linear [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) [acting regularly](wikipedia://en/1997#Regular_functions) on a [non-empty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-empty), [complete](wikipedia://en/1047627) [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *k*, then there is a [*G* fixed-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Fixed_points_and_stabilizer_subgroups) of *V*.

The Lie-Kolchin theorem proves this result under the stronger hypothesis that *V* is a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469).

A more general version of the theorem holds over a field *k* that is not necessarily algebraically closed. A solvable algebraic group *G* is *split over k* or *k-split* if *G* admits a [composition series](wikipedia://en/297493) whose composition factors are isomorphic (over *k*) to the [additive group](wikipedia://en/332229) $\mathbb G_a$ or the [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/1110742) $\mathbb G_m$. If *G* is a connected, *k*-split solvable algebraic group acting regularly on a complete variety *V* having a [*k*-rational point](wikipedia://en/2960200), then there is a *G* fixed-point of *V*.[^o5]
