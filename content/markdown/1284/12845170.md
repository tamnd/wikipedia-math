# Wiedersehen pair

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wiedersehen pair"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831)—specifically, in [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243)—a **Wiedersehen pair** is a pair of distinct points *x* and *y* on a (usually, but not necessarily, two-dimensional) [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) (*M*, *g*) such that every [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) through *x* also passes through *y*, and the same with *x* and *y* interchanged.

:::

For example, on an ordinary sphere where the geodesics are [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082), the Wiedersehen pairs are exactly the pairs of [antipodal points](wikipedia://en/367577).

If every point of an [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriented_surface) manifold (*M*, *g*) belongs to a Wiedersehen pair, then (*M*, *g*) is said to be a **Wiedersehen manifold**. The concept was introduced by the [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Wilhelm Blaschke](wikipedia://en/728998) and comes from the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) term meaning "seeing again". As it turns out, in each dimension *n* the only Wiedersehen manifold (up to [isometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometry_%28Riemannian_geometry%29)) is the standard Euclidean [*n*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). Initially known as the **Blaschke conjecture**, this result was established by combined works of [Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363), [Kazdan](wikipedia://en/12870493), [Weinstein](wikipedia://en/7344764) (for even *n*), and [Yang](wikipedia://en/15315411) (odd *n*).
