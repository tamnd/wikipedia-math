# Toral subalgebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toral subalgebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **toral subalgebra** is a [Lie subalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_subalgebra) of a general linear Lie algebra all of whose elements are [semisimple](wikipedia://en/7252030) (or [diagonalizable](wikipedia://en/246325) over an algebraically closed field).[^o2] Over an algebraically closed field, every toral Lie algebra is [abelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_Lie_algebra);[^o2][^o3] thus, its elements are [simultaneously diagonalizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneously_diagonalizable).

:::

## In semisimple and reductive Lie algebras {#In_semisimple_and_reductive_Lie_algebras}

A subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ of a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) $\mathfrak g$ is called toral if the [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_endomorphism) of $\mathfrak h$ on $\mathfrak g$, $\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak h) \subset \mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak g)$ is a toral subalgebra. A maximal toral Lie subalgebra of a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra, or more generally of a finite-dimensional [reductive Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/863720), over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 is a [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) and vice versa.[^o5] In particular, a maximal toral Lie subalgebra in this setting is [self-normalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-normalizing), coincides with its centralizer, and the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386) of $\mathfrak g$ restricted to $\mathfrak h$ is nondegenerate.

For more general Lie algebras, a Cartan subalgebra may differ from a maximal toral subalgebra.

In a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ over an algebraically closed field of a characteristic zero, a toral subalgebra exists.[^o2] In fact, if $\mathfrak g$ has only nilpotent elements, then it is [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/3358867) ([Engel's theorem](wikipedia://en/1341657)), but then its [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386) is identically zero, contradicting semisimplicity. Hence, $\mathfrak g$ must have a nonzero semisimple element, say *x*; the linear span of *x* is then a toral subalgebra.

[^o3]: Proof (from Humphreys): Let $x \in \mathfrak{h}$. Since $\operatorname{ad}(x)$ is diagonalizable, it is enough to show the eigenvalues of $\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)$ are all zero. Let $y \in \mathfrak{h}$ be an eigenvector of $\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$. Then $x$ is a sum of eigenvectors of $\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)$ and then $-\lambda y = \operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)x$ is a linear combination of eigenvectors of $\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)$ with nonzero eigenvalues. But, unless $\lambda = 0$, we have that $-\lambda y$ is an eigenvector of $\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)$ with eigenvalue zero, a contradiction. Thus, $\lambda = 0$. $\square$
