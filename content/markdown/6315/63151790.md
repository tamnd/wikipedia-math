# Newman–Janis algorithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Newman–Janis algorithm"}

In [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), the **Newman–Janis algorithm (NJA)** is a [complexification](wikipedia://en/755604) technique for finding [exact solutions](wikipedia://en/1686520) to the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540). In 1964, Newman and Janis showed that the [Kerr metric](wikipedia://en/456715) could be obtained from the [Schwarzschild metric](wikipedia://en/264606) by means of a coordinate transformation and allowing the radial coordinate to take on complex values. Originally, no clear reason for why the algorithm works was known.[^o2]

:::

In 1998, Drake and Szekeres gave a detailed explanation of the success of the algorithm and proved the uniqueness of certain solutions. In particular, the only perfect fluid solution generated by NJA is the Kerr metric and the only [Petrov type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrov_type) D solution is the [Kerr–Newman metric](wikipedia://en/1447904).[^o3]

The algorithm works well on *ƒ*(*R*) and Einstein–Maxwell–Dilaton theories, but doesn't return expected results on [Braneworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brane_cosmology) and [Born–Infield theories.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born–Infeld_model)[^o4]
