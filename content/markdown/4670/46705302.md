# Uffe Haagerup

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Uffe Haagerup"}

**Uffe Valentin Haagerup** (19 December 1949 – 5 July 2015) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) from Denmark.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Uffe Haagerup was born in [Kolding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolding), but grew up on the island of [Funen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funen), in the small town of [Fåborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fåborg). The field of mathematics had his interest from early on, encouraged and inspired by his older brother. In fourth grade Uffe was doing [trigonometric](wikipedia://en/18717261) and [logarithmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithms) calculations. He graduated as a student from [Svendborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svendborg) [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Denmark%29) in 1968, whereupon he relocated to Copenhagen and immediately began his studies of mathematics and physics at the University of Copenhagen, again inspired by his older brother who also studied the same subjects at the same university. Early university studies in Einstein's [general theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_theory_of_relativity) and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), sparked a lasting interest in the mathematical field of [operator algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_algebras), in particular [von Neumann algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebras) and [Tomita–Takesaki theory](wikipedia://en/2898625). In 1974 he received his Candidate's degree ([cand. scient.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate#Denmark)) from the University of Copenhagen and in 1981 – at the age of 31 – Uffe was appointed at the University of Odense – now University of Southern Denmark, as the youngest professor of mathematics ([dr. scient.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_doctoral_degrees_awarded_by_country#Denmark)) in the country at the time. Pregraduate summer schools at the university and later on extended professional research stays abroad, helped him discover and build a diverse and lasting international network of colleagues. Haagerup accidentally drowned on 5 July 2015, aged 65, while swimming in the [Baltic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea) close to [Fåborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fåborg) where his family owned a cabin.[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

Uffe Haagerup's mathematical focus has been on the fields of [operator algebra](wikipedia://en/455987), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446),[^o6] but his publications has a broad scope and also involves [free probability theory](wikipedia://en/679696) and [random matrices](wikipedia://en/1648765). He has participated in many international mathematical groups and networks from early on, and has worked as ordinary contributor and participator, organizer, lecturer and editor.

Following his appointment as professor at Odense, Haagerup got acquainted with [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628), when he did research in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia) and later at the [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) in Los Angeles. Jones inspired him to take up studies in and work on [subfactor](wikipedia://en/2780098) theory. Uffe Haagerup has done extensive work with fellow mathematician [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340) on von Neumann algebras. His solution to the so-called "Champagne Problem",[^o7] secured him the Samuel Friedmann Award in April 1985, although it was first published in Acta Mathematica in 1987. Uffe considered this his best work. An early contact and collaboration was established with Swedish colleagues at the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) and the Norwegian group on operator algebra, where Uffe Haagerup has a long history of collaboration with [Erling Størmer](wikipedia://en/24884274) for example.

In the mathematical literature, Uffe Haagerup is known for the Haagerup property, the Haagerup subfactor, the Asaeda-Haagerup subfactor and the Haagerup list.[^o8]

From 2000 to 2006 Uffe served as editor-in-chief of the journal *[Acta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/6860708)*. He was a member of the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters) and the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters). He worked at the Department of Mathematics at the University of Copenhagen from 2010 to 2014,[^o9] where he was involved in the Centre for Symmetry and Deformation (SYM), but was appointed professor of mathematics in 2015 at the University of Southern Denmark in [Odense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odense).[^o10]

## Prizes and honors {#Prizes_and_honors}

Uffe Haagerup received several awards and honours throughout his academic career. Amongst the most academically prestigious were the Danish Ole Rømer Medal, the international [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award) and the [European Latsis Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Latsis_Prize).

- \1985. The Samuel Friedman Award ([UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) and Copenhagen)
- \1986. Invited speaker at [ICM1986](wikipedia://en/627842) (Berkeley)
- \1989. The Ole Rømer Medal (Copenhagen). The Ole Rømer Medal (est. 1944) is a Danish medal awarded by the University of Copenhagen and the municipality of Copenhagen, for outstanding research. It is considered amongst the most honourable scientific awards in the country, established in commemoration of [Ole Rømer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Rømer) on his 300th anniversary.
- \2002. Plenary speaker at ICM2002 (Beijing)
- \2007. Distinguished lecturer at the [Fields Institute of Mathematical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Institute_of_Mathematical_Research) (Toronto)
- \2008. The Humboldt Research Award (Münster)
- 2010–2014 [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) Advanced Grant
- \2012. Plenary speaker at [International Congress on Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/23897905) ICMP12 (Aalborg)
- \2012. 14th European Latsis Prize from the European Science Foundation, ESF (Brussels)[^o12][^o13]
- \2013. Honorary Doctorate from [East China Normal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Normal_University), ECNU (Shanghai)[^o14]
