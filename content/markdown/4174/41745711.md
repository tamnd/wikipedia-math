# 3-3 duoprism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="3-3 duoprism"}

In the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of 4 dimensions, the **3-3 duoprism** or **triangular duoprism** is a [four-dimensional convex polytope](wikipedia://en/24979).

:::

## Descriptions {#Descriptions}

The [duoprism](wikipedia://en/2114155) is a 4-polytope that can be constructed using [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two polygons. In the case of 3-3 duoprism is the simplest among them, and it can be constructed using Cartesian product of two triangles. The resulting duoprism has 9 vertices, 18 edges, and 15 faces—which include 9 [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) and 6 triangles. Its cells are 6 [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584). It has [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , and symmetry 3,2,3, order 72.

The [hypervolume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypervolume) of a uniform 3-3 duoprism with edge length $a$ is

$$V_4 = {3\over 16}a^4.$$

This is the square of the [area of an equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285#Principal_properties),

$$A = {\sqrt3\over 4}a^2.$$

The 3-3 duoprism can be represented as a graph with the same number of vertices and edges. Like the [Berlekamp–van Lint–Seidel graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlekamp–van_Lint–Seidel_graph) and the unknown solution to [Conway's 99-graph problem](wikipedia://en/59939359), every edge is part of a unique triangle and every non-adjacent pair of vertices is the diagonal of a unique square. It is a [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057), a [locally linear graph](wikipedia://en/59938063), a [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) with parameters (9,4,1,2), the $3\times 3$ [rook's graph](wikipedia://en/7324959), and the [Paley graph](wikipedia://en/5719307) of order 9. This graph is also the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of the group $G=\langle a,b:a^3=b^3=1,\ ab=ba\rangle\simeq C_3\times C_3$ with generating set $S=\{a,a^2,b,b^2\}$.

The minimal distance graph of a 3-3 duoprism may be ascertained by the [Cartesian product of graphs](wikipedia://en/2856852) between two identical both [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_3$.

## 3-3 duopyramid {#3-3_duopyramid}

*Image: 3-3 duopyramid ortho.png.* The orthogonal projection of a 3-3 duopyramid

The dual polyhedron of a 3-3 duoprism is called a **3-3 [duopyramid](wikipedia://en/36040636)** or **triangular duopyramid**. It is also known as the [cyclic polytope](wikipedia://en/26652374) C(6,4). It has 9 [tetragonal disphenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetragonal_disphenoid) cells, 18 triangular faces, 15 edges, and 6 vertices. It can be seen in orthogonal projection as a 6-gon circle of vertices, and edges connecting all pairs, just like a [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex) seen in projection.

The [regular complex polygon](wikipedia://en/50885422) <sub>2</sub>{4}<sub>3</sub>, also <sub>3</sub>{ }+<sub>3</sub>{ } has 6 vertices in $\mathbb{C}^2$ with a real representation in $\mathbb{R}^4$ matching the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) of the 3-3 duopyramid. It has 9 2-edges corresponding to the connecting edges of the 3-3 duopyramid, while the 6 edges connecting the two triangles are not included. It can be seen in a hexagonal projection with 3 sets of colored edges. This arrangement of vertices and edges makes a [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) with each vertex from one triangle is connected to every vertex on the other. It is also called a [Thomsen graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomsen_graph) or 4-*cage*.
