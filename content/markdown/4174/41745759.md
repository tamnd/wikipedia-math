::: {#o7 .subject cue=lead name="3-4 duoprism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of 4 dimensions, a **3-4 duoprism**, the second smallest p-q [duoprism](wikipedia://en/2114155), is a [4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979) resulting from the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) and a [square](wikipedia://en/659939).

:::

The *3-4 duoprism* exists in some of the [uniform 5-polytopes in the B5 family](wikipedia://en/14415338#The_B5_family).

## Related complex polygons {#Related_complex_polygons}

*Image: Complex\_polygon\_3x4\_stereographic3.svg.* Stereographic projection of complex polygon, <sub>3</sub>{}×<sub>4</sub>{} has 12 vertices and 7 3-edges, shown here with 4 red triangular 3-edges and 3 blue square 4-edges.

The quasiregular [complex polytope](wikipedia://en/8581934) <sub>3</sub>{}×<sub>4</sub>{}, , in $\mathbb{C}^2$ has a real representation as a 3-4 [duoprism](wikipedia://en/2114155) in 4-dimensional space. It has 12 vertices, and 4 3-edges and 3 4-edges. Its symmetry is <sub>3</sub>\[2\]<sub>4</sub>, order 12.[^o10]

The dual of a *3-4 duoprism* is called a **3-4 [duopyramid](wikipedia://en/36040636)**. It has 12 [digonal disphenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digonal_disphenoid) cells, 24 isosceles triangular faces, 12 edges, and 7 vertices.
