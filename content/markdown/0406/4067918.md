# Vertical and horizontal bundles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vertical and horizontal bundles"}

*Image: Ehresmann connection.png.* Here, we have a fiber bundle over a base space $X$. Each basepoint $x \in X$ corresponds to a fiber $p_x$ of points. At each point in the fiber $p \in p_x$, the vertical fiber is unique. It is the tangent space to the fiber. The horizontal fiber is non-unique. It merely has to be transverse to the vertical fiber.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **vertical bundle** and the **horizontal bundle** are [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) associated to a [smooth fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526#Differentiable_fiber_bundles). More precisely, given a smooth fiber bundle $\pi\colon E\to B$, the vertical bundle $VE$ and horizontal bundle $HE$ are [subbundles](wikipedia://en/2517151) of the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) $TE$ of $E$ whose [Whitney sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_sum) satisfies $VE\oplus HE\cong TE$. This means that, over each point $e\in E$, the fibers $V_eE$ and $H_eE$ form [complementary subspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementary_subspace) of the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) $T_eE$. The vertical bundle consists of all vectors that are tangent to the fibers, while the horizontal bundle requires some choice of complementary subbundle.

:::

To make this precise, define the **vertical space** $V_eE$ at $e\in E$ to be $\ker(d\pi_e)$. That is, the differential $d\pi_e\colon T_eE\to T_bB$ (where $b=\pi(e)$) is a linear surjection whose kernel has the same dimension as the fibers of $\pi$. If we write $F=\pi^{-1}(b)$, then $V_eE$ consists of exactly the vectors in $T_eE$ which are also tangent to $F$. The name is motivated by low-dimensional examples like the trivial line bundle over a circle, which is sometimes depicted as a vertical cylinder projecting to a horizontal circle. A subspace $H_eE$ of $T_eE$ is called a **horizontal space** if $T_eE$ is the [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_vector_spaces) of $V_eE$ and $H_eE$.

The [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of the vertical spaces V<sub>*e*</sub>*E* for each *e* in *E* is the subbundle V*E* of T*E;* this is the vertical bundle of *E*. Likewise, provided the horizontal spaces $H_eE$ vary smoothly with *e*, their disjoint union is a horizontal bundle. The use of the words "the" and "a" here is intentional: each vertical subspace is unique, defined explicitly by $\ker(d\pi_e)$. Excluding trivial cases, there are an infinite number of horizontal subspaces at each point. Also note that arbitrary choices of horizontal space at each point will not, in general, form a smooth vector bundle; they must also vary in an appropriately smooth way.

The horizontal bundle is one way to formulate the notion of an [Ehresmann connection](wikipedia://en/5731754) on a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526). Thus, for example, if *E* is a [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950), then the horizontal bundle is usually required to be *G*-invariant: such a choice is equivalent to a [connection on the principal bundle](wikipedia://en/470632).[^o2] This notably occurs when *E* is the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) associated to some vector bundle, which is a principal $\operatorname{GL}_n$ bundle.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *π*:*E*→*B* be a smooth fiber bundle over a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *B*. The vertical bundle is the [kernel](wikipedia://en/1072915) V*E* := ker(d*π*) of the [tangent map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_map) d*π* : T*E* → T*B*.[^o5]

Since dπ<sub>e</sub> is surjective at each point *e*, it yields a [*regular* subbundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_subbundle) of T*E*. Furthermore, the vertical bundle V*E* is also [integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable).

An [Ehresmann connection](wikipedia://en/5731754) on *E* is a choice of a complementary subbundle H*E* to V*E* in T*E*, called the horizontal bundle of the connection. At each point *e* in *E*, the two subspaces form a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687), such that T<sub>*e*</sub>*E* = V<sub>*e*</sub>*E* ⊕ H<sub>*e*</sub>*E*.

:::

## Example {#Example}

*Image: Vertical and horizontal subspaces for the Mobius strip.jpg.* Vertical and horizontal subspaces for the Möbius strip.

The [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) is a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) over the circle, and the circle can be pictured as the middle ring of the strip. At each point $e$ on the strip, the projection map projects it towards the middle ring, and the fiber is perpendicular to the middle ring. The vertical bundle at this point $V_eE$ is the tangent space to the fiber.

A simple example of a smooth fiber bundle is a [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). Consider the bundle *B*<sub>1</sub> := (*M* × *N*, pr<sub>1</sub>) with bundle projection pr<sub>1</sub> : *M* × *N* → *M* : (*x*, *y*) → *x*. Applying the definition in the paragraph above to find the vertical bundle, we consider first a point (m,n) in *M* × *N*. Then the image of this point under pr<sub>1</sub> is m. The preimage of m under this same pr<sub>1</sub> is {m} × *N*, so that T<sub>(m,n)</sub> ({m} × *N*) = {m} × T*N*. The vertical bundle is then V*B*<sub>1</sub> = *M* × T*N*, which is a subbundle of T(*M* ×*N*). If we take the other projection pr<sub>2</sub> : *M* × *N* → *N* : (*x*, *y*) → *y* to define the fiber bundle *B*<sub>2</sub> := (*M* × *N*, pr<sub>2</sub>) then the vertical bundle will be V*B*<sub>2</sub> = T*M* × *N*.

In both cases, the product structure gives a natural choice of horizontal bundle, and hence an Ehresmann connection: the horizontal bundle of *B*<sub>1</sub> is the vertical bundle of *B*<sub>2</sub> and vice versa.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Various important [tensors](wikipedia://en/29965) and [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) from [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) take on specific properties on the vertical and horizontal bundles, or even can be defined in terms of them. Some of these are:

- A **vertical vector field** is a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) that is in the vertical bundle. That is, for each point *e* of *E*, one chooses a vector $v_e\in V_eE$ where $V_eE \subset T_eE = T_e(E_{\pi(e)} )$ is the vertical vector space at *e*.[^o5]
- A differentiable [r-form](wikipedia://en/221519) $\alpha$ on *E* is said to be a **horizontal form** if $\alpha(v_1,...,v_r)=0$ whenever at least one of the vectors $v_1,..., v_r$ is vertical.
- The [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) vanishes on the horizontal bundle, and is non-zero only on the vertical bundle. In this way, the connection form can be used to define the horizontal bundle: The horizontal bundle is the kernel of the connection form.
- The [solder form](wikipedia://en/9792297) or [tautological one-form](wikipedia://en/1855974) vanishes on the vertical bundle and is non-zero only on the horizontal bundle. By definition, the solder form takes its values entirely in the horizontal bundle.
- For the case of a [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550), the [torsion form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_form) vanishes on the vertical bundle, and can be used to define exactly that part that needs to be added to an arbitrary connection to turn it into a [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176), i.e. to make a connection be torsionless. Indeed, if one writes θ for the solder form, then the torsion tensor Θ is given by Θ = D θ (with D the [exterior covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/849738)). For any given connection ω, there is a *unique* one-form σ on T*E*, called the [contorsion tensor](wikipedia://en/1561997), that is vanishing in the vertical bundle, and is such that ω+σ is another connection 1-form that is torsion-free. The resulting one-form ω+σ is nothing other than the Levi-Civita connection. One can take this as a definition: since the torsion is given by $\Theta = D\theta = d\theta + \omega \wedge \theta$, the vanishing of the torsion is equivalent to having $d\theta = - (\omega +\sigma) \wedge \theta$, and it is not hard to show that σ must vanish on the vertical bundle, and that σ must be *G*-invariant on each fibre (more precisely, that σ transforms in the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of *G*). Note that this defines the Levi-Civita connection without making any explicit reference to any metric tensor (although the metric tensor can be understood to be a special case of a solder form, as it establishes a mapping between the tangent and cotangent bundles of the base space, i.e. between the horizontal and vertical subspaces of the frame bundle).
- In the case where *E* is a principal bundle, then the [fundamental vector field](wikipedia://en/3946232) must necessarily live in the vertical bundle, and vanish in any horizontal bundle.

:::
