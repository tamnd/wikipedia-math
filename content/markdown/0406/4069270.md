# Difference polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Difference polynomials"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the area of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), the **general difference polynomials** are a [polynomial sequence](wikipedia://en/195990), a certain subclass of the [Sheffer polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffer_polynomials), which include the [Newton polynomials](wikipedia://en/216333), **Selberg's polynomials**, and the **Stirling interpolation polynomials** as special cases.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The general difference polynomial sequence is given by

$$p_n(z)=\frac{z}{n} {{z-\beta n -1} \choose {n-1}}$$

where ${z \choose n}$ is the [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668). For $\beta=0$, the generated polynomials $p_n(z)$ are the Newton polynomials

$$p_n(z)= {z \choose n} = \frac{z(z-1)\cdots(z-n+1)}{n!}.$$

The case of $\beta=1$ generates Selberg's polynomials, and the case of $\beta=-1/2$ generates Stirling's interpolation polynomials.

:::

## Moving differences {#Moving_differences}

Given an [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) $f(z)$, define the **moving difference** of *f* as

$$\mathcal{L}_n(f) = \Delta^n f (\beta n)$$

where $\Delta$ is the [forward difference operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_difference_operator). Then, provided that *f* obeys certain summability conditions, then it may be represented in terms of these polynomials as

$$f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty p_n(z) \mathcal{L}_n(f).$$

The conditions for summability (that is, convergence) for this sequence is a fairly complex topic; in general, one may say that a necessary condition is that the analytic function be of less than [exponential type](wikipedia://en/4081509). Summability conditions are discussed in detail in Boas & Buck.

## Generating function {#Generating_function}

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for the general difference polynomials is given by

$$e^{zt}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty p_n(z) \left[\left(e^t-1\right)e^{\beta t}\right]^n.$$

This generating function can be brought into the form of the [generalized Appell representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Appell_representation)

$$K(z,w) = A(w)\Psi(zg(w)) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty p_n(z) w^n$$

by setting $A(w)=1$, $\Psi(x)=e^x$, $g(w)=t$ and $w=(e^t-1)e^{\beta t}$.
