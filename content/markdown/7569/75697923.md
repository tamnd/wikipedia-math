# Rob Reitzen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rob Reitzen"}

**Rob Reitzen** is an American mathematician and professional blackjack player.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Reitzen studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and probabilities at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles).[^o4] He was initially interested in poker, but shifted his focus to [blackjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackjack) after reading Lawrence Revere's *Playing Blackjack as a Business*.[^o4]

In the early 1980s, Reitzen worked with a colleague who later became influential in [horse betting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betting_on_horse_racing) technology in [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong).[^o4] They used emerging computer technology to develop advanced blackjack techniques, including [card counting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Card_counting) systems, [shuffle tracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuffle_track), and improved methods for memorizing card sequences.[^o4]

In the early 1990s, Reitzen co-founded CORE, a company that provided financial and operational support to Native American casinos, including supplying both the bankroll and dealers for blackjack games.[^o5]

Reitzen also specialized in Texas hold'em poker and developed a mathematical strategy that led to the formation of a successful team under his guidance, which achieved notable success on the Full Tilt Poker platform.[^o5] He later developed another mathematical technique with John Wayne and Darrell Miers that was offered as a service to casinos, as well as to stock and futures markets.[^o6]

In 1997, Reitzen was featured in *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_%28magazine%29)* magazine in an article titled "Fleecing Las Vegas", which described his use of a technique known as "The Hammer" at [Caesars Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Palace).[^o7][^o5] The method combined card counting, shuffle tracking, ace location, and card sequence memorization, and reportedly resulted in winnings of \$500,000.[^o7]

In 2019, Reitzen was inducted into the [Blackjack Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackjack_Hall_of_Fame).[^o5]

Reitzen is the founder of Random Order Inc and a co-founder of StyleScan, a B2B software specializing in [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence).[^o8][^o9][^o4]
