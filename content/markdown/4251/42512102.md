# James Maynard (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Maynard"}

**James Alexander Maynard** (born 10 June 1987) is an English mathematician working in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and in particular the theory of prime numbers.[^o19] In 2017, he was appointed research professor at Oxford.[^o3] Maynard is a fellow[^o4] of [St John's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Oxford). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2022[^o5] and the [New Horizons in Mathematics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Horizons_in_Mathematics_Prize) in 2023.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born on 10 June 1987 in Chelmsford, England,[^o19][^o7] Maynard attended [King Edward VI Grammar School, Chelmsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_Grammar_School,_Chelmsford). After completing his bachelor's and master's degrees at [Queens' College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens'_College,_Cambridge), in 2009, Maynard obtained his DPhil degree from [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford), in 2013 under the supervision of [Roger Heath-Brown](wikipedia://en/2984550).[^o19] He then became a fellow by examination at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford).[^o9]

## Career {#Career}

For the 2013–2014 year, Maynard was a CRM-ISM postdoctoral researcher at the [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal).[^o11]

In November 2013, Maynard gave a different [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of [Yitang Zhang](wikipedia://en/39441844)'s theorem[^o12] that there are bounded gaps between [primes](wikipedia://en/23666), and resolved a longstanding [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) by showing that for any $m$ there are infinitely many intervals of bounded length containing $m$ prime numbers.[^o13] This work can be seen as progress on the Hardy–Littlewood $m$-tuples conjecture as it establishes that "a positive proportion of admissible $m$-tuples satisfy the prime $m$-tuples conjecture for every $m$."[^o14] Maynard's approach yielded the [upper bound](wikipedia://en/42693), with $p_n$ denoting the $n$-th prime number,

$$\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(p_{n+1}-p_n\right)\leq 600,$$

which improved significantly upon the best existing bounds due to the [Polymath8](wikipedia://en/34858344#Polymath8) project.[^o15] (In other words, he showed that there are infinitely many prime gaps with size of at most 600.) Subsequently, Polymath8b was created,[^o16] whose collaborative efforts have reduced the gap size to 246, according to an announcement on 14 April 2014 by the [Polymath project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath_project) wiki.[^o15] Further, assuming the [Elliott–Halberstam conjecture](wikipedia://en/1898427) and, separately, its generalised form, the Polymath project wiki states that the gap size has been reduced to 12 and 6, respectively.[^o15]

In August 2014, Maynard (independently of [Ford](wikipedia://en/55700462), [Green](wikipedia://en/1927952), [Konyagin](wikipedia://en/29007447) and [Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498)) resolved a [longstanding conjecture](wikipedia://en/5075259#Lower_bounds) of [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) on large gaps between primes, and received the largest Erdős prize (\$10,000) ever offered.[^o17][^o18]

In 2014, he was awarded the [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize).[^o19][^o20] In 2015, he was awarded a [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913)[^o21] and in 2016 an [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize).[^o22]

In 2016, he showed that, for any given decimal digit, there are infinitely many prime numbers that do not have that digit in their decimal expansion.[^o23][^o24]

In 2019, together with [Dimitris Koukoulopoulos](wikipedia://en/69797343), he proved the [Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duffin–Schaeffer_conjecture).[^o25][^o26]

In 2020, in joint work with [Thomas Bloom](wikipedia://en/71404791), he improved the best-known bound for [square-difference-free sets](wikipedia://en/52290066), showing that a set $A \subset \{1,\dots,N\}$ with no square difference has size at most $\frac{N}{(\log N)^{c\log \log\log N}}$ for some $c > 0$.[^o27][^o28]

Maynard was awarded the Fields Medal 2022 for "contributions to analytic number theory, which have led to major advances in the understanding of the structure of prime numbers and in [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558)".[^o29]

Maynard was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 2023.[^o30]

In July 2026, Maynard was appointed the Regius Professor of Mathematics, and will succeed the previous holder of this position, [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027), on 1 October 2026, and will become a professorial fellow of [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford).[^o31]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Maynard's wife is Eleanor Grant, a medical doctor. They have a son.[^o5][^o33]
