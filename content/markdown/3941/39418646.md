# Simplicial group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more precisely, in the theory of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784), a **simplicial group** is a [simplicial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_object) in the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092). Similarly, a **simplicial abelian group** is a simplicial object in the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748). A simplicial group is a [Kan complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan_complex) (in particular, its [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) make sense). The [Dold–Kan correspondence](wikipedia://en/39431504) says that a simplicial abelian group may be identified with a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404). In fact it can be shown that any simplicial abelian group $A$ is non-canonically [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to a product of [Eilenberg–MacLane spaces](wikipedia://en/1073230), $\prod_{i\geq 0} K(\pi_iA,i).$[^o2]

:::

A [commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid) in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of simplicial abelian groups is a [simplicial commutative ring](wikipedia://en/40782000).

(Eckmann 1945) discusses a simplicial analogue of the fact that a [cohomology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_class) on a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) has a unique [harmonic representative](wikipedia://en/55762) and deduces [Kirchhoff's circuit laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchhoff's_circuit_laws) from these observations.
