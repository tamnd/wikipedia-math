# Saviour Montebello

*Image: Crypt at St Paul's Valletta.jpg.* Burial place of Saviour Montebello (1762–1809) beneath the parish church of St Paul Shipwreck in Valletta

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Saviour Montebello"}

**Saviour Montebello** (9 June 1762 – 18 April 1809) was a Maltese [Doctor of Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Theology), a professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at the [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta), and a [Parish priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_priest) of [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla).[^o3] After [Napoleon](wikipedia://en/69880) took over the [Maltese islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_islands) in 1798, Montebello took an active part in the [resistance of the Maltese against the French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_%281798–1800%29) around his home-town at [Żejtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Żejtun). Afterwards, when the French Napoleonic forces had been ousted, and the British set up a provisional government in [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta), he was the first to be appointed to the Chair of Philosophy at the University of Malta. He held the office for almost four years, mainly teaching [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics). He relinquished the Chair because he was appointed parish priest of [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla), the last to hold the office under this title because subsequently the parish was promoted to a [Collegiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) led by [Archpriests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archpriests). He held the office of parish priest for only five years due to his premature death at age 46.[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Birth and formation {#Birth_and_formation}

Saviour Montebello,[^o7] son of Joseph (1730[^o8]–1768[^o9]) and Catherine ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Née); 1731[^o10]–?[^o11]), was born at [Żejtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Żejtun), [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta), on 9 June 1762.[^o12] His parents had married at Żejtun parish church of [St. Catherine of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catherine_of_Alexandria) on 26 September 1752.[^o13] and Saviour was their fourth child out of six.[^o14] He was baptised on the same day of his birth at Żejtun's parish church by the deputy [parish priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_priest), Joseph Saliba, and given the names Saviour, Francis Xavier and Anthony. His godparents were Joseph Buttigieg, son of Luke, and Rose, wife of Francis Bugeja, both from the Żejtun parish.[^o15]

At the age of 19, in 1781, Montebello entered the bishop's seminary at [Floriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floriana) to study for the priesthood.[^o16] As was required at the time, his family donated him with a patrimony which could pay for his seminary expenses. This consisted of four properties: two partitions of grasslands around *[Tas-Silġ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tas-Silġ)* (limits of Żejtun), and two edifices at Żejtun's village centre. The patrimony was temporarily entrusted to the administration of Michael Pulis,[^o17] the husband of Montebello's older sister, Mary.[^o18]

Montebello was ordained a priest in 1787, six years after entering the seminary,[^o19] most probably by the then [Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_%28Catholic_Church%29) of [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta), [Carmelo Scicluna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmelo_Scicluna) (bishop between 1875 and 1888).[^o20] Thereafter, Montebello seems to have been stably assigned to pastoral work within and around the parish of [Żejtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Żejtun). Such work does not appear to have hindered him from studying for his [Doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in [Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology). This seems to have been done in [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta) (and not abroad). In this case his studies would probably have been pursued, as was normally done, at the *[Collegium Melitense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Melitense)* in [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta). By 1797, that is ten years after his [ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordination), he had already acquired his [S.T.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.T.D.)[^o21]

### Family relations {#Family_relations}

Montebello was well connected through his family. His cousin, Giuseppe Montebello (1713[^o23]–1833[^o24]), from [Tarxien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarxien), was the son of Montebello's uncle, Peter,[^o25] and a well-heeled merchant.[^o26] He was *Capo di Casal Tarxien* (Head of Tarxien),[^o27] elected by the gentry there[^o28] to represent them in all administrative matters.[^o29] During the French presence in [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta) (1798), he was chosen to be part of the committee administering one of the twelve municipalities created by [Napoleon](wikipedia://en/69880), that which included Tarxien, Żejtun, [Żabbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Żabbar) and [Għaxaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Għaxaq).[^o30] Only three months later, however, Montebello was very much involved in the organised [resistance of the Maltese against the French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_%281798–1800%29) between 1798 and 1800. As head of the local provisional government at Tarxien,[^o31] he was appointed *capo battaglione* (battalion leader) with operational responsibilities around that locality.[^o32] Together with others, he contributed financially to the two-year war effort,[^o33] and also donated (on 24 December 1798) a considerable part of his wealth to procure wheat and other supplies from [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily) to ease the food shortage that ensued from the revolt.[^o34] When the British provisionally took over the government of the [Maltese islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_islands) instead of the French Republican Commission, Giuseppe was elected by the gentry at Tarxien (18 February 1799) to be head of their locality, and thus part of the National Congress created by the representative of the British provisional government, [Alexander Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Ball).[^o35] Later, Giuseppe was appointed *Logotenente di Governo* (Government Lieutenant) of Tarxien.[^o36] Afterwards, when the British officially became lords of the land, and partitioned the [Maltese islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_islands) into six districts, Giuseppe was appointed Deputy Lieutenant of the district comprising Tarxien, [Paola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paola,_Malta), Żejtun, Żabbar, [Gudja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudja) and [Luqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luqa) (28 June 1815).[^o37] Giuseppe's house at Tarxien, built by government funds as recompense for his share in the revolt,[^o38] still stands unto this day, and is a tourist attraction.[^o39]

Another influential close relative, Giuseppe Pulis Montebello (1783–1855), was Montebello's nephew.[^o40] He was from Żejtun and resided in [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta) together with his Italian wife Carlotta Bertelli.[^o41] Giuseppe lived for many years in [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno), Italy, where he married.[^o42] They had at least five children.[^o43] He and his wife settled in [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta) for just a decade or so, and returned to [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno) a few months before Giuseppe's death in 1855.[^o44] He was a well-off and self-sufficient merchant.[^o45] He too was elected to the National Assembly (1849–54).[^o46] He was very popular with voters, obtaining the largest number of popular votes.[^o47] Three publications of his are known. In 1824 he published *Chtieb il Kari Yau Dahla'al ilsien Malti* (A Book of Maltese Reading or An Introduction to the Maltese Language);[^o48] in 1826 *Trattat Fuq l'Obblighi Tal-Bniedem* (A Treatise on the Duties of Man);[^o49] and in 1847 *Rendiconto pratico del Campo Esperimentale dell'Est* (A practical Statement on the Experimental Camp in the East).[^o50] The first two are credited to be the first books written entirely in the [Maltese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_language).[^o51]

### Resistance {#Resistance}

Together with many others, including his uncle Giuseppe, between 1798 and 1800 Montebello was part of the [resistance of the Maltese against the French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_%281798–1800%29).[^o53] He was stationed at Żejtun (at *Tal-Borg*),[^o54] and gave his share within a team of resistance fighters. The team's job was to intercept any manoeuvring by or to the French troops blockaded in [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta) through the road leading to [Cottonera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottonera) from the east, and to organise and manage supplies around that area. Despite some skirmishes―which had even left some casualties on both sides[^o55]―Montebello's involvement does not seem to have been military, as some suggested,[^o56] but rather of a logistical nature. In fact, together with two others (Luigi Agius, a [doctor of canon and civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_canon_and_civil_law), and [Vincenzo Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Borg), later a [Companion of the Order of St. Michael and St. George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_St._Michael_and_St._George)) Montebello served as *consultore legale* (legal adviser) under the command of Giuseppe Abela.[^o57] The team's General Headquarters was situated at San Giuseppe ([Hamrun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamrun)), under the general command of [Francesco Saverio Caruana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Saverio_Caruana).[^o58] Caruana was a [Monsignor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsignor)[^o59] who had studied [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) under [Michael Xerri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dun_Mikiel_Xerri).[^o60] He was professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_%28Catholic_Church%29)'s [seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) at [Mdina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mdina),[^o61] and later appointed [Bishop of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_%28Catholic_Church%29) (1831–47).[^o62]

### Professor of Philosophy {#Professor_of_Philosophy}

Following the French [capitulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta#Knights_of_Malta_and_Napoleon) on 5 September 1800,[^o64] Montebello was appointed professor of the chair of philosophy at the [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta). The chair had been suspended by [Napoleon](wikipedia://en/69880) on 18 June 1798,[^o65] but then reinstated by the British by [Alexander Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Ball). On 5 November 1800,[^o66] Montebello was chosen to be professor of [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics),[^o67] with an [honorarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorarium) of 200 [scudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_scudo) *per annum*.[^o68] He was chosen for this office by the newly appointed [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29), Saverio Caruana,[^o69] Montebello's former 'priest-in-arms'. This was done with the approval of [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta)'s provisional government (which included Montebello's cousin, Giuseppe).[^o70]

On the day of his appointment, 5 November,[^o71] together with the other newly appointed professors, Montebello called on the [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) at his residence in [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta) to thank him for their appointments. Caruana received them most kindly and, as was formerly the practice during the time of the [Knights Hospitallers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitallers),[^o72] they all proceeded to the palace of the [bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_%28Catholic_Church%29), [Vincenzo Labini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Labini),[^o73] in [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta) who then administered to them the oath of office. On the following day, 6 November,[^o74] professors, students and members of the better educated classes assembled in the main hall of the [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta) in [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta), where the [Augustinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustinians) Vincenzo Thei (the professor of [moral theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_theology)), delivered a solemn oration for the success of the new studies. The British governor, [Alexander Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Ball), was also present for the occasion. Lectures started a week later, on 13 November, after the solemn intonation of the hymn *[Veni Creator Spiritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veni_Creator_Spiritus)* in the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta) church, also at [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta). Then each professor went to his lecture room and gave a public lecture.

Montebello's academic remit at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta) was of two hours every week.[^o75] A welcome development occurred on 13 March 1802, when the British governor, [Alexander Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Ball), re-established the practice of conferring degrees, thus giving more weight to the academic institution. In the following month the first ceremony of the conferrement of degrees under British rule was held.[^o76] This must have brought on, a few months later, on 5 May 1802, the increase in salary of the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta) professors, including Montebello's, from two to three hundred [scudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_scudo) *per annum* by the British Civil Commissioner, Charles Cameron.[^o77] Following these developments, no other events of note seem to have occurred at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta) up till 1813,[^o78] well after Montebello's time there.

### Parish Priest {#Parish_Priest}

In 1804, Montebello relinquished the chair of philosophy when he was appointed by [Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_%28Catholic_Church%29) Vincenzo Labini[^o80] as parish priest of *Civitatis Cospiquæ* ([Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla)).[^o81] On 3 October of that year, he was succeeded at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta) by the professor of [humanistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanistic) [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature) and secretary of the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta), Peter Mallia, a [doctor of canon and civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_canon_and_civil_law).[^o82]

Montebello became vicar (or *Parochus prælectus*)[^o83] of [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla) on 23 June 1804,[^o84] and immediately took up residence there[^o85] together with his sister Vincenza. He took official possession of the parish (as *Parochus et Rector*)[^o86] on the following 26 July.[^o87] He was the sixteenth parish priest of [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla)[^o88] since the [parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish)'s inception on 15 September 1586.[^o89] However, as it happened, he was also its last parish priest, since in 1822, during the tenure of Montebello's successor as parish priest, Giuseppe Bezzina, the [parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish) became a [Collegiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) by [decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree#Catholic_Church) of [Pope Pius VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII).[^o90]

Apart from the usual pastoral duties as parish priest, at [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla), Montebello founded, in May 1805, the [Confraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confraternity) of [St. Agatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Agatha)[^o91] and a few months later, on 15 September 1805, the [Confraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confraternity) of [St. Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Michael_%28Roman_Catholic%29).[^o92]

### Death {#Death}

Montebello remained parish priest for five years.[^o94] He died suddenly and prematurely at his residence in [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla)[^o95] on 18 April 1809,[^o96] aged 46,[^o97] Though the last baptism he administered was just two days before his death, on the April 16,[^o98] he was apparently too ill to officiate at a marriage celebrated on April 11.[^o99] Oddly enough, his funeral was not held at [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla), as would have been normal practice, but at the parish church of his birthplace, Żejtun, with the participation of the [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy#Catholicism) serving at [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla).[^o100] Stranger still, for some reason yet unknown he was not buried at Żejtun but was transported again, this time to [Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta), and buried in the crypt of the [Collegiate church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) of [St. Paul's Shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_Parish_Church_of_St_Paul's_Shipwreck),[^o101] in the part reserved for priests.[^o102] In gratitude towards the [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy#Catholicism) of [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla), a few days after the funeral, on the 23 April, his sister, Vincenza Montebello, donated 50 [scudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_scudo) to the [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla) collagate *per i poveri di Cospicua* (for the poor of [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla)).[^o103] Several masses were sung by the [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla) [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy#Catholicism) for his repose on the anniversary of his death in the succeeding five years (until 1814).[^o104] More masses were sung for his repose by the clergy at Żejtun almost fifty years later, in 1855, and in succeeding years, by bequest of Giuseppe Pulis Montebello.[^o105]

## Works {#Works}

There is as yet no indication that Montebello ever published any works, philosophical or otherwise. In all probability he did not.

His class notes on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), subjects which he taught for four years at the [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta), do not seem to have survived, not even in indirect form (by his students). At least, nothing is yet known about their whereabouts. Having said this, however, it might not be irrelevant to point out that the hundreds of manuscripts held at the archive of the [Collegiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) of [Bormla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bormla)―one likely place where Montebello's writings might be held―are still not catalogued. This, as yet, makes it impossible to know for sure whether anything of his philosophical notes survived. To date, it is not known whether Montebello drew up any [last will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_will), as was usually done.[^o107]
