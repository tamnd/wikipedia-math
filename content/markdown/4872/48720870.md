# Quasi-exact solvability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-exact solvability"}

A [linear differential operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_differential_operator) *L* is called **quasi-exactly-solvable** (**QES**) if it has a [finite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional) [invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826) of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) $\{\mathcal{V}\}_n$ such that $L: \{\mathcal{V}\}_n \rightarrow \{\mathcal{V}\}_n,$ where *n* is a [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) of $\{\mathcal{V}\}_n$. There are two important cases:

:::

1. $\{\mathcal{V}\}_n$ is the [space](wikipedia://en/5308894) of [multivariate polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_polynomial) of degree not higher than some [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) number; and
1. $\{\mathcal{V}\}_n$ is a subspace of a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). Sometimes, the [functional space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_space) $\{\mathcal{V}\}_n$ is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_%28algebra%29) to the finite-dimensional [representation space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_space) of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) *g* of [first-order differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_differential_operator). In this case, the [operator](wikipedia://en/22213) *L* is called a *g*-Lie-algebraic Quasi-Exactly-Solvable operator. Usually, one can indicate basis where *L* has block-triangular form. If the operator *L* is of the [second order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-order_differential_equation) and has the form of the [Schrödinger operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_operator), it is called a Quasi-Exactly-Solvable Schrödinger operator.

The most studied cases are [one-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-dimensional) $sl(2)$-Lie-algebraic quasi-exactly-solvable (Schrödinger) operators. The best known example is the sextic QES anharmonic oscillator with the Hamiltonian

$$\{\mathcal{H}\} = -\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + a^2 x^6 + 2abx^4 + [b^2 - (4 n + 3 + 2p) a] x^2, \ a \geq 0\ ,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\ ,\ p=\{0,1\},$$

where (*n+1*) [eigenstates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenstate) of positive (negative) parity can be found [algebraically](wikipedia://en/18716923). Their [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) are of the form

$$\Psi (x)\ =\ x^p P_n(x^2) e^{-\frac{a x^4}{4} - \frac{b x^2}{2} } \ ,$$

where $P_n(x^2)$ is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of degree *n* and (energies) [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) are roots of an [algebraic equation](wikipedia://en/404001) of degree (*n+1*). In general, twelve families of one-dimensional QES problems are known, two of them characterized by elliptic potentials.
