# Inclusion (logic)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inclusion"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **inclusion** is the concept that all the contents of one object are also contained within a second object.[^o2]

:::

For example, if *m* and *n* are two [logical matrices](wikipedia://en/4109196), then

$$m \subset n \quad \text{when} \quad \forall i,j \quad m_{ij} = 1 \implies n_{ij} = 1 .$$

The modern symbol for inclusion first appears in [Gergonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Diaz_Gergonne) (1816), who defines it as one idea 'containing' or being 'contained' by another, using the backward letter 'C' to express this. [Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) articulated this clearly in 1870, arguing also that inclusion was a wider concept than equality, and hence a logically simpler one.[^o3] [Schröder](wikipedia://en/448015) (also [Frege](wikipedia://en/48416)) calls the same concept 'subordination'.[^o4]
