# Caustic (mathematics)

*Image: Circle\_caustic.png.* Reflective caustic generated from a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) and parallel rays. On one side, each point is contained in three light rays; on the other side, each point is contained in one light ray.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Caustic"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **caustic** is the [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) of [rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29#Ray) either [reflected](wikipedia://en/295829) or [refracted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refracted) by a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). It is related to the concept of [caustics](wikipedia://en/3498698) in [geometric optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_optics). The ray's source may be a point (called the radiant) or parallel rays from a point at infinity, in which case a direction vector of the rays must be specified.

:::

More generally, especially as applied to [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) and [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763), a caustic is the [critical value set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_value_%28critical_point%29) of a [Lagrangian mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_mapping) (*π* ○ *i*) : *L* ↪ *M* ↠ *B*; where *i* : *L* ↪ *M* is a [Lagrangian immersion](wikipedia://en/28356#Lagrangian_mapping) of a [Lagrangian submanifold](wikipedia://en/28356#Lagrangian_and_other_submanifolds) *L* into a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) *M*, and *π* : *M* ↠ *B* is a [Lagrangian fibration](wikipedia://en/28356#Lagrangian_fibration) of the symplectic manifold *M*. The caustic is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the Lagrangian [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826)'s [base space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_space) *B*.[^o2]

## Explanation {#Explanation}

*Image: Caustics.gif.* The rays refracted by a non-flat surface form caustics where many of them cross.

Concentration of light, especially [sunlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunlight), can burn. The word *caustic*, in fact, comes from the Greek καυστός, burnt, via the Latin *causticus*, burning.

A common situation where caustics are visible is when light shines on a drinking glass. The glass casts a shadow, but also produces a curved region of bright light. In ideal circumstances (including perfectly parallel rays, as if from a point source at infinity), a [nephroid](wikipedia://en/843192)-shaped patch of light can be produced.[^o4][^o5] Rippling caustics are commonly formed when light shines through waves on a body of water.

Another familiar caustic is the [rainbow](wikipedia://en/3871014).[^o6][^o7] Scattering of light by raindrops causes different [wavelengths](wikipedia://en/33125) of light to be refracted into arcs of differing radius, producing the bow.

## Catacaustic {#Catacaustic}

A **catacaustic** is the reflective case.

With a radiant, it is the [evolute](wikipedia://en/842387) of the [orthotomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthotomic) of the radiant.

The planar, parallel-source-rays case: suppose the direction vector is $(a,b)$ and the mirror curve is parametrised as $(u(t),v(t))$. The normal vector at a point is $(-v'(t),u'(t))$; the reflection of the direction vector is (normal needs special normalization)

$$2\mbox{proj}_nd-d=\frac{2n}{\sqrt{n\cdot n}}\frac{n\cdot d}{\sqrt{n\cdot n}}-d=2n\frac{n\cdot d}{n\cdot n}-d=\frac{ (av'^2-2bu'v'-au'^2,bu'^2-2au'v'-bv'^2) }{v'^2+u'^2}$$

Having components of found reflected vector treat it as a tangent

$$(x-u)(bu'^2-2au'v'-bv'^2)=(y-v)(av'^2-2bu'v'-au'^2).$$

Using the simplest [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) form

$$F(x,y,t)=(x-u)(bu'^2-2au'v'-bv'^2)-(y-v)(av'^2-2bu'v'-au'^2)$$

$$=x(bu'^2-2au'v'-bv'^2) -y(av'^2-2bu'v'-au'^2) +b(uv'^2-uu'^2-2vu'v') +a(-vu'^2+vv'^2+2uu'v')$$

$$F_t(x,y,t)=2x(bu'u''-a(u'v''+u''v')-bv'v'') -2y(av'v''-b(u''v'+u'v'')-au'u'')$$

$$+b( u'v'^2 +2uv'v'' -u'^3 -2uu'u'' -2u'v'^2 -2u''vv' -2u'vv'') +a(-v'u'^2 -2vu'u'' +v'^3 +2vv'v'' +2v'u'^2 +2v''uu' +2v'uu'')$$

which may be unaesthetic, but $F=F_t=0$ gives a [linear system](wikipedia://en/722503) in $(x,y)$ and so it is elementary to obtain a parametrisation of the catacaustic. [Cramer's rule](wikipedia://en/166008) would serve.

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

Let the direction vector be (0,1) and the mirror be $(t,t^2).$ Then

$u'=1$ $u''=0$ $v'=2t$ $v''=2$ $a=0$ $b=1$

$$F(x,y,t)=(x-t)(1-4t^2)+4t(y-t^2)=x(1-4t^2)+4ty-t$$

$$F_t(x,y,t)=-8tx+4y-1$$

and $F=F_t=0$ has solution $(0,1/4)$; *i.e.*, light entering a [parabolic](wikipedia://en/23231) mirror parallel to its axis is reflected through the focus.

:::
