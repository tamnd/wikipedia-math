# Arbelos

*Image: Arbelos.svg.* An arbelos (grey region)

*Image: Arbelos sculpture Netherlands 1.jpg.* Arbelos sculpture in [Kaatsheuvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaatsheuvel), Netherlands

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arbelos"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **arbelos** is a plane region bounded by three [semicircles](wikipedia://en/617831) with three apexes such that each corner of each semicircle is shared with one of the others (connected), all on the same side of a [straight line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) (the *baseline*) that contains their [diameters](wikipedia://en/8007).[^o16]

:::

The earliest known reference to this figure is in [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844)'s *[Book of Lemmas](wikipedia://en/17941791)*, where some of its mathematical properties are stated as Propositions 4 through 8.[^o17] The word *arbelos* is Greek for 'shoemaker's knife'. The figure is closely related to the [Pappus chain](wikipedia://en/16856774).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Two of the semicircles are necessarily concave, with arbitrary diameters $a$ and $b$; the third semicircle is [convex](wikipedia://en/29747989), with diameter $a+b.$[^o16] Let the diameters of the smaller semicircles be $\overline{BA}$ and $\overline{AC}$; then the diameter of the larger semircle is $\overline{BC}$.

*Image: Arbelos diagram with points marked.svg.* Some special points on the arbelos.

### Area {#Area}

Let $H$ be the intersection of the larger semicircle with the line perpendicular to $BC$ at $A$. Then the [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_%28geometry%29) of the arbelos is equal to the area of a circle with diameter $\overline{HA}$.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof**: For the proof, reflect the arbelos over the line through the points $B$ and $C$, and observe that twice the area of the arbelos is what remains when the areas of the two smaller circles (with diameters $\overline{BA}$, $\overline{AC}$) are subtracted from the area of the large circle (with diameter $\overline{BC}$). Since the area of a circle is proportional to the square of the diameter ([Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [Elements](wikipedia://en/244107), Book XII, Proposition 2; we do not need to know that the [constant of proportionality](wikipedia://en/81863) is $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$), the problem reduces to showing that $2|AH|^2 = |BC|^2 - |AC|^2 - |BA|^2$. The length $\lvert BC\rvert$ equals the sum of the lengths $\lvert BA\rvert$ and $\lvert AC\rvert$, so this equation simplifies algebraically to the statement that $|AH|^2 = |BA||AC|$. Thus the claim is that the length of the segment $\overline{AH}$ is the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of the lengths of the segments $\overline{BA}$ and $\overline{AC}$. Now (see Figure) the triangle $BHC$, being inscribed in the semicircle, has a right angle at the point $H$ (Euclid, Book III, Proposition 31), and consequently $\lvert HA\rvert$ is indeed a "mean proportional" between $\lvert BA\rvert$ and $\lvert AC\rvert$ (Euclid, Book VI, Proposition 8, Porism). This proof approximates the ancient Greek argument; [Harold P. Boas](wikipedia://en/11754242) cites a paper of [Roger B. Nelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_B._Nelsen)[^o6] who implemented the idea as the following [proof without words](wikipedia://en/18045517).[^o7]

*Image: Arbelos proof2.svg.*

:::

### Rectangle {#Rectangle}

Let $D$ and $E$ be the points where the segments $\overline{BH}$ and $\overline{CH}$ intersect the semicircles $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. The [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) $ADHE$ is actually a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048).

*Proof*: $\angle BDA$, $\angle BHC$, and $\angle AEC$ are right angles because they are inscribed in semicircles (by [Thales's theorem](wikipedia://en/267471)). The quadrilateral $ADHE$ therefore has three right angles, so it is a rectangle. *Q.E.D.*

### Tangents {#Tangents}

The line $DE$ is tangent to semicircle $BA$ at $D$ and semicircle $AC$ at $E$.

*Proof*: Since $ADHE$ is a rectangle, the diagonals $AH$ and $DE$ have equal length and bisect each other at their intersection $O$. Therefore, $|OD| = |OA| = |OE|$. Also, since $\overline{OA}$ is perpendicular to the diameters $\overline{BA}$ and $\overline{AC}$, $\overline{OA}$ is tangent to both semicircles at the point $A$. Finally, because the two tangents to a circle from any given exterior point have equal length, it follows that the other tangents from $O$ to semicircles $BA$ and $AC$ are $\overline{OD}$ and $\overline{OE}$ respectively.

### Archimedes' circles {#Archimedes'_circles}

The altitude $AH$ divides the arbelos into two regions, each bounded by a semicircle, a straight line segment, and an arc of the outer semicircle. The circles [inscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed_circle) in each of these regions, known as the [Archimedes' circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes'_circles) of the arbelos, have the same size.

:::

## Variations and generalisations {#Variations_and_generalisations}

*Image: F-belos.svg.* example of an *f*-belos

The [parbelos](wikipedia://en/64593287) is a figure similar to the arbelos, that uses [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) segments instead of half circles. A generalisation comprising both arbelos and parbelos is the *f*-belos, which uses a certain type of similar differentiable functions.[^o12]

In the [Poincaré half-plane model](wikipedia://en/461942) of the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane), an arbelos models an [ideal triangle](wikipedia://en/5532715).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Image: Arbelos Shoemakers Knife.jpg.* The type of shoemaker's knife that gave its name to the figure

The name *arbelos* comes from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ἡ ἄρβηλος (*he árbēlos*) or ἄρβυλος *árbylos*, meaning "shoemaker's knife", a knife used by [cobblers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoemaking) from antiquity to the current day, whose blade is said to resemble the geometric figure.
