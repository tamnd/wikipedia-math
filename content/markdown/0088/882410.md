# Untouchable number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Untouchable number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **untouchable number** is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) that cannot be expressed as the [sum](wikipedia://en/246160) of all the [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisor) of any positive integer. That is, these numbers are not in the image of the [aliquot sum](wikipedia://en/12374274) function. Their study goes back at least to [Abu Mansur al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mansur_al-Baghdadi) (circa 1000 AD), who observed that both 2 and 5 are untouchable.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Aliquot sums and untouchable numbers.pdf.* If we draw an arrow pointing from each positive integer to the sum of all its proper divisors, there will be no arrow pointing to untouchable numbers like 2 and 5.

- The number 4 is not untouchable, as it is equal to the sum of the proper divisors of 9: 1 + 3 = 4.
- The number 5 is untouchable, as it is not the sum of the proper divisors of any positive integer: 5 = 1 + 4 is the only way to write 5 as the sum of distinct positive integers including 1, but if 4 divides a number, 2 does also, so 1 + 4 cannot be the sum of all of any number's proper divisors (since the list of factors would have to contain both 4 and 2).
- The number 6 is not untouchable, as it is equal to the sum of the proper divisors of 6 itself: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6.

## Properties {#Properties}

::: {#o7 .problem cue=unsolved}

Are there any odd untouchable numbers other than 5?

:::

The number 5 is believed to be the only odd untouchable number, but this has not been proven. It would follow from a slightly stronger version of the [Goldbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldbach_conjecture), since the sum of the proper divisors of *pq* (with *p*, *q* distinct primes) is 1 + *p* + *q*. Thus, if a number *n* can be written as a sum of two distinct primes, then *n* + 1 is not an untouchable number. It is expected that every even number larger than 6 is a sum of two distinct primes, so probably no odd number larger than 7 is an untouchable number, and $1=\sigma(2)-2$, $3=\sigma(4)-4$, $7=\sigma(8)-8$, so only 5 can be an odd untouchable number.[^o8] Thus it appears that besides 2 and 5, all untouchable numbers are [composite numbers](wikipedia://en/82289) (since except 2, all even numbers are composite). No [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670) is untouchable, since, at the very least, it can be expressed as the sum of its own proper [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492). Similarly, none of the [amicable numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amicable_number) or [sociable numbers](wikipedia://en/332307) are untouchable. Also, none of the [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number) are untouchable, since *M*<sub>*n*</sub> = 2<sup>*n*</sup> − 1 is equal to the sum of the proper divisors of 2<sup>*n*</sup>.

No untouchable number is 1 more than a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), since if *p* is prime, then the sum of the proper divisors of *p*<sup>2</sup> is *p* + 1. Also, no untouchable number is 3 more than a prime number, except 5, since if *p* is an odd prime then the sum of the proper divisors of 2*p* is *p* + 3.

:::

## Infinitude {#Infinitude}

There are infinitely many untouchable numbers, a fact that was proven by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284).[^o10] According to Chen and Zhao, their [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) is at least d > 0.06.[^o11]
