# Gerstenhaber algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerstenhaber algebra"}

*Image: Gerstenhaber.jpg.* [Murray Gerstenhaber](wikipedia://en/30237327) at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) in 2010

In mathematics and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), a **Gerstenhaber algebra** (sometimes called an **antibracket algebra** or **braid algebra**) is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) discovered by [Murray Gerstenhaber](wikipedia://en/30237327) (1963) that combines the structures of a [supercommutative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercommutative_ring) and a [graded Lie superalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_Lie_superalgebra). It is used in the [Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism](wikipedia://en/672259). It appears also in the generalization of Hamiltonian formalism known as the [De Donder–Weyl theory](wikipedia://en/33872518) as the algebra of generalized [Poisson brackets](wikipedia://en/294340) defined on differential forms.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **Gerstenhaber algebra** is a graded-commutative algebra with a [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/17944) of degree −1 satisfying the [Poisson identity](wikipedia://en/345704). Everything is understood to satisfy the usual [superalgebra](wikipedia://en/469950) sign conventions. More precisely, the algebra has two products, one written as ordinary multiplication and one written as \[,\], and a **Z**-grading called **degree** (in theoretical physics sometimes called **ghost number**). The **degree** of an element *a* is denoted by |*a*|. These satisfy the identities

- (*ab*)*c* = *a*(*bc*) (The product is associative)
- *ab* = (−1)<sup>\|*a*||*b*|</sup>*ba* (The product is (super) commutative)
- \|*ab*| = |*a*| + |*b*| (The product has degree 0)
- \|\[*a*,*b*\]| = |*a*| + |*b*| − 1 (The Lie bracket has degree −1)
- \[*a*,*bc*\] = \[*a*,*b*\]*c* + (−1)<sup>(|*a*|−1)|*b*|</sup>*b*\[*a*,*c*\] (Poisson identity)
- \[*a*,*b*\] = −(−1)<sup>(|*a*|−1)(|*b*|−1)</sup> \[*b*,*a*\] (Antisymmetry of Lie bracket)
- \[*a*,\[*b*,*c*\]\] = \[\[*a*,*b*\],*c*\] + (−1)<sup>(|*a*|−1)(|*b*|−1)</sup>\[*b*,\[*a*,*c*\]\] (The Jacobi identity for the Lie bracket)

Gerstenhaber algebras differ from [Poisson superalgebras](wikipedia://en/882793) in that the Lie bracket has degree −1 rather than degree 0. The [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370) may also be expressed in a symmetrical form

$$(-1)^{(|a|-1)(|c|-1)}[a,[b,c]]+(-1)^{(|b|-1)(|a|-1)}[b,[c,a]]+(-1)^{(|c|-1)(|b|-1)}[c,[a,b]] = 0.\,$$

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Gerstenhaber showed that the [Hochschild cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochschild_cohomology) H<sup>\*</sup>(*A*,*A*) of an algebra *A* is a Gerstenhaber algebra.
- A [Batalin–Vilkovisky algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batalin–Vilkovisky_algebra) has an underlying Gerstenhaber algebra if one forgets its second order Δ operator.
- The [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is a Gerstenhaber algebra.
- The differential forms on a [Poisson manifold](wikipedia://en/882902) form a Gerstenhaber algebra.
- The [multivector fields](wikipedia://en/30067072) on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) form a Gerstenhaber algebra using the [Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket](wikipedia://en/11216402)
