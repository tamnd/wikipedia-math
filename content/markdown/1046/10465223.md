# L² cohomology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="L² cohomology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **L<sup>2</sup> cohomology** is a [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) for [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) non-compact [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* with [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric). It is defined in the same way as [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) except that one uses [square-integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-integrable) [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519). The notion of square-integrability makes sense because the [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) on *M* gives rise to a [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) on differential forms and a [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811).

:::

L<sup>2</sup> cohomology, which grew in part out of L<sup>2</sup> d-bar estimates from the 1960s, was studied cohomologically, independently by [Steven Zucker](wikipedia://en/6743885) (1978) and [Jeff Cheeger](wikipedia://en/7343761) (1979). It is closely related to [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology); indeed, the results in the preceding cited works can be expressed in terms of intersection cohomology.

Another such result is the **Zucker conjecture**, which states that for a [Hermitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_variety) [locally symmetric variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_symmetric_variety) the L<sup>2</sup> cohomology is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology) (with the [middle perversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_perversity)) of its [Baily–Borel compactification](wikipedia://en/16083822) (Zucker 1982). This was proved in different ways by [Eduard Looijenga](wikipedia://en/45224498) (1988) and by Leslie Saper and [Mark Stern](wikipedia://en/50511729) (1990).
