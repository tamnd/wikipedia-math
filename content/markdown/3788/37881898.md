# Ross's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ross's conjecture"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical theory of probability, **Ross's conjecture** gives a lower bound for the average waiting-time experienced by a customer when arrivals to the queue do not follow the simplest model for random arrivals. It was proposed by [Sheldon M. Ross](wikipedia://en/67376898) in 1978 and proved in 1981 by Tomasz Rolski.[^o5] Equality can be obtained in the bound; and the bound does not hold for finite buffer queues.[^o2]

:::

## Bound {#Bound}

Ross's conjecture is a bound for the mean delay in a queue where arrivals are governed by a [doubly stochastic Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_stochastic_Poisson_process)[^o4] or by a non-stationary [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process).[^o5][^o6] The conjecture states that the average amount of time that a customer spends waiting in a queue is greater than or equal to

$$\frac{\lambda \operatorname E (S^2)}{2 \{1-\lambda \operatorname E (S) \}}$$

where *S* is the service time and λ is the average arrival rate (in the limit as the length of the time period increases).[^o5]
