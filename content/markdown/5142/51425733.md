# Adian–Rabin theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adian–Rabin theorem"}

In the mathematical subject of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Adyan–Rabin theorem** is a result that states that most "reasonable" properties of [finitely presentable groups](wikipedia://en/99494) are algorithmically [undecidable](wikipedia://en/15631055). The theorem is due to [Sergei Adyan](wikipedia://en/5028401) (1955)[^o2][^o3] and, independently, [Michael O. Rabin](wikipedia://en/298404) (1958).[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Markov property (group theory)"}

## Markov property {#Markov_property}

A *Markov property* *P* of finitely presentable groups is one for which:

1. *P* is an abstract property, that is, *P* is preserved under [group isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397).
1. There exists a finitely presentable group $A_+$ with property *P*.
1. There exists a finitely presentable group $A_-$ that cannot be embedded as a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) in any finitely presentable group with property *P*.

For example, being a finite group is a Markov property: We can take $A_+$ to be the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) and we can take $A_-$ to be the infinite [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $\mathbb{Z}$.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Precise statement of the Adyan–Rabin theorem"}

## Precise statement of the Adyan–Rabin theorem {#Precise_statement_of_the_Adyan–Rabin_theorem}

In modern sources, the Adyan–Rabin theorem is usually stated as follows:[^o9][^o10][^o11]

Let *P* be a Markov property of finitely presentable groups. Then there does not exist an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/30403) that, given a finite presentation $G=\langle X \mid R\rangle$, decides whether or not the group $G$ defined by this presentation has property *P*.

The word 'algorithm' here is used in the sense of [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory). More formally, the conclusion of the Adyan–Rabin theorem means that set of all finite presentations

$$\langle x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots \mid R\rangle$$

(where $x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots$ is a fixed countably infinite alphabet, and $R$ is a finite set of relations in these generators and their inverses) defining groups with property *P*, is not a [recursive set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursive_set).

:::

## Historical notes {#Historical_notes}

The statement of the Adyan–Rabin theorem generalizes a similar earlier result for [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799) by [Andrey Markov, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Markov,_Jr.),[^o13][^o14] proved by analogous methods. It was also in the semigroup context that Markov introduced the above notion that group theorists came to call the *Markov property* of finitely presented groups. This Markov, a prominent Soviet logician, is not to be confused with his father, the famous Russian probabilist [Andrey Markov](wikipedia://en/915) after whom [Markov chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chains) and [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process) are named.

According to Don Collins,[^o18] the notion *Markov property*, as defined above, was introduced by [William Boone](wikipedia://en/34399699) in his *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)* review of Rabin's 1958 paper containing Rabin's proof of the Adyan–Rabin theorem.

## Idea of the proof {#Idea_of_the_proof}

In modern sources,[^o9][^o10] the proof of the Adyan–Rabin theorem proceeds by a reduction to the [Novikov–Boone theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novikov–Boone_theorem) via a clever use of [amalgamated products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamated_product) and [HNN extensions](wikipedia://en/2823319).

Let $P$ be a Markov property and let $A_+, A_-$ be as in the definition of the Markov property above. Let $G=\langle X \mid R\rangle$ be a finitely presented group with undecidable word problem, whose existence is provided by the [Novikov–Boone theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novikov–Boone_theorem).

The proof then produces a recursive procedure that, given a word $w$ in the generators $X\cup X^{-1}$ of $G$, outputs a finitely presented group $G_w$ such that if $w=_G 1$ then $G_w$ is isomorphic to $A_+$, and if $w\ne_G 1$ then $G_w$ contains $A_-$ as a subgroup. Thus $G_w$ has property $P$ if and only if $w=_G 1$. Since it is undecidable whether $w=_G 1$, it follows that it is undecidable whether a finitely presented group has property $P$.

::: {#o17 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The following properties of finitely presented groups are Markov and therefore are algorithmically undecidable by the Adyan–Rabin theorem:

1. Being the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059).
1. Being a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707).
1. Being an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974).
1. Being a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735).
1. Being a [nilpotent group](wikipedia://en/144569).
1. Being a [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012).
1. Being a [amenable group](wikipedia://en/657430).
1. Being a [word-hyperbolic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group).
1. Being a [torsion-free group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group).
1. Being a [polycyclic group](wikipedia://en/4000598).
1. Being a group with a [solvable word problem](wikipedia://en/33563).
1. Being a [residually finite group](wikipedia://en/5780875).
1. Being a group of finite [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197).
1. Being an [automatic group](wikipedia://en/4224977).
1. Being a [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600). (One can take $A_+$ to be the trivial group and $A_-$ to be a finitely presented group with unsolvable word problem whose existence is provided by the [Novikov-Boone theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novikov-Boone_theorem). Then [Kuznetsov's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuznetsov's_theorem) implies that $A_-$ does not embed into any finitely presentable simple group. Hence being a finitely presentable simple group is a Markov property.)
1. Being a group of finite [asymptotic dimension](wikipedia://en/52208686).
1. Being a group admitting a uniform embedding into a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

Note that the Adyan–Rabin theorem also implies that the complement of a Markov property in the class of finitely presentable groups is algorithmically undecidable. For example, the properties of being nontrivial, infinite, nonabelian, etc., for finitely presentable groups are undecidable. However, there do exist examples of interesting undecidable properties such that neither these properties nor their complements are Markov. Thus Collins (1969) [^o18] proved that the property of being [Hopfian](wikipedia://en/11486001) is undecidable for finitely presentable groups, while neither being Hopfian nor being non-Hopfian are Markov.
