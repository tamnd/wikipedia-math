# Ilya Nikolaevich Bronshtein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ilya Nikolaevich Bronshtein"}

Nikolaevich

**Ilya Nikolaevich Bronshtein** (Илья Николаевич Бронштейн, Ilja Nikolajewitsch Bronstein, also written as Bronschtein; born 1903, died 1976) was a Russian [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_mathematics).

:::

## Work and life {#Work_and_life}

Bronshtein taught at the [Moscow State Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Technical_University) (MAMI), then the State College of Mechanical Engineering, on the Chair of Advanced Mathematics established in 1939. He also collaborated at the [Zhukovsky Central Aerohydrodynamic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhukovsky_Central_Aerohydrodynamic_Institute).

With [Dmitrii Abramovich Raikov](wikipedia://en/69919109), Bronshtein authored a Russian handbook on elementary mathematics, mechanics and physics (Справочник по елементарнои математике, механике и физике), which was published in 1943.[^o7]

Bronshtein is known as the author of a handbook of mathematics for engineers and students of technical universities,[^o8] which he wrote together with [Konstantin Adolfovic Semendyayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Adolfovic_Semendyayev) around the 1939/1940 timeframe. [Hot lead typesetting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_lead_typesetting) for the work had already started when the [Siege of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad) prohibited further development and the [print matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Print_matrices) were relocated.[^o8] After the [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), they were first considered lost, but could be found again years later, so that the first edition of [Справочник по математике для инженеров и учащихся втузов](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Справочник_по_математике_для_инженеров_и_учащихся_втузов) could finally be published in 1945.[^o8][^o9] This was a major success and went through eleven editions in Russia and was translated into various languages, including German and English, until the publisher [Nauka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauka_%28publisher%29) planned to replace it with a translation of the American [Mathematical Handbook for Scientists and Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Handbook_for_Scientists_and_Engineers) by [Granino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granino_A._Korn) and [Theresa M. Korn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_M._Korn) in 1968.[^o8][^o9] However, in a parallel development starting in 1970, the so called "[Bronshtein and Semendyayev](wikipedia://en/49746051)" (BS), which had been translated into German in 1958, underwent a major overhaul by a team of [East-German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East-Germany) authors around Günter Grosche, Viktor & Dorothea Ziegler (of [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig)), to which Bronshtein himself could no longer contribute due to reasons of age.[^o8] This was published in 1979 and spawned translations into many other languages as well, including a retranslation into Russian and an English edition. In 1986, the 13th Russian edition was published. The German '[Wende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wende_%28GDR%29)' and the later [reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) led to considerable changes in the publishing environment in Germany between 1989 and 1991, which eventually resulted in two independent German publishing branches by [Eberhard Zeidler](wikipedia://en/69796977) (published 1995–2013) and by Gerhard Musiol & Heiner Mühlig (published 1992–2020) to expand and maintain the work up to the present, again with translations into many other languages including English.
