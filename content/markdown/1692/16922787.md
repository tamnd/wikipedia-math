# Salinon

*Image: Salinon.svg.* The salinon (red) and the circle (blue) have the same area.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Salinon"}

The **salinon** (meaning 'salt-cellar' in Greek) is a [geometrical figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrical_figure) that consists of four [semicircles](wikipedia://en/617831). It was first introduced in the *[Book of Lemmas](wikipedia://en/17941791)*, a work attributed to [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Let *A*, *D*, *E*, and *B* be four points on a line in the plane, in that order, with *AD* = *EB*. Let *O* be the bisector of segment *AB* (and of *DE*). Draw semicircles above line *AB* with [diameters](wikipedia://en/8007) *AB*, *AD*, and *EB*, and another semicircle below with diameter *DE*. A salinon is the figure bounded by these four semicircles.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Area {#Area}

Archimedes introduced the salinon in his *Book of Lemmas* by applying Book II, Proposition 10 of [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107). Archimedes noted that "the area of the figure bounded by the circumferences of all the semicircles \[is\] equal to the area of the circle on CF as diameter."[^o9]

Namely, if $r_1$ is the radius of large enclosing semicircle, and $r_2$ is the radius of the small central semicircle, then the area of the salinon is:

$$A=\frac{1}{4}\pi\left(r_1+r_2\right)^2.$$

### Arbelos {#Arbelos}

Should points *D* and *E* converge with *O*, it would form an [arbelos](wikipedia://en/887197), another one of Archimedes' creations, with [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) along the [*y*-axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-axis).[^o9]

:::
