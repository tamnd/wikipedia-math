# Paul the Persian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul the Persian"}

**Paul the Persian** or **Paulus Persa** was a 6th-century [East Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East) theologian and philosopher in the court of the [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire) king [Khosrow I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrow_I) (r. 531–579). Paul's treatises on [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308) would influence medieval [Islamic philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_philosophy). Notable works include his commentary in [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language) on Aristotle's *[Logic](wikipedia://en/499019)*, in which Paul declares the superiority of science over faith.[^o2]

:::

Paul may have studied [Greek philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_philosophy) at the [School of Nisibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Nisibis) in modern [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) and at the [Academy of Gondishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Gondishapur) in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran).[^o5][^o3] Scholars have variously identified him with Paul of Nisibis[^o4] or Paul of [Basra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra).[^o5] The administrative capital of the Sasanid Empire was [Ctesiphon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesiphon) in modern [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq).

## Life and known works {#Life_and_known_works}

Paul the Persian is mentioned in the 9th-century *[The Chronicle of Seert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chronicle_of_Seert)* and from the *Chronicon Ecclesiasticum* of the 13th-century [Syriac Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church) historian [Bar-Hebraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-Hebraeus). These sources indicate that he was born in Dershahr in [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia). Bar-Hebraeus mentions that he lived during the time of the [Nestorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestorianism) patriarch [Ezekiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel_%28Nestorian_Patriarch%29) (567-580). According to Bar-Hebraeus, Paul was a cleric in the [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East) and well versed in [theological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) and philosophical subjects.

Paul wrote two known works. One was an introduction to the philosophy of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308) which he delivered before [Khosrow I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrow_I) and was later translated into Syriac by [Severus Sebokht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severus_Sebokht). This work was also translated into [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) at a later date.[^o7] The other work is *On Interpretation*, which has never been published.

Both the *Chronicle of Seert* and Bar-Hebraeus record that Paul aspired to the office of [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of [Fars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fars_%28East_Syrian_Ecclesiastical_Province%29) and, failing to be elected, converted to [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism).[^o8] However, their claim is not otherwise documented and may be the product of the rivalry between the "[Jacobite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church)" Syriac Orthodox Church and the "Nestorian" Church of the East. The entry in the *Chronicle of Seert* reads:

\[Khosrow\] was very learned in philosophy, which he had studied, it is said, under Mar Bar Samma, bishop of Qardu, and under Paul the Persian Philosopher, who, being unable to obtain the metropolitan see of Persia, renounced the Christian religion.[^o9]
