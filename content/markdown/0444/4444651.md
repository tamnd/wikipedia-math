# Evil number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Evil number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **evil number** is a non-negative integer that has an even [number of 1s](wikipedia://en/1127884) in its [binary expansion](wikipedia://en/238686). These numbers give the positions of the zero values in the [Thue–Morse sequence](wikipedia://en/491903), and for this reason they have also been called the **Thue–Morse set**.[^o3] Non-negative integers that are not evil are called [odious numbers](wikipedia://en/2924960).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The first evil numbers are 0, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 15, 17, 18, 20, 23, 24, 27, 29, 30, 33, 34, 36, 39, and so on.[^o2]

:::

## Equal sums {#Equal_sums}

The partition of the non-negative integers into the odious and evil numbers is the unique partition of these numbers into two sets that have equal [multisets](wikipedia://en/305303) of pairwise sums.[^o7]

As 19th-century mathematician Eugène Prouhet showed, the partition into evil and odious numbers of the numbers from $0$ to $2^k-1$, for any $k$, provides a solution to the [Prouhet–Tarry–Escott problem](wikipedia://en/16400356) of finding sets of numbers whose sums of powers are equal up to the $k$th power.[^o8]

## In computer science {#In_computer_science}

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), an evil number is said to have [even parity](wikipedia://en/194467#Parity).
