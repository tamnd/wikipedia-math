# Hartogs–Rosenthal theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hartogs–Rosenthal theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hartogs–Rosenthal theorem** is a classical result in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) on the [uniform approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_approximation) of continuous functions on compact subsets of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) by [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210). The theorem was proved in 1931 by the German mathematicians [Friedrich Hartogs](wikipedia://en/2977779) and [Arthur Rosenthal](wikipedia://en/33183847) and has been widely applied, particularly in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The Hartogs–Rosenthal theorem states that if *K* is a compact subset of the complex plane with [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) zero, then any continuous complex-valued function on *K* can be uniformly approximated by rational functions.

:::

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

By the [Stone–Weierstrass theorem](wikipedia://en/28858) any complex-valued continuous function on *K* can be uniformly approximated by a polynomial in $z$ and $\overline{z}$.

So it suffices to show that $\overline{z}$ can be uniformly approximated by a rational function on *K*.

Let *g(z)* be a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) of compact support on **C** equal to 1 on *K* and set

$$f(z)=g(z)\cdot \overline{z}.$$

By the [generalized Cauchy integral formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_integral_formula#Smooth_functions)

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint_{{C\backslash K}} \frac{\partial f}{\partial \bar{w}}\frac{dw\wedge d\bar{w}}{w-z},$$

since *K* has measure zero.

Restricting *z* to *K* and taking [Riemann approximating sums](wikipedia://en/176478) for the integral on the right hand side yields the required uniform approximation of $\bar{z}$ by a rational function.[^o6]

:::
