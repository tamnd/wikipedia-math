# Lyle Jones

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lyle Jones"}

**Lyle Vincent Jones** (March 11, 1924 – April 13, 2016) was an American psychologist known for his pioneering work in [psychometrics](wikipedia://en/24982). He was an early architect of the [National Assessment of Educational Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assessment_of_Educational_Progress).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Jones was born in [Grandview, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandview,_Washington) on March 11, 1924. He began taking classes at [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College) in 1942. A year later, immediately after completing his freshman year, he left Reed to begin active duty in the [United States Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Corps).[^o4] He continued to serve in the Air Corps until 1946, when he attended the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) to complete his bachelor's degree. He then received his master's degree, also from the University of Washington, before receiving his Ph.D. from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1950.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

After receiving his Ph.D., Jones began teaching at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) before joining the faculty of the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) (UNC-Chapel Hill) in 1957.[^o7] At UNC-Chapel Hill, he worked with [Louis Leon Thurstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Leon_Thurstone) at the Psychometric Laboratory (later renamed the L. L. Thurstone Psychometric Laboratory). Jones served as director of the Psychometric Laboratory from 1957 until he retired in 1992, except for a five-year hiatus.[^o8] For ten years (1969–79), he was also vice chancellor at UNC-Chapel Hill and dean of the graduate school there.[^o7][^o9] Beginning in 1992, he was a research professor at UNC-Chapel Hill.[^o5]

### Other positions {#Other_positions}

Jones was the managing editor of *[Psychometrika](wikipedia://en/22991646)* from 1956 to 1961, the president of the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500) from 1962 to 1963, and president of the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association)'s Division 5 from 1963 to 1964.[^o11]

## Death and recognition {#Death_and_recognition}

Jones died on April 13, 2016, in [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina), at the age of 92.[^o8] In his honor, UNC-Chapel Hill established the Lyle V. Jones Award, which it awards every year to a graduate student in the L. L. Thurstone Psychometric Laboratory "who epitomizes both academic excellence and community engagement within the lab."[^o13]
