# Trevor Wooley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trevor Wooley"}

**Trevor Dion Wooley** is a British mathematician, the Andris A. Zoltners Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University).[^o3] His fields of interest include [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) and Diophantine problems, [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), the [Hardy-Littlewood circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy-Littlewood_circle_method), and the theory and applications of exponential sums. He has made significant breakthroughs on [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569), for which he was awarded the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) in 1998.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Wooley read mathematics in [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge), receiving a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1987 with first class honours, and a master's degree in 1991.[^o5] Meanwhile, he completed his PhD, supervised by [Bob Vaughan](wikipedia://en/4871951), in 1990 from [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London).[^o5]

After postdoctoral research in the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), he became an assistant professor of mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1991. He was promoted to associate professor in 1995, and full professor in 1998; he chaired the mathematics department from 2002 to 2005.[^o5]

In 2007 he returned to the UK as a professor of pure mathematics at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), where he became head of pure mathematics from 2015 to 2016. At Bristol, his doctoral students included [Thomas Bloom](wikipedia://en/71404791).[^o6] He took his current position as Andris A. Zoltners Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in 2019.[^o5]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Wooley was the recipient of the 1993 [Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o8] He was awarded the 1998 [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) "for his work in [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333), in particular on [problems of Waring's type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waring_problem)".[^o9] He was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM), held in [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing) in 2002,[^o10] and after receiving the 2012 [Fröhlich Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fröhlich_Prize)[^o11] he spoke again at the 2014 ICM in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul).[^o10]

He was elected as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 2007,[^o12] and as a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012.[^o13]
