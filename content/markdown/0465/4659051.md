# Francis Anderson (philosopher)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Francis Anderson"}

**Sir Francis Anderson** (3 September 1858 – 24 June 1941) was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people)-born [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_people) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [educator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educator).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Francis Anderson was born in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), the son of Francis Anderson, a [manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturer), and his wife Elizabeth Anna Lockart, née Ellison.[^o4] Anderson was educated at Old Wynd and Oatlands public schools and became a pupil-teacher at the age of 14. He went on to the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), matriculating in 1876 and graduated [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Scotland%29) in 1883. He was awarded [Sir Richard Jebb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Claverhouse_Jebb)'s prize for [Greek literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_literature), took first place in the philosophical classes of Professors Veitch and Caird, and won two scholarships. For two years he was assistant to the professor of [moral philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_philosophy), studied in the theological faculty with the intention of entering the ministry, but apparently was not ordained.

## Honours and legacy {#Honours_and_legacy}

Anderson was knighted in the 1936 [King's Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Birthday_Honours) and died in Sydney on 24 June 1941. for "educational and social welfare services".

His [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) on *Liberty, Equality and Fraternity* was published by the Association of Psychology and Philosophy.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He married twice, firstly to [Maybanke Selfe Wolstenholme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maybanke_Anderson), and then to Josephine Wight who survived him; there were no children.
