# Edward Kofler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Kofler"}

**Edward Kofler** (16 November 1911 – 22 April 2007) was a Polish-born Swiss [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made important contributions to [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and [fuzzy logic](wikipedia://en/49180) by working out the theory of [linear partial information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_partial_information).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kofler was born in Brzeżany, [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (now [Berezhany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berezhany), western Ukraine) and [graduated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation) as a disciple of among others [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735) and [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) from the [University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv) Poland (now Ukraine) and the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University), having studied [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924). After graduation in 1939 Kofler returned to his family in [Kolomyia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomyia) (today Kolomea in Ukraine), where he taught mathematics in a Polish high school. After [German attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) on the town 1 July 1941 he succeeded to escape to [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan) together with his wife. At [Alma-Ata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma-Ata) he managed a Polish school with orphanage in [exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile) and worked there as mathematics teacher. After World War II ended he returned home with the orphanage. He was accompanied by his wife and their baby son. The family settled in Poland. From 1959 he accepted the position of lecturer at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in the faculty of economics. In 1962 he gained a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) with his thesis *Economic Decisions, Applying Game Theory*. Then in 1962 he became assistant professor at the faculty of social science in the same university, specializing in [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390).

In 1969 he migrated to [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich), Switzerland, where he was employed at the Institute for Empirical Research in Economics at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) and scientific advisor at the [Swiss National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Science_Foundation) (Schweizerische Nationalfonds zur Förderung der wissenschaftlichen Forschung). In Zurich in 1970 Kofler developed his [linear partial information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_partial_information) (LPI) theory allowing qualified decisions to be made on the basis of fuzzy logic: incomplete or fuzzy [a priori](wikipedia://en/6679056) [information](wikipedia://en/18985062).

Kofler was visiting professor at the [University of Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saint_Petersburg) (former [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad), Russia), [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) (Germany), [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University) ([Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), Canada) and [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) (England). He collaborated with many well known specialists in [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), such as [Oskar R. Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_R._Lange) in Poland, Nicolai Vorobiev in the Soviet Union, [Günter Menges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Günter_Menges) in Germany, and Heidi Schelbert and Peter Zweifel in Zurich. He was the author of many books and articles. He died in Zurich.
