# Hessian equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hessian equation"}

In mathematics, ***k*-Hessian equations** (or **Hessian equations** for short) are [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) (PDEs) based on the [Hessian matrix](wikipedia://en/412108). More specifically, a Hessian equation is the *k*-trace, or the *k*th [elementary symmetric polynomial](wikipedia://en/1440695) of eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix. When *k* ≥ 2, the *k*-Hessian equation is a fully nonlinear partial differential equation.[^o2] It can be written as ${\cal S}_k[u]=f$, where $1\leqslant k \leqslant n$, ${\cal S}_k[u]=\sigma_k(\lambda({\cal D}^2u))$, and $\lambda({\cal D}^2u)=(\lambda_1,\cdots,\lambda_n)$, are the eigenvalues of the *Hessian matrix* ${\cal D}^2u=[\partial_i \partial_ju]_{1\leq i,j \leq n}$ and $\sigma_k(\lambda)=\sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_k}\lambda_{i_1}\cdots\lambda_{i_k}$, is a $k$ th elementary symmetric polynomial.[^o3][^o4]

:::

Much like differential equations often study the actions of [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886) (e.g. [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) and [elliptic equations](wikipedia://en/662787)), Hessian equations can be understood as simply eigenvalue equations acted upon by the Hessian differential operator. Special cases include the [Monge–Ampère equation](wikipedia://en/4914066)[^o5] and [Poisson's equation](wikipedia://en/193735) (the Laplacian being the trace of the Hessian matrix). The *2−*hessian operator also appears in conformal mapping problems. In fact, the *2−*hessian equation is unfamiliar outside Riemannian geometry and elliptic regularity theory, that is closely related to the scalar curvature operator, which provides an intrinsic curvature for a three-dimensional manifold.

These equations are of interest in geometric PDEs (a subfield at the interface between both [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474) and PDEs) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).
