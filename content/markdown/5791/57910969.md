# Christopher J. Bishop

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christopher J. Bishop"}

**Christopher J. Bishop** is an American mathematician on the faculty at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University). He received his bachelor's in mathematics from [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) in 1982, going on from there to spend a year at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University), receiving at Cambridge a Certificate of Advanced Study in mathematics, before entering the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1983 for his doctoral studies in mathematics. As a graduate student in Chicago, his advisor, [Peter Jones](wikipedia://en/1604461), took a position at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), causing Bishop to spend the years 1985–87 at Yale as a visiting graduate student and programmer. Nonetheless, he received his PhD from the University of Chicago in 1987.[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Upon receiving his PhD, Bishop went to [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in Berkeley from 1987–88. After that, he was the Henrik Assistant Professor at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) from 1988–91. In 1992 he joined, and remains on, the faculty of Stony Brook University, attaining the rank of full professor there in 1997.[^o3]

## Research {#Research}

Bishop is known for his contributions to [geometric function theory](wikipedia://en/5458932),[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9] [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652),[^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14] [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518),[^o15][^o16] and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927);[^o17][^o18] and in particular for topics such as fractals, harmonic measure, conformal and quasiconformal mappings and Julia sets. Along with [Peter Jones](wikipedia://en/1604461), he is the namesake of the class of Bishop-Jones curves.[^o19]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Bishop was awarded the 1992 A. P. [Sloan Foundation fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Foundation_fellowship).[^o21] He was an invited speaker at the 2018 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).[^o22] He was included in the 2019 class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to the theory of harmonic measures, quasiconformal maps and transcendental dynamics"[^o23] and was a 2019 Simons Fellow in Mathematics.[^o24] He is on the editorial board of the journal [Annales Academiae Scientiarum Fennicae Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_Academiae_Scientiarum_Fennicae_Mathematica) as of July 1, 2021.[^o25] In November 2021 he was appointed a Distinguished Professor at the State University of New York.[^o26] In 2024, he received the [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Berwick_Prize) of the London Mathematical Society.
