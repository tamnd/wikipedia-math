# Isaac ibn al-Ahdab

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isaac ibn al-Ahdab"}

**Itzḥak ben Shlomo ibn al-Aḥdab** (or **ibn al-Ḥadib**) **ben Tzaddiq ha-Sefardi** (יצחק בן שלמה בן צדיק אלאחדב הספרדי, c. 1350 – c. 1426) was a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) mathematician, astronomer, and poet.[^o2]

:::

Ibn al-Aḥdab was born in [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_%28historical_region%29) to a prominent Jewish family. He was a student of Judah ben Asher II, the great-grandson of [Asher ben Yeḥiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asher_ben_Jehiel) of Cologne, who was killed in the [anti-Jewish massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Spain#1300–1391) of 1391. By 1396 Ibn al-Aḥdab had fled Spain and was in [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily), where he lived (in [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) and [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo)) until his death around 1426.[^o3]

## Work {#Work}

He studied the algebra of Maghrebi mathematician [Ibn al-Bannā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_al-Banna'_al-Marrakushi) and published *The Epistle of the Number*, a translation and detailed commentary on Ibn al-Bannā's 13th century treatise *Talḵīṣ ʿAmal al-Ḥisāb* ("A summary of the operations of calculation").[^o5] The work is notable in being the first known Hebrew-language treatise to include extensive algebraic theories and operations.[^o6][^o7]

His astronomical works include *Oraḥ selulah (Upraised Path)*, a set of tables in Hebrew for conjunctions and oppositions of the Sun and the Moon,[^o8] *Keli Ḥemdah (Precious Instrument),* which describes a unique [equatorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorium) of his own invention, functioning on the [Ptolemaic theory of epicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_epicycle),*[^o9]* and *Keli Memutsa (Intermediate Instrument)*, which describes another unique instrument of his own design, a combination [astrolabe](wikipedia://en/73664)-[quadrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrant_%28instrument%29)*.[^o9]* [Bernard R. Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_R._Goldstein) published a partial translation of *Keli Ḥemdah* in 1987.[^o9] *Oraḥ selulah* survives in 25 MSS, *Keli Ḥemdah* in 15 MSS, and *Keli Memutsa* in 1 MS.[^o10]

He is the author of a commentary on the [Passover Haggadah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover_Haggada), titled *Pesach Doros* (*Passover of later generations)* and printed by Mekhon Bet Aharon ṿe-Yiśraʼel in 2000.[^o10]

*Leshon ha-Zahav (Golden Language)*, an explication of the names for units and measurements found in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible).[^o10]

He is probably the author of a commentary on [Maimonides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimonides)' *[Laws of the Sanctification of the Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishneh_Torah),* found in the same MS as *Leshon ha-Zahav* with no author given.[^o10]

He also wrote songs, published as *Shirei Rabbeinu Itzḥak ben Shlomo ibn al-Aḥdab* (1987). He is known to have composed a work called *Shir ha-Shirim,* but it has not survived.[^o10]
