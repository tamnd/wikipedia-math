# Residue at infinity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Residue at infinity"}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), a branch of mathematics, the **residue at infinity** is a [residue](wikipedia://en/73102) of a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) on an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158) having an infinite external radius. The *infinity* $\infty$ is a point added to the local space $\mathbb C$ in order to render it [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) (in this case it is a [one-point compactification](wikipedia://en/55611)). This space denoted $\hat{\mathbb C}$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799).[^o2] One can use the residue at infinity to calculate some [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a holomorphic function *f* on an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158) $A(0, R, \infty)$ (centered at 0, with inner radius $R$ and infinite outer radius), the **residue at infinity** of the function *f* can be defined in terms of the usual [residue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_%28mathematics%29) as follows:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty) = -\operatorname{Res}\left( {1\over z^2}f\left({1\over z}\right), 0 \right).$$

Thus, one can transfer the study of $f(z)$ at infinity to the study of $f(1/z)$ at the origin.

Note that $\forall r > R$, we have

$$\operatorname{Res}(f, \infty) = {-1\over 2\pi i}\int_{C(0, r)} f(z) \, dz.$$

Since, for holomorphic functions the sum of the residues at the isolated singularities plus the residue at infinity is zero, it can be expressed as:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f(z), \infty) = -\sum_k \operatorname{Res}\left(f\left(z\right), a_k\right).$$

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

One might first guess that the definition of the residue of $f(z)$ at infinity should just be the residue of $f(1/z)$ at $z=0$. However, the reason that we consider instead $-\frac{1}{z^2}f\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)$ is that one does not take residues of *functions*, but of *[differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519)*, i.e. the residue of $f(z)dz$ at infinity is the residue of $f\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)d\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)=-\frac{1}{z^2}f\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)dz$ at $z=0$.
