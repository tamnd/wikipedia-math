# James Hodgson (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Hodgson"}

**James Hodgson** (1672–1755) was an English astronomer, mathematical teacher, lecturer and writer.

*Image: James Hodgson White.jpg.* James Hodgson, engraving by [George White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_White_%28artist%29) after [Thomas Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gibson_%28artist%29)

:::

## Life {#Life}

The nephew of [Christopher Wren](wikipedia://en/52946), Hodgson was an assistant to the first Astronomer Royal, [John Flamsteed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Flamsteed), at the [Royal Observatory, Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Observatory,_Greenwich), between 1695 and 1702.[^o3] In 1703 he was elected Fellow, and in 1733 one of the council, of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society). He was master of mathematics at the [Royal Mathematical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_School_of_Mathematics) at [Christ's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_Hospital) from 1709 until his death. Hodgson married Flamsteed's niece in 1702, took part in the controversies in which Flamsteed was engaged and helped bring his works to posthumous publication.[^o4] He promoted Flamsteed's work in his textbook *A System of the Mathematics* (1723) and has been credited with improving standards and examination success at the School.[^o5]

Hodgson died on 25 June 1755, leaving a widow and several children.[^o4]

## Works {#Works}

*Image: Hodgson - The valuation of annuities upon lives, 1747 - 220.tif.* *The valuation of annuities upon lives*, 1724 .

When Flamsteed died Hodgson assisted his widow in the publication of her husband's works, and he appears as co-editor of the *Atlas Cœlestis*, published in 1729. The share which Joseph Crosthwaite had in seeing the works through the press was not acknowledged.[^o4]

Hodgson wrote papers in the *[Philosophical Transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Transactions)* (vols. xxxvii–xlix.), and also:[^o4]

- *The Theory of Navigation*, 1706.
- *The Laws of Stereographick Projection*, printed in *Miscellanea Curiosa*, vol. ii., 1708.
- *A System of the Mathematics*, 1723.
- *The Doctrine of Fluxions founded on Sir Isaac Newton's Method*, 1736.
- *An Introduction to Chronology*, 1747.
- *A Treatise on Annuities*, 1747.
- *The Theory of Jupiter's Satellites*, 1750.

He prefixed a short treatise on *The Theory of Perspective* to the English translation of the French Jesuit [Jean Dubreuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Dubreuil)'s work on perspective, which went to a fourth edition in 1765.[^o4]
