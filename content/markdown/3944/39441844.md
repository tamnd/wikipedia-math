# Yitang Zhang

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yitang Zhang"}

[Zhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_%28surname%29)

**Yitang** **Zhang** (; born February 5, 1955)[^o5] is a Chinese-American mathematician primarily working in the field of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is a professor of mathematics at [Sun Yat-sen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen_University).[^o6][^o7]

:::

In 2013, while working as a lecturer at the [University of New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Hampshire), Zhang submitted an important paper on the [Twin Prime Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Prime_Conjecture) to the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*, establishing the first finite bound on the least gap between consecutive primes that is attained infinitely often. Later that year, he was awarded the [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411), and then in 2014, the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177), [Rolf Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Schock_Prize), and a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship).

Zhang became a professor of mathematics at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) in fall 2015.[^o8] He retired from UCSB in 2025 and returned to China as a professor at [Sun Yat-sen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen_University).

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Zhang was born in Shanghai, China. His [ancestral home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_home_%28Chinese%29) is in [Pinghu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinghu), [Zhejiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang). He lived in Shanghai with his grandmother until he attended college. At around 9 years old, he found a proof of the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034).[^o10] He first learned about [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) and [Goldbach's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758) when he was 10 years old.[^o10]

During the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), Zhang and his mother were [sent to the countryside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_to_the_Countryside_Movement) to work in the fields. He worked as a laborer for 10 years, during which he was unable to attend high school.[^o10] After the end of the Cultural Revolution, Zhang enrolled at [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University) in 1978, graduating with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics in 1982. He then pursued graduate studies at the university under number theorist Pan Chengbiao and earned a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in mathematics in 1984.[^o11]

With recommendations from [Ding Shisun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding_Shisun), the president of Peking University, and Deng Donggao, the chair of the math department at Peking University,[^o12] Zhang won a full scholarship to pursue doctoral studies at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), beginning in January 1985. He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics in December 1991.

## Career {#Career}

Zhang's PhD work was on the two-dimensional [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823). After graduation, Zhang had trouble finding an academic position. In a 2013 interview with *Nautilus* magazine, Zhang said he did not get a job after graduation. "During that period it was difficult to find a job in academics. That was a job market problem. Also, my advisor \[Tzuong-Tsieng Moh\] did not write me letters of recommendation."[^o14] Zhang made this claim again in [George Csicsery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Csicsery)'s documentary film "Counting from Infinity: Yitang Zhang and the Twin Prime Conjecture"[^o15] while discussing his difficulties at Purdue and in the years that followed.[^o10] Moh claimed that Zhang never came back to him requesting recommendation letters.[^o12] In a detailed profile published in *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* magazine in February 2015, [Alec Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Wilkinson) wrote Zhang "parted unhappily" with Moh, and that Zhang "left Purdue without Moh's support, and, having published no papers, was unable to find an academic job".[^o23] In 2018, responding to reports of his treatment of Zhang, Moh posted an update on his website. Moh wrote that Zhang "failed miserably" in proving the two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture, "never published any paper on algebraic geometry" after leaving Purdue, and "wasted seven years of his own life and my time".[^o16]

After some years, Zhang managed to find a position as a lecturer at the [University of New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Hampshire), where he was hired by [Kenneth Appel](wikipedia://en/311544) in 1999. Prior to getting back to academia, he worked for several years as an accountant and a delivery worker for a New York City restaurant. He also worked in a motel in [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky) and in a [Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subway_%28restaurant%29) sandwich shop.[^o3] A profile published in [Quanta Magazine](wikipedia://en/51804976) reports that Zhang used to live in his car during the initial job-hunting days.[^o10] He served as lecturer at UNH from 1999[^o17] until around January 2014, when UNH appointed him to a full professorship as a result of his breakthrough on prime numbers.[^o32] Zhang stayed for a semester at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), NJ, in 2014, and he joined the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) in fall 2015.[^o18] He took a full-time position at [Sun Yat-sen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen_University) in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), China on June 27, 2025.[^o7] As of September 2025, Zhang works at the university's main campus in Guangzhou, but plans to primarily work at the university's Hong Kong Institute of Advanced Study in [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong) after its construction is completed.[^o19]

## Research {#Research}

On April 17, 2013, Zhang announced a proof that there are infinitely many pairs of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) that differ by less than 70 million. This result implies the existence of an infinitely repeatable [prime 2-tuple](wikipedia://en/11421646),[^o3] thus establishing a theorem akin to the [twin prime conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_prime_conjecture). Zhang's paper was accepted by *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* in early May 2013,[^o21] his first publication since his last paper in 2001.[^o22] The proof was refereed by leading experts in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).[^o23] Research programs such as the [Polymath Project](wikipedia://en/34858344#Polymath) built upon Zhang's result, eventually lowering the bounded gap to 212 .[^o24]

If *P*(*N*) stands for the proposition that there is an infinitude of pairs of prime numbers (not necessarily consecutive primes) that differ by exactly *N*, then Zhang's result is equivalent to the statement that there exists at least one even integer *k* \< 70,000,000 such that *P*(*k*) is true. The classical form of the twin prime conjecture is *P*(2); and in fact it has been conjectured that *P*(*k*) holds for *all* even integers *k*.[^o25][^o26] While these stronger conjectures remain unproven, a result due to [James Maynard](wikipedia://en/42512102) in November 2013, employing a different technique, showed that *P*(*k*) holds for some *k* ≤ 600.[^o27] Subsequently, in April 2014, the [Polymath project 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath_project) lowered the bound to *k* ≤ 246.[^o28] If the [Elliott–Halberstam conjecture](wikipedia://en/1898427) and its generalization, respectively, hold, then *k* ≤ 12 and *k* ≤ 6 follow using current methods.[^o23][^o28]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Zhang was awarded the 2013 [Morningside Special Achievement Award in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/39615068),[^o30] the 2013 [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411),[^o31] the 2014 [Frank Nelson Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Number Theory,[^o32][^o33] and the 2014 [Rolf Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Schock_Prize)[^o34] in Mathematics.

He is a recipient of the 2014 [MacArthur award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program),[^o35] and was elected as an [Academia Sinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Sinica) Fellow during the same year.[^o11] He was an [invited speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) at the 2014 International Congress of Mathematicians.

In 2021, Zhang was awarded an honorary Doctor of Science degree from the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong). [^o36]

## Political views {#Political_views}

In 1989 Zhang joined a group interested in [Chinese democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_movements_of_China) (中国民联). In a 2013 interview, he affirmed that his political views on the subject had not changed since.[^o23][^o38] In 2025, Zhang cited the "political climate" of the United States as a reason for his return to China.[^o39]
