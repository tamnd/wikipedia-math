# Alan Baker (philosopher)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alan Baker"}

**Alan R. Baker** is a professor of philosophy in [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) ([Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania), United States), specializing in the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) and the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science). In 2026, he was the recipient of a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship). Baker is also a former U.S. [shogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi) champion.

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Baker did his undergraduate studies at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), earning a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in philosophy with [first class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_honours) in 1991. He then moved to the [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.) for graduate school, earning a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1995 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1999, both in philosophy from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). His doctoral supervisors were [Paul Benacerraf](wikipedia://en/1432190) and [Gideon Rosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon_Rosen). After working as an assistant professor at [Xavier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_University), he moved to [Swarthmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore,_Pennsylvania) in 2003.[^o3]

Philosophically, Baker is a [mathematical realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_realism) who has used examples from [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology) to show the necessity of mathematics in scientific reasoning.[^o5]

In 2005 *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* published an excerpt from the exam from his *"Introduction to Metaphysics and Epistemology"* course in its *"pop quiz"* column.[^o6][^o7]

## Shogi {#Shogi}

In 2005, Baker founded a shogi club at Swarthmore College, outside [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), which is one of only two college-based shogi clubs in the United States. The other club is [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) Shogi Club, which was founded in August 2017.[^o9]

Baker is also a former U.S. shogi champion, having won the 13th U.S. Shogi Championship in 2008.[^o9] his [ELO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system) rating of 2107 placed him in 20th place on the Federation of European Shogi Associations (FESA) bi-annual rating list.[^o10][^o11] Since 2017, Baker has been captain of the United States 1st Team in the A Division of the online World Shogi League. Under his captaincy, the time has won the division twice, most recently in 2025.

Tournament results:

- 2008: Winner, 13th U.S. [Shogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi) Championship.
- 2008: 3rd place, Individual Tournament, 4th [International Shogi Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Shogi_Association#International_activities) ([Tendō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendō,_Yamagata)).[^o12]
- 2009: 2nd place, British Open Shogi Championship.[^o10]
- 2014: Winner, Group B Individual Tournament, 6th International Shogi Forum ([Shizuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizuoka,_Shizuoka))[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Baker's father was British philosopher [Gordon Park Baker](wikipedia://en/6943143).
