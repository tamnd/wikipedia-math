# 257-gon

Regular polygon stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="257-gon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **257-gon** is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with 257 sides. The sum of the interior angles of any non-[self-intersecting](wikipedia://en/50055439) 257-gon is 45,900°.

:::

## Regular 257-gon {#Regular_257-gon}

The area of a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) 257-gon is (with *t* = edge length)

$$A = \frac{257}{4} t^2 \cot \frac{\pi}{257}\approx 5255.751t^2.$$

A whole regular 257-gon is not visually discernible from a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), and its perimeter differs from that of the [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262) by about 24 [parts per million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parts_per_million).

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

The regular 257-gon (one with all sides equal and all angles equal) is of interest for being a [constructible polygon](wikipedia://en/523430): that is, it can be [constructed using a compass and an unmarked straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge_construction). This is because 257 is a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime), being of the form 2<sup>2<sup>*n*</sup></sup> + 1 (in this case *n* = 3). Thus, the values $\cos \frac{\pi}{257}$ and $\cos \frac{2\pi}{257}$ are 128-degree [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158), and like all [constructible numbers](wikipedia://en/7439) they can be written using [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208) and no higher-order roots.

Although it was known to [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) by 1801 that the regular 257-gon was constructible, the first explicit constructions of a regular 257-gon were given by [Magnus Georg Paucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Georg_Paucker) (1822)[^o5] and [Friedrich Julius Richelot](wikipedia://en/20346321) (1832).[^o6] Another method involves the use of 150 circles, 24 being [Carlyle circles](wikipedia://en/39453637): this method is pictured below, along with a full construction showing all steps. One of these Carlyle circles solves the [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175) *x*<sup>2</sup> + *x* − 64 = 0.[^o7]

### Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The *regular 257-gon* has [Dih<sub>257</sub> symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 514. Since 257 is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) there is one subgroup with dihedral symmetry: Dih<sub>1</sub>, and 2 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: Z<sub>257</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>.

:::

## 257-gram {#257-gram}

A 257-gram is a 257-sided [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303). As 257 is prime, there are 127 regular forms generated by [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) {257/*n*} for all [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) 2 ≤ *n* ≤ 128 as $\left\lfloor \frac{257}{2} \right\rfloor = 128$.

Below is a view of {257/128}, with 257 nearly radial edges, with its star vertex [internal angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) 180°/257 (~0.7°).
