# Barnette–Bosák–Lederberg graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Barnette–Bosák–Lederberg graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Barnette–Bosák–Lederberg graph** is a [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) (that is, 3-[regular](wikipedia://en/85821)) [polyhedral graph](wikipedia://en/24656270) with no [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle), the smallest such graph possible.[^o3] It was discovered in the mid-1960s by [Joshua Lederberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Lederberg), David Barnette, and Juraj Bosák, after whom it is named. It has 38 vertices and 57 edges.[^o4][^o5]

:::

Other larger non-Hamiltonian cubic polyhedral graphs include the 46-vertex [Tutte graph](wikipedia://en/24511700) and a 44-vertex graph found by [Emanuels Grīnbergs](wikipedia://en/35369474) using [Grinberg's theorem](wikipedia://en/22816079). The Barnette–Bosák–Lederberg graph has a similar construction to the Tutte graph but is composed of two Tutte fragments, connected through a [pentagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859659), instead of three connected through a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606). Without the constraint of having exactly three edges at every vertex, much smaller non-Hamiltonian polyhedral graphs are possible, including the [Goldner–Harary graph](wikipedia://en/24674758) and the [Herschel graph](wikipedia://en/24670148).
