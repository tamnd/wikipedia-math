# Log-rank conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Log-rank conjecture"}

In [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392), the **log-rank conjecture** states that the deterministic [communication complexity](wikipedia://en/50329) of a two-party [Boolean function](wikipedia://en/753349) is polynomially related to the logarithm of the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of its input matrix.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Let $D(f)$ denote the [deterministic communication complexity](wikipedia://en/50329#Formal_definition) of a function, and let $\operatorname{rank}(f)$ denote the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of its input matrix $M_f$ (over the reals). Since every protocol using up to $c$ bits partitions $M_f$ into at most $2^c$ monochromatic rectangles, and each of these has rank at most 1,

$$D(f) \geq \log_2 \operatorname{rank}(f).$$

The log-rank conjecture states that $D(f)$ is also *upper-bounded* by a polynomial in the log-rank: for some constant $C$,

$$D(f) = O((\log \operatorname{rank}(f))^C).$$

Lovett [^o4] proved the upper bound

$$D(f) = O\left(\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}(f)} \log \operatorname{rank}(f)\right).$$

This was improved by Sudakov and Tomon,[^o5] who removed the logarithmic factor, showing that

$$D(f) = O\left(\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}(f)}\right).$$

This is the best currently known upper bound.

The best known lower bound, due to Göös, Pitassi and Watson,[^o6] states that $C \geq 2$. In other words, there exists a sequence of functions $f_n$, whose log-rank goes to infinity, such that

$$D(f_n) = \tilde\Omega((\log \operatorname{rank}(f_n))^2).$$

In 2019, an approximate version of the conjecture for randomised communication has been disproved.[^o7]
