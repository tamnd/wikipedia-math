# Moshe Jarden

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Moshe Jarden"}

**Moshe Jarden** (משה ירדן) is an Israeli mathematician specializing in [field arithmetic](wikipedia://en/14721989).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Moshe Jarden was born in 1942 in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv). His father, Dr. Dov Jarden (1911–1986), was a mathematician, writer and linguist, who transmitted him his love to mathematics. In 1970 he received his Ph.D. in mathematics from the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), with [Hillel Furstenberg](wikipedia://en/6450152) as his thesis advisor. He accomplished his postdoctorate during 1971–1973 at the Institut of Mathematics, [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University), with [Peter Roquette](wikipedia://en/41199245) as his mentor, and habilitated there in 1972. During these years in Heidelberg, he initiated an intense and long-term cooperation with German mathematicians, especially with [Peter Roquette](wikipedia://en/41199245), Wulf-Dieter Geyer, [Gerhard Frey](wikipedia://en/1021312), and Juergen Ritter. His achievements in mathematics, as well as the foundation of this fruitful cooperation with German mathematicians, earned him the L. Meithner-A.v.Humboldt Prize by the [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation) in 2001. In 1974, Moshe Jarden returned to [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel), and joined the School of Mathematics of [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University). He became a full professor in 1982, and the incumbent of the Cissie and Aaron Beare chair in Algebra and [Number Theory](wikipedia://en/21527) in 1998. One of his great achievements is the publication of the book "Field Arithmetic" in the series [Ergebnisse der Mathematik und ihrer Grenzgebiete](wikipedia://en/15767827) of Springer, which earned him the Landau Prize.

## Work {#Work}

The main contribution of Moshe Jarden in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in general, is his research on families of large algebraic extensions of Hilbertian fields (in particular global fields), parametrized by the [automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphisms) of the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of the base field. Notable results in this domain are the zero theorem, the transfer theorem, the free generators theorem, the Frey-Jarden theorem about the rank of algebraic varieties over large algebraic fields, Geyer-Jarden theorem about torsion points on elliptic curves over large algebraic fields, and the strong approximation theorem over such fields.

The remarkable development of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) over a class of large fields, called *ample fields*,[^o6] is described in the second book of Jarden: *Algebraic Patching*.[^o7]

## Field arithmetic {#Field_arithmetic}

In 1979, Moshe Jarden came to Irvine and met [Michael D. Fried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Fried). This was during this visit that Fried suggested to write a joint book on the topics they had worked about so far, and proposed the name "Field arithmetic".

This book,[^o9] where Diophantine fields are explored through their absolute Galois groups, was influential in the domain of field theory. Owing to this work, the intense activity of Moshe Jarden, and his influence on his colleagues, "[Field Arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Arithmetic)" has become a recognized name of a branch of algebra, with its own classification number (12E30 Field Arithmetic). The impact of this book can also be measured by the number of open problems presented in the first and second editions (twenty two), which were mostly solved between the publication of the first and third editions.

In 1987, Moshe Jarden won the Landau Prize for the publication of "Field Arithmetic". Some years after the release of the first edition, it was realized that the term "field arithmetic" had already been coined by the mathematician [Paulo Ribenboim](wikipedia://en/9129242) somewhat earlier, who had published a book in French named "L'Arithmétique des Corps".

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He was awarded the Landau prize for the book "Field Arithmetic" in 1987, and the L. Meithner-A.v.Humboldt Prize in 2001 for his achievements in mathematics.
