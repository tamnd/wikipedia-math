# Harald Bergström

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harald Bergström"}

**Harald Bergström** (1 April 1908 – 23 May 2001) was a Swedish mathematician, specializing in probability theory.[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Harald Bergström studied mathematics, physics and chemistry at the [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) and received in 1931 his master's degree. From 1932 to 1934 he taught at secondary schools and then worked as a research assistant at the [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University). There he received his Ph.D. in 1938 and taught until 1945.

In 1946 he moved to [Chalmers University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalmers_University_of_Technology) in Göteborg, where in 1949 he was appointed a professor of applied mathematics. In 1960 he was also appointed a professor for numerical analysis and mathematical statistics. In 1974 he retired as professor emeritus.

At the University of Uppsala, Harald Bergström did research mainly on [algebraic number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_fields) and related topics. After his move to Chalmers University of Technology, he focused on probability theory and statistics. He was supervisor to [Peter Jagers](wikipedia://en/68355638).[^o5] He made important contributions to [central limit theorems](wikipedia://en/39406) and the theory of [alpha stable distributions](wikipedia://en/1117869). The widely used [Bergström's inequality](wikipedia://en/38128#Sedrakyan's_lemma_-_Positive_real_numbers) is named, amongst others, after him.

He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in 1936 at Oslo and in 1950 at Cambridge, Massachusetts. In 1973 Chalmers University of Technology published a book of essays in honour of his sixty-fifth birthday.[^o6]
