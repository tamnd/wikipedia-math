# Henry Priestley (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Priestley"}

**Henry James Priestley** (10 April 1883 – 26 February 1932) was the first Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Henry James Priestley was born in [Crouch Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crouch_Hill), London. He was educated at Mill Hill School and [Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge), University of Cambridge, where he gained his B.A. with honours first class (second division).[^o4] He was fifth [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) of 1905. In 1909 he received his M.A. from Cambridge.[^o5][^o8]

## Career {#Career}

After graduating from Cambridge, Priestley lectured in mathematics at the Victoria University of Manchester (now [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester)) in 1907.[^o7][^o8] He married Margery Hewitt in 1909 in London. In 1910, The [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland), in Brisbane, Australia advertised extensively, four new professor positions in the newly formed University. After Professor [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford) of the University of Manchester declined it, Priestley's application was considered and voted favourably upon.[^o9] Priestley would become Professor of mathematics and physics. He and his wife arrived in Brisbane in 1911, on the same boat as his colleague Professor [John Lundie Michie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lundie_Michie). However Priestley soon realised he couldn't teach all the necessary subjects alone, and so [Thomas Parnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Parnell_%28scientist%29), who was teaching at [the University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Melbourne), found his application to lecture in physics successful.[^o9] By 1919 the senate of the University decided to create separate departments of mathematics and physics. Priestley was offered either chair and chose mathematics.[^o4] Thomas Parnell was offered the physics chair.

In 1912 Priestley became a member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) and subsequently published papers in its proceedings and in other journals, mainly in the areas of applied mathematics and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). He believed the University would not succeed unless its academics published extensively to improve its reputation.[^o8] Priestley had considerable influence on university affairs, and despaired at the state of the buildings and library.[^o8] He served as Dean of the Faculty of Science (1911–19). He was president of the Board of Faculties (later professorial board) (1922–25) and was a member of many standing committees of the senate and board.[^o4] He was a keen member of the University Musical Society. He was also a supporter of hockey at the university, his daughter later becoming a member of the women's team.[^o10] His wife was active in the establishment of the Women's College at the University.[^o8]

A founder of the Queensland branch of the Mathematical Association of Great Britain, Priestley was its president for many years. He was President of the [Royal Society of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Queensland) in 1923. He spoke at lecture tours throughout Queensland, notably on [Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s theory of relativity.[^o4]

Priestley died on 26 February 1932 of a cerebral tumour after a long illness, survived by his wife, three sons and a daughter. His obituary was given in the newspapers by his colleague, Professor [John Lundie Michie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lundie_Michie).[^o11]

His eldest son Henry Thomas Priestley, was a key advocate for the establishment of Townsville University College (now [James Cook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook_University)) in 1960.[^o4]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

A major building at the University of Queensland bears his name.[^o8]
