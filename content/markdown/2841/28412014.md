# Voronoi pole

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Voronoi pole"}

In [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), the positive and negative **Voronoi poles** of a [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) in a [Voronoi diagram](wikipedia://en/177668) are certain vertices of the diagram, chosen in pairs in each cell of the diagram to be far from the site generating that pair. They have applications in [surface reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_reconstruction).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Voronoi diagram with poles.svg.* **Example** Here $x$ is the positive pole of $V_{p}$ and $y$ its negative. As the cell corresponding to $q$ is unbounded, only the negative pole $z$ exists.

Let $V$ be the Voronoi diagram for a set of sites $P$, and let $V_p$ be the Voronoi cell of $V$ corresponding to a site $p\in P$. If $V_p$ is bounded, then its **positive pole** is the vertex of the boundary of $V_p$ that has maximal distance to the point $p$. If the cell is unbounded, then a positive pole is not defined.

Furthermore, let $\bar{u}$ be the vector from $p$ to the positive pole, or, if the cell is unbounded, let $\bar{u}$ be a vector in the average direction of all unbounded Voronoi edges of the cell. The **negative pole** is then the Voronoi vertex $v$ in $V_p$ with the largest distance to $p$ such that the vector $\bar{u}$ and the vector from $p$ to $v$ make an angle larger than $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

:::

## History and application {#History_and_application}

The poles were introduced in 1998 in two papers by [Nina Amenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Amenta), Marshall Bern, and [Manolis Kellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolis_Kellis), for the problem of [surface reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_reconstruction). As they showed, any [smooth surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_surface) that is sampled with sampling density inversely proportional to its [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) can be accurately reconstructed, by constructing the [Delaunay triangulation](wikipedia://en/8864) of the combined set of sample points and their poles, and then removing certain triangles that are nearly parallel to the line segments between pairs of nearby poles.
