# Alison Miller

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Alison Miller"}

**Alison Beth Miller** is an American mathematician who was the first American female gold medalist at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208). She also holds the distinction of placing in the top 16 of the [Putnam Competition](wikipedia://en/378276) four times, the last three of which were recognized by the [Elizabeth Lowell Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Lowell_Putnam) award for outstanding performance by a woman on the contest.[^o4]

:::

## Early life, education and career {#Early_life,_education_and_career}

Miller was home-schooled in [Niskayuna, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niskayuna,_New_York), and in 2000 came in third place in the [Scripps Howard National Spelling Bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Howard_National_Spelling_Bee).[^o6] She competed for the U.S. in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in 2004, where she became the first American female gold medalist.[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10]

As an undergraduate, she studied mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). She won the [Elizabeth Lowell Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Lowell_Putnam) award for outstanding performance by a woman in the [Putnam Competition](wikipedia://en/378276) in 2005, 2006, and 2007,[^o11] matching the record set ten years earlier by [Ioana Dumitriu](wikipedia://en/44832995). She coached American girls participating in the [China Girls Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Girls_Mathematical_Olympiad) in 2007, the first year that the U.S. was represented in that Olympiad.[^o12][^o13]

In 2008, she became a co-winner of the [Alice T. Schafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_T._Schafer) Prize for excellence in mathematics by an undergraduate woman from the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) for her three undergraduate research papers.[^o11][^o14] That year she also received her B.A. degree with Highest Honors in Mathematics from Harvard University.[^o15] Her senior thesis, for which she won the [Hoopes Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoopes_Prize),[^o16][^o17] was titled "Explicit Class Field Theory in Function Fields: Gross-Stark Units and Drinfeld Modules." She was then awarded a [Churchill Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Scholarship) to study for a year at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in England.[^o10][^o15][^o18]

She earned her Ph.D. from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2014, under the supervision of [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088); her dissertation concerned [knot invariants](wikipedia://en/166898).[^o2] After graduation, she was a postdoctoral researcher at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) before becoming an associate editor for [Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646).[^o19]

She should not be confused with Allison N. Miller, a mathematician at [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College).[^o20]
