# Orrin Frink

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orrin Frink"}

**Orrin Frink Jr.** (31 May 1901 – 4 March 1988)[^o3] was an American mathematician who introduced [Frink ideals](wikipedia://en/21687353) in 1954.

:::

Frink earned a doctorate from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1926 or 1927[^o3] and worked on the faculty of [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) for 41 years, 11 of them as department chair.[^o3] His time at Penn State was interrupted by service as assistant chief engineer at the Special Projects Laboratory at [Wright-Patterson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright-Patterson_Air_Force_Base) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), and by two Fulbright fellowships to [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin), Ireland in the 1960s.[^o5]

[Aline Huke Frink](wikipedia://en/68040337), his wife, was also a mathematician at Penn State.[^o5] Their eldest son, also named Orrin Frink, became a professor of [Slavic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) at [Ohio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_University) and [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University). Their other children are Peter Hill Frink (1939–2021), who was an architect specializing in theaters and assembly places, John Allen Frink (b.1941), who was a senior consultant in IT for the DuPont Company, and Elizabeth Frink Boyer (b.1945), who was an art teacher and textile designer.
