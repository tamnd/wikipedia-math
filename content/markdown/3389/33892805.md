# George F. D. Duff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George F. D. Duff"}

**George Francis Denton Duff** (July 29, 1926 – March 2, 2001) was a Canadian mathematician who did research in [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and [wave](wikipedia://en/33516) phenomena. He took an interest in harnessing the extraordinarily large tides in the [Bay of Fundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Fundy) for generating electricity.

:::

While studying at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), Duff became a [Putnam fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_fellow) in 1948.[^o3] After that, Duff was a PhD student of [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He became a professor at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1952. There, he supervised the Ph.D. theses of 13 students and served as chair of the Mathematics Department from 1968 to 1975.

Duff was the president of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) from 1971 to 1973.[^o4] He was an Invited Plenary speaker at [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver) in 1974.[^o5]
