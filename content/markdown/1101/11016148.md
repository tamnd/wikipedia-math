# Martin measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin measure"}

In [descriptive set theory](wikipedia://en/396619), the **Martin measure** is a [filter](wikipedia://en/19719) on the set of [Turing degrees](wikipedia://en/764405) of sets of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474), named after [Donald A. Martin](wikipedia://en/2270985). Under the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300) it can be shown to be an [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $D$ be the set of Turing degrees of sets of natural numbers. Given some equivalence class $[X]\in D$, we may define the *cone* (or *upward cone*) of $[X]$ as the set of all Turing degrees $[Y]$ such that $X\le_T Y$;[^o4] that is, the set of Turing degrees that are "at least as complex" as $X$ under [Turing reduction](wikipedia://en/1188375). In order-theoretic terms, the cone of $[X]$ is the [upper set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_set) of $[X]$.

Assuming the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300), the **cone lemma** states that if *A* is a set of Turing degrees, either *A* includes a cone or the complement of *A* contains a cone.[^o4] It is similar to [Wadge's lemma](wikipedia://en/4575515) for Wadge degrees, and is important for the following result.

We say that a set $A$ of Turing degrees has measure 1 under the Martin measure exactly when $A$ contains some cone. Since it is possible, for any $A$, to construct a game in which player I has a winning strategy exactly when $A$ contains a cone and in which player II has a winning strategy exactly when the complement of $A$ contains a cone, the axiom of determinacy implies that the measure-1 sets of Turing degrees form an ultrafilter.

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

It is easy to show that a countable intersection of cones is itself a cone; the Martin measure is therefore a [countably complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_%28set_theory%29#Completeness) filter. This fact, combined with the fact that the Martin measure may be transferred to $\omega_1$ by a simple mapping, tells us that $\omega_1$ is [measurable](wikipedia://en/248102) under the axiom of determinacy. This result shows part of the important connection between determinacy and [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644).
