# Matrix coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matrix coefficient"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **matrix coefficient** (or **matrix element**) is a function on a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of a special form, which depends on a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of the group and additional data. Precisely, it is a function on a [compact topological group](wikipedia://en/750326) *G* obtained by [composing](wikipedia://en/195947) a representation of *G* on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *V* with a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) from the [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of *V* into *V* underlying [field](wikipedia://en/10603). It is also called a **representative function**.[^n1] They arise naturally from finite-dimensional representations of *G* as the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859)-entry functions of the corresponding matrix representations. The [Peter–Weyl theorem](wikipedia://en/294474) says that the matrix coefficients on *G* are dense in the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of square-integrable functions on *G*.

:::

Matrix coefficients of representations of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) turned out to be intimately related with the theory of [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110), providing a unifying approach to large parts of this theory. Growth properties of matrix coefficients play a key role in the classification of [irreducible representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_representations) of [locally compact groups](wikipedia://en/1651204), in particular, reductive real and [*p*-adic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic) groups. The formalism of matrix coefficients leads to a generalization of the notion of a [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000). In a different direction, [mixing](wikipedia://en/1308185) properties of certain [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) are controlled by the properties of suitable matrix coefficients.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **matrix coefficient** (or **matrix element**) of a linear representation $\rho$ of a group $G$ on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $V$ is a function $f_{v,\eta}$ on the group, of the type

$$f_{v,\eta}(g) = \eta(\rho(g)v)$$

where $v$ is a vector in $V$, $\eta$ is a continuous [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) on $V$, and $g$ is an element of $G$. This function takes scalar values on $G$. If $V$ is a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), then by the [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/26452), all matrix coefficients have the form

$$f_{v,w}(g) = \langle \rho(g)v, w \rangle$$

for some vectors $v$ and $w$ in $V$.

For $V$ of finite dimension, and $v$ and $w$ taken from a [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005), this is actually the function given by the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) entry in a fixed place.

## Applications {#Applications}

### Finite groups {#Finite_groups}

Matrix coefficients of irreducible representations of finite groups play a prominent role in representation theory of these groups, as developed by [Burnside](wikipedia://en/251883), [Frobenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Frobenius) and [Schur](wikipedia://en/617522). They satisfy [Schur orthogonality relations](wikipedia://en/7947411). The [character](wikipedia://en/691741) of a representation ρ is a sum of the matrix coefficients *f*<sub>*v*<sub>i</sub>,η<sub>i</sub></sub>, where {*v*<sub>i</sub>} form a basis in the representation space of ρ, and {η<sub>i</sub>} form the [dual basis](wikipedia://en/1182982).

### Finite-dimensional Lie groups and special functions {#Finite-dimensional_Lie_groups_and_special_functions}

Matrix coefficients of representations of Lie groups were first considered by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274). [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) realized that many classical [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) and [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) are expressible as the matrix coefficients of representation of Lie groups *G*. This description provides a uniform framework for proving many hitherto disparate properties of special functions, such as addition formulas, certain recurrence relations, orthogonality relations, integral representations, and [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) properties with respect to differential operators.[^o10] Special functions of mathematical physics, such as the [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367), the [hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/2055223) and its generalizations, [Legendre](wikipedia://en/100349) and [Jacobi](wikipedia://en/30863587) orthogonal polynomials and [Bessel functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_functions) all arise as matrix coefficients of representations of Lie groups. [Theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745) and [real analytic Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/5562599), important in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), also admit such realizations.

### Automorphic forms {#Automorphic_forms}

A powerful approach to the theory of classical [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), initiated by Gelfand, [Graev](wikipedia://en/61954293), and [Piatetski-Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piatetski-Shapiro), views them as matrix coefficients of certain infinite-dimensional unitary representations, [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation) of [adelic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelic_group). This approach was [further developed](wikipedia://en/171905) by [Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986), for general [reductive algebraic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) over [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358).
