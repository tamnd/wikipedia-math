# Quaternion-Kähler symmetric space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quaternion-Kähler symmetric space"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **quaternion-Kähler symmetric space** or **Wolf space** is a [quaternion-Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/2640559) which, as a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), is a [Riemannian symmetric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_symmetric_space). Any quaternion-Kähler symmetric space with positive Ricci curvature is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected), and is a Riemannian product of quaternion-Kähler symmetric spaces associated to compact [simple Lie groups](wikipedia://en/292831).

:::

For any compact simple [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) *G*, there is a unique *G*/*H* obtained as a quotient of *G* by a subgroup

$$H = K \cdot \mathrm{Sp}(1).\,$$

Here, Sp(1) is the compact form of the SL(2)-triple associated with the highest root of *G*, and *K* its [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) in *G*. These are classified as follows.

::: {#o2 .table cue=table}

| *G* | *H* | quaternionic dimension | geometric interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{SU}(p+2)\,$ | $\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(p) \times \mathrm{U}(2))$ | *p* | [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of complex *2*-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{C}^{p+2}$ |
| $\mathrm{SO}(p+4)\,$ | $\mathrm{SO}(p) \cdot \mathrm{SO}(4)$ | *p* | [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of oriented real *4*-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^{p+4}$ |
| $\mathrm{Sp}(p+1)\,$ | $\mathrm{Sp}(p) \cdot \mathrm{Sp}(1)$ | *p* | [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of quaternionic *1*-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{H}^{p+1}$ |
| $E_6\,$ | $\mathrm{SU}(6)\cdot\mathrm{SU}(2)$ | 10 | Space of symmetric subspaces of $(\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb O)P^2$ isometric to $(\mathbb C\otimes \mathbb H)P^2$ |
| $E_7\,$ | $\mathrm{Spin}(12)\cdot\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ | 16 | [Rosenfeld projective plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenfeld_projective_plane) $(\mathbb H\otimes\mathbb O)P^2$ over $\mathbb H\otimes\mathbb O$ |
| $E_8\,$ | $E_7\cdot\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ | 28 | Space of symmetric subspaces of $(\mathbb{O}\otimes\mathbb O)P^2$ isomorphic to $(\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb O)P^2$ |
| $F_4\,$ | $\mathrm{Sp}(3)\cdot\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ | 7 | Space of the symmetric subspaces of $\mathbb{OP}^2$ which are isomorphic to $\mathbb{HP}^2$ |
| $G_2\,$ | $\mathrm{SO}(4)\,$ | 2 | Space of the subalgebras of the [octonion algebra](wikipedia://en/51436) $\mathbb{O}$ which are isomorphic to the [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/51440) $\mathbb{H}$ |

:::

The [twistor spaces](wikipedia://en/2640559#Twistor_spaces) of quaternion-Kähler symmetric spaces are the homogeneous holomorphic [contact manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold), classified by Boothby: they are the [adjoint varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_variety) of the complex [semisimple Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group).

These spaces can be obtained by taking a [projectivization](wikipedia://en/21861125) of a minimal [nilpotent orbit](wikipedia://en/10198492) of the respective complex Lie group. The holomorphic contact structure is apparent, because the nilpotent orbits of semisimple Lie groups are equipped with the [Kirillov-Kostant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirillov-Kostant_form) holomorphic symplectic form. This argument also explains how one can associate a unique Wolf space to each of the simple complex Lie groups.
