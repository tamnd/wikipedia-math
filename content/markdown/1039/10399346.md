# Lagrange multipliers on Banach spaces

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lagrange multipliers on Banach spaces"}

In the field of [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the method of **Lagrange multipliers on Banach spaces** can be used to solve certain infinite-dimensional [constrained](wikipedia://en/7418540) [optimization problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29). The method is a generalization of the classical method of [Lagrange multipliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_multipliers) as used to find [extrema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremum) of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of finitely many variables.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Lagrange multiplier theorem for Banach spaces"}

## The Lagrange multiplier theorem for Banach spaces {#The_Lagrange_multiplier_theorem_for_Banach_spaces}

Let *X* and *Y* be [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). Let *U* be an [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358) of *X* and let *f* : *U* → **R** be a continuously [differentiable function](wikipedia://en/330206). Let *g* : *U* → *Y* be another continuously differentiable function, the *constraint*: the objective is to find the extremal points (maxima or minima) of *f* subject to the constraint that *g* is zero.

Suppose that *u*<sub>0</sub> is a *constrained extremum* of *f*, i.e. an extremum of *f* on

$$g^{-1} (0) = \{ x \in U \mid g(x) = 0 \in Y \} \subseteq U.$$

Suppose also that the [Fréchet derivative](wikipedia://en/2735727) D*g*(*u*<sub>0</sub>) : *X* → *Y* of *g* at *u*<sub>0</sub> is a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102). Then there exists a **Lagrange multiplier** *λ* : *Y* → **R** in *Y*<sup>∗</sup>, the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) to *Y*, such that

$$\mathrm{D} f (u_{0}) = \lambda \circ \mathrm{D} g (u_{0}). \quad \mbox{(L)}$$

Since D*f*(*u*<sub>0</sub>) is an element of the dual space *X*<sup>∗</sup>, equation (L) can also be written as

$$\mathrm{D} f (u_{0}) = \left( \mathrm{D} g (u_{0}) \right)^{*} (\lambda),$$

where (D*g*(*u*<sub>0</sub>))<sup>∗</sup>(*λ*) is the [pullback](wikipedia://en/9413508) of *λ* by D*g*(*u*<sub>0</sub>), i.e. the action of the [adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) map (D*g*(*u*<sub>0</sub>))<sup>∗</sup> on *λ*, as defined by

$$\left( \mathrm{D} g (u_{0}) \right)^{*} (\lambda) = \lambda \circ \mathrm{D} g (u_{0}).$$

:::

## Connection to the finite-dimensional case {#Connection_to_the_finite-dimensional_case}

In the case that *X* and *Y* are both finite-dimensional (i.e. [linearly isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_isomorphism) to **R**<sup>*m*</sup> and **R**<sup>*n*</sup> for some [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers) *m* and *n*) then writing out equation (L) in [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) form shows that *λ* is the usual Lagrange multiplier vector; in the case *n* = 1, *λ* is the usual Lagrange multiplier, a real number.

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

In many optimization problems, one seeks to minimize a functional defined on an infinite-dimensional space such as a Banach space.

Consider, for example, the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $X = H_0^1([-1,+1];\mathbb{R})$ and the functional $f : X \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ given by

$$f(u) = \int_{-1}^{+1} u'(x)^{2} \, \mathrm{d} x.$$

Without any constraint, the minimum value of *f* would be 0, attained by *u*<sub>0</sub>(*x*) = 0 for all *x* between −1 and +1. One could also consider the constrained optimization problem, to minimize *f* among all those *u* ∈ *X* such that the mean value of *u* is +1. In terms of the above theorem, the constraint *g* would be given by

$$g(u) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{-1}^{+1} u(x) \, \mathrm{d} x - 1.$$

However this problem can be solved as in the finite dimensional case since the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ is only a scalar.
