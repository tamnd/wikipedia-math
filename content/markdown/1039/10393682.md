# Hemi-icosahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hemi-icosahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hemi-icosahedron** is an [abstract regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/1211056), containing half the faces of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). It can be realized as a [projective polyhedron](wikipedia://en/26952055) (a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) by 10 triangles), which can be visualized by constructing the projective plane as a hemisphere where opposite points along the boundary are connected and dividing the hemisphere into three equal parts.

:::

## Geometry {#Geometry}

It has 10 triangular faces, 15 edges, and 6 vertices.

It is also related to the nonconvex [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223), the [tetrahemihexahedron](wikipedia://en/2851834), which could be topologically identical to the hemi-icosahedron if each of the 3 square faces were divided into two triangles.

## The complete graph K6 {#The_complete_graph_K6}

It has the same vertices and edges as the 5-dimensional [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex) which has a complete graph of edges, but only contains half of the (20) faces.

From the point of view of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) this is an embedding of $K_6$ (the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) with 6 vertices) on a [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325). With this embedding, the [dual graph](wikipedia://en/2536864) is the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) --- see [hemi-dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/10393833).
