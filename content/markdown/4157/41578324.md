# William C. Davidon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William C. Davidon"}

**William Cooper Davidon** (March 18, 1927 – November 8, 2013) was an American [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of physics and mathematics, and a peace [activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activist). As the mastermind of the March 8, 1971, [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI) office break-in, in [Media, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media,_Pennsylvania), Davidon was the informal leader of the [Citizens' Commission to Investigate the FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Commission_to_Investigate_the_FBI). The Media break-in resulted in the disclosure of [COINTELPRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COINTELPRO), which in turn led to subsequent investigations and reforms of the FBI.[^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Davidon was born in [Fort Lauderdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale), Florida, in 1927. He attended [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) and graduated from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) with a B.S. (1947), masters (1950), and Ph.D. (1954) in physics.[^o6][^o7] From 1954 to 1956, Davidon was a research associate at the [Enrico Fermi Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi_Institute). From 1956 to 1961, he was an associate physicist at the [Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory), where he developed the first [quasi-Newton algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-Newton_algorithm),[^o8] now known as the [Davidon–Fletcher–Powell formula](wikipedia://en/11231265).

Davidon was professor of physics at [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College) from 1961 to 1981, and then Professor of Mathematics (1981–1991), as his interests shifted to include [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis).[^o9] Davidon was a 1966 [Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Scholar).[^o10] He retired in 1991.

Davidon moved to [Highlands Ranch, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlands_Ranch,_Colorado), in 2010. He died November 8, 2013, of Parkinson's disease.[^o11]

## Activism {#Activism}

In 1966, Davidon traveled to [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam), with [A. J. Muste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Muste), sponsored by the [Committee for Non-Violent Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_Non-Violent_Action). He also announced that year that he would be refusing to pay his federal income tax in protest against the Vietnam War.[^o13] Later, he became a sponsor of the War Tax Resistance project, which practiced and advocated [tax resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_resistance) as a form of anti-war protest.[^o14] In 1971, he was named an "unindicted co-conspirator" in the [Harrisburg Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg_Seven) case. During much of this time, he served on the board of directors of the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union), Philadelphia affiliate.[^o11]

As the leader of the [Citizens' Commission to Investigate the FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Commission_to_Investigate_the_FBI), Davidon was instrumental in planning and organizing a break-in of the FBI's [Media, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media,_Pennsylvania) office. The documents stolen there led to the disclosure of [COINTELPRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COINTELPRO).[^o15] According to *The Burglary*,[^o16] a book published shortly after his death, Davidon also had engaged in [draft board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_board) raids, stealing or destroying files, and subsequent to Media participated in two acts of [sabotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabotage) against military materiel intended for use in [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War). Due at least in part to his exceptionally careful planning and his co-conspirators' total commitment to secrecy and discretion, neither he nor anyone else was ever charged in any of those actions, despite an intense, five-year FBI investigation.

## Family {#Family}

In 1963, Davidon married [Ann Morrissett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Morrissett) (1925–2004), a noted pacifist/feminist essayist and activist. They had two daughters [Sarah Davidon](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sarahdavidon/) and [Ruth Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Davidon). Davidon and Morrissett divorced in 1978.[^o10] He subsequently married Maxine Libros,[^o11][^o18] who died in 2010. Davidon had a son, Alan (1949– ), from his first marriage to Phyllis Wise (1927– ).[^o19] Davidon also had a child with Zuzana (Suzanne) Libich, née Freund (b. 1928, [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) – d. 2006, [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich)), an interpreter and peace activist during the 1968 [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring). They met at the [Pugwash Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pugwash_Conference_on_Science_and_World_Affairs) in [Marienbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienbad) in 1969. Aimée (b. 1970) is an artist and engineer in [Los Angeles, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California).
