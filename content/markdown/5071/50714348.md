# Local twistor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local twistor"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **local twistor bundle** is a specific [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) with [connection](wikipedia://en/391816) that can be associated to any [conformal manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_manifold), at least locally. Intuitively, a local twistor is an association of a [twistor space](wikipedia://en/2688710) to each point of space-time, together with a conformally invariant connection that relates the twistor spaces at different points. This connection can have [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) that obstructs the existence of "global" twistors (that is, solutions of the twistor equation in open sets).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Let *M* be a pseudo-Riemannian conformal manifold with a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) and a conformal metric of signature (*p*,*q*). The conformal group is the [pseudo-orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-orthogonal_group) $SO(p+1,q+1)$. There is a [conformal Cartan connection](wikipedia://en/412272) on a bundle, the [tractor bundle](wikipedia://en/5803388), of *M*. The [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) of $SO(p+1,q+1)$ admits a fundamental representation, the [spin representation](wikipedia://en/10160091), and the [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874) is the local twistor bundle.

:::

## Representation via Weyl spinors {#Representation_via_Weyl_spinors}

Local twistors can be represented as pairs of [Weyl spinors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_spinor) on *M* (in general from different spin representations, determined by the reality conditions specific to the signature). In the case of a four-dimensional [Lorentzian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold), such as the space-time of general relativity, a local twistor has the form

$$Z^\alpha = \begin{bmatrix}\omega^A\\\pi_{A'}\end{bmatrix}.$$

Here we use index conventions from (Penrose Rindler 1986), and $\omega^A$ and $\pi_{A'}$ are two-component complex spinors for the Lorentz group $SL(2,\mathbb C)$.

## Local twistor transport {#Local_twistor_transport}

The connection, sometimes called *local twistor transport*, is given by

$$dZ^\alpha = \begin{bmatrix}d\omega^A - i\theta^{AA'}\pi_{A'}\\ d\pi_{A'}-iP_{AA'}\omega^A\end{bmatrix}.$$

Here $\theta^{AA'}$ is the [canonical one-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_one-form) and $P_{AA'}$ the [Schouten tensor](wikipedia://en/6129873), contracted on one index with the canonical one-form. An analogous equation holds in other dimensions, with appropriate [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) multipliers between the two Weyl spin representations (Sparling 1986). In this formalism, the [twistor equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twistor_equation) is the requirement that a local twistor be parallel under the connection.

## Canonical filtration {#Canonical_filtration}

In general, the local twistor bundle *T* is equipped with a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of vector bundles

$$0\to \Pi\to T\to \Omega\to 0$$

where $\Pi$ and $\Omega$ are two Weyl spin bundles. The bundle $\Pi$ is a distinguished sub-bundle, that corresponds to the marked point of contact of the conformal Cartan connection. That is, there is a canonical marked one-dimensional subspace *X* in the tractor bundle, and $\Pi$ is the annihilator of *X* under Clifford multipliction. In four dimensions, $\Pi$ is the space of spinors $\pi_{A'}$ and $\Omega$ the space of $\omega^A$. Under the [Plücker embedding](wikipedia://en/1190270), the tractor bundle in four dimensions is isomorphic to the [exterior square](wikipedia://en/221537) of the local twistor bundle, and $\Pi$ consists of all the twistors incident with

$$X^{\alpha\beta}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\ 0&\epsilon_{A'B'}\end{bmatrix}$$

where $\epsilon_{A'B'}$ is the symplectic form on $\Pi$.

## Curvature {#Curvature}

The curvature of the local twistor connection involves both the [Weyl curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_curvature) and the [Cotton tensor](wikipedia://en/1074140). (It is the Cartan conformal curvature.) The curvature preserves the space $\Pi$, and on $\Pi$ it involves only the conformally-invariant Weyl curvature.
