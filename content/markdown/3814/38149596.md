# Désiré André

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Désiré André"}

*Image: Désiré André.jpg.* Désiré André, French mathematician (1840–1918)

**Désiré André** (André Antoine Désiré) (March 29, 1840, Lyon – September 12, 1917, Paris) was a French mathematician, best known for his work on [Catalan numbers](wikipedia://en/216488) and [alternating permutations](wikipedia://en/4813617).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He is the son of Auguste Antoine Désiré André, shoemaker in Lyon, and his wife Antoinette Magdalene Jar.

He entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1860 and passed the [Agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in Mathematics in 1863. He defended his doctoral thesis on 25 March 1877.

He was a student of [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) (1822–1901) and [Joseph Bertrand](wikipedia://en/488196) (1822–1900).

Starting as a teacher at the Lycée de Troyes, he went on to [Collège Sainte-Barbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_Sainte-Barbe), then to the [University of Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Burgundy) and finally became professor of mathematics at [Collège Stanislas de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_Stanislas_de_Paris) from 1885 to 1900.

He was a laureate of the Ministère de l'Instruction Publique, member of the [Circolo Matematico di Palermo](wikipedia://en/32146789) and the Commission Internationale Permanente de Bibliographie Mathématique. He was made a Knight of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour) in July 1897.[^o3]

Désiré André played a role in the French mathematical community of his time, including the [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France). He was the treasurer of the association from its inception in 1873 to 1876. He became chairman of the Société in 1889 or 1890.

He was the author of a 1909 book on notation in elementary mathematics, *Des notations mathématiques, énumération, choix et usage*.

He was president of the Société philomathique de Paris.

He lived at 28 rue Vauquelin, Paris.
