# Lisa Orloff Clark

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lisa Orloff Clark"}

**Lisa Orloff Clark** is a New Zealand mathematician, and as of 2023 is a full professor at [Victoria University of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Wellington) and head of the School of Mathematics and Statistics. She works in the field of algebra and also on inquiry-based learning in mathematics education.

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Clark completed a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) titled *Classifying the Type of Groupoid C\*-algebras* at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) in 2004.[^o4] Clark then joined the faculty of the Victoria University of Wellington, rising to full professor in 2023.[^o5] She has published on [C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/7184), [groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543), Steinberg algebras and [Leavitt path algebras](wikipedia://en/64956453).

Clark is the Head of the School of Mathematics and Statistics at Victoria University of Wellington.[^o6] She is on the editorial board of the *Journal of Inquiry-Based Learning in Mathematics*.[^o7]

In 2018 Clark was an associate investigator on a [Marsden grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsden_grant) led by [Astrid an Huef](wikipedia://en/53960754) and Iain Raeburn, titled *Through the looking glass: sharpening the classification program through implications for operator algebras of graphs and groupoids*.[^o8] In 2021 Clark and an Huef were joint principal investigators on another successful Marsden grant, called *Establishing a structure theory for C\*-algebras of non-Hausdorff groupoids*.[^o9][^o5]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

In 2017 Clark was awarded the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society)'s top prize, the Kalman Prize for best paper.[^o5][^o11] She was a plenary speaker at the NZMS colloquium in 2019.[^o12]

In 2022 Clark was awarded the [Aitken Lectureship](wikipedia://en/75557217), a joint award between the New Zealand and London Mathematical Societies. Clark gave lectures at [Queen's University Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_Belfast), [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University), [Aberystwyth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University) and at the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) annual meeting.[^o13]
