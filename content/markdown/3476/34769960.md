# Exportation (logic)

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Exportation"}

**Exportation.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Exportation"}

**Exportation**[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6] is a [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [rule of replacement](wikipedia://en/34770456) in [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154). The rule allows [conditional statements](wikipedia://en/658808) having [conjunctive](wikipedia://en/18152) [antecedents](wikipedia://en/804755) to be replaced by statements having conditional [consequents](wikipedia://en/804778) and vice versa in [logical proofs](wikipedia://en/1197184). It is the rule that:

:::

$$((P \land Q) \to R) \Leftrightarrow (P \to (Q \to R))$$

Where "$\Leftrightarrow$" is a [metalogical](wikipedia://en/387934) [symbol](wikipedia://en/24028642) representing "can be replaced in a proof with." In strict terminology, $((P \land Q) \to R) \Rightarrow (P \to (Q \to R))$ is the law of exportation, for it "exports" a proposition from the antecedent of $(P \land Q) \to R$ to its consequent. Its converse, the law of **importation**, $(P \to (Q \to R))\Rightarrow ((P \land Q) \to R)$, "imports" a proposition from the consequent of $P \to (Q \to R)$ to its antecedent.

## Formal notation {#Formal_notation}

The *exportation* rule may be written in [sequent](wikipedia://en/252355) notation:

$$((P \land Q) \to R) \dashv\vdash (P \to (Q \to R))$$

where $\dashv\vdash$ is a metalogical symbol meaning that $(P \to (Q \to R))$ is a [syntactic equivalent](wikipedia://en/169324) of $((P \land Q) \to R)$ in some [logical system](wikipedia://en/396102);

or in [rule form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference_rule):

$\frac{(P \land Q) \to R}{P \to (Q \to R)}$, $\frac{P \to (Q \to R)}{(P \land Q) \to R}.$

where the rule is that wherever an instance of "$(P \land Q) \to R$" appears on a line of a proof, it can be replaced with "$P \to (Q \to R)$", and vice versa.

[Import-export](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import-export_%28logic%29) is a name given to the statement as a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) or truth-functional [tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335) of propositional logic:

$$((P \land Q) \to R) \leftrightarrow (P \to (Q \to R))$$

where $P$, $Q$, and $R$ are propositions expressed in some [logical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_system).

## Natural language {#Natural_language}

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

It rains and the sun shines implies that there is a rainbow. Thus, if it rains, then the sun shines implies that there is a rainbow.

If my car is on, when I switch the gear to D the car starts going. If my car is on and I have switched the gear to D, then the car must start going.

:::

## Proof {#Proof}

The following proof uses a [classically](wikipedia://en/55868) valid chain of equivalences. Rules used are [material implication](wikipedia://en/244516), [De Morgan's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Morgan's_law), and the [associative property](wikipedia://en/1335) of [disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjunction_%28logic%29).

::: {#o13 .table cue=table}

| *Proposition* | *Derivation* |
|---|---|
| $P\rightarrow (Q\rightarrow R)$ | Given |
| $\neg P \lor (Q \rightarrow R)$ | [material implication](wikipedia://en/244516) |
| $\neg P \lor (\neg Q \lor R)$ | material implication |
| $(\neg P \lor \neg Q) \lor R$ | [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity) |
| $\neg (P \land Q) \lor R$ | [De Morgan's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Morgan's_law) |
| $(P \land Q) \rightarrow R$ | material implication |

:::

Due to the use of [material implication](wikipedia://en/244516) in the first two steps, this is not an intuitionistically valid proof.

:::

## Relation to functions {#Relation_to_functions}

Exportation is associated with [currying](wikipedia://en/6600) via the [Curry–Howard correspondence](wikipedia://en/254299).
