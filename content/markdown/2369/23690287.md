::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal crystal family"}

In [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), the **hexagonal crystal family** is one of the six [crystal families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_family), which includes two crystal systems (hexagonal and **trigonal**) and two lattice systems (hexagonal and **rhombohedral**). While commonly confused, the trigonal crystal system and the rhombohedral lattice system are not equivalent (see section [crystal systems](wikipedia://en/23690287#Crystal_systems) below).[^o3] In particular, there are crystals that have trigonal symmetry but belong to the hexagonal lattice (such as α-[quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz)).

:::

The hexagonal crystal family consists of the 12 point groups such that at least one of their space groups has the hexagonal lattice as underlying lattice, and is the union of the hexagonal crystal system and the trigonal crystal system.[^o4] There are 52 space groups associated with it, which are exactly those whose [Bravais lattice](wikipedia://en/661808) is either hexagonal or rhombohedral.

## Lattice systems {#Lattice_systems}

The hexagonal crystal family consists of two [lattice systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_system): hexagonal and rhombohedral. Each lattice system consists of one [Bravais lattice](wikipedia://en/661808).

In the hexagonal family, the crystal is conventionally described by a right [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) unit cell with two equal axes (*a* by *a*), an included angle of 120° (*γ*) and a height (*c*, which can be different from *a*) perpendicular to the two base axes.

The hexagonal unit cell for the rhombohedral Bravais lattice is the R-centered cell, consisting of two additional lattice points which occupy one body diagonal of the unit cell. There are two ways to do this, which can be thought of as two notations which represent the same structure. In the usual so-called obverse setting, the additional lattice points are at coordinates (, , ) and (, , ), whereas in the alternative reverse setting they are at the coordinates (,,) and (,,).[^o10] In either case, there are 3 lattice points per unit cell in total and the lattice is non-primitive.

The Bravais lattices in the hexagonal crystal family can also be described by rhombohedral axes.[^o11] The unit cell is a [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406) (which gives the name for the rhombohedral lattice). This is a unit cell with parameters *a* = *b* = *c*; *α* = *β* = *γ* ≠ 90°.[^o12] In practice, the hexagonal description is more commonly used because it is easier to deal with a coordinate system with two 90° angles. However, the rhombohedral axes are often shown (for the rhombohedral lattice) in textbooks because this cell reveals the *$\overline{3}$m* symmetry of the crystal lattice.

The rhombohedral unit cell for the hexagonal Bravais lattice is the D-centered cell, consisting of two additional lattice points which occupy one body diagonal of the unit cell with coordinates (, , ) and (, , ). However, such a description is rarely used.

The hexagonal crystal family consists of two [crystal systems](wikipedia://en/456410): trigonal and hexagonal. A crystal system is a set of [point groups](wikipedia://en/1138942) in which the point groups themselves and their corresponding [space groups](wikipedia://en/463721) are assigned to a [lattice system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_system) (see table in [Crystal system#Crystal classes](wikipedia://en/456410#Crystal_classes)).

The trigonal crystal system consists of the 5 point groups that have a single three-fold rotation axis, which includes space groups 143 to 167. These 5 point groups have 7 corresponding space groups (denoted by R) assigned to the rhombohedral lattice system and 18 corresponding space groups (denoted by P) assigned to the hexagonal lattice system. Hence, the trigonal crystal system is the only crystal system whose point groups have more than one lattice system associated with their space groups.

The hexagonal crystal system consists of the 7 point groups that have a single six-fold rotation axis. These 7 point groups have 27 space groups (168 to 194), all of which are assigned to the hexagonal lattice system.

## Hexagonal close packed {#Hexagonal_close_packed}

*Image: Hexagonal close packed.svg.* Hexagonal close packed (hcp) unit cell

Hexagonal close packed (hcp) is one of the two simple types of atomic packing with the highest density, the other being the face-centered cubic (fcc). However, unlike the fcc, it is not a Bravais lattice, as there are two nonequivalent sets of lattice points. Instead, it can be constructed from the hexagonal Bravais lattice by using a two-atom motif (the additional atom is at about (, , )) associated with each lattice point.[^o27]

## Multi-element structures {#Multi-element_structures}

Compounds that consist of more than one element (e.g. [binary compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_compound)) often have crystal structures based on the hexagonal crystal family. Some of the more common ones are listed here. These structures can be viewed as two or more interpenetrating sublattices where each sublattice occupies the [interstitial sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitial_site) of the others.

::: {#o30 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Wurtzite (crystal structure)"}

### Wurtzite structure {#Wurtzite_structure}

Wurtzite structure type

*Image: Wurtzite cellGIF.gif.* Wurtzite unit cell as described by symmetry operators of the space group.[^o31]

*Image: Wurtzite-unit-cell-3D-balls.png.* Another representation of the wurtzite [unit cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_cell)[^o32]

*Image: Wurtzite polyhedra.png.* Another representation of the wurtzite structure[^o33]

The wurtzite crystal structure is referred to by the [Strukturbericht designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strukturbericht_designation) B4 and the [Pearson symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_symbol) hP4. The corresponding [space group](wikipedia://en/463721#Notation) is [No. 186](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_space_groups) (in International Union of Crystallography classification) or **P6<sub>3</sub>mc** (in [Hermann–Mauguin notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann–Mauguin_notation)). The Hermann-Mauguin symbols in **P6<sub>3</sub>mc** can be read as follows:[^o34]

- **6<sub>3</sub>..** : a six fold screw rotation around the c-axis
- **.m.** : a mirror plane with normal {100}
- **..c** : glide plane in the **c**-directions with normal {120}.

Among the compounds that can take the wurtzite structure are [wurtzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurtzite) itself ([ZnS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZnS) with up to 8% [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) instead of [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc)), [silver iodide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_iodide) (AgI), [zinc oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_oxide) (ZnO), [cadmium sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium_sulfide) (CdS), [cadmium selenide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium_selenide) (CdSe), [silicon carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_carbide) (α-SiC), [gallium nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallium_nitride) (GaN), [aluminium nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_nitride) (AlN), [boron nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boron_nitride#Wurtzite_form_%28w-BN%29) (w-BN) and other [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductors).[^o35] In most of these compounds, wurtzite is not the favored form of the bulk crystal, but the structure can be favored in some nanocrystal forms of the material.

In materials with more than one crystal structure, the prefix "w-" is sometimes added to the [empirical formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_formula) to denote the wurtzite crystal structure, as in [w-BN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W-BN).

Each of the two individual atom types forms a sublattice which is [hexagonal close-packed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_close-packed) (HCP-type). When viewed all together, the atomic positions are the same as in [lonsdaleite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonsdaleite) (hexagonal [diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond)). Each atom is [tetrahedrally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_molecular_geometry) coordinated. The structure can also be described as an HCP lattice of zinc with sulfur atoms occupying half of the [tetrahedral voids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitial_site) or vice versa.

The wurtzite structure is [non-centrosymmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrosymmetry) (i.e., lacks [inversion symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inversion_symmetry)). Due to this, wurtzite crystals, such as GaN, InN, and AlN, can have properties such as [piezoelectricity](wikipedia://en/24975) and [pyroelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroelectricity), which centrosymmetric crystals lack.[^o36][^o37] The polar nature of these crystals enables electronic devices such as the high electron mobility transistors (HEMT).[^o38]

:::

### Nickel arsenide structure {#Nickel_arsenide_structure}

Nickel arsenide structure type The nickel arsenide structure consists of two interpenetrating sublattices: a primitive hexagonal nickel sublattice and a [hexagonal close-packed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_close-packed) arsenic sublattice. Each nickel atom is [octahedrally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_molecular_geometry) [coordinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_number) to six arsenic atoms, while each arsenic atom is [trigonal prismatically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_prismatic_molecular_geometry) coordinated to six nickel atoms.[^o40] The structure can also be described as an HCP lattice of arsenic with nickel occupying each [octahedral void](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitial_site).

Compounds adopting the NiAs structure are generally the [chalcogenides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcogen), [arsenides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenide), [antimonides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony) and [bismuthides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismuth) of [transition metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_metal).[^o41]

*Image: Nickel-arsenide-3D-unit-cell.png.* The unit cell of nickeline

The following are the members of the nickeline group:[^o42]

- [Achavalite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achavalite): FeSe
- [Breithauptite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breithauptite): NiSb
- Freboldite: CoSe
- Kotulskite: Pd(Te,Bi)
- Langistite: (Co,Ni)As
- Nickeline: NiAs
- Sobolevskite: Pd(Bi,Te)
- Sudburyite: (Pd,Ni)Sb
