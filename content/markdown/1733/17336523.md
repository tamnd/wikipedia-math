# Descriptive interpretation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Descriptive interpretation"}

According to [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987), in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), an interpretation is a **descriptive interpretation** (also called a **factual interpretation**) if at least one of the undefined symbols of its [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102) becomes, in the interpretation, a *[descriptive sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_sign)* (i.e., the name of single objects, or observable properties).[^o2] In his *Introduction to Semantics* (Harvard Uni. Press, 1942) he makes a distinction between formal interpretations which are *[logical interpretations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_interpretation)* (also called *mathematical interpretation* or *logico-mathematical interpretation*) and *descriptive interpretations*: a formal interpretation is a *descriptive interpretation* if it is not a *logical interpretation*.[^o2]

:::

Attempts to [axiomatize](wikipedia://en/188401) the [empirical sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_science), Carnap said, use a *descriptive interpretation* to model reality.:[^o2] the aim of these attempts is to construct a [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102) for which [reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality) is the only interpretation.[^o3] - the world is an interpretation (or model) of these sciences, only insofar as these sciences are true.[^o3]

Any non-empty set may be chosen as the domain of a descriptive interpretation, and all n-ary relations among the elements of the domain are candidates for assignment to any predicate of degree n.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A sentence is either *true* or *false* under an *interpretation* which assigns values to the logical variables. We might for example make the following assignments:

**Individual constants**

- a: Socrates
- b: Plato
- c: Aristotle

**Predicates**:

- Fα: α is sleeping
- Gαβ: α hates β
- Hαβγ: α made β hit γ

**Sentential variables:**

- *p* "It is raining."

Under this interpretation the sentences discussed above would represent the following English statements:

- *p*: "It is raining."
- *F*(*a*): "Socrates is sleeping."
- *H*(*b*,*a*,*c*): "Plato made Socrates hit Aristotle."
- *x*(*F*(*x*)): "Everybody is sleeping."
- *z*(*G*(*a*,*z*)): "Socrates hates somebody."
- *x**y**z*(*H*(*x*,*y*,*z*)): "Somebody made everybody hit somebody."
- *x**z*(*F*(*x*)*G*(*a*,*z*)): Everybody is sleeping and Socrates hates somebody.
- *x**y**z* (*G*(*a*,*z*)*H*(*x*,*y*,*z*)): Either Socrates hates somebody or somebody made everybody hit somebody.
