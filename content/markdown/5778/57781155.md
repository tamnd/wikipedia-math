# Skew-merged permutation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Skew-merged permutation"}

In the theory of [permutation patterns](wikipedia://en/24360911), a **skew-merged permutation** is a [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) that can be partitioned into an increasing sequence and a decreasing sequence. They were first studied by (Stankova 1994) and given their name by (Atkinson 1998).[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

The two smallest permutations that cannot be partitioned into an increasing and a decreasing sequence are 3412 and 2143. (Stankova 1994) was the first to establish that a skew-merged permutation can also be equivalently defined as a permutation that avoids the two patterns 3412 and 2143.[^n1]

A permutation is skew-merged if and only if its associated [permutation graph](wikipedia://en/17625375) is a [split graph](wikipedia://en/9928681), a graph that can be partitioned into a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) (corresponding to the descending subsequence) and an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) (corresponding to the ascending subsequence). The two forbidden patterns for skew-merged permutations, 3412 and 2143, correspond to two of the three forbidden [induced subgraphs](wikipedia://en/1655188) for split graphs, a four-vertex cycle and a graph with two disjoint edges, respectively. The third forbidden induced subgraph, a five-vertex cycle, cannot exist in a permutation graph.[^n2]

:::

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Enumeration"}

## Enumeration {#Enumeration}

For $n=1,2,3,\dots$ the number of skew-merged permutations of length $n$ is

1, 2, 6, 22, 86, 340, 1340, 5254, 20518, 79932, 311028, 1209916, 4707964, 18330728, ... .

(Atkinson 1998) was the first to show that the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of these numbers is

$$\frac{1-3x}{(1-2x)\sqrt{1-4x}},$$

from which it follows that the number of skew-merged permutations of length $n$ is given by the formula

$$\binom{2n}{n}-\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}2^{n-m-1}\binom{2m}{m}$$

and that these numbers obey the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$P_n=\frac{(9n-8)P_{n-1} - (26n-46)P_{n-2} + (24n-60)P_{n-3}}{n}.$$

Another derivation of the generating function for skew-merged permutations was given by (Albert Vatter 2013).[^n3]

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Testing whether one permutation is a pattern in another can be solved efficiently when the larger of the two permutations is skew-merged, as shown by (Albert Lackner Lackner Vatter 2016).[^n4]
