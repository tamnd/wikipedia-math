# Valentina Escott-Price

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Valentina Escott-Price"}

**Valentina Escott-Price** is a Russian statistician and professor who specialises in biostatistics and genetic epidemiology. Escott-Price is a Professor at the MRC Centre for Neuro‑Psychiatric Genetics and Genomics at [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University). Her research focuses on using advances in technology and large‑scale datasets to identify genetic risk factors and biological pathways involved in complex disorders, and on applying integrative analytical approaches to add biological insight to genetic findings.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Escott-Price is from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^o4] She studied mathematics at [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) in 1991.[^o5][^o6] Escott-Price completed in her doctorate in statistics at the School of Mathematics at [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University).[^o5][^o6] After completing her thesis, she developed a database for a [schizophrenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophrenia) research team. During her spare time she volunteered to do some genetic data analysis.[^o4] Her early work focused on schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and depression.[^o4]

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

Escott-Price began her academic career as a Research Assistant in the School of Mathematics at [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University). In 2003, she was appointed Lecturer in Biostatistics and Genetic Epidemiology in the Bioinformatics and Biostatistics Unit at the School of Medicine. She was awarded a RCUK Research Fellowship in 2005.[^o6] She joined the MRC Centre for Neuro‑Psychiatric Genetics and Genomics in 2010. In 2016, Escott-Price was promoted to Professor.[^o5]

Escott-Price focuses on the use of large‑scale genomic datasets and modern analytical methods to identify genetic risk factors and biological pathways involved in psychiatric and neurological disorders.[^o8] She analyses large genome‑wide, exome and sequencing datasets from cohorts comprising tens of thousands of individuals.[^o5] Advances in computational methods have created opportunities for applying [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488)[^o9] to improve risk prediction and gene discovery, including through federated learning and multi‑omic integration.[^o9]

As a member of the International Genomics of Alzheimer's Project, Escott-Price identified 11 new susceptibility loci for [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease) and two additional Alzheimer's‑associated genes. She examines the genetic architecture of neurodegenerative diseases, including the evaluation of polygenic risk for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease and the relationship between individual polygenic risk scores and disease susceptibility.[^o5]
