# Jacques Dixmier

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Jacques Dixmier"}

**Jacques Dixmier** (born 24 May 1924) is a French mathematician. He worked on [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987), especially [C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/7184), and wrote several of the standard reference books on them, and introduced the [Dixmier trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixmier_trace) and the [Dixmier mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixmier_mapping).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Dixmier received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1949 from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), and his students include [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340).[^o2]

In 1949 upon the initiative of [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and [Pierre Samuel](wikipedia://en/2604194), Dixmier became a member of [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394), in which he made essential contributions to the Bourbaki volume on [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).[^o6] After retiring as professor emeritus from the [University of Paris VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VI), he spent five years at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques).

Often, there is made the erroneous claim that Dixmier originated the name *[von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185)* for the operator algebras introduced by [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), but Dixmier said in an interview that the name originated from a proposal by [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084).[^o6]

Dixmier was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in 1966 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) with the talk *Espace dual d'une algèbre, ou d'un groupe localement compact* and again in 1978 in Helsinki with the talk *Algèbres enveloppantes*.
