# L. H. C. Tippett

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="L. H. C. Tippett"}

**Leonard Henry Caleb Tippett** (8 May 1902 – 9 November 1985), known professionally as **L. H. C. Tippett**, was an English [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661).

:::

Tippett was born in London but spent most of his early life in [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall) and attended [St Austell County Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltair_School), where his contemporaries included the historian [A. L. Rowse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._L._Rowse). Tippett graduated in physics in the early 1920s from [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London). He studied for his [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in statistics under [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695) at the [Galton Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton_Laboratory), [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) and [R. A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher) at [Rothamsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station). He spent his entire career, 1925 to 1965, on the staff of the [Shirley Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Institute), [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester) becoming in 1952 one of the first assistant directors. Along with [R.A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.A._Fisher) and [Emil Gumbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Gumbel), he pioneered [extreme value theory](wikipedia://en/50732). The [Fisher–Tippett distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Tippett_distribution) is named after him.

At the Shirley Institute he applied statistics to the problem of [yarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn) breakage rates in [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving). In the late 1920s and 1930s, he became known for his 'snap-reading' method of observation which led to improved production efficiency and operative utilization. As a result of his work in the textile industry, he was awarded the Shewart Medal of the [American Society for Quality Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality_Control).

Tippett published "Random Sampling Numbers" in 1927 and thus invented the [random number table](wikipedia://en/2714849).

In 1965 he retired to [St Austell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Austell), [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall) and in this period became an [UNIDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIDO) consultant, being active in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India). He died in 1985 after being hit by a van whilst walking from his home to the St. Austell Choral Society to sing in the [St. Matthew Passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Matthew_Passion).

## Awards {#Awards}

- Warner Medal of the [Textile Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_Institute)
- [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association), 1950[^o4]
- [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Silver of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664), 1954
- Honorary MSc [UMIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMIST)
- President of the [Manchester Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/15199159)
- President of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664), 1965
- Shewart Medal of the [American Society for Quality Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality_Control)
