# Arthur F. Veinott

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur F. Veinott"}

**Arthur Fales Veinott Jr.** (October 12, 1934 – December 12, 2012), also known as **Pete Veinott**[^o3] was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) operations researcher and academic.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in [Boston, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston,_Massachusetts) on October 12, 1934.[^o5]

He earned his Bachelors of Arts and Science degrees from [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University) in 1956. He studied at Columbia University and earned a Doctorate of Science in 1960, under the supervision of [Cyrus Derman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Derman).[^o5]

He died on December 12, 2012.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

He first arrived at Stanford in 1962 and served until 2009.[^o7] During his career, he played a key role in the creation and development of the Department of Operations Research, including serving as department chair from 1975 to 1985.[^o7]

He founded the journal [Mathematics of Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_Operations_Research).[^o8]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

- He received the INFORMS [John von Neumann Theory Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Theory_Prize) in 2007.[^o8]
- He was made an inaugural INFORMS Fellow in 2002.[^o8]
- He was elected to the National Academy of Engineering in 1986.[^o10]
