# Fence (mathematics)

*Image: Zigzag poset.svg.* The [Hasse diagram](wikipedia://en/338199) of a six-element fence.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **fence**, also called a **zigzag poset**, is a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) (poset) in which the order [relations](wikipedia://en/22517558) form a path with alternating orientations:

:::

$$a<b>c<d>e<f>h<i \cdots$$

or

$$a>b<c>d<e>f<h>i \cdots$$

A fence may be [finite](wikipedia://en/11742), or it may be formed by an infinite alternating sequence extending in both directions. The [incidence posets](wikipedia://en/44460457) of [path graphs](wikipedia://en/4587190) form examples of fences.

A [linear extension](wikipedia://en/11726298) of a fence is called an [alternating permutation](wikipedia://en/4813617); [André's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André's_problem) of counting the number of different linear extensions has been studied since the 19th century.[^o2] The solutions to this counting problem, the so-called Euler zigzag numbers or up/down numbers, are:

$$1, 1, 2, 4, 10, 32, 122, 544, 2770, 15872, 101042.$$

.

The number of [antichains](wikipedia://en/348973) in a fence is a [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number); the [distributive lattice](wikipedia://en/310883) with this many elements, generated from a fence via [Birkhoff's representation theorem](wikipedia://en/20489385), has as its graph the [Fibonacci cube](wikipedia://en/21616365).[^o3]

A partially ordered set is [series-parallel](wikipedia://en/28826384) if and only if it does not have four elements forming a fence.[^o4]

Several authors have also investigated the number of [order-preserving maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-preserving_map) from fences to themselves, or to fences of other sizes.[^o5]

An **up-down poset** $Q(a,b)$ is a generalization of a zigzag poset in which there are $a$ downward orientations for every upward one and $b$ total elements.[^o6] For instance, $Q(2,9)$ has the elements and relations

$$a>b>c<d>e>f<g>h>i.$$

In this notation, a fence is a partially ordered set of the form $Q(1,n)$.
