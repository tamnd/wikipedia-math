# Eberhard Hopf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eberhard Hopf"}

**Eberhard Frederich Ferdinand Hopf** (April 4, 1902, in [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) – July 24, 1983, in [Bloomington, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Indiana), USA) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), one of the founding fathers of [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) and a pioneer of [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042) who also made significant contributions to the subjects of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234), [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). The [Hopf maximum principle](wikipedia://en/15912991) is an early result of his (1927) that is one of the most important techniques in the theory of [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hopf was born in Salzburg, Austria-Hungary, but his scientific career was divided between Germany and the United States. He received his Ph.D. in mathematics in 1926 and his *[Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation)* in mathematical astronomy from the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin) in 1929.

In 1971, Hopf was the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) [Gibbs](wikipedia://en/37332) Lecturer.[^o4] In 1981, he received the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) from the American Mathematical Society for seminal contributions to research.
