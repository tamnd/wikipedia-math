# Axial symmetry

*Image: Surface of revolution illustration.png.* A [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) has axial symmetry around an axis in 3-dimensions.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Axial symmetry"}

**Axial symmetry** is [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) around an axis or [line (geometry)](wikipedia://en/946975). An object is said to be *axially symmetric* if its appearance is unchanged if [transformed](wikipedia://en/43194879) around an axis. The main types of axial symmetry are *[reflection symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873)* and *[rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096)* (including [circular symmetry](wikipedia://en/2217599) for plane figures and *[cylindrical symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_symmetry)* for surfaces of revolution).[^o2] For example, a [baseball bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_bat) (without trademark or other design), or a plain white [tea saucer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saucer), looks the same if it is rotated by any angle about the line passing lengthwise through its center, so it is axially symmetric.

:::

Axial symmetry can also be [discrete](wikipedia://en/1372580) with a fixed angle of rotation, 360°/*n* for *n*-fold symmetry.

*Image: Pentagonal\_prism.png.* Discrete axial symmetry, order 5, in a [pentagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859659)
