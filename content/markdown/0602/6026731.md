# Lexicographic product of graphs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lexicographic product of graphs"}

*Image: Graph-lexicographic-product.svg.* The lexicographic product of graphs.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **lexicographic product** or **(graph) composition** $G \bullet H$ of graphs $G$ and $H$ is a graph such that

:::

- the vertex set of $G \bullet H$ is the [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $V(G) \times V(H)$; and
- any two vertices $(u,v)$ and $(x,y)$ are adjacent in $G \bullet H$ if and only if either $u$ is adjacent to $x$ in $G$ or $u = x$ and $v$ is adjacent to $y$ in $H$.

If the edge relations of the two graphs are [order relations](wikipedia://en/361924), then the edge relation of their lexicographic product is the corresponding [lexicographic order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicographical_order).

It is one of 4 common [graph products](wikipedia://en/11492935) including Cartesian, tensor, and strong.

The lexicographic product was first studied by (txt).[^n1] As (Feigenbaum Schäffer 1986) showed, the problem of recognizing whether a graph is a lexicographic product is equivalent in complexity to the [graph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/1950766).[^n2]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The lexicographic product is in general [noncommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity): $G \bullet H \ne H \bullet G$. However it satisfies a [distributive law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributivity) with respect to disjoint union: $(A + B) \bullet C = A \bullet C + B \bullet C$. In addition it satisfies an identity with respect to [complementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complement_%28graph_theory%29): $C(G \bullet H) = C(G) \bullet C(H)$. In particular, the lexicographic product of two [self-complementary graphs](wikipedia://en/15093884) is self-complementary.

The [independence number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_number) of a lexicographic product may be easily calculated from that of its factors:[^n3]

$$\alpha(G \bullet H) = \alpha(G)\alpha(H).$$

The [clique number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_number) of a lexicographic product is as well multiplicative:

$$\omega(G \bullet H) = \omega(G)\omega(H).$$

The [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of a lexicographic product is equal to the [*b*-fold chromatic number](wikipedia://en/692369#Definitions) of *G*, for *b* equal to the chromatic number of *H*:

$\chi(G \bullet H) = \chi_{b}(G)$, where $b = \chi(H)$.

The lexicographic product of two graphs is a [perfect graph](wikipedia://en/670531) if and only if both factors are perfect.[^n4]
