# Miklós Laczkovich

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Miklós Laczkovich"}

*Image: 10 éves a Wikipédia konferencia 026.jpg.* Laczkovich in 2011

**Miklós Laczkovich** (born 21 February 1948) is a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people) mathematician mainly noted for his work on [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478) and geometric [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory). His most famous result is the solution of [Tarski's circle-squaring problem](wikipedia://en/216811) in 1989.[^o2]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Laczkovich received his degree in mathematics in 1971 at [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University), where he has been teaching ever since, currently leading the Department of Analysis. He was also a professor at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), where he is now a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus).[^o4] He became corresponding member (1993), then member (1998) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences). He has held several guest professor positions in the UK, Canada, Italy and the United States.

Also being a prolific author, he published over 100 papers and two books, one of which, *Conjecture and Proof*, was an international success. One of his results is the solution of the Kemperman problem: if *f* is a real function which satisfies 2*f*(*x*) ≤ f(*x* + *h*) + *f*(*x* + 2*h*) for every *h* > 0, then *f* is monotonically increasing.
