# Apex (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apex"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **apex** (: **apices**) is the [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) which is in some sense the "highest" of the figure to which it belongs. The term is typically used to refer to the vertex opposite from some "[base](wikipedia://en/9579379)". The word is derived from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for 'summit, peak, tip, top, extreme end'. The term apex may be used in different contexts:

:::

- In an [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428), the apex is the vertex where the two sides of equal length meet, opposite the unequal third side.

*Image: Isosceles\_triangle\_el.svg.* Here the point A is the apex

- In a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045) or [cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29), the apex is the vertex at the "top" (opposite the [base](wikipedia://en/9579379)). In a pyramid, the vertex is the point that is part of all the lateral faces, or where all the lateral edges meet.

*Image: pyramid.svg.* The apex and base of a square pyramid
