# Compact quasi-Newton representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compact quasi-Newton representation"}

The **compact representation** for [quasi-Newton methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-Newton_methods) is a [matrix decomposition](wikipedia://en/253873), which is typically used in [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) based [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29) [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms) or for solving [nonlinear systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_systems). The decomposition uses a low-rank representation for the direct and/or inverse [Hessian](wikipedia://en/412108) or the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of a nonlinear system. Because of this, the compact representation is often used for large problems and [constrained optimization](wikipedia://en/4171950).

:::

*Image: Main-figure0.pdf.* The compact representation (right) of a dense Hessian approximation (left) is an initial matrix (typically diagonal) plus low rank decomposition. It has a small memory footprint (shaded areas) and enables efficient matrix computations

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The compact representation of a quasi-Newton matrix for the inverse Hessian $H_k$ or direct Hessian $B_k$ of a nonlinear [objective function](wikipedia://en/442137) $f(x):\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ expresses a sequence of recursive rank-1 or rank-2 matrix updates as one rank-$k$ or rank-$2k$ update of an initial matrix.[^o4][^o5] Because it is derived from quasi-Newton updates, it uses differences of iterates and gradients $\nabla f(x_k) = g_k$ in its definition $\{s_{i-1}=x_{i}-x_{i-1},y_{i-1}=g_{i}-g_{i-1} \}_{i=1}^k$. In particular, for $r=k$ or $r=2k$ the rectangular $n \times r$ matrices $U_k, J_k$ and the $r \times r$ square symmetric systems $M_k, N_k$ depend on the $s_i,y_i$'s and define the quasi-Newton representations

$$H_k = H_0 + U_k M^{-1}_k U^T_k, \quad \text{ and } \quad B_k = B_0 + J_k N^{-1}_k J^T_k$$

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

### Applications {#Applications}

Because of the special matrix decomposition the compact representation is implemented in state-of-the-art optimization software.[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11] When combined with limited-memory techniques it is a popular technique for [constrained optimization](wikipedia://en/4171950) with gradients.[^o12] [Linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) operations can be done efficiently, like [matrix-vector products](wikipedia://en/125280), [solves](wikipedia://en/13035) or [eigendecompositions](wikipedia://en/13576645). It can be combined with [line-search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-search) and [trust region](wikipedia://en/2975766) techniques, and the representation has been developed for many quasi-Newton updates. For instance, the matrix vector product with the direct quasi-Newton Hessian and an arbitrary vector $g \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is:

$$\begin{aligned} p^{(0)}_k &= J^T_k g \\ \text{solve} \quad N_k p^{(1)}_k &= p^{(0)}_k \quad \quad \text{(}N_k \text{ is small)} \\ p^{(2)}_k &= J_k p^{(1)}_k \\ p^{(3)}_k &= H_0 g \\ p^{\phantom{(4)}}_k &= p^{(2)}_k + p^{(3)}_k \end{aligned}$$

:::

### Background {#Background}

In the context of the [GMRES](wikipedia://en/4329521) method, Walker[^o14] showed that a product of [Householder transformations](wikipedia://en/485424) (an identity plus rank-1) can be expressed as a compact matrix formula. This led to the derivation of an explicit matrix expression for the product of $k$ identity plus rank-1 matrices.[^o12] Specifically, for $S_k = \begin{bmatrix} s_0 & s_1 & \ldots s_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$ $~Y_k = \begin{bmatrix} y_0 & y_1 & \ldots y_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$ $~(R_k)_{ij} = s^T_{i-1} y_{j-1},$ $~\rho_{i-1} = 1/s^T_{i-1} y_{i-1}$ and $~V_i = I - \rho_{i-1} y_{i-1} s^T_{i-1}$ when $1 \le i \le j \le k$ the product of $k$ rank-1 updates to the identity is

$$\prod_{i=1}^k V_{i-1} = \left( I - \rho_{0} y_{0} s^T_{0} \right) \cdots \left( I - \rho_{k-1} y_{k-1} s^T_{k-1} \right) = I - Y_k R^{-1}_k S^T_k$$

The [BFGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFGS) update can be expressed in terms of products of the $V_i$'s, which have a compact matrix formula. Therefore, the BFGS recursion can exploit these [block matrix](wikipedia://en/457464) representations

::: {#o15 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\begin{aligned} H_k &= V_{k-1} H_{k-1} V^T_{k-1} + \rho_{k-1} s_{k-1} s^T_{k-1} \\ &= \left( V_{k-1} \cdots V_1 V_0) H_0 (V^T_0 V^T_1 \cdots V^T_{k-1} \right) + \\ &\phantom{=} \rho_0 \left( V_{k-1} \cdots V_1 \right) s_0 s^T_0 \left( V^T_1 \cdots V^T_{k-1} \right) + \\ &\phantom{=} \quad \vdots \\ &\phantom{=} \rho_{k-2} V_{k-1} s_{k-2} s^T_{k-2} V^T_{k-1} + \\ &\phantom{=} \rho_{k-1} s_{k-1} s^T_{k-1} \end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

:::

:::

## Recursive quasi-Newton updates {#Recursive_quasi-Newton_updates}

A [parametric family](wikipedia://en/19653337) of quasi-Newton updates includes many of the most known formulas.[^o17] For arbitrary vectors $v_{k}$ and $c_{k}$ such that $v^T_{k}y_{k}\ne 0$ and $c^T_{k}s_{k} \ne 0$ general recursive update formulas for the inverse and direct Hessian estimates are

::: {#o18 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$H_{k+1} = H_{k} + \frac{(s_{k} - H_{k}y_{k} )v^T_{k} + v_{k}(s_{k} - H_{k}y_{k})^T }{v^T_{k} y_{k}} - \frac{(s_{k}-H_{k}y_{k})^T y_{k}}{(v^T_{k} y_{k})^2} v_{k} v^T_{k} \tag{2}$$

:::

::: {#o19 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$B_{k+1} = B_{k} + \frac{(y_{k} - B_{k}s_{k} )c^T_{k} + c_{k}(y_{k} - B_{k}s_{k})^T }{c^T_{k} s_{k}} - \frac{(y_{k}-B_{k}s_{k})^T s_{k}}{(c^T_{k} s_{k})^2} c_{k} c^T_{k} \tag{3}$$

:::

By making specific choices for the parameter vectors $v_{k}$ and $c_{k}$ well known methods are recovered

*Table 1: Quasi-Newton updates parametrized by vectors $v_{k}$ and $c_{k}$*

::: {#o20 .table cue=table name="Table 1: Quasi-Newton updates parametrized by vectors v_{k} and c_{k}"}

| $v_{k}$ | $\text{method}$ | $c_{k}$ | $\text{method}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $s_{k}$ | [BFGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFGS) | $s_{k}$ | PSB (Powell Symmetric Broyden) |
| $y_{k}$ | $\text{Greenstadt's}$ | $y_{k}$ | [DFP](wikipedia://en/11231265) |
| $s_{k}-H_{k}y_{k}$ | [SR1](wikipedia://en/11227519) | $y_{k}-B_{k}s_{k}$ | [SR1](wikipedia://en/11227519) |
|  |  | $P_k^{\text{S}} s_k$[^o21] | MSS (Multipoint-Symmetric-Secant) |

:::

## Compact Representations {#Compact_Representations}

Collecting the updating vectors of the recursive formulas into matrices, define

$$S_k = \begin{bmatrix} s_0 & s_1 & \ldots & s_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$$

$$Y_k = \begin{bmatrix} y_0 & y_1 & \ldots & y_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$$

$$V_k = \begin{bmatrix} v_0 & v_1 & \ldots & v_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$$

$$C_k = \begin{bmatrix} c_0 & c_1 & \ldots & c_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$$

upper triangular

$$\big(R_k\big)_{ij} := \big(R^{\text{SY}}_k\big)_{ij} = s^T_{i-1}y_{j-1}, \quad \big(R^{\text{VY}}_k\big)_{ij} = v^T_{i-1}y_{j-1} , \quad \big(R^{\text{CS}}_k\big)_{ij} = c^T_{i-1}s_{j-1}, \quad \quad \text{ for } 1 \le i \le j \le k$$

lower triangular

$$\big(L_k\big)_{ij} := \big(L^{\text{SY}}_k\big)_{ij} = s^T_{i-1}y_{j-1}, \quad \big(L^{\text{VY}}_k\big)_{ij} = v^T_{i-1}y_{j-1} , \quad \big(L^{\text{CS}}_k\big)_{ij} = c^T_{i-1}s_{j-1}, \quad \quad \text{ for } 1 \le j < i \le k$$

and diagonal

$$(D_k)_{ij} := \big(D^{\text{SY}}_k\big)_{ij} = s^T_{i-1}y_{j-1}, \quad \quad \text{ for } 1 \le i = j \le k$$

With these definitions the compact representations of general rank-2 updates in ((2)) and ((3)) (including the well known quasi-Newton updates in Table 1) have been developed in Brust:[^o23]

::: {#o24 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$H_k = H_0 + U_k M^{-1}_k U^T_k, \tag{4}$$

:::

$$U_k = \begin{bmatrix} V_k & S_k - H_0 Y_k \end{bmatrix}$$

$$M_k = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{k \times k} & R^{\text{VY}}_k \\ \big( R^{\text{VY}}_k \big)^T & R_k+R^T_k-(D_k+Y^T_k H_0 Y_k) \end{bmatrix}$$

and the formula for the direct Hessian is

::: {#o25 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$B_k = B_0 + J_k N^{-1}_k J^T_k, \tag{5}$$

:::

$$J_k = \begin{bmatrix} C_k & Y_k - B_0 S_k \end{bmatrix}$$

$$N_k = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{k \times k} & R^{\text{CS}}_k \\ \big( R^{\text{CS}}_k \big)^T & R_k+R^T_k-(D_k+S^T_k B_0 S_k) \end{bmatrix}$$

For instance, when $V_k = S_k$ the representation in ((4)) is the compact formula for the BFGS recursion in ((1)).

### Specific Representations {#Specific_Representations}

Prior to the development of the compact representations of ((2)) and ((3)), equivalent representations have been discovered for most known updates (see Table 1).

#### [BFGS](wikipedia://en/1926409) {#BFGS}

Along with the SR1 representation, the BFGS (Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno) compact representation was the first compact formula known.[^o12] In particular, the inverse representation is given by

$H_k = H_0 + U_k M^{-1}_k U^T_k, \quad U_k = \begin{bmatrix} S_k & H_0 Y_k \end{bmatrix}, \quad M^{-1}_k = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} R^{-T}_k(D_k+Y^T_k H_0 Y_k) R^{-1}_k & -R^{-T}_k \\ -R^{-1}_k & 0 \end{smallmatrix} \right]$ The direct Hessian approximation can be found by applying the [Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity](wikipedia://en/430493) to the inverse Hessian:

$$B_k = B_0 + J_k N^{-1}_k J^T_k, \quad J_k = \begin{bmatrix} B_0 S_k & Y_k \end{bmatrix}, \quad N_k = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} S^T B_0 S_k & L_k \\ L^T_k & -D_k \end{smallmatrix} \right]$$

#### [SR1](wikipedia://en/11227519) {#SR1}

The SR1 (Symmetric Rank-1) compact representation was first proposed in.[^o12] Using the definitions of $D_k, L_k$ and $R_k$ from above, the inverse Hessian formula is given by

$$H_k = H_0 + U_k M^{-1}_k U^T_k, \quad U_k = S_k-H_0 Y_k, \quad M_k = R_k+R^T_k-D_k-Y^T_k H_0 Y_k$$

The direct Hessian is obtained by the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity and has the form

$$B_k = B_0 + J_k N^{-1}_k J^T_k, \quad J_k = Y_k-B_0 S_k, \quad N_k = D_k+L_k+L^T_k-S^T_k B_0 S_k$$

#### MSS {#MSS}

The multipoint symmetric secant (MSS) method is a method that aims to satisfy multiple secant equations. The recursive update formula was originally developed by Burdakov.[^o30] The compact representation for the direct Hessian was derived in [^o31]

$$B_k = B_0 + J_k N^{-1}_k J^T_k, \quad J_k = \begin{bmatrix} S_k & Y_k - B_0 S_k \end{bmatrix}, \quad N_k = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} W_k(S^T_k B_0 S_k - (R_k - D_k +R^T_k))W_k & W_k \\ W_k & 0 \end{smallmatrix} \right]^{-1}, \quad W_k = (S^T_k S_k)^{-1}$$

Another equivalent compact representation for the MSS matrix is derived by rewriting $J_k$ in terms of $J_k = \begin{bmatrix} S_k & B_0 Y_k \end{bmatrix}$.[^o32] The inverse representation can be obtained by application for the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity.

#### [DFP](wikipedia://en/11231265) {#DFP}

Since the DFP (Davidon Fletcher Powell) update is the dual of the BFGS formula (i.e., swapping $H_k \leftrightarrow B_k$, $H_0 \leftrightarrow B_0$ and $y_k \leftrightarrow s_k$ in the BFGS update), the compact representation for DFP can be immediately obtained from the one for BFGS.[^o34]

#### PSB {#PSB}

The PSB (Powell-Symmetric-Broyden) compact representation was developed for the direct Hessian approximation.[^o36] It is equivalent to substituting $C_k = S_k$ in ((5))

$$B_k = B_0 + J_k N^{-1}_k J^T_k, \quad J_k = \begin{bmatrix} S_k & Y_k-B_0 S_k \end{bmatrix}, \quad N_k = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & R^{\text{SS}}_k \\ (R^{\text{SS}}_k)^T & R_k+R^T_k-(D_k + S^T_k B_0 S_k) \end{smallmatrix} \right]$$

#### Structured BFGS {#Structured_BFGS}

For structured optimization problems in which the objective function can be decomposed into two parts $f(x) = \widehat{k}(x) + \widehat{u}(x)$, where the gradients and Hessian of $\widehat{k}(x)$ are known but only the gradient of $\widehat{u}(x)$ is known, structured BFGS formulas exist. The compact representation of these methods has the general form of ((5)), with specific $J_k$ and $N_k$.[^o38]

#### Reduced BFGS {#Reduced_BFGS}

The reduced compact representation (RCR) of BFGS is for linear equality constrained optimization $\text{ minimize } f(x) \text{ subject to: } Ax = b$, where $A$ is [underdetermined](wikipedia://en/15890311). In addition to the matrices $S_k, Y_k$ the RCR also stores the projections of the $y_i$'s onto the nullspace of $A$

$$Z_k = \begin{bmatrix} z_0 & z_1 & \cdots z_{k-1} \end{bmatrix}, \quad z_i = P y_i, \quad P = I - A (A^TA)^{-1} A^T, \quad 0 \le i \le k-1$$

For $B_k$ the compact representation of the BFGS matrix (with a multiple of the identity $B_0$) the (1,1) block of the inverse [KKT](wikipedia://en/2397362) matrix has the compact representation[^o40]

$$K_k = \begin{bmatrix} B_k & A^T \\ A & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B_0 = \frac{1}{\gamma_k}I, \quad H_0 = \gamma_k I, \quad \gamma_k > 0$$

$$\big(K^{-1}_k \big)_{11} = H_0 + U_k M^{-1}_k U^T_k, \quad U_k = \begin{bmatrix} A^T & S_k & Z_k \end{bmatrix}, \quad M_k = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} - AA^T / \gamma_k & \\ & G_k \end{smallmatrix} \right], \quad G_k = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} R^{-T}_k(D_k+Y^T_k H_0 Y_k) R^{-1}_k & -H_0 R^{-T}_k \\ -H_0R^{-1}_k & 0 \end{smallmatrix} \right]^{-1}$$

## Limited Memory {#Limited_Memory}

*Image: Lmupdates fit.gif.* Pattern in a limited-memory updating strategy. With a memory parameter of $m=7$, the first $k \le m$ iterations fill the matrix (e.g., an upper triangular for the compact representation, $R_k = \text{triu}(S^T_kY_k)$). For $k > m$ the limited-memory techniques discards the oldest information and add a new column to the end.

The most common use of the compact representations is for the [limited-memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-memory_BFGS) setting where $m \ll n$ denotes the memory parameter, with typical values around $m \in [5,12]$ (see e.g.,[^o40][^o12]). Then, instead of storing the history of all vectors one limits this to the $m$ most recent vectors $\{ (s_i, y_i \}_{i=k-m}^{k-1}$ and possibly $\{ v_i \}_{i=k-m}^{k-1}$ or $\{ c_i \}_{i=k-m}^{k-1}$. Further, typically the initialization is chosen as an adaptive multiple of the identity $H^{(0)}_k = \gamma_k I$, with $\gamma_k = y^T_{k-1} s_{k-1} / y^T_{k-1} y_{k-1}$ and $B^{(0)}_k = \frac{1}{\gamma_k} I$. Limited-memory methods are frequently used for large-scale problems with many variables (i.e., $n$ can be large), in which the limited-memory matrices $S_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ and $Y_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ (and possibly $V_k, C_k$) are tall and very skinny: $S_k = \begin{bmatrix} s_{k-l-1} & \ldots & s_{k-1} \end{bmatrix}$ and $Y_k = \begin{bmatrix} y_{k-l-1} & \ldots & y_{k-1} \end{bmatrix}$.

## Implementations {#Implementations}

Open source implementations include:

- [ACM TOMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Transactions_on_Mathematical_Software) algorithm 1030 implements a L-SR1 solver[^o44][^o45]
- [R](wikipedia://en/376707)'s `optim` general-purpose optimizer routine uses the L-BFGS-B method.
- [SciPy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SciPy)'s optimization module's minimize method also includes an option to use L-BFGS-B.
- [IPOPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPOPT) with first order information

Non open source implementations include:

- [Artelys Knitro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artelys_Knitro) [nonlinear programming](wikipedia://en/679709) (NLP) solvers use compact quasi-Newton matrices [^o8]
- [L-BFGS-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-memory_BFGS) (ACM TOMS algorithm 778)[^o46]
