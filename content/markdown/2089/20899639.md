# Schur's property

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schur's property"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Schur's property**, named after [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522), is the property of [normed spaces](wikipedia://en/21538) that is satisfied precisely if [weak convergence](wikipedia://en/33662) of **sequences** entails convergence in norm.

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

When we are working in a normed space *X* and we have a sequence $(x_{n})$ that converges weakly to $x$, then a natural question arises. Does the sequence converge in perhaps a more desirable manner? If so, does the sequence converge to $x$ in norm? A canonical example of this property, and commonly used to illustrate the Schur property, is the $\ell_1$ [sequence space](wikipedia://en/1701075).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose that we have a normed space $(X, \|\cdot\|)$, $x$ an arbitrary member of $X$, and $(x_{n})$ an arbitrary sequence in the space. We say that $X$ has **Schur's property** if $(x_{n})$ converging weakly to $x$ implies that $\lim_{n\to\infty} \Vert x_n - x\Vert = 0$. In other words, the weak and strong topologies share the same convergent sequences. Note however that weak and strong topologies are always distinct in infinite-dimensional space.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The space *ℓ<sup>1</sup>* of sequences whose series is absolutely convergent has the Schur property.

:::

## Name {#Name}

This property was named after the early 20th century mathematician [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522) who showed that *ℓ<sup>1</sup>* had the above property in his 1921 paper.[^o8]
