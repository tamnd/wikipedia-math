# Geometric combinatorics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric combinatorics"}

**Geometric combinatorics** is a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in general and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) in particular. It includes a number of subareas such as [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519) (the study of [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) of [convex polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron)), [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/4165882) (the study of [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292), in particular combinatorics of their intersections), and [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), which in turn has many applications to [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927). Other important areas include [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry) of [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra), such as the [Cauchy theorem](wikipedia://en/8317687) on rigidity of convex polytopes. The study of [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693), [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847), and [kissing numbers](wikipedia://en/408555) is also a part of geometric combinatorics. Special polytopes are also considered, such as the [permutohedron](wikipedia://en/12745947), [associahedron](wikipedia://en/19698446) and [Birkhoff polytope](wikipedia://en/7771277).

:::
