# Clique-sum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clique-sum"}

*Image: Clique-sum.svg.* A clique-sum of two planar graphs and the Wagner graph, forming a *K*<sub>5</sub>-minor-free graph.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of mathematics, a **clique sum** (or **clique-sum**) is a way of combining two graphs by gluing them together at a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466), analogous to the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) operation in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). If two graphs *G* and *H* each contain cliques of equal size, the clique-sum of *G* and *H* is formed from their [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/9938459) by identifying pairs of vertices in these two cliques to form a single shared clique, and then deleting all the clique edges (the original definition, based on the notion of [set sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_sum)) or possibly deleting some of the clique edges (a loosening of the definition). A *k*-clique-sum is a clique-sum in which both cliques have exactly (or sometimes, at most) *k* vertices. One may also form clique-sums and *k*-clique-sums of more than two graphs, by repeated application of the clique-sum operation.

:::

Different sources disagree on which edges should be removed as part of a clique-sum operation. In some contexts, such as the decomposition of [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165) or [strangulated graphs](wikipedia://en/36971277), no edges should be removed. In other contexts, such as the [SPQR-tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPQR-tree) decomposition of graphs into their 3-vertex-connected components, all edges should be removed. And in yet other contexts, such as the [graph structure theorem](wikipedia://en/21318521) for minor-closed families of simple graphs, it is natural to allow the set of removed edges to be specified as part of the operation.

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

Clique-sums have a close connection with [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086): If two graphs have treewidth at most *k*, so does their *k*-clique-sum. Every [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) is the 1-clique-sum of its edges. Every [series–parallel graph](wikipedia://en/9938559), or more generally every graph with [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) at most two, may be formed as a 2-clique-sum of triangles. The same type of result extends to larger values of *k*: every graph with [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) at most *k* may be formed as a clique-sum of graphs with at most *k* + 1 vertices; this is necessarily a *k*-clique-sum.[^o3]

There is also a close connection between clique-sums and [graph connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_connectivity): if a graph is not [(*k* + 1)-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) (so that there exists a set of *k* vertices the removal of which disconnects the graph) then it may be represented as a *k*-clique-sum of smaller graphs. For instance, the [SPQR tree](wikipedia://en/11220797) of a biconnected graph is a representation of the graph as a 2-clique-sum of its [triconnected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triconnected_component).

## Application in graph structure theory {#Application_in_graph_structure_theory}

*Image: Strangulated graph.svg.* A [strangulated graph](wikipedia://en/36971277), formed as a clique-sum of a [maximal planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) (yellow) and two [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165) (red and blue)

Clique-sums are important in graph structure theory, where they are used to characterize certain families of graphs as the graphs formed by clique-sums of simpler graphs. The first result of this type[^o5] was a theorem of (Wagner 1937), who proved that the graphs that do not have a five-vertex complete graph as a [minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_%28graph_theory%29) are the 3-clique-sums of [planar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_graphs) with the eight-vertex [Wagner graph](wikipedia://en/17598450); this structure theorem can be used to show that the [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949) is equivalent to the case *k* = 5 of the [Hadwiger conjecture](wikipedia://en/1657860). The [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165) are exactly the graphs that can be formed by clique-sums of cliques without deleting any edges, and the [strangulated graphs](wikipedia://en/36971277) are the graphs that can be formed by clique-sums of cliques and [maximal planar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_planar_graph) without deleting edges.[^o7] The graphs in which every [induced cycle](wikipedia://en/1655188) of length four or greater forms a minimal separator of the graph (its removal partitions the graph into two or more disconnected components, and no subset of the cycle has the same property) are exactly the clique-sums of cliques and maximal [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), again without edge deletions.[^o8] (Johnson McKee 1996) use the clique-sums of chordal graphs and series–parallel graphs to characterize the partial [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) having [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_definite_matrix) completions.

It is possible to derive a clique-sum decomposition for any graph family closed under graph minor operations: the graphs in every minor-closed family may be formed from clique-sums of graphs that are "nearly embedded" on surfaces of bounded [genus](wikipedia://en/61891), meaning that the embedding is allowed to omit a small number of *apexes* (vertices that may be connected to an arbitrary subset of the other vertices) and *vortices* (graphs with low [pathwidth](wikipedia://en/5133142) that replace faces of the surface embedding).[^o10] These characterizations have been used as an important tool in the construction of [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105) and subexponential-time exact algorithms for [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) optimization problems on minor-closed graph families.[^o11]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The theory of clique-sums may also be generalized from graphs to [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321).[^o3] Notably, *Seymour's decomposition theorem* characterizes the [regular matroids](wikipedia://en/13415343) (the matroids representable by [totally unimodular matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_unimodular_matrix)) as the 3-sums of [graphic matroids](wikipedia://en/745714) (the matroids representing spanning trees in a graph), cographic matroids, and a certain 10-element matroid.[^o3][^o14]
