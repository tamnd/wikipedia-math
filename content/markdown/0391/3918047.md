# Gosper curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gosper curve"}

*Image: Gosper curve 4.svg.* A fourth-stage Gosper curve

*Image: Gosper curve 1.svg.* The line from the red to the green point shows a single step of the Gosper curve construction

The **Gosper curve**, named after [Bill Gosper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gosper), also known as the **Peano-Gosper Curve**[^o2] and the **flowsnake** (a [spoonerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoonerism) of [snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959)), is a [space-filling curve](wikipedia://en/352398) whose limit set is [rep](wikipedia://en/33478101)-7. It is a [fractal curve](wikipedia://en/4972605) similar in its construction to the [dragon curve](wikipedia://en/367064) and the [Hilbert curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_curve).

:::

The Gosper curve can also be used for efficient hierarchical hexagonal clustering and indexing.[^o3]

## Lindenmayer system {#Lindenmayer_system}

The Gosper curve can be represented using an [L-system](wikipedia://en/46565) with rules as follows:

- Angle: 60°
- Axiom: $A$
- Replacement rules:
  - $$A \mapsto A-B--B+A++AA+B-$$
  - $$B \mapsto +A-BB--B-A++A+B$$

In this case both A and B mean to move forward, + means to turn left 60 degrees and - means to turn right 60 degrees - using a "turtle"-style program such as [Logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo_programming_language).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The space filled by the curve is called the **Gosper island**. The first few iterations of it are shown below:

| *Image: Gosper Island 0.svg.* | *Image: Gosper Island 1.svg.* | *Image: Gosper Island 2.svg.* | *Image: Gosper Island 3.svg.* | *Image: Gosper Island 4.svg.* |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The Gosper Island can [tile](wikipedia://en/321671) the [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852). In fact, seven copies of the Gosper island can be joined to form a shape that is [similar](wikipedia://en/39377), but scaled up by a factor of $\sqrt{7}$ in all dimensions. As can be seen from the diagram below, performing this operation with an intermediate iteration of the island leads to a scaled-up version of the next iteration. Repeating this process indefinitely produces a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of the plane. The curve itself can likewise be extended to an infinite curve filling the whole plane.
