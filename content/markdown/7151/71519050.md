# Le Potier's vanishing theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Le Potier's vanishing theorem"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Le Potier's vanishing theorem** is an extension of the [Kodaira vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/2988093), on [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524). The theorem states the following[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10] (Le Potier 1975): Let X be a *n*-dimensional compact [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) and E a [holomorphic vector bundle](wikipedia://en/4794668) of rank r over X, here $H^{p,q}(X,E)$ is [Dolbeault cohomology](wikipedia://en/3189601) group, where $\Omega ^{p}_{X}$ denotes the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of [holomorphic *p*-forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolbeault_complex) on *X*. If E is an [ample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ample), then

$H^{p,q}(X, E) = 0$ for $p + q \geq n + r$ .

from [Dolbeault theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolbeault_theorem),

$H^{q}(X, \Omega ^{p}_{X} \otimes E ) = 0$ for $p + q \geq n + r$ .

By [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175), the statements are equivalent to the assertions:

$H^{i}(X, \Omega ^{j}_{X} \otimes E^* ) = 0$ for $j + i \leq n - r$ .

:::

In case of r = 1, and let E is an ample (or positive) line bundle on X, this theorem is equivalent to the [Nakano vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/44745308). Also, (Schneider 1974) found another proof.

(Sommese 1978) generalizes Le Potier's vanishing theorem to k-ample and the statement as follows:

Le Potier–Sommese vanishing theorem: Let X be a *n*-dimensional [algebraic manifold](wikipedia://en/4499397) and E is a k-ample holomorphic vector bundle of rank r over X, then

$H^{p,q}(X, E) = 0$ for $p + q \geq n + r + k$ .

(Demailly 1988) gave a counterexample, which is as follows:[^o15]

Conjecture of (Sommese 1978): Let X be a *n*-dimensional compact complex manifold and E a holomorphic vector bundle of rank r over X. If E is an ample, then

$H^{p,q}(X, \Lambda^a E ) = 0$ for $p + q \geq n + r - a + 1$ is false for $n=2r \geq 6 .$
