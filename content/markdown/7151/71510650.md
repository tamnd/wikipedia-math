# George Gamow Memorial Lectures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Gamow Memorial Lectures"}

The **George Gamow Memorial Lectures** are an annual series of lectures at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder), named in honor of the physicist and science popularizer [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow), author of *[One Two Three... Infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Two_Three..._Infinity)* and the *[Mr Tompkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_Tompkins)* series.[^o2]

:::

## Background {#Background}

The lectures were established by Gamow's widow, [Barbara Perkins Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Perkins_Gamow), and the University's Department of Physics in 1971, to "promote public understanding of the nature and role of science." In 1975, Mrs. Gamow included in her will an endowment to maintain the lectures. The George Gamow lecturers have included 27 [Nobel Laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates) in the sciences. Lectures are held at the university's Macky Auditorium, and are free and open to the public.[^o4]

[Frances Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Arnold), Nobel Laureate in Chemistry, 2018, was to lecture on “Innovation by Evolution: Bringing New Chemistry to Life”[^o5] but her lecture was postponed due to the COVID-19 pandemic.[^o6] In 2024, the Lectures resumed with Dr. [Andrea Ghez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Ghez), Nobel Laureate in Physics, 2020.

- 1979 [Melvin Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Calvin), [Nobel Laureate in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate_in_Chemistry), 1961
- 1980 [Paul A. M. Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742), Nobel Laureate in Physics, 1933

- 1982 [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam), mathematician

- 1985 [Luis Walter Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Walter_Alvarez), Nobel Laureate in Physics, 1968 and [Walter Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Alvarez), geologist
- 1987 [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam), Nobel Laureate in Physics, 1979 and [Rosalyn Sussman Yalow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalyn_Sussman_Yalow), Nobel Laureate in Physiology of Medicine, 1977
- 1989 [Stephen Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Schneider_%28scientist%29), climatologist

- 1994 [Susan Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Solomon), atmospheric chemist
- 1995 [Eric Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Allin_Cornell) and [Carl Wieman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Wieman), who would share the 2001 Nobel Prize in Physics and [Sylvia Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Earle), marine biologist

- 1998 [Stanley B. Prusiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_B._Prusiner), Nobel Laureate in Physiology or Medicine, 1997, on "Prions – A New Paradigm in Biology and Medicine"[^o13]
- 1999 [Robert Ballard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Ballard), oceanographer
- 2000 [Martin Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Rees), [Astronomer Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer_Royal), on "Six Cosmic Numbers: Understanding the Beginning and the End"[^o14]
- 2001 [Eric Kandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Kandel), Nobel Laureate in Physiology or Medicine, 2000, on "The Past, the Future and the Biology of Memory Storage"[^o15]

- 2006 [Geoffrey Marcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Marcy), astronomer
- 2007 [Lisa Randall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Randall), theoretical physicist, on "Warped Passages: Unraveling the Mysteries of the Universe's Hidden Dimensions"[^o17]
- 2008 [Kerry Emmanuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerry_Emanuel), meteorologist
- 2009 Joy Hirsch, neuroscientist[^o18]
- 2010 [Richard Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Alley), geologist, on "Learning While Burning: Peak (Whale) Oil, Changing Climate and Our Future"[^o19]

- 2012 [Adam G. Riess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Riess), Nobel Laureate in Physics, 2011, on "Supernovae and the Discovery of the Accelerating Universe"<https://vimeo.com/43828580>
- 2013 [Brian Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Greene), theoretical physicist, on "The Hidden Reality: From Unification to Multiverse"[^o21][^o22]
- 2014 [David J. Wineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._Wineland), Nobel Laureate in Physics, 2012, on "Quantum Computing and Schrödinger 's Cat"[^o23]<https://vimeo.com/93675310>
- 2015 [Jane Goodall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Goodall), primatologist, on "Sowing the Seeds of Hope"[^o24]<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y9Cm_7Fl-j8> This lecture was held at the University's Coors Event Center due to popular demand.[^o25]
- 2016 [Kip S. Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne), who would win the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2017, on "Probing the Warped Side of the Universe with Gravitational Waves: From the Big Bang to Black Holes”[^o26]<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YQL7D81cDI4>
- 2018 [Vint Cerf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vint_Cerf), computer scientist, on "Digital Preservation"[^o27]
- 2024 [Andrea Ghez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Ghez), astrophysicist, Nobel laureate in Physics, 2020, on "From the Possibility to the Certainty of a Supermassive Black Hole"[^o28]<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DriJ3JoJK0Q>
- 2025 [Jennifer Doudna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Doudna), biochemist, Nobel laureate in Physiology or Medicine, 2020, on "Genome Editing the Future: Improving Human and Planet Health with CRISPR"[^o29]
