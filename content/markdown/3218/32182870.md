# Twisted Poincaré duality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Twisted Poincaré duality"}

In mathematics, the **twisted Poincaré duality** is a theorem removing the restriction on [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) to [oriented manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriented_manifold). The existence of a global orientation is replaced by carrying along local information, by means of a [local coefficient system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_coefficient_system).

:::

## Twisted Poincaré duality for de Rham cohomology {#Twisted_Poincaré_duality_for_de_Rham_cohomology}

Another version of the theorem with real coefficients features [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) with values in the **orientation bundle**. This is the **[flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_vector_bundle)** real [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) denoted $o(M)$, that is trivialized by coordinate charts of the manifold $M$, with transition maps the sign of the [Jacobian determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_determinant) of the charts transition maps. As a [flat line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_vector_bundle), it has a de Rham cohomology, denoted by

$H^* (M; \mathbb{R}^w)$ or $H^* (M; o(M))$.

For *M* a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) manifold, the top degree cohomology is equipped with a so-called **trace morphism**

$$\theta\colon H^d (M; o(M)) \to \mathbb{R},$$

that is to be interpreted as integration on *M*, *i.e.*, evaluating against the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440).

Poincaré duality for differential forms is then the conjunction, for *M* connected, of the following two statements:

- The trace morphism is a linear isomorphism.
- The cup product, or [exterior product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_product) of differential forms

$$\cup \colon H^* (M; \mathbb{R})\otimes H^{d-*}(M, o(M)) \to H^d(M, o(M)) \simeq \mathbb{R}$$

is non-degenerate.

The oriented [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) is contained in this statement, as understood from the fact that the orientation bundle *o(M)* is trivial if the manifold is oriented, an orientation being a global trivialization, *i.e.*, a nowhere vanishing parallel section.
