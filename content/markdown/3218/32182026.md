# Switching circuit theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Switching circuit theory"}

**Switching circuit theory** is the mathematical study of the properties of networks of idealized switches. Such networks may be strictly [combinational logic](wikipedia://en/89371), in which their output state is only a function of the present state of their inputs; or may also contain [sequential elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequential_logic), where the present state depends on the present state and past states; in that sense, sequential circuits are said to include "memory" of past states. An important class of sequential circuits are [state machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_machine). Switching circuit theory is applicable to the design of telephone systems, computers, and similar systems. Switching circuit theory provided the mathematical foundations and tools for [digital system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_electronics) design in almost all areas of modern technology.[^o13]

:::

In an 1886 letter, [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) described how logical operations could be carried out by electrical switching circuits.[^o6] During 1880–1881 he showed that [NOR gates alone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOR_logic) (or alternatively [NAND gates alone](wikipedia://en/3521614)) can be used to reproduce the functions of all the other [logic gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_gate), but this work remained unpublished until 1933.[^o7] The first published proof was by [Henry M. Sheffer](wikipedia://en/2993325) in 1913, so the NAND logical operation is sometimes called [Sheffer stroke](wikipedia://en/29388); the [logical NOR](wikipedia://en/209154) is sometimes called *Peirce's arrow*.[^o8] Consequently, these gates are sometimes called *universal logic gates*.[^o9]

In 1898, Martin Boda described a switching theory for [signalling block systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signalling_block_system).[^o14][^o18]

Eventually, [vacuum tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_tube) replaced relays for logic operations. [Lee De Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_De_Forest)'s modification, in 1907, of the [Fleming valve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleming_valve) can be used as a logic gate. [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741) introduced a version of the 16-row [truth table](wikipedia://en/25512250) as proposition 5.101 of *[Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus](wikipedia://en/30062)* (1921). [Walther Bothe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Bothe), inventor of the [coincidence circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coincidence_circuit), got part of the 1954 [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize) in physics, for the first modern electronic AND gate in 1924. [Konrad Zuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Zuse) designed and built electromechanical logic gates for his computer [Z1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z1_%28computer%29) (from 1935 to 1938).

The theory was independently established through the works of [NEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEC) engineer [Akira Nakashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Nakashima) in Japan,[^o15] [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) in the United States,[^o16] and [Victor Shestakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Shestakov) in the Soviet Union.[^o17] The three published a series of papers showing that the [two-valued Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/2584449), can describe the operation of switching circuits.[^o18][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13] However, Shannon's work has largely overshadowed the other two, and despite some scholars arguing the similarities of Nakashima's work to Shannon's, their approaches and theoretical frameworks were markedly different.[^o2] Also implausible is that Shestakov's influenced the other two due to the language barriers and the relative obscurity of his work abroad.[^o2] Furthermore, Shannon and Shestakov defended their theses the same year in 1938,[^o3] and Shestakov did not publish until 1941.[^o3]

Ideal switches are considered as having only two exclusive states, for example, open or closed. In some analysis, the state of a switch can be considered to have no influence on the output of the system and is designated as a "don't care" state. In complex networks it is necessary to also account for the finite switching time of physical switches; where two or more different paths in a network may affect the output, these delays may result in a ["logic hazard"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_%28logic%29) or "[race condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_condition)" where the output state changes due to the different propagation times through the network.
