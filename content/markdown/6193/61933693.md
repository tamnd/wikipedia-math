# Elaine Chew

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Elaine Chew"}

**Elaine Chew** is an [operations researcher](wikipedia://en/43476) and pianist focused on the study of musical structures as they apply to musical performance, composition and cognition,[^o5] the analysis of electrocardiographic traces of [arrhythmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_arrhythmia),[^o6][^o7] and [digital therapeutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_therapeutics).[^o2][^o8] She is currently Professor of Engineering at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London), where she is jointly appointed in the Department of Engineering (Faculty of Natural, Mathematical & Engineering Sciences) and the Department of Cardiovascular Imaging in the School of Biomedical Engineering & Imaging Sciences (Faculty of Life Sciences & Medicine).[^o9]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), Chew grew up in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore), returning to the US after high school for further studies.[^o11] She received a Bachelor of Arts and Sciences in Mathematical and Computational Sciences with honours and Music with distinction from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). Her PhD thesis in the Operations Research Center at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) was focused on the mathematics of tonality.[^o12] Chew holds diplomas in piano performance from [Trinity College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_London).[^o13]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Chew has designed a theory of tonality called the [spiral array model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_array_model).[^n1][^o16] This is a mathematical model using spirals to describe how humans perceive pitches, chords and keys in mainstream Western music. Chew wrote *Mathematical and Computational Modeling of Tonality,* a book about her work on mathematical and computational techniques for automated analysis and visualisation of tonal structures, in 2014.[^o17]

Chew was an assistant professor at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) (USC) from 2001 to 2011,[^o18] where she was the inaugural honouree of the [Viterbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Viterbi) Early Career Chair[^o19] and founded the Music Computation and Cognition Laboratory. At USC, Chew encouraged her students to use technology to explore expressivity in music.[^o20] Chew was Professor of Digital Media in the School of Electronic Engineering and Computer Science at [Queen Mary University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_University_of_London) from 2011 to 2019, where she founded the Music, Performance, and Expressivity Laboratory at the Centre for Digital Music. From 2019 to 2022, Chew was a senior [Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (CNRS) researcher at the Science et Technologies de la Musique et du Son (STMS) Laboratory and also affiliated with the [Institut de Recherche et Coordination Acoustique/Musique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_Recherche_et_Coordination_Acoustique/Musique) (IRCAM), [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University), and the [French Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_%28France%29).[^o21]

As a concert pianist, Chew plays for audiences while communicating her research, often by showing mathematical visualisations alongside the performances.[^o22][^o23][^o24]

- [Presidential Early Career Award in Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Early_Career_Award_for_Scientists_and_Engineers) (2005)[^o27]
- Fellowships at Harvard's [Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) (2007 and 2017)[^o28]
- Fellow of the (US) [National Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences)'s Kavli Frontiers of Science Symposia[^o29] and of the (US) [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering)'s Frontiers of Engineering Symposia for outstanding young scientists and engineers[^o30]
