# Principal indecomposable module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Principal indecomposable module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [module theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_theory), a **principal indecomposable module** has many important relations to the study of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404)'s [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), especially its [simple modules](wikipedia://en/29430), [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488), and [indecomposable modules](wikipedia://en/810549).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A (left) **principal indecomposable module** of a ring *R* is a (left) [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of *R* that is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of *R* and is an [indecomposable module](wikipedia://en/810549). Alternatively, it is an indecomposable, projective, [cyclic module](wikipedia://en/4128720). Principal indecomposable modules are also called **PIM**s for short.

:::

## Relations {#Relations}

The projective indecomposable modules over some rings have very close connections with those rings' simple, projective, and indecomposable modules.

If the ring *R* is [Artinian](wikipedia://en/489273) or even [semiperfect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiperfect_ring), then *R* is a direct sum of principal indecomposable modules, and there is one isomorphism class of PIM per isomorphism class of simple module. To each PIM *P* is associated its **head**, *P*/*JP*, which is a simple module, being an indecomposable semi-simple module. To each simple module *S* is associated its [projective cover](wikipedia://en/10273917) *P*, which is a PIM, being an indecomposable, projective, cyclic module.

Similarly over a [semiperfect ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiperfect_ring), every indecomposable projective module is a PIM, and every finitely generated projective module is a direct sum of PIMs.

In the context of [group algebras](wikipedia://en/349223) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) over [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) (which are semiperfect rings), the [representation ring](wikipedia://en/16673816) describes the indecomposable modules, and the [modular characters](wikipedia://en/1110685) of simple modules represent both a subring and a quotient ring. The representation ring over the complex field is usually better understood and since PIMs correspond to modules over the complexes using *p*-modular system, one can use PIMs to transfer information from the complex representation ring to the representation ring over a field of positive characteristic. Roughly speaking this is called block theory.

Over a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) that is not a [PID](wikipedia://en/25039), the [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) measures the difference between projective indecomposable modules and principal indecomposable modules: the projective indecomposable modules are exactly the (modules isomorphic to) nonzero ideals and the principal indecomposable modules are precisely the (modules isomorphic to) nonzero principal ideals.
