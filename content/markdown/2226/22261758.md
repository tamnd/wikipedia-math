# Robert Groves

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Groves"}

**Robert Martin Groves** (born September 27, 1948)[^o3] is an American [sociologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) and [survey methodology](wikipedia://en/272134) scholar, currently serving as the senior advisor to the president of Georgetown University, following a term as interim president of [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University) from November 2024 to July 2026.

:::

He served as executive vice president and provost of Georgetown University from August 2012 to November 2024 and as the 23rd [director of the United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_the_United_States_Census_Bureau) from 2009 to 2012.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Groves was born in [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), and grew up in [Metairie, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metairie,_Louisiana). He graduated in 1966 from [De La Salle High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Salle_High_School_%28New_Orleans%29) in [New Orleans, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans).[^o3][^o5]

In 1970, Groves graduated *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude)* and [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) from [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), receiving a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree with a major in [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology).[^o6][^o7] He later received two [Master of Arts degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) (sociology and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), both 1973) and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (sociology, 1975) from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan).[^o7][^o8]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

For much of his career, starting in 1975, Groves worked as a [research professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_professor) in [survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_survey) [methodology](wikipedia://en/20041877) at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). He was also a researcher in the [Joint Program in Survey Methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Program_in_Survey_Methodology), housed at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park). In August 2012, he became the Provost of [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University), succeeding [James J. O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J._O'Donnell)[^o10] and is also the Gerard J. Campbell, S.J. Professor in the Math and Statistics Department as well as the Sociology Department.[^o11]

In 1982 Groves was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o12] He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2011 and is a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o10]

## Government career {#Government_career}

When Groves was an associate director at the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070) in the early 1990s, he argued that potentially millions of [minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group) who typically voted [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_%28United_States%29) were being undercounted. Groves advocated for the use of statistical adjustments to account for this discrepancy. [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush)'s [Commerce Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Secretary_of_Commerce) [Robert Mosbacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mosbacher) blocked this suggestion. The Census Bureau is a component of the [U.S. Department of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Commerce).[^o7]

On April 2, 2009, Groves was nominated by President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) to head the Census Bureau.[^o14] During his confirmation hearings, Republican [senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senator) raised concerns based on Groves' previous actions that he would apply statistical adjustments to populations believed to be undercounted, thereby inflating their numbers and affecting the apportionment of congressional seats. Groves ruled out the use of statistical adjustments during the hearings and his nomination quickly proceeded to the full Senate in May. However, senators [Richard Shelby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Shelby) and [David Vitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Vitter) continued to delay his confirmation due to lingering worries regarding statistical adjustments. They also sought assurance from the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_administration) that the controversial community group [ACORN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Community_Organizations_for_Reform_Now) would not be involved in grassroots outreach related to the [2010 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Census). On July 13, 2009, after several weeks of opposition by the two senators, [Senate majority leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_majority_leader) [Harry Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Reid) used a procedural motion to force a vote that confirmed Groves, 76–15.[^o15] He took office July 15, 2009.[^o16][^o17]

On June 13, 2014, President Obama announced his appointment of Groves to the [National Science Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Board) of the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation).[^o18]

## Publishing history {#Publishing_history}

Groves is the author of several books, including:

- *Surveying Victims* (2008)
- *Survey Errors and Survey Costs* (2004) first published in 1989 , and named "one of the 50 most influential books in survey research" by the [American Association for Public Opinion Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_Public_Opinion_Research)[^o11]
- *Survey Methodology* (2010) Second edition of the (2004) first edition
- *Survey Nonresponse* (2001)
- *Nonresponse in Household Interview Surveys* (1998)

Groves is the editor of several books, including:

- *Measurement Errors in Surveys* (2004)
- *Telephone Survey Methodology* (2001)
