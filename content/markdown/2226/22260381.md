# Irreducible ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Irreducible ideal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [proper](wikipedia://en/25977#Examples_and_properties) [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) is said to be **irreducible** if it cannot be written as the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of two strictly larger ideals.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) is irreducible.[^o5] Let $J$ and $K$ be ideals of a commutative ring $R$, with neither one contained in the other. Then there exist $a\in J \setminus K$ and $b\in K \setminus J$, where neither is in $J \cap K$ but the product is. This proves that a reducible ideal is not prime. A concrete example of this are the ideals $2 \mathbb Z$ and $3 \mathbb Z$ contained in $\mathbb Z$. The intersection is $6 \mathbb Z$, and $6 \mathbb Z$ is not a prime ideal.
- Every irreducible ideal of a [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) is a [primary ideal](wikipedia://en/3021435),[^o2] and consequently for Noetherian rings an irreducible decomposition is a [primary decomposition](wikipedia://en/1208391).[^o6]
- Every primary ideal of a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039) is an irreducible ideal.
- Every irreducible ideal is [primal](wikipedia://en/25360385).[^o7]
- Every irreducible ideal that is also radical is prime. The ideal $4\mathbb{Z}$ is an example of an irreducible ideal in $\ce{\mathbb{Z}}$ that is not radical and not a prime ideal.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

An element of an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) is [prime](wikipedia://en/60692) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the ideal [generated by it](wikipedia://en/145126) is a non-zero prime ideal. This is not true for irreducible ideals; an irreducible ideal may be generated by an element that is not an [irreducible element](wikipedia://en/60693), as is the case in $\mathbb Z$ for the ideal $4 \mathbb Z$ since it is not the intersection of two strictly greater ideals.

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), if an ideal $I$ of a ring $R$ is irreducible, then $V(I)$ is an [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_space) subset in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) on the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) $\operatorname{Spec} R$. The [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) does not hold; for example the ideal $(x^2,xy,y^2)$ in $\mathbb C[x,y]$ defines the irreducible [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) consisting of just the origin, but it is not an irreducible ideal as $(x^2,xy,y^2) = (x^2,y) \cap (x,y^2)$.
