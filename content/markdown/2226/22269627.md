# Pieri's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pieri's formula"}

In mathematics, **Pieri's formula**, named after [Mario Pieri](wikipedia://en/2452874), describes the product of a [Schubert cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_cycle) by a special Schubert cycle in the [Schubert calculus](wikipedia://en/3061740), or the product of a [Schur polynomial](wikipedia://en/3147062) by a complete symmetric function.

:::

In terms of Schur functions *s*<sub>λ</sub> indexed by [partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) λ, it states that

$$\displaystyle s_\mu h_r=\sum_\lambda s_\lambda$$

where *h*<sub>*r*</sub> is a [complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial](wikipedia://en/10176565) and the sum is over all partitions λ obtained from μ by adding *r* elements, no two in the same column. By applying the ω involution on the ring of symmetric functions, one obtains the dual Pieri rule for multiplying an [elementary symmetric polynomial](wikipedia://en/1440695) with a Schur polynomial:

$$\displaystyle s_\mu e_r=\sum_\lambda s_\lambda$$

The sum is now taken over all partitions λ obtained from μ by adding *r* elements, no two in the same *row*.

Pieri's formula implies [Giambelli's formula](wikipedia://en/22269939). The [Littlewood–Richardson rule](wikipedia://en/16004359) is a generalization of Pieri's formula giving the product of any two Schur functions. [Monk's formula](wikipedia://en/22444842) is an analogue of Pieri's formula for flag manifolds.
