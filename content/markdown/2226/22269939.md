# Giambelli's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giambelli's formula"}

In mathematics, **Giambelli's formula**, named after [Giovanni Giambelli](wikipedia://en/22359761), expresses [Schubert classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_class) as determinants in terms of special Schubert classes.

:::

It states

$$\displaystyle \sigma_\lambda= \det(\sigma_{\lambda_i+j-i})_{1\le i,j\le r}$$

where σ<sub>λ</sub> is the Schubert class of a [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) λ.

Giambelli's formula may be derived as a consequence of [Pieri's formula](wikipedia://en/22269627). The [Porteous formula](wikipedia://en/34781467) is a generalization to morphisms of vector bundles over a variety.

In the theory of symmetric functions, the same identity, known as the [first Jacobi-Trudi identity](wikipedia://en/3147062#Jacobi−Trudi_identities) expresses [Schur functions](wikipedia://en/3147062) as determinants in terms of [complete symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_symmetric_function). There is also the **dual** [second Jacobi-Trudi identity](wikipedia://en/3147062#Jacobi−Trudi_identities) which expresses [Schur functions](wikipedia://en/3147062) as determinants in terms of [elementary symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_symmetric_function). The corresponding identity also holds for Schubert classes.

There is another [Giambelli identity](wikipedia://en/3147062#Giambelli_identity), expressing [Schur functions](wikipedia://en/3147062) as determinants of matrices whose entries are Schur functions corresponding to *hook partitions* contained within the same [Young diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_diagram). This too is valid for Schubert classes, as are all Schur function identities. For instance, hook partition Schur functions can be expressed bilinearly in terms of elementary and complete symmetric functions, and Schubert classes satisfy these same relations.
