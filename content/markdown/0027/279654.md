# Truncated octahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated octahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated octahedron** is the [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847) that arises from a regular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) by removing six [equilateral square pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilateral_square_pyramid), one at each of the octahedron's vertices. It is also the [cantitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690#Cantitruncation) form of a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521).[^o3] The truncated octahedron has 14 faces (8 regular [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733) and 6 [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29)), 36 edges, and 24 vertices. Since each of its faces has [point symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_symmetry), the truncated octahedron is a 6-[zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402). It is also the [Goldberg polyhedron](wikipedia://en/39781140) G<sub>IV</sub>(1,1), containing square and hexagonal faces. Like the cube, it can tessellate (or "pack") 3-dimensional space, as a [permutohedron](wikipedia://en/12745947).

:::

The truncated octahedron is also known as the **orthic [tetrakaidecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrakaidecahedron)**. [Buckminster Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckminster_Fuller) called it the "mecon".

Its [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) is the [tetrakis hexahedron](wikipedia://en/722554). If the original truncated octahedron has unit edge length, then its dual tetrakis hexahedron has edge lengths $\tfrac{9}{8}$$\sqrt{2}$ and $\tfrac{3}{2}$$\sqrt{2}$.

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

As suggested by its name, a truncated octahedron is constructed from a [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458) by cutting off all vertices. This resulting polyhedron has six squares and eight hexagons, leaving out six [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455). It can additionally be constructed by [cantitruncating](wikipedia://en/7083690#Cantitruncation) a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521).[^o7]

The [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) of the vertices of a truncated octahedron with edge length 1 are all permutations of

$$\bigl(\pm\sqrt{2}, \pm\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, 0\bigr).$$

The truncated octahedron is the result of the regular octahedron's edges being expanded. The edges are separated and pushed away in the direction of two-fold rotational symmetry axes passing through the midpoint of opposite edges in the [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805). With the distance between the closest parallel edges that is the regular octahedron's edge-length, connecting the endpoints of each edge to form hexagons and triangles, the highlight shows that the third [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), the [triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1128465), coincides with the hexagonal faces.

(Loeb 1986) constructs a truncated octahedron by attaching eight cubes to each face. In this hinge, eight segments on each half-cube form hexagons, and the hexagons bisect the cubes. It thus can fold inward and outward, forming a truncated octahedron and a cube, respectively.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Setting the edge length of the regular octahedron equal to $3a$, it follows that the length of each edge of a square pyramid (to be removed) is $a$ (the square pyramid has four [equilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilateral_square_pyramid) triangles as faces, the first [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034)). The volume of each of these equilateral square pyramids is $\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{6}a^3$. Because six of them are removed by truncation, the volume $V$ of the truncated octahedron is given by

$$V = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3} (3a)^3 - 6 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{6} a^3 = 8\sqrt{2}a^3 \approx 11.3137 a^3.$$

The surface area $A$ of this truncated octahedron can be obtained by summing all polygons' area, six squares and eight hexagons:

$$A = (6 + 12\sqrt{3})a^2 \approx 26.7846a^2.$$

*Image: Truncated octahedron.stl.* 3D model of a truncated octahedron

The truncated octahedron is one of the thirteen [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847). In other words, it is a highly symmetric, semi-regular polyhedron with two or more different regular polygonal faces meeting at a vertex. The [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of a truncated octahedron is the [tetrakis hexahedron](wikipedia://en/722554). They both have the same three-dimensional symmetry group as the regular octahedron does, the [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805) $\mathrm{O}_\mathrm{h}$. Each vertex is surrounded by a square and two hexagons, so its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is $4 \cdot 6^2$.

A truncated octahedron has two different [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046), angles between two polygonal faces. The angle between square and hexagonal faces is $\arccos(-1/\sqrt{3}) \approx 125.26^\circ$, and the angle between two adjacent hexagonal faces is $\arccos (-1/3) \approx 109.47^\circ$.

## Other classifications {#Other_classifications}

### As a permutohedron {#As_a_permutohedron}

*Image: Symmetric group 4; permutohedron 3D; transpositions (1-based).png.* Truncated octahedron as a permutahedron of order 4

The truncated octahedron can be described as a [permutohedron](wikipedia://en/12745947) of order 4, or **4-permutohedron**, meaning it can be represented with even more symmetric coordinates in four dimensions: all permutations of $(1, 2, 3, 4)$ form the vertices of a truncated octahedron in the three-dimensional subspace $x + y + z + w = 10$. Therefore, each vertex corresponds to a permutation of $(1, 2, 3, 4)$ and each edge represents a single pairwise swap of two elements. With this labeling, the swaps are of elements whose values differ by one. If, instead, the truncated octahedron is labeled by the inverse permutations, then the edges correspond to swaps of elements whose positions differ by one. With this alternative labeling, the edges and vertices of the truncated octahedron form the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_4$, the group of four-element permutations, as generated by swaps of consecutive positions.

### As a space-filling polyhedron {#As_a_space-filling_polyhedron}

*Image: Truncated octahedra.png.* Truncated octahedra tiling space

The truncated octahedron is a [space-filling polyhedron](wikipedia://en/58607583); that is, it can tile space by translating its copies face-to-face in order to form a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654). It is classified as [plesiohedron](wikipedia://en/53396858), meaning it can be defined as the [Voronoi cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_cell) of a symmetric [Delone set](wikipedia://en/38607512). Plesiohedra, [translated](wikipedia://en/204682) without rotating, can be repeated to fill space. The truncated octahedron is one of the five three-dimensional primary [parallelohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallelohedra); the other four are the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), the [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668), the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), and the [elongated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3515524). The truncated octahedron is generated from six line segments with four triples of coplanar segments. In the most symmetric form, it is generated from six line segments parallel to the face diagonals of a cube. The truncated octahedron tile a honeycomb known as a [bitruncated cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3736299). More generally, every permutohedron and parallelohedron is a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402), a polyhedron that is [centrally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrally_symmetric) and can be defined by a [Minkowski sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_sum).

## Appearances {#Appearances}

The truncated octahedron has been used as a fourteen-sided [die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice), dating back to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) in [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period), although a cube was also used as well.

The truncated octahedron appears in the structure in the framework of a [faujasite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faujasite)-type of [zeolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeolite) crystals. The structure consists of sodalite cages that resemble truncated octahedra, connecting each other by hexagonal prisms

In [solid-state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics), the first [Brillouin zone](wikipedia://en/562067) of the [face-centered cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-centered_cubic) lattice is a truncated octahedron.

The shape of a truncated octahedron appears as the [Wigner–Seitz cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner–Seitz_cell) of a [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium). The background for this discovery dates back to [Eugene Wigner](wikipedia://en/10101) and [Frederick Seitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Seitz)'s proposal on the Wigner–Seitz cell's application to [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics) on solving the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) for free electrons in sodium.

The truncated octahedron (in fact, the generalized truncated octahedron) appears in the [error analysis](wikipedia://en/44281046) of [quantization index modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_index_modulation) (QIM) in conjunction with repetition coding.[^o15]

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

The truncated octahedron can be dissected into a central [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), surrounded by 8 [triangular cupolae](wikipedia://en/1128465) on each face, and 6 [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) above the vertices.[^o17] Removing the central octahedron and 2 or 4 triangular cupolae creates two [Stewart toroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_toroid), with dihedral and tetrahedral symmetry.

It is possible to slice a [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) by a hyperplane so that its sliced cross-section is a truncated octahedron.[^o18]

The [cell-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-transitive) [bitruncated cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3736299) can also be seen as the [Voronoi tessellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_tessellation) of the [body-centered cubic lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_structure). The truncated octahedron is one of five three-dimensional primary [parallelohedra](wikipedia://en/25207334#Zonohedra_that_tile_space).

## Truncated octahedral graph {#Truncated_octahedral_graph}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **truncated octahedral graph** is the [graph of vertices and edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-skeleton) of the truncated octahedron. It has 24 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 36 edges, and is a [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [Archimedean graph](wikipedia://en/44969442).[^o23] It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o24]

As a [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508), it can be represented by [LCF notation](wikipedia://en/24186720) in multiple ways: \[3, −7, 7, −3\]<sup>6</sup>, \[5, −11, 11, 7, 5, −5, −7, −11, 11, −5, −7, 7\]<sup>2</sup>, and \[−11, 5, −3, −7, −9, 3, −5, 5, −3, 9, 7, 3, −5, 11, −3, 7, 5, −7, −9, 9, 7, −5, −7, 3\].
