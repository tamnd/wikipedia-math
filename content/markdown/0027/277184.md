# Mathematical notation

For information on rendering mathematical formulae, see Help:Displaying a formula and Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Mathematics.

*Image: Highlighted LaTeX example.webp.* Highlighted [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) mathematical notation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematical notation"}

**Mathematical notation** consists of using [symbols](wikipedia://en/73634) for representing [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245), unspecified [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690), [relations](wikipedia://en/22517558), and any other [mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961) and assembling them into [expressions](wikipedia://en/609125) and [formulas](wikipedia://en/164040). Mathematical notation is widely used in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science), and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) for representing complex [concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept) and [properties](wikipedia://en/508057) in a concise, unambiguous, and accurate way.

:::

For example, the physicist [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s formula $E=mc^2$ is the quantitative representation in mathematical notation of [mass–energy equivalence](wikipedia://en/422481).[^o2]

Mathematical notation was first introduced by [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238) at the end of the 16th century and largely expanded during the 17th and 18th centuries by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525), [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627), [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281), and overall [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902).

## Symbols and typeface {#Symbols_and_typeface}

The use of many symbols is the basis of mathematical notation. They play a similar role as words in [natural languages](wikipedia://en/21173). They may play different roles in mathematical notation similarly as verbs, adjective and nouns play different roles in a sentence.

### Letters as symbols {#Letters_as_symbols}

Letters are typically used for naming—in [mathematical jargon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mathematical_jargon), one says *representing*—[mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961). The [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet) and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet) alphabets are used extensively, but a few letters of other alphabets are also used sporadically, such as the [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_alphabet) $\aleph$, [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_script) $\text{Ш}$, and [Hiragana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragana) $\text{よ}$. Uppercase and lowercase letters are considered as different symbols. For Latin alphabet, different typefaces also provide different symbols. For example, $r, R, \mathbb{R}, \mathcal R, \mathfrak r,$ and $\mathfrak R$ could theoretically appear in the same mathematical text with six different meanings. Normally, roman upright typeface is not used for symbols, except for symbols representing a standard function, such as the symbol "$\sin$" of the [sine function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_function).[^o5]

In order to have more symbols, and for allowing related mathematical objects to be represented by related symbols, [diacritics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diacritic), [subscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscript) and [superscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superscript) are often used. For example, $\hat {f'_1}$ may denote the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) called $f_1.$

### Other symbols {#Other_symbols}

Symbols are not only used for naming mathematical objects. They can be used for [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245) $(+, -, /, \oplus, \ldots),$ for [relations](wikipedia://en/22517558) $(=, <, \le, \sim, \equiv, \ldots),$ for [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) $(\implies, \land, \lor, \ldots),$ for [quantifiers](wikipedia://en/43507260) $(\forall, \exists),$ and for other purposes.

Some symbols are similar to Latin or Greek letters, some are obtained by deforming letters, some are traditional [typographic symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typographic_symbol), but many have been specially designed for mathematics.

### International standard mathematical notation {#International_standard_mathematical_notation}

The [International Organization for Standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organization_for_Standardization) (ISO) is an [international standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_standard) development organization composed of representatives from the national [standards organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standards_organization) of member countries. The international standard [ISO 80000-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_80000-2) (previously, [ISO 31-11](wikipedia://en/3371683)) specifies symbols for use in mathematical equations. The standard requires use of italic fonts for variables (e.g., *E* = *mc*<sup>2</sup>) and roman (upright) fonts for mathematical constants (e.g., e or π).

## Expressions and formulas {#Expressions_and_formulas}

An [expression](wikipedia://en/609125) is a written arrangement of [symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_%28mathematics%29) following the context-dependent, [syntactic](wikipedia://en/546039) conventions of mathematical notation. Symbols can denote numbers, [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109), [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245), and [functions](wikipedia://en/185427).[^o10] Other symbols include [punctuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punctuation) marks and [brackets](wikipedia://en/11219603), used for [grouping](wikipedia://en/73545085) where there is not a well-defined [order of operations](wikipedia://en/212980).

Expressions are commonly distinguished from *[formulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_formula)*: expressions are a kind of [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961), whereas formulas are statements *about* mathematical objects.[^o11] This is analogous to [natural language](wikipedia://en/21173), where a [noun phrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun_phrase) refers to an object, and a whole [sentence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentence_%28linguistics%29) refers to a [fact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact). For example, $8x-5$ is an expression, while the [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) $8x-5 \geq 3$ is a formula.

To *evaluate* an expression means to find a numerical [value](wikipedia://en/4095147) equivalent to the expression.[^o12][^o13] Expressions can be *evaluated* or *simplified* by replacing [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245) that appear in them with their result. For example, the expression $8\times 2-5$ simplifies to $16-5$, and evaluates to $11.$

:::

## History {#History}

### Numbers {#Numbers}

It is believed that a notation to represent numbers was first developed at least 50,000 years ago.[^o27] Early mathematical ideas such as [finger counting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_counting)[^o25] have also been represented by collections of rocks, sticks, bone, clay, stone, wood carvings, and knotted ropes. The [tally stick](wikipedia://en/30872676) is a way of counting dating back to the [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic). Perhaps the oldest known mathematical texts are those of ancient [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer). The [Census Quipu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_quipu) of the Andes and the [Ishango Bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishango_Bone) from Africa both used the [tally mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tally_mark) method of accounting for numerical concepts.

The concept of [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero) and the introduction of a notation for it are important developments in early mathematics, which predates for centuries the concept of zero as a number. It was used as a placeholder by the [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_numerals) and [Greek Egyptians](wikipedia://en/166697), and then as an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) by the [Mayans](wikipedia://en/19823), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_numerals) and [Muslims](wikipedia://en/1786) (see [the history of zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_zero)).

### Modern notation {#Modern_notation}

Until the 16th century, mathematics was essentially [rhetorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetorical_algebra), in the sense that everything but explicit numbers was expressed in words. However, some authors, such as [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110), used some symbols as abbreviations.

[Michael Stifel](wikipedia://en/26485293)'s (1487–1567) most important work, Arithmetica integra (1544), contains important innovations in mathematical notation. It has the first use of multiplication by juxtaposition (with no symbol between the terms) in Europe. He is the first to use the term *exponent*.

The first systematic use of formulas, and, in particular, the use of symbols ([variables](wikipedia://en/3728109)) for unspecified numbers, is generally attributed to [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238) (1540–1603). However, he used different symbols than those that are now standard.

Later, [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) (1596–1650) introduced the modern notation for variables and [equations](wikipedia://en/9284); in particular, the use of $x,y,z$ for [unknown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unknown_%28mathematics%29) quantities and $a,b,c$ for known ones ([constants](wikipedia://en/24758132)). He introduced also the notation $i$ and the term *imaginary* for the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358).

The 18th and 19th centuries saw the standardization of mathematical notation as used today. [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) (1707–1783) was responsible for many of the notations currently in use: the [functional notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_notation) $f(x),$ $e$ for the base of the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476), $\sum$ for [summation](wikipedia://en/246160), etc.[^o26] He also popularized the use of $\pi$ for the [Archimedes constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes_constant) (proposed by [William Jones](wikipedia://en/239126), based on an earlier notation of [William Oughtred](wikipedia://en/475249)).[^o28]

Since then many new notations have been introduced, often specific to a particular area of mathematics. Some notations are named after their inventors, such as [Leibniz's notation](wikipedia://en/410712), [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567), the [Einstein summation convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_summation_convention), etc.

### Typesetting {#Typesetting}

General [typesetting systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typesetting_system) are generally not well suited for mathematical notation. One of the reasons is that, in mathematical notation, the symbols are often arranged in two-dimensional figures, such as in:

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac {\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}^n}{n!}.$$

[TeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX) () is a mathematically oriented typesetting system that was created in 1978 by [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095). It is widely used in mathematics, through its extension called [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) ( or ), and is a *de facto* standard. (The above expression is written in LaTeX.)

More recently, another approach for mathematical typesetting is provided by [MathML](wikipedia://en/52313). However, it is not well supported in web browsers, which are its primary target.

## Non-Latin-based mathematical notation {#Non-Latin-based_mathematical_notation}

[Modern Arabic mathematical notation](wikipedia://en/12266333) is based mostly on the [Arabic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet) and is used widely in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), especially in pre-[tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education). (Western notation uses [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786), but the Arabic notation also replaces Latin letters and related symbols with Arabic script.)

In addition to Arabic notation, mathematics also makes use of [Greek letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet) to denote a wide variety of mathematical objects and variables. On some occasions, certain [Hebrew letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_alphabet) are also used (such as in the context of [infinite cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cardinal)).

Some mathematical notations are mostly diagrammatic, and so are almost entirely script independent. Examples are [Penrose graphical notation](wikipedia://en/2973987) and [Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/5358554).

Braille-based mathematical notations used by blind people include [Nemeth Braille](wikipedia://en/696794) and [GS8 Braille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GS8_Braille).

## Meaning and interpretation {#Meaning_and_interpretation}

The [syntax](wikipedia://en/26860) of notation defines how symbols can be combined to make [well-formed expressions](wikipedia://en/609125#Well-defined_expressions), without any given meaning or interpretation. The [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107) of notation interprets what the symbols represent and assigns a meaning to the expressions and formulas. The reverse process of taking a statement and writing it in logical or mathematical notation is called [translation](wikipedia://en/73032003).

### Interpretation {#Interpretation}

Given a [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939), an [interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) assigns a [domain of discourse](wikipedia://en/229180) to the language. Specifically, it assigns each of the constant symbols to objects of the domain, function letters to functions within the domain, predicate letters to statements, and vairiables are assumed to range over the domain.

### Map–territory relation {#Map–territory_relation}

The [map–territory relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map–territory_relation) describes the relationship between an object and the representation of that object, such as the [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228) and a [map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map) of it. In mathematics, this is how the number 4 relates to its representation "4". The quotation marks are the formally correct usage, distinguishing the number from its name. However, it is fairly common practice in math to commit this fallacy saying "Let x denote...", rather than "Let "x" denote..." which is generally harmless.
