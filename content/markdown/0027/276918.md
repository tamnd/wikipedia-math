# Division sign

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Division sign"}

The **division sign** (**÷**) is a [mathematical symbol](wikipedia://en/73634) consisting of a short horizontal line with a dot above and another dot below, used in [Anglophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone) countries to indicate the operation of [division](wikipedia://en/53696). This usage is not universal and the symbol has different meanings in other countries. Consequently, its use to denote division is deprecated in the [ISO 80000-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_80000-2) standard for notations used in mathematics, science and technology.[^o7] In older textbooks, it is called an *[obelus](wikipedia://en/63530168)*, though that term is also used of other symbols.

:::

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

*Image: Skjermbilete 2012-11-03 kl. 02.48.36.png.* An obelus being used as a sign of subtraction in this excerpt from an official Norwegian trading statement form called «Næringsoppgave 1» used for tax purposes.

The obelus, a historical [grapheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapheme) consisting of a horizontal line with (or without) one or more dots, was first used as a symbol for division in 1659, in the [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) book *Teutsche Algebra* by [Johann Rahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Rahn), although previous writers had used the same symbol for subtraction.[^o4] Some near-contemporaries believed that [John Pell](wikipedia://en/491570), who edited the book, may have been responsible for this use of the symbol.[^o4] Other symbols for division include the [slash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash_%28punctuation%29) or solidus /, the [colon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_%28punctuation%29) :, and the [fraction bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_%28mathematics%29#Vocabulary) (the horizontal bar in a vertical fraction).[^o5][^o6] The ISO 80000-2 standard for mathematical notation in science and technology recommends only the solidus / or "fraction bar" for division, or the "colon" : for [ratios](wikipedia://en/87837); it says that the ÷ sign "should not be used" for division.[^o7] Nevertheless, ÷ continues to be used on [scientific calculators](wikipedia://en/439711).

In [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia), the same ÷ sign was sometimes used to denote a [range of values](wikipedia://en/49172), and in [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) countries it was, and sometimes still is, used as a negation sign:[^o8] the [Unicode Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode_Consortium) has allocated a separate [code point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point), 2052 for this usage uniquely;[^o9][^o10] the exact form of the symbol displayed is typeface (font) dependent.

## In computer systems {#In_computer_systems}

### Encoding {#Encoding}

The symbol was assigned to [code point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) 0xF7 in [ISO 8859-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_8859-1), as the "division sign". This encoding was transferred to [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) as U+00F7.[^o13] In [HTML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML), it can be [encoded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_encodings_in_HTML) as ÷ or ÷ (at [HTML level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_XML_and_HTML_character_entity_references) 3.2), or as ÷.

[Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) provides various division symbols:[^o14]

::: {#o15 .table cue=table}

| Codepoint | Name | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| U+00F7 | Division Sign | ÷ |
| U+27CC | Long Division | ⟌ |
| U+2215 | [Division Slash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_slash) | ∕ |
| U+2A38 | Circled Division Sign | ⨸ |
| U+2797 | Heavy Division Sign | ➗ |
| U+2298 | Circled Division Slash | ⊘ |
| U+22C7 | Division Times | ⋇ |
| U+29BC | Circled Anticlockwise-Rotated Division Sign | ⦼ |

:::

::: {#o17 .application cue=heading name="Use"}

### Use {#Use}

Most programming languages use only the 128 [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) characters, and so do not use the ÷ character. However, the programming language [APL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APL_%28programming_language%29) uses ÷ for the unary [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) operator and the binary division operator.

:::
