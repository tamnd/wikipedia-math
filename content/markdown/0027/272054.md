# Weakly compact cardinal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weakly compact cardinal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **weakly compact cardinal** is a certain kind of [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) introduced by (Erdős Tarski 1961); weakly compact cardinals are [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644), meaning that their existence cannot be proven from the [standard axioms of set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC). (Tarski originally called them "not strongly incompact" cardinals.)

:::

Formally, a cardinal κ is defined to be weakly compact if it is uncountable and for every function *f*: \[κ\] <sup>2</sup> → {0, 1} there is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) κ that is [homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_%28large_cardinal_property%29) for *f*. In this context, \[κ\] <sup>2</sup> means the set of 2-element subsets of κ, and a subset *S* of κ is homogeneous for *f* if either all of \[*S*\]<sup>2</sup> maps to 0 or all of it maps to 1.

The name "weakly compact" refers to the fact that if a cardinal is weakly compact then a certain related [infinitary language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitary_language) satisfies a version of the [compactness theorem](wikipedia://en/152207); see below.

## Equivalent formulations {#Equivalent_formulations}

The following are equivalent for any [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) cardinal κ:

1. κ is weakly compact.
1. for every λ\<κ, [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) n ≥ 2, and function f: \[κ\]<sup>n</sup> → λ, there is a set of cardinality κ that is [homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_%28large_cardinal_property%29) for f. (Drake 1974, chapter 7 theorem 3.5)
1. κ is [inaccessible](wikipedia://en/248076) and has the [tree property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_property), that is, every [tree](wikipedia://en/704160) of height κ has either a level of size κ or a branch of size κ.
1. Every linear order of cardinality κ has an ascending or a descending sequence of [order type](wikipedia://en/4499021) κ. (W. W. Comfort, S. Negrepontis, *The Theory of Ultrafilters*, p.185)
1. κ is $\Pi^1_1$-[indescribable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_indescribable_cardinal).
1. κ has the extension property. In other words, for all *U* ⊂ *V*<sub>κ</sub> there exists a transitive set *X* with κ ∈ *X*, and a subset *S* ⊂ *X*, such that (*V*<sub>κ</sub>, ∈, *U*) is an [elementary substructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_substructure) of (*X*, ∈, *S*). Here, *U* and *S* are regarded as unary [predicates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28mathematical_logic%29).
1. For every set S of cardinality κ of subsets of κ, there is a non-trivial κ-complete filter that decides S.
1. κ is κ-[unfoldable](wikipedia://en/853783).
1. κ is inaccessible and the [infinitary language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitary_language) *L*<sub>κ,κ</sub> satisfies the weak compactness theorem.
1. κ is inaccessible and the [infinitary language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitary_language) *L*<sub>κ,ω</sub> satisfies the weak compactness theorem.
1. κ is inaccessible and for every [transitive set](wikipedia://en/2462396) $M$ of cardinality κ with κ $\in M$, ${}^{<\kappa}M\subset M$, and satisfying a sufficiently large fragment of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC), there is an [elementary embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_embedding) $j$ from $M$ to a transitive set $N$ of cardinality κ such that $^{<\kappa}N\subset N$, with [critical point](wikipedia://en/4888510) $crit(j)=$κ. (Hauser 1991, Theorem 1.3)
1. $\kappa=\kappa^{<\kappa}$ ($\kappa^{<\kappa}$ defined as $\sum_{\lambda<\kappa}\kappa^\lambda$) and every $\kappa$-complete filter of a $\kappa$-complete [field of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676) of cardinality $\leq\kappa$ is contained in a $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter. (W. W. Comfort, S. Negrepontis, *The Theory of Ultrafilters*, p.185)
1. $\kappa$ has Alexander's property, i.e. for any space $X$ with a $\kappa$-subbase $\mathcal A$ with cardinality $\leq\kappa$, and every cover of $X$ by elements of $\mathcal A$ has a subcover of cardinality $<\kappa$, then $X$ is $\kappa$-compact. (W. W. Comfort, S. Negrepontis, *The Theory of Ultrafilters*, p.182--185)
1. $(2^{\kappa})_\kappa$ is $\kappa$-compact. (W. W. Comfort, S. Negrepontis, *The Theory of Ultrafilters*, p.185)

A language *L*<sub>κ,κ</sub> is said to satisfy the weak compactness theorem if whenever Σ is a set of sentences of cardinality at most κ and every subset with less than κ elements has a model, then Σ has a model. [Strongly compact cardinals](wikipedia://en/1957057) are defined in a similar way without the restriction on the cardinality of the set of sentences.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every weakly compact cardinal is a [reflecting cardinal](wikipedia://en/18449365), and is also a limit of reflecting cardinals. This means also that weakly compact cardinals are [Mahlo cardinals](wikipedia://en/248081), and the set of Mahlo cardinals less than a given weakly compact cardinal is [stationary](wikipedia://en/1679067).

If $\kappa$ is weakly compact, then there are chains of well-founded elementary end-extensions of $(V_\kappa,\in)$ of arbitrary length $<\kappa^+$.[^o8]<sup>p.6</sup>

Weakly compact cardinals remain weakly compact in $L$.[^o9] Assuming V = L, a cardinal is weakly compact iff it is 2-stationary.[^o10]

:::
