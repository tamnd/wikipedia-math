# Wheels, Life and Other Mathematical Amusements

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wheels, Life and Other Mathematical Amusements"}

***Wheels, Life and Other Mathematical Amusements*** is a book by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) published in 1983. The Basic Library List Committee of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) has recommended its inclusion in undergraduate mathematics libraries.[^o3]

:::

## Contents {#Contents}

*Wheels, Life and Other Mathematical Amusements* is a book of 22 mathematical games columns that were revised and extended after being previously published in *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*.[^o7] It is Gardner's 10th collection of columns, and includes material on [Conway's Game of Life](wikipedia://en/37035), [supertasks](wikipedia://en/314383), [intransitive dice](wikipedia://en/4519119), braided [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra), [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231), the [Collatz conjecture](wikipedia://en/37895), mathematical card tricks, and [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) such as [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).[^o5]

## Reception {#Reception}

[Dave Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Langford) reviewed *Wheels, Life and Other Mathematical Amusements* for *[White Dwarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Dwarf_%28magazine%29)* #55, and stated that "Here too are revisions of the three famous pieces on Conway's solitaire game *Life*, which has absorbed several National Debts' worth of computer time since 1970. Fascinating."[^o7] The book was positively reviewed in several other mathematics and science journals.[^o8]
