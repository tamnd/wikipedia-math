# Doris Fischer-Colbrie

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Doris Fischer-Colbrie"}

**Doris Helga Fischer-Colbrie** (born in 1949)[^o3] is a [ceramic artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic_art) and former [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o4] She received her Ph.D. in mathematics in 1978 from [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where her advisor was [H. Blaine Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson).

:::

Many of her contributions to the theory of [minimal surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surfaces) are now considered foundational to the field. In particular, her collaboration with [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen) is a landmark contribution to the interaction of stable minimal surfaces with nonnegative [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622).[^o6] A particular result, also obtained by [Manfredo do Carmo](wikipedia://en/45041862) and Chiakuei Peng, is that the only complete stable minimal surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ are planes.[^o7] Her work on unstable minimal surfaces gave the basic tools by which to relate the assumption of finite index to conditions on stable subdomains and [total curvature](wikipedia://en/19172363).[^o8][^o9]

After positions at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University), Fischer-Colbrie left academia to become a ceramic artist. She is married to Schoen, with whom she has two children.[^o10]
