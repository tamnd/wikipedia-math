# Angus MacFarlane-Grieve

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Angus MacFarlane-Grieve"}

**Alexander Angus MacFarlane-Grieve**, (11 May 1891 – 2 August 1970) was a British academic, mathematician, [rower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowing_%28sport%29), and decorated [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army) officer. He served with the [Highland Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Light_Infantry) during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I). He was [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_%28college%29) of [University College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Durham) from 1939 to 1954, and additionally Master of [Hatfield College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_College,_Durham) from 1940 to 1949.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

MacFarlane-Grieve was born on 11 May 1891,[^o3] and was [baptised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptised) at [St Mary Abbots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary_Abbots), an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) church in [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington), London.[^o6] He was educated at [The Perse School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perse_School), a [private school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_schools_in_the_United_Kingdom) in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), England.[^o3] He went on to study [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [University College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Durham), and graduated with a Bachelor of Arts (BA) degree in 1913.[^o4] He [rowed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowing_%28sport%29) for both his college ([University College Boat Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Boat_Club_%28Durham%29)) and for the university ([Durham University Boat Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University_Boat_Club)).[^o3] He was President of the DUBC during his final year, from 1912 to 1913.[^o3]

## Military service {#Military_service}

On 15 August 1914, having been a member of the [Officer Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_Training_Corps) while at university, MacFarlane-Grieve was commissioned into the 4th Battalion, [The Highland Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Highland_Light_Infantry), [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army), as a [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant) (on probation). In December 1914, his commission and rank were confirmed. In July 1915, he attended the Scottish Command School of Signalling at [Peebles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peebles), Tweeddale, Scotland.[^o10] He then became one of three officers commanding the 4th Battalion's signal section.[^o11] By June 1917, he had been promoted to [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_%28British_Army_and_Royal_Marines%29).[^o12] By the end of the war, he held the [acting rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting_rank) of [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel_%28United_Kingdom%29).[^o13]

On 4 August 1923, he was transferred to the Regular Army Reserve of Officers (thereby ending his army career) and was promoted to [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_%28United_Kingdom%29) with seniority from 4 May 1922. While an academic at Durham, he was an officer of the Durham University contingent of the Officer Training Corps.[^o19]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 1923, having left the army, MacFarlane-Grieve returned to [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University) to become a lecturer in military subjects.[^o4] Between 1923 and 1939, he was also [Bursar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursar) of [University College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Durham).[^o16] In 1939, at the age of 47, he was appointed [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_%28college%29) of University College.[^o3][^o4] He was the first head of the college not to be not in [Holy Orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Orders): i.e. he was the first [layman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layman).[^o3] With [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) causing a shrinkage in student numbers, he was additionally appointed acting Master of [Hatfield College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_College,_Durham) in 1940. He stood down from that acting appointment in 1949 and was succeeded by [Eric Birley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Birley).[^o16] From 1948 to 1953, he was Sub-Warden of the Durham Colleges and therefore the deputy of the [Vice-Chancellor and Warden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Vice-Chancellors_and_Wardens_of_the_Durham_University) with specific responsibility for the [Colleges of Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleges_of_Durham_University).[^o3][^o4]

In 1953, MacFarlane-Grieve inherited the [family estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_estate) at [Edenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edenhall), [Scottish Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Borders), and retired early from Durham.[^o16]

## Honours {#Honours}

In May 1917, MacFarlane-Grieve was awarded the [Silver Medal of Military Valor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Medal_of_Military_Valor) by the [King of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Italy) "for distinguished services rendered during the course of the campaign".[^o12] In June 1917, he was awarded the [Military Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Cross) (MC) "for distinguished service in the field". In February 1932, he was awarded the [Efficiency Decoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficiency_Decoration) (TD) for long service.
