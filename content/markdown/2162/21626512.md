# Jack Silver

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jack Silver"}

**Jack Howard Silver** (23 April 1942 – 22 December 2016[^o3]) was an American [set theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_theorist) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Silver made several contributions to set theory in the areas of [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644) and the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335) *L*.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Born in Montana, he earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Mathematics at Berkeley in 1966 under [Robert Vaught](wikipedia://en/3550212) before taking a position at the same institution the following year. He held an [Alfred P. Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) from 1970 to 1972.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In his 1975 paper "On the Singular Cardinals Problem", Silver [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that if a [cardinal](wikipedia://en/6173) *κ* is [singular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cardinal) with [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) [cofinality](wikipedia://en/6693) and 2<sup>*λ*</sup> = *λ*<sup>\+</sup> for all infinite cardinals *λ* \< *κ*, then 2<sup>*κ*</sup> = *κ*<sup>\+</sup>. Prior to Silver's proof, many mathematicians believed that a [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) argument would yield that the negation of the theorem is [consistent](wikipedia://en/75802) with [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC). He introduced the notion of a *master condition*, which became an important tool in forcing proofs involving large cardinals.[^o7]

Silver proved the consistency of [Chang's conjecture](wikipedia://en/25403351) using the Silver collapse (which is a variation of the [Levy collapse](wikipedia://en/15550368#Levy_collapsing)). He proved that, assuming the consistency of a [supercompact cardinal](wikipedia://en/248116), it is possible to construct a model where 2<sup>*κ*</sup> = *κ*<sup>\++</sup> holds for some [measurable cardinal](wikipedia://en/248102) *κ*. With the introduction of the so-called [Silver machines](wikipedia://en/5363856) he was able to give a fine structure free proof of [Jensen's](wikipedia://en/3785996) [covering lemma](wikipedia://en/326356). He is also credited with discovering [Silver indiscernibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_indiscernible) and generalizing the notion of a [Kurepa tree](wikipedia://en/8267820) (called Silver's Principle). He discovered [0#](wikipedia://en/248085) ("zero sharp") in his 1966 Ph.D. thesis, discussed in the graduate textbook *Set Theory: An Introduction to Large Cardinals* by Frank R. Drake.[^o8]

Silver's original work involving large cardinals was perhaps motivated by the goal of showing the inconsistency of an uncountable measurable cardinal; instead he was led to discover indiscernibles in *L* assuming a measurable cardinal exists.
