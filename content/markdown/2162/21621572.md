# Comonotonicity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Comonotonicity"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **comonotonicity** mainly refers to the perfect positive dependence between the components of a [random vector](wikipedia://en/49821), essentially saying that they can be represented as increasing functions of a single random variable. In two dimensions it is also possible to consider perfect negative dependence, which is called countermonotonicity.

:::

Comonotonicity is also related to the comonotonic additivity of the [Choquet integral](wikipedia://en/10252066).[^o2]

The concept of comonotonicity has applications in [financial risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_risk_management) and [actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373), see e.g. (Dhaene Denuit Goovaerts Vyncke 2002a) and (Dhaene Denuit Goovaerts Vyncke 2002b). In particular, the sum of the components $X_{1} + X_{2} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + X_{n}$ is the riskiest if the [joint probability distribution](wikipedia://en/879637) of the random vector $(X_{1}, X_{2}, . . . , X_{n})$ is comonotonic.[^o5] Furthermore, the $\alpha$-[quantile](wikipedia://en/25184) of the sum equals the sum of the $\alpha$-quantiles of its components, hence comonotonic random variables are quantile-additive.[^o6][^o7] In practical risk management terms it means that there is minimal (or eventually no) [variance reduction](wikipedia://en/5187715) from diversification.

For extensions of comonotonicity, see (Jouini Napp 2004) and (Puccetti Scarsini 2010).

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Comonotonicity of subsets of $R^{n}$ {#Comonotonicity_of_subsets_of}

A subset $S$ of $\mathbf{R}^{n}$ is called *comonotonic*[^o13] (sometimes also *nondecreasing*[^o14]) if, for all $(x_{1}, x_{2}, . . . , x_{n})$ and $(y_{1}, y_{2}, . . . , y_{n})$ in $S$ with $x_{i} < y_{i}$ for some $i \in \{1, 2, . . . , n$}, it follows that $x_{j} \le y_{j}$ for all $j \in \{1, 2, . . . , n$}.

This means that $S$ is a [totally ordered set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_set).

### Comonotonicity of probability measures on $R^{n}$ {#Comonotonicity_of_probability_measures_on}

Let $\mu$ be a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on the $n$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbf{R}^{n}$ and let $F$ denote its multivariate [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793), that is

$$F(x_1,\ldots,x_n):=\mu\bigl(\{(y_1,\ldots,y_n)\in{\mathbb R}^n\mid y_1\le x_1,\ldots,y_n\le x_n\}\bigr),\qquad (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in{\mathbb R}^n.$$

Furthermore, let $F_{1}, . . . , F_{n}$ denote the cumulative distribution functions of the $n$ one-dimensional [marginal distributions](wikipedia://en/506077) of $\mu$, that means

$$F_i(x):=\mu\bigl(\{(y_1,\ldots,y_n)\in{\mathbb R}^n\mid y_i\le x\}\bigr),\qquad x\in{\mathbb R}$$

for every $i \in \{1, 2, . . . , n$}. Then $\mu$ is called *comonotonic*, if

$$F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}F_i(x_i),\qquad (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in{\mathbb R}^n.$$

Note that the probability measure $\mu$ is comonotonic [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its [support](wikipedia://en/6591796) $S$ is comonotonic according to the above definition.[^o16]

### Comonotonicity of $R^{n}$-valued random vectors {#Comonotonicity_of_-valued_random_vectors}

An $\mathbf{R}^{n}$-valued random vector $X = (X_{1}, . . . , X_{n})$ is called *comonotonic*, if its multivariate [distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) (the [pushforward measure](wikipedia://en/9412979)) is comonotonic, this means

$$\Pr(X_1\le x_1,\ldots,X_n\le x_n)=\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}} \Pr(X_i\le x_i),\qquad (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in{\mathbb R}^n.$$

:::

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

An $\mathbf{R}^{n}$-valued random vector $X = (X_{1}, . . . , X_{n})$ is comonotonic if and only if it can be represented as

$$(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=_\text{d}(F_{X_1}^{-1}(U),\ldots,F_{X_n}^{-1}(U)), \,$$

where $=_{d}$ stands for equality in distribution, on the right-hand side are the [left-continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-continuous) generalized inverses[^o20] of the cumulative distribution functions $F_{X_{1}}, . . . , F_{X_{n}}$, and $U$ is a [uniformly distributed random variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29) on the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324). More generally, a random vector is comonotonic if and only if it agrees in distribution with a random vector where all components are [non-decreasing functions](wikipedia://en/48260) (or all are non-increasing functions) of the same random variable.[^o21]

:::

## Upper bounds {#Upper_bounds}

### Upper Fréchet–Hoeffding bound for cumulative distribution functions {#Upper_Fréchet–Hoeffding_bound_for_cumulative_distribution_functions}

Let $X = (X_{1}, . . . , X_{n})$ be an $\mathbf{R}^{n}$-valued random vector. Then, for every $i \in \{1, 2, . . . , n$},

$$\Pr(X_1\le x_1,\ldots,X_n\le x_n) \le \Pr(X_i\le x_i),\qquad (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in{\mathbb R}^n,$$

hence

$$\Pr(X_1\le x_1,\ldots,X_n\le x_n)\le\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}} \Pr(X_i\le x_i),\qquad (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in{\mathbb R}^n,$$

with equality everywhere if and only if $(X_{1}, . . . , X_{n})$ is comonotonic.

### Upper bound for the covariance {#Upper_bound_for_the_covariance}

Let $(X, Y)$ be a bivariate random vector such that the [expected values](wikipedia://en/9653) of $X$, $Y$ and the product $XY$ exist. Let $(X^{*}, Y^{*})$ be a comonotonic bivariate random vector with the same one-dimensional marginal distributions as $(X, Y)$.[^o25] Then it follows from [Höffding's formula for the covariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Höffding's_formula_for_the_covariance)[^o26] and the upper Fréchet–Hoeffding bound that

$$\text{Cov}(X,Y)\le\text{Cov}(X^*,Y^*)$$

and, correspondingly,

$$\operatorname E[XY]\le \operatorname E[X^*Y^*]$$

with equality if and only if $(X, Y)$ is comonotonic.[^o27]

Note that this result generalizes the [rearrangement inequality](wikipedia://en/937598) and [Chebyshev's sum inequality](wikipedia://en/937664).
