# Glossary of algebraic topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glossary of algebraic topology"}

This is a glossary of properties and concepts in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) in mathematics.

:::

See also: [glossary of topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_topology), [list of algebraic topology topics](wikipedia://en/347694), [glossary of category theory](wikipedia://en/2390225), [glossary of differential geometry and topology](wikipedia://en/507354), [timeline of manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_manifolds).

- **Convention**: Throughout the article, *I* denotes the unit interval, *S*<sup>*n*</sup> the [*n*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) and *D*<sup>*n*</sup> the *n*-disk. Also, throughout the article, spaces are assumed to be **reasonable**; this can be taken to mean for example, a space is a CW complex or [compactly generated](wikipedia://en/899382) [weakly Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_Hausdorff_space). Similarly, no attempt is made to be definitive about the definition of a [spectrum](wikipedia://en/1033697). A [simplicial set](wikipedia://en/1027784) is not thought of as a space; i.e., we generally distinguish between simplicial sets and their geometric realizations.
- **Inclusion criterion**: As there is no [glossary of homological algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_homological_algebra) in Wikipedia right now, this glossary also includes a few concepts in homological algebra (e.g., chain homotopy); some concepts in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732) and [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) are also fair game. On the other hand, the items that appear in [glossary of topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_topology) are generally omitted. [Abstract homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_homotopy_theory) and [motivic homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivic_homotopy_theory) are also outside the scope. [Glossary of category theory](wikipedia://en/2390225) covers (or will cover) concepts in theory of [model categories](wikipedia://en/2895304). See the [glossary of symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/48563756) for the topics in symplectic topology such as quantization.

::: {#o34 .entry cue=term name="cellular"}

**cellular**

A map ƒ:*X*→*Y* between CW complexes is [cellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_map) if $f(X^n) \subset Y^n$ for all *n*.

The [cellular approximation theorem](wikipedia://en/21537120) says that every map between CW complexes is homotopic to a [cellular map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_map) between them.

The [cellular homology](wikipedia://en/2113969) is the (canonical) homology of a CW complex. Note it applies to CW complexes and not to spaces in general. A cellular homology is highly computable; it is especially useful for spaces with natural cell decompositions like projective spaces or Grassmannian.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=term name="chain homotopy"}

**chain homotopy**

Given chain maps $f, g: (C, d_C) \to (D, d_D)$ between chain complexes of modules, a [chain homotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_homotopy) *s* from *f* to *g* is a sequence of module homomorphisms $s_i: C_i \to D_{i+1}$ satisfying $f_i - g_i = d_D \circ s_i + s_{i-1} \circ d_C$. It is also called a [homotopy operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_operator).

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=term name="chain map"}

**chain map**

A chain map $f: (C, d_C) \to (D, d_D)$ between chain complexes of modules is a sequence of module homomorphisms $f_i: C_i \to D_i$ that commutes with the differentials; i.e., $d_D \circ f_i = f_{i-1} \circ d_C$.

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=term name="chain homotopy equivalence"}

**chain homotopy equivalence**

A chain map that is an isomorphism up to chain homotopy; that is, if *ƒ*:*C*→*D* is a chain map, then it is a chain homotopy equivalence if there is a chain map *g*:*D*→*C* such that *g*ƒ and ƒ*g* are chain homotopic to the identity homomorphisms on *C* and *D*, respectively.

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=term name="change of fiber"}

**change of fiber**

The [change of fiber](wikipedia://en/48849543) of a fibration *p* is a homotopy equivalence, up to homotopy, between the fibers of *p* induced by a path in the base.

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=term name="character variety"}

**character variety**

The [character variety](wikipedia://en/14688049)[^o40] of a group π and an algebraic group *G* (e.g., a reductive complex Lie group) is the [geometric invariant theory quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_invariant_theory_quotient) by *G*:

$$\mathcal{X}(\pi, G) = \operatorname{Hom}(\pi, G)/ \! /G.$$

:::

::: {#o41 .entry cue=term name="characteristic class"}

**characteristic class**

Let Vect(*X*) be the set of isomorphism classes of vector bundles on *X*. We can view $X \mapsto \operatorname{Vect}(X)$ as a contravariant functor from **Top** to **Set** by sending a map ƒ:*X* → *Y* to the pullback ƒ<sup>\*</sup> along it. Then a [characteristic class](wikipedia://en/363530) is a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022) from Vect to the cohomology functor H<sup>\*</sup>. Explicitly, to each vector bundle *E* we assign a cohomology class, say, *c*(*E*). The assignment is natural in the sense that ƒ<sup>\*</sup>c(*E*) = c(ƒ<sup>\*</sup>*E*).

::: {#o66 .entry cue=term name="constant"}

**constant**

::: {#o69 .entry cue=term name="covering"}

**covering**

A map *p*: *Y* → *X* is a [covering](wikipedia://en/250323) or a covering map if each point of *x* has a neighborhood *N* that is [evenly covered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenly_covered) by *p*; this means that the pre-image of *N* is a disjoint union of open sets, each of which maps to *N* homeomorphically.

It is *n*-sheeted if each fiber *p*<sup>−1</sup>(*x*) has exactly *n* elements.

It is [universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_covering) if *Y* is simply connected.

A morphism of a covering is a map over *X*. In particular, an automorphism of a covering *p*:*Y*→*X* (also called a [deck transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deck_transformation)) is a map *Y*→*Y* over *X* that has inverse; i.e., a homeomorphism over *X*.

A [*G*-covering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-covering) is a covering arising from a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a space *X* by a group *G*, the covering map being the quotient map from *X* to the [orbit space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_space) *X/G*. The notion is used to state the universal property: if *X* admits a universal covering (in particular connected), then

$\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(X, x_0), G)$ is the set of isomorphism classes of *G*-coverings.

In particular, if *G* is abelian, then the left-hand side is $\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(X, x_0), G) = \operatorname{H}^1(X; G)$ (cf. [nonabelian cohomology](wikipedia://en/40814966).)

[covering dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_dimension).

**CW complex**

A [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) is a space *X* equipped with a CW structure; i.e., a filtration

$$X^0 \subset X^1 \subset X^2 \subset \cdots \subset X$$

such that (1) *X*<sup>0</sup> is discrete and (2) *X*<sup>*n*</sup> is obtained from *X*<sup>*n*-1</sup> by attaching *n*-cells.

::: {#o75 .entry cue=term name="deformation retract"}

**deformation retract**

A subspace $A \subset X$ is called a [deformation retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_retract) of *X* if there is a homotopy $h_t : X \to X$ such that $h_0$ is the identity, $h_1(X) \subset A$ and ${h_1}|_A$ is the identity (i.e., $h_1$ is a [retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retract_%28category_theory%29) of $A \hookrightarrow X$ in the sense in category theory). It is called a [strong deformation retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_deformation_retract) if, in addition, $h_t$ satisfies the requirement that ${h_t}|_A$ is the identity. For example, a homotopy $h_t : B \to B, \, x \mapsto (1-t)x$ exhibits that the origin is a strong deformation retract of an open ball *B* centered at the origin.

::: {#o100 .entry cue=term name="fiber-homotopy equivalence"}

**fiber-homotopy equivalence**

Given *D*→*B*, *E*→*B*, a map ƒ:*D*→*E* over *B* is a [fiber-homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/48812181) if it is invertible up to homotopy over *B*. The basic fact is that if *D*→*B*, *E*→*B* are fibrations, then a homotopy equivalence from *D* to *E* is a fiber-homotopy equivalence.

:::

::: {#o101 .entry cue=term name="fiber sequence"}

**fiber sequence**

The [fiber sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_sequence) of a map $f: X \to Y$ is the sequence $F_f \overset{p} \to X \overset{f}\to Y$ where $F_f \overset{p} \to X$ is the homotopy fiber of *f*; i.e., the pullback of the path space fibration $PY \to Y$ along *f*.

:::

::: {#o102 .entry cue=term name="fiber square"}

**fiber square**

[fiber square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_square)

::: {#o103 .entry cue=term name="fibration"}

**fibration**

A map *p*:*E* → *B* is a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) if for any given homotopy $g_t: X \to B$ and a map $h_0: X \to E$ such that $p \circ h_0 = g_0$, there exists a homotopy $h_t: X \to E$ such that $p \circ h_t = g_t$. (The above property is called the [homotopy lifting property](wikipedia://en/522786).) A covering map is a basic example of a fibration.

:::

::: {#o104 .entry cue=term name="fibration sequence"}

**fibration sequence**

One says $F \to X \overset{p}\to B$ is a fibration sequence to mean that *p* is a fibration and that *F* is homotopy equivalent to the homotopy fiber of *p*, with some understanding of base points.

**homotopy equivalence**

::: {#o142 .entry cue=term name="homotopy group"}

**homotopy group**

For a based space *X*, let $\pi_n X = [S^n, X]$, the set of homotopy classes of based maps. Then $\pi_0 X$ is the set of path-connected components of *X*, $\pi_1 X$ is the fundamental group of *X* and $\pi_n X, \, n \ge 2$ are the (higher) *n*-th [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) of *X*.

For based spaces $A \subset X$, the [relative homotopy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_homotopy_group) $\pi_n(X, A)$ is defined as $\pi_{n-1}$ of the space of paths that all start at the base point of *X* and end somewhere in *A*. Equivalently, it is the $\pi_{n-1}$ of the homotopy fiber of $A \hookrightarrow X$.

If *E* is a spectrum, then $\pi_k E = \varinjlim_{n} \pi_{k + n} E_n.$

If *X* is a based space, then the [stable *k*-th homotopy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_group) of *X* is $\pi_k^s X = \varinjlim_{n} \pi_{k + n} \Sigma^n X$. In other words, it is the *k*-th homotopy group of the suspension spectrum of *X*.

::: {#o184 .entry cue=term name="mapping"}

**mapping**

*Image: Mapping cone.svg.* The mapping cone of a map ƒ:*X*→*Y* is obtained by gluing the cone over *X* to *Y*.

The [mapping cone](wikipedia://en/1707756) (or cofiber) of a map ƒ:*X*→*Y* is $C_f = Y \cup_f CX$.

The [mapping cylinder](wikipedia://en/1663142) of a map ƒ:*X*→*Y* is $M_f = Y \cup_f (X \times I)$. Note: $C_f = M_f/(X \times \{0\})$.

The reduced versions of the above are obtained by using reduced cone and reduced cylinder.

The [mapping path space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapping_path_space) *P*<sub>*p*</sub> of a map *p*:*E*→*B* is the pullback of $B^I \to B$ along *p*. If *p* is fibration, then the natural map *E*→*P*<sub>*p*</sub> is a [fiber-homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/48812181); thus, one can replace *E* by the mapping path space without changing the homotopy type of the fiber. A mapping path space is also called a **mapping cocylinder**.

As a set, the [mapping space](wikipedia://en/78365422) from a space *X* to a space *Y* is the set of all continuous maps from *X* to *Y*. It is topologized in such a way the mapping space is a space; tha is, an object in the category of spaces used in algebraic topology; e.g., the [category of compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/71508609). This topology may or may not be compact-open topology.

**path lifting**

A [path lifting function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_lifting_function) for a map *p*: *E* → *B* is a section of $E^I \to P_p$ where $P_p$ is the [mapping path space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapping_path_space) of *p*. For example, a covering is a fibration with a unique path lifting function. By formal consideration, a map is a fibration if and only if there is a path lifting function for it.

:::

::: {#o214 .entry cue=term name="path space"}

**path space**

The [path space](wikipedia://en/71507666) of a based space *X* is $PX = \operatorname{Map}(I, X)$, the space of based maps, where the base point of *I* is 0. Put in another way, it is the (set-theoretic) fiber of $X^I \to X, \, \chi \mapsto \chi(0)$ over the base point of *X*. The projection $PX \to X, \, \chi \mapsto \chi(1)$ is called the [path space fibration](wikipedia://en/48842115), whose fiber over the base point of *X* is the loop space $\Omega X$. See also [mapping path space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapping_path_space).

::: {#o227 .entry cue=term name="pullback"}

**pullback**

Given a map *p*:*E*→*B*, the [pullback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullback_%28topology%29) of *p* along *ƒ*:*X*→*B* is the space $f^*E = \{ (e, x) \in E \times X | p(e) = f(x) \}$ (succinctly it is the [equalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equalizer_%28mathematics%29) of *p* and *f*). It is a space over *X* through a projection.

:::

::: {#o228 .entry cue=term name="Puppe sequence"}

**Puppe sequence**

The [Puppe sequence](wikipedia://en/4734210) refers ro either of the sequences

$$X \overset{f}\to Y \to C_f \to \Sigma X \to \Sigma Y \to \cdots,$$

$$\cdots \to \Omega X \to \Omega Y \to F_f \to X \overset{f}\to Y$$

where $C_f, F_f$ are homotopy cofiber and homotopy fiber of *f*.

:::

::: {#o229 .entry cue=term name="pushout"}

**pushout**

Given $A \subset B$ and a map $f: A \to X$, the [pushout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushout_%28topology%29) of *X* and *B* along *f* is

$$X \cup_f B = X \sqcup B/(a \sim f(a));$$

that is *X* and *B* are glued together along *A* through *f*. The map *f* is usually called the attaching map.

An important example is when *B* = *D*<sup>*n*</sup>, *A* = *S*<sup>*n*-1</sup>; in that case, forming such a pushout is called attaching an *n*-cell (meaning an *n*-disk) to *X*.

**rational**

The [rational homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/13998981).

The [rationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalization_%28algebraic_topology%29) of a space *X* is, roughly, the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_space) of *X* at zero. More precisely, *X*<sub>0</sub> together with *j*: *X* → *X*<sub>0</sub> is a rationalization of *X* if the map $\pi_* X \otimes \mathbb{Q} \to \pi_* X_0 \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ induced by *j* is an isomorphism of vector spaces and $\pi_* X_0 \otimes \mathbb{Q} \simeq \pi_* X_0$.

The [rational homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_homotopy_type) of *X* is the weak homotopy type of *X*<sub>0</sub>.

**simple-homotopy equivalence**

A map ƒ:*X*→*Y* between finite simplicial complexes (e.g., manifolds) is a [simple-homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/20897472) if it is homotopic to a composition of finitely many [elementary expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_expansion) and [elementary collapses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_collapse). ::: {#o253 .entry cue=term name="singular"}

**singular**

Given a space *X* and an abelian group π, the [singular homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_homology_group) of *X* with coefficients in π is

$\operatorname{H}_*(X; \pi) = \operatorname{H}_*(C_*(X) \otimes \pi)$ where $C_*(X)$ is the [singular chain complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_chain_complex) of *X*; i.e., the *n*-th degree piece is the free abelian group generated by all the maps $\triangle^n \to X$ from the standard *n*-simplex to *X*. A singular homology is a special case of a [simplicial homology](wikipedia://en/1060236); indeed, for each space *X*, there is the [singular simplicial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_simplicial_complex) of *X* [^o254] whose homology is the singular homology of *X*.

The [singular simplices functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_simplices_functor) is the functor $\mathbf{Top} \to s\mathbf{Set}$ from the category of all spaces to the category of simplicial sets, that is the right adjoint to the [geometric realization functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_realization_functor).

The **singular simplicial complex** of a space *X* is the [normalized chain complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalized_chain_complex) of the singular simplex of *X*.

$$\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}^{*+n}(\text{Th}(E); \mathbb{Z})\simeq \operatorname{H}^*(X; \mathbb{Z}).$$

[Thom's first](wikipedia://en/71215326) and [second](wikipedia://en/71410654) isotopy lemmas.[^o277]
