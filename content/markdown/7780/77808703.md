# Catalan Mathematical Society

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Catalan Mathematical Society"}

The **Catalan Mathematical Society** (Catalan: Societat Catalana de Matemàtiques, **SCM**) is a [mathematical society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_society) in [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona), founded in 1931. The SCM is a member of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470), and is recognised by the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) as one of the national mathematical societies of Spain. It is an affiliated society of the [Institute for Catalan Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Catalan_Studies), and they are headquartered together in Barcelona.

:::

The Catalan Mathematical Society was founded in 1931 as the mathematics section of the Catalan Society of Physical, Chemical, and Mathematical Sciences. In 1986 the Society of Sciences split into separate Catalan Physical Society, Catalan Chemical Society, Catalan Society of Technology, and Mathematical Societies.

## Activities and publications {#Activities_and_publications}

Every three years the Catalan Mathematical Society hosts the Barcelona Mathematical Days, a research congress covering all areas of mathematics. The SCM also organises the Spanish Mathematical Olympiad and Girls Mathematical Olympiad, in partnership with the [Royal Spanish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/65501555).

The Catalan Mathematical Society manages several publications:

- The newsletter of the Catalan Mathematical Society is the *SCM-Notícies*, which is published in [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language).
- The Catalan Mathematical Society publishes its bulletin *Butlletí* twice a year. It is the preeminent mathematical journal published in Catalan.
- The journal *Noubiaix* is jointly published by the SCM and the Catalan Federation of Associations of Mathematics Teachers. It is published in Catalan, and covers mathematics education at all levels.
- *Reports@scm* is an electronic English-language journal for short research papers by early-career mathematicians.

## Prizes {#Prizes}

The Barcelona Dynamical Systems Prize of the SCM is awarded to the authors of a research paper in English on the topic of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). The award is managed by Catalan mathematician Carles Simó, and has been given biennially since 2015. Past winners of the prize are:

- 2015 – Alberto Enciso and Daniel Peralta-Salas
- 2017 – Jordi-Lluís Figueras, Àlex Haro, and Alejandro Luque
- 2019 – [Vadim Kaloshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Kaloshin) and [Alfonso Sorrentino](wikipedia://en/75412567)
- 2023 – Massimiliano Berti, Alberto Maspero, and Paolo Ventura

The Maria Antònia Canals Prize is awarded jointly with three other Spanish mathematical societies for an "innovative classroom experience", and aims to promote innovation in mathematics teaching. It is named for Catalan mathematician [Maria Antònia Canals](wikipedia://en/61410076).

The Évariste Galois Prize is awarded annually since 1962 to a student for research work included in their master's thesis or at an early stage of their doctoral studies. The prize is named for French mathematician [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815).

The Emmy Noether Prize has been annually awarded since 2016 to the best [bachelor's thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_thesis) defended that year by a student of mathematics at either the [Autonomous University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Barcelona), the [University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Barcelona), or the [Polytechnic University of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Catalonia). It is named for German mathematician [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896).

The Albert Dou prize is awarded biennially to the author of a work "showing the importance of mathematics in our world." It was first awarded in 2010, and is named for Catalan mathematician [Albert Dou i Mas de Xexàs](wikipedia://en/66891342), who died in 2009.
