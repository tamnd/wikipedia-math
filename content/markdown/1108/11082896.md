# Medical statistics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Medical statistics"}

**Medical statistics** (also **health statistics**) deals with applications of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) to [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine) and the [health sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_science), including [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997), [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health), [forensic medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_medicine), and [clinical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_research).[^o2] Medical statistics has been a recognized branch of statistics in the United Kingdom for more than 40 years, but the term has not come into general use in North America, where the wider term '[biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878)' is more commonly used.[^o3] However, "biostatistics" more commonly connotes all applications of statistics to [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology).[^o3] Medical statistics is a subdiscipline of statistics.

> It is the science of summarizing, collecting, presenting and interpreting data in medical practice, and using them to estimate the magnitude of associations and test hypotheses. It has a central role in medical investigations. It not only provides a way of organizing information on a wider and more formal basis than relying on the exchange of anecdotes and personal experience, but also takes into account the intrinsic variation inherent in most biological processes.[^o4]

:::

## Use in medical hypothesis testing {#Use_in_medical_hypothesis_testing}

In medical hypothesis testing, the medical research is often evaluated by means of the [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911), the [P value](wikipedia://en/554994), or both.[^o7]

### Confidence interval {#Confidence_interval}

*Image: Confidence intervals visual.jpg.* This [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) presents two different [confidence intervals](wikipedia://en/280911).[^o2]

Frequently reported in medical research studies is the confidence interval (CI), which indicates the consistency and variability of the medical results of repeated medical trials. In other words, the confidence interval shows the range of values where the expected true estimate would exist within this specific range, if the study was performed many times.[^o2]

Most biomedical research is not able to use a total population for a study. Instead, samples of the total population are what are often used for a study. From the sample, inferences can be made of the total population by means of a sample statistic and the estimation of error, presented as a range of values.[^o2][^o7]

### P value {#P_value}

Frequently used in medical studies is the statistical significance of P \< 0.05.[^o7]

The P value is the probability of no effect or no difference ([null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis)) of obtaining a result essentially equal to what was actually observed. The P stands for probability and measures how likely it is that any observed difference between groups is due to chance. The P value function between 0 and 1. The closer to 0, the less likely the results are due to chance. The closer to 1, the higher the probability that the results are actually due to chance.[^o7]

:::

## Pharmaceutical statistics {#Pharmaceutical_statistics}

**Pharmaceutical statistics** is the application of statistics to matters concerning the [pharmaceutical industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_industry). This can be from issues of [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), to analysis of drug trials, to issues of commercialization of a medicine.[^o2]

There are many professional bodies concerned with this field including:

- European Federation of Statisticians in the Pharmaceutical Industry
- [Statisticians In The Pharmaceutical Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statisticians_In_The_Pharmaceutical_Industry)

## Clinical biostatistics {#Clinical_biostatistics}

**Clinical biostatistics** is concerned with research into the principles and methodology used in the design and analysis of [clinical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_research) and to apply [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579) to [clinical medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_medicine).[^o2][^o13]

Clinical biostatistics is taught in [postgraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate) biostatistical and applied statistical degrees, for example as part of the [BCA Master of Biostatistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCA_Master_of_Biostatistics) program in Australia.

:::

## Basic concepts {#Basic_concepts}

**For describing situations**

- [Incidence (epidemiology)](wikipedia://en/167008) vs. [Prevalence](wikipedia://en/126688) vs. [Cumulative incidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulative_incidence)
- Many [medical tests](wikipedia://en/12439068) (such as [pregnancy tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_test)) have two possible results: positive or negative. However, tests will sometimes yield incorrect results in the form of [false positives or false negatives](wikipedia://en/43286898). False positives and false negatives can be described by the statistical concepts of [type I and type II errors](wikipedia://en/5657877), respectively, where the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) is that the patient will test negative. The [precision](wikipedia://en/41932) of a medical test is usually calculated in the form of [positive predictive values (PPVs) and negative predicted values (NPVs)](wikipedia://en/1556952). PPVs and NPVs of medical tests depend on intrinsic properties of the test as well as the [prevalence](wikipedia://en/126688) of the condition being tested for. For example, if any pregnancy test was administered to a population of individuals who were biologically incapable of becoming pregnant, then the test's PPV will be 0% and its NPV will be 100% simply because true positives and false negatives cannot exist in this population.
- [Mortality rate](wikipedia://en/191305) vs. [standardized mortality ratio](wikipedia://en/6952389) vs. [age-standardized mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age-standardized_mortality_rate)
- [Pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic) vs. [epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic) vs. [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_%28epidemiology%29) vs. [syndemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndemic)
- [Serial interval](wikipedia://en/7318441) vs. [incubation period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubation_period)
- [Cancer cluster](wikipedia://en/1001555)
- [Sexual network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_network)
- [Years of potential life lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_potential_life_lost)

- [Perinatal mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perinatal_mortality_rate)
- [Low birth weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_weight)

## Related statistical theory {#Related_statistical_theory}

- [Survival analysis](wikipedia://en/419259)
- [Proportional hazards models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_hazards_models)
- Active control trials: clinical trials in which a kind of new treatment is compared with some other active agent rather than a placebo.
- ADLS(Activities of daily living scale): a scale designed to measure physical ability/disability that is used in investigations of a variety of chronic disabling conditions, such as arthritis. This scale is based on scoring responses to questions about self-care, grooming, etc.[^o16]
- Actuarial statistics: the statistics used by actuaries to calculate liabilities, evaluate risks and plan the financial course of insurance, pensions, etc.[^o17]
