# Branching theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Branching theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **branching theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) about [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181). Intuitively, it states that every non-constant [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) is [locally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally) a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $X$ and $Y$ be Riemann surfaces, and let $f : X \to Y$ be a non-constant holomorphic map. Fix a point $a \in X$ and set $b := f(a) \in Y$. Then there exist $k \in \mathbb{N}$ and [charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chart_%28topology%29) $\psi_{1} : U_{1} \to V_{1}$ on $X$ and $\psi_{2} : U_{2} \to V_{2}$ on $Y$ such that

- $\psi_{1} (a) = \psi_{2} (b) = 0$; and
- $\psi_{2} \circ f \circ \psi_{1}^{-1} : V_{1} \to V_{2}$ is $z \mapsto z^{k}.$

This theorem gives rise to several definitions:

- We call $k$ the *[multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790)* of $f$ at $a$. Some authors denote this $\nu (f, a)$.
- If $k > 1$, the point $a$ is called a *[branch point](wikipedia://en/483173)* of $f$.
- If $f$ has no branch points, it is called *unbranched*. See also [unramified morphism](wikipedia://en/58653736).

:::
