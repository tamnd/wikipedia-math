# Radonifying operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radonifying operator"}

In [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), a **radonifying operator** (ultimately named after [Johann Radon](wikipedia://en/339331)) between [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333) is one that takes a [cylinder set measure](wikipedia://en/6571387) (CSM) on the first space to a true measure on the second space. It acquired its name because the [pushforward measure](wikipedia://en/9412979) on the second space was historically thought of as a [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given two [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) $E$ and $G$, a CSM $\{ \mu_{T} | T \in \mathcal{A} (E) \}$ on $E$ and a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) $\theta \in \mathrm{Lin} (E; G)$, we say that $\theta$ is *radonifying* if the push forward CSM (see below) $\left\{ \left. \left( \theta_{*} (\mu_{\cdot}) \right)_{S} \right| S \in \mathcal{A} (G) \right\}$ on $G$ "is" a measure, i.e. there is a measure $\nu$ on $G$ such that

$$\left( \theta_{*} (\mu_{\cdot}) \right)_{S} = S_{*} (\nu)$$

for each $S \in \mathcal{A} (G)$, where $S_{*} (\nu)$ is the usual push forward of the measure $\nu$ by the linear map $S : G \to F_{S}$.[^o4]

:::

## Push forward of a CSM {#Push_forward_of_a_CSM}

Because the definition of a CSM on $G$ requires that the maps in $\mathcal{A} (G)$ be [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective), the definition of the push forward for a CSM requires careful attention. The CSM

$$\left\{ \left. \left( \theta_{*} (\mu_{\cdot}) \right)_{S} \right| S \in \mathcal{A} (G) \right\}$$

is defined by

$$\left( \theta_{*} (\mu_{\cdot}) \right)_{S} = \mu_{S \circ \theta}$$

if the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) $S \circ \theta : E \to F_{S}$ is surjective. If $S \circ \theta$ is not surjective, let $\tilde{F}$ be the image of $S \circ \theta$, let $i : \tilde{F} \to F_{S}$ be the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516), and define

$$\left( \theta_{*} (\mu_{\cdot}) \right)_{S} = i_{*} \left( \mu_{\Sigma} \right),$$

where $\Sigma : E \to \tilde{F}$ (so $\Sigma \in \mathcal{A} (E)$) is such that $i \circ \Sigma = S \circ \theta$.
