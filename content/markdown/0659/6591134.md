# Isaac of Stella

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isaac of Stella"}

**Isaac of Stella**, [O.Cart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthusians) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): **Isaac de l'Étoile**; c. 1100 – c. 1170s) was a French [Cistercian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistercians) and later [Carthusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthusians) monk, theologian and philosopher.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in England, after studies in Paris, he entered the [Order of Cistercians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Cistercians), probably at Pontigny, during the reforms of Saint [Bernard of Clairvaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux). In 1147 he became [abbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbot) of the small [monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery) of Stella, outside Poitiers.[^o3]

At some time in his later life, most probably in 1167, he left Stella to set up a monastery on the [Île de Ré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Île_de_Ré) on the Atlantic coast.[^o4] He later returned to Stella, where it is known that he lived into the 1170s since in one of his sermons he refers to meeting 'Saint' Bernard - and Bernard was only canonised in 1174.[^o5]

Isaac's most popular work was an allegorical commentary on the canon of the Mass in the form of a letter to [John of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Canterbury), bishop of Poitiers. His 55 surviving sermons (and three sermon fragments),[^o6] as well as his *Letter to Alcher on the Soul*, constitute his real theological contribution. The *Letter* (1962) was addressed to [Alcher of Clairvaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcher_of_Clairvaux), and combined [Aristotelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelianism) and Neoplatonic theories about psychology with Christian mysticism. It exercised a significant role in later mystical speculation due to the incorporation of large sections of Isaac's work in the anthropological compendium known as *De spiritu et anima* (*The Spirit and the Soul*), which circulated under the name of Augustine and was widely used in the 13th century.[^o7]

Isaac's works make use of logical argumentation, influenced by [Augustine of Hippo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo)'s [Neoplatonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplatonism).
