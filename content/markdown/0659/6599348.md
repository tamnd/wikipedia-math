# Clark–Ocone theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clark–Ocone theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Clark–Ocone theorem** (also known as the **Clark–Ocone–Haussmann theorem** or **formula**) is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of [stochastic analysis](wikipedia://en/47895). It expresses the value of some [function](wikipedia://en/185427) *F* defined on the [classical Wiener space](wikipedia://en/6810363) of continuous paths starting at the origin as the sum of its [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) value and an [Itô integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral) with respect to that path. It is named after the contributions of [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) J.M.C. Clark (1970), [Daniel Ocone](wikipedia://en/22323814) (1984) and U.G. Haussmann (1978).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *C*<sub>0</sub>(\[0, *T*\]; **R**) (or simply *C*<sub>0</sub> for short) be classical Wiener space with Wiener measure *γ*. Let *F* : *C*<sub>0</sub> → **R** be a BC<sup>1</sup> function, i.e. *F* is [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) and [Fréchet differentiable](wikipedia://en/2735727) with bounded derivative D*F* : *C*<sub>0</sub> → Lin(*C*<sub>0</sub>; **R**). Then

$$F(\sigma) = \int_{C_{0}} F(p) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma(p) + \int_{0}^{T} \mathbf{E} \left[ \left. \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \nabla_{H} F (-) \right| \Sigma_{t} \right] (\sigma) \, \mathrm{d} \sigma_{t}.$$

In the above

- *F*(*σ*) is the value of the function *F* on some specific path of interest, *σ*;
- the first integral,

$$\int_{C_{0}} F(p) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma(p) = \mathbf{E}[F]$$

is the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of *F* over the whole of Wiener space *C*<sub>0</sub>;

- the second integral,

$$\int_0^T \cdots \, \mathrm{d} \sigma (t)$$

is an [Itô integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral);

- Σ<sub>∗</sub> is the natural [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014) of [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) *B* : \[0, *T*\] × Ω → **R**: Σ<sub>*t*</sub> is the smallest [*σ*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_algebra) containing all *B*<sub>*s*</sub><sup>−1</sup>(*A*) for times 0 ≤ *s* ≤ *t* and Borel sets *A* ⊆ **R**;
- **E**\[·|Σ<sub>*t*</sub>\] denotes [conditional expectation](wikipedia://en/435099) with respect to the sigma algebra Σ<sub>*t*</sub>;
- <sup>∂</sup>/<sub>∂*t*</sub> denotes [differentiation](wikipedia://en/7921) with respect to time *t*; ∇<sub>*H*</sub> denotes the [*H*-gradient](wikipedia://en/6599701); hence, <sup>∂</sup>/<sub>∂*t*</sub>∇<sub>*H*</sub> is the [Malliavin derivative](wikipedia://en/6814674).

More generally, the conclusion holds for any *F* in *L*<sup>2</sup>(*C*<sub>0</sub>; **R**) that is differentiable in the sense of Malliavin.

:::

## Integration by parts on Wiener space {#Integration_by_parts_on_Wiener_space}

The Clark–Ocone theorem gives rise to an [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) formula on classical Wiener space, and to write [Itô integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral) as [divergences](wikipedia://en/8328):

Let *B* be a standard Brownian motion, and let *L*<sub>0</sub><sup>2,1</sup> be the Cameron–Martin space for *C*<sub>0</sub> (see [abstract Wiener space](wikipedia://en/6592746). Let *V* : *C*<sub>0</sub> → *L*<sub>0</sub><sup>2,1</sup> be a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) such that

$$\dot{V} = \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} : [0, T] \times C_{0} \to \mathbb{R}$$

is in *L*<sup>2</sup>(*B*) (i.e. is [Itô integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral), and hence is an [adapted process](wikipedia://en/6583420)). Let *F* : *C*<sub>0</sub> → **R** be BC<sup>1</sup> as above. Then

$$\int_{C_{0}} \mathrm{D} F (\sigma) (V(\sigma)) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (\sigma) = \int_{C_{0}} F (\sigma) \left( \int_{0}^{T} \dot{V}_{t} (\sigma) \, \mathrm{d} \sigma_{t} \right) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (\sigma),$$

i.e.

$$\int_{C_{0}} \left\langle \nabla_{H} F (\sigma), V (\sigma) \right\rangle_{L_{0}^{2, 1}} \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (\sigma) = - \int_{C_{0}} F (\sigma) \operatorname{div}(V) (\sigma) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (\sigma)$$

or, writing the integrals over *C*<sub>0</sub> as expectations:

$$\mathbb{E} \big[ \langle \nabla_{H} F, V \rangle \big] = - \mathbb{E} \big[ F \operatorname{div} V \big],$$

where the "divergence" div(*V*) : *C*<sub>0</sub> → **R** is defined by

$$\operatorname{div} (V) (\sigma) := - \int_{0}^{T} \dot{V}_{t} (\sigma) \, \mathrm{d} \sigma_{t}.$$

The interpretation of stochastic integrals as divergences leads to concepts such as the [Skorokhod integral](wikipedia://en/18420736) and the tools of the [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875).
