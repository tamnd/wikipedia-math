# Helmut Maier

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Helmut Maier"}

**Helmut Maier** (born 17 October 1953) is a German mathematician and professor at the University of Ulm, Germany. He is known for his contributions in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and particularly for the so-called [Maier's matrix method](wikipedia://en/40198086) as well as [Maier's theorem](wikipedia://en/22084488) for primes in short intervals. He has also done important work in exponential sums and trigonometric sums over special sets of integers and the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Helmut Maier graduated with a Diploma in Mathematics from the [University of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulm) in 1976, under the supervision of Hans-Egon Richert. He received his PhD from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) in 1981, under the supervision of J. Ian Richards.

## Research and academic positions {#Research_and_academic_positions}

Maier's PhD thesis was an extension of his paper *Chains of large gaps between consecutive primes*.[^o7] In this paper Maier applied for the first time what is now known as [Maier's matrix method](wikipedia://en/40198086). This method later on led him and other mathematicians to the discovery of unexpected irregularities in the distribution of prime numbers.[^o4] There have been various other applications of Maier's Matrix Method, such as on irreducible polynomials and on strings of consecutive primes in the same residue class.[^o8][^o9]

After postdoctoral positions at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and the [Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study,_Princeton), Maier obtained a permanent position at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia). While in Georgia he proved that the usual formulation of the Cramér model for the distribution of prime numbers is wrong. This was a completely unexpected result.[^o4] Jointly with [Carl Pomerance](wikipedia://en/2134159) he studied the values of Euler's $\varphi(n)$-function and large gaps between primes. During the same period Maier investigated as well the size of the coefficients of cyclotomic polynomials and later collaborated with [Sergei Konyagin](wikipedia://en/29007447) and [Eduard Wirsing](wikipedia://en/57927311) on this topic. He also collaborated with [Hugh Lowell Montgomery](wikipedia://en/2984701) on the size of the sum of the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) under the assumption of the [Riemann Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_Hypothesis). Maier and [Gérald Tenenbaum](wikipedia://en/36258646) in joint work investigated the sequence of divisors of integers, solving the famous *propinquity* problem of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284).[^o10] Since 1993, Maier has been a professor at the [University of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulm), Germany.

Collaborators of Helmut Maier include [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), C. Feiler, [John Friedlander](wikipedia://en/9704785), [Andrew Granville](wikipedia://en/2910434), D. Haase, A. J. Hildebrand, Michel Laurent Lapidus, J. W. Neuberger, A. Sankaranarayanan, A. Sárközy, [Wolfgang P. Schleich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_P._Schleich), [Cameron Leigh Stewart](wikipedia://en/29028436).
