# Metabelian group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Metabelian group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **metabelian group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) whose [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974). Equivalently, a group *G* is metabelian if and only if there is an abelian [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) *A* such that the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) *G/A* is abelian.

:::

Subgroups of metabelian groups are metabelian, as are images of metabelian groups.

Metabelian groups are [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012). In fact, they are precisely the solvable groups of [derived length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_length) at most 2.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any abelian group is metabelian.
- Any [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) is metabelian, as it has a cyclic normal subgroup of [index](wikipedia://en/230581) 2. More generally, any [generalized dihedral group](wikipedia://en/7149681) is metabelian, as it has an abelian normal subgroup of index 2.
- Any [metacyclic group](wikipedia://en/7515561) is metabelian.
- If *F* is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), the group [Aff(*F*)](wikipedia://en/298828) of [affine maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_map) $x \mapsto ax+b$ (where $a\neq 0$) acting on *F* is metabelian. Here the abelian normal subgroup is the group of pure translations $x\mapsto x+b$, and the abelian quotient group is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the group of [homotheties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotheties) $x\mapsto ax$. If *F* is a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with $q$ elements, this metabelian group is of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) $q(q-1)$.
- The group of [direct isometries](wikipedia://en/652164#Direct_and_indirect_isometries) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) is metabelian. This is similar to the above example, as the elements are again affine maps. The translations of the plane form an abelian normal subgroup of the group, and the corresponding quotient is the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177).
- The finite [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518#Heisenberg_group_modulo_an_odd_prime_p) *H*<sub>3,*p*</sub> of order *p*<sup>3</sup> is metabelian. The same is true for any Heisenberg group defined over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) (group of [upper-triangular](wikipedia://en/374222) 3 × 3 matrices with entries in a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346)).
- All [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569) of class 3 or less are metabelian.
- The [lamplighter group](wikipedia://en/5491540) is metabelian.
- All groups of order *p*<sup>5</sup> are metabelian (for prime *p*).[^o4]
- All groups *G* with abelian subgroups *A* and *B* such that *G=AB* are metabelian.
- All groups of order less than 24 are metabelian.

In contrast to this last example, the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>4</sub> of order 24 is not metabelian, as its commutator subgroup is the non-abelian [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) *A*<sub>4</sub>.

:::
