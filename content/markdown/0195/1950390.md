# Arithmetic genus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic genus"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **arithmetic genus** of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) is one of a few possible generalizations of the [genus of an algebraic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_of_an_algebraic_curve) or [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181).

:::

## Projective varieties {#Projective_varieties}

Let *X* be a [projective scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_scheme) of dimension *r* over a field *k*, the *arithmetic genus* $p_a$ of *X* is defined as

$$p_a(X)=(-1)^r (\chi(\mathcal{O}_X)-1).$$

Here $\chi(\mathcal{O}_X)$ is the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420#Generalizations) of the structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X$.[^o3]

## Complex projective manifolds {#Complex_projective_manifolds}

The arithmetic genus of a [complex projective manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_manifold) of dimension *n* can be defined as a combination of [Hodge numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_number), namely

$$p_a=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} (-1)^j h^{n-j,0}.$$

When *n=1*, the formula becomes $p_a=h^{1,0}$. According to the [Hodge theorem](wikipedia://en/501590#Hodge_theory_for_complex_projective_varieties), $h^{0,1}=h^{1,0}$. Consequently $h^{0,1}=h^1(X)/2=g$, where *g* is the usual (topological) meaning of genus of a surface, so the definitions are compatible.

When *X* is a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538), applying *h*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup> = *h*<sup>*q*,*p*</sup> recovers the earlier definition for projective varieties.

## Kähler manifolds {#Kähler_manifolds}

By using *h*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup> = *h*<sup>*q*,*p*</sup> for compact Kähler manifolds this can be reformulated as the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) in [coherent cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_cohomology) for the [structure sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_sheaf) $\mathcal{O}_M$:

$$p_a=(-1)^n(\chi(\mathcal{O}_M)-1).\,$$

This definition therefore can be applied to some other [locally ringed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_ringed_space).
