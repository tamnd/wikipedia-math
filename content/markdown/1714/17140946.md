# William Mitchinson Hicks

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Mitchinson Hicks"}

**William Mitchinson Hicks**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (23 September 1850, in [Launceston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launceston,_Cornwall), [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall) – 17 August 1934, in [Crowhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowhurst,_East_Sussex), [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex)) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist). He studied at [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), graduating in 1873, and became a fellow at the college.[^o5]

:::

Hicks spent most of his career at [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), contributing to the development of the university there. He was principal of Firth College from 1892 to 1897. In 1897, Firth College merged with two other colleges to form the [University College of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_of_Sheffield), and Hicks was its first principal until 1905, when the college received its own [royal charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_charter) and became the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield). Hicks was the first vice chancellor of the university, serving from 1905.

From 1883 to 1892, he was Professor of Physics and Mathematics at Sheffield, and was Professor of Physics there from 1892 to 1917. He was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1885.[^o3] He was awarded the Royal Society's [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal) in 1912: *"On the ground of his researches in mathematical physics."*[^o3] In 1921, Hicks won the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110).[^o6]

The [Hicks Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hicks_Building) at the University of Sheffield, which houses the departments of [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), the [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) and [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939) Workshop (formally known as the Central Mechanical Workshops) and the School of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), is named in his honour. [Hicks equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hicks_equation) is named after him.
