# Jules Hoüel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jules Hoüel"}

**Guillaume-Jules Hoüel** (7 April 1823 – 14 June 1886) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1843 and received his doctoral degree in 1855 from the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). He was sought by [Urbain Le Verrier](wikipedia://en/176356) at the [Paris Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Observatory), but chose instead to return to Thaon to study there. In 1859 he began to teach at [Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordeaux).

:::

In 1863 Hoüel expressed his doubts about the verifiability of the [parallel postulate](wikipedia://en/33731493) of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331).[^o3]

In 1867 Hoüel produced French translations of two key publications of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610): [Lobachevski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevski)'s *Geometrical Studies on the Theory of Parallels*[^o4] and [Bolyai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolyai)'s *Science of Absolute Space*.[^o5]

Hoüel published a four volume work titled *Théorie Élémentaire des Quantités Complexes*. Volume four, published in 1874, began with a discussion of properties of algebraic operations (commutativity, associativity, distribution, and inverses) and used the algebra of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and [versors](wikipedia://en/2644621) to describe [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405).[^o6] However, in 1890 [P. G. Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._G._Tait) revealed his dissatisfaction with Hoüel's changes in notation with text that Tait had given for Hoüel's use. Tait wrote:[^o7]

The earliest offender in this class was the late M. Hoüel who, while availing himself of my permission to reproduce, in his *Théorie Élémentaire des Quantités Complexes*, large parts of this volume, made his pages absolutely repulsive by introducing fancied improvements in the notation.

In 1876 Hoüel reviewed a [Russian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) quaternion textbook written by Romer (1868)[^o8]
