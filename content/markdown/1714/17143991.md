# Charles Méray

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Méray"}

**Hugues Charles Robert Méray** (12 November 1835, in [Chalon-sur-Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalon-sur-Saône), [Saône-et-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saône-et-Loire) – 2 February 1911, in [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon)) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is noted as the first to publish an arithmetical theory of [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689). His work did not have much of a role in the history of mathematics because France, at that time, was less interested in such matters than [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany).[^o3]

:::

He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1900 in Paris; his contributed paper was presented by [Charles-Ange Laisant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Ange_Laisant).[^o4]

Méray was also a speaker of [Esperanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto). From 1900 until his death, Méray campaigned for the introduction of Esperanto into the scientific world and presented his case to the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1901.[^o5]
