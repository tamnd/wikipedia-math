# BTZ black hole

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="BTZ black hole"}

The **Bañados–Teitelboim–Zanelli** (**BTZ**) **black hole**, named after [Máximo Bañados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Máximo_Bañados), [Claudio Teitelboim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Teitelboim), and [Jorge Zanelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Zanelli), is a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) solution for [(2+1)-dimensional topological gravity](wikipedia://en/30557864) with a negative [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992).

:::

## History {#History}

In 1992, Bañados, Teitelboim, and Zanelli discovered the BTZ black hole [solution](wikipedia://en/1686520) (Bañados Teitelboim Zanelli 1992). This came as a surprise, because when the cosmological constant is zero, a [vacuum solution](wikipedia://en/1441464) of (2+1)-dimensional gravity is necessarily flat (the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848) vanishes in three dimensions, while the [Ricci tensor](wikipedia://en/276582) vanishes due to the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540), so the full [Riemann tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) vanishes), and it can be shown that no black hole solutions with [event horizons](wikipedia://en/29320146) exist.[^o4] But thanks to the negative cosmological constant in the BTZ black hole, it is able to have remarkably similar properties to the 3+1 dimensional [Schwarzschild](wikipedia://en/264606) and [Kerr](wikipedia://en/456715) black hole solutions, which model real-world black holes.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The similarities to the ordinary black holes in 3+1 dimensions:

- It admits a [no hair theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_hair_theorem), fully characterizing the solution by its [ADM-mass](wikipedia://en/3839357), angular momentum and charge.
- It has the same [thermodynamical properties](wikipedia://en/339350) as traditional black hole solutions such as Schwarzschild or Kerr black holes, e.g. its entropy is captured by a law directly analogous to the [Bekenstein bound](wikipedia://en/1482061) in (3+1)-dimensions, essentially with the surface area replaced by the BTZ black hole's circumference.
- Like the Kerr black hole, a rotating BTZ black hole contains an inner and an outer horizon, analogous to an [ergosphere](wikipedia://en/1508445).

Since (2+1)-dimensional gravity has no [Newtonian limit](wikipedia://en/488419), one might fear that the BTZ black hole is not the final state of a [gravitational collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_collapse). It was however shown, that this black hole could arise from collapsing matter and we can calculate the energy-moment tensor of BTZ as same as (3+1) black holes. (Carlip 1995, section 3 Black Holes and Gravitational Collapse)

The BTZ solution is often discussed in the realm on (2+1)-dimensional [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).

:::

## Case without charge {#Case_without_charge}

The metric in the absence of charge is

$$ds^2 = -\frac{(r^2 - r_+^2)(r^2 - r_-^2)}{l^2 r^2}dt^2 + \frac{l^2 r^2 dr^2}{(r^2 - r_+^2)(r^2 - r_-^2)} + r^2 \left(d\phi - \frac{r_+ r_-}{l r^2} dt \right)^2$$

where $r_+,~r_-$ are the black hole radii and $l$ is the radius of AdS<sub>3</sub> space. The mass and angular momentum of the black hole is

$$M = \frac{r_+^2 + r_-^2}{l^2},~~~~~J = \frac{2r_+ r_-}{l}$$

BTZ black holes without any electric charge are locally isometric to [anti-de Sitter space](wikipedia://en/301670). More precisely, it corresponds to an [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) of the [universal covering space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_covering_space) of AdS<sub>\3.</sub>[^o9]

A rotating BTZ black hole admits [closed timelike curves](wikipedia://en/287091).
