# Half-disk topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Half-disk topology"}

In mathematics, and particularly [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), the **half-disk topology** is an example of a [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) given to the set $X$, given by all points $(x,y)$ in the plane such that $y\ge 0$.[^o2] The set $X$ can be termed the closed upper half plane.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

We consider $X$ to consist of the open upper half plane $P$, given by all points $(x,y)$ in the plane such that $y>0$; and the *x*-axis $L$, given by all points $(x,y)$ in the plane such that $y=0$. Clearly $X$ is given by the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) $P\cup L$. The open upper half plane $P$ has a topology given by the [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697) [metric topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_topology#Open_and_closed_sets.2C_topology_and_convergence).[^o2] We extend the topology on $P$ to a topology on $X=P\cup L$ by adding some additional open sets. These extra sets are of the form ${(x,0)}\cup (P\cap U)$, where $(x,0)$ is a point on the line $L$ and $U$ is a [neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_%28topology%29) of $(x,0)$ in the plane, open with respect to the [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric) (defining the disk radius).[^o2]

:::

## Properties of $X$ {#Properties_of_X}

This topology results in a space satisfying the following properties.

- $X$ is [Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_Space) (and thus also $T_0$ and $T_1$).
- $X$ is not [regular](wikipedia://en/53058) and therefore not [normal](wikipedia://en/48629).
- $L$ with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) of $X$ is [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061), so $X$ is not [second-countable](wikipedia://en/901459).
- $X$ is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855). A countably dense subset is given by the rational points $X\cap\mathbb{Q}$.
