# Helly space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helly space"}

In mathematics, and particularly [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Helly space**, named after [Eduard Helly](wikipedia://en/2202570), consists of all [monotonically increasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonically_increasing) [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) ƒ : \[0,1\] → \[0,1\], where \[0,1\] denotes the [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) given by the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all *x* such that 0 ≤ *x* ≤ 1.[^o2] In other words, for all 0 ≤ *x* ≤ 1 we have 0 ≤ ƒ(*x*) ≤ 1 and also if *x* ≤ *y* then ƒ(*x*) ≤ ƒ(*y*).

:::

Let the closed interval \[0,1\] be denoted simply by *I*. We can form the space *I<sup>I</sup>* by taking the [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of closed intervals:[^o3]

$$I^I = \prod_{i \in I} I_i$$

The space *I<sup>I</sup>* is exactly the space of functions ƒ : \[0,1\] → \[0,1\]. For each point *x* in \[0,1\] we assign the point ƒ(*x*) in *I<sub>x</sub>* = \[0,1\].[^o4]

Helly's space is [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) as a subset of $\mathbb{R}^{[0,1]}$.[^o5]

## Topology {#Topology}

The Helly space is a subset of *I<sup>I</sup>*. The space *I<sup>I</sup>* has its own topology, namely the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910).[^o3] The Helly space has a topology; namely the [induced topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_topology) as a subset of *I<sup>I</sup>*.[^o2] It is [normal Haudsdorff](wikipedia://en/48629), [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), and [first-countable](wikipedia://en/904940) but not [second-countable](wikipedia://en/901459).
