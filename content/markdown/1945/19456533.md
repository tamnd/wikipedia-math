# Minimum-distance estimation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minimum-distance estimation"}

**Minimum-distance estimation** (**MDE**) is a conceptual method for fitting a statistical model to data, usually the [empirical distribution](wikipedia://en/2690455). Often-used estimators such as [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) can be thought of as [special cases](wikipedia://en/31308010) of minimum-distance estimation.

:::

While [consistent](wikipedia://en/1497569) and [asymptotically normal](wikipedia://en/3001287), minimum-distance estimators are generally not [statistically efficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_estimator) when compared to [maximum likelihood estimators](wikipedia://en/140806), because they omit the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) usually present in the [likelihood function](wikipedia://en/44968). This, however, substantially reduces the [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/6511) of the optimization problem.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\displaystyle X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be an [independent and identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_and_identically-distributed_random_variables) (iid) [random](wikipedia://en/25685) [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_sample) from a [population](wikipedia://en/27585) with [distribution](wikipedia://en/5793) $F(x;\theta)\colon \theta\in\Theta$ and $\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^k (k\geq 1)$.

Let $\displaystyle F_n(x)$ be the [empirical distribution function](wikipedia://en/2690455) based on the sample.

Let $\hat{\theta}$ be an [estimator](wikipedia://en/10043) for $\displaystyle \theta$. Then $F(x;\hat{\theta})$ is an estimator for $\displaystyle F(x;\theta)$.

Let $d[\cdot,\cdot]$ be a [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) returning some measure of ["distance"](wikipedia://en/39378#General_case) between its two arguments. The functional $\displaystyle d$ is also called the criterion function.

If there exists a $\hat{\theta}\in\Theta$ such that $d[F(x;\hat{\theta}),F_n(x)]=\inf\{d[F(x;\theta),F_n(x)]; \theta\in\Theta\}$, then $\hat{\theta}$ is called the **minimum-distance estimate** of $\displaystyle \theta$.

(Drossos Philippou 1980, 121)

:::

## Statistics used in estimation {#Statistics_used_in_estimation}

Most theoretical studies of minimum-distance estimation, and most applications, make use of "distance" measures which underlie already-established [goodness of fit](wikipedia://en/2474821) tests: the test statistic used in one of these tests is used as the distance measure to be minimised. Below are some examples of statistical tests that have been used for minimum-distance estimation.

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Chi-square criterion"}

### Chi-square criterion {#Chi-square_criterion}

The [chi-square test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson's_chi-square_test) uses as its criterion the sum, over predefined groups, of the squared difference between the increases of the empirical distribution and the estimated distribution, weighted by the increase in the estimate for that group.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Cramér–von Mises criterion"}

### Cramér–von Mises criterion {#Cramér–von_Mises_criterion}

The [Cramér–von Mises criterion](wikipedia://en/5509769) uses the integral of the squared difference between the empirical and the estimated distribution functions (Parr Schucany 1980, 616).

:::

::: {#o12 .statement cue=named-heading name="Kolmogorov–Smirnov criterion"}

### Kolmogorov–Smirnov criterion {#Kolmogorov–Smirnov_criterion}

The [Kolmogorov–Smirnov test](wikipedia://en/16714) uses the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of the [absolute difference](wikipedia://en/28021681) between the empirical and the estimated distribution functions (Parr Schucany 1980, 616).

:::

::: {#o15 .statement cue=named-heading name="Anderson–Darling criterion"}

### Anderson–Darling criterion {#Anderson–Darling_criterion}

The [Anderson–Darling test](wikipedia://en/3479881) is similar to the Cramér–von Mises criterion except that the integral is of a weighted version of the squared difference, where the weighting relates the variance of the empirical distribution function (Parr Schucany 1980, 616).

:::

## Theoretical results {#Theoretical_results}

The theory of minimum-distance estimation is related to that for the asymptotic distribution of the corresponding statistical [goodness of fit](wikipedia://en/2474821) tests. Often the cases of the [Cramér–von Mises criterion](wikipedia://en/5509769), the [Kolmogorov–Smirnov test](wikipedia://en/16714) and the [Anderson–Darling test](wikipedia://en/3479881) are treated simultaneously by treating them as special cases of a more general formulation of a distance measure. Examples of the theoretical results that are available are: [consistency](wikipedia://en/1497569) of the parameter estimates; the asymptotic covariance matrices of the parameter estimates.
