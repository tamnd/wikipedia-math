# Charles Terrot (bishop)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Terrot"}

**Charles Hughes Terrot** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) (19 September 1790 – 2 April 1872) was a Scottish Episcopalian minister, theologian and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He served as [Primus of the Scottish Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primus_of_the_Scottish_Episcopal_Church) from 1857 to 1862.[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

*Image: 19 Northumberland Street, Edinburgh.jpg.* Terrot's home at 19 Northumberland Street, Edinburgh

*Image: The grave of Charles Terrot, New Calton Cemetery.jpg.* The grave of Charles Terrot, New Calton Cemetery

*Image: Grave of Charles Hughes Terrot, Warriston Cemetery.JPG.* Grave of Charlotte Maddon, Charles Hughes Terrot's wife, [Warriston Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warriston_Cemetery)

Charles Terrot was born on 19 September 1790 at [Cuddalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuddalore) in southern India, the son of Captain Elias Terrot of the Indian Army who was killed at the siege of [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore) a few weeks after Charles' birth. His mother, Mary Fonteneau, returned to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) soon after, and raised Charles in [Berwick-upon-Tweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick-upon-Tweed).

He was educated at [Carlisle Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_Grammar_School) and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated BA in 1812. He became a Fellow of Trinity in 1813.

In 1813 he served as a Deacon in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol) before moving to [Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester) as a priest. He returned to Scotland in 1814 to serve as an Incumbent in [Haddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haddington,_East_Lothian). In 1833 he served in St Pauls in Edinburgh leading to his rise to Dean in 1837 and Bishop in 1841. During this time he lived at 19 Northumberland Street in [Edinburgh's New Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Town,_Edinburgh).[^o6]

He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1840, his proposer being [James David Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_David_Forbes), and served as their vice president from 1844 to 1860. He was also a member of the Architectural Society of Scotland.[^o7]

From 1856 until 1872 he was assisted in his role as Bishop by his chaplain [Charles Richard Teape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Richard_Teape);[^o8] [Thomas Baker Morrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Baker_Morrell) was his coadjutor-bishop from 1863 until 1869, shortly after Terrot's retirement.

He died at home at 9 Carlton Street in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 2 April 1872 and is buried in [New Calton Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Calton_Burial_Ground) with his daughter.

His second wife died before him and is buried separately in [Warriston Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warriston_Cemetery).

## Family {#Family}

He married twice, firstly in 1815 to Sarah Ingram Wood (d.1855), secondly, and briefly, in 1859 to Charlotte Madden (d.1862). He had a daughter, Sarah Terrot (d.1901) by his first marriage.[^o7]

## Publications {#Publications}

- *The Epistle of Paul to the Romans* (1828)[^o7]
- *Principles of Biblical Interpretation* (1832)[^o7]
