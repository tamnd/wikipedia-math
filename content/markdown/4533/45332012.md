# List of sums of reciprocals

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of sums of reciprocals"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **sum of reciprocals** (or **sum of inverses**) is defined as the sum of [reciprocals](wikipedia://en/229940) of some series of [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) (counting numbers). It is a sum of [unit fractions](wikipedia://en/491316). If infinitely many numbers have their reciprocals summed, generally the terms are given as a sequence whose $n$th term is defined as the sum of the first $n$ reciprocals.

:::

For sums of reciprocals over a series of finitely many numbers, key questions include whether there is a simple expression for the value of the sum, whether the sum must be less than a certain value, and whether the sum is ever an integer.

When the sum of reciprocals is over an [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series), questions include: First, does the sequence of sums [diverge](wikipedia://en/876428)—that is, does it eventually exceed any given number—or does it [converge](wikipedia://en/1743431), meaning there is some number that it gets arbitrarily close to without ever exceeding it? (A set of positive integers is said to be [large](wikipedia://en/3152856) if the sum of its reciprocals diverges, and small if it converges.) Second, if it converges, what is a simple expression for the value it converges to? Is that value [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) or [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689), and is that value [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1158) or [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325)?

## Finitely many terms {#Finitely_many_terms}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="Harmonic mean"}

- The [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) of a set of positive integers is the number of numbers times the reciprocal of the sum of their reciprocals.

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Optic equation"}

- The [optic equation](wikipedia://en/45342125) requires the sum of the reciprocals of two positive integers *a* and *b* to equal the reciprocal of a third positive integer *c*. All solutions are given by *a* = *mn* + *m*<sup>2</sup>, *b* = *mn* + *n*<sup>2</sup>, *c* = *mn*. This equation appears in various contexts in elementary [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fermat–Catalan conjecture"}

- The [Fermat–Catalan conjecture](wikipedia://en/22736152) concerns a certain [Diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109), equating the sum of two terms, each a positive integer raised to a positive integer power, to a third term that is also a positive integer raised to a positive integer power (with the base integers having no prime factor in common). The conjecture asks whether the equation has an infinitude of solutions in which the sum of the reciprocals of the three exponents in the equation must be less than 1. The purpose of this restriction is to preclude the known infinitude of solutions in which two exponents are 2 and the other exponent is any even number.

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Harmonic number"}

- The *n*-th [harmonic number](wikipedia://en/214729), which is the sum of the reciprocals of the first *n* positive integers, is never an integer except for the case *n* = 1.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="József Kürschák"}

- Moreover, [József Kürschák](wikipedia://en/6023762) proved in 1918 that the sum of the reciprocals of consecutive natural numbers (whether starting from 1 or not) is never an integer.

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="reciprocals of the first n primes"}

- The sum of the [reciprocals of the first *n* primes](wikipedia://en/249208#Partial_sums) is not an integer for any *n*.

:::

- There are 14 distinct combinations of four integers such that the sum of their reciprocals is 1, of which six use four distinct integers and eight repeat at least one integer.[^o9]

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Egyptian fraction"}

- An [Egyptian fraction](wikipedia://en/336349) is the sum of a finite number of reciprocals of positive integers. According to the proof of the [Erdős–Graham problem](wikipedia://en/3467973), if the set of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) greater than one is [partitioned](wikipedia://en/340240) into finitely many subsets, then one of the subsets can be used to form an Egyptian fraction representation of 1.

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Erdős–Straus conjecture"}

- The [Erdős–Straus conjecture](wikipedia://en/2431002) states that for all integers *n* ≥ 2, the rational number 4/*n* can be expressed as the sum of three reciprocals of positive integers.

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fermat quotient"}

- The [Fermat quotient](wikipedia://en/22834255#Special_values) with base 2, which is $\frac{2^{p-1}-1}{p}$ for odd prime *p*, when expressed in [mod](wikipedia://en/20087) *p* and multiplied by −2, equals the sum of the reciprocals mod *p* of the numbers lying in the first half of the range {1, *p* − 1}.

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="Triangle"}

- In any [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), the sum of the reciprocals of the [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241#Inradius_theorems) equals the reciprocal of the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) (regardless of whether or not they are integers).

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="Right triangle"}

- In a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420), the sum of the reciprocals of the squares of the altitudes from the legs (equivalently, of the squares of the legs themselves) equals the reciprocal of the square of the altitude from the hypotenuse (the [inverse Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/62694924)). This holds whether or not the numbers are integers; there is a formula (see [here](wikipedia://en/27430123#Pythagorean_triangles_with_integer_altitude_from_the_hypotenuse)) that generates all integer cases.

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euclidean plane"}

- A triangle not necessarily in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) can be specified as having angles $\frac{\pi}{p},$ $\frac{\pi}{q},$ and $\frac{\pi}{r}.$ Then the triangle is in Euclidean space if the sum of the reciprocals of *p, q,* and *r* equals 1, [spherical space](wikipedia://en/650405) if that sum is greater than 1, and [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/241291) if the sum is less than 1.

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Harmonic divisor number"}

- A [harmonic divisor number](wikipedia://en/462698) is a positive integer whose divisors have a [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) that is an integer. The first five of these are 1, 6, 28, 140, and 270. It is not known whether any harmonic divisor numbers (besides 1) are odd, but there are no odd ones less than 10<sup>24</sup>.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Divisor"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) of a [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670) is 2.

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sphere"}

- When eight points are distributed on the surface of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) with the aim of maximizing the distance between them in some sense, the resulting shape corresponds to a [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045). Specific methods of distributing the points include, for example, minimizing the sum of all reciprocals of squares of distances between points.

:::

## Infinitely many terms {#Infinitely_many_terms}

### Convergent series {#Convergent_series}

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sum-free sequence"}

- A [sum-free sequence](wikipedia://en/1938936) of increasing positive integers is one for which no number is the sum of any [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the previous ones. The sum of the reciprocals of the numbers in any sum-free sequence is less than $2.8570$.

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Heptagonal number"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [heptagonal numbers](wikipedia://en/509019) converges to a known value that is not only [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689) but also [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325), and for which there exists a [complicated formula](wikipedia://en/509019#Sum_of_reciprocals).

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Twin prime"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997), of which there may be finitely many or infinitely many, is known to be finite and is called [Brun's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brun's_constant), approximately $1.90216$. (By convention, the reciprocal of $5$ appears twice in the sum.)

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=list-entry name="Proth prime"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [Proth primes](wikipedia://en/62508931), of which there may be finitely many or infinitely many, is known to be finite, approximately $0.747392479$.[^o25]

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="Prime quadruplet"}

- The [prime quadruplets](wikipedia://en/812561) are pairs of twin primes with only one odd number between them. The sum of the reciprocals of the numbers in prime quadruplets is approximately $0.8706$.

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="perfect powers"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [perfect powers](wikipedia://en/4023591#Examples_and_sums) (including duplicates) is $1$.

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="perfect powers"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [perfect powers](wikipedia://en/4023591#Examples_and_sums) (excluding duplicates) is approximately $0.8745$.

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=list-entry name="Integral"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the powers $\ n^n\$ is approximately equal to $1.2913$. The sum is exactly equal to a definite [integral](wikipedia://en/15532): $$\ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{\ n^n } = \int_0^1 \frac{\ dx\ }{\ x^x}\$$

:::

This identity was discovered by [Johann Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/268886) in 1697, and is now known as one of the two [Sophomore's dream](wikipedia://en/9353592) identities.

::: {#o31 .entry cue=list-entry name="Goldbach–Euler theorem"}

- The [Goldbach–Euler theorem](wikipedia://en/14498167) states that the sum of the reciprocals of the numbers that are 1 less than a perfect power (excluding duplicates) is 1.

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=list-entry name="triangular numbers"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of all the non-zero [triangular numbers](wikipedia://en/213915#Other_properties) is $2$.

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=list-entry name="Reciprocal Fibonacci constant"}

- The [reciprocal Fibonacci constant](wikipedia://en/11827553) is the sum of the reciprocals of the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number), which is known to be finite and irrational and approximately equal to 3.3599. For other finite sums of subsets of the reciprocals of Fibonacci numbers, see [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number#Reciprocal_sums).

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=list-entry name="Exponential factorial"}

- An [exponential factorial](wikipedia://en/3313244) is an operation [recursively defined](wikipedia://en/146806) as $\ a_0 = 1, ~ a_n = n^{a_{n - 1}} ~.$ For example, $\ a_4 = 4^{3^{2^1}}\$ where the exponents are evaluated from the top down. The sum of the reciprocals of the exponential factorials from 1 onward is approximately 1.6111 and is transcendental.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=list-entry name="Powerful number"}

- A "[powerful number](wikipedia://en/462499)" is a positive integer for which every prime appearing in its [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization) appears there at least twice. The sum of the reciprocals of the powerful numbers is close to 1.9436.[^o36]

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=list-entry name="factorials"}

- The reciprocals of the [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606) sum to the [transcendental number](wikipedia://en/30325) [$e$](wikipedia://en/9633) (one of two constants called "[Euler's number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler's_number)").

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=list-entry name="Square number"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [square numbers](wikipedia://en/213919) (the [Basel problem](wikipedia://en/490303)) is the transcendental number $\tfrac{\,\pi^{2}\,}{6}$, or $\zeta(2)$ where $\zeta$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=list-entry name="Apéry's constant"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the cubes of positive integers is called [Apéry's constant](wikipedia://en/2216711) $\zeta(3)$ , and equals approximately 1.2021. This number is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689), but it is not known whether or not it is [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325).

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=list-entry name="Powers of 2"}

- The reciprocals of the non-negative integer [powers of 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_2) sum to $2$. This is a particular case of the sum of the reciprocals of any geometric series where the first term and the common ratio are positive integers. If the first term is $a$ and the common ratio is $r$ then the sum is $\tfrac{r}{\,a (r - 1)}$.

:::

::: {#o41 .entry cue=list-entry name="Kempner series"}

- The [Kempner series](wikipedia://en/17961670) is the sum of the reciprocals of all positive integers not containing the digit "9" in base $10$. Unlike the [harmonic series](wikipedia://en/142488), which does not exclude those numbers, this series converges, specifically to approximately $22.9207$.

:::

::: {#o42 .entry cue=list-entry name="Palindromic number"}

- A [palindromic number](wikipedia://en/105956) is one that remains the same when its digits are reversed. The sum of the reciprocals of the palindromic numbers converges to approximately $3.3703$.

:::

::: {#o43 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pentatope number"}

- A [pentatope number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentatope_number) is a number in the fifth cell of any row of [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) starting with the five-term row $1 4 6 4 1$. The sum of the reciprocals of the pentatope numbers is $\tfrac{4}{\,3\,}$.

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sylvester's sequence"}

- [Sylvester's sequence](wikipedia://en/4535485) is an [integer sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) in which each member of the sequence is the product of the previous members, plus one. The first few terms of the sequence are $2, 3, 7, 43, 1807$. The sum of the reciprocals of the numbers in Sylvester's sequence is $1$.

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=list-entry name="Riemann zeta function"}

- The [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) $\zeta(s)$ is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of a [complex variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable) $s$ that [analytically continues](wikipedia://en/80842) the sum of the infinite series $$\ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{\ n^s\ } ~$$ to an analytic function on the entire complex plane except for *s* = 1, where $\zeta(s)$ has a pole. This series converges if and only if the real part of $s$ is greater than $1$.

:::

::: {#o46 .entry cue=list-entry name="Mersenne numbers"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of all the [Mersenne numbers](wikipedia://en/18908) is [Erdős–Borwein constant](wikipedia://en/211842).

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fermat number"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of all the [Fermat numbers](wikipedia://en/91127) (numbers of the form $\ 2^{(2^n)} + 1\$) is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689).

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pronic number"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the [pronic numbers](wikipedia://en/652733) (products of two consecutive integers) (excluding $0$) is $1$ (see [Telescoping series](wikipedia://en/508070)).

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=list-entry name="difference"}

- The [difference](wikipedia://en/74141#Notation_and_terminology) between $H_{k(n+1)}$ and $H_{k}$ [approaches](wikipedia://en/18019#Harmonic_number_difference) the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of $n+1$ as $k$ increases.

:::

### Divergent series {#Divergent_series}

::: {#o51 .entry cue=list-entry name="harmonic series"}

- The $n$-th partial sum of the [harmonic series](wikipedia://en/142488), which is the sum of the reciprocals of the first $n$ positive integers, diverges as $n$ goes to infinity, albeit extremely slowly: The sum of the first $43$ terms is less than $100$. The difference between the cumulative sum and the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of $n$ converges to the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant), commonly denoted as $\gamma,$ which is approximately $0.5772$.

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=list-entry name="sum of the reciprocals of the primes"}

- The [sum of the reciprocals of the primes](wikipedia://en/249208) diverges.

:::

::: {#o53 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dirichlet's theorem"}

- Given coprime positive integers $a$ and $b$, the sum of the reciprocal of the primes of the form $an + b$ diverges. This result is used to prove the [Dirichlet's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_theorem_on_arithmetic_progressions), which states that there are infinitely many primes $p$ satisfying $p\equiv a\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }b)$.

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares"}

- The sum of the reciprocals of the primes of the form $4n + 1$ is divergent. By [Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares](wikipedia://en/1850216), it follows that the sum of reciprocals of numbers of the form $a^2 + b^2,$ where $a$ and $b$ are non-negative integers, not both equal to $0$, diverges, with or without repetition.

:::

- If $a(k)$ is any ascending series of positive integers with the property that there exists $N$ such that $a(k + 1) - a(k) < N$ for all $k$, then the sum of the reciprocals $\tfrac{1}{\,a(k)\,}$ diverges.

::: {#o55 .entry cue=list-entry name="Erdős conjecture on arithmetic progressions"}

- The [Erdős conjecture on arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/4312009) states that if the sum of the reciprocals of the members of a set $A$ of positive integers diverges, then $A$ contains [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389) of any length, however great. the conjecture remains unproven.

:::
