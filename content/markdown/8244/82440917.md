# Jürgen Pilz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jürgen Pilz"}

**Jürgen Pilz** (born 1951) is a German mathematician and statistician. He is known for work in [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412), [spatial statistics](wikipedia://en/9467349), [experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_design), and [environmental statistics](wikipedia://en/8919339).[^o3] Pilz is [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) of Applied Statistics at [Alpen-Adria University Klagenfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpen-Adria-Universitat_Klagenfurt) and since 2021 Senior Lecturer in the international master's program in applied data science at the [Carinthia University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia_University_of_Applied_Sciences).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Pilz was born in 1951 in Langenau, Germany. He received a PhD in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) in 1978 at the [Technical University Bergakademie Freiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Bergakademie_Freiberg) (TU BAF).He completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in mathematics at the same university in 1988.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

From 1989 to 1993 Pilz was Professor of Mathematical Geology in the Department of Geosciences at TU BAF. In 1991–1992 he held a [Humboldt Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation) at the [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin), in the collaborative research program on mathematical geology and [geoinformatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoinformatics).

In 1994 he was appointed professor and chair of applied statistics at Alpen-Adria University (AAU), Austria, where he remained until his retirement in 2020. He served as (founding) head of the Department of Statistics from 2007 to 2015 and again from 2018 to 2019.[^o8]

Since October 2020 he has been professor emeritus at AAU and since October 2021 senior lecturer at the Carinthia University of Applied Sciences, in the international master's program in applied data science.[^o9]

Pilz has also held guest professorships at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen), [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University), the [University of Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Augsburg), the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), and the [University of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury).[^o10]

## Research {#Research}

Pilz's research covers both methodological and applied statistics, with a focus on Bayesian approaches and spatial data analysis. His main areas of work include Bayesian statistics, spatial statistics, industrial statistics, environmental statistics, experimental design, Bayesian epidemiology, and Bayesian machine learning.[^o12][^o13]

Early in his career, Pilz contributed to the development of Bayesian estimation and experimental design in [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997), including work on robust [Bayesian designs](wikipedia://en/1706860) and optimal design under prior information. He has published extensively on Bayesian [kriging](wikipedia://en/477026), spatial interpolation, prediction under uncertainty in covariance structures, and sampling design for spatially correlated data.[^o14] His research also includes copula-based geostatistical models and mixture models for spatial dependence in environmental and climate data.[^o15]

Pilz has applied statistical methods to a range of domains, including environmental science (rainfall and drought analysis, climate model evaluation), epidemiology (disease mapping and cancer rate smoothing), geoscience (landslide risk analysis), agricultural processes (pesticide control, crop yield prediction) and industrial advanced process control, with a particular emphasis on semiconductor manufacturing.[^o16][^o17]

In recent years, his work has included statistical learning and uncertainty estimation in [deep neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_neural_network), Bayesian methods for variable selection in regression, ensemble feature selection frameworks, and applications of Bayesian deep learning to point cloud segmentation and image restoration.[^o18]

He has also taken part in European research projects, such as SECOQC (quantum cryptography networks), INTAMAP (automatic mapping of environmental variables), EPT300 (reliability of semiconductor power devices), and iDev40 (Bayesian deep learning for industrial applications).[^o19][^o20]

Pilz has supervised 45 PhD students and more than 100 Master students.[^o21]
