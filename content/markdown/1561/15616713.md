# Kline sphere characterization

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kline sphere characterization"}

In mathematics, a **Kline sphere characterization,** named after [John Robert Kline](wikipedia://en/21005350), is a [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) characterization of a two-dimensional [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) in terms of what sort of subset separates it. Its proof was one of the first notable accomplishments of [R. H. Bing](wikipedia://en/967353); Bing gave an alternate proof using [brick partitioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_partitioning) in his paper *Complementary domains of continuous curves* [^o2]

:::

A [simple closed curve](wikipedia://en/89246) in a two-dimensional sphere (for instance, its equator) [separates](wikipedia://en/6233) the sphere into two pieces upon removal. If one removes a pair of points from a sphere, however, the remainder is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233). Kline's sphere characterization states that the converse is true: If a nondegenerate [locally connected](wikipedia://en/6959754) [metric](wikipedia://en/20018) [continuum](wikipedia://en/24758520) is separated by any simple closed curve but by no pair of points, then it is a two-dimensional sphere.
