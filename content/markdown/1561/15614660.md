# List of International Mathematical Olympiads

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of International Mathematical Olympiads"}

*Image: IMO logo.svg.* Logo of the International Mathematical Olympiad

The first of the [International Mathematical Olympiads](wikipedia://en/82208) (IMOs) was held in [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania) in 1959. The oldest of the [International Science Olympiads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Science_Olympiad), the IMO has since been held annually, except in 1980. That year, the competition initially planned to be held in [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia) was cancelled due to the [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Afghanistan).[^o15] Because the competition was initially founded for [Eastern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) countries participating in the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact), under the influence of the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc),[^o2] the earlier IMOs were hosted only in Eastern European countries, gradually spreading to other nations.[^o3]

:::

The first IMO was held in [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania) in 1959. Seven countries entered – [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland), Romania and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) – with the hosts finishing as the top-ranked nation.[^o4] The number of participating countries has since risen: 14 countries took part in 1969, 50 in 1989, and 104 in 2009.[^o5]

[North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea) is the only country whose entire team has been caught cheating, resulting in its disqualification at the 32nd IMO in 1991 and the 51st IMO in 2010.[^o6] (However, the 2010 case was controversial.[^o7][^o8]) There have been other disqualifications of contestants due to cheating, but such cases are not officially made public.[^o9] In January 2011, [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) gave €1 million to the IMO organization to help cover the costs of the events from 2011 to 2015.[^o10]
