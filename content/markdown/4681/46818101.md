# Variables sampling plan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Variables sampling plan"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **variables sampling plan** is an [acceptance sampling](wikipedia://en/25719356) technique. Plans for [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) are intended for quality characteristics that are measured on a continuous scale. This plan requires the knowledge of the [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) (e.g. [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462)). The historical [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_mathematics) of this technique dates back to the seminal work of [W. Allen Wallis](wikipedia://en/8710912) (1943). The purpose of a plan for variables is to assess whether the process is operating far enough from the specification limit. Plans for variables may produce a similar OC curve to attribute plans with significantly less sample size.

:::

The decision criterion of these plans are

$\bar{X}+ k\sigma \leqslant USL$ or $\bar{X}- k\sigma \geqslant LSL$

where $\bar{X}$ and $\sigma$ are the [sample mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_mean) and the [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590) respectively, $k$ is the [critical distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_distance), $USL$ and $LSL$ are the upper and lower regulatory limits. When the above expression is satisfied the proportion nonconforming is lower than expected and therefore the lot is accepted.

A variables sampling plan can be designed so that the OC curve passes through two points (AQL,$\alpha$) and (LQL,$\beta$). AQL and LQL are the [Acceptable quality limit](wikipedia://en/17216134) and the limiting quality level respectively. $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the producer and consumer's risks. The required [sample size](wikipedia://en/1776839) ($n$) and the critical distance ($k$) can be obtained as

$$k = \frac{Z_{LQL}Z_{\alpha}+Z_{AQL}Z_{\beta}}{Z_{\alpha}+Z_{\beta}}$$

where $Z$ is the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) function.

When the [dispersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersion_%28statistics%29) is known the required sample size ($n$) is obtained from

$$n = \left ( \frac{Z_\alpha +Z_\beta}{Z_{AQL}-Z_{LQL}}\right )^{2}$$

while for unknown $\sigma$ the sample size is approximately

$$n = \left ( \frac{Z_\alpha+Z_\beta}{Z_{AQL}-Z_{LQL}}\right )^{2}\left ( 1+\frac{k^2}{2} \right )$$

The [MIL-STD-414](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iarchive:MIL-STD-414) provides tables to obtain the required sample size and the critical distance according to the type of inspection.
