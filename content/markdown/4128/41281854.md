# Thomas Barker (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Barker"}

**Thomas Barker** (9 September 1838 – 20 November 1907) was a Scottish mathematician, professor of pure mathematics at [Owens College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born 9 September 1838, he was son of Thomas Barker, farmer, of Murcar, Balgonie, near [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen), and of his wife Margaret. Three other children died in infancy. He was educated at [Aberdeen Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Grammar_School), and at [King's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Aberdeen) in the same town, where he graduated in 1857 with distinction in mathematics.[^o3]

Barker entered [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) as minor scholar and subsizar in 1858.[^o4] He became foundation scholar in 1860, Sheepshanks astronomical exhibitioner in 1861, and came out in the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) of 1862 as [senior wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_wrangler); he was also [first Smith's prizeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Smith's_prizeman). He was elected to a fellowship in the autumn of 1862, and was assistant tutor of Trinity till 1865, when he was appointed professor of pure mathematics in Owens College, [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester). He held this post for twenty years.[^o3]

Barker was a follower of [Augustus De Morgan](wikipedia://en/18949289) and [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298). He was interested in the logical basis rather than in the applications of mathematics, and was an austere teacher. He disliked publication.[^o3]

After resigning his chair in 1885, Barker lived in retirement, first at [Whaley Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whaley_Bridge) and then at [Buxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton). He pursued the study of [cryptogamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptogam) botany. He died unmarried at Buxton on 20 November 1907, and was buried in [Southern Cemetery, Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cemetery,_Manchester).[^o3]

## Pupils {#Pupils}

Barker had a number of distinguished mathematicians and physicists as pupils: they included [John Walton Capstick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walton_Capstick),[^o6] [John Hopkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hopkinson), [John Henry Poynting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Henry_Poynting), [Arthur Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Schuster), and [Joseph John Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_John_Thomson).[^o3]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

By his will Barker provided for the foundation in the University of Manchester of a professorship of cryptogamic botany, and for the endowment of bursaries in mathematics and botany.[^o3]
