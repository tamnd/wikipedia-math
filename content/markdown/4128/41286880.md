# Henri Wald

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henri Wald"}

*Image: Henri Wald in 1978.png.* Wald in 1978

**Henri "Ricu" Wald** (October 31, 1920 – July 14, 2002),[^o2] also known as **Henry Wald**,[^o2] was a Romanian [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065), and [essayist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essayist).[^o3]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Wald was born to a family of small merchants in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), the capital of [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania).[^o3] Wald first became interested in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at age sixteen, during a lecture given by one of his teachers.[^o2] He was forced to leave high school in 1940 due to discriminatory [anti-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Jewish) laws.[^o3] Thus, Wald studied at a private college for Jewish students.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Wald served in a forced labor detachment due to him being [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish).[^o3] In terms of his political views, Wald was a [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) and "a radical [antifascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fascism)".[^o3] Wald began publishing in September 1944 in the newspaper *Tribuna poporului* (The People's Tribune).[^o3] In addition, he also contributed to several additional leftist periodicals and to some cultural publications as well.[^o3] Wald graduated from the faculty of philosophy at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest) in 1946, after writing a dissertation called “The Petty Bourgeois Mentality.”[^o2][^o3] Wald became a professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) in Bucharest in 1948, a job which he occupied until 1962 (when he was fired due to his opposition to the official "[nationalist-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_communism_in_Romania)" stance of the then-Romanian government).[^o3] Wald worked in several other educational institutions as well until his retirement in 1983.[^o3] Due to his political commitments, Wald also worked in the [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda) department for the [Communist Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Romania) regime in the late 1940s and early 1950s.[^o3] Later on in his life, Wald became significantly closer to Judaism, which he thought was a "matrix of humanist thinking".[^o3] Late in his life, the [Romanian Radio Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Radio_Broadcasting_Company)’s Center for Oral History (C.O.H.) conducted an interview with Wald where he talked about and discussed his life story.[^o2] Wald died in 2002, at the age of 80.[^o3][^o2]
