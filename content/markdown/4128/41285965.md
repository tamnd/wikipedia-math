# Unital (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unital"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **unital** is a set of $n^3+1$ [points](wikipedia://en/593693) arranged into subsets of size *n* + 1 so that every pair of distinct points of the set are contained in exactly one subset. This is equivalent to saying that a unital is a 2-(*n*<sup>3</sup> + 1, *n* + 1, 1) [block design](wikipedia://en/1188184). Some unitals may be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) of order *n*<sup>2</sup> (the subsets of the design become sets of [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear) points in the projective plane). In this case of *embedded unitals*, every line of the plane intersects the unital in either 1 or *n* + 1 points. In the [Desarguesian planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desarguesian_plane), PG(2,*q*<sup>2</sup>), the classical examples of unitals are given by nondegenerate Hermitian curves. There are also many non-classical examples. The first and the only known unital with non [prime power](wikipedia://en/1630483) parameters, *n*=*6*, was constructed by Bhaskar Bagchi and Sunanda Bagchi.[^n1] It is still unknown if this unital can be embedded in a projective plane of order *36*, if such a plane exists.

:::

## Unitals {#Unitals}

### Classical {#Classical}

A **correlation** of a projective geometry is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) on its subspaces that reverses containment. In particular, a correlation interchanges [points](wikipedia://en/593693) and [hyperplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplanes).[^n2]

A correlation of order two is called a **polarity**.

A polarity is called a **unitary polarity** if its associated [sesquilinear form](wikipedia://en/860138) *s* with companion automorphism *α* satisfies

*s*(*u*,*v*) = *s*(*v*,*u*)<sup>*α*</sup> for all vectors *u*, *v* of the underlying [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370).

A point is called an **absolute point** of a polarity if it lies on the image of itself under the polarity.

The absolute points of a unitary polarity of the projective geometry PG(*d*,*F*), for some *d* ≥ 2, is a **nondegenerate Hermitian variety**, and if *d* = 2 this variety is called a **nondegenerate Hermitian curve**.[^n3]

In PG(2,*q*<sup>2</sup>) for some prime power *q*, the set of points of a nondegenerate Hermitian curve form a unital,[^n4] which is called a *classical unital*.

Let $\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}(2,q^2)$ be a nondegenerate Hermitian curve in $PG(2,q^2)$ for some prime power $q$. As all nondegenerate Hermitian curves in the same plane are projectively equivalent, $\mathcal{H}$ can be described in terms of [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) as follows:[^n5]

$$\mathcal{H} = \{(x_0, x_1, x_2)\colon x_0^{q+1} + x_1^{q+1} + x_2^{q+1} = 0 \}.$$

### Ree unitals {#Ree_unitals}

Another family of unitals based on [Ree groups](wikipedia://en/1739637) was constructed by H. Lüneburg.[^n6] Let Γ = R(*q*) be the Ree group of type <sup>2</sup>G<sub>2</sub> of order (*q*<sup>3</sup> + 1)*q*<sup>3</sup>(*q* − 1) where *q* = 3<sup>2*m*+1</sup>. Let *P* be the set of all *q*<sup>3</sup> + 1 [Sylow 3-subgroups](wikipedia://en/53993) of Γ. Γ [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) doubly transitively on this set by [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_theory%29) (it will be convenient to think of these subgroups as *points* that Γ is acting on.) For any *S* and *T* in *P*, the pointwise [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28group_theory%29), Γ<sub>*S*,*T*</sub> is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *q* - 1, and thus contains a unique [involution](wikipedia://en/293450), μ. Each such involution fixes exactly *q* + 1 points of *P*. Construct a [block design](wikipedia://en/1188184) on the points of *P* whose blocks are the fixed point sets of these various involutions μ. Since Γ acts doubly transitively on *P*, this will be a 2-design with parameters 2-(*q*<sup>3</sup> + 1, *q* + 1, 1) called a Ree unital.[^n7]

Lüneburg also showed that the Ree unitals can not be embedded in projective planes of order *q*<sup>2</sup> ([Desarguesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desarguesian_plane) or not) such that the automorphism group Γ is induced by a [collineation group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collineation_group) of the plane.[^n8] For *q* = 3, Grüning[^n9] proved that a Ree unital can not be embedded in any projective plane of order 9.[^n10]

### Unitals with $n = 3$ {#Unitals_with}

In the [four projective planes of order 9](wikipedia://en/21092112#Examples) (the [Desarguesian plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desarguesian_plane) PG(2,9), the [Hall plane](wikipedia://en/35791745) of order 9, the dual Hall plane of order 9 and the [Hughes plane](wikipedia://en/21116300) of order 9.), an exhaustive computer search by Penttila and Royle[^n11] found 18 unitals (up to equivalence) with *n* = 3 in these four planes: two in PG(2,9) (both Buekenhout), four in the [Hall plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_plane_of_order_9) (two Buekenhout, two not), and so another four in the dual Hall plane, and eight in the Hughes plane. However, one of the Buekenhout unitals in the Hall plane is self-dual,[^o17] and thus gets counted again in the dual Hall plane. Thus, there are 17 distinct embeddable unitals with *n* = 3. On the other hand, a nonexhaustive computer search found over 900 mutually nonisomorphic designs which are unitals with *n* = 3.[^n12]

### Isomorphic versus equivalent unitals {#Isomorphic_versus_equivalent_unitals}

Since unitals are [block designs](wikipedia://en/1188184), two unitals are said to be *isomorphic* if there is a design [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between them, that is, a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between the point sets which maps blocks to blocks. This concept does not take into account the property of embeddability, so to do so we say that two unitals, embedded in the same ambient plane, are *equivalent* if there is a [collineation](wikipedia://en/4072976) of the plane which maps one unital to the other.[^n10]

## Buekenhout's Constructions {#Buekenhout's_Constructions}

By examining the classical unital in $PG(2,q^2)$ in the [Bruck/Bose model](wikipedia://en/2628762#Geometric_construction_with_spreads_%28Bruck/Bose%29), Buekenhout[^o22] provided two constructions, which together proved the existence of an embedded unital in any finite 2-dimensional [translation plane](wikipedia://en/2628762). Metz[^o23] subsequently showed that one of Buekenhout's constructions actually yields non-classical unitals in all finite Desarguesian planes of square order at least 9. These *Buekenhout-Metz* unitals have been extensively studied.[^o24][^o25]

The core idea in Buekenhout's construction is that when one looks at $PG(2,q^2)$ in the higher-dimensional Bruck/Bose model, which lies in $PG(4,q)$, the equation of the Hermitian curve satisfied by a classical unital becomes a quadric surface in $PG(4,q)$, either a point-cone over a 3-dimensional ovoid if the line represented by the spread of the Bruck/Bose model meets the unital in one point, or a non-singular quadric otherwise. Because these objects have known intersection patterns with respect to planes of $PG(4,q)$, the resulting point set remains a unital in any translation plane whose generating spread contains all of the same lines as the original spread within the quadric surface. In the ovoidal cone case, this forced intersection consists of a single line, and any spread can be mapped onto a spread containing this line, showing that every translation plane of this form admits an embedded unital.

## Hermitian varieties {#Hermitian_varieties}

Hermitian varieties are in a sense a generalisation of [quadrics](wikipedia://en/145570), and occur naturally in the [theory of polarities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_polarities).

::: {#o28 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

Let *K* be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) with an involutive [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) $\theta$. Let *n* be an integer $\geq 1$ and *V* be an *(n+1)*-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over *K*.

A Hermitian variety *H* in *PG(V)* is a set of points of which the representing vector lines consisting of isotropic points of a non-trivial Hermitian [sesquilinear form](wikipedia://en/860138) on *V*.

:::

::: {#o30 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Representation"}

### Representation {#Representation}

Let $e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_n$ be a basis of *V*. If a point *p* in the [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) has homogeneous coordinates $(X_0,\ldots,X_n)$ with respect to this basis, it is on the Hermitian variety if and only if :

$$\sum_{i,j = 0}^{n} a_{ij} X_{i} X_{j}^{\theta} =0$$

where $a_{i j}=a_{j i}^{\theta}$ and not all $a_{ij}=0$

If one constructs the [Hermitian matrix](wikipedia://en/189682) *A* with $A_{i j}=a_{i j}$, the equation can be written in a compact way :

$$X^t A X^{\theta}=0$$

where $X= \begin{bmatrix} X_0 \\ X_1 \\ \vdots \\ X_n \end{bmatrix}.$

:::

### Tangent spaces and singularity {#Tangent_spaces_and_singularity}

Let *p* be a point on the Hermitian variety *H*. A line *L* through *p* is by definition [tangent](wikipedia://en/30807) when it is contains only one point (*p* itself) of the variety or lies completely on the variety. One can prove that these lines form a subspace, either a hyperplane of the full space. In the latter case, the point is singular.
