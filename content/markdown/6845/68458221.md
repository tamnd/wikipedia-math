# Cuthbert Edmund Cullis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cuthbert Edmund Cullis"}

*Image: C E Cullis.jpg.*

**Cuthbert Edmund Cullis** (15 April 1868 – 20 March 1954) was an English mathematician who worked as a professor of mathematics at the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) and was influential in standardizing notation and conventions in the algebra of matrices and determinants.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Cullis was the son of Frederick John, a dock surveyor in [Gloucester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucester) and Louisa (née Corbett) Cullis. One of his two sisters, [Winifred C. Cullis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winifred_Cullis), became a physiologist. He was educated at King Edward High School, Birmingham after which he joined [Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge). After being seventh [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1891 he went to [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena), Germany and received a doctorate under [Carl Johannes Thomae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Johannes_Thomae) with a thesis titled *Die Bewegung Durchlöcherter Körper in einer Inkompressiblen Flüssigkeit*. He became a lecturer at Hartley College and moved to the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) in 1910 where he served as Hardinge Professor of Mathematics until his retirement in 1925.[^o3]

Cullis published extensively in the Bulletin of the Calcutta Mathematical Society and among his contribution was the work demonstrating that the [Moebius ring](wikipedia://en/37817) was a section of the cubic surface defined by:

y(x<sup>2</sup>+y<sup>2</sup>+z<sup>2</sup>-a<sup>2</sup>)-2z(x<sup>2</sup>+y<sup>2</sup>+ax)=0

His most important work was *Matrices and Determinoids* (1913-1925) to be published in three volumes although only two volumes and a part of the third volume were finally published. He defined what is now called the Cullis-Radić [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) for rectangular matrices.[^o4][^o5]

Cullis died in Gloucester and bequeathed £1000 to Gonville & Caius college to support needy students.[^o6]
