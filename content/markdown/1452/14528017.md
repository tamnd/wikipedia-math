# List of incomplete proofs

A technical argument by a trusted author, which is hard to check and looks similar to arguments known to be correct, is hardly ever checked in detail.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of incomplete proofs"}

This page lists notable examples of incomplete or incorrect published [mathematical proofs](wikipedia://en/82285). Most of these were accepted as complete or correct for several years but later discovered to contain gaps or errors. There are both examples where a complete proof was later found, or where the alleged result turned out to be false.

:::

## Results later proved rigorously {#Results_later_proved_rigorously}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euclid's Elements"}

- [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107). Euclid's proofs are essentially correct, but strictly speaking sometimes contain gaps because he tacitly uses some unstated assumptions, such as the existence of [intersection points](wikipedia://en/8240558). In 1899 [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) gave a complete set of ([second order](wikipedia://en/341428)) axioms for Euclidean geometry, called [Hilbert's axioms](wikipedia://en/838151), and between 1926 and 1959 [Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) gave some complete sets of [first order axioms](wikipedia://en/10983), called [Tarski's axioms](wikipedia://en/2181360).

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Isoperimetric inequality"}

- [Isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182). For three dimensions it states that the shape enclosing the maximum volume for its surface area is the sphere. It was formulated by [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) but not proved rigorously until the 19th century, by [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834).

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Infinitesimals"}

- [Infinitesimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimals). In the 18th century there was widespread use of infinitesimals in calculus, though these were not really well defined. Calculus was put on firm foundations in the 19th century, and [Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024) put infinitesimals in a rigorous basis with the introduction of [nonstandard analysis](wikipedia://en/21708) in the 20th century.

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fundamental theorem of algebra"}

- [Fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414) (see [History](wikipedia://en/51414#History)). Many incomplete or incorrect attempts were made at proving this theorem in the 18th century, including by [d'Alembert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_le_Rond_d'Alembert) (1746), [Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) (1749), [de Foncenex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Daviet_de_Foncenex) (1759), [Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793) (1772), [Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783) (1795), [Wood](wikipedia://en/25831625) (1798), and [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Carl_Friedrich_Gauss) (1799). The first rigorous proof was published by [Argand](wikipedia://en/543727) in 1806.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions"}

- [Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/101453). In 1808 Legendre published an attempt at a proof of Dirichlet's theorem, but as Dupré pointed out in 1859 one of the lemmas used by Legendre is false. [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) gave a complete proof in 1837.

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="Kronecker–Weber theorem"}

- The proofs of the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](wikipedia://en/1082550) by [Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) (1853) and [Weber](wikipedia://en/2643615) (1886) both had gaps. The first complete proof was given by Hilbert in 1896.

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alfred Kempe"}

- In 1879, [Alfred Kempe](wikipedia://en/9092767) published a purported proof of the [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949), whose validity as a proof was accepted for eleven years before it was refuted by [Percy Heawood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Heawood). [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait) gave another incorrect proof in 1880 which was shown to be incorrect by [Julius Petersen](wikipedia://en/76861) in 1891. Kempe's proof did, however, suffice to show the weaker [five color theorem](wikipedia://en/1982872). The four-color theorem was eventually proved by [Kenneth Appel](wikipedia://en/311544) and [Wolfgang Haken](wikipedia://en/311546) in 1976.[^o10]

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Schröder–Bernstein theorem"}

- [Schröder–Bernstein theorem](wikipedia://en/44218028). In 1896 [Schröder](wikipedia://en/448015) published a proof sketch[^o12] which, however, was shown to be faulty by [Alwin Reinhold Korselt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alwin_Reinhold_Korselt) in 1911[^o13] (confirmed by Schröder).[^o14][^o15]

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fermat's Last Theorem"}

- [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953). An initial proof was released by [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) in June 1993 but was found to contain an error in September of that year.[^o17] Wiles would go on to publish a [corrected proof](wikipedia://en/21950759) in 1995.[^o18]

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Jordan curve theorem"}

- [Jordan curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444). There has been some controversy about whether Jordan's original proof of this in 1887 contains gaps. [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen) in 1905 claimed that Jordan's proof is incomplete, but in 2007 [Hales](wikipedia://en/2388356) said that the gaps are minor and that Jordan's proof is essentially complete.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Lebesgue"}

- In 1905 [Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514) tried to prove the (correct) result that a function implicitly defined by a [Baire function](wikipedia://en/3575729) is Baire, but his proof incorrectly assumed that the [projection](wikipedia://en/56899835) of a [Borel set](wikipedia://en/43327) is Borel. [Suslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Yakovlevich_Suslin) pointed out the error and was inspired by it to define [analytic sets](wikipedia://en/634261) as continuous [images](wikipedia://en/579311) of Borel sets.

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dehn's lemma"}

- [Dehn's lemma](wikipedia://en/4362183). [Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602) published an attempted proof in 1910, but [Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368525) found a gap in 1929. It was finally proved in 1956 by [Christos Papakyriakopoulos](wikipedia://en/1463636).

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hilbert's sixteenth problem"}

- [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750) about the finiteness of the number of limit cycles of a plane polynomial vector field. [Henri Dulac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Dulac) published a partial solution to this problem in 1923, but in about 1980 [Écalle](wikipedia://en/57975076) and [Ilyashenko](wikipedia://en/56631455) independently found a serious gap, and fixed it in about 1991.[^o23]

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=list-entry name="Lazar Lyusternik"}

- In 1929 [Lazar Lyusternik](wikipedia://en/4201372) and [Lev Schnirelmann](wikipedia://en/747223) published a proof of the [theorem of the three geodesics](wikipedia://en/48445265), which was later found to be flawed. The proof was completed by [Werner Ballmann](wikipedia://en/24812368) about 50 years later.

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Littlewood–Richardson rule"}

- [Littlewood–Richardson rule](wikipedia://en/16004359). [Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_de_Beauregard_Robinson) published an incomplete proof in 1938, though the gaps were not noticed for many years. The first complete proofs were given by [Marcel-Paul Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337) in 1977 and Thomas in 1974.

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="Class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields"}

- [Class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_numbers_of_imaginary_quadratic_fields). In 1952 [Heegner](wikipedia://en/621799) published a solution to this problem. His paper was not accepted as a complete proof as it contained a gap, and the first complete proofs were given in about 1967 by [Baker](wikipedia://en/18950800) and [Stark](wikipedia://en/788807). In 1969 Stark showed how to fill the gap in Heegner's paper.

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="Igor Shafarevich"}

- In 1954 [Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815) published a proof that [every finite solvable group is a Galois group over the rationals](wikipedia://en/47258989). However [Schmidt](wikipedia://en/63781826) pointed out a gap in the argument at the [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) 2, which Shafarevich fixed in 1989.

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Nielsen realization problem"}

- [Nielsen realization problem](wikipedia://en/3063191). Kravetz claimed to solve this in 1959 by first showing that [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) is negatively curved, but in 1974 [Masur](wikipedia://en/46960501) showed that it is not negatively curved. The Nielsen realization problem was finally solved in 1980 by [Kerckhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Kerckhoff).

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Yamabe problem"}

- [Yamabe problem](wikipedia://en/11757994). [Yamabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidehiko_Yamabe) claimed a solution in 1960, but [Trudinger](wikipedia://en/15173259) discovered a gap in 1968, and a complete proof was not given until 1984.

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=list-entry name="Mordell conjecture"}

- [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture) over [function fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_function_field). [Manin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_I._Manin) published a proof in 1963, but (Coleman 1990) found and corrected a gap in the proof.

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=list-entry name="Britton"}

- In 1973 [Britton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Britton_%28mathematician%29) published a 282-page attempted solution of [Burnside's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnside's_problem). In his proof he assumed the existence of a set of parameters satisfying some inequalities, but [Adian](wikipedia://en/5028401) pointed out that these inequalities were inconsistent. [Novikov](wikipedia://en/1139033) and Adian had previously found a correct solution around 1968.

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=list-entry name="Classification of finite simple groups"}

- [Classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445). In 1983, [Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/1569478) announced that the proof of the classification had been completed, but he had been misinformed about the status of the proof of classification of [quasithin groups](wikipedia://en/12939181), which had a serious gap in it. A complete proof for this case was published by [Aschbacher](wikipedia://en/1652651) and Smith in 2004.

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=list-entry name="Spencer Bloch"}

- In 1986, [Spencer Bloch](wikipedia://en/25794633) published the paper "Algebraic Cycles and Higher K-theory" which introduced a [higher Chow group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Chow_group), a precursor to [motivic cohomology](wikipedia://en/809314). The paper used an incorrect moving lemma; the lemma was later replaced by 30 pages of complex arguments that "took many years to be accepted as correct."[^o35]

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Kepler conjecture"}

- [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311). Hsiang published an incomplete proof of this in 1993. In 1998 [Hales](wikipedia://en/2388356) published a proof depending on long computer calculations.

:::

## Incorrect results {#Incorrect_results}

::: {#o38 .entry cue=list-entry name="knight tours"}

- In 1759 Euler claimed that there were no closed [knight tours](wikipedia://en/38501) on a chess board with 3 rows, but in 1917 Ernest Bergholt found tours on 3 × 10 and 3 × 12 boards.[^o39]

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=list-entry name="Graeco-Latin square"}

- Euler's conjecture on [Graeco-Latin squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graeco-Latin_square). In the 1780s Euler conjectured that no such squares exist for any oddly even number *n* ≡ 2 (mod 4). In 1959, [R. C. Bose](wikipedia://en/1162266) and [S. S. Shrikhande](wikipedia://en/10371533) constructed [counterexamples](wikipedia://en/143151) of order 22. Then [E. T. Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._T._Parker) found a counterexample of order 10 using a one-hour computer search. Finally Parker, Bose, and Shrikhande showed this conjecture to be false for all *n* ≥ 10.

:::

::: {#o41 .entry cue=list-entry name="A. M. Legendre"}

- In 1798 [A. M. Legendre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._M._Legendre) claimed that 6 is not the sum of 2 rational cubes,[^o42] which as [Lamé](wikipedia://en/52145) pointed out in 1865 is false as 6 = (37/21)<sup>3</sup> + (17/21)<sup>3</sup>.

:::

::: {#o43 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gian Francesco Malfatti"}

- In 1803, [Gian Francesco Malfatti](wikipedia://en/1475987) claimed to prove that a certain arrangement of three circles would cover the maximum possible area inside a right triangle. However, to do so he made certain unwarranted assumptions about the configuration of the circles. It was shown in 1930 that circles in a different configuration could cover a greater area, and in 1967 that Malfatti's configuration was *never* optimal. See [Malfatti circles](wikipedia://en/1475894).

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=list-entry name="André-Marie Ampère"}

- In 1806 [André-Marie Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André-Marie_Ampère) claimed to prove that a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) is [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) at most points (though it is not entirely clear what he was claiming as he did not give a precise definition of a function). However, in 1872 [Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) gave an example of a continuous function that was not differentiable anywhere: The [Weierstrass function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_function).

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=list-entry name="Intersection theory"}

- [Intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602). In 1848 [Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) claimed that [the number of conics tangent to 5 given conics](wikipedia://en/50076509) is 7776 = 6<sup>5</sup>, but later realized this was wrong. The correct number 3264 was found by Berner in 1865 and by [Ernest de Jonquieres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_de_Jonquieres) around 1859 and by [Chasles](wikipedia://en/2252716) in 1864 using his theory of characteristics. However these results, like many others in classical intersection theory, do not seem to have been given complete proofs until the work of [Fulton](wikipedia://en/9642149) and [Macpherson](wikipedia://en/10238121) in about 1978.

:::

::: {#o46 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dirichlet's principle"}

- [Dirichlet's principle](wikipedia://en/1240011). This was used by [Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) in 1851, but Weierstrass found a counterexample to one version of this principle in 1870, and Hilbert stated and proved a correct version in 1900.

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=list-entry name="groups"}

- (txt) incorrectly claimed that there are three different [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) 6. This mistake is strange because in an earlier 1854 paper he correctly stated that there are just two such groups.

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=list-entry name="Evgraf Fedorov"}

- In 1885, [Evgraf Fedorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgraf_Fedorov) classified the [convex polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedra) with congruent rhombic faces, but missed a case. [Stanko Bilinski](wikipedia://en/44812552) in 1960 rediscovered the [Bilinski dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/50652823) (forgotten after its previous 1752 publication) and proved that, with the addition of this shape, the classification was complete.[^o50]

:::

::: {#o51 .entry cue=list-entry name="Wronskian"}

- [Wronskians](wikipedia://en/302846). In 1887 [Mansion](wikipedia://en/55811631) claimed in his textbook that if a Wronskian of some functions vanishes everywhere then the functions are linearly dependent. In 1889 Peano pointed out the counterexample *x*<sup>2</sup> and *x*|*x*|. The result is correct if the functions are [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478).

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=list-entry name="Algebraic curve"}

- (txt) published a purported example of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) in 3-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) that could not be defined as the [zeros](wikipedia://en/264210) of 3 polynomials, but in 1941 [Perron](wikipedia://en/1485469) found 3 equations defining Vahlen's curve. In 1961 [Kneser](wikipedia://en/17620650) showed that any algebraic curve in projective 3-space can be given as the zeros of 3 polynomials.[^o54]

:::

::: {#o55 .entry cue=list-entry name="Miller"}

- In 1898 [Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Abram_Miller) published a paper incorrectly claiming to prove that the [Mathieu group](wikipedia://en/380207) M<sub>24</sub> does not exist, though in 1900 he pointed out that his proof was wrong.

:::

::: {#o56 .entry cue=list-entry name="Little"}

- [Little](wikipedia://en/48704630) claimed in 1900 that the [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591) of a reduced knot diagram is an invariant. However, in 1974 Perko discovered a counterexample called the [Perko pair](wikipedia://en/16618607), a pair of knots listed as distinct in tables for many years that are in fact the same.

:::

::: {#o57 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hilbert's twenty-first problem"}

- [Hilbert's twenty-first problem](wikipedia://en/1965023). In 1908 [Plemelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Plemelj) claimed to have shown the existence of [Fuchsian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuchsian_differential_equation) [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) with any given [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) group, but in 1989 [Bolibruch](wikipedia://en/23820717) discovered a counterexample.

:::

::: {#o58 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ackermann"}

- In 1925 [Ackermann](wikipedia://en/62727) published a proof that a weak system can prove the consistency of a version of analysis, but [von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) found an explicit mistake in it a few years later. [Gödel's incompleteness theorems](wikipedia://en/58863) showed that it is not possible to prove the consistency of analysis using weaker systems.

:::

::: {#o59 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hall"}

- Groups of order 64. In 1930 Miller published a paper claiming that there are 294 groups of order 64. [Hall](wikipedia://en/7758584) and Senior showed in 1964 that the correct number is 267.

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=list-entry name="Kurt Gödel"}

- [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) proved in 1933 that the truth of a certain class of sentences of [first-order arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_arithmetic), known in the literature as \[∃<sup>\*</sup>∀<sup>2</sup>∃<sup>\*</sup>, *all*, (0)\], was [decidable](wikipedia://en/913118). That is, there was a method for deciding correctly whether any statement of that form was true. In the final sentence of that paper, he asserted that the same proof would work for the decidability of the larger class \[∃<sup>\*</sup>∀<sup>2</sup>∃<sup>\*</sup>, *all*, (0)\]<sub>\=</sub>, which also includes formulas that contain an equality predicate. However, in the mid-1960s, [Stål Aanderaa](wikipedia://en/24884314) showed that Gödel's proof would *not* go through for the larger class, and in 1982 [Warren Goldfarb](wikipedia://en/15629024) showed that validity of formulas from the larger class was in fact undecidable.[^o61][^o62]

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=list-entry name="Grunwald–Wang theorem"}

- [Grunwald–Wang theorem](wikipedia://en/20901695). [Wilhelm Grunwald](wikipedia://en/33280886) published an incorrect proof in 1933 of an incorrect theorem, and George Whaples later published another incorrect proof. [Shianghao Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shianghao_Wang) found a counterexample in 1948 and published a corrected version of the theorem in 1950.

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=list-entry name="Severi"}

- In 1934 [Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) claimed that the [space of rational equivalence classes of cycles](wikipedia://en/4282051) on an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) is finite-dimensional, but (Mumford 1968) showed that this is false for surfaces of positive geometric genus.

:::

::: {#o66 .entry cue=list-entry name="Jacobian conjecture"}

- [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823). [Keller](wikipedia://en/1193984) asked this as a question in 1939, and in the next few years there were several published incomplete proofs, including 3 by B. Segre, but [Vitushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoli_Georgievich_Vitushkin) found gaps in many of them. More incomplete proofs were later announced. (txt) discuss the errors in some of these incomplete proofs. In 2026, [Levent Alpöge](wikipedia://en/83763682) found a counterexample for all n > 2.

:::

::: {#o68 .entry cue=list-entry name="B. Segre"}

- One of many examples from algebraic geometry in the first half of the 20th century: (Severi 1946) claimed that a degree-*n* surface in 3-dimensional projective space has at most (${}^{n+2}_{3}$)−4 nodes, [B. Segre](wikipedia://en/3534031) pointed out that this was wrong; for example, for degree 6 the maximum number of nodes is 65, achieved by the [Barth sextic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barth_sextic), which is more than the maximum of 52 claimed by Severi.

:::

::: {#o70 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rokhlin invariant"}

- [Rokhlin invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokhlin_invariant). [Rokhlin](wikipedia://en/7703611) incorrectly claimed in 1951 that the third stable stem of the [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697) is of order 12. In 1952 he discovered his error: it is in fact cyclic of order 24. The difference is crucial as it results in the existence of the Rokhlin invariant, a fundamental tool in the theory of 3- and 4-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).

:::

::: {#o71 .entry cue=list-entry name="Derived functor"}

- In 1961, Jan-Erik Roos published an incorrect theorem about the vanishing of the first [derived functor](wikipedia://en/433875) of the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) under certain general conditions.[^o72] However, in 2002, [Amnon Neeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnon_Neeman) constructed a counterexample.[^o73] Roos showed in 2006 that the theorem holds if one adds the assumption that the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) has a set of [generators](wikipedia://en/3171371).[^o74]

:::

::: {#o75 .entry cue=list-entry name="Schur multiplier"}

- The [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) of the [Mathieu group M<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/15200699) is particularly notorious as it was miscalculated more than once: (Burgoyne Fong 1966) first claimed it had order 3, then in a 1968 correction claimed it had order 6; its order is in fact (currently believed to be) 12. This caused an error in the title of [Janko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvonimir_Janko)'s paper *A new finite simple group of order 86,775,570,046,077,562,880 which possesses M<sub>24</sub> and the full covering group of M<sub>22</sub> as subgroup* on [J4](wikipedia://en/10796995): it does not have the full covering group as a subgroup, as the full covering group is larger than was realized at the time.

:::

::: {#o77 .entry cue=list-entry name="N-groups"}

- The original statement of the classification of [N-groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-groups) by [Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189) in 1968 accidentally omitted the [Tits group](wikipedia://en/606719), though he soon fixed this.

:::

::: {#o78 .entry cue=list-entry name="Finite field"}

- In 1975, Leitzel, Madan, and Queen incorrectly claimed that there are only 7 function fields over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) with genus > 0 and class number 1, but in 2013 Stirpe found another; there are in fact exactly 8.

:::

::: {#o79 .entry cue=list-entry name="Busemann–Petty problem"}

- [Busemann–Petty problem](wikipedia://en/33294058). Zhang published two papers in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* in 1994 and 1999, in the first of which he proved that the Busemann–Petty problem in **R**<sup>4</sup> has a negative solution, and in the second of which he proved that it has a positive solution.

:::

::: {#o80 .entry cue=list-entry name="Algebraic stack"}

- [Algebraic stacks](wikipedia://en/717377). The book (Laumon Moret-Bailly 2000) on algebraic stacks mistakenly claimed that [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) of algebraic stacks induce morphisms of lisse-étale [topoi](wikipedia://en/21391464). The results depending on this were repaired by (Olsson 2007).

:::

## Theories proven inconsistent {#Theories_proven_inconsistent}

::: {#o84 .entry cue=list-entry name="Frege"}

- [Frege](wikipedia://en/48416)'s [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) in his 1879 book *[Begriffsschrift](wikipedia://en/900125)* turned out to be inconsistent because of [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095), found in 1901.

:::

::: {#o85 .entry cue=list-entry name="Church"}

- [Church](wikipedia://en/49731)'s original published attempt in 1932 to define a formal system was inconsistent, as was his correction in 1933. The consistent part of his system later became the [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203).

:::

::: {#o86 .entry cue=list-entry name="Quine"}

- [Quine](wikipedia://en/33833) published his original description of the system [Mathematical Logic](wikipedia://en/945957) in 1940, but in 1942 [Rosser](wikipedia://en/679224) showed it was inconsistent. [Wang](wikipedia://en/1056047) found a correction in 1950; the consistency of this revised system is still unclear.

:::

::: {#o87 .entry cue=list-entry name="Reinhardt cardinal"}

- In 1967 Reinhardt proposed [Reinhardt cardinals](wikipedia://en/4687321), which [Kunen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Kunen) showed to be inconsistent with ZFC in 1971, though they are not known to be inconsistent with [ZF](wikipedia://en/152214).

:::

::: {#o88 .entry cue=list-entry name="Per Martin-Löf"}

- [Per Martin-Löf](wikipedia://en/1925764)'s original version of [intuitionistic type theory](wikipedia://en/345023) proposed in 1971 was shown to be inconsistent by [Jean-Yves Girard](wikipedia://en/579673) in 1972, and was replaced by a corrected version.

:::

## Status unclear {#Status_unclear}

::: {#o90 .entry cue=list-entry name="Uniform convergence"}

- [Uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652). In his *[Cours d'Analyse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cours_d'Analyse)* of 1821, [Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Cauchy) "proved" that if a sum of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) [converges pointwise](wikipedia://en/340198), then its limit is also continuous. However, [Abel](wikipedia://en/21573) observed in 1826 that this is not the case. For the conclusion to hold, "pointwise convergence" must be replaced with "[uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652)". It is not entirely clear that Cauchy's original result was wrong, because his definition of pointwise convergence was a little vague and may have been stronger than the one currently in use, and there are ways to interpret his result so that it is correct.[^o91] There are many counterexamples using the standard definition of pointwise convergence. For example, a [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) of [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) and [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine) functions, all continuous, may converge pointwise to a discontinuous function such as a [step function](wikipedia://en/170939).

:::

::: {#o92 .entry cue=list-entry name="Carmichael's totient function conjecture"}

- [Carmichael's totient function conjecture](wikipedia://en/19103379) was stated as a theorem by [Robert Daniel Carmichael](wikipedia://en/605140) in 1907, but in 1922 he pointed out that his proof was incomplete. As of 2016 the problem is still open.

:::

::: {#o93 .entry cue=list-entry name="Italian school of algebraic geometry"}

- [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424). Most gaps in proofs are caused either by a subtle technical oversight, or before the 20th century by a lack of precise definitions. A major exception to this is the Italian school of algebraic geometry in the first half of the 20th century, where lower standards of rigor gradually became acceptable. The result was that there are many papers in this area where the proofs are incomplete, or the theorems are not stated precisely. This list contains a few representative examples, where the result was not just incompletely proved but also hopelessly wrong.

:::

::: {#o94 .entry cue=list-entry name="George David Birkhoff"}

- In 1933 [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906) and Waldemar Joseph Trjitzinsky published a very general theorem[^o95] on the asymptotics of sequences satisfying linear recurrences. The theorem was popularized by Jet Wimp and [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) in 1985.[^o96] However, while the result is probably true, as of now (2021) Birkhoff and Trjitzinsky's proof is not generally accepted by experts, and the theorem is (acceptedly) proved only in special cases.[^o97]

:::

::: {#o98 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hilbert's sixteenth problem"}

- A strengthening of [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750) asking whether there exists a uniform finite upper bound for the number of limit cycles of planar polynomial vector fields of given degree *n*. In the 1950s, [Evgenii Landis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgenii_Landis) and [Ivan Petrovsky](wikipedia://en/4598657) published a purported solution, but it was shown wrong in the early 1960s.[^o23]

:::

::: {#o99 .entry cue=list-entry name="Zarankiewicz"}

- In 1954 [Zarankiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Zarankiewicz) claimed to have solved [Turán's brick factory problem](wikipedia://en/22039971) about the crossing number of [complete bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/525320), but [Kainen](wikipedia://en/22674877) and [Ringel](wikipedia://en/1571968) later noticed a gap in his proof.

:::

::: {#o100 .entry cue=list-entry name="American Journal of Mathematics"}

- Complex structures on the 6-sphere. In 1969 Alfred Adler published a paper in the [American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics) claiming that the 6-sphere has no complex structure. His argument was incomplete, and this is (as of 2016) still a major open problem.

:::

::: {#o101 .entry cue=list-entry name="Closed geodesic"}

- [Closed geodesics](wikipedia://en/19903176). In 1978 [Wilhelm Klingenberg](wikipedia://en/33387331) published a proof that smooth [compact manifolds without boundary](wikipedia://en/669475) have infinitely many closed geodesics. His proof was controversial, and there is currently (as of 2016) no consensus on whether his proof is complete.

:::

::: {#o102 .entry cue=list-entry name="Homotopy hypothesis"}

- In 1991, Kapranov and Voevodsky published a paper claiming to prove a version of the [homotopy hypothesis](wikipedia://en/40965653). Later, Simpson showed the result of the paper is not true but conjectured that a variant of the result might be true, the variant now known as the [Simpson conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson_conjecture).

:::

::: {#o103 .entry cue=list-entry name="Telescope conjecture"}

- [Telescope conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope_conjecture). [Ravenel](wikipedia://en/27877012) announced a refutation of this in 1992, but later withdrew it, and the conjecture is still open.

:::

::: {#o104 .entry cue=list-entry name="Daniel Biss"}

- Matroid bundles. In 2003 [Daniel Biss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Biss) published a paper in the Annals of Mathematics claiming to show that matroid bundles are equivalent to real vector bundles, but in 2009 published a correction pointing out a serious gap in the proof.[^o105] His correction was based on a 2007 paper by Mnëv.[^o106]

:::

::: {#o107 .entry cue=list-entry name="Shinichi Mochizuki"}

- In 2012, the Japanese mathematician [Shinichi Mochizuki](wikipedia://en/36940578) released online a series of papers in which he claims to prove the [abc conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108). Despite the publication in a peer-reviewed journal later, his proof has not been accepted as correct in the mainstream mathematical community.[^o108][^o109]

:::
