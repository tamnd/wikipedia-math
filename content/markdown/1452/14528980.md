# Structural break

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Structural break"}

*Image: Chowtest2.svg.* [Linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) with a structural break

In [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **structural break** is an unexpected change over time in the [parameters](wikipedia://en/413291) of [regression models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_model), which can lead to huge [forecasting](wikipedia://en/246074) errors and unreliability of the model in general.[^o2][^o3][^o4] This issue was popularised by [David Hendry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hendry), who argued that lack of stability of coefficients frequently caused forecast failure, and therefore we must routinely test for structural stability. Structural stability − i.e., the time-invariance of regression coefficients − is a central issue in all applications of [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) models.[^o5]

:::

## Structural break tests {#Structural_break_tests}

### A single break in mean with a known breakpoint {#A_single_break_in_mean_with_a_known_breakpoint}

For [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) models, the [Chow test](wikipedia://en/2883675) is often used to test for a single break in mean at a known time period $K$ for $K \in [1,T]$.[^o11][^o12] This test assesses whether the coefficients in a regression model are the same for periods $[1,2, ...,K]$ and $[K + 1, ...,T]$.[^o12]

### Other forms of structural breaks {#Other_forms_of_structural_breaks}

Other challenges occur where there are:

Case 1: a known number of breaks in mean with unknown break points;

Case 2: an unknown number of breaks in mean with unknown break points;

Case 3: breaks in variance.

The [Chow test](wikipedia://en/2883675) is not applicable in these situations, since it only applies to models with a known breakpoint and where the error variance remains constant before and after the break.[^o10][^o11][^o12] Bayesian methods exist to address these difficult cases via [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) inference.[^o13][^o14]

In general, the [CUSUM](wikipedia://en/15191637) (cumulative sum) and CUSUM-sq (CUSUM squared) tests can be used to test the constancy of the coefficients in a model. The bounds test can also be used.[^o12][^o15] For cases 1 and 2, the **sup-Wald** (i.e., the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of a set of [Wald statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wald_statistics)), **sup-LM** (i.e., the supremum of a set of [Lagrange multiplier statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_multiplier_statistics)), and **sup-LR** (i.e., the supremum of a set of [likelihood ratio statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likelihood_ratio_statistics)) tests developed by [Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Andrews) (1993, 2003) may be used to test for parameter instability when the number and location of structural breaks are unknown.[^o16][^o17] These tests were shown to be superior to the CUSUM test in terms of [statistical power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power),[^o16] and are the most commonly used tests for the detection of structural change involving an unknown number of breaks in mean with unknown break points.[^o5] The sup-Wald, sup-LM, and sup-LR tests are [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/26024794) in general (i.e., the asymptotic [critical values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing#Definition_of_terms) for these tests are applicable for sample size $n$ as $n \to \infty$),[^o16] and involve the assumption of [homoskedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoskedasticity) across break points for finite samples;[^o5] however, an [exact test](wikipedia://en/2485378) with the sup-Wald statistic may be obtained for a linear regression model with a fixed number of regressors and [independent and identically distributed (IID)](wikipedia://en/30876902) [normal](wikipedia://en/21462) errors.[^o16] A method developed by Bai and Perron (2003) also allows for the detection of multiple structural breaks from data.[^o18]

The **MZ test** developed by Maasoumi, Zaman, and Ahmed (2010) allows for the simultaneous detection of one or more breaks in both mean and variance at a *known* break point.[^o5][^o19] The **sup-MZ test** developed by Ahmed, Haider, and Zaman (2016) is a generalization of the MZ test which allows for the detection of breaks in mean and variance at an *unknown* break point.[^o5]

### Structural breaks in cointegration models {#Structural_breaks_in_cointegration_models}

For a [cointegration](wikipedia://en/1822603) model, the Gregory–Hansen test (1996) can be used for one unknown structural break,[^o21] the Hatemi–J test (2006) can be used for two unknown breaks[^o22] and the Maki (2012) test allows for multiple structural breaks.

## Statistical packages {#Statistical_packages}

There are many [statistical packages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_packages) that can be used to find structural breaks, including [R](wikipedia://en/376707),[^o24] [GAUSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAUSS), and [Stata](wikipedia://en/1809002), among others. For example, a list of R packages for time series data is summarized at the changepoint detection section of the Time Series Analysis Task View,[^o25] including both classical and Bayesian methods.[^o26][^o14]
