# Anders C. Hansen

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Anders C. Hansen"}

**Anders C. Hansen** is a Norwegian mathematician, who is currently a Professor of Mathematics at [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he is the head of the Applied Functional and Harmonic Analysis group, and also Professor II at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo).[^o5] He works in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) (applied), [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) (computational), [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) .[^o6]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Hansen studied mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and the [Norwegian University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology), where he was awarded a PhD (2008), a MA (2005) and a BA (2002) respectively.[^o8]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

He was a [von Kármán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Kármán) instructor at [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) from 2008 to 2009, held a junior [research fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_fellow) at [Homerton College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homerton_College,_Cambridge) from 2009 to 2012, and held a [Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Skłodowska-Curie_Actions) fellowship at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) in 2012. Since 2012, he has held a [Royal Society University Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_University_Research_Fellowship) (URF)[^o3] at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he is now a professor at the [Faculty of Mathematics, University of Cambridge](wikipedia://en/3254271) and a Bye-Fellow of [Peterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse).[^o10]

Among other results, he has established the Solvability Complexity Index (SCI) and its following classification hierarchy.[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15] It is linked [^o12] to [Steve Smale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Smale)'s question [^o16] on the existence of iterative convergent algorithms for polynomial root finding answered by [Curt McMullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curt_McMullen)[^o17][^o18] and [Peter Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_G._Doyle),[^o18] as well as [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208)'s work and the [Arithmetical hierarchy](wikipedia://en/186475).

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 2017, he was awarded the [Leverhulme Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverhulme_Prize) for having "solved very hard problems and opened new directions in areas of great impact in applied analysis \[...\] Notably, by introducing the Solvability Complexity Index he has made a major contribution to the advancement of Smale's programme on the foundation of computational mathematics".[^o20][^o21] In 2018, he was awarded the [IMA Prize in Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/10324088#Prize_in_Mathematics_and_its_Applications)[^o22] for having "made a transformative impact on the mathematical sciences and their applications \[...\] in particular, for his development of the Solvability Complexity Index and its corresponding classification hierarchy".[^o23] In 2019, he was awarded the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) for having "contributed fundamentally to the mathematics of data, sampling theory, computational harmonic analysis and compressed sensing" and "especially his development of the Solvability Complexity Index and its corresponding classification hierarchy ".[^o24]
