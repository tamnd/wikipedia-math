# Four factor formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Four factor formula"}

The **four-factor formula**, also known as **Fermi's four factor formula**, is used in [nuclear engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_engineering) to determine the multiplication of a [nuclear chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction) in an infinite medium.

:::

*Four-factor formula: $k_{\infty} = \eta f p \varepsilon$[^o3]*

::: {#o2 .table cue=table name="Four-factor formula: k_{\\infty} = \\eta f p \\varepsilon"}

| Symbol | Name | Meaning | Formula | Typical thermal reactor value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\eta$ | Reproduction factor (eta) | $\tfrac{\operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{produced}\text{ from } \operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{fissions}}{\operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{absorption}\text{ in } \operatorname{fuel} \operatorname{isotope}}$[^o4] | $\eta = \frac{\nu \sigma_f^F}{\sigma_a^F} = \frac{\nu \Sigma_f^F}{\Sigma_a^F}$ | \1.65 |
| $f$ | Thermal utilization factor | $\tfrac{\operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{absorbed}\text{ by }\text{ the } \operatorname{fuel} \operatorname{isotope}}{\operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{absorbed} \operatorname{anywhere}}$[^o4] | $f = \frac{\Sigma_a^F}{\Sigma_a}$ | \0.71 |
| $p$ | [Resonance escape probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonance_escape_probability) | $\tfrac{\operatorname{fission} \operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{slowed}\text{ to } \operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{energies} \operatorname{without} \operatorname{absorption}}{\operatorname{total} \operatorname{fission} \operatorname{neutrons}}$ | $p \approx \exp \left( -\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N} N_i I_{r,A,i}}{\left( \overline{\xi} \Sigma_p \right)_{mod}} \right)$ | \0.87 |
| $\varepsilon$ | Fast fission factor | $\tfrac{\operatorname{total} \operatorname{number}\text{ of } \operatorname{fission} \operatorname{neutrons}}{\operatorname{number}\text{ of } \operatorname{fission} \operatorname{neutrons}\text{ from } \operatorname{just} \operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{fissions}}$ | $\varepsilon \approx 1 + \frac{1-p}{p}\frac{u_f \nu_f P_{FAF}}{f \nu_t P_{TAF} P_{TNL}}$ | \1.02 |

:::

The symbols are defined as:[^o5]

- $\nu$, $\nu_f$ and $\nu_t$ are the average number of neutrons produced per fission in the medium (2.43 for [uranium-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium-235)).
- $\sigma_f^F$ and $\sigma_a^F$ are the microscopic fission and absorption thermal cross sections for fuel, respectively.
- $\Sigma_a^F$ and $\Sigma_a$ are the macroscopic absorption thermal cross sections in fuel and in total, respectively.
- $\Sigma_f^F$ is the macroscopic fission cross-section.
- $N_i$ is the [number density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_density) of atoms of a specific [nuclide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclide).
- $I_{r,A,i}$ is the resonance integral for absorption of a specific [nuclide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclide).
  - $$I_{r,A,i} = \int_{E_{th}}^{E_0} dE' \frac{\Sigma_p^{mod}}{\Sigma_t(E')} \frac{\sigma_a^i(E')}{E'}$$
- $\overline{\xi}$ is the average lethargy gain per scattering event.
  - Lethargy is defined as decrease in neutron energy.
- $u_f$ (fast utilization) is the probability that a fast neutron is absorbed in fuel.
- $P_{FAF}$ is the probability that a fast neutron absorption in fuel causes fission.
- $P_{TAF}$ is the probability that a [thermal neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_temperature) absorption in fuel causes fission.
- $P_{TNL}$ is the thermal non-leakage probability

## Multiplication {#Multiplication}

The multiplication factor, $k$, is defined as (see [Nuclear chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction)):

$$k = \frac{\mbox{neutron population following nth generation}}{\mbox{neutron population during nth generation}}$$

- If $k$ is greater than 1, the chain reaction is *supercritical,* and the neutron population will grow exponentially.
- If $k$ is less than 1, the chain reaction is *subcritical,* and the neutron population will exponentially decay.
- If $k = 1$, the chain reaction is *critical* and the neutron population will remain constant.

In an infinite medium, neutrons cannot leak out of the system and the multiplication factor becomes the infinite multiplication factor, $k = k_{\infty}$, which is approximated by the four-factor formula.
