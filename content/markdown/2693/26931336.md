# Cathleen Synge Morawetz Prize

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cathleen Synge Morawetz Prize"}

The **AMS–SIAM Cathleen Synge Morawetz Prize in Applied Mathematics** is awarded jointly by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) and the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) in honor of [Cathleen Synge Morawetz](wikipedia://en/1254108).[^o2][^o3] Prior to 2025, it was known as the AMS–SIAM George David Birkhoff Prize in Applied Mathematics, in honor of [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906).[^o2] It is currently awarded every three years for an outstanding contribution to "applied mathematics in the highest and broadest sense".[^o2] The recipient of the prize has to be a member of one of the awarding societies, as well as a resident of the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada), or [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico). The prize was endowed in 1967 and first issued in 1968. As of 2026, it amounts to [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US$)5,000.[^o2]

:::

| 1988 | [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb) | "for his profound analysis of problems arising in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416)."[^o6] |

R. S. Varadhan for important contributions to the martingale characterization of diffusion processes, to the theory of large deviations for functionals of occupation times of [Markov processes](wikipedia://en/60876), and to the study of random media."[^o6] |
| 1998 | [Paul Rabinowitz](wikipedia://en/22335165) | "for his deep influence on the field of [nonlinear analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_analysis)."[^o7] |

| 2006 | [Cathleen Synge Morawetz](wikipedia://en/1254108) | "for her deep and influential work in partial differential equations, most notably in the study of [shock waves](wikipedia://en/146253), transonic flow, scattering theory, and conformally invariant estimates for the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691)."[^o9] |
