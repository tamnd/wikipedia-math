# Nathaniel Torporley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nathaniel Torporley"}

**Nathaniel Torporley** (1564–1632) was an English [clergyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergyman), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and [astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrologer).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire), and was admitted to [Shrewsbury free grammar school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_School) as an 'oppidan' in 1571. He matriculated at [Christ Church, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford), 17 November 1581, as a 'plebeian,' and graduated B.A. on 5 February 1584; and proceeded M.A. from [Brasenose College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasenose_College) on 8 July 1591. Entering holy orders, he was appointed rector of [Salwarpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salwarpe) in [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire) on 14 June 1608, a living he held until 1622. He also occurs as rector of [Liddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liddington), [Wiltshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiltshire), in 1611, though he seems to have resided chiefly at [Sion College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sion_College), London.

Torporley associated with [Thomas Harriot](wikipedia://en/780445)[^o3] and acquired a knowledge of mathematics and astronomy. He was supported by [Henry Percy, 9th Earl of Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Percy,_9th_Earl_of_Northumberland), who for several years gave him an annual pension. On 27 November 1605, just after the discovery of the [Gunpowder Plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_Plot), Torporley was examined by the council for having cast the king's nativity. For two or more years he resided in France, and was [amanuensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanuensis) to [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238) of [Fontenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontenay-le-Comte), against whom he published a pamphlet under the name of Poulterey. He died in Sion College, London, and was buried in [St. Alphege's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Alphege's_Church) on 17 April 1632.

## Works {#Works}

He published *Diclides Coelometricae; seu Valuae Astronomicae universales, omnia artis totius munera Psephophoretica in sat modicis Finibus Duarum Tabularum methodo Nova, generali et facillima continentes*, London, 1602. With this was presented a preface, entitled *Directionis accuratae consummata Doctrina, Astrologis hactenus plurimum desiderata*; and *Tabula praemissilis ad Declinationes et coeli meditationes*, in five parts.

He left a [nuncupative will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuncupative_will), dated 14 April 1632, by which he bequeathed to the library of Sion College all his mathematical books, astronomical instruments, notes, maps, and a brass clock. Among these books were some manuscripts: *Congestor: Opus Mathematicum*, *Philosophia*, *Atomorum Atopia demonstrata*, *Corrector Analyticus Artis posthunc*.[^o5] Administration of the will was granted on 6 January 1633 to his sister, Susanna Tasker.

He was executor of the papers of Harriot, his teacher. He worked over them, but the work that eventually appeared, the *Artis Analyticae Praxis* (1631), was edited together by [Walter Warner](wikipedia://en/21433743).[^o6]
