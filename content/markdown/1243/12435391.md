# Residuated Boolean algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Residuated Boolean algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **residuated Boolean algebra** is a [residuated lattice](wikipedia://en/2483542) whose lattice structure is that of a [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959). Examples include Boolean algebras with the [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) taken to be conjunction, the set of all formal languages over a given alphabet $\Sigma$ under concatenation, the set of all [binary relations](wikipedia://en/3931) on a given set $X$ under relational composition, and more generally the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of any [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259), again under relational composition. The original application was to [relation algebras](wikipedia://en/4363670) as a finitely axiomatized generalization of the binary relation example, but there exist interesting examples of residuated Boolean algebras that are not relation algebras, such as the language example.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **residuated Boolean algebra** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $(L,\wedge,\vee,\neg,0,1,\bullet,\mathbf I,/,\backslash)$ such that $(L,\wedge,\vee,\bullet,\mathbf I,/,\backslash)$ is a [residuated lattice](wikipedia://en/2483542), and; $(L,\wedge,\vee,\neg,0,1)$ is a Boolean algebra.

An equivalent signature better suited to the [relation algebra](wikipedia://en/4363670) application is $(L,\wedge,\vee,\neg,0,1,\bullet,\mathbf I,\triangleright,\triangleleft)$ where the unary operations $x\backslash$ and $x\triangleright$ are intertranslatable in the manner of [De Morgan's laws](wikipedia://en/64669) via

$x\backslash y=\neg(x\triangleright\neg y)$, $x\triangleright y=\neg(x\backslash\neg y)$,

and dually $/y$ and $\triangleleft y$ as

$x/y=\neg(\neg x\triangleleft y)$, $x\triangleleft y=\neg(\neg x/y)$,

with the residuation axioms in the [residuated lattice](wikipedia://en/2483542) article reorganized accordingly (replacing $z$ by $\neg z$) to read

$$(x\triangleright z)\wedge y=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ (x\bullet y)\wedge z=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ (z\triangleleft y)\wedge x=0$$

This [De Morgan dual](wikipedia://en/64669) reformulation is motivated and discussed in more detail in the section below on conjugacy.

Since residuated lattices and Boolean algebras are each definable with finitely many equations, so are residuated Boolean algebras, whence they form a finitely axiomatizable [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

1. Any Boolean algebra, with the monoid multiplication $\bullet$ taken to be conjunction and both residuals taken to be [material implication](wikipedia://en/658808) $x\to y$. Of the remaining 15 binary Boolean operations that might be considered in place of conjunction for the monoid multiplication, only five meet the monotonicity requirement, namely $0,1,x,y$ and $x\vee y$. Setting $y=z=0$ in the residuation axiom $y\le x\backslash z\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\bullet y\le z$, we have $0\le x\backslash 0\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\bullet 0\le 0$, which is falsified by taking $x=1$ when $x\bullet y=1$, $x$, or $x\vee y$. The dual argument for $z/y$ rules out $x\bullet y=y$. This just leaves $x\bullet y=0$ (a constant [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) independent of $x$ and $y$), which satisfies almost all the axioms when the residuals are both taken to be the constant operation $x/y=x\backslash y=1$. The axiom it fails is $x\bullet\mathbf I=x=\mathbf I\bullet x$, for want of a suitable value for $\mathbf I$. Hence conjunction is the only binary Boolean operation making the monoid multiplication that of a residuated Boolean algebra.
1. The power set $2^{X^2}$ made a Boolean algebra as usual with $\cap$, $\cup$ and complement relative to $X^2$, and made a monoid with relational composition. The monoid unit $\mathbf I$ is the identity relation $\{(x,x)|x\in X\}$. The right residual $R\backslash S$ is defined by $x(R\backslash S)y\ \Leftrightarrow\ \forall z\in X,zRx\Rightarrow zSy$. Dually the left residual $S/R$ is defined by $y(S/R)x\ \Leftrightarrow\ \forall z\in X,xRz\Rightarrow ySz$.
1. The power set $2^{\Sigma^*}$ made a Boolean algebra as for Example 2, but with language concatenation for the monoid. Here the set $\Sigma$ is used as an alphabet while $\Sigma^*$ denotes the set of all finite (including empty) words over that alphabet. The concatenation $LM$ of languages $L$ and $M$ consists of all words $uv$ such that $u\in L$ and $v\in M$. The monoid unit is the language $\{\varepsilon\}$ consisting of just the empty word $\varepsilon$. The right residual $M\backslash L$ consists of all words $w$ over $\Sigma$ such that $Mw\subseteq L$. The left residual $L/M$ is the same with $wM$ in place of $Mw$.

:::

## Conjugacy {#Conjugacy}

The De Morgan duals $\triangleright$ and $\triangleleft$ of residuation arise as follows. Among residuated lattices, Boolean algebras are special by virtue of having a complementation operation $\neg$. This permits an alternative expression of the three inequalities

$$y\le x\backslash z\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\bullet y\le z\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\le z/y$$

in the axiomatization of the two residuals in terms of disjointness, via the equivalence $x\le y\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\wedge\neg y=0$. Abbreviating $x\wedge y=0$ to $x\# y$ as the expression of their disjointness, and substituting $\neg z$ for $z$ in the axioms, they become with a little Boolean manipulation

$$\neg(x\backslash\neg z)\#y\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\bullet y\#z\ \Leftrightarrow\ \neg(\neg z/y)\# x$$

Now $\neg(x\backslash\neg z)$ is reminiscent of [De Morgan duality](wikipedia://en/64669), suggesting that $x\backslash$ be thought of as a [unary operation](wikipedia://en/161006) $f$, defined by $f(y)=x\backslash y$, that has a De Morgan dual $\neg f(\neg y)$, analogous to $\forall x\phi(x)=\neg\exists x\neg\phi(x)$. Denoting this dual operation as $x\triangleright$, we define $x\triangleright z$ as $\neg x\backslash\neg z$. Similarly we define another operation $z\triangleleft y$ as $\neg(\neg z/y)$. By analogy with $x\backslash$ as the residual operation associated with the operation $x\bullet$, we refer to $x\triangleright$ as the conjugate operation, or simply *conjugate*, of $x\bullet$. Likewise $\triangleleft y$ is the *conjugate* of $\bullet y$. Unlike residuals, conjugacy is an equivalence relation between operations: if $f$ is the conjugate of $g$ then $g$ is also the conjugate of $f$, i.e. the conjugate of the conjugate of $f$ is $f$. Another advantage of conjugacy is that it becomes unnecessary to speak of right and left conjugates, that distinction now being inherited from the difference between $x\bullet$ and $\bullet x$, which have as their respective conjugates $x\triangleright$ and $\triangleleft x$. (But this advantage accrues also to residuals when $x\backslash$ is taken to be the residual operation to $x\bullet$.)

All this yields (along with the Boolean algebra and monoid axioms) the following equivalent axiomatization of a residuated Boolean algebra.

$$y\#x\triangleright z\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\bullet y\#z\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\#z\triangleleft y$$

With this signature it remains the case that this axiomatization can be expressed as finitely many equations.

## Converse {#Converse}

In Examples 2 and 3 it can be shown that $x\triangleright\mathbf I=\mathbf I\triangleleft x$. In Example 2 both sides equal the [converse](wikipedia://en/3941020) $x\breve{}$ of $x$, while in Example 3, both sides are $\mathbf I$ when $x$ contains the empty word and $0$ otherwise. In the former case $x\breve{}=x$. This is impossible for the latter because $x\triangleright\mathbf I$ retains hardly any information about $x$. Hence in Example 2 we can substitute $x\breve{}$ for $x$ in $x\triangleright\mathbf I=x\breve{}=\mathbf I\triangleleft x$ and cancel (soundly) to give

$$x\breve{}\triangleright\mathbf I=x=\mathbf I\triangleleft x\breve{}.$$

$x\breve{}\breve{}=x$ can be proved from these two equations. [Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920)'s notion of a **[relation algebra](wikipedia://en/4363670)** can be defined as a residuated Boolean algebra having an operation $x\breve{}$ satisfying these two equations.

The cancellation step in the above is not possible for Example 3, which therefore is not a relation algebra, $x\breve{}$ being uniquely determined as $x\triangleright\mathbf I$.

Consequences of this axiomatization of converse include $x\breve{}\breve{}=x$, $\neg(x\breve{})=(\neg x)\breve{}$, $(x\vee y)\breve{}=x\breve{}\vee y\breve{}$, and $(x\bullet y)\breve{}=y\breve{}\bullet x\breve{}$.

:::
