# Walter Ledermann

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Ledermann"}

**Walter Ledermann** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (18 March 1911, Berlin, Germany – 22 May 2009, London, England) was a German and British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on [matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_theory), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), and [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes). He was elected to the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1944.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Ledermann studied at the Köllnisches Gymnasium and Leibniz Gymnasium in Berlin, from which he graduated in 1928 at the age of 17.[^o6] He went on to study at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin), but due to the rise of [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) and [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism), was forced to flee Germany shortly after he completed his undergraduate studies in 1934.[^o6] Through the [International Student Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_University_Service) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), he was able to obtain a scholarship to study at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews) in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland). His doctoral work at St Andrews was supervised by [Herbert Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Turnbull).[^o2] He was awarded his PhD in 1936. Whilst working at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) with Professor Sir [Godfrey Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_Thomson), Ledermann was granted a DSc in 1940 for his work with Thomson on intelligence testing.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

## Career {#Career}

He taught at the universities of [Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dundee), [St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester), and finally [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex). At Sussex, Ledermann was appointed professor in 1965, where he continued to teach until he was 89.[^o10] He wrote various mathematics textbooks.
