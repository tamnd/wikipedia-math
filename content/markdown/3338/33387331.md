# Wilhelm Klingenberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelm Klingenberg"}

**Wilhelm Paul Albert Klingenberg** (28 January 1924 – 14 October 2010) was a German mathematician who worked on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and in particular on [closed geodesics](wikipedia://en/19903176).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Klingenberg was born in 1924 as the son of a Protestant minister. In 1934 the family moved to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin); he joined the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) in 1941. After the war, he studied mathematics at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel), where he finished his Ph.D. in 1950 with [Karl-Heinrich Weise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Heinrich_Weise), with a thesis in affine [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

After some time as an assistant of [Friedrich Bachmann](wikipedia://en/59096172), he worked in the group of [Wilhelm Blaschke](wikipedia://en/728998) at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg), where he defended his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1954. He then visited [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), working in the group of [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) and [Beniamino Segre](wikipedia://en/3534031), after which he obtained a faculty position at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) (with [Kurt Reidemeister](wikipedia://en/520188)), where he stayed until 1963.

In 1954–55 Klingenberg spent a year at [Indiana University Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington); during this time he also visited [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). In 1956–58 he accepted invitations to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). In 1962 he visited the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) as a guest of [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), who he knew from his time in Hamburg. Later he became a full (C4-) professor at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz), and in 1966 a full (C4-) professor at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), a position he kept till his retirement in 1989.

Klingenberg married Christine Klingenberg née Kob in 1953 and has two sons and a daughter.

In 1966 he was an invited speaker at the quadrennial [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow); his talk was on "Morse theory in the space of closed curves".

## Work {#Work}

Klingenberg's area of work was geometry, especially [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243). Besides many articles he published several books. One of his major achievements was the proof of the [sphere theorem](wikipedia://en/4685719) in joint work with [Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363) in 1960: The sphere theorem states that a [complete](wikipedia://en/7832), [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) contained in the interval (1, 4\] is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to the [sphere](wikipedia://en/39782).
