# Singularity function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Singularity function"}

**Singularity functions** are a class of [discontinuous functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discontinuous_function) that contain [singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity), i.e., they are discontinuous at their singular points. Singularity functions have been heavily studied in the field of mathematics under the alternative names of [generalized functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_functions) and [distribution theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29).[^o2][^o3][^o4] The functions are notated with brackets, as $\langle x-a\rangle ^n$ where *n* is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). The "$\langle \rangle$" are often referred to as **singularity brackets**. The functions are defined as:

:::

{| class="wikitable"

\|- ! n ! $\langle x-a\rangle ^n$ |- | $< 0$ | $\frac{d^{|n+1|}}{dx^{|n+1|}}\delta(x-a)\,$ |- | -2 |$\frac{d}{dx}\delta(x-a)\,$ |- | -1 |$\delta(x-a)\,$ |- | 0 | $H(x-a)\,$ |- | 1 | $(x-a)H(x-a)\,$ |- | 2 | $(x-a)^2H(x-a)$ |- | $\ge 0$ | $(x-a)^nH(x-a)$ |}

where: $\delta(x)$ is the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021), also called the unit impulse. The first derivative of $\delta(x)$ is also called the [unit doublet](wikipedia://en/24129233). The function $H(x)$ is the [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299): $H(x) = 0$ for $x < 0$ and $H(x) = 1$ for $x > 0$. The value of $H(0)$ will depend upon the particular convention chosen for the Heaviside step function. Note that this will only be an issue for $n = 0$ since the functions contain a multiplicative factor of $x - a$ for $n > 0$. $\langle x-a\rangle^1$ is also called the [ramp function](wikipedia://en/5092636).

## Integration {#Integration}

Integrating $\langle x-a \rangle^n$ can be done in a convenient way in which the [constant of integration](wikipedia://en/147939) is automatically included so the result will be $0$ at $x = a$.

$$\int\langle x-a \rangle^n dx = \begin{cases} \langle x-a \rangle^{n+1}, & n< 0 \\ \frac{\langle x-a \rangle^{n+1}}{n+1}, & n \ge 0 \end{cases}$$

## Example beam calculation {#Example_beam_calculation}

The deflection of a simply supported beam, as shown in the diagram, with constant cross-section and [elastic modulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_modulus), can be found using [Euler–Bernoulli beam theory](wikipedia://en/2064935). Here, we are using the [sign convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_convention) of downward forces and sagging bending moments being positive.

*Image: Loaded beam.svg.*

Load distribution:

$$w=-3\text{ N}\langle x-0 \rangle^{-1}\ +\ 6\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-2\text{ m} \rangle^0\ -\ 9\text{ N}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^{-1}\ -\ 6\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-4\text{ m} \rangle^0\$$

[Shear force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_force):

$$S=\int w\, dx$$

$$S=-3\text{ N}\langle x-0\rangle^0\ +\ 6\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-2\text{ m}\rangle^1\ -\ 9\text{ N}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^0\ -\ 6\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^1\,$$

Bending moment:

$$M = -\int S\, dx$$

$$M=3\text{ N}\langle x-0\rangle^1\ -\ 3\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-2\text{ m}\rangle^2\ +\ 9\text{ N}\langle x-4\text{ m} \rangle^1\ +\ 3\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^2\,$$

Slope:

$$u'=\frac{1}{EI}\int M\, dx$$

Because the slope is not zero at x = 0, a constant of integration, c, is added

$$u'=\frac{1}{EI}\left(\frac{3}{2}\text{ N}\langle x-0\rangle^2\ -\ 1\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-2\text{ m}\rangle^3\ +\ \frac{9}{2}\text{ N}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^2\ +\ 1\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^3\ +\ c\right)\,$$

Deflection:

$$u=\int u'\, dx$$

$$u=\frac{1}{EI}\left(\frac{1}{2}\text{ N}\langle x-0\rangle^3\ -\ \frac{1}{4}\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-2\text{ m}\rangle^4\ +\ \frac{3}{2}\text{ N}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^3\ +\ \frac{1}{4}\text{ Nm}^{-1}\langle x-4\text{ m}\rangle^4\ +\ cx\right)\,$$

The [boundary condition](wikipedia://en/436779) u = 0 at x = 4 m allows us to solve for c = −7 Nm<sup>2</sup>
