# Random cluster model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Random cluster model"}

In [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), etc. the **random cluster model** is a [random graph](wikipedia://en/358477) that generalizes and unifies the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744), [Potts model](wikipedia://en/1162226), and [percolation model](wikipedia://en/187805). It is used to study [random](wikipedia://en/19196523) [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) structures, [electrical networks](wikipedia://en/9559), etc.[^o2][^o3] It is also referred to as the **RC model** or sometimes the **FK representation** after its founders Cees Fortuin and [Piet Kasteleyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Kasteleyn).[^o4] The random cluster model has a critical limit, described by a [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774).

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), and $\omega: E \to \{0,1\}$ be a **bond configuration** on the graph that maps each edge to a value of either 0 or 1. We say that a bond is *closed* on edge $e\in E$ if $\omega(e)=0$, and **open** if $\omega(e)=1$. If we let $A(\omega) = \{e\in E : \omega(e)=1 \}$ be the set of open bonds, then an **open cluster** or **FK cluster** is any connected component in $A(\omega)$ union the set of vertices. Note that an open cluster can be a single vertex (if that vertex is not [incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidence_%28graph%29) to any open bonds).

Suppose an edge is open independently with probability $p$ and closed otherwise, then this is just the standard Bernoulli percolation process. The [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) of a configuration $\omega$ is given as

$$\mu(\omega) = \prod_{e \in E} p^{\omega(e)}(1-p)^{1-\omega(e)}.$$

The RC model is a generalization of percolation, where each cluster is weighted by a factor of $q$. Given a configuration $\omega$, we let $C(\omega)$ be the number of open clusters, or alternatively the number of [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28graph_theory%29) formed by the open bonds. Then for any $q>0$, the probability measure of a configuration $\omega$ is given as

$$\mu(\omega) = \frac{1}{Z} q^{C(\omega)}\prod_{e \in E} p^{\omega(e)}(1-p)^{1-\omega(e)}.$$

*Z* is the [partition function](wikipedia://en/312252), or the sum over the unnormalized weights of all configurations,

$$Z = \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \left\{q^{C(\omega)}\prod_{e \in E(G)} p^{\omega(e)}(1-p)^{1-\omega(e)} \right\}.$$

The partition function of the RC model is a specialization of the [Tutte polynomial](wikipedia://en/3892303), which itself is a specialization of the multivariate Tutte polynomial.[^o7]

:::

## Special values of *q* {#Special_values_of_q}

The parameter $q$ of the random cluster model can take arbitrary complex values. This includes the following special cases:

- $q\to 0$: [linear resistance networks](wikipedia://en/9559).[^o2]
- $q < 1$: negatively-correlated percolation.
- $q=1$: Bernoulli [percolation](wikipedia://en/299184), with $Z=1$.
- $q=2$: the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744).
- $q\in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$: $q$-state [Potts model](wikipedia://en/1162226).

## Edwards-Sokal representation {#Edwards-Sokal_representation}

The Edwards-Sokal (ES) representation[^o10] of the Potts model is named after Robert G. Edwards and [Alan D. Sokal](wikipedia://en/326674). It provides a unified representation of the Potts and random cluster models in terms of a [joint distribution](wikipedia://en/879637) of spin and bond configurations.

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a graph, with the number of vertices being $n = |V|$ and the number of edges being $m = |E|$. We denote a spin configuration as $\sigma\in \mathbb{Z}_q^n$ and a bond configuration as $\omega\in \{0,1\}^m$. The joint measure of $(\sigma,\omega)$ is given as

$$\mu(\sigma,\omega) = Z^{-1}\psi(\sigma)\phi_p(\omega)1_A(\sigma,\omega),$$

where $\psi$ is the uniform measure, $\phi_p$ is the [product measure](wikipedia://en/2986564) with density $p = 1-e^{-\beta}$, and $Z$ is an appropriate normalizing constant. Importantly, the indicator function $1_A$ of the set

$$A = \{ (\sigma,\omega) : \sigma_i = \sigma_j \text{ for any edge } (i,j) \text{ where } \omega = 1 \}$$

enforces the constraint that a bond can only be open on an edge if the adjacent spins are of the same state, also known as the [SW rule](wikipedia://en/21888215).

The statistics of the Potts spins can be recovered from the cluster statistics (and vice versa), thanks to the following features of the ES representation:[^o3]

- The [marginal measure](wikipedia://en/506077) $\mu(\sigma)$ of the spins is the [Boltzmann measure](wikipedia://en/4107) of the *q*-state Potts model at inverse temperature $\beta$.
- The marginal measure $\phi_{p, q}(\omega)$ of the bonds is the random-cluster measure with parameters *q* and *p.*
- The [conditional measure](wikipedia://en/504458) $\mu(\sigma \,|\, \omega)$ of the spin represents a uniformly random assignment of spin states that are constant on each connected component of the bond arrangement $\omega$.
- The conditional measure $\phi_{p,q}(\omega \,|\, \sigma)$ of the bonds represents a percolation process (of ratio *p*) on the subgraph of $G$ formed by the edges where adjacent spins are aligned.
- In the case of the Ising model, the probability that two vertices $(i,j)$ are in the same connected component of the bond arrangement $\omega$ equals the [two-point correlation function](wikipedia://en/1651916) of spins $\sigma_i \text{ and } \sigma_j$,[^o11] written $\phi_{p,q}(i \leftrightarrow j) = \langle \sigma_i\sigma_j \rangle$.

### Frustration {#Frustration}

There are several complications of the ES representation once [frustration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrical_frustration) is present in the [spin model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_model) (e.g. the Ising model with both [ferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism) and anti-ferromagnetic couplings in the same lattice). In particular, there is no longer a correspondence between the spin statistics and the cluster statistics,[^o13] and the [correlation length of the RC model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percolation_critical_exponents#Critical_behavior_close_to_the_percolation_threshold) will be greater than the correlation length of the spin model. This is the reason behind the inefficiency of the SW algorithm for simulating frustrated systems.

## Two-dimensional case {#Two-dimensional_case}

If the underlying graph $G$ is a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314), there is a duality between the random cluster models on $G$ and on the [dual graph](wikipedia://en/2536864) $G^*$.[^o22] At the level of the partition function, the duality reads

$$\tilde{Z}_G(q,v) = q^{|V|-|E|-1}v^{|E|} \tilde{Z}_{G^*}\left(q, \frac{q}{v}\right) \qquad \text{with} \qquad v = \frac{p}{1-p}\quad \text{and}\quad \tilde{Z}_G(q,v) = (1-p)^{-|E|}Z_G(q,v)$$

On a self-dual graph such as the [square lattice](wikipedia://en/2874853), a [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423) can only occur at the self-dual coupling $v_\text{self-dual}=\sqrt{q}$.[^o15]

The random cluster model on a planar graph can be reformulated as a **loop model** on the corresponding [medial graph](wikipedia://en/28280731). For a configuration $\omega$ of the random cluster model, the corresponding loop configuration is the set of self-avoiding loops that separate the clusters from the dual clusters. In the [transfer matrix approach](wikipedia://en/8964423), the loop model is written in terms of a [Temperley-Lieb algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperley-Lieb_algebra) with the parameter $\delta = q+q^{-1}$. In two dimensions, the random cluster model is therefore closely related to the [O(n) model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%28n%29_model), which is also a loop model.

In two dimensions, the critical random cluster model is described by a [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913) with the central charge

$$c = 13 - 6\beta^2 - 6\beta^{-2} \qquad \text{with} \qquad q = 4\cos^2(\pi\beta^2)\ .$$

Known exact results include the conformal dimensions of the fields that detect whether a point belongs to an FK cluster or a [spin cluster](wikipedia://en/1162226#Relation_with_the_random_cluster_model). In terms of [Kac indices](wikipedia://en/348029#Singular_vectors), these conformal dimensions are respectively $2h_{0,\frac12}$ and $2h_{\frac12,0}$, corresponding to the [fractal dimensions](wikipedia://en/285907) $2-2h_{0,\frac12}$ and $2-2h_{\frac12,0}$ of the clusters.

## History and applications {#History_and_applications}

RC models were introduced in 1969 by Fortuin and [Kasteleyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Kasteleyn), mainly to solve combinatorial problems.[^o2][^o17][^o11] After their founders, it is sometimes referred to as **FK models**.[^o4] In 1971 they used it to obtain the [FKG inequality](wikipedia://en/20283423). Post 1987, interest in the model and applications in [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics) reignited. It became the inspiration for the [Swendsen–Wang algorithm](wikipedia://en/21888215) describing the time-evolution of Potts models.[^o18] [Michael Aizenman](wikipedia://en/22411175) and coauthors used it to study the [phase boundaries](wikipedia://en/4742308) in 1D Ising and Potts models.[^o19][^o17]
