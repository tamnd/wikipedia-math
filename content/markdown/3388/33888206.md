# Crisis (dynamical systems)

*Image: Bifurcations and crises of Ikeda attractor.png.* Bifurcations and crises in the [Ikeda map](wikipedia://en/3185317).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crisis"}

In [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [astrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrodynamics), in the theory of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), a **crisis** is the sudden appearance or disappearance of a [strange attractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_attractor) as the parameters of a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) are varied.[^o2][^o3] This [global bifurcation](wikipedia://en/2326042#Global_bifurcations) occurs when a [chaotic](wikipedia://en/6295) attractor comes into contact with an [unstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable) [periodic orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit) or its [stable manifold](wikipedia://en/5466649).[^o4] As the orbit approaches the unstable orbit it will diverge away from the previous attractor, leading to a qualitatively different behaviour. Crises can produce [intermittent](wikipedia://en/4662960) behaviour.

:::

Grebogi, Ott, Romeiras, and Yorke distinguished between three types of crises:[^o5]

- The first type, a **boundary** or an **exterior crisis**, the attractor is suddenly destroyed as the parameters are varied. In the postbifurcation state the motion is transiently chaotic, moving chaotically along the former attractor before being attracted to a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738), periodic orbit, [quasiperiodic orbit](wikipedia://en/1590390), another strange attractor, or diverging to infinity.
- In the second type of crisis, an **interior crisis**, the size of the chaotic attractor suddenly increases. The attractor encounters an unstable fixed point or periodic solution that is inside the [basin of attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basin_of_attraction).
- In the third type, an **attractor merging crisis**, two or more chaotic attractors merge to form a single attractor as the critical parameter value is passed.

Note that the reverse case (sudden appearance, shrinking or splitting of attractors) can also occur. The latter two crises are sometimes called explosive bifurcations.[^o6]

While crises are "sudden" as a parameter is varied, the dynamics of the system over time can show long transients before orbits leave the neighbourhood of the old attractor. Typically there is a time constant τ for the length of the transient that diverges as a power law (τ ≈ |*p* − *p*<sub>c</sub>|<sup>*γ*</sup>) near the critical parameter value *p*<sub>c</sub>. The exponent *γ* is called the critical crisis exponent.[^o7] There also exist systems where the divergence is stronger than a power law, so-called super-persistent chaotic transients.[^o8]
