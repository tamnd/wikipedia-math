# Gelfand–Mazur theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gelfand–Mazur theorem"}

In [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), the **Gelfand–Mazur theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) named after [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) and [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162) which states that a [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665) with unit over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) in which every nonzero element is [invertible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible) is [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), i. e., the only complex Banach algebra that is a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) is the complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$.

:::

The theorem follows from the fact that the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of any element of a complex Banach algebra is nonempty: for every element $a$ of a complex Banach algebra $A$ there is some complex number $\lambda$ such that $\lambda 1 - a$ is not invertible. This is a consequence of the complex-analyticity of the [resolvent](wikipedia://en/3378540) function. By assumption, $\lambda 1 - a = 0$. So $a = \lambda \cdot 1$. This gives an isomorphism from $A$ to $\mathbb{C}$.

The theorem can be strengthened to the claim that there are (up to isomorphism) exactly three real Banach division algebras: the field of reals $\mathbb{R}$, the field of complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$, and the division algebra of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) $\mathbb{H}$. This result was proved first by Stanisław Mazur alone, but it was published in France without a proof, when the author refused the editor's request to shorten his proof. Gelfand (independently) published a proof of the complex case a few years later.
