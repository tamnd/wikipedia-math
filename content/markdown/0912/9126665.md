# Square root of 3

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Square root of 3"}

The **square root of 3** is the positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) that, when multiplied by itself, gives the number [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_%28number%29). It is denoted mathematically as **$\sqrt {3}$** or **$3^{1/2}$**. It is more precisely called the **principal square root of 3** to distinguish it from the negative number with the same property. The [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of 3 is an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689). It is also known as **Theodorus's constant**, after [Theodorus of Cyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodorus_of_Cyrene), who proved its irrationality.[^o3]

:::

In 2013, its numerical value in decimal notation was computed to ten billion digits.[^o4] Its [decimal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_expansion), written here to 60 decimal places, is given by :

\1.73205

[Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) reported a range for its value: $(\frac{1351}{780})^{2}>3>(\frac{265}{153})^{2}$.[^o5]

The upper limit **$\frac {1351}{780}$** is an accurate approximation for **$\sqrt {3}$** to $\frac {1}{608,400}$ (six decimal places, relative error $3 \times 10^{-7}$) and the lower limit **$\frac {265}{153}$** to $\frac {2}{23,409}$ (four decimal places, relative error $1\times 10^{-5}$).

## Rational approximations {#Rational_approximations}

The square root of 3 is an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689), meaning it can not be exactly represented as a fraction $x/y$ where $x$ and $y$ are [integers](wikipedia://en/14563). However, it can be approximated arbitrarily closely by such [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024).

Particularly good approximations are the integer solutions of [Pell's equations](wikipedia://en/24877),

$$x^2 - 3y^2 = 1$$

which can be algebraically rearranged into the form

$$\frac{x}{y} = \sqrt{3 + \frac{1}{y^2}} .$$

The first several solutions are given below:

## Geometry and trigonometry {#Geometry_and_trigonometry}

*Image: Square root of 3 in cube.svg.* The [space diagonal](wikipedia://en/1337505) of the [unit cube](wikipedia://en/407763) is $\sqrt{3}$.

The square root of 3 can be found as the [leg](wikipedia://en/2568071) length of an equilateral triangle that encompasses a circle with a diameter of 1.

If an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) with sides of length 1 is cut into two equal halves, by bisecting an internal angle across to make a right angle with one side, the right angle triangle's [hypotenuse](wikipedia://en/251366) is length one, and the sides are of length **$\frac{1}{2}$** and **$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$**. From this, $\tan{60^\circ}=\sqrt{3}$, $\sin{60^\circ}=\frac {\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and $\cos{30^\circ}=\frac {\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

The square root of 3 also appears in algebraic expressions for various other [trigonometric constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_trigonometric_constants), including[^o9] the sines of other angles. For example, $\tan{15^\circ}=2-\sqrt{3}$ and $\tan{75^\circ}=2+\sqrt{3}$.

It is the distance between parallel sides of a regular [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) with sides of length 1. It is also the length of the longest side of a triangle formed from two adjacent sides of a regular hexagon; following from the law of cosines:

$$\begin{aligned} c^2 &= a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos\gamma, \\[3mu] \end{aligned}$$

Since each angle of a regular hexagon is 120°, we can substitute 120° for $\gamma$ in the equation above.

$$\begin{aligned} c^2 &= 1^2 + 1^2 - 2ab\cos 120^\circ \\ &= 1 + 1 - 2(-1/2) \\ &= 2 - (-1) \\ &= 3 \\ c = \sqrt 3 \end{aligned}$$

It is the length of the [space diagonal](wikipedia://en/1337505) of a unit [cube](wikipedia://en/6285).

*Image: Vesica piscis2.svg.* The areas in blue– an equilateral triangle and a segment– form together a sector of one sixth of the circle (60°)

The [vesica piscis](wikipedia://en/471830) has a major axis to minor axis ratio equal to $\sqrt{3}:1$. This can be shown by constructing two equilateral triangles within it.

### Electrical engineering {#Electrical_engineering}
