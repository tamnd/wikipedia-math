# Paulo Ribenboim

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paulo Ribenboim"}

**Paulo Ribenboim** (born March 13, 1928) is a [Brazilian-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian-Canadian) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specializes in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ribenboim was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family in [Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recife), Brazil. He received his BSc in mathematics from the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo) in 1948, and won a fellowship to study with [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084) in France at the [University of Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nancy) in the early 1950s, where he became a close friend of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042).[^o4] He has contributed to the theory of [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) and of [valuations](wikipedia://en/347049).[^o5]

Ribenboim has authored 246 publications including 13 books. He has been at [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) in [Kingston, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario), since the 1960s, where he remains a professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus).

[Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084) was one of his doctoral advisors. [Andrew Granville](wikipedia://en/2910434), [Ján Mináč](wikipedia://en/74607395), Karl Dilcher and Aron Simis have been doctoral students of Ribenboim.[^o6]

The [Ribenboim Prize](wikipedia://en/57933020) of the [Canadian Number Theory Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Number_Theory_Association) is named in his honor.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1951, Ribenboim married Huguette Demangelle, a French Catholic woman whom he met in France. The couple have two children and five grandchildren, and have lived in Canada since 1962.
