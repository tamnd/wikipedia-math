# Tails (operating system)

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Tails"}

**Tails**, or "**The Amnesic Incognito Live System**", is a [security-focused](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security-focused_operating_system) [Debian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debian)-based [Linux distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_distribution) running on a computer, aimed at preserving [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_privacy) and [anonymity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymity) against [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_surveillance). It connects to the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) exclusively through the anonymity [overlay network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlay_network) [Tor](wikipedia://en/20556944).[^o7] The system is designed to be [booted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booting) as a [live DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_DVD) or [live USB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_USB) and never writes to the [hard drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_drive) or [SSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSD), leaving no [digital footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_footprint) on the machine unless explicitly told to do so.

:::

In 2024, Tails merged with the [Tor Project](wikipedia://en/43283728).[^o16][^o17]

## History {#History}

Tails was first released on June 23, 2009. It is the next iteration of development on [Incognito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incognito_%28Operating_System%29), a discontinued [Gentoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentoo_Linux)-based Linux distribution. The original project was called Amnesia. The operating system was born when Amnesia was merged with Incognito.[^o9] The Tor Project provided financial support for its development in the beginnings of the project.[^o10] Tails also received funding from the [Open Technology Fund](wikipedia://en/49417443), [Mozilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla), and the [Freedom of the Press Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_Press_Foundation).[^o11]

[Laura Poitras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Poitras), [Glenn Greenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Greenwald), [Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732) and [Barton Gellman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Gellman) have each said that Tails was an important tool they used in their work with [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) whistleblower [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden).[^o9][^o12][^o13][^o14]

From release 3.0 (2017) onwards, Tails requires a [64-bit processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64-bit_processor) to run.[^o15]

In 2023, the Tails Project approached the [Tor Project](wikipedia://en/43283728) to merge operations. The merger was completed on September 26, 2024, stating that, "By joining forces, the Tails team can now focus on their core mission of maintaining and improving Tails OS, exploring more and complementary use cases while benefiting from the larger organizational structure of The Tor Project."[^o16][^o17]

## Features {#Features}

Tails must be installed on a DVD or USB stick dedicated to running Tails only, for security,[^o19] and must be run on a [PC-compatible computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC-compatible_computer), not a [mobile phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone) or [tablet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_computer).[^o20] Though the system can also run as a [virtual machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_machine), running Tails in a virtual machine is potentially insecure.[^o21]

Tails' [pre-installed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-installed_software) [desktop environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desktop_environment) is [GNOME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNOME). The system includes essential software for functions such as reading and editing documents, editing images, watching videos, printing, and instant messaging.[^o22] It also includes specialized security software such as [GnuPG](wikipedia://en/38809) to [sign](wikipedia://en/59644) and [encrypt](wikipedia://en/10294) files, the cryptocurrency wallet [Electrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrum_%28software%29), and [OnionShare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OnionShare) to share files anonymously. Other software from Debian can be installed at the user's behest.[^o23] Despite being [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software), Tails contains non-free [firmware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firmware) [blobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_blob).[^o24]

Tails forces all network connections to use [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_%28anonymity_network%29) and includes a modified version of [Tor Browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_Browser) that comes with the [uBlock Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBlock_Origin) browser extension.[^o25]

By design, Tails is "amnesic". It runs in the computer's [random access memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_access_memory) (RAM) and does not write to the [hard drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_drive) or [SSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSD). The user may choose to keep files, applications or some settings on their Tails drive in "Persistent Storage", which is [encrypted](wikipedia://en/10294) by default, but is not hidden and easily detectable, though not readable, by forensic analysis.[^o26] While shutting down, Tails [overwrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_erasure) most of the used RAM to avoid a [cold boot attack](wikipedia://en/15893148).[^o27]

## Hardware support {#Hardware_support}

Tails is based on [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux). Linux generally supports a wide range of hardware, including many older devices, due to standardized drivers. There may be problems with some hardware for which Linux drivers are not available, or not supported by Tails. Wi-Fi hardware, in particular, can be incompatible without a workaround, requiring a cable for Internet connection. Touchpads and graphics can cause problems which can be worked around.[^o29][^o30][^o31]

## Security incidents {#Security_incidents}

In 2014, [Das Erste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Erste) reported that the US [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) (NSA)'s [XKeyscore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XKeyscore) surveillance system sets threat definitions for people who search for Tails using a search engine or visit the Tails website. A comment in XKeyscore's source code calls Tails "a [comsec](wikipedia://en/40922) mechanism advocated by [extremists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremism) on extremist forums".[^o33][^o34]

In the same year, [Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel) published slides from an internal NSA presentation dating to June 2012, in which the NSA deemed Tails on its own as a "major threat" to its mission and in conjunction with other privacy tools as "catastrophic".

In 2017, the US [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation) (FBI) used malicious code developed by [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook), identifying sexual extortionist and Tails user [Buster Hernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buster_Hernandez) through a [zero-day vulnerability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-day_vulnerability) in the system's default video player, [GNOME Videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNOME_Videos). The exploit was never explained to or discovered by the Tails developers, but it is believed that the vulnerability was patched in a later release of Tails. Hernandez had eluded authorities for a long time; the FBI and Facebook had searched for him with no success, and resorted to developing the custom hacking tool. He was arrested in 2017, and in 2021 he was sentenced to 75 years in prison.[^o35]
