# Gelfand–Kirillov dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gelfand–Kirillov dimension"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **Gelfand–Kirillov dimension** (or **GK dimension**) of a right [module](wikipedia://en/276410) *M* over a [*k*-algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) *A* is:

:::

$$\operatorname{GKdim} = \sup_{V, M_0} \limsup_{n\to\infty} \log_n \dim_k M_0 V^n$$

where the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) is taken over all [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) $V \subset A$ and $M_0 \subset M$.

An algebra is said to have *polynomial growth* if its Gelfand–Kirillov dimension is finite.

## Basic facts {#Basic_facts}

- The Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a finitely generated [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) *A* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is the [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) of *A* (or equivalently the [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) of the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of *A* over the base field.)
- In particular, the GK dimension of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ Is *n*.
- (Warfield) For any [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) *r* ≥ 2, there exists a finitely generated algebra whose GK dimension is *r*.[^o3]

## In the theory of D-Modules {#In_the_theory_of_D-Modules}

Given a right module *M* over the [Weyl algebra](wikipedia://en/491095) $A_n$, the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of *M* over the Weyl algebra coincides with the dimension of *M*, which is by definition the degree of the [Hilbert polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_polynomial) of *M*. This enables to prove additivity in [short exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) for the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension and finally to prove [Bernstein's inequality](wikipedia://en/491095#Properties_of_the_Weyl_algebra), which states that the dimension of *M* must be at least *n*. This leads to the definition of [holonomic D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755) as those with the minimal dimension *n*, and these modules play a great role in the [geometric Langlands program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Langlands_program).
