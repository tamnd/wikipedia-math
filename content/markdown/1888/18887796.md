# Jean-Michel Bismut

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Michel Bismut"}

**Jean-Michel Bismut** (born 26 February 1948) is a French mathematician who has been a professor at the [Université Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Paris-Sud) since 1981.[^o3] His mathematical career covers two apparently different branches of mathematics: [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). Ideas from probability play an important role in his works on geometry.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bismut's early work was related to [stochastic differential equations](wikipedia://en/1361454), stochastic control, and [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875), to which he made fundamental contributions.

Bismut received in 1973 his [Docteur d'État](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docteur_d'État) in Mathematics, from the Université Paris-VI, a thesis entitled *Analyse convexe et probabilités*. In his thesis, Bismut established a stochastic version of Pontryagin's maximum principle in [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) by introducing and studying the backward stochastic differential equations which have been the starting point of an intensive research in stochastic analysis and it stands now as a major tool in Mathematical Finance.[^o5] [^o6]

Using the quasi-invariance of the Brownian measure, Bismut gave a new approach to the [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875) and a probabilistic proof of Hörmander's theorem. He established his celebrated integration by parts for the Brownian motion on [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).

Since 1984, Bismut works on differential geometry. He found a [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) proof for the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752). And he established a local version of the Atiyah-Singer families index theorem for Dirac operators, by introducing the [Bismut superconnection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismut_superconnection) which plays a central role in modern aspects of index theory.

Bismut-Freed developed the theory of [Quillen metrics](wikipedia://en/49946643) on the smooth determinant line bundle associated with a family of Dirac operators. Bismut-Gillet-Soulé gave a curvature theorem for the Quillen metric on the holomorphic determinant of a direct image by a holomorphic proper submersion. This and Bismut—Lebeau's embedding formula for analytic torsions play a crucial role in the proof of the arithmetic Riemann-Roch theorem in [Arakelov theory](wikipedia://en/16364229), in which analytic torsion is an essential analytic ingredient in the definition of the direct image.

Bismut gave a natural construction of a Hodge theory whose corresponding Laplacian is a hypoelliptic operator acting on the total space of the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652). This operator interpolates formally between the classical elliptic Laplacian on the base and the generator of the geodesic flow. One striking application is Bismut's explicit formulas for all [orbital integrals](wikipedia://en/23462941) at semi-simple elements of any [reductive Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_Lie_group).

He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in the summer of 1984.[^o7] In 1990, he was awarded the Prix [Ampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André-Marie_Ampère) of the Academy of Sciences. He was elected as a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1991. In 2021 he received the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematics (jointly with [Jeff Cheeger](wikipedia://en/7343761)).[^o8]

In 1986, he was an invited speaker in the Geometry section at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berkeley,[^o9] and in 1998 he was a plenary speaker at the ICM in Berlin.[^o10][^o11]

He was a member of the Fields Medal Committee for ICM 1990. [^o12] From 1999 until 2006, a member of the executive committee (from 2003 until 2006 as vice-president), International Mathematical Union (IMU).[^o13] He was an editor of [Inventiones Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventiones_Mathematicae) from 1989 until 1996 and managing editor from 1996 until 2008.[^o14]
