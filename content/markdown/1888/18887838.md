# Equivariant algebraic K-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equivariant algebraic K-theory"}

In mathematics, the **equivariant algebraic K-theory** is an [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) associated to the category $\operatorname{Coh}^G(X)$ of [equivariant coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/41883327) on an algebraic scheme *X* with [action of a linear algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_algebraic_group_action) *G*, via Quillen's [Q-construction](wikipedia://en/41403415); thus, by definition,

:::

$$K_i^G(X) = \pi_i(B^+ \operatorname{Coh}^G(X)).$$

In particular, $K_0^G(C)$ is the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) of $\operatorname{Coh}^G(X)$. The theory was developed by R. W. Thomason in 1980s.[^o2] Specifically, he proved equivariant analogs of fundamental theorems such as the localization theorem.

Equivalently, $K_i^G(X)$ may be defined as the $K_i$ of the category of coherent sheaves on the [quotient stack](wikipedia://en/40579087) $[X/G]$.[^o3][^o4] (Hence, the equivariant K-theory is a specific case of the [K-theory of a stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-theory_of_a_stack).)

A version of the [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/582530) holds in the setting of equivariant (algebraic) K-theory.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Fundamental theorems"}

## Fundamental theorems {#Fundamental_theorems}

Let *X* be an equivariant algebraic scheme.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Localization theorem"}

**Localization theorem.**

Given a closed immersion $Z \hookrightarrow X$ of equivariant algebraic schemes and an open immersion $Z - U \hookrightarrow X$, there is a long exact sequence of groups

$$\cdots \to K^G_i(Z) \to K^G_i(X) \to K^G_i(U) \to K^G_{i-1}(Z) \to \cdots$$

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

One of the fundamental examples of equivariant K-theory groups are the equivariant K-groups of $G$-equivariant coherent sheaves on a points, so $K^G_i(*)$. Since $\text{Coh}^G(*)$ is equivalent to the category $\text{Rep}(G)$ of finite-dimensional representations of $G$. Then, the Grothendieck group of $\text{Rep}(G)$, denoted $R(G)$ is $K_0^G(*)$.[^o11]

### Torus ring {#Torus_ring}

Given an algebraic torus $\mathbb{T}\cong \mathbb{G}_m^k$ a finite-dimensional representation $V$ is given by a direct sum of $1$-dimensional $\mathbb{T}$-modules called the **weights** of $V$.[^o13] There is an explicit isomorphism between $K_\mathbb{T}$ and $\mathbb{Z}[t_1,\ldots, t_k]$ given by sending $[V]$ to its associated character.[^o14]
