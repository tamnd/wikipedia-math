# Michael Friedman (philosopher)

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Friedman"}

**Michael Friedman** (April 2, 1947 – March 24, 2025) was an American philosopher who was Emeritus [Patrick Suppes](wikipedia://en/4824664) Professor of Philosophy of Science and Professor, by courtesy, of German Studies at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). Friedman was best known for his work in the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), especially on [scientific explanation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_explanation) and the [philosophy of physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_physics), and for his [historical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_philosophy) work on [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant). Friedman has done historical work on figures in [continental philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_philosophy) such as [Martin Heidegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Heidegger) and [Ernst Cassirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Cassirer). He also served as the co-director of the Program in History and Philosophy of Science and Technology at Stanford University.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Friedman earned his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from [Queens College, City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_College,_City_University_of_New_York) in 1969 and his PhD from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1973.[^o8] Before moving to Stanford in 2002, Friedman taught at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago), [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University), and [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) as a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar).

Friedman was a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_&_Sciences) from 1997 until his death in 2025. In 2024 he was elected an honorary member of the [International Academy of Philosophy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_Philosophy_of_Science) (AIPS). Four of his articles have been selected as among the "ten best" of their year by *[The Philosopher's Annual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Philosopher's_Annual)*.

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Friedman's early work was on the nature of scientific explanation and the philosophy of physics. His first book, *Foundations of Space-Time Theories*, was published by [Princeton University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Press) in 1983 won the [Matchette Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Association#Prizes) (now known as the Book Prize) from the [American Philosophical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Association), to recognize work by a younger scholar. It also won the [Lakatos Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakatos_Award) from the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) to recognize outstanding work in philosophy of science.

*Kant and the Exact Sciences* was described in *[Philosophical Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Review)* as "a very important book," "required reading for researchers on the relation between the exact sciences and Kant's philosophy."[^o10]

[UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley) [German philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_philosophy) professor [Hans Sluga](wikipedia://en/1504912) described Friedman's 2000 book *A Parting of the Ways: Carnap, Cassirer, and Heidegger*, a book that detailed the philosophies of [Carnap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnap), [Cassirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Cassirer), and [Heidegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidegger), as "eye-opening" and "ambitious". The book shed new light on the split between [analytic philosophy](wikipedia://en/159211) and [Continental philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_philosophy).[^o11]

In his book *Dynamics of Reason*, Friedman "provides the fullest account to date not only of \[his\] neo-Kantian, historicized, dynamical conception of relativized *a priori* principles of mathematics and physics, but also of the pivotal role that \[he\] sees philosophy as playing in making scientific revolutions rational."[^o12]

In 2015, he was awarded the [Fernando Gil International Prize for the Philosophy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Gil_International_Prize_for_the_Philosophy_of_Science) for his book *Kant's Construction of Nature*.

Friedman was an [honorary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_professor) at the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario).

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Friedman was married to [Graciela De Pierris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciela_De_Pierris), a professor of philosophy at Stanford who has published work on [early modern philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_philosophy).[^o14]

Friedman died in Stanford on March 24, 2025, at the age of 77.[^o15]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

### Books {#Books}

- *Foundations of Space-Time Theories: Relativistic Physics and the Philosophy of Science* (Princeton University Press, 1983)
- *Kant and the Exact Sciences* (Harvard University Press, 1992)
- *Reconsidering Logical Positivism* (Cambridge University Press, 1999)
- *A Parting of the Ways: Carnap, Cassirer, and Heidegger* (Open Court, 2000)
- *Dynamics of Reason: The 1999 Kant Lectures at Stanford University* (CSLI/University of Chicago Press, 2001)
- *Immanuel Kant: Metaphysical Foundations of Natural Science* (Cambridge University Press, 2004) (editor)
- *The Kantian Legacy in Nineteenth-Century Science* (MIT Press, 2006) (co-editor with Alfred Nordmann)
- *The Cambridge Companion to Carnap* (2007) (co-editor with Richard Creath)
- *Kant's Construction of Nature: A Reading of the Metaphysical Foundations of Natural Science* (Cambridge University Press, 2013)
