# Richard Kadison

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Kadison"}

**Richard Vincent Kadison** (July 25, 1925 – August 22, 2018)[^o5] was an American mathematician known for his contributions to the study of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Born in New York City in 1925,[^o5][^o6] Kadison was a Gustave C. Kuemmerle Professor in the Department of Mathematics of the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).[^o7]

Kadison was a member of the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Academy_of_Sciences) (elected in 1996),[^o8][^o9] and a foreign member of the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters) (elected 1974)[^o5] and of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o10] He was a 1969 [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o11]

Kadison was awarded the 1999 [Leroy P. Steele Prize for Lifetime Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7][^o12] In 2012, he became a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Kadison served as an officer in the merchant marines for several years after 1943.[^o15] He was a skilled gymnast with a specialty in rings, making the 1952 US Olympic Team but later withdrawing due to an injury.[^o16] He married Karen M. Holm on June 5, 1956, and they had one son, Lars.[^o16]

Kadison died after a short illness on August 22, 2018.[^o5]
