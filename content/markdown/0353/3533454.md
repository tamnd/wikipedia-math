# Alexander Razborov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Razborov"}

**Aleksandr Aleksandrovich Razborov** (Алекса́ндр Алекса́ндрович Разбо́ров; born February 16, 1963), sometimes known as **Sasha Razborov**, is a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computational theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_theorist). He is Andrew McLeish Distinguished Service Professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).

:::

## Research {#Research}

In his best known work, joint with [Steven Rudich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Rudich), he introduced the notion of *[natural proofs](wikipedia://en/662262)*, a class of strategies used to prove fundamental lower bounds in [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/7543). In particular, Razborov and Rudich showed that, under the assumption that certain kinds of [one-way functions](wikipedia://en/363890) exist, such proofs cannot give a resolution of the [P = NP](wikipedia://en/6115) problem, so new techniques will be required in order to solve this question.

## Awards {#Awards}

*Image: Александр Разборов.jpg.* Razborov in 2011

- [Nevanlinna Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevanlinna_Prize) (1990) for introducing the "approximation method" in proving [Boolean circuit](wikipedia://en/7087423) [lower bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_bound) of some essential [algorithmic](wikipedia://en/775) problems,[^o5]
- [Erdős Lecturer](wikipedia://en/56645465), [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), 1998.
- [Corresponding member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_member) of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) (2000)[^o6][^o7]
- [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) (2007, with [Steven Rudich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Rudich)) for the paper "[Natural Proofs](wikipedia://en/662262)."[^o8][^o9]
- [David P. Robbins Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins_Prize) for the paper "On the minimal density of triangles in graphs" (Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 17 (2008), no. 4, 603–618), and for introducing a new powerful method, flag algebras, to solve problems in extremal combinatorics
- [Gödel Lecturer](wikipedia://en/69216431) (2010) with the lecture titled *Complexity of Propositional Proofs.*[^o10]
- [Andrew MacLeish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_MacLeish) Distinguished Service Professor (2008) in the Department of Computer Science, [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).
- Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences (AAAS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (2020).[^o11]
