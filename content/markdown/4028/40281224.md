::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal tiling honeycomb"}

In the field of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/318742), the **hexagonal tiling honeycomb** is one of 11 [regular paracompact honeycombs](wikipedia://en/39591384#Regular_paracompact_honeycombs) in 3-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742). It is *paracompact* because it has [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) composed of an infinite number of faces. Each cell is a [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) whose vertices lie on a [horosphere](wikipedia://en/13487128), a surface in hyperbolic space that approaches a single [ideal point](wikipedia://en/4995922) at infinity.

:::

The [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of the hexagonal tiling honeycomb is {6,3,3}. Since that of the [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) is {6,3}, this honeycomb has three such hexagonal tilings meeting at each edge. Since the Schläfli symbol of the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) is {3,3}, the [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of this honeycomb is a tetrahedron. Thus, four hexagonal tilings meet at each vertex of this honeycomb, six hexagons meet at each vertex, and four edges meet at each vertex.[^o10]

In this projection, the hexagons grow infinitely small towards the infinite boundary, [asymptoting](wikipedia://en/3107) towards a single ideal point. It can be seen as similar to the [order-3 apeirogonal tiling](wikipedia://en/38758064), {∞,3} of H<sup>2</sup>, with [horocycles](wikipedia://en/11084460) circumscribing vertices of [apeirogonal](wikipedia://en/5658261) faces.

## Symmetry constructions {#Symmetry_constructions}

*Image: Hyperbolic subgroup tree 336-direct.png.* [Subgroup relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram#Subgroup_relations_of_paracompact_hyperbolic_groups)

It has a total of five reflectional constructions from five related Coxeter groups all with four mirrors and only the first being regular: \[6,3,3\], \[3,6,3\], \[6,3,6\], \[6,3<sup>\[3\]</sup>\] and \[3<sup>\[3,3\]</sup>\] , having 1, 4, 6, 12 and 24 times [larger fundamental domains respectively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact_uniform_honeycomb#Enumeration). In [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502) subgroup markups, they are related as: \[6,(3,3)<sup>\*</sup>\] (remove 3 mirrors, index 24 subgroup); \[3,6,3<sup>\*</sup>\] or \[3<sup>\*</sup>,6,3\] (remove 2 mirrors, index 6 subgroup); \[1<sup>\+</sup>,6,3,6,1<sup>\+</sup>\] (remove two orthogonal mirrors, index 4 subgroup); all of these are isomorphic to \[3<sup>\[3,3\]</sup>\]. The ringed Coxeter diagrams are , , , and , representing different types (colors) of hexagonal tilings in the [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777).

## Related polytopes and honeycombs {#Related_polytopes_and_honeycombs}

The hexagonal tiling honeycomb is a [regular hyperbolic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/505253#Tessellations_of_hyperbolic_3-space) in 3-space, and one of 11 which are paracompact.

It is [one of 15 uniform paracompact honeycombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact_uniform_honeycomb#.5B6.2C3.2C3.5D_family) in the \[6,3,3\] Coxeter group, along with its dual, the [order-6 tetrahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/40281358).

It is part of a sequence of [regular polychora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polychora), which include the [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821) {3,3,3}, [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) {4,3,3}, and [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358) {5,3,3} of Euclidean 4-space, along with other hyperbolic honeycombs containing [tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/30606) [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719).

It is also part of a sequence of regular honeycombs of the form {6,3,p}, which are each composed of [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) cells:
