# Chiral Potts model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chiral Potts model"}

The **chiral Potts model** is a [spin model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_model) on a [planar lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) studied by Helen Au-Yang Perk and Jacques Perk, among others. It may be viewed as a generalization of the [Potts model](wikipedia://en/1162226), and as with the [Potts model](wikipedia://en/1162226), the model is defined by configurations which are assignments of *[spins](wikipedia://en/19593829)* to each vertex of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), where each spin can take one of $N$ values. To each edge joining vertices with assigned spins $n$ and $n'$, a [Boltzmann weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_factor) $W(n,n')$ is assigned. For this model, [chiral](wikipedia://en/32703814) means that $W(n,n') \neq W(n',n)$. When the weights satisfy the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810), it is integrable, in the sense that certain quantities can be exactly evaluated.

:::

For the **integrable chiral Potts model**, the weights are defined by a high [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) [curve](wikipedia://en/253260), the [chiral Potts curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_Potts_curve).[^o2][^o3] Unlike the other solvable models,[^o4][^o5] whose weights are parametrized by curves of genus less or equal to one, so that they can be expressed in terms of [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367), rational functions for the genus zero case, or by [theta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta_functions) for the genus 1 case, this model involves high genus theta functions, for which the theory is less well-developed.

The related *chiral clock model*, which was introduced in the 1980s by David Huse and Stellan Ostlund independently, is not exactly solvable, in contrast to the chiral Potts model.

## The model {#The_model}

This model is out of the class of all previously known models and raises a host of unsolved questions which are related to some of the most intractable problems of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) which have been with us for 150 years. The chiral Potts models are used to understand the commensurate-incommensurate phase transitions.[^o7] For N = 3 and 4, the integrable case was discovered in 1986 in Stony Brook and published the following year.[^o2][^o8]

### Self-dual case {#Self-dual_case}

The model is called [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual) if the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the weight function returns the same function. A special (genus 1) case had been solved in 1982 by Fateev and [Zamolodchikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zamolodchikov).[^o10] By removing certain restrictions of the work of Alcaraz and Santos,[^o11] a more general self-dual case of the integrable chiral Potts model was discovered.[^o2] The weight are given in product form[^o12][^o13] and the parameters in the weight are shown to be on the [Fermat curve](wikipedia://en/897529), with genus greater than 1.

### General case {#General_case}

The general solution for all *k* (the temperature variable) was found.[^o3] The weights were also given in product form and it was tested computationally (on [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran)) that they satisfy the star–triangle relation. The proof was published later.[^o15]

::: {#o17 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

### Order parameter {#Order_parameter}

From the series[^o7][^o19] the [order parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_parameter) was conjectured[^o20] to have the simple form

$$\langle \sigma^n\rangle=(1-k'^2)^\beta,\quad \beta=n(N-n)/2N^2.$$

It took many years to prove this conjecture, as the usual [corner transfer matrix](wikipedia://en/20112889) technique could not be used, because of the higher genus curve. This conjecture was proven by Baxter in 2005[^o21][^o22] using functional equations and the "broken rapidity line" technique of [Jimbo](wikipedia://en/22363355) *et al.*[^o23] assuming two mild [analyticity](wikipedia://en/61478) conditions of the type commonly used in the field of Yang–Baxter integrable models. Most recently, in a series of papers[^o24][^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28][^o29][^o30] an algebraic ([Ising-like](wikipedia://en/292744)) way of obtaining the order parameter has been given, giving more insight into the algebraic structure.

### Connection to six vertex model {#Connection_to_six_vertex_model}

In 1990 Bazhanov and Stroganov[^o32] showed that there exist ***L***-operators ([Lax operator](wikipedia://en/5527029)) which satisfy the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810)

$$L_{i_1\alpha}^{j_1\beta}(x)L_{i_2\beta}^{j_2\gamma} (y)R_{j_1j_2}^{k_1k_2}(y/x)= R_{i_1i_2}^{j_1j_2} (y/x)L_{j_2\alpha}^{k_2\beta} (y)L_{j_1\beta}^{k_1\gamma}(x),\quad 0<i,j,k\le1,\quad 0\le \alpha, \beta,\gamma\le N-1.$$

where the 2 × 2 ***R***-operator ([R-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996)) is the [six vertex model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_vertex_model) ***R***-matrix (see [Vertex model](wikipedia://en/4924962)). The product of four chiral Potts weight ***S*** was shown to intertwine two ***L***-operators as

$$L_{i_1\alpha_1}^{i_2\alpha_2} {\hat L}_{i_2\beta_1}^{i_3\beta_2} S_{\alpha_2\beta_2}^{\alpha_3\beta_3}= S_{\alpha_1\beta_1}^{\alpha_2\beta_2} {\hat L}_{i_1\beta_2}^{i_2\beta_3} L_{i_2\alpha_2}^{i_3\alpha_3},\quad 0<i_i\le1,\quad 0\le \alpha_i, \beta_i\le N-1.$$

This inspired a breakthrough, namely the functional relations for the [transfer matrices](wikipedia://en/4567165) of the chiral Potts models were discovered.[^o33]

### Free energy and interfacial tension {#Free_energy_and_interfacial_tension}

Using these functional relations, Baxter was able to calculate the eigenvalues of the transfer matrix of the chiral Potts model,[^o35] and obtained the [critical exponent](wikipedia://en/2029677) for the [specific heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat) α=1-2/N, which was also conjectured in reference 12. The [interfacial tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interfacial_tension) was also calculated by him with the exponent μ=1/2+1/N.[^o36][^o37]

:::

## Relation with knot theory {#Relation_with_knot_theory}

The integrable chiral Potts weights are given in product form [^o3] as

$$W_{pq}(n)\!=\!\Big({\mu_p\over\mu_q}\Big)^{\!\!n}\prod_{j=1}^n {y_q-x_p\omega^j\over y_p-x_q\omega^j},\quad \overline W_{pq}(n)\!=\!\big({\mu_p\mu_q}\big)^{\!n}\!\prod_{j=1}^n {\omega x_p\!-\!x_q\omega^j\over y_q\!-\!y_p\omega^j},$$

where $\omega^N = 1$ is a [primitive root of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_root_of_unity) and we associate with each [rapidity](wikipedia://en/4520673) variable p three variables $(x_p, y_p, \mu_p)$ satisfying

$$x_p^N+y_p^N=k(1+x_p^N y_p^N),\quad k'^2+k^2=1,\quad k'\mu_p^N=1-k y_p^N,\quad k'\mu_p^{-N}=1-k x_p^N.$$

It is easy to see that

$$W_{pp}(a-b)=1,\quad \overline W_{pp}(a-b)=\delta_{a,b}$$

which is similar to [Reidemeister move](wikipedia://en/1152374) I. It was also known that the weights satisfy the inversion relation,

$$W_{pq}(a-b)W_{qp}(a-b)=1,\quad \sum_{d=0}^{N-1}\overline W_{pq}(a-d)\overline W_{qp}(d-a')=r_{pq}\delta_{a,a'}.$$

This is equivalent to Reidemeister move II. The star-triangle relation

$$\sum^{N}_{d=1}\,{\overline W}_{pr}(a-d)\,W_{pq}(d-c)\,{\overline W}_{rq}(d-b) =R_{pqr}\,\overline W_{pq}(a-b)\,{ W}_{pr}(b-c)\,W_{rq}(a-c)$$

is equivalent to Reidemeister move III. These are shown in the figures below. [^o39]
