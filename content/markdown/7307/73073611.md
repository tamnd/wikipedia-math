# Stephen Blyth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stephen Blyth"}

**Stephen James Blyth** (born September 1967)[^o3] is a British mathematician and academic. From October 2022 to September 2025 he was [Principal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_principals_of_Lady_Margaret_Hall,_Oxford) of [Lady Margaret Hall, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Margaret_Hall,_Oxford).[^o4] He had been Professor of the Practice of Statistics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) since 2012,[^o5][^o6] and was also chief executive officer of the [Harvard Management Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Management_Company) between January 2015 and July 2016.[^o7][^o8]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Blyth [matriculated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculated) into [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge), in 1985 to study the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos).[^o10] He graduated with a [first class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_honours) Bachelor of Arts (BA) degree,[^o10] and was the 3rd [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in that year.[^o4] He then moved to the United States, where he studied statistics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o6] He was awarded a Master of Arts (AM) degree in 1989.[^o11] His [doctoral dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_dissertation) was titled "Local Regression Coefficients and the Correlation Curve" and his advisor was Kjell Doksum.[^o12] His Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) degree was awarded in 1992.[^o12]

After graduating with his PhD, he returned to England and was a lecturer in the Department of Mathematics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) from 1992 to 1994.[^o7][^o11] He then moved into the [financial industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_industry), working at [HSBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSBC), [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley) and [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank).[^o5][^o11] In 2006, he returned to Harvard University where he joined the [faculty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_personnel).[^o6] He taught statistics in the [Harvard Faculty of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Faculty_of_Arts_and_Sciences),[^o7] rising to become Professor of the Practice of Statistics in 2012.[^o6] He also worked with the [Harvard Management Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Management_Company) (HMC) which manages Harvard University's endowment and related financial assets.[^o7] By 2014, he was managing director and head of public markets.[^o7] In September 2014, he was announced as the next president and chief executive officer of the Harvard Management Company:[^o7] he took up the post on 1 January 2015.[^o13] He took medical leave from 23 May 2016,[^o8] and stepped down as CEO on 27 July 2016.[^o8]

In December 2021, Blyth was announced as the next [Principal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_%28college%29) of [Lady Margaret Hall, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Margaret_Hall,_Oxford).[^o5][^o10] He took up the post on 1 October 2022 and was installed during a service in the College Chapel on 7 October 2022.[^o4]

In September 2025 it was announced that he would in Autumn 2025 join the [Financial Policy Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Policy_Committee) of the Bank of England, to serve a three year term.[^o14] On 23 October the [Treasury Committee of the House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_Select_Committee) approved his appointment.[^o15]
