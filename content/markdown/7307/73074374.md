# Yan's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yan's theorem"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Yan's theorem** is a [separation](wikipedia://en/6629185) and existence result. It is of particular interest in [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics) where one uses it to prove the [Kreps-Yan theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreps-Yan_theorem).

:::

The theorem was published by [Jia-An Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jia-An_Yan).[^o2] It was proven for the [L<sup>1</sup> space](wikipedia://en/45194) and later generalized by [Jean-Pascal Ansel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pascal_Ansel) to the case $1\leq p<+\infty$.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Yan's theorem"}

## Yan's theorem {#Yan's_theorem}

Notation:

$\overline{\Omega}$ is the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of a set $\Omega$.

$$A-B=\{f-g:f\in A,\;g\in B\}.$$

$I_A$ is the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of $A$.

$q$ is the [conjugate index](wikipedia://en/4350689) of $p$.

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ be a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325), $1\leq p<+\infty$ and $B_+$ be the space of non-negative and bounded [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685). Further let $K\subseteq L^p(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ be a [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) subset and $0\in K$.

Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

1. For all $f\in L_+^p(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ with $f\neq 0$ exists a constant $c>0$, such that $cf \not\in \overline{K-B_+}$.
1. For all $A\in \mathcal{F}$ with $P(A)>0$ exists a constant $c>0$, such that $cI_A \not\in \overline{K-B_+}$.
1. There exists a random variable $Z\in L^q$, such that $Z>0$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) and

$$\sup\limits_{Y\in K}\mathbb{E}[ZY]<+\infty.$$

:::
