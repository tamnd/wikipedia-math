# Thomas A. Garrity

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas A. Garrity"}

**Thomas Anthony Garrity** (born 25 April 1959)[^o5] is an American mathematician. He teaches at Williams College, where he is the Webster Atwell Class of 1921 Professor of Mathematics.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Thomas Anthony Garrity was born in 1959.[^o5] He completed his bachelor's degree in mathematics at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) in 1981.[^o3] He attended [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) for doctoral studies, completing a PhD in mathematics in 1986 under the supervision of professor [William Fulton](wikipedia://en/9642149). Garrity's doctoral thesis was titled *On Ample [Vector Bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) and Negative Curvature*.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Garrity is currently a professor of mathematics at [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College), where he has taught since 1989.[^o8]

### Research {#Research}

In 1989, Garrity and three other collaborators found an algorithm in [NC](wikipedia://en/22073) to factorize [rational polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_polynomials) over the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers).[^o10]

In 1991, Garrity discovered the concept of "geometric continuity", which generalizes several other notions of continuity for both explicit and [implicit surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implicit_surfaces).[^o11]

In 1999, Garrity came up with the concept of a *simplex sequence*, which is an alternate approach to the [Hermite problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermite_problem) (of which the [Jacobi-Perron algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobi-Perron_algorithm) is yet another approach).[^o12] For the case of ordered pairs, if the simplex sequence is eventually periodic, then the two numbers must be of degree at most three.[^o12]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Garrity was a 2004 recipient of one of the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Awards for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o14]
