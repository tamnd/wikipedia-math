# Telba Irony

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Telba Irony"}

**Telba Zalkind Irony** is a Brazilian [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), [operations researcher](wikipedia://en/43476), and proponent of [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412). After working at the [Food and Drug Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration), as chief of biostatistics at the Office of Device Evaluation and deputy director of biostatistics and epidemiology at the [Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Biologics_Evaluation_and_Research), she moved to [Janssen R&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janssen_Pharmaceuticals), a division of [Johnson & Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_&_Johnson), as Senior Scientific Director in Quantitative Sciences.

:::

Irony was born in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Paulo), and studied physics and statistics at the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo), earning both a bachelor's degree and master's degree there. She obtained her Ph.D. in industrial engineering and operations research in 1989 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) with a dissertation *Modeling, Information Extraction and Decision Making a Bayesian Approach to Some Engineering Problems* supervised by [Richard E. Barlow](wikipedia://en/61548302). Irony was an assistant professor of operations research in the department of engineering at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) from 1990 to 1998. She joined the [Food and Drug Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration) in 1998, where she works in the biostatistics division.

In 2010, she became a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association). In 2014, she won the Excellence in Analytical Science Award of the Food and Drug Administration "for spearheading innovative regulatory science studies, culminating in the release of novel guidance documents; supporting complex policy decision-making; and changing the submission review paradigm".
