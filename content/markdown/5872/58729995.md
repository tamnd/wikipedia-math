# Jeanette McLeod

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jeanette McLeod"}

**Jeanette Claire McLeod** is a New Zealand mathematician specialising in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), including the theories of [Latin squares](wikipedia://en/37447) and [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477). She is a senior lecturer in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at the [University of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury), a principal investigator for Te Pūnaha Matatini, a Centre of Research Excellence associated with the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), an honorary senior lecturer at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University), and the president for a term of three years from 2018 to 2020 of the [Combinatorial Mathematics Society of Australasia](wikipedia://en/53927291).

:::

McLeod earned her Ph.D. in 2007 from Australian National University. Her dissertation, *Methods in Asymptotic Combinatorics*, was supervised by [Brendan McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_McKay_%28mathematician%29). She is one of the cofounders of Maths Craft New Zealand, a project to popularise mathematics using crafts such as [crochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crochet) and [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami).

In 2019, McLeod and fellow Canterbury mathematician Phil Wilson won the [Cranwell Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranwell_Medal) for Science Communication from the [New Zealand Association of Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Association_of_Scientists) for their work on Maths Craft.[^o2] McLeod's advocacy for creative practice within science and research saw her profiled in a *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* careers article in 2021.[^o3]
