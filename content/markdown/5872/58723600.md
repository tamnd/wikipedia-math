# Bobby Seagull

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bobby Seagull"}

**Jay Bobby Seagull** (born 13 February 1984) is a British mathematics teacher, broadcaster and writer. He appeared on the television programme *[University Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Challenge)* in 2017, and in 2018 on *Monkman & Seagull's Genius Guide to Britain.* His second book, *The Life-Changing Magic of Numbers*, was published in 2018.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Seagull, the second of his parents' four sons all of whom were called Jay, grew up in the [London Borough of Newham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Newham).[^o4] His Malayali parents emigrated to [East Ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Ham) from [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala) in India.[^o5][^o6] He said on episode ten of *The Answer Trap* that "my first name is Jay because in my family there's a South Indian tradition, so Jay Dave, Jay Bobby, Jay John, Jay Thomas, and then my family name is Jose, from Kerala in South India".[^o7] Asked by an interviewer in 2017 about his "pretty unusual" name, Bobby Seagull explained, "My dad was very taken by the book *[Jonathan Livingston Seagull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Livingston_Seagull)*".[^o4] He attended St Michael's Primary School followed by [St Bonaventure's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bonaventure's) in [Upton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upton_Park,_London).[^o5] He is a fan of local [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.).[^o8]

When he was growing up, his father would take his boys regularly to East Ham Library, where Seagull was encouraged to find books that interested him.[^o5] His headteacher was [Michael Wilshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Wilshaw).[^o5] After seeing an advertisement for sixth form scholarships at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College), he subsequently applied and studied at Eton.[^o5] Seagull started studying [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Lady Margaret Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Margaret_Hall), [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), but left his degree and graduated from [Royal Holloway, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway,_University_of_London).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

After graduating from [Royal Holloway, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway,_University_of_London), Seagull joined [Lehman Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_Brothers) as a trader.[^o5] He was there during the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), and has spoken about the days before it filed for [bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy_of_Lehman_Brothers).[^o5][^o10] He left banking and worked as an [accountant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountant) for [Pricewaterhouse Coopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PricewaterhouseCoopers).[^o5] During this time, Seagull was involved with voluntary work and training new graduates.[^o5]

Seagull did a [PGCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_Certificate_in_Education) at [Hughes Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Hall,_Cambridge) and completed a master's degree in education at [Emmanuel College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge) whilst finishing his [newly qualified teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_qualified_teacher) (NQT) year.[^o11] He has taught at [Chesterton Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterton_Community_College),[^o12] Cambridge and [East London Science School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London_Science_School).[^o5] Seagull is a part-time Maths teacher at a secondary school in London.[^o5] He has created mathematics raps for his school classes.[^o13] He is also leading a course on [money management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_management) at the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University).[^o14] Since the start of 2018, Seagull has been pursuing a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) concerning ["mathematical anxiety and phobia"](wikipedia://en/8111444).[^o15]

He has been a [columnist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columnist) for the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times)* since January 2018.[^o16][^o17] Seagull has since become a national campaigner to improve maths literacy.[^o5][^o18] He is concerned that in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) it is acceptable to celebrate being bad at maths as if it is a badge of honour.[^o5] He is also an advocate for maths teachers, supporting them in the creation of new materials and campaigning for better pay.[^o5] He has said he wants to be the "Jamie Oliver of maths..Jamie Oliver helped to introduce healthy food in schools. I would love to be the equivalent for mathematics where I can help change how people nationally think about it".[^o19] He has created maths puzzles for [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) [Bitesize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitesize), as well as working with [Rachel Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Riley).[^o20]

Seagull releases regular maths challenges on [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4).[^o21] In 2018 he taught mathematics to three [BBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News) presenters, [Naga Munchetty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Munchetty), [Jayne McCubbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayne_McCubbin) and [Tim Muffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Muffett), before they retook their [General Certificate of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education) (GCSE) mathematics paper.[^o13] He presents the podcast *Maths Appeal* with [Susan Okereke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Okereke).[^o22]

### *University Challenge* {#University_Challenge}

Whilst working at [East London Science School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London_Science_School), Seagull was made captain of the [Emmanuel College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge) *[University Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Challenge)* team.[^o5] During the competition he was praised for his enthusiasm, and his name started to trend on [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter).[^o5] He met [Eric Monkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Monkman) in the semi-final, where Monkman's team ([Wolfson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfson_College,_Cambridge)) won.[^o24] Whilst the pair started as rivals, they went on to form a friendship and began broadcasting together.

### Broadcasting {#Broadcasting}

Seagull and Monkman were given their own radio show, *Monkman and Seagull's Polymathematic Adventure*, in 2017.[^o26][^o27] Seagull and Monkman co-hosted their first television series, *[Monkman & Seagull's Genius Guide to Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkman_&_Seagull's_Genius_Guide_to_Britain)*, in 2018.[^o28][^o29] In the show, the pair travelled the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) to identify Britain's technological achievements.[^o30] In 2020 they followed up with *[Monkman & Seagull's Genius Adventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkman_&_Seagull's_Genius_Adventures).*

In 2019 Seagull became an advocate for investment in public libraries, working with the [Chartered Institute of Library and Information Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_Institute_of_Library_and_Information_Professionals) (CILIP) to front the 'Libraries Deliver' campaign as a national 'Library Champion'. In 2020, Seagull was awarded Honorary Fellowship of CILIP.[^o31]

Seagull has appeared on the [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube) channel Football Daily on a number of occasions, specifically in their Stat Wars series. He has also participated as a panellist on the [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4) discussion programme *[Any Questions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Any_Questions?)*.

In 2021, he and quiz expert Frank Paul became the resident "trappers" on the [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4) game show *The Answer Trap*. Their role is to create plausible-sounding but wrong answers to questions asked of the contestants, which are hidden among the genuine responses.

In December 2022 Seagull competed alongside Eric Monkman in BBC's *[Celebrity Antiques Road Trip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity_Antiques_Road_Trip)*.[^o32]

In 2023, Seagull was on *[Pilgrimage: The Road Through Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrimage_%28TV_series%29)*, a [BBC Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Two) series following a group of celebrities going on a pilgrimage to [Fátima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fátima,_Portugal). He was the only Roman Catholic pilgrim in the group.[^o33]

In April 2023, Seagull appeared on *[Indian Matchmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Matchmaking)*, a [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) series where people of Indian heritage enlist the help of a matchmaker to find a partner to marry. He took part in [series five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunted_%282015_TV_series%29#Series_5_%282023%29) of *[Celebrity Hunted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunted_%282015_TV_series%29#Celebrity_Hunted)* in 2023, alongside [Saffron Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron_Barker).[^o34]

He was awarded an MBE in the 2026 New Year's Honours list.[^o35]
