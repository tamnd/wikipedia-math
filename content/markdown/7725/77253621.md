# Soheyla Feyzbakhsh

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Soheyla Feyzbakhsh"}

**Soheyla Feyzbakhsh** (سهیلا فیض‌بخش)[^o3] is a mathematician whose research connects [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) to [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). Originally from Iran, she works in the UK as Royal Society university research fellow and senior lecturer in mathematics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London). She shared the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s 2025 [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) with her collaborator [Richard Thomas](wikipedia://en/33826774).

:::

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

Feyzbakhsh research follows a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) of Japanese mathematician [Shigeru Mukai](wikipedia://en/37801794), according to which any [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116) can be uniquely determined by a single curve within it. By bringing in notions from string theory, involving the stability of curves with respect to perturbations, she was able to "complete and generalize Mukai's program", and by relating the invariants of the surface to the invariants of the curve within it, she showed how to control the higher-rank [Donaldson–Thomas invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson–Thomas_invariant) of a surface by the [Gromov–Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/2301291) of the curve, and to control those in turn by the rank-zero Donaldson–Thomas invariants.

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Feyzbakhsh studied mathematics and electrical engineering as an undergraduate at [Ferdowsi University of Mashhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdowsi_University_of_Mashhad) in Iran, earning a double baccalaureate in 2013. After continuing her studies in a diploma program at the [International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), Italy, she went to the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) in Scotland for doctoral study in pure mathematics. She completed her Ph.D. in 2018 with the dissertation *Bridgeland stability conditions, stability of the restricted bundle, Brill-Noether theory and Mukai's program* supervised by [Arend Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arend_Bayer).

After postdoctoral research as a Chapman Fellow and EPSRC Postdoctoral Fellow at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) from 2018 to 2023, and as a Marie-Curie Fellow at [Paris-Saclay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_University) from 2021 to 2022, she became a senior lecturer and Royal Society university research fellow at Imperial College in 2024.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Feyzbakhsh was a 2023 recipient of the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), "for her spectacular applications of wall-crossing techniques to questions in classical and enumerative algebraic geometry". She was a 2024 recipient of the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110) of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and of the Boris Dubrovin medal of the [International School for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_for_Advanced_Studies) in Trieste, "for her impressive results in algebraic geometry, with relevant implications for mathematical physics, in particular string theory". For 2025 she was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075), shared with [Richard Thomas](wikipedia://en/33826774).[^o7]
