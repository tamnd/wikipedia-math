# Swiss Society of Economics and Statistics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Swiss Society of Economics and Statistics"}

The **Swiss Society of Economics and Statistics** (SSES) (Schweizerische Gesellschaft für Volkswirtschaft und Statistik (SGVS), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Société suisse d'économie et de statistique* (SSES); [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language): *Società svizzera di economia e di statistica* (SSES)) is a Swiss [learned society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_society) for [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and an association comprising all Swiss universities with faculties of economics,[^o2] based in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich).[^o3] Kurt Schmidheiny from the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel) serves as the current president of the SSES.[^o4] The society is a member of the Swiss Academy of Humanities and Social Sciences.[^o5] The SSES holds an ex officio position on the executive council of the [Verein für Socialpolitik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verein_für_Socialpolitik).[^o6] The society publishes the *[Swiss Journal of Economics and Statistics](wikipedia://en/77195746)*, an [open-access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_access) [peer-reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review) [academic journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_journal).[^o7]

:::

## History {#History}

The society was founded in 1864[^o9] as the Swiss Statistical Society ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Schweizerische Statistische Gesellschaft*, (*SSG*)).[^o11] In 1937, it changed its name to the Swiss Society for Statistics and Economics (Schweizerische Gesellschaft für Statistik und Volkswirtschaft, (*SGSV*)).[^o10] In 2001, the name was slightly altered to the Swiss Society of Economics and Statistics ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Schweizerische Gesellschaft für Volkswirtschaft und Statistik*, (*SGVS*)).[^o11][^o12]

The topics discussed at the annual meetings of the SSES are regularly covered in Swiss newspapers.[^o13] These meetings attract figures in municipal, [cantonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_Switzerland), and [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_administration_of_Switzerland) in Switzerland, alongside economists and scientists from Switzerland and abroad.[^o14] Past topics have included the [industrialisation of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation_in_Switzerland),[^o15] [full employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_employment) and the [right to work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_work),[^o16][^o17] [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466) and the social position of women,[^o18] [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition),[^o14] [dirigisme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirigisme) and [regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_economics),[^o19][^o20] and the [European debt crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_debt_crisis).[^o21]

The SSES gives several annual awards,[^o22] including an Economic Journalism Award for [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist).[^o23][^o24]
