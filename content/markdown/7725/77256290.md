# Stef Tijs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stef Tijs"}

**Stef Tijs** (August 31, 1937 – June 13, 2023), or **Stephanus Hendrikus Tjis**[^o2] was a Dutch mathematician and a [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) pioneer in [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Netherlands).[^o3][^o4] He contributed to most subfields in game theory and particularly in [cooperative game theory](wikipedia://en/657958), where he introduced the Tijs value (later known as the τ value) in 1981 as a solution to [n-person games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-player_game) alternative to the [Shapley value](wikipedia://en/198965) and others.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Tijs was born in [Ginneken en Bavel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginneken_en_Bavel).[^o2] He studied at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) for a bachelor's degree in chemistry from 1954 to 1959, later he switched to mathematics, obtaining a MSc degree at the same university in 1963. Tijs was a research assistant in mathematics at [Radboud University Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen) from 1963 to 1969 and an assistant professor in mathematics there from 1969. He received his PhD in mathematics for work on [matrix games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_game) from the [Radboud University Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen) under the supervision of Arnoud van Rooij and [Freddy Delbaen](wikipedia://en/72871254) (who was younger than Tijs) in 1975.[^o9] He became an associate professor at Nijmegen in 1975 and gradually built up a Dutch school of game theory there with an international outlook. In 1982, Tijs initiated the game theory seminar in Nijmegen, which later prompted the Netherlands to join the European Meeting on Game Theory (also called SING) that is held annually from 2000.[^o10] Tijs was promoted to full professor at [Radboud University Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen) in 1985. In 1991, he moved to [Tilburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilburg_University), where he became a professor at the Department of Econometrics and Operations Research and the Center of Economic Research. He stayed for the remainder of his career. Between 2003 and 2005, Tijs was also a professor of mathematics at [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa) in Italy.[^o11][^o4]

Tijs received an honorary doctorate from the [Miguel Hernández University of Elche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Hernández_University_of_Elche) in 2000.[^o12] He was a fellow and a council member of the [Game Theory Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Theory_Society).[^o13] He became a Knight of the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion) in 2003.[^o4]
