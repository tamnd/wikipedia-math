# Johnson graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johnson graph"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Johnson graphs** are a special class of [undirected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) defined from systems of sets. The vertices of the Johnson graph $J(n,k)$ are the $k$-element [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of an $n$-element set; two vertices are adjacent when the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of the two vertices (subsets) contains $(k-1)$-elements.[^o3] Both Johnson graphs and the closely related [Johnson scheme](wikipedia://en/22649423) are named after [Selmer M. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selmer_M._Johnson).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

- Both $J(n,1)$ and $J(n,n-1)$ are the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{n}$.
- $J(4,2)$ is the [octahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_graph).[^o6]
- $J(5,2)$ is the [complement](wikipedia://en/1175649) of the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926),[^o3] hence the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of $K_{5}$. More generally, for all $n$, the Johnson graph $J(n,2)$ is the line graph of $K_{n}$ and the complement of the [Kneser graph](wikipedia://en/2724154) $K(n,2).$

:::

## Graph-theoretic properties {#Graph-theoretic_properties}

- $J(n,k)$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to $J(n,n-k).$
- For all $0 \leq j \leq \operatorname{diam}(J(n,k))$, any pair of vertices at distance $j$ share $k-j$ elements in common.
- $J(n,k)$ is [Hamilton-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton-connected), meaning that every pair of vertices forms the endpoints of a [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437) in the graph. In particular this means that it has a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle).[^o8]
- It is also known that the Johnson graph $J(n,k)$ is $k(n-k)$-vertex-connected.[^o9]
- $J(n,k)$ forms the [vertex-edge graph](wikipedia://en/74718534) of an (*n* − 1)-dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471), called a [hypersimplex](wikipedia://en/44749312).[^o10]
- Any [maximal clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_clique) is either of the form $\{ S \cup \{x \} \mid x \in \{1, \dots, n\} \setminus S \}$ for a $(k - 1)$-element subset $S$ and $k < n - 1$, or of the form $\{S \setminus \{x\} \mid x \in S\}$ for a $(k + 1)$-element set $S$ for $k > 1$, or of the form $\{\{1\}, \{2\}\}$ in the edge case $(n, k) = (2, 1)$.[^o11]
- The [clique number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_number) of $J(n,k)$ is given by an expression in terms of its least and greatest [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue): $\omega(J(n,k)) = 1 - \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$, or, by the above explicit description of maximal cliques, $\omega(J(n,k)) = \max\{k + 1, n - k + 1\}.$
- The [clique cover number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_cover_number) of $J(n, k)$ satisfies $\theta(J(n, 1)) = 1$ for $n > 1$, $\theta(J(n, 2)) = n - 2$ for $n > 2$ and $\theta(J(n, 3)) = \lfloor (n-1)^2/4 \rfloor$ for $n > 5$ but is not known in general.[^o12]
- The [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of $J(n,k)$ is at most $n, \chi(J(n,k)) \leq n.$[^o13]
- Each Johnson graph is *locally grid*, meaning that the [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) of the [neighbors](wikipedia://en/7309251) of any vertex is a [rook's graph](wikipedia://en/7324959). More precisely, in the Johnson graph $J(n,k)$, each neighborhood is a $k\times (n-k)$ rook's graph.[^o14]

## Automorphism group {#Automorphism_group}

There is a [distance-transitive](wikipedia://en/7768222) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $\operatorname{Aut}(J(n,k))$ [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to $\operatorname{Sym}(n)$. In fact, $\operatorname{Aut}(J(n,k)) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(n)$, except that when $n = 2k \geq 4$, $\operatorname{Aut}(J(n,k)) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(n) \times C_2$.[^o16]

## Intersection array {#Intersection_array}

As a consequence of being distance-transitive, $J(n,k)$ is also [distance-regular](wikipedia://en/7768943). Letting $d$ denote its [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341), the intersection array of $J(n,k)$ is given by

$$\left\{ b_{0}, \ldots, b_{d-1}, c_{1}, \ldots c_{d} \right \}$$

where:

$$\begin{aligned} b_{j} &= (k - j)(n - k - j) && 0 \leq j < d \\ c_{j} &= j^2 && 0 < j \leq d \end{aligned}$$

It turns out that unless $J(n,k)$ is $J(8,2)$, its intersection array is not shared with any other distinct distance-regular graph; the intersection array of $J(8,2)$ is shared with three other distance-regular graphs that are not Johnson graphs.[^o3]

## Eigenvalues and eigenvectors {#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors}

- The characteristic polynomial of $J(n,k)$ is given by

$$\phi(x) := \prod_{j=0}^{\operatorname{diam}(J(n,k))} \left (x-A_{n,k}(j)\right )^{\binom{n}{j} - \binom{n}{j-1}}.$$

where $A_{n,k}(j) = (k-j)(n-k-j)-j.$[^o16]

- The [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) of $J(n,k)$ have an explicit description.[^o19]

## Johnson scheme {#Johnson_scheme}

The Johnson graph $J(n,k)$ is closely related to the [Johnson scheme](wikipedia://en/22649423), an [association scheme](wikipedia://en/2238152) in which each pair of $k$-element sets is associated with a number, half the size of the [symmetric difference](wikipedia://en/262288) of the two sets.[^o21] The Johnson graph has an edge for every pair of sets at distance one in the association scheme, and the distances in the association scheme are exactly the [shortest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_path) distances in the Johnson graph.[^o22]

The Johnson scheme is also related to another family of distance-transitive graphs, the [odd graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_graph), whose vertices are $k$-element subsets of an $(2k+1)$-element set and whose edges correspond to [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) pairs of subsets.[^o21]

::: {#o24 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="Open problems"}

## Open problems {#Open_problems}

The vertex-expansion properties of Johnson graphs, as well as the structure of the corresponding extremal sets of vertices of a given size, are not fully understood. However, an asymptotically tight lower bound on expansion of large sets of vertices was recently obtained.[^o25]

In general, determining the chromatic number of a Johnson graph is an [open problem](wikipedia://en/1333335).[^o26][^o27]

:::
