# Absolute risk

*Image: Illustration of risk reduction.svg.* The group exposed to treatment (left) has 1/4 or 25% absolute risk of an adverse outcome (dark). The unexposed group (right) has 1/2 or 50% absolute risk of an adverse outcome.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Absolute risk"}

**Absolute risk** (or AR) is the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) or chance of an [event](wikipedia://en/22961). It is usually used for the number of events (such as a disease) that occurred in a group, divided by the number of people in that group.[^o2]

:::

Absolute risk is one of the most understandable ways of communicating health risks to the general public.[^o3]

In difference to absolute risk, the [relative risk](wikipedia://en/2622159) (RR) is the ratio of the probability of an [outcome](wikipedia://en/39778851) in an exposed group to the probability of the outcome in an unexposed group.
