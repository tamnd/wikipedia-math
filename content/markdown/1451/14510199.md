# James R. Jackson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James R. Jackson"}

**James Richard "Jim" Jackson** (May 16, 1924 – March 20, 2011) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), well known for his contribution to [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578).

:::

Jackson was born in [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver) and raised in [Beverly Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills).[^o4] He served in the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). After his service, he earned an [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1946, an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1950 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1952, with a thesis titled *Abstract Function Spaces and Their Homotopy Theory.*[^o3] All of these degrees were from [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) where Jackson remained in the [School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Anderson_School_of_Management) for his career until his retirement in 1985.[^o5]

While at [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) he developed the [Jackson's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson's_theorem_%28queueing_theory%29) and some of the first models that could predict the performance of networks with several nodes. Jackson's work was inspired by his experience in the [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles) aircraft industry,[^o6] but the results found applications in the design of computers, manufacturing and the then emerging [packet switched networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packet_switched_network), such as those undertaken by [Leonard Kleinrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Kleinrock) in 1961.[^o7]

He spent his retirement in [Tehachapi, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehachapi,_California).[^o4]
