# Spectral risk measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spectral risk measure"}

A **Spectral risk measure** is a [risk measure](wikipedia://en/12498127) given as a [weighted average](wikipedia://en/189822) of outcomes where bad outcomes are, typically, included with larger weights. A spectral risk measure is a function of [portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_%28finance%29) returns and outputs the amount of the [numeraire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeraire) (typically a [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency)) to be kept in reserve. A spectral risk measure is always a [coherent risk measure](wikipedia://en/5235067), but the converse does not always hold. An advantage of spectral measures is the way in which they can be related to [risk aversion](wikipedia://en/177700), and particularly to a [utility function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_function), through the weights given to the possible portfolio returns.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider a [portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_%28finance%29) $X$ (denoting the portfolio payoff). Then a spectral risk measure $M_{\phi}: \mathcal{L} \to \mathbb{R}$ where $\phi$ is non-negative, non-increasing, [right-continuous](wikipedia://en/6122#Directional_and_semi-continuity), integrable function defined on $[0,1]$ such that $\int_0^1 \phi(p)dp = 1$ is defined by

$$M_{\phi}(X) = -\int_0^1 \phi(p) F_X^{-1}(p) dp$$

where $F_X$ is the [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) for *X*.[^o9][^o5]

If there are $S$ equiprobable outcomes with the corresponding payoffs given by the [order statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_statistics) $X_{1:S}, ... X_{S:S}$. Let $\phi\in\mathbb{R}^S$. The measure $M_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^S\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ defined by $M_{\phi}(X)=-\delta\sum_{s=1}^S\phi_sX_{s:S}$ is a **spectral measure of risk** if $\phi\in\mathbb{R}^S$ satisfies the conditions

1. Nonnegativity: $\phi_s\geq0$ for all $s=1, \dots, S$,
1. Normalization: $\sum_{s=1}^S\phi_s=1$,
1. Monotonicity : $\phi_s$ is non-increasing, that is $\phi_{s_1}\geq\phi_{s_2}$ if ${s_1}<{s_2}$ and ${s_1}, {s_2}\in\{1,\dots,S\}$.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Spectral risk measures are also [coherent](wikipedia://en/5235067). Every spectral risk measure $\rho: \mathcal{L} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies:

1. Positive Homogeneity: for every portfolio *X* and positive value $\lambda > 0$, $\rho(\lambda X) = \lambda \rho(X)$;
1. Translation-Invariance: for every portfolio *X* and $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, $\rho(X + a) = \rho(X) - a$;
1. Monotonicity: for all portfolios *X* and *Y* such that $X \geq Y$, $\rho(X) \leq \rho(Y)$;
1. Sub-additivity: for all portfolios *X* and *Y*, $\rho(X+Y) \leq \rho(X) + \rho(Y)$;
1. Law-Invariance: for all portfolios *X* and *Y* with [cumulative distribution functions](wikipedia://en/5793) $F_X$ and $F_Y$ respectively, if $F_X = F_Y$ then $\rho(X) = \rho(Y)$;
1. Comonotonic Additivity: for every [comonotonic](wikipedia://en/21621572) random variables *X* and *Y*, $\rho(X+Y) = \rho(X) + \rho(Y)$. Note that *X* and *Y* are comonotonic if for every $\omega_1,\omega_2 \in \Omega: \; (X(\omega_2) - X(\omega_1))(Y(\omega_2) - Y(\omega_1)) \geq 0$.[^o9]

In some texts the input *X* is interpreted as losses rather than payoff of a portfolio. In this case, the translation-invariance property would be given by $\rho(X+a) = \rho(X) + a$, and the monotonicity property by $X \geq Y \implies \rho(X) \geq \rho(Y)$ instead of the above.

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [expected shortfall](wikipedia://en/8307819) is a spectral measure of risk.
- The [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) is *trivially* a spectral measure of risk.
