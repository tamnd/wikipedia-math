# Set-valued function

*Image: Multivalued\_function.svg.* This diagram represents a multi-valued, but not a proper (single-valued) [function](wikipedia://en/185427), because the element 3 in *X* is associated with two elements, *b* and *c*, in *Y*.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Set-valued function"}

A **set-valued function**, also called a **correspondence** or **set-valued [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558)**, is a mathematical [function](wikipedia://en/185427) that maps elements from one set, the [domain of the function](wikipedia://en/50263), to subsets of another set.[^o2][^o3] Set-valued functions are used in a variety of mathematical fields, including [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) and [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924).

:::

Set-valued functions are also known as [multivalued functions](wikipedia://en/222367) in some references,[^o4] but this article and the article [Multivalued function](wikipedia://en/222367) follow the authors who make a distinction.

## Distinction from multivalued functions {#Distinction_from_multivalued_functions}

*Image: Multivalued\_functions\_illustration.svg.* Illustration distinguishing multivalued functions from set-valued relations according to the criterion in page 29 of *New Developments in Contact Problems* by Wriggers and Panatiotopoulos (2014).

Although other authors may distinguish them differently (or not at all), Wriggers and Panatiotopoulos (2014) distinguish multivalued functions from set-valued functions (which they called *set-valued relations*) by the fact that multivalued functions only take multiple values at finitely (or denumerably) many points, and otherwise behave like a [function](wikipedia://en/185427).[^o3] Geometrically, this means that the graph of a multivalued function is necessarily a line of zero area that doesn't loop, while the graph of a set-valued relation may contain solid filled areas or loops.[^o3]

Alternatively, a [multivalued function](wikipedia://en/222367) is a set-valued function $f$ that has a further [continuity](wikipedia://en/6122) property, namely that the choice of an element in the set $f(x)$ defines a corresponding element in each set $f(y)$ for $y$ close to $x$, and thus defines [locally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally) an ordinary function.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The [argmax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argmax) of a function is in general, multivalued. For example, $\operatorname{argmax}_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \cos(x) = \{2 \pi k\mid k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.

:::

## Set-valued analysis {#Set-valued_analysis}

**Set-valued analysis** is the study of sets in the spirit of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649).

Instead of considering collections of only points, set-valued analysis considers collections of sets. If a collection of sets is endowed with a topology, or inherits an appropriate topology from an underlying topological space, then the convergence of sets can be studied.

Much of set-valued analysis arose through the study of [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255) and [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), partly as a generalization of [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201); the term "[variational analysis](wikipedia://en/40237498)" is used by authors such as [R. Tyrrell Rockafellar](wikipedia://en/26300644) and [Roger J-B Wets](wikipedia://en/26298695), [Jonathan Borwein](wikipedia://en/1145010) and [Adrian Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Lewis), and [Boris Mordukhovich](wikipedia://en/19668679). In optimization theory, the convergence of approximating [subdifferentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdifferential) to a subdifferential is important in understanding necessary or sufficient conditions for any minimizing point.

There exist set-valued extensions of the following concepts from point-valued analysis: [continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28mathematics%29), [differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiation_%28mathematics%29), [integration](wikipedia://en/15532),[^o9] [implicit function theorem](wikipedia://en/581005), [contraction mappings](wikipedia://en/6239), [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), [fixed-point theorems](wikipedia://en/1011848),[^o10] [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29), and [topological degree theory](wikipedia://en/18203720). In particular, [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) are generalized to [inclusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29), while differential equations are generalized to [differential inclusions](wikipedia://en/13725281).

One can distinguish multiple concepts generalizing [continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28mathematics%29), such as the [closed graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_graph) property and [upper and lower hemicontinuity](wikipedia://en/2945778). There are also various generalizations of [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) to multifunctions.

::: {#o12 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Set-valued functions arise in [optimal control theory](wikipedia://en/362565), especially [differential inclusions](wikipedia://en/13725281) and related subjects as [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), where the [Kakutani fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/2658571) for set-valued functions has been applied to prove existence of [Nash equilibria](wikipedia://en/45337). This among many other properties loosely associated with approximability of upper hemicontinuous multifunctions via continuous functions explains why upper hemicontinuity is more preferred than lower hemicontinuity.

Nevertheless, lower semi-continuous multifunctions usually possess continuous selections as stated in the [Michael selection theorem](wikipedia://en/17970233), which provides another characterisation of [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) spaces.[^o13][^o14] Other selection theorems, like Bressan-Colombo directional continuous selection, [Kuratowski and Ryll-Nardzewski measurable selection theorem](wikipedia://en/46310740), Aumann measurable selection, and Fryszkowski selection for decomposable maps are important in [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565) and the theory of [differential inclusions](wikipedia://en/13725281).

:::
