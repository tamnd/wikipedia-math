# Chief series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chief series"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **chief series** is a maximal [normal series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_series) for a [group](wikipedia://en/19447).

:::

It is similar to a [composition series](wikipedia://en/297493), though the two concepts are distinct in general: a chief series is a maximal *normal* series, while a composition series is a maximal *[subnormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnormal_series)* series.

Chief series can be thought of as breaking the group down into less complicated pieces, which may be used to characterize various qualities of the group.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A chief series is a maximal normal series for a group. Equivalently, a chief series is a composition series of the group *G* under the action of [inner automorphisms](wikipedia://en/61839).

In detail, if *G* is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447), then a **chief series** of *G* is a finite collection of [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918) *N*<sub>*i*</sub> ⊆ *G*,

$$1=N_0\subseteq N_1\subseteq N_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq N_n=G,$$

such that each [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) *N*<sub>*i*+1</sub>/*N*<sub>*i*</sub>, for *i* = 1, 2,..., *n* − 1, is a [minimal normal subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_normal_subgroup) of *G*/*N*<sub>*i*</sub>. Equivalently, there does not exist any subgroup *A* normal in *G* such that *N*<sub>*i*</sub> \< *A* \< *N*<sub>*i*+1</sub> for any *i*. In other words, a chief series may be thought of as "full" in the sense that no normal subgroup of *G* may be added to it.

The factor groups *N*<sub>*i*+1</sub>/*N*<sub>*i*</sub> in a chief series are called the **chief factors** of the series. Unlike [composition factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_factor), chief factors are not necessarily [simple](wikipedia://en/47600). That is, there may exist a subgroup *A* normal in *N*<sub>*i*+1</sub> with *N*<sub>*i*</sub> \< *A* \< *N*<sub>*i*+1</sub>, but *A* is not normal in *G*. However, the chief factors are always [characteristically simple](wikipedia://en/4966729), that is, they have no proper nontrivial [characteristic subgroups](wikipedia://en/7198). In particular, a finite chief factor is a [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of isomorphic simple groups.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Existence {#Existence}

Finite groups always have a chief series, though infinite groups need not have a chief series. For example, the group of integers **Z** with addition as the operation does not have a chief series. To see this, note **Z** is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) and [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974), and so all of its subgroups are normal and cyclic as well. Supposing there exists a chief series *N*<sub>*i*</sub> leads to an immediate contradiction: *N*<sub>1</sub> is cyclic and thus is generated by some integer *a*, however the subgroup generated by 2*a* is a nontrivial normal subgroup properly contained in *N*<sub>1</sub>, contradicting the definition of a chief series.

### Uniqueness {#Uniqueness}

When a chief series for a group exists, it is generally not unique. However, a form of the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan–Hölder_theorem) states that the chief factors of a group are unique up to isomorphism, independent of the particular chief series they are constructed from[^o8] In particular, the number of chief factors is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) of the group *G*, as well as the [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of the chief factors and their multiplicities.

### Other properties {#Other_properties}

In abelian groups, chief series and composition series are identical, as all subgroups are normal.

Given any normal subgroup *N* ⊆ *G*, one can always find a chief series in which *N* is one of the elements (assuming a chief series for *G* exists in the first place.) Also, if *G* has a chief series and *N* is normal in *G*, then both *N* and *G*/*N* have chief series. The converse also holds: if *N* is normal in *G* and both *N* and *G*/*N* have chief series, *G* has a chief series as well.

:::
