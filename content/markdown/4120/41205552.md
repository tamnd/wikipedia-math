# Caius Iacob

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Caius Iacob"}

**Caius Iacob** (March 29, 1912 – February 6, 1992) was a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) mathematician, professor at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), and titular member of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy). After the [fall of communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution) in 1989, he was elected to the [Senate of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Arad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arad,_Romania), the son of Lazăr Iacob and Camelia, née Moldovan. His father was professor of Canon Law, and served as delegate for Arad at the [Great National Assembly of Alba Iulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_National_Assembly_of_Alba_Iulia) of 1 December 1918.[^o6][^o7] Caius Iacob attended the [Moise Nicoară High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moise_Nicoară_National_College) in his native city, and then completed his secondary education at the [Emanuil Gojdu High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuil_Gojdu_National_College) in [Oradea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oradea). After passing his [baccalaureate examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) with the highest mark in the nation, he was admitted in 1928 at the Faculty of Sciences of the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), from where he graduated in 1931, aged nineteen.[^o6][^o8] Iacob continued his studies at the Faculty of Sciences of the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), with thesis advisor [Henri Villat](wikipedia://en/32126284). He defended his thesis, *Sur la détermination des fonctions harmoniques par certaines conditions aux limites: applications à l'hydrodynamique*, on 24 June 1935.[^o6]

His most important work was in the studies of classical hydrodynamics, fluid mechanics, mathematical analysis, and compressible-flow theory.

Iacob started his academic career in 1935 at [Politehnica University of Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politehnica_University_of_Timișoara), after which he became a professor at the University of Bucharest and at [Babeș-Bolyai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University) in [Cluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca). In 1955, he was elected a corresponding [member of the Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy), becoming a titular member in 1963.[^o10] From 1980 to the end of his life he served as President of the Mathematics section of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy).[^o6][^o8]

He was awarded several prizes for his work: the Henri de Parville Prize by the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) (1940),[^o6][^o8] the State Prize of the Romanian People's Republic (1952),[^o6] and the [Order of the Star of the Romanian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Star_of_the_Romanian_Socialist_Republic), 3rd class (1964).[^o11]

In May 1990, he was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Romanian_general_election) [senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania) for the [Christian Democratic National Peasants' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_National_Peasants'_Party) — the only member of the party to be elected to the upper chamber of the [Parliament of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Romania) that year.[^o12] He died in Bucharest in February 1992.[^o6][^o8]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Iacob was one of the founders of the Institute of Applied Mathematics of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy) in 1991. Ten years later, the institute merged with the Center for Mathematical Statistics of the academy (that had been founded by [Gheorghe Mihoc](wikipedia://en/3421581) in 1964), becoming the current Gheorghe Mihoc–Caius Iacob Institute of Mathematical Statistics and Applied Mathematics of the Romanian Academy.[^o14]

A high school[^o15] and a middle school,[^o16] as well as a street and a plaza in Arad also bear his name.
