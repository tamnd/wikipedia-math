# Mary Perry Smith

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mary Perry Smith"}

**Mary Perry Smith** (May 29, 1926 – August 10, 2015) was an American mathematics educator who cofounded the [Mathematics, Engineering, Science Achievement](wikipedia://en/1648182) program and the [Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Filmmakers_Hall_of_Fame).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Perry Smith was born on May 29, 1926, and was originally from [Evansville, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana), one of six children of a minister in the [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church); her maternal grandfather, Henry Allen Perry had been a chaplain and mathematics teacher at the [Tuskegee Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuskegee_Institute), where her parents met. As a child she moved frequently, to [Kokomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokomo,_Indiana), [Logansport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logansport,_Indiana), [Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson,_Indiana), [Crawfordsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawfordsville,_Indiana), and [Frankfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort,_Indiana), all in Indiana. She earned a bachelor's degree from [Ball State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_State_University) in mathematics and science in three years, as one of a small number of African-American students there, and continued at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) for a master's degree in counseling and guidance (with minors in biochemistry and statistics), finishing in 1948.

## Later life and career {#Later_life_and_career}

Unable to find a job because of the discrimination in teacher hiring in Indiana, she followed her older brother to the newly opened [Texas State University for Negroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Southern_University) in Houston, where she taught mathematics for three years.

After marrying [Norvel L. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norvel_L._Smith) (later to become the president of [Merritt College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merritt_College) and the first African American head of a college in California) and moving with her husband to [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California), she joined a doctoral program in educational psychology at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), but moved to part-time study and then stopped out to become a teacher, first in a junior high school in San Francisco from 1953 until 1961, and then at [Oakland Technical High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Technical_High_School), where she taught geometry for 17 years.

## Service {#Service}

In 1969, Perry Smith cofounded the [Mathematics, Engineering, Science Achievement](wikipedia://en/1648182) (MESA) program for under-privileged pre-college students in California. In 1974, she cofounded the [Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Filmmakers_Hall_of_Fame) in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California), initially as part of the Oakland Museum’s Cultural and Ethnics Affairs Guild and, in 1978, as a separate organization; she also served as its president. She left her teaching position to work for the MESA program in 1977, as statewide program director, and also served on the board of the [Oakland Museum of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Museum_of_California). She died in 2015.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Her papers are kept in the [Bancroft Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bancroft_Library) of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).
