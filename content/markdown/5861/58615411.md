# Turing's method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Turing's method"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Turing's method** is used to verify that for any given [Gram point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_point) $g_{m}$ there lie *m* + 1 zeros of $\zeta(s)$, in the region $0 < \operatorname{Im}(s) < \operatorname{Im}(g_{m})$, where $\zeta(s)$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).[^o2] It was discovered by [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) and published in 1953,[^o3] although that proof contained errors and a correction was published in 1970 by R. Sherman Lehman.[^o4]

:::

For every integer *i* with $i < n$ we find a list of Gram points $\{g_i \mid 0\leqslant i \leqslant m \}$ and a complementary list $\{h_i \mid 0\leqslant i \leqslant m \}$, where $g_{i}$ is the smallest number such that

$$(-1)^i Z(g_i + h_i) > 0,$$

where *Z*(*t*) is the Hardy [Z function](wikipedia://en/1415146). Note that $g_{i}$ may be negative or zero. Assuming that $h_m = 0$ and there exists some integer *k* such that $h_k = 0$, then if

$$1 + \frac{1.91 + 0.114\log(g_{m+k}/2\pi) + \sum_{j=m+1}^{m+k-1}h_j}{g_{m+k} - g_m} < 2,$$

and

$$-1 - \frac{1.91 + 0.114\log(g_m/2\pi) + \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}h_{m-j}}{g_m - g_{m-k}} > -2,$$

Then the bound is achieved and we have that there are exactly *m* + 1 zeros of $\zeta(s)$, in the region $0 < \operatorname{Im}(s) < \operatorname{Im}(g_{m})$.
