# Wada Nei

Wada

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wada Nei"}

**Wada Yenzō Nei**, also known as **Wada Yasushi**, was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) in the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period). His birth name was Kōyama Naoaki; but he changed his name to Wada Nei, by which he became more widely known.[^o2]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Wada studied under the patronage of the hereditary Chief court calendar-maker[^o4] in the court of the emperor.[^o5]

Wada became a student of [Kusaka Sei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusaka_Sei), who had been a student of [Ajima Chokuyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajima_Chokuyen). Wada extended [Ajima Chokuyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajima_Chokuyen)'s development of an integral calculus within the [Enri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enri) (円理, "circle principle") context. He worked on the computation of [minimum and maximum values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima) (roughly by [equating](wikipedia://en/9284) the first [derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivate) to 0) and gave reasoning and insight to the computation method that was given without explanation by [Seki Takakazu](wikipedia://en/532653) about 100 years earlier. He was also the first Japanese mathematician to study [roulettes](wikipedia://en/846500).
