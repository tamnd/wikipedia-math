# Frank Bonsall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Bonsall"}

**Frank Featherstone Bonsall** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o2] (31 March 1920, [Crouch End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crouch_End), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) – 22 February 2011,[^o3] [Harrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrogate)) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o4]

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Bonsall was born on 31 March 1920, the youngest son of Wilfred C Bonsall and Sarah Frank. His older brother was [Arthur Bonsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Bonsall). He married Gillian Patrick, a [Somerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_College,_Oxford) graduate, in 1947.[^o8] Bonsall and his wife were keen hill-walkers.[^o2] He wrote two articles for The Scottish Mountaineering Club on the definition of a [Munro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munro). After his retirement, Bonsall and his wife moved to Harrogate.

## Career {#Career}

Bonsall graduated from [Bishop's Stortford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop's_Stortford_College) in 1938, and studied at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford).[^o8] He served in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), in the [Corps of Royal Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_of_Royal_Engineers), and in India from 1944 to 1946.[^o9]

He lectured at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) from 1947 to 1948; was visiting associate professor at [Oklahoma State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_State_University) from 1950 to 1951; taught at [Newcastle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University), with [Werner Wolfgang Rogosinski](wikipedia://en/33763420) in the 1950s. He taught at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), from 1963 to 1984.[^o10] In 1963, a second chair in Mathematics was established (the Maclaurin chair). Bonsall took up the chair in 1965, but spent the following year as a visiting professor at Yale.[^o6] In 1966, he was awarded the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097)'s [Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prize).

Despite not himself having a PhD, Bonsall supervised many PhD candidates who knew him affectionately as "FFB".
