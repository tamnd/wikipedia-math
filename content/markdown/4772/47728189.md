# Gerrit Lekkerkerker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerrit Lekkerkerker"}

*Image: Lekkerkerker.jpg.* Gerrit Lekkerkerker in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), 1987

**Cornelis Gerrit Lekkerkerker** ([Harmelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmelen), 7 February 1922 – 24 July 1999) was a Dutch mathematician.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Lekkerkerker studied mathematics at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) during the periods 1940-1943 and 1945-1949 under [Jurjen Koksma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurjen_Koksma) and [Jan Popken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Popken). After completing his studies in 1949, he started work at the Amsterdam [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063) (National Research Institute for Mathematics and Computer Science), where he worked under Koksma in the [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) division. In the academic year 1953-1954, he studied in Rome.[^o3] In 1955 he received his doctorate under the guidance of Popken with the thesis *On the Zeros of a Class of [Dirichlet-Series](wikipedia://en/393258)*; in [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Over de nulpunten in een klasse van Dirichletreeksen*.

Beginning in 1961, he was a professor at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), succeeding [Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn](wikipedia://en/3166399). He was the director of the Mathematical Institute during the student protests of 1969-1973. In 1984, although he had not yet reached [retirement age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retirement_age), he resigned his professorship over differences in the teaching of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), feeling that his [academic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_freedom) was under threat.[^o3]

He was a co-editor of *Mededelingen van het Wiskundig Genootschap* (correspondence of the Mathematical Society).[^o3]

## Research {#Research}

Lekkerkerker worked on [analytic](wikipedia://en/251513) and [geometric](wikipedia://en/250153) number theory. He wrote the standard work *Geometry of Numbers*, first published in 1969. Later he worked on topics in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). In the 1970s, he turned his attention towards the mathematical treatment of topics in [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics) (e.g. [neutron transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_transport)) and [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics) (e.g. radiation transport within stars). Afterwards he worked with Rutger Hangelbroek, who did his doctorate under Lekkerkerker in 1973 at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen), and Hans Kaper of the [Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory). He also contributed to topics in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).[^o3]

After retiring, he remained active in mathematics: for example, he brought out a second edition of *Geometry of Numbers* along with Peter Grüber. This field now found new applications in [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869).[^o3]

While [Zeckendorf's theorem](wikipedia://en/1377405) on the representation of integers by sums of non-consecutive [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) is named after a paper by [Edouard Zeckendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edouard_Zeckendorf) in 1972, the same result had been published 20 years earlier by Lekkerkerker.[^o6]

## Works {#Works}

- 1969: *Geometry of Numbers*, Noord-Holland, 2nd edition 1987 with [Peter M. Gruber](wikipedia://en/35098363), 2008 translated to [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), Nauka, Moscow
