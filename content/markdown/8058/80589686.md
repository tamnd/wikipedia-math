# Pythagorean hodograph curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pythagorean hodograph curve"}

In mathematics, a **Pythagorean hodograph curve** or **PH curve** is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) defined by a polynomial [parametric equation](wikipedia://en/576108) for which the [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed) (the derivative of [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401)) also has a polynomial parametric equation.[^n1] This allows the arc length itself to be determined by integrating the speed. Additionally, dividing by the speed gives a rational parameterization of the [unit normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_normal) to the curve, and of the [parallel curves](wikipedia://en/912825) to the given curves. For these reasons, researchers have investigated the use of Pythagorean hodograph curves as [splines](wikipedia://en/457680) in [geometric design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_design).

:::

Here, "hodograph" is another word for a [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921).[^n2] They are called Pythagorean hodograph curves because their derivatives obey an equation analogous to the equation in the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034).

## Plane curves {#Plane_curves}

A [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) with polynomial parameterization $(x(t),y(t))$ is a Pythagorean hodograph curve when there exists a polynomial $\sigma(t)$ satisfying the equation of the Pythagorean theorem:[^n3]

$$\sigma(t)^2=x'(t)^2+y'(t)^2.$$

Here, $\sigma(t)$ is the speed traveled by a point that takes position $(x(t),y(t))$ at time $t$.[^n4]

### Real characterization {#Real_characterization}

The curves of this form can be generated by a formula analogous to a formula for generating [Pythagorean triples](wikipedia://en/24172). Let $u(t)$, $v(t)$, and $w(t)$ be any three polynomials, and set

$$\begin{aligned} x'(t)&=\bigl(u(t)^2-v(t)^2\bigr)w(t)\\ y'(t)&=2u(t)v(t)w(t)\\ \sigma(t)&=\bigl(u(t)^2+v(t)^2\bigr)w(t)\\ \end{aligned}$$

Then these three polynomials obey the Pythagorean equation defining a Pythagorean hodograph curve, and the parameterization $\bigl(x(t),y(t)\bigr)$ of the curve itself can be obtained by integrating $x'(t)$ and $y'(t)$. Every Pythagorean hodograph curve takes this form.[^n5]

### Complex characterization {#Complex_characterization}

A simpler alternative formulation of this characterization applies to the *regular* Pythagorean hodograph curves, those whose derivative never vanishes over the range of parameters of interest.[^n6] It uses the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), in which a curve may be described by a single parametric equation $r(t)$. In this plane, for every regular polynomial curve $r(t)$, the curve

$$\hat r(t)=\int r'(t)^2 dt$$

defines a regular Pythagorean hodograph curve, and every regular Pythagorean hodograph curve can be obtained in this way. Because this is an [indefinite integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_integral), it can be offset by an arbitrary constant, corresponding to an arbitrary translation of the given curve. Choosing this constant to make the curve start at the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) makes the correspondence between regular curves $r(t)$ and regular Pythagorean hodograph curves $\hat r(t)$ into a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942).[^n7]

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

A line, parameterized by choosing $x$ and $y$ to both be linear functions of a parameter $t$, is automatically a Pythagorean hodograph curve. Its speed is a constant, a degree-zero polynomial.[^n8]

There are no quadratic Pythagorean hodograph curves.[^n8]

*Image: CubiqueTschirnhausen.svg.* The [Tschirnhausen cubic](wikipedia://en/10475041)

The simplest nonlinear curves that are Pythagorean hodograph curves are [cubic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_curve). Not every cubic curve can be parameterized in this way. The cubic Pythagorean hodograph curves can be described as [Bézier curves](wikipedia://en/4664), defined by a sequence of control points $u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4$ for which $u_1u_2u_3$ and $u_2u_3u_4$ are [similar triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similar_triangle).[^n9] Alternatively, if these points are taken to belong to the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) with the differences between them defined as $\Delta_i=u_{i+1}-u_i$, then these differences must obey the equation $\Delta_1\Delta_3=\Delta_2^2$.[^n10] Every such curve is an arc of a scaled [Tschirnhausen cubic](wikipedia://en/10475041), and every arc of the Tschirnhausen cubic is a Pythagorean hodograph curve.[^n11]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

### Arc length {#Arc_length}

The only curve that can be parameterized by [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) with constant speed is a line.[^n12] In part for this reason, computing the lengths of many types of curve has been difficult, with closed forms known only for certain special curves.[^n13] Even as simple a curve as the [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) does not have a [closed-form expression](wikipedia://en/585143) for its perimeter, which can instead be expressed as an [elliptic integral](wikipedia://en/9960).[^n14] However, for Pythagorean hodograph curves, the arc length may be obtained in closed form by integrating the speed $\sigma(t)$.[^n15]

### Tangents, normals, and offsets {#Tangents,_normals,_and_offsets}

The unit [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) to a Pythagorean hodograph curve is obtained by dividing the parameterization to the curve by its speed, and the unit [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) is obtained by rotating the tangent vector by 90°. This gives a parameterization by [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) rather than by polynomials.[^n16] The [parallel curves](wikipedia://en/912825) of a Pythagorean hodograph curve may be obtained as linear combinations of the parameterizations of the given curve and its normal vector.[^n17]

:::

## Three dimensions {#Three_dimensions}

A three-dimensional [space curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve) with polynomial parameterization $\bigl(x(t),y(t),z(t)\bigr)$ is a Pythagorean hodograph curve when there exists a polynomial $\sigma(t)$ satisfying the equation:[^n18]

$$\sigma(t)^2=x'(t)^2+y'(t)^2+z'(t)^2.$$

These curves can be generated from polynomials over the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) analogously to the way plane Pythagorean hodograph curves can be generated from [complex polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_polynomial).[^n18]
