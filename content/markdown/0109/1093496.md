# Nassim Nicholas Taleb

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nassim Nicholas Taleb"}

**Nassim Nicholas Taleb** (; born 12 September 1960) is a [Lebanese-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese-American) New York University professor, essayist, [mathematical statistician](wikipedia://en/888711), former [option trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_trader), [risk analyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_analysis), and [aphorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphorist).[^o3][^o4] His work concerns problems of [randomness](wikipedia://en/19196523), [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity), and [uncertainty](wikipedia://en/63778).

:::

Taleb wrote ***Incerto***, a five-volume work on the nature of uncertainty published between 2001 and 2018 which includes the notable volumes *[The Black Swan](wikipedia://en/8561256)* and *[Antifragile](wikipedia://en/37758875)*. He has taught at several universities, serving as a Distinguished Professor of Risk Engineering at the [New York University Tandon School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Tandon_School_of_Engineering) since September 2008.[^o5][^o40] He has also been a practitioner of [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680) and is currently an adviser at [Universa Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universa_Investments). *[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times)* described his 2007 book *The Black Swan* as one of the 12 most influential books since [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).[^o6]

Taleb criticized risk management methods used by the finance industry and warned about [financial crises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_crises), subsequently profiting from 1987's [Black Monday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Monday_%281987%29), and later from the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^o7] He advocates what he calls a "black swan robust" society, meaning a society that can withstand difficult-to-predict events.[^o8] He proposes what he has termed "[antifragility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifragility)" in systems; that is, an ability to benefit and grow from a certain class of random events, errors, and volatility,[^o9][^o10] as well as "convex tinkering" as a method of scientific discovery, by which he means that decentralized [experimentation](wikipedia://en/59861) outperforms directed research.[^o11]

## Early life and family background {#Early_life_and_family_background}

*Image: Taleb student.png.* Taleb as a student

Taleb was born in [Amioun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amioun), Lebanon, to Minerva Ghosn and Nagib Taleb, an [oncologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncologist) and a researcher in [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology). His rich and influential parents were [Lebanese Greek Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Greek_Orthodox_Christians),[^o13] and had [French citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nationality_law). His maternal grandfather Q43475996 and great-grandfather Q43476186 were both [deputy prime ministers of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Lebanon) in the 1940s through the 1970s, and his four-times great grandfather was one of the [board of directors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon_Mutasarrifate#The_Protocol) to the administrator of [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon).[^o96] His paternal grandfather Nassim Taleb was a supreme court judge.[^o14][^o15] Taleb attended a French school in Beirut, the [Grand Lycée Franco-Libanais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Lycée_Franco-Libanais).[^o20][^o16] His family saw its political prominence and wealth reduced by the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War), which began in 1975.[^o17] He is a [Greek Orthodox Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Christian).[^o13]

## Education {#Education}

Taleb received Bachelor and Master of Science degrees from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris).[^o19] He holds an [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBA) from the [Wharton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School) at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (1983),[^o20][^o7] and a PhD in [management science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_Science) from the [University of Paris (Dauphine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University) (1998),[^o21] under the direction of [Hélyette Geman](wikipedia://en/38066732).[^o21] His dissertation focused on the mathematics of derivatives pricing.[^o21][^o22]

## Career {#Career}

### Finance {#Finance}

Taleb has been a practitioner of [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680)[^o25] as a [hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund) manager,[^o26][^o27] and a [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_%28finance%29) [trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trader_%28finance%29).[^o20][^o28] He has held the following positions:[^o29][^o30] managing director and [proprietary trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_trader) at [UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS_AG), currency trader at [Credit Suisse First Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Suisse_First_Boston), chief currency derivatives trader for [Banque Indosuez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banque_Indosuez), managing director and worldwide head of financial [option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_%28finance%29) [arbitrage](wikipedia://en/3162) at [CIBC Wood Gundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIBC_Wood_Gundy), derivatives arbitrage trader at [Bankers Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankers_Trust) (now [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank)), proprietary trader at [BNP Paribas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNP_Paribas), independent option market maker on the [Chicago Mercantile Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Mercantile_Exchange) and hedge fund manager for [Empirica Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirica_Capital).[^o31]

Taleb reportedly became [financially independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financially_independent) after the [crash of 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Monday_%281987%29) from his hedged short [Eurodollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurodollar) position while working as a trader for First Boston.[^o20] Next, Taleb pursued work toward his PhD in Paris, [completing the degree program in 1998](wikipedia://en/1093496#Education). He returned to New York City and [founded Empirica Capital in 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Spitznagel#Early_life_and_education). During the market downturn in 2000, at the end of the [dot com bubble and burst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot_com_bubble), Empirica's Empirica Kurtosis LLC fund was reported to have made a 56.86% return. Taleb's investing strategies continued to be highly successful during the [Nasdaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq) dive in 2000.[^o35] Several consecutive years of low market volatility and less spectacular returns followed, and Empirica closed in 2004.[^o31] In 2007, Taleb joined his former Empirica partner, [Mark Spitznagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Spitznagel),[^o31] as an adviser to [Universa Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universa_Investments), an asset management company based on the "black swan" idea, owned and managed by Spitznagel in Miami, Florida.[^o7]

Taleb attributed the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) to the mismatch between reality and statistical distributions used in finance. Taleb's investing approach produced significant returns once again, with some Universa funds returning 65% to 115% in October 2008.[^o7][^o32] In a 2007 *[Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Journal)* article, Taleb claimed he retired from trading and would be a full-time author.[^o33] He describes the nature of his involvement as "totally passive" from 2010 on.[^o29] The *Wall Street Journal* journalist Scott Patterson wrote that "Taleb's involvement with Universa made him fabulously wealthy, the cash from the fund far outdistancing the substantial profits from his bestsellers".[^o34]

Taleb considers himself less a businessman than an [epistemologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemologist) of [randomness](wikipedia://en/19196523), and says that he used trading to attain independence and freedom from authority.[^o35] He advocated for [tail risk hedging](wikipedia://en/7361848),[^o36] which is intended to mitigate investors' exposure to extreme market moves. Tail risk hedging safeguards investors by reaping rewards from rare events, thus Taleb's investment management career has included several jackpots followed by lengthy dry spells.[^o20][^o7]

At the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum) annual meeting in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos) in 2009, Taleb had harsh words for bankers, suggesting that bankers' recklessness will not be repeated "if you have punishment".[^o37][^o38]

### Academia {#Academia}

Taleb shifted his career emphasis to mathematical research in 2006. Since 2008, he has taught classes at [New York University Tandon School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Tandon_School_of_Engineering), as Distinguished Professor of Risk Engineering.[^o40][^o41] and was a Distinguished Research Scholar at the [Said Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Business_School) BT Center, [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) from 2009 to 2013.[^o42] Taleb also held positions at NYU's [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences), the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst), and the [London Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Business_School).

Taleb has been Co-Editor in Chief of the academic journal *Risk and Decision Analysis* since September 2014,[^o43] jointly teaches regular courses with [Paul Wilmott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wilmott) in London, and occasionally participates in teaching courses toward the [Certificate in Quantitative Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_certification_in_financial_services#Certificate_in_Quantitative_Finance).[^o44] He is also a faculty member of the [New England Complex Systems Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Complex_Systems_Institute).[^o45]

### Writing career {#Writing_career}

Taleb's first non-technical book, *[Fooled by Randomness](wikipedia://en/585779)*, about the underestimation of the role of randomness in life, published in 2001, was selected by [*Fortune*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_%28magazine%29) as one of the smartest 75 books known.[^o47]

His second non-technical book, *[The Black Swan](wikipedia://en/8561256)*, about unpredictable events, was published in 2007, selling close to three million copies, as of February 2011. It spent 36 weeks on the [New York Times Bestseller list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times_Bestseller_list),[^o48] 17 as hardcover and 19 weeks as paperback, and was translated into 50 languages.[^o20][^o49] The book has been credited with predicting the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^o50]

In a 2008 article in *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)*, journalist [Bryan Appleyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Appleyard) described Taleb as "the hottest thinker in the world".[^o28] [Daniel Kahneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kahneman) proposed the inclusion of Taleb's name among the world's top intellectuals, saying "Taleb has changed the way many people think about uncertainty, particularly in the financial markets. His book, *The Black Swan*, is an original and audacious analysis of the ways in which humans try to make sense of unexpected events."[^o51]

A book of [aphorisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphorism), *[The Bed of Procrustes: Philosophical and Practical Aphorisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bed_of_Procrustes)*, was released in December 2010.

*[Antifragile: Things That Gain from Disorder](wikipedia://en/37758875)* was published in November 2012[^o56] and *[Skin in the Game: Hidden Asymmetries in Daily Life](wikipedia://en/56664246)* was published in February 2018.

Taleb's five volume philosophical essay on uncertainty, titled *Incerto*, includes *Fooled by Randomness* (2001), *The Black Swan* (2007–2010), *The Bed of Procrustes* (2010), *Antifragile* (2012), and *Skin in the Game* (2018). It was originally published in November 2016 including only the first four books. The fifth book was added in August 2019.

Taleb's non-technical writing style has been described as mixing a narrative, often semi-autobiographical style with short philosophical tales and historical and scientific commentary. The sales of Taleb's first two books garnered an advance of \$4 million, for a follow-up book on anti-fragility.[^o20]

## Ideas and theories {#Ideas_and_theories}

*Image: Genealogy map of topics treated by Nassim Taleb.jpg.* Genealogy map of topics treated by Nassim Taleb

Taleb's book *[The Bed of Procrustes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bed_of_Procrustes:_Philosophical_and_Practical_Aphorisms)* summarizes the central problem: "we humans, facing limits of knowledge, and things we do not observe, the unseen and the unknown, resolve the tension by squeezing life and the world into crisp commoditized ideas". Taleb disagrees with [Platonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism) (i.e., theoretical) approaches to reality to the extent that they lead people to have the wrong [map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map–territory_relation) of reality, rather than no map at all. He opposes most economic and grand social science theorizing, which in his view, suffers acutely from the problem of overuse of Plato's [theory of forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_forms).[^o8]

He has also proposed that biological, economic, and other systems exhibit an ability to benefit and grow from volatility—including particular types of random errors and events—a characteristic of these systems that he terms *[antifragility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifragility).*[^o9][^o10] Relatedly, he also believes that universities are better at public relations and claiming credit than generating knowledge. He argues that knowledge and technology are usually generated by what he calls "[stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222) tinkering" rather than by top-down directed research,[^o54][^o55] and has proposed option-like experimentation as a way to outperform directed research as a method of scientific discovery, an approach he terms *convex tinkering.*[^o56]

Taleb has called for discontinuation of the [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Economics), saying that the damage from economic theories can be devastating.[^o57][^o58] He opposes top-down knowledge as an academic illusion.[^o59] Together with Espen Gaarder Haug, Taleb asserts that option pricing is determined in a "heuristic way" by market participants, not by a model, and that models are "lecturing birds on how to fly".[^o59] Teacher and author Pablo Triana has explored this topic with reference to Haug and Taleb.[^o60] Triana has stated that Taleb might be correct in recommending that retail banks be treated as [utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utility), i.e. forbidden to take potentially disastrous risks, whereas hedge funds and other less-regulated investment entities need not be subject to similar restrictions.[^o61]

In his writings, Taleb has identified and discussed the error of comparing real-world randomness with the "structured randomness" in [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics) (where probabilities are computable) or games of chance such as casino gambling, in which the probabilities are purposefully constructed by casino management.[^o62][^o63] Taleb calls this the "[ludic fallacy](wikipedia://en/8810311)". He argues that predictive models suffer from [Platonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism), gravitating towards mathematical purity and failing to take certain key ideas into account such as the impossibility of possessing all relevant information; that small unknown variations in the data can have a huge impact; and flawed theories/models based on empirical data and that fail to consider events that have not taken place but could take place. Discussing the ludic fallacy in *The Black Swan*, he writes, "The dark side of the moon is harder to see; beaming light on it costs energy. In the same way, beaming light on the unseen is costly, in both computational and mental effort."

In the second edition of *The Black Swan*, he posited that the foundations of [quantitative economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analyst) are faulty and highly self-referential. He states that statistics is fundamentally incomplete as a field, as it cannot predict the risk of rare events, a problem that is acute in proportion to the rarity of these events. With the mathematician [Raphael Douady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Douady), he called the problem *statistical undecidability* (Douady and Taleb, 2010).[^o64]

Taleb has described his main challenge as mapping his ideas of "robustification" and "[antifragility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifragility)", that is, how to live and act in a world we do not understand and build robustness to black swan events. Taleb introduced the idea of the "fourth quadrant" in the exposure domain.[^o65] One of its applications is in his definition of the most effective (that is, least fragile) risk management approach: what he calls the "[barbell strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbell_strategy)" which is based on avoiding the middle in favor of linear combination of extremes, across all domains from politics to economics to one's personal life. These are deemed by Taleb to be more robust to estimation errors. For instance, he suggests that investing money in 'medium risk' investments is pointless, because risk is difficult, if not impossible to compute. His preferred strategy is to be both hyper-conservative and hyper-aggressive at the same time. For example, an investor might put 80 to 90% of their money in extremely safe instruments, such as treasury bills, with the remainder going into highly risky and diversified speculative bets. An alternative suggestion is to engage in highly speculative bets with a limited downside.

Taleb asserts that by adopting these strategies, a portfolio can be "robust", i.e. gain a positive exposure to black swan events while limiting losses suffered by such random events.[^o66] Together with [Donald Geman](wikipedia://en/19582884) and [Hélyette Geman](wikipedia://en/38066732), he modeled a [maximum entropy](wikipedia://en/201718) barbell "to constrain only what can be constrained (in a robust manner) and to maximize entropy elsewhere", based on an insight by E. T. Jaynes that economic life increases in entropy under regulatory and other constraints.[^o67] Taleb also applies a similar barbell-style approach to health and exercise. Instead of doing steady and moderate exercise daily, he suggests that it is better to do a low-effort exercise such as walking slowly most of the time, while occasionally expending extreme effort. He claims that the human body evolved to live in a random environment, with various unexpected but intense efforts and much rest.[^o68]

Taleb appeared with [Ron Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul)[^o69] and [Ralph Nader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Nader)[^o70] on their respective shows in support of *Skin in the Game*, which was dedicated to both men.[^o71] After the [2022 invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), however, Taleb publicly supported an aggressive response against Russia and denounced "naive libertarians, who think I'm like them because they like my books."[^o72]

Taleb wrote in *[Antifragile](wikipedia://en/37758875)* and in scientific papers[^o73] that if the statistical structure of habits in modern society differ too greatly from the ancestral environment of humanity, the analysis of consumption should focus less on composition and more on frequency. In other words, studies that ignore the random nature of supply of nutrients are invalid.

Taleb's scathing counter-intuitive takedown of widely-circulated alt-right talking points about race and IQ received both criticism and praise, when he published his "IQ is largely a pseudo-scientific swindle" on Medium.[^o74] Posting a video on YouTube on the same topic, Taleb demonstrates that IQ as a test does not measure intelligence, which is a multi-factor phenomenon, but rather tests for the "absence of stupidity" or the "presence of unintelligence". Taleb argues in the paper that not only is IQ unscientific, and not validated by any evidence, it is additionally immoral and racist, calling national IQ rankings "a complete fraud."

Taleb authored a paper with [Yaneer Bar-Yam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaneer_Bar-Yam) and [Joseph Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Norman) called *Systemic risk of pandemic via novel pathogens – Coronavirus: A note*. The paper, published on 26 January 2020, took the position that the [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2) was not being taken seriously enough by policy makers and medical professionals.[^o75]

## Criticism and reactions {#Criticism_and_reactions}

[Aaron Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Brown_%28financial_author%29), a [quantitative analyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_%28finance%29) and [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor), said regarding *The Black Swan* that "the book reads as if Taleb has never heard of [nonparametric methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics), [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954), visualization tools or [robust estimation](wikipedia://en/2885691)."[^o77] Nonetheless, he calls the book "essential reading" and urges statisticians to overlook the insults to get the "important philosophic and mathematical truths." Taleb replied in the second edition of *The Black Swan* that "One of the most common (but useless) comments I hear is that some solutions can come from 'robust statistics.' I wonder how using these techniques can create information where there is none".[^o78] In 2007, Westfall and [Hilbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Hilbe) complained that Taleb's criticism is "often unfounded and sometimes outrageous."[^o79] Taleb, writes [John Kay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kay_%28economist%29), "describes writers and professionals as knaves or fools, mostly fools ... Yet beneath his rage and mockery are serious issues. The risk management models in use today exclude the very events against which they claim to protect the businesses that employ them. These models import a veneer of technical sophistication ... "[^o80] Berkeley statistician [David Freedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Freedman_%28statistician%29) said that efforts by statisticians to refute Taleb's stance have been unconvincing.[^o81]

Taleb contends that statisticians can be [pseudoscientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscientists) when it comes to risks of rare events and risks of blowups, and mask their incompetence with complicated equations.[^o82] This stance has attracted criticism: the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) devoted the August 2007 issue of *[The American Statistician](wikipedia://en/16876079)* to *The Black Swan*. The magazine offered a mixture of praise and criticism for Taleb's main points, with a focus on Taleb's writing style and his representation of the statistical literature. Robert Lund, a mathematics professor at [Clemson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemson_University), writes that in *Black Swan*, Taleb is "reckless at times and subject to grandiose overstatements; the professional statistician will find the book ubiquitously naive."[^o83] However, Lund acknowledges that "there are many points where I agree with Taleb," and writes that "the book is a must" for anyone "remotely interested in finance and/or philosophical probability."

Taleb and Nobel laureate [Myron Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myron_Scholes) have traded personal attacks, particularly after Taleb's paper with Espen Gaarder Haug, in which Taleb alleged that nobody uses the [Black–Scholes–Merton formula](wikipedia://en/113515). Taleb accused Scholes of being responsible for the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), and suggested that "this guy should be in a retirement home doing [Sudoku](wikipedia://en/1365807). His funds have blown up twice. He shouldn't be allowed in Washington to lecture anyone on risk." Scholes retorted that Taleb simply "popularises ideas and is making money selling books". Scholes claimed that Taleb does not cite previous literature, and for this reason Taleb is not taken seriously in academia.[^o84] Haug and Taleb (2011) listed hundreds of research documents showing the Black–Scholes formula was not derived by Scholes, and argued that the economics establishment ignored literature by practitioners and mathematicians such as [Ed Thorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Thorp), who, many years earlier, had developed a more sophisticated version of the formula.[^o85]

Taleb's outspoken and directed commentary against parts of the finance industry—e.g., saying at Davos in 2009 that he was "happy" that [Lehman Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_Brothers) collapsed—was followed by reports of threats and personal attacks.[^o86]

Taleb is known for his strong, and sometimes personal, criticisms of public figures and institutions. He has referred to former UK prime minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) as "a very dishonourable fellow" and Blair's successor, [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown) as "an idiot". He has criticized prominent economists like [Larry Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Summers), [Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman) and [Joseph Stiglitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stiglitz), stating that "they cause more crises than they solve". He has also been critical of the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum) at [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos), calling it the "International Association of Namedroppers" and stating "they think it's their mission to solve a problem they don't understand."[^o87]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Taleb has had non-smoking-related throat cancer.[^o13] Taleb married Cynthia Shelton in 1988.[^o89] He has a son and a daughter.[^o13]

A polyglot, Taleb is fluent in French, English, and Classical and Lebanese Arabic. He also speaks Italian and Spanish, and reads classical texts in Greek, Latin, [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic), Ancient Hebrew, and [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanite_languages) script.[^o90]

According to the [United States Justice Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Justice_Department) records, Taleb repeatedly declined meeting [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein) and attending his parties, claiming he "smelled a rat". Epstein confirmed that they "never met".[^o91]
