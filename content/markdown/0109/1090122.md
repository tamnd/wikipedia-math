# Norman Levinson

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Norman Levinson"}

**Norman Levinson** (August 11, 1912 in [Lynn, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn,_Massachusetts) – October 10, 1975 in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston)) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Some of his major contributions were in the study of [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), non-linear [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324). He worked closely with [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185) in his early career. He joined the faculty of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1937. In 1954, he was awarded the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and in 1971 the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) (after winning in 1970 the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award)) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for his paper *A Motivated Account of an Elementary Proof of the Prime Number Theorem*.[^o4] In 1974 he published a 54-pages long paper[^o5], later simplified by Matthew Young to 7 pages[^o6], proving that more than a third of the zeros of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) lie on the critical line, a result later improved to two fifths by [Conrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrey).[^o7]

:::

He received both his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) and his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) in 1934, where he had studied under [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185)[^o8] and took almost all of the graduate-level courses in mathematics. He received the MIT [Redfield Proctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redfield_Proctor) Traveling Fellowship to study at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), with the assurance that MIT would reward him with a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) upon his return regardless of whatever he produced at Cambridge. Within the first four months in Cambridge, he had already produced two papers. In 1935, MIT awarded him with the PhD in mathematics.

His death in 1975 was caused by a [brain tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_tumor). He was married since 1938; his widow Zipporah died at age 93 in 2009. He had two daughters and four grandchildren. Norman Levinson's doctoral students include [Raymond Redheffer](wikipedia://en/22117165) and [Harold Shapiro](wikipedia://en/1781665).[^o2]
