# Igor Shafarevich

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Igor Shafarevich"}

**Igor Rostislavovich Shafarevich** (И́горь Ростисла́вович Шафаре́вич; 3 June 1923 – 19 February 2017) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who contributed to [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). Outside mathematics, he wrote books and articles that criticised [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism), as well as other literature which some described as [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism).

:::

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

From his early years, Shafarevich made fundamental contributions to several parts of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) including [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [arithmetic algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_algebraic_geometry). In particular, in algebraic number theory, the [Shafarevich–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/38771064) extends the commutative reciprocity map to the case of [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673), which are central extensions of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) by [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707).

Shafarevich was the first mathematician to give a completely self-contained formula for the Hilbert pairing, thus initiating an important branch of the study of explicit formulas in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Another famous (and slightly incomplete) result is [Shafarevich's theorem on solvable Galois groups](wikipedia://en/47258989), giving the realization of every finite [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012) as a Galois group over the [rationals](wikipedia://en/19727024).

Another development is the [Golod–Shafarevich theorem](wikipedia://en/8845470) on towers of [unramified extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unramified_extension) of [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).

Shafarevich and his school greatly contributed to the study of algebraic geometry of [surfaces](wikipedia://en/4342970). He started a famous [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) seminar on [classification of algebraic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_algebraic_surfaces) that updated the treatment of [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733) around 1960 and was largely responsible for the early introduction of the [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) approach to algebraic geometry in the Soviet school. His investigation in arithmetic of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) led him, independently of [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933), to the introduction of the group related to [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) over number fields, the [Tate–Shafarevich group](wikipedia://en/25711736) (usually called 'Sha', and denoted as 'Ш', the first [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic) letter of his surname).

He contributed the [Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich formula](wikipedia://en/35140129) and to the [Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion](wikipedia://en/35128259).

With former student [Ilya Piatetski-Shapiro](wikipedia://en/802738), he proved a version of the [Torelli theorem](wikipedia://en/3002552) for [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116).

He formulated the [Shafarevich conjecture](wikipedia://en/172199), which stated the finiteness of the set of [Abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties) over a number field having fixed dimension and prescribed set of primes of [bad reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_reduction). The conjecture was proved by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) as a partial step in his proof of the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture).

Shafarevich's students included [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086), [Alexey Parshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Parshin), [Igor Dolgachev](wikipedia://en/22777439), [Evgeny Golod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Golod), [Alexei Kostrikin](wikipedia://en/31810848), [Suren Arakelov](wikipedia://en/10705349), [G. V. Belyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._V._Belyi), Victor Abrashkin, Andrey Todorov, Andrey N. Tyurin, and [Victor Kolyvagin](wikipedia://en/4559839).

He was a member of the [Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts) in the department of Mathematics, Physics and Earth Sciences. In 1960, he was elected a Member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina).[^o5] In 1981, he was elected as a foreign member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o6]

In 2017, Shafarevich was awarded the [Leonhard Euler Gold Medal](wikipedia://en/67910317) by the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences).

## Soviet politics {#Soviet_politics}

Shafarevich came into conflict with the Soviet authorities in the early 1950s but was protected by [Ivan Petrovsky](wikipedia://en/4598657), the Rector of [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University). He belonged to a group of [Pochvennichestvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pochvennichestvo)-influenced dissidents who endorsed the [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) tradition. Shafarevich published a book, *The Socialist Phenomenon* (French edition 1975, English edition 1980), which was cited by [Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Solzhenitsyn) in his 1978 address to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).

In the 1970s, Shafarevich, with [Valery Chalidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valery_Chalidze), [Grigori Podyapolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigori_Podyapolski) and [Andrei Tverdokhlebov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Tverdokhlebov), became one of [Andrei Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Sakharov)'s [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights) investigators and so was dismissed from Moscow University. Shafarevich opposed political interference in universities.

## *The Socialist Phenomenon* {#The_Socialist_Phenomenon}

Shafarevich's book *The Socialist Phenomenon*,[^o9] which was published in the [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) by [Harper & Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper_%28publisher%29) in 1980, analyzed numerous examples of socialism from ancient times to various medieval heresies and a variety of modern thinkers and socialist states. From those examples, he claimed that all the basic principles of socialist ideology derive from the urge to suppress [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism). *The Socialist Phenomenon* consists of three major parts:[^o10]

1. [Chiliastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiliastic) Socialism: Identifies socialist ideas amongst the ancient Greeks, especially [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954), in numerous medieval heretic groups such as the [Cathars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathars), [Brethren of the Free Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brethren_of_the_Free_Spirit), [Taborites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taborites), [Anabaptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabaptists), in various religious groups during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War), in modern writers such as [Thomas More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_More), [Tommaso Campanella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommaso_Campanella) and numerous [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) writers in 18th-century France.[^o11]
1. State Socialism: Describes the socialism of the [Incas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incas), the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) state in [Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay), [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China).[^o12]
1. Analysis: Identifies three persistent abolition themes in socialism: the abolition of [private property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property), the abolition of the [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_family), and the abolition of religion (mainly but not exclusively [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity)).[^o13]

Shafarevich argued that ancient socialism (such as Mesopotamia and Egypt) was not ideological, as an ideology socialism was a reaction to the emergence of individualism in the [Axial Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_Age). He compared [Thomas More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_More)'s ([*Utopia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia_%28More_book%29)) and [Tommaso Campanella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommaso_Campanella)'s ([*City of the Sun*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_City_of_the_Sun)) visions with what is known about the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire) and concluded that there are striking similarities. He claimed that we become persons through our relationship with God and argued that socialism is essentially [nihilistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism) and is unconsciously motivated by a death instinct. He concluded that we have the choice of pursuing death or life.

## Religious views {#Religious_views}

Shafarevich adhered to [Russian Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Christianity) and incorporated the [neo-Platonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Platonic) views of [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) into his understanding of the relation of mathematics and religion.[^o15]

In his talk to the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences) upon receiving a prize, Shafarevich presented his view of the relationship between mathematics and religion. He noted the multiple discoveries in mathematics, such as that of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610), to suggest that pure mathematics reflects an objective reality, not a set of conventional definitions or a formalism. He claimed that the growth of mathematics itself is not directed or organic. To have a unity and direction, mathematics needs a goal. It can be practical applications or God as the source for the direction of development. Shafarevich opted for the latter, as pure mathematics is not in itself driven by practical applications.[^o16]

## Russian politics {#Russian_politics}

Intellectuals On 21 December 1991 he took part in the first congress of the [Russian All-People's Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_All-People's_Union), headed by [Sergei Baburin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Baburin). In October 1992, he became a member of the founding committee of the [National Salvation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Salvation_Front_%28Russia%29). In 1993, he was an unsuccessful candidate for the [State Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Duma) with [Mikhail Astafyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Astafyev)'s [Constitutional Democratic Party - Party of Popular Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Democratic_Party_-_Party_of_Popular_Freedom).

Shafarevich was a member of the editorial board of the magazine *[Nash Sovremennik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_Sovremennik)* and in 1991–1992 of the editorial board of *Den* of [Alexander Prokhanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Prokhanov), which was banned in October 1993 following the [1993 Russian constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Russian_constitutional_crisis) and later reopened under the title *[Zavtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zavtra)*. In 1994, he joined the "All-Russian National Right Wing Centre", led by [Mikhail Astafyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Astafyev).

## Accusations of antisemitism {#Accusations_of_antisemitism}

Shafarevich's essay *Russophobia*[^o19] was expanded into his book *Three Thousand-Year-Old Mystery* (Трехтысячелетняя загадка) and resulted in accusations of [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism).[^o20][^o21][^o22] He completed the *Russophobia* essay in 1982 and it was initially circulated as *[samizdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samizdat)*. In the Soviet Union, it was first officially published in 1989. At the same time, Shafarevich condemned the methods that were used to [screen out applicants of Jewish origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_Soviet_mathematics) when entering prestigious Moscow universities in the 1970s and early 1980s.[^o23]

In *Russophobia*, he argued in the essay that great nations experience periods in their history of reformist elitist groups ('small nations') having values that differ fundamentally from the values of the majority of the people but gaining the upper hand in the society. In Shafarevich's opinion, the role of such a 'small nation' in Russia was played by a small group of [intelligentsiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsiya), dominated by [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews), "who were full of hatred against traditional Russian way of life and played an active role in the terrorist regimes of [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) and [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)".[^o31][^o24]

Its publication led to a request by the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) (NAS) to Shafarevich to resign his membership,[^o25] because the NAS charter prohibited stripping an existing membership.[^o26][^o27] In an open letter to the NAS, Shafarevich explained that *Russophobia* is not antisemitic.[^o28] Shafarevich also noted that since NAS enlisted him without his request or knowledge, delisting him was its internal matter. Nevertheless, when the United States [invaded Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq), Shafarevich faxed his resignation.[^o29]

Accusations of antisemitism continued and involved Shafarevich's other publications.[^o30] [Semyon Reznik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Reznik) targets the *Russophobia* essay for factual inaccuracies: Shafarevich misassigned Jewish ethnicity to a number of non-Jews involved in the execution of [Nicholas II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia), repeated the false assertion of graffiti in [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish) at the murder site and suggested that Shafarevich's phrase "Nicholas II was [shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_of_the_Romanov_family) specifically as the *[Tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar)*, and this ritual act drew a line under an epoch in Russian history" – is read by some as a [blood libel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_libel).[^o31] (An accusation which ignores the remainder of Shafarevich's sentence: "so it can only be compared with the execution of Charles I in England or of Louis XVI in France".)[^o19] [Aron Katsenelinboigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aron_Katsenelinboigen) wrote that Shafarevish's work "lives up to the best traditions of antisemitic propaganda".[^o32]

Later, Shafarevich expanded on his views in his book *Three Thousand-Year-Old Mystery* in which he further claimed that Jews effectively marginalise and exclude non-Jews in all types of intellectual endeavors. The work was published in Russian in 2002; an introductory section explains the relationship with the *Russophobia* essay, explaining that the essay developed from an appendix to an intended work of wider scope, which he started writing in *samizdat*.[^o33]

In 2005, Shafarevich was amongst the signatories of the [Letter of 5000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_5000).

The issue of Shafarevich's alleged antisemitism has been the subject of a 2009 doctoral thesis at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki), which was later turned into a book[^o3] in which the author, Krista Berglund, stated that Shafarevich's views have been misconstrued as antisemitic.[^o34]
