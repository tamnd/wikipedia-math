# Non-well-founded set theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-well-founded set theory"}

**Non-well-founded set theories** (sometimes [unhyphenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen), as **nonwellfounded**;[^o2] or **poorly founded**[^o3]) are variants of [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) that allow sets to be elements of themselves and otherwise violate the rule of [well-foundedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-foundedness). In non-well-founded set theories, the [foundation axiom](wikipedia://en/2113) of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) is replaced by axioms implying its negation.

:::

The study of non-well-founded sets was initiated by [Dmitry Mirimanoff](wikipedia://en/30354118) in a series of papers between 1917 and 1920, in which he formulated the distinction between well-founded and non-well-founded sets; he did not regard well-foundedness as an [axiom](wikipedia://en/928). Although a number of axiomatic systems of non-well-founded sets were proposed afterwards, they did not find much in the way of applications until the book *Non-Well-Founded Sets* by [Peter Aczel](wikipedia://en/24089125) introduced [hyperset theory](wikipedia://en/1091767#Applications) in 1988.[^n1][^n2][^n3]

The theory of non-well-founded sets has been applied in the [logical](wikipedia://en/46426065) [modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_%28abstract%29) of non-terminating [computational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) processes in computer science ([process algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_algebra) and [final semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_semantics)), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) and [natural language](wikipedia://en/21173) [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107) ([situation theory](wikipedia://en/13279194)), philosophy (work on the [liar paradox](wikipedia://en/17967)), and in a different setting, [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis).[^n4]

## Details {#Details}

In 1917, Dmitry Mirimanoff introduced[^n5][^n6][^n7][^n8] the concept of [well-foundedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-founded_set) of a set:

A set, $x_{0}$, is well-founded if it has no infinite descending membership sequence $\cdots \in x_2 \in x_1 \in x_0.$

In ZFC, there is no infinite descending ∈-sequence by the [axiom of regularity](wikipedia://en/2113). In fact, the axiom of regularity is often called the *foundation axiom* since it can be proved within ZFC<sup>−</sup> (that is, ZFC without the axiom of regularity) that well-foundedness implies regularity. In variants of ZFC without the [axiom of regularity](wikipedia://en/2113), the possibility of non-well-founded sets with set-like ∈-chains arises. For example, a set $A$ such that $A \in A$ is non-well-founded.

Although Mirimanoff also introduced a notion of isomorphism between possibly non-well-founded sets, he considered neither an axiom of foundation nor of anti-foundation.[^n7] In 1926, [Paul Finsler](wikipedia://en/27007280) introduced the first axiom that allowed non-well-founded sets. After Zermelo adopted Foundation into his own system in 1930 (from [von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942)'s 1925–1929 work), interest in non-well-founded sets waned for decades.[^n9] An early non-well-founded set theory was [Willard Van Orman Quine](wikipedia://en/33833)'s [New Foundations](wikipedia://en/945957), although it is not merely ZF with a replacement for Foundation.

Several proofs of the independence of Foundation from the rest of ZF were published in 1950s particularly by [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580) (1954), following an announcement of the result in an earlier paper of his from 1941, and by [Ernst Specker](wikipedia://en/22209158) who gave a different proof in his [Habilitationsschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitationsschrift) of 1951, proof which was published in 1957. Then in 1957 [Rieger's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rieger's_theorem) was published, which gave a general method for such a proof to be carried out, rekindling some interest in non-well-founded axiomatic systems.[^n10] The next axiom proposal came in a 1960 congress talk of [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875) (never published as a paper), proposing an alternative axiom now called [SAFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott's_anti-foundation_axiom).[^n11] Another axiom proposed in the late 1960s was [Maurice Boffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Boffa)'s axiom of [superuniversality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superuniversality), described by Aczel as the highpoint of research of its decade.[^n12] Boffa's idea was to make foundation fail as badly as it can (or rather, as extensionality permits): Boffa's axiom implies that every [extensional](wikipedia://en/294056) [set-like](wikipedia://en/3931) relation is isomorphic to the elementhood predicate on a [transitive class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_class).

A more recent approach to non-well-founded set theory, pioneered by M. Forti and F. Honsell in the 1980s, borrows from computer science the concept of a [bisimulation](wikipedia://en/397571). Bisimilar sets are considered indistinguishable and thus equal, which leads to a strengthening of the [axiom of extensionality](wikipedia://en/52376). In this context, axioms contradicting the axiom of regularity are known as **anti-foundation axioms**, and a set that is not necessarily well-founded is called a **hyperset**.

Four mutually exclusive anti-foundation axioms are well-known, sometimes abbreviated by the first letter in the following list:

1. **A**FA ("Anti-Foundation Axiom") – due to M. Forti and F. Honsell (this is also known as [**A**czel's anti-foundation axiom](wikipedia://en/8933657));
1. **S**AFA ("Scott’s AFA") – due to [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875),
1. **F**AFA ("Finsler’s AFA") – due to [Paul Finsler](wikipedia://en/27007280),
1. **B**AFA ("Boffa’s AFA") – due to [Maurice Boffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Boffa).

They essentially correspond to four different notions of equality for non-well-founded sets. The first of these, AFA, is based on [accessible pointed graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessible_pointed_graph) (apg) and states that two hypersets are equal if and only if they can be pictured by the same apg. Within this framework, it can be shown that the equation $x = \{x\}$ has one and only one solution, the unique [Quine atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quine_atom) of the theory.

Each of the axioms given above extends the universe of the previous, so that: $V \subseteq A \subseteq S \subseteq F \subseteq B$. In the Boffa universe, the distinct Quine atoms form a [proper class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_class).[^n13]

It is worth emphasizing that hyperset theory is an extension of classical set theory rather than a replacement: the well-founded sets within a hyperset domain conform to classical set theory.

## Applications {#Applications}

In published research, non-well-founded sets are also called hypersets, in parallel to the [hyperreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51429) of [nonstandard analysis](wikipedia://en/21708).[^o21][^o22]

The hypersets were extensively used by [Jon Barwise](wikipedia://en/912858) and [John Etchemendy](wikipedia://en/5503538) in their 1987 book *The Liar*, on the [liar's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar's_paradox). The book's proposals contributed to the [theory of truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_truth).[^o21] The book is also a good introduction to the topic of non-well-founded sets.[^o21]
