# Tromino

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tromino"}

*Image: Trominoes.svg.* All possible free trominos

A **tromino** or **triomino** is a [polyomino](wikipedia://en/72634) of size 3, that is, a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) in the [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) made of three equal-sized [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) connected edge-to-edge.[^o2]

:::

## Symmetry and enumeration {#Symmetry_and_enumeration}

When [rotations](wikipedia://en/39789) and [reflections](wikipedia://en/295829) are not considered to be distinct shapes, there are only two different [*free*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_polyomino) trominoes: "I" and "L" (the "L" shape is also called "V").

Since both free trominoes have [reflection symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873), they are also the only two *one-sided* trominoes (trominoes with reflections considered distinct). When rotations are also considered distinct, there are six *fixed* trominoes: two I and four L shapes. They can be obtained by rotating the above forms by 90°, 180° and 270°.[^o5]

## Rep-tiling and Golomb's tromino theorem {#Rep-tiling_and_Golomb's_tromino_theorem}

*Image: Geometrical dissection of an L-triomino (rep-4).gif.* Geometrical dissection of an L-tromino (rep-4)

Both types of tromino can be dissected into *n*<sup>2</sup> smaller trominos of the same type, for any integer *n* > 1. That is, they are [rep-tiles](wikipedia://en/33478101).[^o8] Continuing this dissection recursively leads to a tiling of the plane, which in many cases is an [aperiodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145). In this context, the L-tromino is called a *chair*, and its tiling by recursive subdivision into four smaller L-trominos is called the [chair tiling](wikipedia://en/63508559).[^o9]

Motivated by the [mutilated chessboard problem](wikipedia://en/9308202), [Solomon W. Golomb](wikipedia://en/1860707) used this tiling as the basis for what has become known as Golomb's tromino theorem: if any square is removed from a 2<sup>*n*</sup> × 2<sup>*n*</sup> chessboard, the remaining board can be completely covered with L-trominoes. To prove this by [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881), partition the board into a quarter-board of size 2<sup>*n−1*</sup> × 2<sup>*n−1*</sup> that contains the removed square, and a large tromino formed by the other three quarter-boards. The tromino can be recursively dissected into unit trominoes, and a dissection of the quarter-board with one square removed follows by the induction hypothesis. In contrast, when a chessboard of this size has one square removed, it is not always possible to cover the remaining squares by I-trominoes.[^o10]
