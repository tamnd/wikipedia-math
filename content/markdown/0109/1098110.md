# Adams Prize

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adams Prize"}

The **Adams Prize** is a prize[^o2] awarded each year by the Faculty of Mathematics at [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) to a UK-based mathematician for distinguished research in mathematical sciences.

:::

The prize is named after the mathematician [John Couch Adams](wikipedia://en/176360) and was endowed by members of St John's College and approved by the senate of the university in 1848, to commemorate Adams' [role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_Neptune) in the discovery of the planet [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune). Originally open only to Cambridge graduates, the current stipulation is that the mathematician must reside in the UK and be under forty years of age.[^o3]

The Adams Prize is awarded in three parts: the first is paid directly to the candidate; another third is paid to the candidate's institution to fund research expenses; and the final third is paid on publication of a survey paper in the winner's field in a major mathematics journal.

The prize has been awarded to many well-known mathematicians, including [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696) and [Sir William Hodge](wikipedia://en/384430). The first female recipient, in 2002, was [Susan Howson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Howson_%28mathematician%29), then a lecturer at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham), for her work on [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves).

- 2015–16: Applied analysis[^o5]
- 2016–17: Statistical analysis of big data[^o6]
- 2017–18: The mathematics of astronomy and cosmology[^o7]
- 2018–19: The mathematics of networks[^o8]
