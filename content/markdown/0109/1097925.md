# Conserved current

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conserved current"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) a **conserved current** is a [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_%28physics%29), $j^\mu$, that satisfies the [continuity equation](wikipedia://en/410793) $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$. The continuity equation represents a conservation law, hence the name.

:::

Indeed, integrating the continuity equation over a volume $V$, large enough to have no net currents through its surface, leads to the conservation law

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Q = 0\;,$$

where $Q = \int_V j^0 dV$ is the [conserved quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_%28physics%29).

In [gauge theories](wikipedia://en/23912155) the gauge fields couple to conserved currents. For example, the [electromagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field) couples to the [conserved electric current](wikipedia://en/3094621).

## Conserved quantities and symmetries {#Conserved_quantities_and_symmetries}

Conserved current is the flow of the [canonical conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_conjugate) of a quantity possessing a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [translational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_symmetry). The [continuity equation](wikipedia://en/410793) for the conserved current is a statement of a *[conservation law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law_%28physics%29)*. Examples of canonical conjugate quantities are:

- [Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) and [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) - the continuous translational symmetry of time implies the [conservation of energy](wikipedia://en/67088)
- [Space](wikipedia://en/27667) and [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431) - the continuous translational symmetry of space implies the [conservation of momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_momentum)
- Space and [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839) - the continuous *rotational* symmetry of space implies the [conservation of angular momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_angular_momentum)
- [Wave function](wikipedia://en/145343) [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) and [electric charge](wikipedia://en/9804) - the continuous phase angle symmetry of the wave function implies the [conservation of electric charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_electric_charge)

Conserved currents play an extremely important role in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), because [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159) connects the existence of a conserved current to the existence of a [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) of some quantity in the system under study. In practical terms, all conserved currents are the [Noether currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noether_current), as the existence of a conserved current implies the existence of a symmetry. Conserved currents play an important role in the theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), as the existence of a conserved current points to the existence of [constants of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constants_of_motion), which are required to define a [foliation](wikipedia://en/371227) and thus an [integrable system](wikipedia://en/2573213). The conservation law is expressed as the vanishing of a 4-[divergence](wikipedia://en/8328), where the Noether [charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_%28physics%29) forms the zeroth component of the [4-current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-current).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Electromagnetism {#Electromagnetism}

The *conservation of charge*, for example, in the notation of [Maxwell's equations](wikipedia://en/19737),

$$\frac{\partial \rho} {\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} = 0$$

where

- ρ is the *free* electric charge density (in units of C/m<sup>3</sup>)
- **J** is the **current density** $$\mathbf J = \rho \mathbf v$$ with **v** as the velocity of the charges.

The equation would apply equally to masses (or other conserved quantities), where the word *mass* is substituted for the words *electric charge* above.

### Complex scalar field {#Complex_scalar_field}

The [Klein-Gordon Lagrangian density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein-Gordon_equation#Lagrangian_formulation)

$$\mathcal{L}=\partial_\mu\phi^*\,\partial^\mu\phi +V(\phi^*\,\phi)$$

of a complex scalar field $\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mapsto\mathbb{C}$ is invariant under the symmetry transformation

$$\phi\mapsto\phi'=\phi\,e^{i\alpha}\, .$$

Defining $\delta\phi=\phi'-\phi$ we find the Noether current

$$j^\mu:= \frac{d\mathcal{L}}{d \dot{\mathbf{q}}} \cdot \mathbf{Q}_r = \frac{d\mathcal{L}}{d(\partial_\mu)\phi}\,\frac{d(\delta\phi)}{d\alpha}\bigg|_{\alpha=0}+\frac{d\mathcal{L}}{d(\partial_\mu)\phi^*}\,\frac{d(\delta\phi^*)}{d\alpha}\bigg|_{\alpha=0}= i\,\phi\,(\partial^\mu\phi^*)-i\,\phi^*\,(\partial^\mu\phi)$$

which satisfies the continuity equation. Here $\mathbf{Q}_r$ is the generator of the symmetry, which is $\frac{d (\delta \mathbf{q})}{d \alpha_r}$ in the case of a single parameter $\alpha$.
