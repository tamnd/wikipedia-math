# Hall–Higman theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hall–Higman theorem"}

In [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Hall–Higman theorem**, due to (txt, Theorem B), describes the possibilities for the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667107) of an element of [prime power](wikipedia://en/1630483) order for a [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of a [*p*-solvable group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-solvable_group).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that *G* is a *p*-solvable group with no normal *p*-subgroups, [acting faithfully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting_faithfully) on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [field of characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_characteristic) *p*. If *x* is an element of order *p*<sup>*n*</sup> of *G* then the minimal polynomial is of the form (*X* − 1)<sup>*r*</sup> for some *r* ≤ *p*<sup>*n*</sup>. The Hall–Higman theorem states that one of the following 3 possibilities holds:

- *r* = *p*<sup>*n*</sup>
- *p* is a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime) and the [Sylow 2-subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_2-subgroup) of *G* are [non-abelian](wikipedia://en/4791400) and *r* ≥ *p*<sup>*n*</sup> −*p*<sup>*n*−1</sup>
- *p* = 2 and the Sylow *q*-subgroups of *G* are non-abelian for some [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) *q* = 2<sup>*m*</sup> − 1 less than 2<sup>*n*</sup> and *r* ≥ 2<sup>*n*</sup> − 2<sup>*n*−*m*</sup>.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The group [SL<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/144823)(**F**<sub>3</sub>) is 3-solvable (in fact [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012)) and has an obvious 2-dimensional representation over a field of characteristic *p*=3, in which the elements of order 3 have minimal polynomial (*X*−1)<sup>2</sup> with *r*=3−1.

:::
