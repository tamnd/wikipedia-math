# Howard Jerome Keisler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Howard Jerome Keisler"}

**Howard Jerome Keisler** (born 3 December 1936) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), currently professor emeritus at [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). His research has included [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) and [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis).

:::

His Ph.D. advisor was [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley); his dissertation is *Ultraproducts and Elementary Classes* (1961).

Following [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024)'s work resolving what had long been thought to be inherent logical contradictions in the literal interpretation of [Leibniz's notation](wikipedia://en/410712) that Leibniz himself had proposed, that is, interpreting "dx" as literally representing an [infinitesimally](wikipedia://en/160990) small quantity, Keisler published *[Elementary Calculus: An Infinitesimal Approach](wikipedia://en/20425855)*, a first-year calculus textbook conceptually centered on the use of infinitesimals, rather than the [epsilon, delta approach](wikipedia://en/285759#%28ε,_δ%29-definition_of_limit), for developing the calculus.

He is also known for extending the [Henkin construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henkin_construction) (of [Leon Henkin](wikipedia://en/356165)) to what are now called [Henkin–Keisler models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henkin–Keisler_model).[^o4] He is also known for the [Rudin–Keisler ordering](wikipedia://en/31911#Ordering_on_ultrafilters) along with [Mary Ellen Rudin](wikipedia://en/2976742).

He held the named chair of [Vilas Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilas_Professor_of_Mathematics) at Wisconsin.

Among Keisler's graduate students, several have made notable mathematical contributions, including [Frederick Rowbottom](wikipedia://en/3889349) who discovered [Rowbottom cardinals](wikipedia://en/3317885). Several others have gone on to careers in computer science research and product development, including: Michael Benedikt, a professor of computer science at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), [Kevin J. Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_J._Compton), a professor of computer science at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), Curtis Tuckey, a developer of software-based collaboration environments; [Joseph Sgro](wikipedia://en/33792013), a neurologist and developer of [vision processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vision_processor) hardware and software, and [Edward L. Wimmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_L._Wimmers), a database researcher at [IBM Almaden Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Almaden_Research_Center).

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5]

His son Jeffrey Keisler is a [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright) Distinguished Chair at the University of Massachusetts, Boston, College of Management.[^o6]
