# Guido Fubini

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Guido Fubini"}

**Guido Fubini**[^o3] (19 January 1879 – 6 June 1943) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for [Fubini's theorem](wikipedia://en/255245) and the [Fubini–Study metric](wikipedia://en/2574486).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), he was steered towards mathematics at an early age by his teachers and his father, who was himself a teacher of mathematics. In 1896 he entered the [Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa), where he studied [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) under [Ulisse Dini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulisse_Dini) and [Luigi Bianchi](wikipedia://en/701065). His 1900 doctoral thesis was about [Clifford's parallelism](wikipedia://en/22384832) in [elliptic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_space).[^o5]

After earning his doctorate, he took up a series of professorships. In 1901 he began teaching at the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania) in [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily); shortly afterwards he moved to the [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa); and in 1908 he moved to the [Politecnico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politecnico_di_Torino) in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) and then the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin), where he stayed for a few decades.

During this time his research focused primarily on topics in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), especially [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759); but he also studied the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610), and [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), among other topics. With the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), he shifted his work towards more applied topics, studying the accuracy of artillery fire; after the war, he continued in an applied direction, applying results from this work to problems in [electrical circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_circuit) and [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics).

In 1938, when Fubini at the age of 59 was nearing retirement, [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini)'s [Fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascists) adopted the [anti-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Jewish) policies advocated for several years by [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)'s [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis). As a [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew), Fubini feared for the safety of his family, and so accepted an invitation by [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) to teach there; he died in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) four years later.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

A main-belt asteroid, [22495 Fubini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22495_Fubini), was named in his honour.
