# Valuation (algebra)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Valuation"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) (in particular in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) or [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/28730822)), a **valuation** is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) on a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) that provides a measure of the size or multiplicity of elements of the field. It generalizes to [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) the notion of size inherent in consideration of the degree of a [pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_%28complex_analysis%29) or [multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790) of a [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_%28complex_analysis%29) in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), the degree of divisibility of a number by a prime number in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and the geometrical concept of [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28geometry%29) between two [algebraic](wikipedia://en/248808) or [analytic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_variety) in algebraic geometry. In all of these examples, the valuation assumes integer values and is therefore called a **discrete valuation**, but in general, the integers are replaced by an [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [totally ordered group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_group).

:::

A field with a valuation on it is called a **valued field**.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Discrete valuations {#Discrete_valuations}

A **discrete valuation** on a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427):[^n1]

$$\nu:K\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$$

satisfying the conditions:

$$\nu(x\cdot y)=\nu(x)+\nu(y)$$

$$\nu(x+y)\geq\min\big\{\nu(x),\nu(y)\big\}$$

$$\nu(x)=\infty\iff x=0$$

for all $x,y\in K$.

Note that often the trivial valuation which takes on only the values $0,\infty$ is explicitly excluded.

A field with a non-trivial discrete valuation is called a **discrete valuation field**.

### Relation to discrete valuation rings {#Relation_to_discrete_valuation_rings}

To every field $K$ with discrete valuation $\nu$ we can associate the subring

$$\mathcal{O}_\nu := \left\{ x \in K \mid \nu(x) \geq 0 \right\}$$

of $K$, which is a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633). Conversely, the valuation $\nu: A \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}$ on a discrete valuation ring $A$ can be extended in a unique way to a discrete valuation on the [quotient field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_field) $K=\text{Quot}(A)$; the associated discrete valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_\nu$ is just $A$.

Discrete valuation rings $\mathcal{O}_\nu$ are [local rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_rings) with maximal ideal

$$\mathfrak{m}_\nu := \left\{ x \in \mathcal{O}_\nu \mid \nu(x) > 0 \right\},$$

so there is a notion of *residue field* $\kappa=\mathcal{O}_\nu/\mathfrak{m}_\nu$. Some authors call $\mathcal{O}_\nu$ a *local $K$-algebra* when $\kappa$ and $K$ are isomorphic.

### General (Krull) valuations {#General_(Krull)_valuations}

One starts with the following objects:

- a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ and its [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/1110742) *K*<sup>×</sup>,
- an [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [totally ordered group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_group) $(\Gamma, +, \ge)$.

The ordering and [group law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_law) on $\Gamma$ are extended to the set $\Gamma \cup \{\infty$} by the rules

- $\infty \ge \alpha$ for all $\alpha$ ∈ $\Gamma$,
- $\infty + \alpha = \alpha + \infty = \infty + \infty = \infty$ for all $\alpha$ ∈ $\Gamma$.

Then a **valuation of $K$** is any [map](wikipedia://en/516931)

$$v : K \to \Gamma \cup \{\infty\}$$

that satisfies the following properties for all *a*, *b* in *K*:

- $v(a) = \infty$ if and only if $a = 0$,
- $$v(ab) = v(a) + v(b),$$
- $v(a + b) \ge \min(v(a), v(b))$, with equality if *v*(*a*) ≠ *v*(*b*).

A valuation *v* is **trivial** if *v*(*a*) = 0 for all *a* in *K*<sup>×</sup>, otherwise it is **non-trivial**.

The second property asserts that any valuation is a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) on *K*<sup>×</sup>. The third property is a version of the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941) on [metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_spaces) adapted to an arbitrary $\Gamma$ (see #Multiplicative notation below). For valuations used in [geometric](wikipedia://en/2202) applications, the first property implies that any non-empty [germ](wikipedia://en/352714) of an analytic variety near a point contains that point.

The valuation can be interpreted as the order of the [leading-order term](wikipedia://en/37503542). The third property then corresponds to the order of a sum being the order of the larger term, unless the two terms have the same order, in which case they may cancel and the sum may have larger order.

For many applications, $\Gamma$ is an additive subgroup of the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$ in which case ∞ can be interpreted as +∞ in the [extended real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_real_numbers); note that $\min(a, +\infty) = \min(+\infty, a) = a$ for any real number *a*, and thus +∞ is the unit under the binary operation of minimum. The real numbers (extended by +∞) with the operations of minimum and addition form a [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468), called the min [tropical semiring](wikipedia://en/3892336), and a valuation *v* is almost a semiring homomorphism from *K* to the tropical semiring, except that the homomorphism property can fail when two elements with the same valuation are added together.

### Associated objects {#Associated_objects}

Similarly to the discrete case, there are several objects defined from a given valuation $v : K \to \Gamma \cup \{\infty\}$;

- the **value group** or **valuation group** $\Gamma_{v}$ = *v*(*K*<sup>×</sup>), a subgroup of $\Gamma$ (though *v* is usually assumed surjective so that $\Gamma_{v} = \Gamma$),
- the **[valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856)** *R<sub>v</sub>* is the set (subring) of *a* ∈ $K$ with *v*(*a*) ≥ 0,
- the **prime ideal** *m<sub>v</sub>* is the set of *a* ∈ *K* with *v*(*a*) > 0 (it is in fact a [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) of *R<sub>v</sub>*, and the only such),
- the **residue field** *k<sub>v</sub>* = *R<sub>v</sub>*/*m<sub>v</sub>*.

### Multiplicative notation and absolute values {#Multiplicative_notation_and_absolute_values}

The concept was developed by [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) in his book [*Geometric Algebra*](wikipedia://en/31102294) writing the group in [multiplicative notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_notation) as $(\Gamma, \cdot, \ge)$:[^o10]

Instead of ∞, we adjoin a formal symbol *O* to Γ, with the ordering and group law extended by the rules

- $O \le \alpha$ for all $\alpha$ ∈ $\Gamma$,
- $O \cdot \alpha = \alpha \cdot O = O$ for all $\alpha$ ∈ $\Gamma$.

Then a *valuation* of $K$ is any map

$$| \cdot |_{v} : K \to \Gamma \cup \{O\}$$

satisfying the following properties for all *a*, *b* ∈ *K*:

- $|a|_{v} = O$ if and only if $a = 0$,
- $$|ab|_{v} = |a|_{v} \cdot |b|_{v},$$
- $|a+b|_{v} \le \max(|a|_{v}, |b|_{v})$, with equality if $|a|_{v} \ne |b|_{v}$.

(Note that the directions of the inequalities are reversed from those in the additive notation.)

If $\Gamma$ is a subgroup of the [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571) under multiplication, the last condition is the [ultrametric](wikipedia://en/333996) inequality, a stronger form of the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941) $|a+b|_{v} \le |a|_{v} + |b|_{v}$, and $| \cdot |_{v}$ is an [absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667). In this case, we may pass to the additive notation with value group $\Gamma_+ \subseteq (\mathbb{R}, +)$ by taking $v_{+}(a) = -\log |a|_{v}$.

Each valuation on $K$ defines a corresponding linear [preorder](wikipedia://en/23582): $a \preccurlyeq b \Leftrightarrow |a|_{v} \le |b|_{v}$. Conversely, given a "$\preccurlyeq$" satisfying the required properties, we can define valuation $|a|_{v} = \{b: b \preccurlyeq a \land a \preccurlyeq b$}, with multiplication and ordering based on $K$ and $\preccurlyeq$.

::: {#o12 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

### Terminology {#Terminology}

In this article, we use the terms defined above, in the additive notation. However, some authors use alternative terms:

- our "valuation" (satisfying the ultrametric inequality) is called an "exponential valuation" or "non-Archimedean absolute value" or "ultrametric absolute value";
- our "absolute value" (satisfying the triangle inequality) is called a "valuation" or an "Archimedean absolute value".

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

### Equivalence of valuations and relation to valuation rings {#Equivalence_of_valuations_and_relation_to_valuation_rings}

Two valuations *v*<sub>1</sub> and *v*<sub>2</sub> of $K$ with valuation group Γ<sub>1</sub> and Γ<sub>2</sub>, respectively, are said to be **equivalent** if there is an order-preserving [group isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397) $\varphi : \Gamma_{1} \to \Gamma_{2}$ such that *v*<sub>2</sub>(*a*) = φ(*v*<sub>1</sub>(*a*)) for all *a* in *K*<sup>×</sup>. This is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259).

Two valuations of *K* are equivalent if and only if they have the same valuation ring. On the other hand, any [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856) *O* of *K* is the valuation ring of a canonical valuation $v : K \to \Gamma \cup \{\infty\}$, where $\Gamma = K^{\times}/O^{\times}$, an abelian group linearly ordered by $xO^{\times} \le yO^{\times}$ whenever $y/x \in O$, and *v* is the quotient map $v(x) = xO^{\times}$.

An [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of valuations of a field is called a [**place**](wikipedia://en/5042667#Places). *[Ostrowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/3360343)* gives a complete classification of places of the field of [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers) $\mathbb{Q}:$ these are precisely the equivalence classes of valuations for the [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) [completions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) of $\mathbb{Q}.$

### Extension of valuations {#Extension_of_valuations}

Let *v* be a valuation of $K$ and let *L* be a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of $K$. An **extension of *v*** (to *L*) is a valuation *w* of *L* such that the [restriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_restriction) of *w* to $K$ is *v*. The set of all such extensions is studied in the [ramification theory of valuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramification_theory_of_valuations).

Let *L*/*K* be a [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) and let *w* be an extension of *v* to *L*. The [index](wikipedia://en/230581) of Γ<sub>*v*</sub> in Γ<sub>*w*</sub>, e(*w*/*v*) = \[Γ<sub>*w*</sub> : Γ<sub>*v*</sub>\], is called the **reduced ramification index** of *w* over *v*. It satisfies e(*w*/*v*) ≤ \[*L* : *K*\] (the [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) of the extension *L*/*K*). The **relative degree** of *w* over *v* is defined to be *f*(*w*/*v*) = \[*R<sub>w</sub>*/*m<sub>w</sub>* : *R<sub>v</sub>*/*m<sub>v</sub>*\] (the degree of the extension of residue fields). It is also less than or equal to the degree of *L*/*K*. When *L*/*K* is [separable](wikipedia://en/363340), the **ramification index** of *w* over *v* is defined to be e(*w*/*v*)*p<sup>i</sup>*, where *p<sup>i</sup>* is the [inseparable degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inseparable_degree) of the extension *R<sub>w</sub>*/*m<sub>w</sub>* over *R<sub>v</sub>*/*m<sub>v</sub>*.

::: {#o18 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Complete valued field"}

### Complete valued fields {#Complete_valued_fields}

When the ordered abelian group $\Gamma$ is the additive group of the [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers), the associated valuation is equivalent to an absolute value, and hence induces a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) on the field $K$. If $K$ is [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) with respect to this metric, then it is called a **complete valued field**. If *K* is not complete, one can use this metric to construct its [Cauchy completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_completion) and obtain a unique valuation extending the one on $K$, as in the examples below; different valuations can define different completion fields.

In general, a valuation induces a [uniform structure](wikipedia://en/32339) on $K$, and $K$ is called a complete valued field if it is [complete](wikipedia://en/32339#Completeness) as a uniform space. There is a related property known as [spherical completeness](wikipedia://en/33175921): it is equivalent to completeness if $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z},$ but stronger in general.

## Examples {#Examples}

### p-adic valuation {#p-adic_valuation}

An elementary example is the [$p$-adic valuation](wikipedia://en/16047618) ν<sub>*p*</sub> associated to a prime integer *p*, on the rational numbers $K=\mathbb{Q},$ with valuation ring $R=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)},$ where $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ is the localization of $\mathbb{Z}$ at the prime ideal $(p)$. The valuation group is the additive integers $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z};$ the residue field is $k=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}/p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.$ For an integer $a \in R= \mathbb{Z},$ the valuation ν<sub>*p*</sub>(*a*) measures the divisibility of *a* by powers of *p*:

$$\nu_p(a) = \max\{e \in \mathbb{Z} \mid p^e \text{ divides } a\};$$

and for a fraction, ν<sub>*p*</sub>(*a*/*b*) = ν<sub>*p*</sub>(*a*) − ν<sub>*p*</sub>(*b*).

Writing this multiplicatively yields the [$p$-adic absolute value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_absolute_value), which conventionally has as base $1/p = p^{-1}$, so $|a|_p := p^{-\nu_p(a)}$.

The completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ with respect to ν<sub>*p*</sub> is the field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of [p-adic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_numbers).

The *p*-adic valuation on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ extends uniquely to its [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, which has valuation group $\Gamma=\mathbb{Q}$ and the [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ as its valuation ring. Hence, $\mathbb{C}_p$, the completion of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ (which is again [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed)), also has valuation group $\mathbb{Q}$.[^o22]

### Order of vanishing {#Order_of_vanishing}

Given a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) $X$, we can consider the field $K=M(X)$ of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) $X\to\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}$. For a fixed point $p\in X$, we define a discrete valuation on $K$ as follows: $\nu(f)=j$ if and only if $j$ is the largest integer such that the function $f(z)/(z-p)^j$ can be extended to a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) at $p$. This means: if $\nu(f)=j>0$ then $f$ has a root of order $j$ at the point $p$; if $\nu(f)=j<0$ then $f$ has a [pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_%28complex_analysis%29) of order $-j$ at $p$. In a similar manner, one also defines a discrete valuation on the [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) for every regular point $p$ on the curve.

Let K = **F**(x), the rational functions on the affine line **X** = **F**<sup>1</sup>, and take a point *a* ∈ X. For a polynomial $f(x) = a_k (x{-}a)^k + a_{k+1}(x{-}a)^{k+1}+\cdots+ a_n(x{-}a)^n$ with $a_k\neq 0$, define *v*<sub>*a*</sub>(*f*) = k, the order of vanishing at *x* = *a*; and *v*<sub>*a*</sub>(*f* /*g*) = *v*<sub>*a*</sub>(*f*) − *v*<sub>*a*</sub>(*g*). Then the valuation ring *R* consists of rational functions with no pole at *x* = *a*, and the completion is the [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) ring **F**((*x*−*a*)). This can be generalized to the field of [Puiseux series](wikipedia://en/2604429) *K*{{*t*}} (fractional powers), the [Levi-Civita field](wikipedia://en/22105661) (its Cauchy completion), and the field of [Hahn series](wikipedia://en/21368973), with valuation in all cases returning the smallest exponent of *t* appearing in the series.

### $\pi$-adic valuation {#-adic_valuation}

Generalizing the previous examples, let $R$ be a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039), $K$ be its [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271), and $\pi$ be an [irreducible element](wikipedia://en/60693) of $R$. Since every principal ideal domain is a [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546), every non-zero element *a* of $R$ can be written (essentially) uniquely as

$$a=\pi^{e_a}p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_n^{e_n}$$

where the *e<sub>i</sub>* are non-negative integers and the *p<sub>i</sub>* are irreducible elements of $R$ that are not [associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_%28ring_theory%29) of $\pi$. In particular, the integer *e<sub>a</sub>* is uniquely determined by *a*.

The **π-adic valuation of *K*** is then given by

- $$v_\pi(0)=\infty$$
- $$v_\pi(a/b)=e_a-e_b,\text{ for }a,b\in R, a, b\neq0.$$

If π' is another irreducible element of $R$ such that (π') = (π) (that is, they generate the same ideal in $R$), then the π-adic valuation and the π'-adic valuation are equal. Thus, the π-adic valuation can be called the *P*-adic valuation, where *P* = (π). Its valuation ring is *R<sub>P</sub>*.

::: {#o26 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Dedekind valuation"}

### *P*-adic valuation on a Dedekind domain {#P-adic_valuation_on_a_Dedekind_domain}

The previous example can be generalized to [Dedekind domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_domains). Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain, $K$ its field of fractions, and let $P$ be a non-zero prime ideal of $R$. Then, the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) of $R$ at $P$, denoted *R<sub>P</sub>*, is a principal ideal domain whose field of fractions is $K$. The construction of the previous section applied to the prime ideal *PR<sub>P</sub>* of *R<sub>P</sub>* yields the **$P$-adic valuation of $K$**.

For instance, if $R$ is the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) $K$, the completion of $K$ with respect to a $P$-adic valuation is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ for the prime number *p* with $P\cap\mathbb{Q}=(p)$, a (non-Archimedean) [local field](wikipedia://en/145375).

## Vector spaces over valuation fields {#Vector_spaces_over_valuation_fields}

Suppose that $\Gamma$ ∪ {0} is the set of non-negative real numbers under multiplication. Then we say that the valuation is **non-discrete** if its range (the valuation group) is infinite (and hence has an [accumulation point](wikipedia://en/226505) at 0).

Suppose that *X* is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over *K* and that *A* and *B* are subsets of *X*. Then we say that ***A* absorbs *B*** if there exists a *α* ∈ *K* such that *λ* ∈ *K* and *\|λ| ≥ |α|* implies that *B ⊆ λ A*. *A* is called **radial** or **absorbing** if *A* absorbs every finite subset of *X*. Radial subsets of *X* are invariant under finite intersection. Also, *A* is called **circled** if *λ* in *K* and *\|λ| ≥ |α|* implies *λ A ⊆ A*. The set of circled subsets of *L* is invariant under arbitrary intersections. The **circled hull** of *A* is the intersection of all circled subsets of *X* containing *A*.

Suppose that *X* and *Y* are vector spaces over a non-discrete valuation field *K*, let *A ⊆ X*, *B ⊆ Y*, and let *f : X → Y* be a linear map. If *B* is circled or radial then so is $f^{-1}(B)$. If *A* is circled then so is *f(A)* but if *A* is radial then *f(A)* will be radial under the additional condition that *f* is surjective.
