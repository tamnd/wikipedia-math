# Costate equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Costate equation"}

The **costate equation** is related to the state equation used in [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565).[^o2][^o3] It is also referred to as **auxiliary**, **adjoint**, **influence**, or **multiplier equation**. It is stated as a [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometry%29) of first order [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309)

:::

$$\dot{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}$$

where the right-hand side is the vector of [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) of the negative of the [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/5987577) with respect to the state variables.

## Interpretation {#Interpretation}

The costate variables $\lambda(t)$ can be interpreted as [Lagrange multipliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_multipliers) associated with the state equations. The state equations represent constraints of the minimization problem, and the costate variables represent the [marginal cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_cost) of violating those constraints; in economic terms the costate variables are the [shadow prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_price).[^o5][^o6]

## Solution {#Solution}

The state equation is subject to an initial condition and is solved forwards in time. The costate equation must satisfy a [transversality condition](wikipedia://en/9900080) and is solved backwards in time, from the final time towards the beginning. For more details see [Pontryagin's maximum principle](wikipedia://en/831689).[^o8]
