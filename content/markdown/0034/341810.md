# Joseph Liouville

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Liouville"}

Liouville

**Joseph Liouville** ( ; ; 24 March 1809 – 8 September 1882)[^o3][^o4] was a French mathematician who worked on a number of different fields in mathematics, including [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).[^o5] Many mathematical concepts are named after him, including [Liouville's theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/312301) and [Liouville's theorem for complex numbers](wikipedia://en/312293).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

He was born in [Saint-Omer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Omer,_Pas-de-Calais) in France on 24 March 1809.[^o7][^o8] His parents were Claude-Joseph Liouville (an army officer) and Thérèse Liouville (née Balland).

Liouville gained admission to the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in 1825 and graduated in 1827. Just like [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842) before him, Liouville studied engineering at [École des Ponts et Chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_des_Ponts_et_Chaussées) after graduating from the Polytechnique, but opted instead for a career in mathematics.[^o5] After some years as an assistant at various institutions including the [École Centrale Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Centrale_Paris), he was appointed as professor at the École Polytechnique in 1838. He began delivering lectures on mathematics at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) in 1851 secured a chair in [rational mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) at the Faculté des Sciences in 1857. However, he suffered under a heavy teaching load and his health started to deteriorate.[^o5]

Liouville founded the *[Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_de_Mathématiques_Pures_et_Appliquées)* in 1836, basing it on *[Crelle's Journal](wikipedia://en/973547)*. It soon became a leading mathematical periodical in France and became known as "Liouville's Journal" even after he had resigned as editor-in-chief in 1875.[^o5] He was elected to the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1839, and became an associate member of the [Bureau des Longitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_des_Longitudes).[^o5]

Liouville was also involved in politics for some time, and he became a member of the [Constituting Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_France) in following the [1848 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution_of_1848). However, following the rise of [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) to power, Liouville ended his political activities.[^o5]

In 1851, he was elected a foreign member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1853, he was elected as a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o9] As a mathematician, he maintained contact with many foreign colleagues, including William Thomson ([Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin)), [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340), and [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002).[^o5] As a lecturer, he offered support and encouragement to many young talents, among them, [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422), [Joseph Bertrand](wikipedia://en/488196), and [Joseph-Alfred Serret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph-Alfred_Serret).[^o5]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

### Analysis, algebra, and number theory {#Analysis,_algebra,_and_number_theory}

*Image: Journal math liouville.jpg.* Title page of the first volume of *Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées* in 1836.

In 1835, Liouville established the existence of [non-elementary integrals](wikipedia://en/1520379) and [a criterion](wikipedia://en/9482601) for integration in finite terms, that is, in terms of [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412).[^o5] He gave an example of such a non-elementary integral, $\int \frac{e^x}{x} \, dx$.[^o12]

In 1838, Liouville published a method for establishing the existence of solutions to [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) of the second order involving successive approximations, now attached to the name of [Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000), who gave [a more general approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard's_Method) in the early 1890s.[^o18]

In algebra, Liouville was one of the first to grasp the significance of the contributions of the late [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815), whose work had been forwarded to him by Auguste Chevalier, a friend of Galois.[^o13] Liouville edited and published the work of Galois in his own journal in 1846, after which the [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) attracted the attention of many mathematicians, among them, [Paolo Ruffini](wikipedia://en/547426), [Joseph-Alfred Serret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph-Alfred_Serret), and [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842).[^o18] By helping to popularize the works of Galois, Liouville participated, albeit indirectly, in the development of [modern algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) in general and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) in particular.[^o14]

Research on the solutions of algebraic equations spurred interest in [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1158) and transcendental [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689).[^o18] In 1844,[^o15] Liouville was the first to prove the existence of [transcendental numbers](wikipedia://en/30325). He did so by demonstrating some results on approximating algebraic irrationals using rational numbers and established [an inequality](wikipedia://en/50964) that served as a criterion for transcendence.[^o18] In essence, his inequality proclaims that rationals are poor approximations of irrational algebraic numbers.[^o12] He then gave an explicit example. He showed that any number of the form

$$\frac{a_1}{10} + \frac{a_2}{10^{2!}} + \frac{a_3}{10^{3!}} + \cdots,$$

where the $a_i$ are integers from 0 to 9, is transcendental.[^o18] Future demonstrations of the transcendence of [Euler's number](wikipedia://en/9633) $e$ by Charles Hermite and of $\pi$ ([pi](wikipedia://en/23601)) by [Ferdinand von Lindemann](wikipedia://en/335736) were based on Liouville's contributions.[^o12]

The [Liouville function](wikipedia://en/104463), an important concept in number theory, is named in his honor.

In his work on [elliptic integrals](wikipedia://en/9960), he based the whole subject on the general properties of [doubly periodic functions](wikipedia://en/3643495) and he demonstrated the [transcendence](wikipedia://en/30325) of [Abelian functions](wikipedia://en/1138853).[^o18] It was in this context that he discovered [Liouville's theorem](wikipedia://en/312293) in complex analysis, bounded [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372) are constant.[^o5] He also gave a short proof of the [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414).[^o14]

A similar result is Liouville's theorem for [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762), or solutions to [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941). It states that bounded harmonic functions in Euclidean space are constant. [Edward Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561) gave a short proof in 1961, exploiting the mean-value property of harmonic functions.[^o16]

### Mathematical physics {#Mathematical_physics}

Since the middle eighteenth century, mathematicians and physicists had been studying a variety of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) with [boundary values](wikipedia://en/436779) using the [separation of variables](wikipedia://en/387750#Partial_differential_equations) to resolve them into systems ordinary differential equations, which carried their own parameters. Solutions found for specific values of these parameters, called [eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/2161429), were known as [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601). The separation of variables in different coordinate systems led to new special functions, such as the [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700) and [Legendre polynomials](wikipedia://en/100349), as eigenfunctions of ordinary differential equations.[^o18] Liouville and his friend, [Jacques Charles François Sturm](wikipedia://en/1181554), sought to tackle the general problem for any linear differential equations of the second order. In a series papers published in the 1830s, the two men established the [Sturm–Liouville theory](wikipedia://en/490990).[^o18] Though the significance of this contribution took some time for mathematicians to grasp,[^o19] it is now a standard procedure to solve certain types of [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234).[^o20] Their work was inspired by the analysis of [heat diffusion](wikipedia://en/166415) in a cylinder by [Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Joseph_Fourier),[^o20] and may be viewed as a generalization of [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659).[^o19] In its original formulation, the Sturm–Liouville theory was not fully rigorous in that it did not adequately address the completeness of the set of eigenfunctions (or [orthogonal basis set)](wikipedia://en/755400) and the convergence of the expansion in terms of the eigenfunctions.[^o18] These issues were subsequently addressed by mathematicians such as [Maxime Bôcher](wikipedia://en/3180285), [Max Mason](wikipedia://en/3186969), and [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916).[^o19]

Independent of [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573), Liouville studied special integral equations and employed [a method of successive substitutions](wikipedia://en/203807), anticipating [Carl Neumann](wikipedia://en/915551) by a few decades. However, his contributions in this domain were subsequently subsumed by the work of [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra), a pioneer of the general theory of integral equations.[^o18] Liouville's research on the Sturm–Liouville theory and linear integral equations constituted early advances in the study of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).[^o14]

In 1837,[^o21] Liouville sought approximate solutions to linear second-order differential equations with spatially varying coefficients and obtained, in modern language, an [asymptotic series](wikipedia://en/642090).[^o18] [George Green](wikipedia://en/243343) independently found this technique in the same year in a paper on water waves in a canal.[^o22] The [Liouville–Green method](wikipedia://en/700154) was rediscovered in 1923 by [Harold Jeffreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Jeffreys),[^o23] and again in 1926 by [Gregor Wentzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Wentzel), [Hans Kramers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kramers), [Léon Brillouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Léon_Brillouin), who were studying the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) of quantum mechanics.[^o18]

*Image: Simple Harmonic Motion Orbit.gif.* An animation illustrating Liouville's theorem for [simple harmonic motion](wikipedia://en/28437). The area of the phase-space portrait is constant.

Liouville was attracted to the study of mechanics thanks to the works of [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) and [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^o14] Liouville proved in a 1838 paper on differential equations[^o24] that [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) volume of a conservative mechanical system constant, a result now known as [Liouville's theorem](wikipedia://en/312301) in [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319).[^o5] Following [Josiah Willard Gibbs](wikipedia://en/37332), Liouville's theorem is recognized a fundamental result for statistical mechanics.[^o25] In a related context, Liouville introduced the notion of [action-angle coordinates](wikipedia://en/5435566) as a description of completely [integrable systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_systems). The modern formulation of this is sometimes called the [Liouville–Arnold theorem](wikipedia://en/40071018), and the underlying concept of integrability is referred to as Liouville integrability.

In his study of electrodynamics, Liouville developed the [Riemann–Liouville integral](wikipedia://en/567743) to consider differentiation and integration of a [fractional order](wikipedia://en/229939).[^o26]

He also studied [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342)[^o5] and ellipsoidal figures of equilibrium, including those of unequal axes, that is, [Jacobi ellipsoids](wikipedia://en/34439226).[^o14]

His unpublished manuscripts indicated that he had already known of the [Rayleigh–Ritz method](wikipedia://en/2111994) for approximating the eigenvalues of a [boundary-value problem](wikipedia://en/436779) as early as 1845, decades before William Strutt ([Lord Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Rayleigh)) introduced it his *Theory of Sound* (1877).[^o5]
