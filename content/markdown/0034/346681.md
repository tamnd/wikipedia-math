# Σ-compact space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Σ-compact space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is said to be ***σ*-compact** if it is the union of [countably](wikipedia://en/6026) many [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [subspaces](wikipedia://en/910926).[^o2]

:::

A space is said to be ***σ*-locally compact** if it is both *σ*-compact and [(weakly) locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_locally_compact).[^o3] That terminology can be somewhat confusing as it does not fit the usual pattern of σ-(property) meaning a countable union of spaces satisfying (property); that's why such spaces are more commonly referred to explicitly as *σ-compact (weakly) locally compact*, which is also equivalent to being [exhaustible by compact sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhaustible_by_compact_sets).[^o4]

## Properties and examples {#Properties_and_examples}

- Every [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042) is *σ*-compact, and every *σ*-compact space is [Lindelöf](wikipedia://en/310480) (i.e. every [open cover](wikipedia://en/317552) has a countable [subcover](wikipedia://en/317552)).[^o6] The reverse implications do not hold, for example, standard [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) (**R**<sup>*n*</sup>) is *σ*-compact but not compact,[^o7] and the [lower limit topology](wikipedia://en/310517) on the real line is Lindelöf but not *σ*-compact.[^o8] In fact, the [countable complement topology](wikipedia://en/801246) on any uncountable set is Lindelöf but neither *σ*-compact nor locally compact.[^o9] However, it is true that any locally compact Lindelöf space is *σ*-compact.
- (The [irrational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_numbers)) $\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$ is not *σ*-compact.[^o10]
- A [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637), [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550) that is also *σ*-compact, must be [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) at at least one point.
- If *G* is a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) and *G* is locally compact at one point, then *G* is locally compact everywhere. Therefore, the previous property tells us that if *G* is a *σ*-compact, Hausdorff topological group that is also a Baire space, then *G* is locally compact. This shows that for Hausdorff topological groups that are also Baire spaces, *σ*-compactness implies local compactness.
- The previous property implies for instance that **R**<sup>*ω*</sup> is not *σ*-compact: if it were *σ*-compact, it would necessarily be locally compact since **R**<sup>*ω*</sup> is a topological group that is also a Baire space.
- Every [hemicompact space](wikipedia://en/5076058) is *σ*-compact.[^o11] The converse, however, is not true;[^o12] for example, the space of [rationals](wikipedia://en/19727024), with the usual topology, is *σ*-compact but not hemicompact.
- The [product](wikipedia://en/24910) of a finite number of *σ*-compact spaces is *σ*-compact. However the product of an infinite number of *σ*-compact spaces may fail to be *σ*-compact.[^o13]
- A *σ*-compact space *X* is second category (respectively Baire) if and only if the set of points at which is *X* is locally compact is nonempty (respectively dense) in *X*.[^o14]
