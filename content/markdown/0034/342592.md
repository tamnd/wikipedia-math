# Touchard polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Touchard polynomials"}

*Image: Touchard\_Polynomials.png.* Touchard Polynomials

The **Touchard polynomials**, studied by (txt),[^o3] also called the **exponential polynomials** or **Bell polynomials**, comprise a [polynomial sequence](wikipedia://en/195990) of [binomial type](wikipedia://en/200077) defined by

:::

$$T_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n S(n,k)x^k=\sum_{k=0}^n \left\{ {n \atop k} \right\}x^k,$$

where $S(n,k)=\left\{ {n \atop k} \right\}$ is a [Stirling number of the second kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling_number_of_the_second_kind), i.e., the number of [partitions of a set](wikipedia://en/340240) of size *n* into *k* disjoint non-empty subsets.[^o4][^o5][^o6]

The first few Touchard polynomials are

$$T_1(x)=x,$$

$$T_2(x)=x^2+x,$$

$$T_3(x)=x^3+3x^2+x,$$

$$T_4(x)=x^4+6x^3+7x^2+x,$$

$$T_5(x)=x^5+10x^4+25x^3+15x^2+x.$$

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

The value at 1 of the *n*th Touchard polynomial is the *n*th [Bell number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_numbers), i.e., the number of [partitions of a set](wikipedia://en/340240) of size *n*:

$$T_n(1)=B_n.$$

If *X* is a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) λ, then its *n*th moment is E(*X*<sup>*n*</sup>) = *T*<sub>*n*</sub>(λ), leading to the definition:

$$T_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac {x^k k^n} {k!}.$$

Using this fact one can quickly prove that this [polynomial sequence](wikipedia://en/195990) is of [binomial type](wikipedia://en/200077), i.e., it satisfies the sequence of identities:

$$T_n(\lambda+\mu)=\sum_{k=0}^n {n \choose k} T_k(\lambda) T_{n-k}(\mu).$$

The Touchard polynomials constitute the only polynomial sequence of binomial type with the coefficient of *x* equal 1 in every polynomial.

The Touchard polynomials satisfy the Rodrigues-like formula:

$$T_n \left(e^x \right) = e^{-e^x} \frac{d^n}{dx^n}\;e^{e^x}.$$

The Touchard polynomials satisfy the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$T_{n+1}(x)=x \left(T_{n}(x) + T'_{n}(x)\right)$$

and

$$T_{n+1}(x)=x\sum_{k=0}^n{n \choose k}T_k(x).$$

In the case *x* = 1, this reduces to the recurrence formula for the [Bell numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_numbers).

A generalization of both this formula and the definition, is a generalization of Spivey's formula[^o12]

$$T_{n+m}(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \left\{ {n \atop k} \right\} x^k \sum_{j=0}^m \binom{m}{j} k^{m-j} T_j(x)$$

Using the [umbral notation](wikipedia://en/214124) *T*<sup>*n*</sup>(*x*)=*T*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*), these formulas become:

$$T_n(\lambda+\mu)=\left(T(\lambda)+T(\mu) \right)^n,$$

$$T_{n+1}(x)=x \left(1+T(x) \right)^n.$$

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the Touchard polynomials is

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty {T_n(x) \over n!} t^n=e^{x\left(e^t-1\right)},$$

which corresponds to the [generating function of Stirling numbers of the second kind](wikipedia://en/2229292#Generating_functions).

Touchard polynomials have [contour integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_integral) representation:

$$T_n(x)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{e^{x({e^t}-1)}}{t^{n+1}}\,dt.$$

:::

### Zeroes {#Zeroes}

All zeroes of the Touchard polynomials are real and negative. This fact was observed by L. H. Harper in 1967.[^o14]

The absolute value of the leftmost zero is bounded from above by[^o15]

$$\frac1n\binom{n}{2}+\frac{n-1}{n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{2}^2-\frac{2n}{n-1}\left(\binom{n}{3}+3\binom{n}{4}\right)},$$

although it is conjectured that the leftmost zero grows linearly with the index *n*.

The [Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425) $M(T_n)$ of the Touchard polynomials can be estimated as follows:[^o16]

$$\frac{\lbrace\textstyle{n\atop \Omega_n}\rbrace}{\binom{n}{\Omega_n}}\le M(T_n)\le\sqrt{n+1}\left\{{n\atop K_n}\right\},$$

where $\Omega_n$ and $K_n$ are the smallest of the maximum two *k* indices such that $\lbrace\textstyle{n\atop k}\rbrace/\binom{n}{k}$ and $\lbrace\textstyle{n\atop k}\rbrace$ are maximal, respectively.

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

- Complete [Bell polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_polynomial) $B_n(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n)$ may be viewed as a multivariate generalization of Touchard polynomial $T_n(x)$, since $T_n(x) = B_n(x,x,\dots,x).$
- The Touchard polynomials (and thereby the [Bell numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_numbers)) can be generalized, using the real part of the above integral, to non-integer order:

$$T_n(x)=\frac{n!}{\pi} \int^{\pi}_0 e^{x \bigl(e^{\cos(\theta)} \cos(\sin(\theta))-1 \bigr)} \cos \bigl(x e^{\cos(\theta)} \sin(\sin(\theta)) -n\theta\bigr) \, d\theta\, .$$

:::
