# Sexy primes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sexy primes"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **sexy primes** are [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) that differ from another prime by $6$. For example, the numbers $5$ and $11$ are a pair of sexy primes, because both are prime and $11 - 5 = 6$.

:::

The term "sexy prime" is a [pun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pun) stemming from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word for six: sex.

If $p + 2$ or $p + 4$ (where $p$ is the lower prime) is also prime, then the sexy prime is part of a [prime triplet](wikipedia://en/7493205).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Sexy prime conjecture"}

## Sexy prime conjecture {#Sexy_prime_conjecture}

::: {#o4 .problem cue=unsolved}

Are there infinitely many sexy primes?

:::

The **sexy prime conjecture** is a weaker analogue of the twin prime conjecture. It asserts that there exist infinitely many pairs of prime numbers that differ by 6, known as *sexy primes*.

Let $p_{n}$ denote the $n$th prime number, and define

$$H_m = \liminf_{n \to \infty} (p_{n+m} - p_n)$$

The quantity $H_{m}$ is the smallest gap that occurs infinitely often between primes separated by $m$ positions in the sequence of prime numbers. In particular, the sexy prime conjecture is equivalent to the statement

$$H_1 \leq 6,$$

which asserts that prime gaps of size at most 6 occur infinitely often. Since every prime gap greater than 2 is even, this is equivalent to the existence of infinitely many sexy prime pairs.

In August 2014, the [Polymath](wikipedia://en/34858344) group, seeking the proof of the twin prime conjecture, showed that if the [generalized Elliott–Halberstam conjecture](wikipedia://en/1898427) is proven, one can show the existence of infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes that differ by at most 6 and as such they are either [twin](wikipedia://en/41997), [cousin](wikipedia://en/343085) or sexy primes[^o5]. Thus, the proof of the sexy prime conjecture is equivalent to the proof of the generalized Elliott-Halberstam conjecture.

:::
