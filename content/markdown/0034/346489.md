# Algebraic quantum field theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic quantum field theory"}

**Algebraic quantum field theory** (**AQFT**) is an application to **local quantum physics** of [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) theory. It is also referred to as the **Haag–Kastler [axiomatic framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_framework)** for [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), because it was introduced by (txt). The axioms are stated in terms of an algebra given for every open set in [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space), and mappings between those.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Haag–Kastler axioms"}

## Haag–Kastler axioms {#Haag–Kastler_axioms}

Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the set of all open and bounded subsets of Minkowski space. An algebraic quantum field theory is defined via a set $\{\mathcal{A}(O)\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}}$ of [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) $\mathcal{A}(O)$ on a common [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal{H}$ satisfying the following axioms:[^o5]

- *Isotony*: $O_1 \subset O_2$ implies $\mathcal{A}(O_1) \subset \mathcal{A}(O_2)$.
- *Causality*: If $O_1$ is space-like separated from $O_2$, then $[\mathcal{A}(O_1),\mathcal{A}(O_2)]=0$.
- *Poincaré covariance*: A strongly continuous unitary representation $U(\mathcal{P})$ of the Poincaré group $\mathcal{P}$ on $\mathcal{H}$ exists such that $\mathcal{A}(gO) = U(g) \mathcal{A}(O) U(g)^*,\,\,g \in \mathcal{P}.$
- *Spectrum condition*: The joint spectrum $\mathrm{Sp}(P)$ of the energy-momentum operator $P$ (i.e. the generator of space-time translations) is contained in the closed forward lightcone.
- *Existence of a vacuum vector*: A cyclic and Poincaré-invariant vector $\Omega\in\mathcal{H}$ exists.

The net algebras $\mathcal{A}(O)$ are called *local algebras* and the C\* algebra $\mathcal{A} := \overline{\bigcup_{O\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{A}(O)}$ is called the *quasilocal algebra*.

:::

## Category-theoretic formulation {#Category-theoretic_formulation}

Let **Mink** be the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) of [open subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) of Minkowski space M with [inclusion maps](wikipedia://en/492516) as [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891). We are given a [covariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_functor) $\mathcal{A}$ from **Mink** to **uC\*alg**, the category of [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) C\* algebras, such that every morphism in **Mink** maps to a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) in **uC\*alg** (**isotony**).

The [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487) acts [continuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28topology%29) on **Mink**. There exists a [pullback](wikipedia://en/9413508) of this [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29), which is continuous in the [norm topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_topology) of $\mathcal{A}(M)$ ([Poincaré covariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_covariance)).

Minkowski space has a [causal structure](wikipedia://en/14246049). If an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) *V* lies in the [causal complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_complement) of an open set *U*, then the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of the maps

$$\mathcal{A}(i_{U,U\cup V})$$

and

$$\mathcal{A}(i_{V,U\cup V})$$

[commute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_operation) (spacelike commutativity). If $\bar{U}$ is the [causal completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_completion) of an open set *U*, then $\mathcal{A}(i_{U,\bar{U}})$ is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) (primitive causality).

A [state](wikipedia://en/454959) with respect to a C\*-algebra is a [positive linear functional](wikipedia://en/454930) over it with unit [norm](wikipedia://en/990534). If we have a state over $\mathcal{A}(M)$, we can take the "[partial trace](wikipedia://en/814148)" to get states associated with $\mathcal{A}(U)$ for each open set via the [net](wikipedia://en/22170) [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538). The states over the open sets form a [presheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf) structure.

According to the [GNS construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNS_construction), for each state, we can associate a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of $\mathcal{A}(M).$ [Pure states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_state) correspond to [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) and [mixed states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_state_%28physics%29) correspond to [reducible representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reducible_representation). Each irreducible representation (up to [equivalence](wikipedia://en/9259)) is called a [superselection sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superselection_sector). We assume there is a pure state called the [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) such that the Hilbert space associated with it is a [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) of the [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487) compatible with the Poincaré covariance of the net such that if we look at the [Poincaré algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_algebra), the spectrum with respect to [energy-momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy-momentum_4-vector) (corresponding to [spacetime translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincare_group)) lies on and in the positive [light cone](wikipedia://en/310474). This is the vacuum sector.

## QFT in curved spacetime {#QFT_in_curved_spacetime}

More recently, the approach has been further implemented to include an algebraic version of [quantum field theory in curved spacetime](wikipedia://en/1057601). Indeed, the viewpoint of local quantum physics is in particular suitable to generalize the [renormalization](wikipedia://en/291453) procedure to the theory of quantum fields developed on curved backgrounds. Several rigorous results concerning QFT in presence of a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) have been obtained.
