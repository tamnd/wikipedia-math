# Simple theorems in the algebra of sets

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simple theorems in the algebra of sets"}

The **simple theorems in the algebra of sets** are some of the elementary properties of the [algebra](wikipedia://en/106364) of [union](wikipedia://en/47949) ([infix operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infix_operator): ∪), [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) (infix operator: ∩), and [set complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_complement) ([postfix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postfix_%28linguistics%29) ') of sets.

:::

These properties assume the existence of at least two sets: a given [universal set](wikipedia://en/303405), denoted **U**, and the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566), denoted {}. The algebra of sets describes the properties of all possible [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of **U**, called the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of **U** and denoted *P*(**U**). *P*(**U**) is assumed [closed](wikipedia://en/293802) under union, intersection, and set complement. The algebra of sets is an [interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) or [model](wikipedia://en/19858) of [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959), with union, intersection, set complement, **U**, and {} interpreting Boolean [sum](wikipedia://en/7962), [product](wikipedia://en/18152), [complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_negation), 1, and 0, respectively.

The properties below are stated without [proof](wikipedia://en/82285), but can be derived from a small number of properties taken as [axioms](wikipedia://en/928). A "\*" follows the algebra of sets interpretation of [Huntington's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_V._Huntington) (1904) classic postulate set for [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/54476844). These properties can be visualized with [Venn diagrams](wikipedia://en/61701). They also follow from the fact that *P*(**U**) is a [Boolean lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_lattice). The properties followed by "L" interpret the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) axioms.

Elementary [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) courses sometimes leave students with the impression that the subject matter of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) is no more than these properties. For more about elementary set theory, see [set](wikipedia://en/26691), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [algebra of sets](wikipedia://en/961805), and [naive set theory](wikipedia://en/4944). For an introduction to set theory at a higher level, see also [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory), [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173), [ordinal number](wikipedia://en/26547932), [Cantor–Bernstein–Schroeder theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor–Bernstein–Schroeder_theorem), [Cantor's diagonal argument](wikipedia://en/51426), [Cantor's first uncountability proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor's_first_uncountability_proof), [Cantor's theorem](wikipedia://en/341442), [well-ordering theorem](wikipedia://en/33458), [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), and [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442).

The properties below include a defined binary operation, [relative complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_complement), denoted by the infix operator "\\". The "relative complement of *A* in *B*," denoted *B* \\*A*, is defined as (*A* ∪*B*) and as *A* ∩*B*.

::: {#o2 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=inline-head name="PROPOSITION 1"}

**PROPOSITION 1**. For any **U** and any subset *A* of **U**:

- {} = **U**;
- *'U'* = {};
- *A* \\ {} = *A*;
- {} \\ *A* = {};
- *A* ∩ {} = {};
- *A* ∪ {} = *A*; \*
- *A* ∩ **U** = *A*; \*
- *A* ∪ **U** = **U**;
- *A* ∪ *A* = **U**; \*
- *A* ∩ *A* = {}; \*
- *A* \\ *A* = {};
- **U** \\ *A* = *A*;
- *A* \\ **U** = {};
- *A* = *A*;
- *A* ∩ *A* = *A*;
- *A* ∪ *A* = *A*.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=inline-head name="PROPOSITION 2"}

**PROPOSITION 2**. For any sets *A*, *B*, and *C*:

- *A* ∩ *B* = *B* ∩ *A*; \* L
- *A* ∪ *B* = *B* ∪ *A*; \* L
- *A* ∪ (*A* ∩ *B*) = *A*; L
- *A* ∩ (*A* ∪ *B*) = *A*; L
- (*A* ∪ *B*) \\ *A* = *B* \\ *A*;
- *A* ∩ *B* = {} [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *B* \\ *A* = *B*;
- (*A* ∪ *B*) ∪ (*A* ∪ *B*) = *A*;
- (*A* ∩ *B*) ∩ *C* = *A* ∩ (*B* ∩ *C*); L
- (*A* ∪ *B*) ∪ *C* = *A* ∪ (*B* ∪ *C*); L
- *C* \\ (*A* ∩ *B*) = (*C* \\ *A*) ∪ (*C* \\ *B*);
- *C* \\ (*A* ∪ *B*) = (*C* \\ *A*) ∩ (*C* \\ *B*);
- *C* \\ (*B* \\ *A*) = (*C* \\ *B*) ∪(*C* ∩ *A*);
- (*B* \\ *A*) ∩ *C* = (*B* ∩ *C*) \\ *A* = *B* ∩ (*C* \\ *A*);
- (*B* \\ *A*) ∪ *C* = (*B* ∪ *C*) \\ (*A* \\ *C*).

The [distributive laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributive_law):

- *A* ∩ (*B* ∪ *C*) = (*A* ∩ *B*) ∪ (*A* ∩ *C*); \*
- *A* ∪ (*B* ∩ *C*) = (*A* ∪ *B*) ∩ (*A* ∪ *C*). \*

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=inline-head name="PROPOSITION 3"}

**PROPOSITION 3**. Some properties of ⊆:

- *A* ⊆ *B* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *A* ∩ *B* = *A*;
- *A* ⊆ *B* if and only if *A* ∪ *B* = *B*;
- *A* ⊆ *B* if and only if *B* ⊆ *A*;
- *A* ⊆ *B* if and only if *A* \\ *B* = {};
- *A* ∩ *B* ⊆ *A* ⊆ *A*∪ *B*.

:::
