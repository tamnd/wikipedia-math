# 60 (number)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="60"}

**60** (**sixty**) (en-us-sixty.ogg) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/59_%28number%29) and preceding [61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/61_%28number%29). Being three times 20, it is called *threescore* in older literature (*[kopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopa_%28number%29)* in Slavic, *Schock* in Germanic).

:::

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

60 is the 4th [superior highly composite number](wikipedia://en/1759451),[^o4] the 4th [colossally abundant number](wikipedia://en/1759217),[^o5] the 9th [highly composite number](wikipedia://en/208732), a [unitary perfect number](wikipedia://en/553027), and an [abundant number](wikipedia://en/321831).

The smallest group that is not solvable is the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) A<sub>5</sub>, which has 60 elements. The group $A_5$ is also the rotational symmetry group of the regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) and of the regular [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407).

There are 60 one-sided [hexominoes](wikipedia://en/379520), the [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634) made from six squares.

## Cultural number systems {#Cultural_number_systems}

*Image: Babylonian numerals.svg.* Babylonian cuneiform numerals

The [Babylonian cuneiform numerals](wikipedia://en/152323) had a [base](wikipedia://en/21170) of 60, inherited from the Sumerian and Akkadian civilizations, and possibly motivated by its large number of divisors. The [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) measurement of time and of geometric angles is a legacy of the Babylonian system. For example, in time, there are 60 [seconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second) in a [minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute), and 60 minutes in an [hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hour).[^o9] (a legacy of the [Babylonian number system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_number_system))[^o10]

The number system in the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire) was based on 60, reflected in the counting system of the [Maasina Fulfulde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasina_Fulfulde), a variant of the [Fula language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_language) spoken in contemporary [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali).[^o11] The Ekagi of [Western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea) used base 60,[^o12] and the [sexagenary cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagenary_cycle) plays a role in [Chinese calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calendar) and numerology.

From [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Lithuanian_Commonwealth) in Slavic and Baltic languages 60 has its own name *[kopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopa_%28number%29)* (kopa, капа́, kapa, kopa, копа, копа́), in Germanic languages: Schock, skok, schok, Skock, Skokk and in sexagena refer to 60 = 5 [dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dozen) = $\tfrac{1}{2}$ [small gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_gross). This quantity was used in [international medieval treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Thorn_%281411%29) e.g. for ransom of captured [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights).

## In science and technology {#In_science_and_technology}

*Image: C60 Molecule.svg.* [Buckminsterfullerene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckminsterfullerene) C<sub>60</sub> has 60 carbon atoms in each molecule, arranged in a [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282).

The first [fullerene](wikipedia://en/10628) to be discovered was [buckminsterfullerene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckminsterfullerene) C<sub>60</sub>, an [allotrope of carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotropes_of_carbon) with 60 [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom) in each [molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule), arranged in a [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282). This ball is known as a buckyball, and looks like a [soccer ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_ball).

The [atomic number](wikipedia://en/673) of [neodymium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neodymium) is 60, and [cobalt-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt-60) (<sup>60</sup>Co) is a [radioactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive) [isotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotopes_of_cobalt) of [cobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt).

The electrical [utility frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_frequency) in western Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, the Philippines, the United States, and several other countries in the Americas is 60 [Hz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz).

An [exbibyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exbibyte) (sometimes called [exabyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exabyte)) is 2<sup>60</sup> [bytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte).

## In religion {#In_religion}

In Hinduism, the 60th birthday of a man is called Sashti poorthi. A ceremony called Sashti (60) Abda (years) Poorthi (completed) in Sanskrit is conducted to felicitate this birthday. It represents a milestone in his life. There are 60 years mentioned in the historic Indian calendars.

## In other fields {#In_other_fields}

*Image: Stoppuhr.jpg.* There are 60 seconds in a minute, and 60 minutes in an hour.

It is:

- The number of feet in the standard measurement tool to evaluate an automotive launch on a [dragstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragstrip), as the time taken to travel the first 60 ft of the track.
- The number of miles per hour an automobile accelerates to from rest ([0-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0-60)) as one of the standard measurements of performance
- The number of years in a [sexagenary cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagenary_cycle)
