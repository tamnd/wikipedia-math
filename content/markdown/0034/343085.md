# Cousin prime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cousin prime"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **cousin primes** are [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers) that differ by four. Compare this with [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997), pairs of prime numbers that differ by two, and [sexy primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexy_prime), pairs of prime numbers that differ by six.

:::

The cousin primes (sequences and in [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OEIS)) below 1000 are:

(3, 7), (7, 11), (13, 17), (19, 23), (37, 41), (43, 47), (67, 71), (79, 83), (97, 101), (103, 107), (109, 113), (127, 131), (163, 167), (193, 197), (223, 227), (229, 233), (277, 281), (307, 311), (313, 317), (349, 353), (379, 383), (397, 401), (439, 443), (457, 461), (463,467), (487, 491), (499, 503), (613, 617), (643, 647), (673, 677), (739, 743), (757, 761), (769, 773), (823, 827), (853, 857), (859, 863), (877, 881), (883, 887), (907, 911), (937, 941), (967, 971)

## Properties {#Properties}

The only prime belonging to two pairs of cousin primes is 7. One of the numbers $n, n + 4, n + 8$ will always be divisible by 3, so $n = 3$ is the only case where all three are primes.

An example of a large [proven](wikipedia://en/2549312) cousin prime pair is $(p, p + 4)$ for

$$p = 4111286921397 \times 2^{66420} + 1$$

which has 20008 digits. In fact, this is part of a [prime triple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_triple) since $p$ is also a [twin prime](wikipedia://en/41997) (because $p - 2$ is also a proven prime).

, the largest-known pair of cousin primes was found by S. Batalov and has 86,138 digits. The primes are:

$$p = (29571282950 \times (2^{190738} - 1) + 4) \times 2^{95369} - 1$$

$p+4 = (29571282950 \times (2^{190738} - 1) + 4) \times 2^{95369} + 3$[^o5]

If the first [Hardy–Littlewood conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_conjecture) holds, then cousin primes have the same asymptotic density as [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997). An analogue of [Brun's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brun's_constant) for twin primes can be defined for cousin primes, called **Brun's constant for cousin primes**, with the initial term (3, 7) omitted, by the convergent sum:[^o6]

$$B_4 = \left(\frac{1}{7} + \frac{1}{11}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{13} + \frac{1}{17}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{19} + \frac{1}{23}\right) + \cdots.$$

Using cousin primes up to 2<sup>42</sup>, the value of $B_{4}$ was estimated by Marek Wolf in 1996 as

$B_4 \approx 1.1970449$[^o7]

This constant should not be confused with Brun's constant for [prime quadruplets](wikipedia://en/812561), which is also denoted $B_{4}$.

The [Skewes number](wikipedia://en/250178#Equivalent_for_prime_k-tuples) for cousin primes is 5206837 ((Tóth 2019)).
