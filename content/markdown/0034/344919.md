# Leech lattice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leech lattice"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Leech lattice** is an even [unimodular lattice](wikipedia://en/1648605) Λ<sub>24</sub> in 24-[dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensional) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), **E**<sup>24</sup>. It is one of the best models for the [kissing number problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissing_number_problem). It was discovered by (txt). It may also have been discovered (but not published) by [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911) in 1940.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

The Leech lattice Λ<sub>24</sub> is the unique lattice in 24-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), **E**<sup>24</sup>, with the following list of properties:

- It is [unimodular](wikipedia://en/1648605): it can be generated by the columns of a certain 24×24 [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) with [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) 1.
- It is even: the square of the length of each vector in Λ<sub>24</sub> is an even integer.
- The length of every non-zero vector in Λ<sub>24</sub> is at least 2.

The last condition is equivalent to the condition that unit balls centered at the points of Λ<sub>24</sub> do not overlap. Each is tangent to 196,560 neighbors, and this is known to be the largest number of non-overlapping 24-dimensional unit balls that can [simultaneously touch a single unit ball](wikipedia://en/408555). This arrangement of 196,560 unit balls centred about another unit ball is so efficient that there is no room to move any of the balls; this configuration and its mirror image are the *only* 24-dimensional arrangements where 196,560 unit balls simultaneously touch another. This property is also true in 1, 2, and 8 dimensions, with 2, 6, and 240 unit balls, respectively, based on the [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402), [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719), and [E<sub>8</sub> lattice](wikipedia://en/1800359), respectively.

It has no [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) and in fact is the first [unimodular lattice](wikipedia://en/1648605) with no *roots* (vectors of norm less than 4), and therefore has a centre density of 1. By multiplying this value by the [volume of a unit ball](wikipedia://en/27033090#Volume_and_area) in 24 dimensions, $\tfrac{\pi^{12}}{12!}$, its absolute density can be derived.

(Conway 1983) showed that the Leech lattice is isometric to the set of simple roots (or the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109)) of the [reflection group](wikipedia://en/2422023) of the 26-dimensional even Lorentzian unimodular lattice [II<sub>25,1</sub>](wikipedia://en/32486086). By comparison, the Dynkin diagrams of II<sub>9,1</sub> and II<sub>17,1</sub> are finite.

## Applications {#Applications}

The [binary Golay code](wikipedia://en/344971), independently developed in 1949, is an application in [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869). More specifically, it is an error-correcting code capable of correcting up to three errors in each 24-bit word, and detecting up to four. It was used to communicate with the [Voyager probes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_probes), as it is much more compact than the previously used [Hadamard code](wikipedia://en/7428961).

[Quantizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantizer), or [analog-to-digital converters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog-to-digital_converter), can use lattices to minimise the average [root-mean-square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root-mean-square) error. Most quantizers are based on the one-dimensional [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402), but using multi-dimensional lattices reduces the RMS error. The Leech lattice is a good solution to this problem, as the [Voronoi cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_cell) have a low [second moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_moment).

The [vertex algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra) of the [two-dimensional conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913) describing [bosonic string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosonic_string_theory), compactified on the 24-dimensional [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) **R**<sup>24</sup>/Λ<sub>24</sub> and [orbifolded](wikipedia://en/293504) by a two-element reflection group, provides an explicit construction of the [Griess algebra](wikipedia://en/728596) that has the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422) as its automorphism group. This [monster vertex algebra](wikipedia://en/5032859) was also used to prove the [monstrous moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168) conjectures.

## Constructions {#Constructions}

The Leech lattice can be constructed in a variety of ways. Like all lattices, it can be constructed by taking the [integral](wikipedia://en/14563) span of the columns of its [generator matrix](wikipedia://en/6133005), a 24×24 matrix with [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) 1.

Leech generator matrix[^o10]

### Using the binary Golay code {#Using_the_binary_Golay_code}

The Leech lattice can be explicitly constructed as the set of vectors of the form 2<sup>−3/2</sup>(*a*<sub>1</sub>, *a*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>24</sub>), where the *a*<sub>*i*</sub> are integers such that

$$a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{24}\equiv 4a_1\equiv 4a_2\equiv\cdots\equiv4a_{24}\pmod 8$$

and for each fixed residue class modulo 4, the 24 bit word, whose 1s correspond to the coordinates *i* such that *a*<sub>*i*</sub> belongs to this residue class, is a word in the [binary Golay code](wikipedia://en/344971). The Golay code, together with the related Witt design, features in a construction for the 196560 minimal vectors in the Leech lattice.

The Leech lattice (L mod 8) can be directly constructed by combinations of the 3 following sets:

$$L = (4B + C)\otimes{1_{2^{12}}} + 1_{2^{24}} \otimes 2G,$$

### Using the Lorentzian lattice II<sub>25,1</sub> {#Using_the_Lorentzian_lattice_II25,1}

The Leech lattice can also be constructed as $w^\perp/w$, where $w$ is the [Weyl vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_vector)

$$(0,1,2,3,\dots,22,23,24; 70)$$

in the 26-dimensional even Lorentzian [unimodular lattice](wikipedia://en/1648605) [II<sub>25,1</sub>](wikipedia://en/32486086). The existence of such an integral vector of Lorentzian norm zero relies on the fact that 1<sup>2</sup> + 2<sup>2</sup> + ... + 24<sup>2</sup> is a [perfect square](wikipedia://en/213919) (in fact 70<sup>2</sup>); the [number 24](wikipedia://en/362201) is the only integer bigger than 1 with this property (see [cannonball problem](wikipedia://en/28045698)). This was conjectured by [Édouard Lucas](wikipedia://en/150102), but the proof came much later, based on [elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_functions).

The vector $(0,1,2,3,\dots,22,23,24)$ in this construction is really the Weyl vector of the even sublattice *D*<sub>24</sub> of the odd unimodular lattice *I*<sup>25</sup>. More generally, if *L* is any positive definite unimodular lattice of dimension 25 with at least 4 vectors of norm 1, then the Weyl vector of its norm 2 roots has integral length, and there is a similar construction of the Leech lattice using *L* and this Weyl vector.

### Based on other lattices {#Based_on_other_lattices}

(Conway Sloane 1982) described another 23 constructions for the Leech lattice, each based on a [Niemeier lattice](wikipedia://en/1652072). It can also be constructed by using three copies of the [*E*<sub>8</sub> lattice](wikipedia://en/1800359), in the same way that the binary Golay code can be constructed using three copies of the extended [Hamming code](wikipedia://en/41226), *H*<sub>8</sub>. This construction is known as the Turyn construction of the Leech lattice.

### As a laminated lattice {#As_a_laminated_lattice}

Starting with a single point, Λ<sub>0</sub>, stack copies of the lattice Λ<sub>n</sub> can be stacked to form an (*n* + 1)-dimensional lattice, Λ<sub>*n*+1</sub>, without reducing the minimal distance between points. Λ<sub>1</sub> corresponds to the [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402), Λ<sub>2</sub> is to the [hexagonal lattice](wikipedia://en/2868762), and Λ<sub>3</sub> is the [face-centered cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-centered_cubic) packing. (Conway Sloane 1982b) showed that the Leech lattice is the unique laminated lattice in 24 dimensions.

### As a complex lattice {#As_a_complex_lattice}

The Leech lattice is also a 12-dimensional lattice over the [Eisenstein integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_integers). This is known as the complex Leech lattice, and is isomorphic to the 24-dimensional real Leech lattice. In the complex construction of the Leech lattice, the [binary Golay code](wikipedia://en/344971) is replaced with the [ternary Golay code](wikipedia://en/3070794), and the [Mathieu group *M*<sub>24</sub>](wikipedia://en/15200706) is replaced with the [Mathieu group *M*<sub>12</sub>](wikipedia://en/15200695). The *E*<sub>6</sub> lattice, *E*<sub>8</sub> lattice, and [Coxeter&ndash;Todd lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter&ndash;Todd_lattice) also have constructions as complex lattices, over either the Eisenstein or [Gaussian integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_integers).

### Using the icosian ring {#Using_the_icosian_ring}

The Leech lattice can also be constructed using the ring of [icosians](wikipedia://en/27977943). The icosian ring is abstractly isomorphic to the [*E*<sub>8</sub> lattice](wikipedia://en/1800359), three copies of which can be used to construct the Leech lattice using the Turyn construction.

### Witt's construction {#Witt's_construction}

In 1972 Witt gave the following construction, which he said he found in 1940, on January 28. Suppose that *H* is an *n* × *n* [Hadamard matrix](wikipedia://en/507553), where *n* = 4*ab*. Then the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} Ia&H/2\\H/2&Ib\end{pmatrix}$ defines a bilinear form in 2*n* dimensions, whose kernel has *n* dimensions. The quotient by this kernel is a nonsingular bilinear form taking values in (1/2)**Z**. It has 3 sublattices of index 2 that are integral bilinear forms. Witt obtained the Leech lattice as one of these three sublattices by taking *a* = 2, *b* = 3, and taking *H* to be the 24 × 24 matrix (indexed by **Z**/23**Z** ∪ ∞) with entries Χ(*m*+*n*) where Χ(∞)=1, Χ(0)=−1, and Χ(*n*)=is the quadratic residue symbol mod 23 for nonzero *n*. This matrix *H* is a [Paley matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paley_matrix) with some insignificant sign changes.

### Using a Paley matrix {#Using_a_Paley_matrix}

(Chapman 2001) described a construction using a [skew Hadamard matrix](wikipedia://en/507553#Skew_Hadamard_matrices) of [Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paley_matrix) type. The [Niemeier lattice](wikipedia://en/1652072) with root system $D_{24}$ can be made into a module for the ring of integers of the field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$. Multiplying this Niemeier lattice by a non-principal ideal of the ring of integers gives the Leech lattice.

### Using higher power residue codes {#Using_higher_power_residue_codes}

(Raji 2005) constructed the Leech lattice using higher power residue codes over the ring $Z_4$. A similar construction is used to construct some of the other lattices of rank 24.

### Using octonions {#Using_octonions}

If *L* is the set of [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) with coordinates on the $E_8$ [lattice](wikipedia://en/1800359), then the Leech lattice is the set of triplets $(x,y,z)$ such that

$$x,y,z \in L$$

$$x+y+z \in Ls$$

$$x+y,\ y+z,\ x+z \in L\bar{s}$$

where $s= \frac 1 2 (-e_1 + e_2 + e_3 + e_4 + e_5 + e_6 + e_7)$. This construction is due to (Wilson 2009).

## Symmetries {#Symmetries}

The Leech lattice's [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) is the [Conway group](wikipedia://en/345511) Co<sub>0</sub>, which is of order 8315553613086720000. The [center](wikipedia://en/7125) of Co<sub>0</sub> has two elements, and the quotient of Co<sub>0</sub> by this center is the Conway group Co<sub>1</sub>, a finite simple group, and one of the [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806). Many other sporadic groups, such as the remaining Conway groups and [Mathieu groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu_groups), can be constructed as the stabilizers of various configurations of vectors in the Leech lattice.

The automorphism group was first described by [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807). The 398034000 vectors of norm 8 fall into 8292375 "crosses" of 48 vectors. Each cross contains 24 mutually orthogonal vectors and their negatives, and thus describe the vertices of a 24-dimensional [orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex). Each of these crosses can be taken to be the coordinate system of the lattice, and has the same symmetry of the [Golay code](wikipedia://en/344971), namely $2^{12} \times M_{24}$. Hence the full automorphism group of the Leech lattice has order 8292375 × 4096 × 244823040, or 8315553613086720000.

The Leech lattice and, by extension, the Leech polytope still possess a lower symmetry order than the 24-dimensional regular [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781), which is of order

> (24+1)! = 25! = 15511210043330985984000000,

which is 1865325 times higher, and the 24-dimensional [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783), which has order

> 24! × 2<sup>24</sup> = 10409396852733332453861621760000,

or 1251798417408 times higher. Additionally, despite having such a high *rotational* symmetry group, the Leech lattice does not possess any hyperplanes of reflection symmetry. In other words, the Leech lattice is [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral). Likewise, Leech lattice has a lower symmetry than the Cartesian product of three copies of the [*E*<sub>8</sub> lattice](wikipedia://en/1800359).

:::

## Geometry {#Geometry}

(Conway Parker Sloane 1982) showed that the covering radius of the Leech lattice is $\sqrt 2$; in other words, if a closed ball of this radius is placed around each lattice point, then these just cover Euclidean space. The points at distance at least $\sqrt 2$ from all lattice points are called the *deep holes* of the Leech lattice. There are 23 orbits of them under the automorphism group of the Leech lattice, and these orbits correspond to the 23 [Niemeier lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niemeier_lattices) other than the Leech lattice: the set of vertices of the deep holes is isometric to the affine Dynkin diagram of the corresponding Niemeier lattice.

The Leech lattice has a density of $\tfrac{\pi^{12}}{12!}\approx 0.001930$. (Cohn Kumar 2009) showed that it gives the densest lattice [packing of balls](wikipedia://en/368621) in 24-dimensional space. (txt) improved this by showing that it is the densest sphere packing, even among non-lattice packings.

The 196560 minimal vectors are of three different varieties, known as *shapes*:

- $1104 = \binom {24}{2} \cdot 2^2$ vectors of shape (4<sup>2</sup>,0<sup>22</sup>), for all permutations and sign choices;
- $97152 = 759 \cdot 2^8 \cdot \frac {1}{2}$ vectors of shape (2<sup>8</sup>,0<sup>16</sup>), where the '2's correspond to an octad in the Golay code, and there are any even number of minus signs;
- $98304 = 2^{12} \cdot 24$ vectors of shape (∓3,±1<sup>23</sup>), where the lower sign is used for the 1s of any codeword of the Golay code, and the ∓3 can appear in any position.

The [ternary Golay code](wikipedia://en/3070794), [binary Golay code](wikipedia://en/344971), and Leech lattice give very efficient 24-dimensional [spherical codes](wikipedia://en/22821969) of 729, 4096, and 196560 points, respectively. Spherical codes are higher-dimensional analogues of [Tammes problem](wikipedia://en/19332734), which arose as an attempt to explain the distribution of pores on pollen grains. These are distributed as to maximise the minimal angle between them. In two dimensions, the problem is trivial, but in three dimensions and higher it is not. An example of a spherical code in three dimensions is the set of the 12 vertices of the regular icosahedron.

## Theta series {#Theta_series}

One can associate to any (positive-definite) lattice Λ a [theta function](wikipedia://en/449745) given by

$$\Theta_\Lambda(\tau) = \sum_{x\in\Lambda} e^{i\pi\tau\|x\|^2} \qquad \operatorname{Im} \tau > 0.$$

The theta function of a lattice is then a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) on the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826). Furthermore, the theta function of an even unimodular lattice of rank *n* is actually a [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000) of weight *n*/2 for the full [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019) PSL(2,**Z**). The theta function of an integral lattice is often written as a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) in $q = e^{2i\pi\tau}$ so that the coefficient of *q*<sup>*n*</sup> gives the number of lattice vectors of squared norm 2*n*. In the Leech lattice, there are 196560 vectors of squared norm 4, 16773120 vectors of squared norm 6, 398034000 vectors of squared norm 8, and so on. The theta series of the Leech lattice is

$$\begin{aligned} \Theta_{\Lambda_{24}}(\tau) & = E_{12}(\tau)-\frac{65520}{691} \Delta(\tau) \\[5pt] & = 1 + \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{65520}{691} \left(\sigma_{11} (m) - \tau (m) \right) q^m \\[5pt] & = 1 + 196560q^2 + 16773120q^3 + 398034000q^4 + \cdots, \end{aligned}$$

where $E_{12}(\tau)$ is the normalized [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458) of weight 12, $\Delta(\tau)$ is the [modular discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_discriminant), $\sigma_{11}(n)$ is the [divisor function](wikipedia://en/407249) for exponent 11, and $\tau(n)$ is the [Ramanujan tau function](wikipedia://en/1231708). It follows that for *m* ≥ 1, the number of vectors of squared norm 2*m* is

$$\frac{65520}{691} \left(\sigma_{11} (m) - \tau (m) \right).$$

## History {#History}

Many of the cross-sections of the Leech lattice, including the [Coxeter&ndash;Todd lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter&ndash;Todd_lattice) and [Barnes&ndash;Wall lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnes&ndash;Wall_lattice), in 12 and 16 dimensions, were found much earlier than the Leech lattice. (O'Connor Pall 1944) discovered a related odd unimodular lattice in 24 dimensions, now called the *odd Leech lattice*, one of whose two even neighbors is the Leech lattice. The Leech lattice was discovered in 1965 by (txt, 2.31, p. 262), by improving some earlier sphere packings he found (Leech 1964).

(txt) calculated the order of the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the Leech lattice, and, working with [John G. Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189), discovered three new [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806) as a by-product: the [Conway groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_groups), Co<sub>1</sub>, Co<sub>2</sub>, Co<sub>3</sub>. They also showed that four other (then) recently announced sporadic groups, namely, [Higman-Sims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higman-Sims_group), [Suzuki](wikipedia://en/403176), [McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaughlin_group_%28mathematics%29), and the [Janko group](wikipedia://en/380191) J<sub>2</sub>, could be found inside the Conway groups using the geometry of the Leech lattice. (Ronan, p. 155)

Bei dem Versuch, eine Form aus einer solchen Klasse wirklich anzugeben, fand ich mehr als 10 verschiedene Klassen in Γ<sub>24</sub>

(Witt 1941, p. 324), has a single rather cryptic sentence mentioning that he found more than 10 even unimodular lattices in 24 dimensions without giving further details. (Witt 1998, p. 328–329) stated that he found 9 of these lattices earlier in 1938, and found two more, the [Niemeier lattice](wikipedia://en/1652072) with A${}^{24}_{1}$ root system and the Leech lattice (and also the odd Leech lattice), in 1940.
