# Image (category theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Image"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **image** of a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) is a generalization of the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="General definition"}

## General definition {#General_definition}

Given a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $C$ and a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) $f\colon X\to Y$ in $C$, the **image**[^o4] of $f$ is a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) $m\colon I\to Y$ satisfying the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245):

1. There exists a morphism $e\colon X\to I$ such that $f = m\, e$.
1. For any object $I'$ with a morphism $e'\colon X\to I'$ and a monomorphism $m'\colon I'\to Y$ such that $f = m'\, e'$, there exists a unique morphism $v\colon I\to I'$ such that $m = m'\, v$.

**Remarks:**

1. such a factorization does not necessarily exist.
1. $e$ is unique by definition of $m$ [monic](wikipedia://en/59538).
1. $m'e'=f=me=m've$, therefore $e'=ve$ by $m'$ monic.
1. $v$ is monic.
1. $m = m'\, v$ already implies that $v$ is unique.

*Image: Image Theorie des catégories.png.*

The image of $f$ is often denoted by $\text{Im} f$ or $\text{Im} (f)$.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=inline-head name="Proposition"}

**Proposition:** If $C$ has all [equalizers](wikipedia://en/310767) then the $e$ in the factorization $f= m\, e$ of (1) is an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539).[^o6]

::: {#o7 .proof cue=math-proof}

Let $\alpha,\, \beta$ be such that $\alpha\, e =\beta\, e$, one needs to show that $\alpha=\beta$. Since the equalizer of $(\alpha, \beta)$ exists, $e$ factorizes as $e= q\, e'$ with $q$ monic. But then $f= (m\, q)\, e'$ is a factorization of $f$ with $(m\, q)$ monomorphism. Hence by the universal property of the image there exists a unique arrow $v: I \to Eq_{\alpha,\beta}$ such that $m = m\,q\, v$ and since $m$ is monic $\text{id}_I = q\, v$. Furthermore, one has $m\, q = (m q v)\,q$ and by the monomorphism property of $mq$ one obtains $\text{id}_{Eq_{\alpha,\beta}}= v\, q$.

*Image: E epimorphism.png.*

This means that $I \equiv Eq_{\alpha,\beta}$ and thus that $\text{id}_I = q\, v$ equalizes $(\alpha, \beta)$, whence $\alpha = \beta$.

:::

## Second definition {#Second_definition}

In a category $C$ with all finite [limits](wikipedia://en/39248) and [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimits), the **image** is defined as the [equalizer](wikipedia://en/310767#In_category_theory) $(Im,m)$ of the so-called **cokernel pair** $(Y \sqcup_X Y, i_1, i_2)$, which is the [cocartesian](wikipedia://en/582024) of a morphism with itself over its domain, which will result in a pair of morphisms $i_1,i_2:Y\to Y\sqcup_X Y$, on which the [equalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equalizer_%28category_theory%29) is taken, i.e. the first of the following diagrams is [cocartesian](wikipedia://en/582024), and the second [equalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equalizer_%28category_theory%29).[^o9]

*Image: Cokernel pair.png.*

*Image: Equalizer of the cokernel pair, diagram.png.*

**Remarks:**

1. Finite [bicompleteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicompleteness) of the category ensures that pushouts and equalizers exist.
1. $(Im,m)$ can be called **regular image** as $m$ is a [regular monomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_monomorphism), i.e. the equalizer of a pair of morphisms. (Recall also that an equalizer is automatically a monomorphism).
1. In an abelian category, the cokernel pair property can be written $i_1\, f = i_2\, f\ \Leftrightarrow\ (i_1 - i_2)\, f = 0 = 0\, f$ and the equalizer condition $i_1\, m = i_2\, m\ \Leftrightarrow\ (i_1 - i_2)\, m = 0 \, m$. Moreover, all monomorphisms are regular.

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

If $f$ always factorizes through regular monomorphisms, then the two definitions coincide.

:::

::: {#o11 .proof cue=math-proof}

**First definition implies the second:** Assume that *(1)* holds with $m$ regular monomorphism.

**Equalization:** one needs to show that $i_1\, m= i_2\, m$ . As the cokernel pair of $f,\ i_1\, f= i_2\, f$ and by previous proposition, since $C$ has all equalizers, the arrow $e$ in the factorization $f= m\, e$ is an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539), hence $i_1\, f= i_2\, f\ \Rightarrow \ i_1\, m= i_2\, m$.

**Universality:** in a category with all colimits (or at least all pushouts) $m$ itself admits a cokernel pair $(Y \sqcup_{I}Y, c_1, c_2)$

*Image: Cokernel pair m.png.*

Moreover, as a regular monomorphism, $(I,m)$ is the equalizer of a pair of morphisms $b_1, b_2: Y \longrightarrow B$ but we claim here that it is also the equalizer of $c_1, c_2: Y \longrightarrow Y \sqcup_{I}Y$.

Indeed, by construction $b_1\, m = b_2\, m$ thus the "cokernel pair" diagram for $m$ yields a unique morphism $u': Y \sqcup_{I}Y \longrightarrow B$ such that $b_1 = u'\, c_1,\ b_2 = u'\, c_2$. Now, a map $m': I'\longrightarrow Y$ which equalizes $(c_1, c_2)$ also satisfies $b_1\, m'= u'\, c_1 \, m'= u'\, c_2\, m'= b_2\, m'$, hence by the equalizer diagram for $(b_1, b_2)$, there exists a unique map $h': I'\to I$ such that $m'= m\, h'$.

Finally, use the cokernel pair diagram (of $f$) with $j_1 := c_1,\ j_2 := c_2,\ Z:= Y\sqcup_I Y$ : there exists a unique $u: Y \sqcup_{X}Y \longrightarrow Y\sqcup_I Y$ such that $c_1 = u\, i_1,\ c_2 = u\, i_2$. Therefore, any map $g$ which equalizes $(i_1, i_2)$ also equalizes $(c_1, c_2)$ and thus uniquely factorizes as $g= m\, h'$. This exactly means that $(I,m)$ is the equalizer of $(i_1, i_2)$.

**Second definition implies the first:**

**Factorization:** taking $m' := f$ in the equalizer diagram ($m'$ corresponds to $g$), one obtains the factorization $f = m\, h$.

**Universality:** let $f = m'\, e'$ be a factorization with $m'$ regular monomorphism, i.e. the equalizer of some pair $(d_1, d_2)$.

*Image: Equalizerd1d2.png.*

Then $d_1\, m'= d_2\, m'\ \Rightarrow \ d_1\, f=d_1\, m'\, e= d_2\, m'\, e= d_2\, f$ so that by the "cokernel pair" diagram (of $f$), with $j_1 := d_1,\ j_2 := d_2,\ Z:= D$, there exists a unique $u'': Y \sqcup_{X}Y \longrightarrow D$ such that $d_1 = u''\, i_1,\ d_2 = u''\, i_2$.

Now, from $i_1\, m= i_2\, m$ (*m* from the equalizer of (*i<sub>1</sub>, i<sub>2</sub>*) diagram), one obtains $d_1\, m= u''\, i_1\, m = u''\, i_2\, m = d_2\, m$, hence by the universality in the (equalizer of (*d<sub>1</sub>, d<sub>2</sub>*) diagram, with *f* replaced by *m*), there exists a unique $v: Im \longrightarrow I'$ such that $m = m'\, v$.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

In the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) the image of a morphism $f\colon X \to Y$ is the [inclusion](wikipedia://en/492516) from the ordinary [image](wikipedia://en/579311) $\{f(x) ~|~ x \in X\}$ to $Y$. In many [concrete categories](wikipedia://en/167001) such as [groups](wikipedia://en/800092), [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748) and (left- or right) [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), the image of a morphism is the image of the correspondent morphism in the category of sets.

In any [normal category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_category) with a [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object) and [kernels](wikipedia://en/142616) and [cokernels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cokernel_%28category_theory%29) for every morphism, the image of a morphism $f$ can be expressed as follows:

im *f* = ker coker *f*

In an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) (which is in particular binormal), if *f* is a monomorphism then *f* = ker coker *f*, and so *f* = im *f*.

:::

## Essential Image {#Essential_Image}

A related notion to image is *essential image.*[^o15]

A subcategory $C \subset B$ of a (strict) category is said to be **replete** if for every $x \in C$, and for every isomorphism $\iota: x \to y$, both $\iota$ and $y$ belong to C.

Let $F \colon A \to B$ be a functor between categories. The **essential image** of $F$ is the smallest [replete subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replete_subcategory) of the target category $B$ containing the image of $A$ under $F$.
