# 90 (number)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="90"}

**90** (**ninety**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89_%28number%29) and preceding [91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/91_%28number%29).

:::

In the English language, the numbers 90 and 19 are often confused, as they sound very similar. When carefully enunciated, they differ in which syllable is stressed: 19 /naɪnˈtiːn/ vs 90 /ˈnaɪnti/.

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

90 is a [pronic number](wikipedia://en/652733) as it is the [product](wikipedia://en/20845) of [9](wikipedia://en/173457) and [10](wikipedia://en/208151),[^o4] and along with [12](wikipedia://en/208155) and [56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/56_%28number%29), one of only a few pronic numbers whose digits in [decimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_ten) are also successive. 90 is divisible by the sum of its [base-ten](wikipedia://en/8214) digits, which makes it the thirty-second [Harshad number](wikipedia://en/641307).[^o5]

### Properties of the number {#Properties_of_the_number}

- 90 is the only number to have an aliquot sum of [144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/144_%28number%29) = 12<sup>2</sup>.
- Only three numbers have a set of [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) that generate a sum equal to 90: they are [40](wikipedia://en/341671), [58](wikipedia://en/399312), and [89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89_%28number%29).
- 90 is also the twentieth [abundant](wikipedia://en/321831) and [highly abundant](wikipedia://en/1759247) number (with [20](wikipedia://en/208259) the first [primitive abundant number](wikipedia://en/27077452) and [70](wikipedia://en/343334) the second).
- The number of [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) of 90 is 12. Other smaller numbers with this property are [60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixty), [72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-two) and [84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighty-four). These four and [96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninety-six) are the five double-digit numbers with exactly 12 divisors.
- 90 is the tenth and largest number to hold an [Euler totient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_totient) value of [24](wikipedia://en/362201); no number has a totient that is 90, which makes it the eleventh [nontotient](wikipedia://en/748422) (with [50](wikipedia://en/308628) the fifth).[^o14]

The twelfth [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) [78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/78_%28number%29) is the only number to have an [aliquot sum](wikipedia://en/12374274) equal to 90, aside from the [square](wikipedia://en/213919) of the twenty-fourth prime, [89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89_%28number%29)<sup>2</sup> (which is [centered octagonal](wikipedia://en/1215851)). 90 is equal to the fifth sum of *non-triangular* numbers, respectively between the fifth and sixth triangular numbers, [15](wikipedia://en/208252) and [21](wikipedia://en/209248) (equivalently [16](wikipedia://en/208254) + [17](wikipedia://en/208288) ... + [20](wikipedia://en/208259)). It is also twice [45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45_%28number%29), which is the ninth triangular number, and the second-smallest sum of twelve non-zero integers, from two through thirteen $\{2,3,...,13\}$.

90 can be expressed as the sum of distinct non-zero [squares](wikipedia://en/213919) in six ways, more than any smaller number (see image):

$$(9^{2}+3^{2}),(8^{2}+5^{2}+1^{2}),(7^{2}+5^{2}+4^{2}),(8^{2}+4^{2}+3^{2}+1^{2}),(7^{2}+6^{2}+2^{2}+1^{2}),(6^{2}+5^{2}+4^{2}+3^{2}+2^{2}).$$

*Image: square-sum-90.png.* 90 as the sum of distinct nonzero squares

The square of eleven 11<sup>2</sup> = 121 is the ninetieth indexed [composite number](wikipedia://en/82289), where the sum of integers $\{2,3,...,11\}$ is [65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65_%28number%29), which in-turn represents the composite index of 90.[^o20] In the [fractional part](wikipedia://en/501532) of the [decimal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_expansion) of the reciprocal of [11](wikipedia://en/208156) in [base-10](wikipedia://en/8214), "90" repeats periodically (when leading zeroes are moved to the end).

The eighteenth [Stirling number of the second kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling_number_of_the_second_kind) $S(n,k)$ is 90, from a $n$ of 6 and a $k$ of 3, as the number of ways of dividing a [set](wikipedia://en/27553) of six objects into three [non-empty subsets](wikipedia://en/9566).[^o22] 90 is also the sixteenth [Perrin number](wikipedia://en/30863738) from a sum of [39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/39_%28number%29) and [51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51_%28number%29), whose difference is [12](wikipedia://en/208155).[^o23]

#### Prime sextuplets {#Prime_sextuplets}

The members of the first [prime sextuplet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_sextuplet) ([7](wikipedia://en/208159), [11](wikipedia://en/208156), [13](wikipedia://en/37231), 17, [19](wikipedia://en/208292), [23](wikipedia://en/362203)) generate a [sum](wikipedia://en/61338) equal to 90, and the difference between respective members of the first and second prime sextuplets is also 90, where the second prime sextuplet is ([97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/97_%28number%29), [101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101_%28number%29), [103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/103_%28number%29), [107](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/107_%28number%29), [109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/109_%28number%29), [113](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/113_%28number%29)). The last member of the second prime sextuplet, 113, is the 30th [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). Since prime sextuplets are formed from prime members of lower order [prime *k*-tuples](wikipedia://en/11421646), 90 is also a record maximal gap between various smaller pairs of prime *k*-tuples (which include [quintuplets](wikipedia://en/812561#Prime_quintuplets), [quadruplets](wikipedia://en/812561), and [triplets](wikipedia://en/7493205)).

#### Unitary perfect number {#Unitary_perfect_number}

90 is the third [unitary perfect number](wikipedia://en/553027) (after [6](wikipedia://en/208171) and [60](wikipedia://en/343325)), since it is the sum of its [unitary divisors](wikipedia://en/5488222) excluding itself,[^o28] and because it is equal to the sum of a subset of its divisors, it is also the twenty-first [semiperfect number](wikipedia://en/321918).[^o29]

### Right angle {#Right_angle}

*Image: Right angle.svg.* A [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956) measures ninety [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294).

An angle measuring 90 degrees is called a [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956).[^o31] In normal [space](wikipedia://en/27667), the [interior angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_angles) of a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) measure 90 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294) each, while in a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420), the angle opposing the [hypotenuse](wikipedia://en/251366) measures 90 degrees, with the other two angles adding up to 90 for a total of 180 degrees.

### Icosahedral symmetry {#Icosahedral_symmetry}

*Image: Witting polytope.png.* The [Witting polytope](wikipedia://en/28363938), with ninety [van Oss polytopes](wikipedia://en/8581934#van_Oss_polygon)

#### Solids {#Solids}

The [rhombic enneacontahedron](wikipedia://en/1707682) is a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402) with a total of 90 [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) [faces](wikipedia://en/149926): 60 broad rhombi akin to those in the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) with diagonals in $1:\sqrt2$ ratio, and another 30 slim rhombi with diagonals in $1:\varphi^{2}$ [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). The obtuse angle of the broad rhombic faces is also the [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968), with the [obtuse angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_angle) in the faces of [golden rhombi](wikipedia://en/20825292) equal to the dihedral angle of a [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458) and the [tetrahedral vertex-center-vertex](wikipedia://en/30606#Angles_and_distances) angle, which is also the angle between [Plateau borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau's_laws): 109.471°. It is the dual polyhedron to the [rectified truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43453799), a [near-miss Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/3881930). On the other hand, the [final stellation of the icosahedron](wikipedia://en/2968303) has 90 edges. It also has 92 vertices like the rhombic enneacontahedron, when interpreted as a [simple polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_polyhedron). Meanwhile, the [truncated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/376538) and [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282) both have 90 [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034). A further four uniform [star polyhedra](wikipedia://en/8464397) ([U<sub>37</sub>](wikipedia://en/4157430), [U<sub>55</sub>](wikipedia://en/4157466), [U<sub>58</sub>](wikipedia://en/4157444), [U<sub>66</sub>](wikipedia://en/4157457)) and four uniform [compound polyhedra](wikipedia://en/8606809) ([UC<sub>32</sub>](wikipedia://en/14826806), [UC<sub>34</sub>](wikipedia://en/14848503), [UC<sub>36</sub>](wikipedia://en/14848541), [UC<sub>55</sub>](wikipedia://en/14808143)) contain 90 edges or [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).

#### Witting polytope {#Witting_polytope}

The [self-dual](wikipedia://en/8815#Dual_polytopes_and_tessellations) [Witting polytope](wikipedia://en/28363938) contains ninety [van Oss polytopes](wikipedia://en/8581934#van_Oss_polygon) such that sections by the common [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) of two non-orthogonal [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) of symmetry passing through the [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_%28geometry%29#General_polygons) yield [complex](wikipedia://en/8581934) <sub>3</sub>{4}<sub>3</sub> [Möbius–Kantor polygons](wikipedia://en/51430790).[^o35] The [root vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_vector) of [simple Lie group](wikipedia://en/1691490) [**E<sub>8</sub>**](wikipedia://en/647994) are represented by the [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) arrangement of the $4_{21}$ [polytope](wikipedia://en/19236362), which shares [240](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/240_%28number%29) vertices with the Witting polytope in four-dimensional [complex space](wikipedia://en/39125683). By [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter), the incidence matrix [configuration](wikipedia://en/56419724) of the Witting polytope can be represented as:

$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}40&9&12\\4&90&4\\12&9&40\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ or $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}40&12&12\\2&240&2\\12&12&40\end{smallmatrix}\right]$

This Witting configuration when reflected under the [finite space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_space) $\operatorname{PG}{(3,2^{2})}$ splits into 85 = 45 + 40 points *and* planes, alongside 27 + 90 + 240 = 357 lines.[^o35]

Whereas the rhombic enneacontahedron is the [zonohedrification](wikipedia://en/669402#Zonohedrification) of the regular dodecahedron,[^o36] a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/28363938#The_honeycomb_of_Witting_polytopes) of Witting polytopes holds vertices [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the E<sub>8</sub> [lattice](wikipedia://en/1800359), whose symmetries can be traced back to the regular icosahedron via the [icosian ring](wikipedia://en/27977943).[^o37]

### Cutting an annulus {#Cutting_an_annulus}

The maximal number of pieces that can be obtained by cutting an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158) with twelve cuts is 90 (and equivalently, the number of 12-dimensional [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634) that are *prime*).
