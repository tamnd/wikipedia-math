# Laplace principle (large deviations theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laplace principle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Laplace's principle** is a basic [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in [large deviations theory](wikipedia://en/3772909) which is similar to [Varadhan's lemma](wikipedia://en/13354070). It gives an [asymptotic expression](wikipedia://en/641995) for the [Lebesgue integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration) of exp(−*θφ*(*x*)) over a fixed set *A* as *θ* becomes large. Such expressions can be used, for example, in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) to determining the limiting behaviour of a system as the temperature tends to [absolute zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_zero).

:::

## Statement of the result {#Statement_of_the_result}

Let *A* be a [Lebesgue-measurable](wikipedia://en/18198) [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of *d*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*d*</sup> and let *φ* : **R**<sup>*d*</sup> → **R** be a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) with

$$\int_A e^{-\varphi(x)} \,dx < \infty.$$

Then

$$\lim_{\theta \to \infty} \frac1{\theta} \log \int_A e^{-\theta \varphi(x)} \, dx = - \mathop{\mathrm{ess \, inf}}_{x \in A} \varphi(x),$$

where ess inf denotes the [essential infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_infimum). Heuristically, this may be read as saying that for large *θ*,

$$\int_A e^{-\theta \varphi(x)} \, dx \approx \exp \left(-\theta \mathop{\mathrm{ess \, inf}}_{x \in A} \varphi(x) \right).$$

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

The Laplace principle can be applied to the family of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) **P**<sub>*θ*</sub> given by

$$\mathbf{P}_\theta (A) = \left( \int_A e^{-\theta \varphi(x)} \, dx \right) \bigg/ \left( \int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}} e^{-\theta \varphi(y)} \, dy \right)$$

to give an asymptotic expression for the probability of some event *A* as *θ* becomes large. For example, if *X* is a standard [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462) [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) on **R**, then

$$\lim_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \varepsilon \log \mathbf{P} \big[ \sqrt{\varepsilon} X \in A \big] = - \mathop{\mathrm{ess \, inf}}_{x \in A} \frac{x^2}{2}$$

for every measurable set *A*.
