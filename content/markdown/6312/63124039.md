# Allison Henrich

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Allison Henrich"}

**Allison Henrich** (born 1980) is an American mathematician specializing in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) and also interested in undergraduate-level mathematics research mentorship. She is a professor of mathematics at [Seattle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Henrich entered college planning for an undergraduate teaching career, graduating in 2003 from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) with a [double major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_major) in mathematics and philosophy. She completed a Ph.D. at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) in 2008. Her dissertation, *A Sequence of Degree One Vassiliev Invariants for Virtual Knots*, was supervised by Vladimir Chernov. At Dartmouth, [Carolyn S. Gordon](wikipedia://en/25624170) became another faculty mentor.

She joined the Seattle University mathematics faculty in 2009, and was promoted to full professor in 2019.

## Books {#Books}

Henrich is the coauthor of a book on knot theory, *An Interactive Introduction to Knot Theory* (with Inga Johnson, Dover Publications, 2017). She also coauthored the book *A Mathematician’s Practical Guide to Mentoring Undergraduate Research* (with Michael Dorff and Lara Pudwell, Mathematical Association of America, American Mathematical Society, and Council on Undergraduate Research, 2019).

With [Emille D. Lawrence](wikipedia://en/67329020), Matthew Pons, and David Taylor, she co-edited the book *Living Proof: Stories of Resilience Along the Mathematical Journey* (American Mathematical Society and Mathematical Association of America, 2019). She is also an editor of *Knots, Links, Spatial Graphs, and Algebraic Invariants* (with [Erica Flapan](wikipedia://en/38305559), Aaron Kaestner, and Sam Nelson, American Mathematical Society, 2017).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2015, the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) gave Henrich their Henry L. Alder Award for Distinguished Teaching by a Beginning College or University Mathematics Faculty Member, and also their Paul R. Halmos – Lester R. Ford Award for expository excellence for her article "Unknotting unknots" coauthored with [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman). The award citation for the Alder Award cited her work in interactive learning, in guiding undergraduate mathematics students to become mentors to elementary school students, and in founding a summer research program at for underrepresented undergraduates, hosted at Seattle University.
