# Gloria Conyers Hewitt

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gloria Conyers Hewitt"}

**Gloria Conyers Hewitt** (born October 26, 1935) is an American mathematician. She was the fourth African-American woman to receive a Ph.D. in mathematics.[^o7][^o3] Her main research interests were in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). She is the first African American woman to chair a math department in the United States.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hewitt was born on October 26, 1935, in [Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumter,_South_Carolina), [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina).[^o5] She entered [Fisk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisk_University) in 1952 and graduated in 1956 with a degree in secondary mathematics education. Without her knowledge, department chairman [Lee Lorch](wikipedia://en/8942515) recommended Hewitt to two graduate schools. As a result, she was offered a fellowship at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in her senior year, though she had not applied for it.[^o6] Hewitt received her master's degree from there in 1960, and then her Ph.D. (with a thesis on "Direct and Inverse Limits of Abstract Algebras") in 1962.[^o7][^o6]

## Career {#Career}

In 1961, Hewitt joined the faculty at the [University of Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montana).[^o6] In 1966 she became tenured and promoted to associate professor, then in 1972, to full professor.[^o9] In 1995, she was elected chair of the Department of Mathematical Science.[^o7] She served in that position until she retired in June 1999, with the title of [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus).[^o6][^o9]

While a professor at the University of Montana she participated in multiple other organizations. She served on the executive council of the mathematical honor society, [Pi Mu Epsilon](wikipedia://en/1565972).[^o9] She served as the chair of the committee that writes questions for the mathematics section of the [GREs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Record_Examinations).[^o9] Hewitt was also a faculty consultant for the [Advanced Placement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Placement) examination in calculus.[^o9] In 1995, she was awarded an ETS Certificate of Appreciation after twelve years of service.[^o9]

Hewitt served on the Board of Governors of the Mathematical Association of America.[^o10]

She was known for many mathematics accomplishments but most of all for being one of the first three black women to receive a mathematics award.

Hewitt's works focus on two mathematic areas: abstract algebra and group theory.[^o9] She has eight published research papers and twenty-one unpublished lectures.[^o9]

One would expect Hewitt to have faced many racial and gender oriented obstacles; however, in a personal interview she stated that she did not feel there had been any racial incidents in her career that had a detrimental effect on her studies.[^o6] She did however, write an article in the *[Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annals_of_the_New_York_Academy_of_Sciences)*, titled "The Status of Women in Mathematics".[^o11] Hewitt has said that "Some of my fellow graduate students did all they could to help and encourage me. They included me in most of their activities. I know this situation was not the norm for a lot of Blacks studying mathematics, but I was fortunate enough to be at the right place at the right time."[^o10]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

She was awarded a prestigious [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) postdoctoral Science Faculty [Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship). She was elected to the board of governors of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o7] Her accomplishments have also earned her recognition by [Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006) as a Black History Month 2018 Honoree.[^o13] In 2018, the University of Washington established the [Gloria Hewitt Endowed Graduate Student Support Fund](https://math.washington.edu/news/2018/04/03/gloria-hewitt-graduate-support-fund-established) in honor of Hewitt's immense contributions to the field of mathematics. The fund, established with an initial principal amount of \$50,000, was set up to "promote excellence in the graduate program of the Department of Mathematics, in particular enhancing efforts to achieve a more equitable representation of those under-represented in the field of mathematics.[^o14]
