# Malay Ghosh

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Malay Ghosh"}

**Malay Ghosh** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): মলয় ঘোষ) is an [Indian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian-American) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and currently a [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor) at the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida).[^o3] He obtained a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in 1962 from the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta), and subsequently a [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1964 from the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta). Then he moved to the United States to pursue higher academic studies and obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree in 1969 from the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), under the supervision of [Pranab K. Sen](wikipedia://en/32302045)[^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Ghosh was a faculty member at the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418) in the 1970s before briefly joining [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University). In 1982 he joined the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida). Ghosh is well known for his research in [nonparametric inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonparametric_inference), [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530), [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216), [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412)[^o6] and small-area estimation.[^o7] As a recognition of his seminal contributions, Ghosh served from 1996 to 2001 in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) Census Advisory Committee.[^o8] He has co-authored two books and more than 250 research publications and is the advisor of over 40 Ph.D. students, including Nitis Mukhopadhyay, Parthasarathi Lahiri and Gauri Sankar Datta.[^o9]
