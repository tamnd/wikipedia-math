# Hettie Belle Ege

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hettie Belle Ege"}

**Hettie Belle Ege** (31 March 1861 – 19 November 1942) was an American professor of mathematics. From 1914 to 1916, she was the acting president of [Mills College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mills_College).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Ege was born in [Erie, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie,_Illinois) on 31 March 1861, the daughter of Joseph Arthur Ege and his second wife, Catherine Rebecca Reisch Ege. Her parents were both from Pennsylvania; her father died the year she was born, and her mother remarried in 1869.[^o4] She attended [Western College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_College_for_Women) in [Oxford, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford,_Ohio), graduating in 1886; she later graduated from Mills College in 1903, with further studies at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), and the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Ege taught school in Ohio and Colorado as a young woman. From 1895 to 1930,[^o7] she was a professor of mathematics and Dean of Women at Mills College.[^o8] She also coached the Mills College basketball team.[^o9][^o10] From 1914 to 1916, she was the college's acting president.[^o5][^o11] "Since Mills is to California what Wellesley is to the East," explained a California magazine in 1915, "it is most apparent that the leading spirit of the institution is a dominant figure in the educational activities of the state."[^o12]

In 1918, she chaperoned a unit of 28 Mills students in the [Woman's Land Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman's_Land_Army_of_America), providing ranch labor during wartime shortages.[^o13] In 1920, the college marked her twenty-five years at Mills with a presentation and convocation.[^o14][^o15] At the festivities marking the school's founder's centennial in 1925, Ege received an honorary doctorate.[^o16]

After her retirement in 1930,[^o17] she spent some time in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), in England.[^o18] She was a regular guest at Mills College events[^o19] and alumnae gatherings: in 1933 she was honored as the college's dean emerita, alongside president [Aurelia Henry Reinhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelia_Henry_Reinhardt) and physician [Mariana Bertola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Bertola), at an annual breakfast of the Mills Club of San Francisco.[^o20]

## Death {#Death}

Ege died November 19, 1942, aged 81 years, in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California).[^o22][^o23] Her grave is on the campus of Mills College, and there is a dormitory named for Ege at Mills.[^o24][^o25]
