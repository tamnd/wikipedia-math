# Bennett's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bennett's inequality"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Bennett's [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489)** provides an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that the sum of [independent random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables) deviates from its [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) by more than any specified amount. Bennett's inequality was proved by George Bennett of the [University of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales) in 1962.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $X_{1}, \ldots X_{n}$ be [independent random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables) with finite variance. Further assume $|X_{i} - EX_{i}| \le a$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) for all $i$, and define $S_n = \sum_{i = 1}^n \left[X_i - \operatorname{E}(X_i)\right]$ and $\sigma^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{E}(X_i-\operatorname{E} X_i)^2.$ Then for any $t \ge 0$,

$$\Pr\left( S_n > t \right) \leq \exp\left( - \frac{\sigma^2}{a^2} h\left(\frac{at}{\sigma^2} \right)\right),$$

where $h(u) = (1 + u)\log(1 + u) - u$ and log denotes the natural logarithm.[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Generalizations and comparisons to other bounds {#Generalizations_and_comparisons_to_other_bounds}

For generalizations see Freedman (1975)[^o8] and Fan, Grama and Liu (2012)[^o9] for a [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536) version of Bennett's inequality and its improvement, respectively.

[Hoeffding's inequality](wikipedia://en/1136348) only assumes the summands are bounded almost surely, while Bennett's inequality offers some improvement when the variances of the summands are small compared to their almost sure bounds. However Hoeffding's inequality entails sub-Gaussian tails, whereas in general Bennett's inequality has Poissonian tails.

Bennett's inequality is most similar to the [Bernstein inequalities](wikipedia://en/9075610), the first of which also gives concentration in terms of the variance and almost sure bound on the individual terms. Bennett's inequality is stronger than this bound, but more complicated to compute.[^o6]

In both inequalities, unlike some other inequalities or limit theorems, there is no requirement that the component variables have identical or similar distributions.

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Suppose that each $X_{i}$ is an independent binary random variable with probability $p$. Then Bennett's inequality says that:

$$\Pr\left( \sum_{i = 1}^n X_i > pn + t \right) \leq \exp\left( - np h\left(\frac{t}{np}\right)\right).$$

For $t \geq 10 np$, $h(\frac{t}{np}) \geq \frac{t}{2np} \log \frac{t}{np},$ so

$$\Pr\left( \sum_{i = 1}^n X_i > pn + t \right) \leq \left(\frac{t}{np}\right)^{-t/2}$$

for $t \geq 10 np$.

By contrast, [Hoeffding's inequality](wikipedia://en/1136348) gives a bound of $\exp(-2 t^2/n)$ and the first [Bernstein inequality](wikipedia://en/9075610) gives a bound of $\exp(-\frac{t^2}{2np + 2t/3})$. For $t \gg np$, Hoeffding's inequality gives $\exp(-\Theta(t^2/n))$, Bernstein gives $\exp(-\Theta(t))$, and Bennett gives $\exp(-\Theta(t \log \frac{t}{np}))$.

:::
