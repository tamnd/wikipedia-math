# AKLT model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="AKLT model"}

In [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics), the **Affleck–Kennedy–Lieb–Tasaki** (**AKLT**) **model** is an extension of the one-dimensional [quantum](wikipedia://en/25202) [Heisenberg spin model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_model_%28quantum%29). The proposal and exact solution of this model by [Ian Affleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Affleck), [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb), Tom Kennedy and Hal Tasaki[^o19] provided crucial insight into the physics of the spin-1 Heisenberg chain.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] It has also served as a useful example for such concepts as valence bond solid order, [symmetry-protected topological order](wikipedia://en/36464530)[^o6][^o18][^o7][^o8] and matrix product state wavefunctions.

:::

## Background {#Background}

A major motivation for the AKLT model was the [Majumdar–Ghosh chain](wikipedia://en/24535618). Because two out of every set of three neighboring spins in a Majumdar–Ghosh [ground state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state) are paired into a singlet, or valence bond, the three spins together can never be found to be in a spin 3/2 state. In fact, the Majumdar–Ghosh Hamiltonian is nothing but the sum of all projectors of three neighboring spins onto a 3/2 state.

The main insight of the AKLT paper was that this construction could be generalized to obtain exactly solvable models for spin sizes other than 1/2. Just as one end of a valence bond is a spin 1/2, the ends of two valence bonds can be combined into a spin 1, three into a spin 3/2, etc.

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Affleck et al. were interested in constructing a one-dimensional state with a valence bond between every pair of sites. Because this leads to two spin 1/2s for every site, the result must be the [wavefunction](wikipedia://en/145343) of a spin 1 system.

For every adjacent pair of the spin 1s, two of the four constituent spin 1/2s are stuck in a total spin zero state. Therefore, each pair of spin 1s is forbidden from being in a combined spin 2 state. By writing this condition as a sum of projectors that favor the spin 2 state of pairs of spin 1s, AKLT arrived at the following Hamiltonian

$$\hat H = \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \textit{P}^{(2)}_{\langle ij \rangle} \sim \sum_j \vec{S}_j \cdot \vec{S}_{j+1} + \frac{1}{3} (\vec{S}_j \cdot \vec{S}_{j+1})^2$$

up to a constant, where the $\vec{S_i}$ are spin-1 operators, and $\textit{P}^{(2)}_{\langle ij \rangle}$ the local 2-point projector that favors the spin 2 state of an adjacent pair of spins.

This Hamiltonian is similar to the spin 1, one-dimensional [quantum](wikipedia://en/25202) [Heisenberg spin model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_model_%28quantum%29) but has an additional "biquadratic" spin interaction term.

:::

## Ground state {#Ground_state}

By construction, the ground state of the AKLT Hamiltonian is the valence bond solid with a single valence bond connecting every neighboring pair of sites. Pictorially, this may be represented as

Here the solid points represent spin 1/2s which are put into singlet states. The lines connecting the spin 1/2s are the valence bonds indicating the pattern of singlets. The ovals are projection operators which "tie" together two spin 1/2s into a single spin 1, projecting out the spin 0 or singlet subspace and keeping only the spin 1 or triplet subspace. The symbols "+", "0" and "−" label the standard spin 1 basis states (eigenstates of the $S^z$ operator).[^o20]

### Spin 1/2 edge states {#Spin_1/2_edge_states}

For the case of spins arranged in a ring ([periodic boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/9667552)) the AKLT construction yields a unique ground state. But for the case of an open chain, the first and last spin 1 have only a single neighbor, leaving one of their constituent spin 1/2s unpaired. As a result, the ends of the chain behave like free spin 1/2 moments even though the system consists of spin 1s only.

The spin 1/2 [edge states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_states) of the AKLT chain can be observed in a few different ways. For short chains, the edge states mix into a singlet or a triplet giving either a unique ground state or a three-fold multiplet of ground states. For longer chains, the edge states decouple exponentially quickly as a function of chain length leading to a ground state manifold that is four-fold degenerate.[^o21] By using a numerical method such as [DMRG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DMRG) to measure the local magnetization along the chain, it is also possible to see the edge states directly and to show that they can be removed by placing actual spin 1/2s at the ends.[^o22] It has even proved possible to detect the spin 1/2 edge states in measurements of a quasi-1D magnetic compound containing a small amount of impurities whose role is to break the chains into finite segments.[^o23] In 2021, a direct spectroscopic signature of spin 1/2 edge states was found in isolated quantum spin chains built out of [triangulene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulene), a spin 1 [polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycyclic_aromatic_hydrocarbon).[^o14]

### Matrix product state representation {#Matrix_product_state_representation}

The simplicity of the AKLT ground state allows it to be represented in compact form as a [matrix product state](wikipedia://en/42099889). This is a wavefunction of the form

$$|\Psi\rangle = \sum_{\{s\}} \operatorname{Tr}[A^{s_1} A^{s_2} \ldots A^{s_N}] |s_1 s_2 \ldots s_N\rangle.$$

Here the As are a set of three matrices labeled by $s_j$ and the trace comes from assuming periodic boundary conditions.

The AKLT ground state wavefunction corresponds to the choice:[^o20]

$$A^{+} = +\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\ \sigma^{+}$$

$$A^{0} = -\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}\ \sigma^{z}$$

$$A^{-} = -\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\ \sigma^{-}$$

where $\sigma$ is a [Pauli matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_matrix).

## Generalizations and extensions {#Generalizations_and_extensions}

The AKLT model has been solved on lattices of higher dimension,[^o19][^o24] even in [quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystals) . The model has also been constructed for higher Lie algebras including [SU(*n*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%28n%29),[^o26][^o27] [SO(*n*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%28n%29),[^o28] [Sp(n)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sp%28n%29)[^o29] and extended to the [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) SUq(*n*).[^o25]
