# Karl Heinrich Gräffe

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Karl Heinrich Gräffe"}

**Karl Heinrich Gräffe** (7 November 1799 – 2 December 1873) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who was professor at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Gräffe's father migrated to [North America](wikipedia://en/21139), leaving the family business of jewelry in his hands. Even so, Gräffe succeeded, studying at night, entering the *[Carolineum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig_University_of_Technology)* of Brunswick in 1821. From 1823, he studied at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) with professors [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss) and Thibaut, doctorate in 1825.[^n1]

In 1828 he was appointed professor of the [Zurich Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) and, as of 1833, associate professor at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) from the date of its creation. Simultaneously, also he was professor of the *Obere Industrieschule*.[^n2]

Gräffe is known for having been the first to enunciate a method to approximate the roots of any polynomial, a method known today as the [Dandelin-Gräffe method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dandelin-Gräffe_method).[^n3]
