# Erich Bessel-Hagen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erich Bessel-Hagen"}

*Image: Bessel-Hagen,Erich 1920 Göttingen.jpg.* Erich Bessel-Hagen

**Erich Bessel-Hagen** (12 September 1898 in [Charlottenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottenburg) – 29 March 1946 in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian) of mathematics.[^o2]

:::

Erich Paul Werner Bessel-Hagen was born in 1898 in Charlottenburg, a suburb, later a district in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin). He studied at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) where in 1920 he obtained a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) under the direction of [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625).

His reputation was that of a gentleman as well as a conscientious intellect. This was averred in the early 1940s, when the ruling Nazis increased their persecutions of German officials who have Jewish ancestry. After [Felix Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989) (a professor 30 years his senior) had been retired and placed under restrictions, Bessel-Hagen became the only former colleague who visited him regularly. On noticing that Hausdorff used private math researches to while away time, he started bringing him books he had borrowed from a library which no longer welcomed Jews.

*Image: Bessel-Hagen in Vorlesungen uber Topologie I.jpg.* Kerékjártó's portrait of Bessel-Hagen as a topological space.

Bessel-Hagen was occasionally the victim of practical jokes at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), where he studied for seven years. In the first volume of [Béla Kerékjártó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béla_Kerékjártó)'s [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) textbook *Vorlesungen über Topologie* (1923) the index points the reader trying to find "Bessel-Hagen" to page 151. There is no reference to Bessel-Hagen on this page, but there is a picture of a face-like topological space, intended as a jocular portrait of the big-eared mathematician.[^o4][^o5]
