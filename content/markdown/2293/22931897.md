# Eri Jabotinsky

*Image: Zeev Jabotinsky family.jpg.* Jabotinsky (right) with his parents in the late 1920s

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eri Jabotinsky"}

**Eri Jabotinsky** (עֵרִי זַ׳בּוֹטִינְסְקִי, also transliterated ***Ari***, 26 December 1910 – 6 June 1969) was a [Revisionist Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revisionist_Zionism) activist, [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) politician and academic mathematician. He was the son of [Ze'ev Jabotinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ze'ev_Jabotinsky), the founder of the opposition movement within [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) at the time, and later served in the Knesset between 1949 and 1951 as a member of the opposition [Herut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herut) party of [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin). Following his break with the party, he pursued his academic career. He published several works in mathematical [iteration theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iteration_theory) and was in his later life a professor in the [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jabotinsky was born in [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa) in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) in 1910. In 1919 the family [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah) to British-Mandate Palestine. Following the arrest of his father the following year, he moved to [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), attending high school in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) and later earning a degree in electrical engineering. Between 1933 and 1935 he worked as an engineer in an aircraft factory. In 1935 he returned to British Mandate Palestine, and worked as an engineer at the [Naharayim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naharayim) power station in the [Jordan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Valley_%28Middle_East%29).

A long-term member of Revisionism's [Betar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betar) youth movement, he became one of its representative leaders in 1936, and joined its worldwide board two years later. With Betar and the party's military wing, [Irgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun), he helped coordinate [illegal Jewish immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_Bet) into British Mandate Palestine. He was arrested by the British authorities in November 1937 and imprisoned in [Acre Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_Prison) until February 1938. He was later imprisoned again in 1940, and held for six months. Upon his release he moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), where his father died suddenly. There, together with [Hillel Kook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillel_Kook)'s 'Bergson Boys', [Aryeh Ben-Eliezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryeh_Ben-Eliezer), [Shmuel Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Merlin) and Yitzhak Ben-Ami among others, he founded the Emergency Committee to Save European Jewry.[^o5] He briefly returned to British Mandate Palestine, but was again arrested by the British and expelled for illegal activities in 1944.

Following [Israel's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_%28Israel%29) he returned to Israel in 1948, and was elected to the [first Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Israeli_legislative_election) the following year as a member of the [Herut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herut) party's list of candidates. However, following disagreements over the party's direction and its new leadership by [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin), on 20 February 1951 Jabotinsky and Hillel Kook left Herut, and sat as independents for the rest of the term, although the move was not recognised by the House Committee.[^o6]

After leaving the Knesset, he earned a PhD in mathematics in 1957 from the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem). He also lectured on electricity theory at the [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology) between 1955 and his death in 1969.[^o7] He published several works on mathematical [iteration theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iteration_theory) and popularized [Jabotinsky matrices](wikipedia://en/14320253). According to Detlef Gronau he is known for devising the "[Jabotinsky equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabotinsky_equations)".[^o3]

He was the father-in-law of [Anatoly Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Rubin).
