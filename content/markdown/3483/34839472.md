# David Jerison

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Jerison"}

**David Saul Jerison** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659). He is currently a professor of mathematics and a MacVicar Faculty Fellow at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

The son of mathematician [Meyer Jerison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meyer_Jerison) and Miriam Schwartz, Jerison did his undergraduate studies at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and received a bachelor's degree in 1975. He then received his Ph.D. in 1980 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) advised by [Elias M. Stein](wikipedia://en/418073). After postdoctoral research at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), he joined MIT in 1981.[^o3]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1985, he received an A.P. [Sloan Foundation Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) and a [Presidential Young Investigator Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Young_Investigator_Award).[^o7] In 1994, Jerison was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Zurich.[^o8] In 1999, he was elected as a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o9] He became a MacVicar Fellow in 2004.[^o3] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o10] In 2012, he received, jointly with [John M. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Lee), the [Stefan Bergman Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bergman_Prize) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11]
