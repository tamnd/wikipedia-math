# Polar homology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polar homology"}

In [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), a **polar homology** is a group which captures holomorphic invariants of a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) in a similar way to usual [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of a [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_%28mathematics%29) in [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562). Polar homology was defined by B. Khesin and A. Rosly in 1999.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *M* be a [complex projective manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_manifold). The space $C_k$ of polar *k*-chains is a vector space over ${\mathbb C}$ defined as a quotient $A_k/R_k$, with $A_k$ and $R_k$ vector spaces defined below.

### Defining *A<sub>k</sub>* {#Defining_Ak}

The space $A_k$ is freely generated by the triples $(X, f, \alpha)$, where *X* is a smooth, *k*-dimensional complex manifold, $f:\; X \mapsto M$ a holomorphic map, and $\alpha$ is a rational *k*-form on *X*, with first order poles on a [divisor with normal crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_crossing_divisor).

### Defining *R<sub>k</sub>* {#Defining_Rk}

The space $R_k$ is generated by the following relations.

1. $$\lambda (X, f, \alpha)=(X, f, \lambda\alpha)$$
1. $(X,f,\alpha)=0$ if $\dim f(X) < k$.
1. $\ \sum_i(X_i,f_i,\alpha_i)=0$ provided that

$$\sum_if_{i*}\alpha_i\equiv 0,$$

where

$dim \;f_i(X_i)=k$ for all $i$ and the push-forwards $f_{i*}\alpha_i$ are considered on the smooth part of $\cup_i f_i(X_i)$.

### Defining the boundary operator {#Defining_the_boundary_operator}

The boundary operator $\partial:\; C_k \mapsto C_{k-1}$ is defined by

$$\partial(X,f,\alpha)=2\pi \sqrt{-1}\sum_i(V_i, f_i, res_{V_i}\,\alpha),$$

where $V_i$ are components of the polar divisor of $\alpha$, *res* is the [Poincaré residue](wikipedia://en/7062375), and $f_i=f|_{V_i}$ are restrictions of the map *f* to each component of the divisor.

Khesin and Rosly proved that this boundary operator is well defined, and satisfies $\partial^2=0$. They defined the **polar cohomology** as the quotient $\operatorname{ker}\; \partial / \operatorname{im} \; \partial$.

:::
