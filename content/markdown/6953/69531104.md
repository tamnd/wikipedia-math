# Orthologic triangles

*Image: OrthologicTriangles.png.* Two orthologic triangles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthologic triangles"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), two [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) are said to be **orthologic** if the [perpendiculars](wikipedia://en/76944) from the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of one of them to the corresponding sides of the other are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157) (i.e., they [intersect](wikipedia://en/8240558) at a single [point](wikipedia://en/593693)). This is a [symmetric](wikipedia://en/44362809) property; that is, if the perpendiculars from the vertices $A, B, C$ of triangle $\triangle ABC$ to the sides $EF, FD, DE$ of triangle $\triangle DEF$ are concurrent then the perpendiculars from the vertices $D, E, F$ of $\triangle DEF$ to the sides $BC, CA, AB$ of $\triangle ABC$ are also concurrent. The points of concurrence are known as the **orthology centres** of the two triangles.[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Some pairs of orthologic triangles {#Some_pairs_of_orthologic_triangles}

The following are some triangles associated with the reference triangle ABC and orthologic with it.[^o5]

- [Medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743)
- [Anticomplementary triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticomplementary_triangle)
- The triangle whose vertices are the points of contact of the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) with the sides of ABC
- [Tangential triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_triangle)
- [Extouch triangle](wikipedia://en/11125142)
- The triangle formed by the bisectors of the external angles of triangle ABC
- The [pedal triangle](wikipedia://en/152983) of any point P in the plane of triangle ABC (and as a special case the [orthic triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthic_triangle))
