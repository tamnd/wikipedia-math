# Central triangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Central triangle"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **central triangle** is a triangle in the plane of the reference triangle. The [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) relative to the reference triangle are expressible in a certain cyclical way in terms of two [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) having the same degree of [homogeneity](wikipedia://en/622844). At least one of the two functions must be a [triangle center function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_center_function). The [excentral triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excentral_triangle) is an example of a central triangle. The central triangles have been classified into three types based on the properties of the two functions.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Triangle center function {#Triangle_center_function}

A [triangle center function](wikipedia://en/12401488) is a real valued function $F(u,v,w)$ of three real variables $u, v, w$ having the following properties:

- [Homogeneity property](wikipedia://en/622844): $F(tu,tv,tw) = t^n F(u,v,w)$ for some constant $n$ and for all $t > 0$. The constant $n$ is the degree of homogeneity of the function $F(u,v,w).$
- Bisymmetry property: $F(u,v,w) = F(u,w,v).$

### Central triangles of Type 1 {#Central_triangles_of_Type_1}

Let $f(u,v,w)$ and $g(u,v,w)$ be two triangle center functions, not both identically zero functions, having the same degree of homogeneity. Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths of the reference triangle $\triangle ABC$. An $(f, g)$-central triangle of Type 1 is a triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ the trilinear coordinates of whose vertices have the following form:[^o6][^o7]

$$\begin{array}{rcccccc} A' =& f(a,b,c) &:& g(b,c,a) &:& g(c,a,b) \\ B' =& g(a,b,c) &:& f(b,c,a) &:& g(c,a,b) \\ C' =& g(a,b,c) &:& g(b,c,a) &:& f(c,a,b) \end{array}$$

### Central triangles of Type 2 {#Central_triangles_of_Type_2}

Let $f(u,v,w)$ be a triangle center function and $g(u,v,w)$ be a function function satisfying the homogeneity property and having the same degree of homogeneity as $f(u,v,w)$ but not satisfying the bisymmetry property. An $(f, g)$-central triangle of Type 2 is a triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ the trilinear coordinates of whose vertices have the following form:[^o6]

$$\begin{array}{rcccccc} A' =& f(a,b,c) &:& g(b,c,a) &:& g(c,b,a) \\ B' =& g(a,c,b) &:& f(b,c,a) &:& g(c,a,b) \\ C' =& g(a,b,c) &:& g(b,a,c) &:& f(c,a,b) \end{array}$$

### Central triangles of Type 3 {#Central_triangles_of_Type_3}

Let $g(u,v,w)$ be a triangle center function. An $g$-central triangle of Type 3 is a triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ the trilinear coordinates of whose vertices have the following form:[^o6]

$$\begin{array}{rrcrcr} A' =& 0 \quad\ \ &:& g(b,c,a) &:& - g(c,b,a) \\ B' =& - g(a,c,b) &:& 0 \quad\ \ &:& g(c,a,b) \\ C' =& g(a,b,c) &:& - g(b,a,c) &:& 0 \quad\ \ \end{array}$$

This is a degenerate triangle in the sense that the points $A', B', C'$ are collinear.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

If $f = g$, the $(f, g)$-central triangle of Type 1 degenerates to the triangle center $A'$. All central triangles of both Type 1 and Type 2 relative to an equilateral triangle degenerate to a point.

:::

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Type 1 {#Type_1}

- The [excentral triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excentral_triangle) of triangle $\triangle ABC$ is a central triangle of Type 1. This is obtained by taking $f(u,v,w) = -1,\ g(u,v,w) = 1.$
- Let $X$ be a triangle center defined by the triangle center function $g(a,b,c).$ Then the cevian triangle of $X$ is a $(0, g)$-central triangle of Type 1.[^o15]
- Let $X$ be a triangle center defined by the triangle center function $f(a,b,c).$ Then the anticevian triangle of $X$ is a $(-f, f)$-central triangle of Type 1.[^o16]
- The Lucas central triangle is the $(f, g)$-central triangle with $$f(a,b,c) = a(2S+S_2), \quad g(a,b,c) = aS_A,$$ where $S$ is twice the area of triangle ABC and $S_A = \tfrac{1}{2}(b^2 + c^2 - a^2).$ [^o17]

### Type 2 {#Type_2}

- Let $X$ be a triangle center. The [pedal](wikipedia://en/152983) and [antipedal triangles](wikipedia://en/152983) of $X$ are central triangles of Type 2.[^o19]
- [Yff Central Triangle](wikipedia://en/36014669#Yff_central_triangle)[^o20]
