# Elizabeth B. Dussan V.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elizabeth B. Dussan V."}

**Elizabeth B. Stein Dussan V,** who was born in 1946, is an American [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969), [condensed matter physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics), and [chemical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering). Her research involves [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), and she is known for her work on [wetting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetting), [porous media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porous_medium), and [fluid-fluid interfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interface_%28matter%29).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Dussan completed her undergraduate degree in applied mathematics from [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University)[^o3] in 1967. Furthermore, she completed her Ph.D. from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in 1972. Her [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation), *On the Motion of a Line Common to Three Different Materials*, was supervised by applied mathematician [Stephen H. Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_H._Davis)., and was a part of her work while studying for her Ph.D.

She is retired as a Scientific Advisor for the [Schlumberger-Doll Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlumberger),[^o4] as well as having taught [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Dussan became a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in 1984, honoring her for the work and research in "spreading of liquids on solid surfaces". Following this achievement, she then became a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) in 1985 "for her deep insights into the mechanisms and the realistic modeling of phenomena involving fluid-fluid interfaces, particularly in situations in which moving contact lines and mutual fluid displacement occur".

She was elected to the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in 2004 "for innovative contributions to the wetting of solids and complex flows in porous media". In 2009, she became one of the inaugural Fellows of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) for her actions and contributions in wetting and flow in porous media.

In 1985, Stony Brook University awarded her their Distinguished Alumni Award as a result of her work as a Guggenheim Fellow of the American Physical Society.
