# Spider diagram

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spider diagram"}

In mathematics, a **unitary spider diagram** adds existential points to an [Euler](wikipedia://en/2427526) or a [Venn diagram](wikipedia://en/61701). The points indicate the existence of an attribute described by the intersection of contours in the Euler diagram. These points may be joined forming a shape like a [spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider). Joined points represent an "or" condition, also known as a [logical disjunction](wikipedia://en/7962).

:::

A **spider diagram** is a boolean expression involving unitary spider diagrams and the logical symbols $\land,\lor,\lnot$. For example, it may consist of the conjunction of two spider diagrams, the disjunction of two spider diagrams, or the negation of a spider diagram.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

*Image: SpiderEuler.jpg.* Logical disjunction superimposed on Euler diagram

In the image shown, the following conjunctions are apparent from the Euler diagram.

$$A \land B$$

$$B \land C$$

$$F \land E$$

$$G \land F$$

In the [universe of discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe_of_discourse) defined by this [Euler diagram](wikipedia://en/2427526), in addition to the conjunctions specified above, all of the sets from *A* through *G*, except for *C*, are available separately. The set *C* is only available as a subset of *B*. Often, in complicated diagrams, [singleton sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton_set) and/or conjunctions may be obscured by other set combinations.

The two spiders in the example correspond to the following logical expressions:

- Red spider: $(F \land E) \lor (G) \lor (D)$
- Blue spider: $(A) \lor (C \land B) \lor (F)$

:::
