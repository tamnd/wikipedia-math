# Heawood number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heawood number"}

*Image: Taxel genus 3 colouring.svg.* A 9-coloured triple torus (genus-3 surface) – dotted lines represent handles

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Heawood number** of a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) is an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) for the number of colors that suffice to color any [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in the surface.

:::

In 1890 Heawood proved for all surfaces *except* the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) that no more than

$$H(S)=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{49-24 e(S)}}{2}\right\rfloor = \left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+48 g(S)}}{2}\right\rfloor$$

colors are needed to color any graph embedded in a surface of [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) $e(S)$, or [genus](wikipedia://en/61891#Orientable_surfaces) $g(S)$ for an orientable surface.[^o2] The number $H(S)$ became known as the Heawood number in 1976.

*Image: Klein\_bottle\_colouring.svg.* A 6-colored Klein bottle, the only exception to the [Heawood conjecture](wikipedia://en/1585274)

Franklin proved that the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of a graph embedded in the [Klein bottle](wikipedia://en/17412) can be as large as $6$, but never exceeds $6$.[^o3] Later it was proved in the works of [Gerhard Ringel](wikipedia://en/1571968), J. W. T. Youngs, and other contributors that the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) with $H(S)$ vertices can be embedded in the surface $S$ unless $S$ is the [Klein bottle](wikipedia://en/17412).[^o4] This established that Heawood's bound could not be improved.

The case of the sphere is the [four-color conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-color_conjecture), which was settled by [Kenneth Appel](wikipedia://en/311544) and [Wolfgang Haken](wikipedia://en/311546) in 1976.[^o5][^o6]
