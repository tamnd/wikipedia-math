# Vietoris–Begle mapping theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vietoris–Begle mapping theorem"}

The **Vietoris–Begle mapping theorem** is a result in the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). It is named for [Leopold Vietoris](wikipedia://en/105043) and [Edward G. Begle](wikipedia://en/5469773). The statement of the theorem, below, is as formulated by [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_spaces), and let $f:X\to Y$ be [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873) and [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122). Suppose that the [fibers](wikipedia://en/579311) of $f$ are [acyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclic_complex), so that

$\tilde H_r(f^{-1}(y)) = 0,$ for all $0\leq r\leq n-1$ and all $y\in Y$,

with $\tilde H_r$ denoting the $r$th [reduced Vietoris homology](wikipedia://en/3110036) [group](wikipedia://en/19447). Then, the induced [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564)

$$f_*:\tilde H_r(X)\to\tilde H_r(Y)$$

is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) for $r\leq n-1$ and a surjection for $r=n$.

Note that as stated the theorem doesn't hold for homology theories like [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041). For example, Vietoris homology groups of the closed [topologist's sine curve](wikipedia://en/292224) and of a segment are isomorphic (since the first projects onto the second with acyclic fibers). But the singular homology differs, since the segment is path connected and the topologist's sine curve is not.

:::
