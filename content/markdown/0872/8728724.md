# Octadecagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octadecagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **octadecagon** (or **octakaidecagon**[^o2]) or 18-gon is an eighteen-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621).[^o3]

:::

## Regular octadecagon {#Regular_octadecagon}

*Image: Achtzehneck mit Diagonalen.svg.* Octadecagon with all 135 diagonals

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) octadecagon* has a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {18} and can be constructed as a quasiregular [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [enneagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enneagon), t{9}, which alternates two types of edges.

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

As 18 = 2 × 3<sup>2</sup>, a regular octadecagon cannot be [constructed](wikipedia://en/523430) using a [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge).[^o7] However, it is constructible using [neusis](wikipedia://en/3752339), or an [angle trisection](wikipedia://en/91111) with a [tomahawk](wikipedia://en/1735473).

*Image: 01-Achtzehneck-Tomahawk.gif.* Octadecagon, an exact construction based on the angle trisection 120° by means of the tomahawk, animation 1 min 34 s.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Symmetries of octadecagon.png.* Symmetries of a regular octadecagon. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Blue mirrors are drawn through vertices, and purple mirrors are drawn through edge. Gyration orders are given in the center.

The *regular octadecagon* has [Dih<sub>18</sub> symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 36. There are 5 subgroup dihedral symmetries: Dih<sub>9</sub>, (Dih<sub>6</sub>, Dih<sub>3</sub>), and (Dih<sub>2</sub> Dih<sub>1</sub>), and 6 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: (Z<sub>18</sub>, Z<sub>9</sub>), (Z<sub>6</sub>, Z<sub>3</sub>), and (Z<sub>2</sub>, Z<sub>1</sub>).

These 15 symmetries can be seen in 12 distinct symmetries on the octadecagon. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o11] Full symmetry of the regular form is **r36** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars), and **i** when reflection lines path through both edges and vertices. Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g18** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

:::

## Dissection {#Dissection}

*Image: 18-gon rhombic dissection-size2.svg.* 18-gon with 144 rhombs

*Image: Equilateral\_pentagonal\_dissection\_of\_regular\_octadecagon.svg.* An [equilateral pentagonal](wikipedia://en/19922843) [dissection](wikipedia://en/4850275#Nonperiodic_monohedral_pentagonal_tilings), with sequential internal angles: 60°, 160°, 80°, 100°, and 140°. Each of the 24 pentagons can be seen as the union of an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) and an 80° [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884).[^n1]

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) states that every [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103) (a 2*m*-gon whose opposite sides are parallel and of equal length) can be dissected into *m*(*m* − 1)/2 parallelograms.[^o14] In particular this is true for regular polygons with evenly many sides, in which case the parallelograms are all rhombi. For the *regular octadecagon*, *m* = 9, and it can be divided into 36: 4 sets of 9 rhombs. This decomposition is based on a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) projection of a [9-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-cube), with 36 of 4608 faces. The list enumerates the number of solutions as 112018190, including up to 18-fold rotations and chiral forms in reflection.

## Uses {#Uses}

*Image: 3.9.18 vertex.png.*

A regular triangle, nonagon, and octadecagon can completely surround a point in the plane, one of 17 different combinations of regular polygons with this property.[^o18] However, this pattern cannot be extended to an [Archimedean tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_by_regular_polygons) of the plane: because the triangle and the nonagon both have an odd number of sides, neither of them can be completely surrounded by a ring alternating the other two kinds of polygon.

The regular octadecagon can tessellate the plane with concave hexagonal gaps. And another tiling mixes in nonagons and octagonal gaps. The first tiling is related to a [truncated hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864030), and the second the [truncated trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864170).

## Related figures {#Related_figures}

An **octadecagram** is an 18-sided star polygon, represented by symbol {18/n}. There are two regular [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303): {18/5} and {18/7}, using the same points, but connecting every fifth or seventh points. There are also five compounds: {18/2} is reduced to 2{9} or two [enneagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enneagon), {18/3} is reduced to 3{6} or three [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733), {18/4} and {18/8} are reduced to 2{9/2} and 2{9/4} or two [enneagrams](wikipedia://en/34963576), {18/6} is reduced to 6{3} or 6 equilateral triangles, and finally {18/9} is reduced to 9{2} as nine [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728).

Deeper truncations of the regular enneagon and enneagrams can produce isogonal ([vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)) intermediate octadecagram forms with equally spaced vertices and two edge lengths. Other truncations form double coverings: t{9/8}={18/8}=2{9/4}, t{9/4}={18/4}=2{9/2}, t{9/2}={18/2}=2{9}.[^o22]
