# Feldman–Hájek theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Feldman–Hájek theorem"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Feldman–Hájek theorem** or **Feldman–Hájek dichotomy** is a fundamental result in the theory of [Gaussian measures](wikipedia://en/6572909). It states that two Gaussian measures $\mu$ and $\nu$ on a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space) $X$ are either [equivalent measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalent_measures) or else [mutually singular](wikipedia://en/4304500):[^o2] there is no possibility of an intermediate situation in which, for example, $\mu$ has a [density](wikipedia://en/43487) with respect to $\nu$ but not vice versa. In the special case that $X$ is a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), it is possible to give an explicit description of the circumstances under which $\mu$ and $\nu$ are equivalent: writing $m_{\mu}$ and $m_{\nu}$ for the means of $\mu$ and $\nu,$ and $C_\mu$ and $C_\nu$ for their [covariance operators](wikipedia://en/33447667), equivalence of $\mu$ and $\nu$ holds if and only if[^o3]

:::

- $\mu$ and $\nu$ have the same [Cameron–Martin space](wikipedia://en/6573013) $H = C_\mu^{1/2}(X) = C_\nu^{1/2}(X)$;
- the difference in their means lies in this common Cameron–Martin space, i.e. $m_\mu - m_\nu \in H$; and
- the operator $(C_\mu^{-1/2} C_\nu^{1/2}) (C_\mu^{-1/2} C_\nu^{1/2})^{\ast} - I$ is a [Hilbert–Schmidt operator](wikipedia://en/976673) on $\bar{H}.$

A simple consequence of the Feldman–Hájek theorem is that dilating a Gaussian measure on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space $X$ (i.e. taking $C_\nu = s C_\mu$ for some scale factor $s \geq 0$) always yields two mutually singular Gaussian measures, except for the trivial dilation with $s = 1,$ since $(s^2 - 1) I$ is Hilbert–Schmidt only when $s = 1.$
