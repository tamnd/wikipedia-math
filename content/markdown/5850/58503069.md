# Alexander Nagel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Nagel"}

**Alexander Joseph Nagel** (born 13 September 1945 in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City)) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specializing in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), functions of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), and linear [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He received in 1966 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) his bachelor's degree and in 1971 from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) his PhD under the supervision of [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972) with thesis *Sheaves of Holomorphic Functions with Boundary Conditions and Sheaf Cohomology in Banach Algebras*. At the [University of Wisconsin, Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin,_Madison), Nagel was from 1970 to 1972 an instructor, from 1972 to 1974 an assistant professor, from 1974 to 1977 an associate professor, and from 1977 to 2012 a full professor, retiring in December 2012 as professor emeritus. He was chair of the mathematics department in 1991–1993 and in 2011–2012, and Associate Dean for Natural Sciences in the [College of Letters and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison_College_of_Letters_and_Science) in 1993–1998.[^o5]

He was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) for the academic year 1987–1988. He shared with [Stephen Wainger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Wainger) the [Stefan Bergman Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bergman_Prize) for 2007–2008.[^o6] Nagel was elected a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 2009 and a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012.

He wrote a series of major papers on fundamental operators in complex analysis, in particular on the question of how the geometry of the underlying domains relates to concrete estimates of the kernels. A deep paper by Nagel, [Stein](wikipedia://en/418073) and Wainger with the unassuming title "Balls and metrics defined by vector fields: Basic properties" had tremendous impact on many branches of analysis and geometry. Alex was a major contributor to the theory of singular Radon transforms, in various stages of development. He is also well known for a fundamental estimate on Fourier transforms of surface carried measures and related applications.[^o5]

Alexander Nagel, the elder son of the philosopher [Ernest Nagel](wikipedia://en/1216778), is the brother of the physicist [Sidney R. Nagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_R._Nagel).
