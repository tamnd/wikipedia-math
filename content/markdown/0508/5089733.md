# Bitopological space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bitopological space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **bitopological space** is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) endowed with *two* [topologies](wikipedia://en/30450). Typically, if the set is $X$ and the topologies are $\sigma$ and $\tau$ then the bitopological space is referred to as $(X,\sigma,\tau)$. The notion was introduced by J. C. Kelly in the study of [quasimetrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasimetric), i.e. distance functions that are not required to be symmetric.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Continuity"}

## Continuity {#Continuity}

A [map](wikipedia://en/516931) $f:X\to X'$ from a bitopological space $(X,\tau_1,\tau_2)$ to another bitopological space $(X',\tau_1',\tau_2')$ is called **continuous** or sometimes **pairwise continuous** if $f$ is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) both as a map from $(X,\tau_1)$ to $(X',\tau_1')$ and as map from $(X,\tau_2)$ to $(X',\tau_2')$.

:::

## Bitopological variants of topological properties {#Bitopological_variants_of_topological_properties}

Corresponding to well-known properties of topological spaces, there are versions for bitopological spaces.

- A bitopological space $(X,\tau_1,\tau_2)$ is **pairwise compact** if each cover $\{U_i\mid i\in I\}$ of $X$ with $U_i\in \tau_1\cup\tau_2$, contains a finite subcover. In this case, $\{U_i\mid i\in I\}$ must contain at least one member from $\tau_1$ and at least one member from $\tau_2$
- A bitopological space $(X,\tau_1,\tau_2)$ is **pairwise Hausdorff** if for any two distinct points $x,y\in X$ there exist disjoint $U_1\in \tau_1$ and $U_2\in\tau_2$ with $x\in U_1$ and $y\in U_2$.
- A bitopological space $(X,\tau_1,\tau_2)$ is **pairwise zero-dimensional** if opens in $(X,\tau_1)$ that are closed in $(X,\tau_2)$ form a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) for $(X,\tau_1)$, and opens in $(X,\tau_2)$ that are closed in $(X,\tau_1)$ form a basis for $(X,\tau_2)$.
- A bitopological space $(X,\sigma,\tau)$ is called **binormal** if for every $F_\sigma$ $\sigma$-closed and $F_\tau$ $\tau$-closed sets there are $G_\sigma$ $\sigma$-open and $G_\tau$ $\tau$-open sets such that $F_\sigma\subseteq G_\tau$ $F_\tau\subseteq G_\sigma$, and $G_\sigma\cap G_\tau= \emptyset.$
