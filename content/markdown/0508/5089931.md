# Roy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roy"}

**Roy** is both a [given name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Given_name) and a family [surname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname) with varied origins.

:::

## France and England {#France_and_England}

*Image: House of Le Roy, Normandie.jpg.* Coat of arms of Le Roy, Normandy. [Bibliothèque nationale de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliothèque_nationale_de_France).

### Origin {#Origin}

Written interchangeably in records as: *Roi*, *Le Roi*, *De Roy*, and *Le Roy*, the surname Roy originated from the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans), the descendants of Norse [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) who settled in [Amigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amigny), a commune in [Manche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manche), [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy) in the 10th century.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

Roy derived from the Old Northern French *roy*, *roi* (), meaning "king", or "the king" (*royne* for "queen").[^o8][^o9] It arose as a Norman [byname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithet) before the [Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest) and was passed on as a family name in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages).[^o10]

The Vikings, after having been given their new lands, adopted the Old French language of the northern regions of France and created the [Duchy of Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy). Their descendants, the Normans, took their language and naming practices with them to England, where a dialect of Old Norman called [Anglo-Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Norman_language) was formed.[^o11]

### Normandy {#Normandy}

*Image: Blason-argent-3-merlettes-sable.svg.* Le Roy of Normandy (Silver, three black [merlettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martlet))

Originally, Roy or Roi was a Norman regal name.[^o13] A man may have had 'kingly' bearing, held a position of authority, was a tournament victor, or it was given as a byname in direct service to the [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King).[^o14] This reflects Norman adaptation of social or martial identifiers – a cultural inheritance from their Viking ancestry, forming a family name that was passed down.[^o15][^o16]

In Normandy, the Roy family originated from [knighthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knighthood) and several knights,[^o17] [abbots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbot)[^o18] and feudal lords ([seigneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seigneur)) of the family passed down the name throughout France and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland).[^o19][^o20][^o21][^o22] Early permanent names belonged to nobles as this was needed to secure: estate inheritance and land ownership, legal proof of hereditary titles and pedigrees documenting alliances, marriages and royal connections.[^o23] Surnames were not common amongst the lower class in the early medieval populations. Commoners used nicknames or profession names, but they changed every generation as a son did not generally inherit his father's descriptive or trade name until they became fixed by the late 14th century.[^o24]

In the 12th century, the Roy family was amongst the other family surnames identified in the city of [Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen) in Normandy. Official medieval records of the period show the name was recorded originating from the same lineage in its [vernacular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular) forms, or translated by scribes into the [Latinised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Latinised_names) *Rex, dictus Rex ("called king")* or the [genitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genitive) form, *Regis*.[^o25][^o14]

*Image: Blason Ordre du Temple.svg.* Coat of arms of the Knights Templar

One of the earliest references cite *Guillaume de Roy* (William of Roy), who was a knight of the [Knights Templar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Templar).[^o26][^o27][^o28] While born *Guillaume Le Roy*, the usage of de Roy ("of Roy"), a [nobiliary particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobiliary_particle), signified hereditary [nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobility).[^o29] Specifically, it identified Guillaume as the lord of a [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) and from the noble house of Roy, who originated from a paternal lineage of knights that was recognized by the [monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch).[^o5][^o30] This was an official legal requirement in the [Latin Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Rule) to be a knight of the Templars.[^o31][^o32][^o33] Guillaume de Roy was recorded as *Guillelmus de Roy*, Templar of the [Diocese of Soissons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Soissons), by the court scribes during the [Trials of the Knights Templar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trials_of_the_Knights_Templar).[^o34]

### Anglo-Norman England {#Anglo-Norman_England}

*Image: Edward III of England (Order of the Garter).jpg.* "Roy" [Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III), King of England. *[Bruges Garter Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruges_Garter_Book)*.

After the Norman Conquest, the victorious Normans and their allies settled England and eventually formed the ruling class of nobles called [Anglo-Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Normans).[^o36][^o6] Roy or Roi was a family name[^o37] and also a title that was held by the kings of England. The royal administration integrated the legal term "roy" into the titles of official state offices: *[Norroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norroy_and_Ulster_King_of_Arms)* "North King" and *[Viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy)* "in place of King".[^o38][^o39][^o40] Anglo-Norman kings would also pass on hereditary [patronymic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronymic) surnames like *[Fitzroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitzroy_%28surname%29)*, from *[Fi(t)z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitz)* meaning "son of" and *Roy* "king", denoting the name bearer as a "son of the king".[^o41][^o42][^o43]

[Le Roy le veult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Roy_le_veult) ("The King wills it"), written and spoken in the original form of "Roy", is a Norman phrase still used in the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom) to this day as [royal assent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_assent). A legacy of a time prior to 1488 when parliamentary and judicial proceedings were conducted in Norman French, the language of the conquerors after 1066.[^o44][^o45]

### North America {#North_America}

*Image: Familles roy coatofarms.png.* Arms of the Association of Roy Families of America

In [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) and in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), the descendants of the families of Roy, Le Roy[^o47] that immigrated to North America have been granted a [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) by the [Governor General of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_General_of_Canada).[^o48][^o49][^o50]

## South Asia {#South_Asia}

In India and Bangladesh, Roy is an [anglicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation) [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) variant of the surname *[Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_%28surname%29)*, meaning "king". [Rai (title)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_%28title%29), is the historical title of royalty and nobility in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), used by rulers and chieftains of many [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states) and as a family name.[^o52]

Before the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), the English spelling of "Roy" did not exist in Bengali records because those records were primarily kept in Bengali, [Persian script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_alphabet) or [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit)-derived scripts. The transition occurred as the British [transliterated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) local titles into English based on how they heard them pronounced.[^o53] In the Bengali language, the Sanskrit root *Raja* or *Rai* is typically pronounced as the English "Roy".

The name is diverse and can be found among upper castes as well as among the scheduled castes.[^o54]

## Scotland {#Scotland}

The name Roy is also found in Scotland, an anglicisation from the [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic) nickname *ruadh*, meaning "red". The name predominantly comes second as a [middle name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_name).[^o56] A famous example of the name usage is that of [Rob Roy MacGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Roy_MacGregor), who was a Scottish outlaw and folk hero.
