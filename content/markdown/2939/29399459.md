# 3-transposition group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="3-transposition group"}

In mathematical [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **3-transposition group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) generated by a conjugacy class of [involutions](wikipedia://en/293450), called the **3-transpositions**, such that the product of any two involutions from the conjugacy class has [order](wikipedia://en/493527) at most 3.

:::

They were first studied by (txt) who discovered the three [Fischer groups](wikipedia://en/327206) as examples of 3-transposition groups.

## History {#History}

(Fischer 1964) first studied 3-transposition groups in the special case when the product of any two distinct 3-transpositions has order 3. He showed that a finite group with this property is solvable, and has a (nilpotent) 3-group of index 2. (Manin 1986) used these groups to construct examples of non-abelian [CH-quasigroups](wikipedia://en/31611212) and to describe the structure of commutative [Moufang loops](wikipedia://en/755329) of exponent 3.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Fischer's theorem"}

## Fischer's theorem {#Fischer's_theorem}

Suppose that *G* is a group that is generated by a conjugacy class *D* of 3-transpositions and such that the [2 and 3 cores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-core) *O*<sub>2</sub>(*G*) and *O*<sub>3</sub>(*G*) are both contained in the center *Z*(*G*) of *G*. Then (Fischer 1971) proved that up to isomorphism *G*/*Z*(*G*) is one of the following groups and *D* is the image of the given conjugacy class:

- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059).
- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is a [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) S<sub>n</sub> for *n*≥5, and *D* is the class of transpositions. (If *n*=6 there is a second class of 3-transpositions).
- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is a [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) Sp<sub>2*n*</sub>(2) with *n*≥3 over the field of order 2, and *D* is the class of transvections. (When *n*=2 there is a second class of transpositions.)
- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is a [projective special unitary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_unitary_group) PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) with *n*≥5, and *D* is the class of transvections
- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is an [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) O<sup>μ</sup><sub>2*n*</sub>(2) with μ=±1 and *n*≥4, and *D* is the class of transvections
- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is an index 2 subgroup PO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,+</sup>(3) of the projective orthogonal group PO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) (with μ=±1 and *n*≥5) generated by the class *D* of reflections of norm +1 vectors.
- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is one of the three [Fischer groups](wikipedia://en/327206) Fi<sub>22</sub>, Fi<sub>23</sub>, Fi<sub>24</sub>.
- *G*/*Z*(*G*) is one of two groups of the form Ω<sub>8</sub><sup>\+</sup>(2).S<sub>3</sub> and PΩ<sub>8</sub><sup>\+</sup>(3).S<sub>3</sub>, where Ω stands for the derived subgroup of the orthogonal group and S<sub>3</sub> is the group of diagram automorphisms for the D<sub>4</sub> Dynkin diagram.

The missing cases with *n* small above either do not satisfy the condition about 2 and 3 cores or have exceptional isomorphisms to other groups on the list.

:::

## Important examples {#Important_examples}

The group S<sub>*n*</sub> has order *n*! and for *n*>1 has a subgroup A<sub>*n*</sub> of index 2 that is simple if *n*>4.

The symmetric group S<sub>*n*</sub> is a 3-transposition group for all *n*>1. The 3-transpositions are the elements that exchange two points, and leaving each of the remaining points fixed. These elements are the [transpositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28mathematics%29) (in the usual sense) of S<sub>n</sub>. (For *n*=6 there is a second class of 3-transpositions, namely the class of the elements of S<sub>6</sub> which are products of 3 disjoint transpositions.)

The symplectic group Sp<sub>2*n*</sub>(2) has order

$$2^{n^2}(2^2-1)(2^4-1)\cdots (2^{2n}-1)$$

It is a 3-transposition group for all *n*≥1. It is simple if *n*>2, while for *n*=1 it is S<sub>3</sub>, and for *n*=2 it is S<sub>6</sub> with a simple subgroup of index 2, namely A<sub>6</sub>. The 3-transpositions are of the form *x*↦*x*+(*x*,*v*)*v* for non-zero *v*.

The special unitary group SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) has order

$$2^{n(n-1)/2}(2^2-1)(2^3+1)\cdots (2^n-(-1)^n)$$

The projective special unitary group PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) is the quotient of the special unitary group SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) by the subgroup *M* of all the scalar linear transformations in SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2). The subgroup *M* is the center of SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2). Also, *M* has order gcd(3,*n*).

The group PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) is simple if *n*>3, while for *n*=2 it is S<sub>3</sub> and for *n*=3 it has the structure 3<sup>2</sup>:Q<sub>8</sub> (Q<sub>8</sub> = [quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130)).

Both SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) and PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) are 3-transposition groups for *n*=2 and for all *n*≥4. The 3-transpositions of SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) for *n*=2 or *n*≥4 are of the form *x*↦*x*+(*x*,*v*)*v* for non-zero vectors *v* of zero norm. The 3-transpositions of PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) for *n*=2 or *n*≥4 are the images of the 3-transpositions of SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) under the natural quotient map from SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) to PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2)=SU<sub>*n*</sub>(2)/*M*.

The orthogonal group O<sub>2*n*</sub><sup>±</sup>(2) has order

$$2\times 2^{n(n-1)}(2^2-1)(2^4-1)\cdots (2^{2n-2}-1)(2^{n}\mp1)$$

(Over fields of characteristic 2, orthogonal group in odd dimensions are isomorphic to symplectic groups.) It has an index 2 subgroup (sometimes denoted by Ω<sub>2*n*</sub><sup>±</sup>(2)), which is simple if *n*>2.

The group O<sub>2*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(2) is a 3-transposition group for all *n*>2 and μ=±1. The 3-transpositions are of the form *x*↦*x*+(*x*,*v*)*v* for vectors *v* such that *Q*(v)=1, where *Q* is the underlying quadratic form for the orthogonal group.

The orthogonal groups O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>±</sup>(3) are the automorphism groups of quadratic forms *Q* over the field of 3 elements such that the discriminant of the [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) (*a*,*b*)=*Q*(*a*+*b*)−*Q*(*a*)−*Q*(*b*) is ±1. The group O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3), where μ and σ are signs, is the subgroup of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) generated by reflections with respect to vectors *v* with *Q*(*v*)=+1 if σ is +, and is the subgroup of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) generated by reflections with respect to vectors *v* with *Q*(*v*)=-1 if σ is −.

For μ=±1 and σ=±1, let PO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3)=O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3)/*Z*, where *Z* is the group of all scalar linear transformations in O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3). If *n*>3, then *Z* is the center of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3).

For μ=±1, let Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) be the derived subgroup of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3). Let PΩ<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3)= Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3)/*X*, where *X* is the group of all scalar linear transformations in Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3). If *n*>2, then *X* is the center of Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3).

If *n*=2*m*+1 is odd the two orthogonal groups O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>±</sup>(3) are isomorphic and have order

$$2\times 3^{m^2}(3^2-1)(3^4-1)\cdots(3^{2m}-1)$$

and O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,−</sup>(3) (center order 1 for *n*>3), and O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,−</sup>(3) (center order 2 for *n*>3), because the two quadratic forms are scalar multiples of each other, up to linear equivalence.

If *n*=2*m* is even the two orthogonal groups O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>±</sup>(3) have orders

$$2\times 3^{m(m-1)}(3^2-1)(3^4-1)\cdots(3^{2m-2}-1)(3^m\mp 1)$$

and O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,−</sup>(3), and O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,−</sup>(3), because the two classes of transpositions are exchanged by an element of the general orthogonal group that multiplies the quadratic form by a scalar. If *n*=2*m*, *m*>1 and *m* is even, then the centre of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,−</sup>(3) has order 2, and the centre of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,−</sup>(3) has order 1. If *n*=2*m*, *m*>2 and *m* is odd, then the centre of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>\+,−</sup>(3) has order 1, and the centre of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,+</sup>(3) ≅ O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−,−</sup>(3) has order 2.

If *n*>3, and μ=±1 and σ=±1, the group O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3) is a 3-transposition group. The 3-transpositions of the group O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3) are of the form *x*↦*x*−(*x*,*v*)*v*/Q(*v*)=*x*+(*x*, *v*)/(*v*,*v*) for vectors *v* with *Q*(*v*)=σ, where *Q* is the underlying quadratic form of O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3).

If *n*>4, and μ=±1 and σ=±1, then O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3) has index 2 in the orthogonal group O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3). The group O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,σ</sup>(3) has a subgroup of index 2, namely Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3), which is simple modulo their centers (which have orders 1 or 2). In other words, PΩ<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) is simple.

If *n*>4 is odd, and (μ,σ)=(+,+) or (−,−), then O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,+</sup>(3) and PO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,+</sup>(3) are both isomorphic to SO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3)=Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3):2, where SO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) is the special orthogonal group of the underlying quadratic form *Q*. Also, Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) is isomorphic to PΩ<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3), and is also non-abelian and simple.

If *n*>4 is odd, and (μ,σ)=(+,−) or (−,+), then O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,+</sup>(3) is isomorphic to Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3)×2, and O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,+</sup>(3) is isomorphic to Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3). Also, Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) is isomorphic to PΩ<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3), and is also non-abelian and simple.

If *n*>5 is even, and μ=±1 and σ=±1, then O<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,+</sup>(3) has the form Ω<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3):2, and PO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ,+</sup>(3) has the form PΩ<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3):2. Also, PΩ<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(3) is non-abelian and simple.

*Fi*<sub>22</sub> has order 2<sup>17</sup>.3<sup>9</sup>.5<sup>2</sup>.7.11.13 = 64561751654400 and is simple.

*Fi*<sub>23</sub> has order 2<sup>18</sup>.3<sup>13</sup>.5<sup>2</sup>.7.11.13.17.23 = 4089470473293004800 and is simple.

*Fi*<sub>24</sub> has order 2<sup>22</sup>.3<sup>16</sup>.5<sup>2</sup>.7<sup>3</sup>.11.13.17.23.29 and has a simple subgroup of index 2, namely *Fi*<sub>24</sub>'.

There are numerous degenerate (solvable) cases and isomorphisms between 3-transposition groups of small degree as follows (Aschbacher 1997, p.46):

### Solvable groups {#Solvable_groups}

The following groups do not appear in the conclusion of Fisher's theorem as they are solvable (with order a power of 2 times a power of 3).

$S_1=SU_1(2)=PSU_1(2)=O_1^{\pm,\pm}(3)=PO_1^{\pm,\pm}(3)=PO_1^{\pm,\mp}(3)$ has order 1.

$S_2=O_2^+(2)=O_1^{\pm,\mp}(3)=O_2^{+,\pm}(3)=PO_2^{+,\pm}(3)=PO_2^{-,\pm}(3)$ has order 2, and it is a 3-transposition group.

$O_2^{-,\pm}(3)=PO_3^{\pm,\mp}(3)=2^2$ is elementary abelian of order 4, and it is **not** a 3-transposition group.

$S_3=Sp_2(2)=SU_2(2)=PSU_2(2)=O_2^-(2)$ has order 6, and it is a 3-transposition group.

$O_3^{\pm,\mp}(3)=2^3$ is elementary abelian of order 8, and it is **not** a 3-transposition group.

$S_4=O_3^{\pm,\pm}(3)=PO_3^{\pm,\pm}(3)$ has order 24, and it is a 3-transposition group.

$PSU_3(2)=3^2:Q_8$ has order 72, and it is **not** a 3-transposition group, where Q<sub>8</sub> denotes the quaternion group.

$O_4^+(2)=(S_3\times S_3):2$ has order 72, and it is **not** a 3-transposition group.

$SU_3(2)=3^{1+2}:Q_8$ has order 216, and it is **not** a 3-transposition group, where 3<sup>1+2</sup> denotes the extraspecial group of order 27 and exponent 3, and Q<sub>8</sub> denotes the quaternion group.

$PO_4^{+,\pm}(3)=(A_4\times A_4):2$ has order 288, and it is **not** a 3-transposition group.

$O_4^{+,\pm}(3)=(SL_2(3)*SL_2(3)):2$ has order 576, where \* denotes the non-direct central product, and it is **not** a 3-transposition group.

## Proof {#Proof}

The idea of the proof is as follows. Suppose that *D* is the class of 3-transpositions in *G*, and *d*∈*D*, and let *H* be the subgroup generated by the set *D<sub>d</sub>* of elements of *D* commuting with *d*. Then *D<sub>d</sub>* is a set of 3-transpositions of *H*, so the 3-transposition groups can be classified by induction on the order by finding all possibilities for *G* given any 3-transposition group *H*. For simplicity assume that the derived group of *G* is perfect (this condition is satisfied by all but the two groups involving triality automorphisms.)

- If *O*<sub>3</sub>(*H*) is not contained in *Z*(*H*) then *G* is the symmetric group *S*<sub>5</sub>
- If *O*<sub>2</sub>(*H*) is not contained in *Z*(*H*) then *L*=*H*/*O*<sub>2</sub>(*H*) is a 3-transposition group, and *L*/*Z*(*L*) is either of type Sp(2*n*, 2) in which case *G*/*Z*(*G*) is of type Sp<sub>2*n*+2</sub>(2), or of type PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) in which case *G*/*Z*(*G*) is of type PSU<sub>*n*+2</sub>(2)
- If *H*/*Z*(*H*) is of type *S<sub>n</sub>* then either *G* is of type *S*<sub>*n*+2</sub> or *n* = 6 and *G* is of type O<sub>6</sub><sup>−</sup>(2)
- If *H*/*Z*(*H*) is of type Sp<sub>2*n*</sub>(2) with 2*n* ≥ 6 then *G* is of type O<sub>2*n*+2</sub><sup>μ</sup>(2)
- *H*/*Z*(*H*) cannot be of type O<sub>2*n*</sub><sup>μ</sup>(2) for *n* ≥ 4.
- If *H*/*Z*(*H*) is of type PO<sub>*n*</sub><sup>μ, π</sup>(3) for *n*>4 then *G* is of type PO<sub>*n*+1</sub><sup>−μπ, π</sup>(3).
- If *H*/*Z*(*H*) is of type PSU<sub>*n*</sub>(2) for *n* ≥ 5 then *n* = 6 and *G* is of type Fi<sub>22</sub> (and *H* is an exceptional double cover of PSU<sub>6</sub>(2))
- If *H*/*Z*(*H*) is of type Fi<sub>22</sub> then *G* is of type Fi<sub>23</sub> and *H* is a double cover of Fi<sub>22</sub>.
- If *H*/*Z*(*H*) is of type Fi<sub>23</sub> then *G* is of type Fi<sub>24</sub> and *H* is the product of Fi<sub>23</sub> and a group of order 2.
- *H*/*Z*(*H*) cannot be of type Fi<sub>24</sub>.

:::

## 3-transpositions and graph theory {#3-transpositions_and_graph_theory}

It is fruitful to treat 3-transpositions as vertices of a [graph](wikipedia://en/12401). Join the pairs that do not commute, i. e. have a product of order 3. The graph is connected unless the group has a direct product decomposition. The graphs corresponding to the smallest symmetric groups are familiar graphs. The 3 transpositions of *S*<sub>3</sub> form a triangle. The 6 transpositions of *S*<sub>4</sub> form an octahedron. The 10 transpositions of *S*<sub>5</sub> form the complement of the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926).

The symmetric group *S<sub>n</sub>* can be generated by *n*–1 transpositions: (1 2), (2 3), ..., (*n*−1 *n*) and the graph of this generating set is a straight line. It embodies sufficient relations to define the group *S<sub>n</sub>*.[^o17]
