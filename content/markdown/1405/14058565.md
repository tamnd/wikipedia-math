# Surface gradient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Surface gradient"}

In [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640), the **surface gradient** is a [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) that is similar to the conventional [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461). The distinction is that the surface gradient takes effect along a surface.

:::

For a [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) $S$ in a [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879) $u$, the surface gradient is defined and notated as

$$\nabla_S u = \nabla u - \mathbf{\hat n} (\mathbf{\hat n} \cdot \nabla u)$$

where $\mathbf{\hat n}$ is a unit [normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_normal) to the surface.[^o2] Examining the definition shows that the surface gradient is the (conventional) gradient with the component normal to the surface removed (subtracted), hence this gradient is tangent to the surface. In other words, the surface gradient is the [orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052) of the gradient onto the surface.

The surface gradient arises whenever the gradient of a quantity over a surface is important. In the study of [capillary surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_surface) for example, the gradient of spatially varying [surface tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_tension) does not make much sense; however, the surface gradient does and serves certain purposes.
