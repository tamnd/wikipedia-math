# Samuel Earnshaw

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Earnshaw"}

**Samuel Earnshaw** (1 February 1805, [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire) – 6 December 1888, [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire)[^o3]) was an English clergyman and mathematician and physicist, noted for his contributions to theoretical physics, especially for proving [Earnshaw's theorem](wikipedia://en/485472).

:::

Earnshaw was born in [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield) and entered [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), graduating [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) and [Smith's Prizeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) in 1831.[^o4]

From 1831 to 1847 Earnshaw worked in Cambridge as tripos coach, and in 1846 was appointed to the parish church St. Michael, Cambridge. For a time he acted as curate to the Revd [Charles Simeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Simeon). In 1847 his health broke down and he returned to Sheffield working as a chaplain and teacher. As a clergyman, he published several sermons and treatises.

## Research {#Research}

Earnshaw published several mathematical and physical articles and books. His most famous contribution, [Earnshaw's theorem](wikipedia://en/485472), shows the impossibility of stability of matter under purely electrostatic forces.

Earnshaw was important in the early development of the theory of [shock waves](wikipedia://en/146253). The [Earnshaw paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnshaw_paradox), which shows it is impossible for an ideal to carry waves, is named after him.

Other topics included optics, calculus, trigonometry and partial differential equations in mathematics.
