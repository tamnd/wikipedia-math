# Θ10

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Θ10"}

In [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), a branch of mathematics, **θ<sub>10</sub>** is a particular [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) of the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) Sp<sub>4</sub>, which has some exceptional properties. The symplectic group Sp<sub>4</sub> is the set of 4×4 matrices that preserve a skew-symmetric non-degenerate matrix, but its unitary representations – that is, the ways of realizing that set as a group of [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) preserving the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) in a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) – are often subtle.

:::

The notation θ<sub>10</sub> was first used for a complex irreducible representation of the finite group Sp<sub>4</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>), where **F**<sub>*q*</sub> is a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with *q* elements. In this finite-field case, for *q* odd, θ<sub>10</sub> has dimension *q*(*q* – 1)<sup>2</sup>/2. It is notable because it is simultaneously [cuspidal](wikipedia://en/6043458), [unipotent](wikipedia://en/32307688), and degenerate, meaning that it has no [Whittaker model](wikipedia://en/20679744). Related representations, also denoted θ<sub>10</sub>, occur for Sp<sub>4</sub> over [local](wikipedia://en/145375) and [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358).

## Finite-field case {#Finite-field_case}

(Srinivasan 1968) introduced θ<sub>10</sub> in her determination of the irreducible characters of the finite symplectic group Sp<sub>4</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) for *q* odd. She showed that θ<sub>10</sub> has degree *q*(*q* – 1)<sup>2</sup>/2.

The subscript 10 is historical. Srinivasan labelled certain characters of Sp<sub>4</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) as θ<sub>1</sub>, θ<sub>2</sub>, ..., θ<sub>13</sub>; the tenth character in this list is the cuspidal unipotent character now known as θ<sub>10</sub>.

In the finite-field setting, θ<sub>10</sub> is the only cuspidal unipotent representation of Sp<sub>4</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>). It is also the smallest example of a cuspidal unipotent representation of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) and the smallest example of a degenerate cuspidal representation. [General linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564) do not have cuspidal unipotent representations, and they do not have degenerate cuspidal representations. Thus θ<sub>10</sub> exhibits features of the representation theory of general reductive groups that are absent for general linear groups.

## Construction {#Construction}

One construction of θ<sub>10</sub> uses the [Weil representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_representation) and [Howe duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howe_duality). Let *V* be a four-dimensional [symplectic vector space](wikipedia://en/292852) over **F**<sub>*q*</sub>, and let *E* be a two-dimensional anisotropic [quadratic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_space) over **F**<sub>*q*</sub>. Then *V* ⊗ *E* is an eight-dimensional symplectic vector space.

The Weil representation of Sp(*V* ⊗ *E*) restricts to an action of Sp(*V*) × O(*E*). Since O(*E*) contains the special orthogonal group SO(*E*) as a subgroup of index two, the representation can be decomposed using the characters of SO(*E*) and the involution coming from the nontrivial coset of O(*E*). In this decomposition, one of the resulting irreducible Sp(*V*)-representations, usually denoted *W*<sup>−</sup><sub>1</sub>, has dimension *q*(*q* – 1)<sup>2</sup>/2. Its character is the representation θ<sub>10</sub>.[^n1]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The representation θ<sub>10</sub> is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982), so it has no nonzero proper invariant subspaces. It is [cuspidal](wikipedia://en/6043458), meaning that it does not arise from a proper [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) by [parabolic induction](wikipedia://en/11118768). Equivalently, in the finite-field setting, its restriction to the [unipotent radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipotent_radical) of a proper parabolic subgroup does not contain the trivial representation.

It is also degenerate: it does not admit a [Whittaker model](wikipedia://en/20679744). For representations of [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386), the existence of a Whittaker model is often called genericity. Thus θ<sub>10</sub> is an example of a cuspidal but non-generic representation.

Finally, θ<sub>10</sub> is unipotent in the sense of [Deligne–Lusztig theory](wikipedia://en/10574794): it occurs in the part of the character theory attached to the trivial character of a [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136). The simultaneous occurrence of all three properties – cuspidal, unipotent, and degenerate – is what makes θ<sub>10</sub> exceptional.

:::

## Local and global analogues {#Local_and_global_analogues}

Representations also denoted θ<sub>10</sub> occur for Sp<sub>4</sub> over local and global fields. (Howe Piatetski-Shapiro 1979) used such representations in their construction of counterexamples to the [generalized Ramanujan conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Ramanujan_conjecture) for quasi-split groups. These examples showed that a naive generalization of the Ramanujan conjecture to all cuspidal automorphic representations of reductive groups could not hold without additional hypotheses, such as genericity.

(Adams 2004) described the real local representation θ<sub>10</sub> for the Lie group Sp<sub>4</sub>(**R**). (Kim Piatetski-Shapiro 2001) studied quadratic base change for θ<sub>10</sub>.
