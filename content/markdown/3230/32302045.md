# Pranab K. Sen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pranab K. Sen"}

**Pranab Kumar Sen** (7 November 1937 – 31 December 2023) was an Indian-American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who was a professor of statistics and the Cary C. Boshamer Professor of Biostatistics at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Pranab Kumar Sen was born in [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta), [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) on 7 November 1937,[^o6] as the second of seven siblings. His father, a railway officer, died of leukemia when Sen was ten, and he was raised by his mother, the daughter of a [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician).[^o21] He began his undergraduate studies at [Presidency College, Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_University,_Kolkata), initially intending to study medicine but shifting to statistics when it was discovered that he was too young for medical college.[^o21] He received a B.S. from the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) in 1955, an M.Sc. in 1957, and a Ph.D. in 1962;[^o6][^o4][^o7] his [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) was Hari Kinkar Nandi.[^o8][^o21] He taught for three years at the University of Calcutta and one more year at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) before joining the UNC faculty in 1965; although he has held visiting positions at other universities, he remained at Chapel Hill for the rest of his career.[^o6][^o4] He was the founding co-editor of two journals, *Sequential Analysis* and *Statistics and Decisions*,[^o21] and was joint editor-in-chief of the *Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference* from 1980 to 1983.[^o6]

Sen died in [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina) on 31 December 2023, at the age of 86.[^o9]

## Research and graduate advising {#Research_and_graduate_advising}

Sen was the author or co-author of multiple books on [non-parametric statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics), the advisor of over 80 Ph.D. students, and the author of over 600 research publications.[^o6][^o22] He is known for inventing the [Hodges–Lehmann estimator](wikipedia://en/9622185) independently of and contemporaneously with Hodges and Lehmann[^o21][^o11] and for the [Theil–Sen estimator](wikipedia://en/32292709), a form of [robust regression](wikipedia://en/2713327) that fits a line to two-dimensional sample points by choosing the slope of the fit line to be the median of the slopes of the lines through pairs of samples.[^o12][^o13]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Sen was a fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics)[^o15] and of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) (ASA).[^o16] He became the Cary C. Boshamer Professor in 1982.[^o6] He was the [Lukacs Distinguished Visiting Professor](wikipedia://en/30407970) at [Bowling Green State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green_State_University) in 1996–1997.[^o17] In 2002, he won the [Gottfried E. Noether Senior Scholar Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_E._Noether_Awards) of the ASA,[^o18] and he was the 2010 winner of its [Wilks Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/15966180) "for outstanding contributions to statistical research, especially in nonparametric statistics and biostatistics; and for exceptional service in mentoring doctoral students."[^o19] In 2012, the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) awarded him an [honorary Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_University_of_Calcutta_honorary_degree_recipients) degree.[^o20]

In 2007, a [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) was dedicated to him on the occasion of his 70th birthday.[^o21][^o22]
