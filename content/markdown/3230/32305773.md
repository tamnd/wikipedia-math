# Georgios Sougdouris

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Georgios Sougdouris"}

**Georgios Sougdouris** (Γεώργιος Σουγδουρής; 1645/7–1725) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks) philosopher and theologian.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Sougdouris was born in [Ioannina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioannina) then part of the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire), where he finished ground level studies. He continued his studies at the [University of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Venice) and in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) where he may have attended courses at the [local university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua).[^o3] In 1681, Sougdouris became a priest. Two years later, he became the head of the Goumas School in Ioannina (a.k.a. Gioumeios or Gioumas School), succeeding [Bessarion Makris](wikipedia://en/32614282). There, he taught natural sciences, mathematics and [Aristotelian philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_philosophy).[^o3] Sougdouris taught at the Goumas school until 1710, and was succeeded by [Methodios Anthrakites](wikipedia://en/23159763).[^o4] He wrote a number of philological, theological and philosophical works like *Εισαγωγή Λογική* (Introduction to Logic), published in Vienna, 1792 and *Επιτομή Γραμματικής* (Concise Grammar), both were extensively used as school textbooks.[^o3][^o4] Moreover, he translated into Greek the work of [Francesco Panigarola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Panigarola) *Rhetorica Ecclesiastica*.[^o3]

Sougdouris got involved in various theological discussions with local scholars.[^o5] Due to his progressive views and teaching methods, he got into conflict with conservative circles and was accused of atheism.[^o6] As a result, the local Orthodox bishop asked for Sougdouris' excommunication but failed.[^o7]
