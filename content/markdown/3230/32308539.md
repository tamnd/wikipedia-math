# Regularized canonical correlation analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regularized canonical correlation analysis"}

**Regularized canonical correlation analysis** is a way of using [ridge regression](wikipedia://en/954328) to solve the [singularity](wikipedia://en/581763) problem in the [cross-covariance matrices](wikipedia://en/21163447) of [canonical correlation analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_correlation_analysis). By converting $\operatorname{cov}(X, X)$ and $\operatorname{cov}(Y, Y)$ into $\operatorname{cov}(X, X) + \lambda I_X$ and $\operatorname{cov}(Y, Y) + \lambda I_Y$, it ensures that the above matrices will have reliable [inverses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_inverse).

:::

The idea probably dates back to [Hrishikesh D. Vinod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrishikesh_Vinod)'s publication in 1976 where he called it "Canonical ridge". It has been suggested for use in the analysis of [functional neuroimaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_neuroimaging) data as such data are often singular. It is possible to compute the regularized canonical vectors in the lower-dimensional space.[^o5]
