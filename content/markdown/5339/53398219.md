# Fluent (mathematics)

*Image: The method of fluxions and infinite series p.20.gif.* Newton's introduction of the notions *"fluent"* and *"fluxion"* in his 1736 book

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fluent"}

A **fluent** is a time-varying quantity or [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109).[^o2] The term was used by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) in his early [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) to describe his form of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427).[^o3] The concept was introduced by Newton in 1665 and detailed in his [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) treatise, *[Method of Fluxions](wikipedia://en/252953)*. Newton described any variable that changed its value as a fluent – for example, the velocity of a ball thrown in the air. The derivative of a fluent is known as a [fluxion](wikipedia://en/52956308), the main focus of Newton's calculus. A fluent can be found from its corresponding fluxion through [integration](wikipedia://en/15532).[^o5]

:::

The terms *fluent* and *fluxion* were used in 1878 by [W. K. Clifford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._K._Clifford) in *[Elements of Dynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elements_of_Dynamic)*: "a quantity must be continuous to be fluent, must therefore be specified either by a line or an angle (which may be placed at the centre of a standard circle and measured by its arc) and rate of change of a length measured on a straight line or circle means velocity of one end of it (if the other be still) or difference of velocity of the two ends."(page 63) He then derives the [product rule](wikipedia://en/248988) (page 64) and [quotient rule](wikipedia://en/209128) (page 65).
