# Plesiohedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plesiohedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **plesiohedron** is a special kind of [space-filling polyhedron](wikipedia://en/58607583), defined as the [Voronoi cell](wikipedia://en/177668) of a symmetric [Delone set](wikipedia://en/38607512). Three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) can be completely filled by copies of any one of these shapes, with no overlaps. The resulting [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) will have symmetries that take any copy of the plesiohedron to any other copy.

:::

The plesiohedra include the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668), [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), and [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654). The largest number of faces that a plesiohedron can have is 38.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A set $S$ of points in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is a [Delone set](wikipedia://en/38607512) if there exists a number $\varepsilon>0$ such that every two points of $S$ are at least at distance $\varepsilon$ apart from each other and such that every point of space is within distance $1/\varepsilon$ of at least one point in $S$. So $S$ fills space, but its points never come too close to each other. For this to be true, $S$ must be infinite. Additionally, the set $S$ is symmetric (in the sense needed to define a plesiohedron) if, for every two points $p$ and $q$ of $S$, there exists a [rigid motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_motion) of space that takes $S$ to $S$ and $p$ to $q$. That is, the symmetries of $S$ [act transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on $S$.[^o5]

The [Voronoi diagram](wikipedia://en/177668) of any set $S$ of points partitions space into regions called Voronoi cells that are nearer to one given point of $S$ than to any other. When $S$ is a Delone set, the Voronoi cell of each point $p$ in $S$ is a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron). The faces of this polyhedron lie on the planes that perpendicularly bisect the line segments from $p$ to other nearby points of $S$.[^o4]

When $S$ is symmetric as well as being Delone, the Voronoi cells must all be [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to each other, for the symmetries of $S$ must also be symmetries of the Voronoi diagram. In this case, the Voronoi diagram forms a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) in which there is only a single [prototile](wikipedia://en/39372) shape, the shape of these Voronoi cells. This shape is called a plesiohedron. The tiling generated in this way is [isohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral_tiling), meaning that it not only has a single prototile ("monohedral") but also that any copy of this tile can be taken to any other copy by a symmetry of the tiling.[^o5]

As with any space-filling polyhedron, the [Dehn invariant](wikipedia://en/338051) of a plesiohedron is necessarily zero.[^o6]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The plesiohedra include the five [parallelohedra](wikipedia://en/25207334). These are polyhedra that can tile space in such a way that every tile is symmetric to every other tile by a translational symmetry, without rotation. Equivalently, they are the Voronoi cells of [lattices](wikipedia://en/469246), as these are the translationally-symmetric Delone sets. Plesiohedra are a special case of the [stereohedra](wikipedia://en/53413889), the prototiles of isohedral tilings more generally.[^o5] For this reason (and because Voronoi diagrams are also known as Dirichlet tesselations) they have also been called "Dirichlet stereohedra"[^o16]

There are only finitely many combinatorial types of plesiohedron. Notable individual plesiohedra include:

- The five parallelohedra: the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) (or more generally the [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975)), [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668), [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), [elongated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3515524), and [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654).[^o9]
- The [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584), the prototile of the [triangular prismatic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/8802963).[^o10] More generally, each of the 11 types of [Laves tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laves_tiling) of the plane by congruent convex polygons (and each of the subtypes of these tilings with different symmetry groups) can be realized as the Voronoi cells of a symmetric Delone set in the plane.[^o11] It follows that the prisms over each of these shapes are plesiohedra. As well as the triangular prisms, these include prisms over certain quadrilaterals, pentagons, and hexagons.
- The [gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/1177963) is a stereohedron but not a plesiohedron, because the points at the centers of the cells of its face-to-face tiling (where they are forced to go by symmetry) have differently-shaped Voronoi cells. However, a flattened version of the gyrobifastigium, with faces made of [isosceles right triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosceles_right_triangle) and [silver rectangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_rectangle), is a plesiohedron.
- The [triakis truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/37100405), the prototile of the [triakis truncated tetrahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/37070120) and the plesiohedron generated by the [diamond lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_lattice)[^o5]
- The [trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3521213), the prototile of the [trapezo-rhombic dodecahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezo-rhombic_dodecahedral_honeycomb) and the plesiohedron generated by the [hexagonal close-packing](wikipedia://en/901260)[^o12]
- The 17-sided Voronoi cells of the [Laves graph](wikipedia://en/44557148)[^o13]

Many other plesiohedra are known. Two different ones with the largest known number of faces, 38, were discovered by crystallographer Peter Engel.[^o5][^o14] For many years, the maximum number of faces of a plesiohedron was an [open problem](wikipedia://en/1333335),[^o15][^o16] but analysis of the possible symmetries of three-dimensional space has shown that this number is at most 38.[^o18]

The Voronoi cells of points uniformly spaced on a [helix](wikipedia://en/179924) fill space, are all congruent to each other, and can be made to have arbitrarily large numbers of faces.[^o17] However, the points on a helix are not a Delone set and their Voronoi cells are not bounded polyhedra.

A modern survey is given by Schmitt.[^o18]
