# Shirley McBay

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shirley McBay"}

**Shirley Ann Mathis McBay** (May 4, 1935 – November 27, 2021) was an American mathematician who was the founder and president of the Quality Education for Minorities (QEM) Network, a nonprofit dedicated to improving minority education.[^o3] She was the dean for student affairs at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) from 1980 to 1990.[^o4] She was the first African American to receive a Ph.D. from the [University of Georgia (UGA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) (1966, mathematics).[^o5] McBay was also the first woman of any race to receive a Ph.D. in mathematics from UGA.[^o6] McBay was recognized by [Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006) as a [Black History Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_History_Month) 2017 Honoree.[^o7]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Shirley Ann Mathis was born in [Bainbridge, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bainbridge,_Georgia), on May 4, 1935.[^o15] She received a B.A. in chemistry from [Paine College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paine_College) in 1954, graduating *summa cum laude*. While also teaching chemistry at [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College), McBay earned an M.S. in chemistry (1957) and M.S. in mathematics (1958) from [Atlanta University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_University).[^o9] In 1964, she earned a [United Negro College Fund Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Negro_College_Fund), sponsored by the [IBM Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Corporation), that allowed her to study at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) and earn a Ph.D. in mathematics in 1966.[^o10][^o9] Her Ph.D. was advised by Thomas Roy Brahana with a dissertation on *The Homology Theory of Metabelian Lie Algebras*.

## Career {#Career}

McBay spent 15 years at [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College) as a faculty member and administrator. McBay's leadership at Spelman led to the creation of the division of natural sciences and an increase in an emphasis on the sciences at the institution.[^o9] She served as chairman of the division until 1975 and as associate academic dean at Spelman from 1973 to 1975.[^o9] During this time, she created pre-freshman summer programs to increase interest in science majors, which led to the creation of a chemistry department and the renovations of existing science buildings.[^o9]

She left Spelman in 1975 and took a position at the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) for five years.[^o3][^o9] While at the National Science Foundation, she became program director of the Minority Institutions Science Improvement Program.[^o9] She then worked for ten years at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) as the dean for student affairs. Thirty months of this time included being the director of the [QEM Project](https://qem.org/), a study of minority education problems. The QEM Project was the impetus for the [Quality Education for Minorities](https://qem.org/) (QEM) Network that McBay founded and was president of from 1990 to 2016.[^o4][^o3]

In December 2021, the University of Georgia's Science Library was renamed the Shirley Mathis McBay Science Library in her honor. [^o13]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Her husband, chemistry professor [Henry C. McBay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cecil_McBay), died in 1995. The couple had married in 1954. McBay died from complications of diabetes on November 27, 2021, in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles), at the age of 86.[^o15][^o16][^o17]
