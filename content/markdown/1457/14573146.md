# Peter Montgomery (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Montgomery"}

**Peter Lawrence Montgomery** (September 25, 1947 – February 18, 2020) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked at the [System Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Development_Corporation) and [Microsoft Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research). He is best known for his contributions to [computational number theory](wikipedia://en/511466) and mathematical aspects of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), including the [Montgomery multiplication](wikipedia://en/904378) method for arithmetic in [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_fields), the use of [Montgomery curves](wikipedia://en/25780671) in applications of [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) to [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491) and other problems, and the [Montgomery ladder](wikipedia://en/28244303), which is used to protect against [side-channel attacks](wikipedia://en/667678) in [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Montgomery began his undergraduate career at the [University of California, Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Riverside), in 1965 and transferred to [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1967, earning a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1969 and an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1971, He joined the [System Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Development_Corporation) (SDC) in 1972, where he worked for many years as a programmer implementing algorithms for the [CDC 7600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDC_7600) and [PDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmed_Data_Processor) series of computers, including the implementation of algorithms for multi-precision arithmetic that led to the invention of what is now known as [Montgomery multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_multiplication). He then returned to academia in 1987, earning his PhD in mathematics from [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1992 under the supervision of [David Cantor](wikipedia://en/44771465). He joined the cryptography group at [Microsoft Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research) in 1998, where he worked until his retirement in 2014.

On February 28, 2020, an [829-bit (RSA-250)](wikipedia://en/511379#RSA-250) RSA key was successfully factorised. The team dedicated the computation to Peter Montgomery, who died on the 18th of the same month.[^o4]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Montgomery is particularly known for his contributions to the [elliptic curve method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_method) of factorization, which include a method for speeding up the second stage of [algebraic-group factorization algorithms](wikipedia://en/14573391) using [FFT](wikipedia://en/11512) techniques for fast polynomial evaluation at equally spaced points. This was the subject of his [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation), for which he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1992 from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles).

He also invented the [block Lanczos algorithm](wikipedia://en/14572979) for finding [nullspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullspace) of a matrix over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), which is very widely used for the [quadratic sieve](wikipedia://en/582340) and [number field sieve](wikipedia://en/152734) methods of factorization; he has been involved in the computations which set a number of [integer factorization records](wikipedia://en/10892972).

He was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1967.[^o6] In that year, he was one of only two contestants, along with child prodigy [Don Zagier](wikipedia://en/1408557) of MIT, to solve all twelve of the exam problems.
