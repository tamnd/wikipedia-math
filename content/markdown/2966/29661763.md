# Quasisymmetric function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasisymmetric function"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and in particular in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), a **quasisymmetric function** is any element in the **ring of quasisymmetric functions** which is in turn a subring of the [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) ring with a countable number of variables. This ring generalizes the [ring of symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/364460). This ring can be realized as a specific limit of the [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) of quasisymmetric polynomials in *n* variables, as *n* goes to infinity. This ring serves as a universal structure in which relations between quasisymmetric polynomials can be expressed in a way independent of the number *n* of variables (but its elements are neither polynomials nor functions).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The **ring of quasisymmetric functions**, denoted QSym, can be defined over any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* such as the [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers). Quasisymmetric functions are [power series](wikipedia://en/60012) of bounded degree in variables $x_1,x_2,x_3, \dots$ with coefficients in *R*, which are shift invariant in the sense that the coefficient of the monomial $x_1^{\alpha_1}x_2^{\alpha_2} \cdots x_k^{\alpha_k}$ is equal to the coefficient of the monomial $x_{i_1}^{\alpha_1} x_{i_2}^{\alpha_2}\cdots x_{i_k}^{\alpha_k}$ for any strictly increasing sequence of positive integers $i_1< i_2< \cdots < i_k$ indexing the variables and any positive integer sequence $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_k)$ of exponents.[^o4] Much of the study of quasisymmetric functions is based on that of [symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_functions).

A quasisymmetric function in finitely many variables is a *quasisymmetric [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000)*. Both symmetric and quasisymmetric polynomials may be characterized in terms of [actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_n$ on a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) in $n$ variables $x_1,\dots, x_n$. One such action of $S_n$ permutes variables, changing a polynomial $p(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ by iteratively swapping pairs $(x_i, x_{i+1})$ of variables having consecutive indices. Those polynomials unchanged by all such swaps form the subring of symmetric polynomials. A second action of $S_n$ conditionally permutes variables, changing a polynomial $p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ by swapping pairs $(x_i, x_{i+1})$ of variables *except* in monomials containing both variables.[^o5][^o33] Those polynomials unchanged by all such conditional swaps form the subring of quasisymmetric polynomials. One quasisymmetric polynomial in four variables $x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4$ is the polynomial

$$x_1^2 x_2 x_3 + x_1^2 x_2 x_4 + x_1^2 x_3 x_4 + x_2^2 x_3 x_4. \,$$

The simplest symmetric polynomial containing these monomials is

$$\begin{aligned} x_1^2 x_2 x_3 + x_1^2 x_2 x_4 + x_1^2 x_3 x_4 + x_2^2 x_3 x_4 + x_1 x_2^2 x_3 + x_1 x_2^2 x_4 + x_1 x_3^2 x_4 + x_2 x_3^2 x_4 \\ {} + x_1 x_2 x_3^2 + x_1 x_2 x_4^2 + x_1 x_3 x_4^2 + x_2 x_3 x_4^2. \, \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Important bases {#Important_bases}

QSym is a [graded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) *R*-[algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring), decomposing as

$$\operatorname{QSym} = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} \operatorname{QSym}_n, \,$$

where $\operatorname{QSym}_n$ is the $R$-[span](wikipedia://en/56353) of all quasisymmetric functions that are [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/1698977) of degree $n$. Two natural [bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_basis) for $\operatorname{QSym}_n$ are the **monomial basis** $\{M_{\alpha} \}$ and the **fundamental basis** $\{F_{\alpha} \}$ indexed by [compositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_%28number_theory%29) $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \ldots , \alpha_k)$ of $n$, denoted $\alpha \vDash n$. The monomial basis consists of $M_0=1$ and all formal power series

$$M_{\alpha} = \sum_{i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_k} x_{i_1}^{\alpha_1} x_{i_2}^{\alpha_2} \cdots x_{i_k}^{\alpha_k}. \,$$

(Note that the elements of the monomial basis may not be monomials.)

The fundamental basis consists $F_0=1$ and all formal power series

$$F_\alpha = \sum_{\alpha \succeq \beta} M_\beta, \,$$

where $\alpha \succeq \beta$ means we can obtain $\alpha$ by adding together adjacent parts of $\beta$, for example, (3,2,4,2) $\succeq$ (3,1,1,1,2,1,2). Thus, when the ring $R$ is the ring of [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers), one has

$$\operatorname{QSym}_n = \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}} \{ M_\alpha \mid \alpha \vDash n \} = \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}} \{ F_\alpha \mid \alpha \vDash n \}. \,$$

Then one can define the algebra of [symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_functions) $\Lambda = \Lambda _0 \oplus \Lambda _1 \oplus \cdots$ as the subalgebra of QSym spanned by the [monomial symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monomial_symmetric_polynomial) $m_0=1$ and all formal power series $m_\lambda = \sum M_\alpha,$ where the sum is over all compositions $\alpha$ which rearrange to the [integer partition](wikipedia://en/104790) $\lambda$. Moreover, we have $\Lambda_n = \Lambda \cap \operatorname{QSym}_n$. For example, $F_{(1,2)}=M_{(1,2)}+M_{(1,1,1)}$ and $m_{(2,1)}=M_{(2,1)}+M_{(1,2)}.$

Other important bases for quasisymmetric functions include the basis of quasisymmetric Schur functions,[^o7] the "type I" and "type II" quasisymmetric power sums,[^o8] and bases related to enumeration in matroids.[^o9][^o10]

## Applications {#Applications}

Quasisymmetric functions have been applied in [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533), symmetric function theory, [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Applications of quasisymmetric functions include enumeration of P-partitions,[^o13][^o14] permutations,[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] tableaux,[^o19] chains of posets,[^o19][^o20] reduced decompositions in finite Coxeter groups (via [Stanley symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/31980740)),[^o19] and parking functions.[^o21] In symmetric function theory and representation theory, applications include the study of [Schubert polynomials](wikipedia://en/31925388),[^o22][^o23] [Macdonald polynomials](wikipedia://en/10354285),[^o24] Hecke algebras,[^o25] and Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials.[^o26] Often quasisymmetric functions provide a powerful bridge between combinatorial structures and symmetric functions.

:::

## Related algebras {#Related_algebras}

As a graded [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714), the dual of the ring of quasisymmetric functions is the ring of noncommutative symmetric functions. Every symmetric function is also a quasisymmetric function, and hence the ring of symmetric functions is a subalgebra of the ring of quasisymmetric functions.

The ring of quasisymmetric functions is the terminal object in category of graded Hopf algebras with a single character.[^o28] Hence any such Hopf algebra has a morphism to the ring of quasisymmetric functions.

One example of this is the [peak algebra](wikipedia://en/42452013).[^o29]

### Other related algebras {#Other_related_algebras}

The [Malvenuto–Reutenauer algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvenuto–Reutenauer_algebra)[^o31] is a Hopf algebra based on permutations that relates the rings of symmetric functions, quasisymmetric functions, and [noncommutative symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/42432800), (denoted Sym, QSym, and NSym respectively), as depicted the following [commutative diagram](wikipedia://en/138484). The duality between QSym and NSym mentioned above is reflected in the main diagonal of this diagram.

*Image: QSymDiagram.png.* (Relationship between QSym and nearby neighbors)

Many related Hopf algebras were constructed from Hopf monoids in the category of species by Aguiar and Majahan.[^o32]

One can also construct the ring of quasisymmetric functions in noncommuting variables.[^o33][^o34]
