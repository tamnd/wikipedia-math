# L-balance theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="L-balance theorem"}

In mathematical finite group theory, the **L-balance theorem** was proved by (Gorenstein Walter 1975). The letter *L* stands for the [layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layer_%28group_theory%29) of a group, and "balance" refers to the property discussed below.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The L-balance theorem of Gorenstein and Walter states that if *X* is a finite group and *T* a 2-subgroup of *X* then

$$L_{2'}(C_X(T)) \le L_{2'}(X)$$

Here *L*<sub>2′</sub>(*X*) stands for the 2-layer of a group *X*, which is the product of all the 2-components of the group, the minimal subnormal subgroups of *X* mapping onto components of *X*/*O*(*X*).

A consequence is that if *a* and *b* are commuting involutions of a group *G* then

$$L_{2'}(L_{2'}(C_a)\cap C_b) = L_{2'}(L_{2'}(C_b)\cap C_a)$$

This is the property called *L*-balance.

More generally similar results are true if the prime 2 is replaced by a prime *p*, and in this case the condition is called *L*<sub>*p*</sub>-balance, but the proof of this requires the classification of finite simple groups (more precisely the [Schreier conjecture](wikipedia://en/7030987)).

:::
