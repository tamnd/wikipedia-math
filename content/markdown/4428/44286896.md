# Growth curve (statistics)

*Image: PSM V85 D564 Table of height and weight for boys.png.* Table of height and weight for boys over time. The growth curve model (also known as GMANOVA) is used to analyze data such as this, where multiple observations are made on collections of individuals over time.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Growth curve"}

The **growth curve model** in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) is a specific multivariate linear model, also known as GMANOVA (Generalized Multivariate Analysis-Of-Variance).[^o2] It generalizes [MANOVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MANOVA) by allowing post-matrices, as seen in the definition.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

**Growth curve model**:[^o5] Let **X** be a *p*×*n* [random matrix](wikipedia://en/1648765) corresponding to the observations, **A** a *p*×*q* within [design matrix](wikipedia://en/949189) with *q* ≤ *p*, **B** a *q*×*k* parameter matrix, **C** a *k*×*n* between individual design matrix with rank(*C*) + *p* ≤ *n* and let **Σ** be a [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix) *p*×*p* matrix. Then

$$X=ABC+\Sigma^{1/2}E$$

defines the growth curve model, where **A** and **C** are known, **B** and **Σ** are unknown, and **E** is a random matrix distributed as *N*<sub>*p*,*n*</sub>(0,*I*<sub>*p*</sub>,<sub>*n*</sub>).

This differs from standard [MANOVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MANOVA) by the addition of **C**, a "postmatrix".[^o10]

:::

## History {#History}

Many writers have considered the growth curve analysis, among them Wishart (1938),[^o7] Box (1950) [^o8] and Rao (1958).[^o9] Potthoff and Roy in 1964;[^o10] were the first in analyzing [longitudinal data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitudinal_data) applying GMANOVA models.

::: {#o12 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

GMANOVA is frequently used for the analysis of surveys, clinical trials, and agricultural data,[^o13] as well as more recently in the context of Radar adaptive detection.[^o14][^o15]

:::

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

In [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), [growth curves such as those used in biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_curve_%28biology%29) are often modeled as being [continuous](wikipedia://en/12442391) [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), e.g. as being [sample paths](wikipedia://en/6673551) that [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) solve [stochastic differential equations](wikipedia://en/1361454).[^o17] Growth curves have been also applied in forecasting market development.[^o18] When variables are measured with error, a [Latent growth modeling](wikipedia://en/6244696) SEM can be used.
