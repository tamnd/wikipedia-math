# Euler's theorem (differential geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euler's theorem"}

*Image: Euler's\_Theorem.png.*

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **Euler's theorem** is a result on the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) of [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) on a surface. The theorem establishes the existence of [principal curvatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_curvatures) and associated *principal directions* which give the directions in which the surface curves the most and the least. The theorem is named for [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) who proved the theorem in (Euler 1760).

:::

More precisely, let *M* be a surface in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), and *p* a point on *M*. A *[normal plane](wikipedia://en/49965903)* through *p* is a plane passing through the point *p* containing the *[normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector)* to *M*. Through each ([unit](wikipedia://en/167053)) [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) to *M* at *p*, there passes a normal plane *P*<sub>*X*</sub> which cuts out a curve in *M*. That curve has a certain [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) κ<sub>*X*</sub> when regarded as a curve inside *P*<sub>*X*</sub>. Provided not all κ<sub>*X*</sub> are equal, there is some unit vector *X*<sub>1</sub> for which *k*<sub>1</sub> = κ<sub>*X*<sub>1</sub></sub> is as large as possible, and another unit vector *X*<sub>2</sub> for which *k*<sub>2</sub> = κ<sub>*X*<sub>2</sub></sub> is as small as possible. Euler's theorem asserts that *X*<sub>1</sub> and *X*<sub>2</sub> are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) and that, moreover, if *X* is any vector making an angle θ with *X*<sub>1</sub>, then

::: {#o3 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\kappa_X = k_1\cos^2\theta + k_2\sin^2\theta.\, \tag{1}$$

:::

The quantities *k*<sub>1</sub> and *k*<sub>2</sub> are called the *[principal curvatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_curvatures)*, and *X*<sub>1</sub> and *X*<sub>2</sub> are the corresponding *[principal directions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_directions_%28geometry%29)*. Equation ((1)) is sometimes called **Euler's equation** (Eisenhart 2004, 124).
