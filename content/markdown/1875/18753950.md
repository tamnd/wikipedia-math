# Schild's ladder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schild's ladder"}

*Image: Schild's ladder step 4.svg.* Two rungs of Schild's ladder. The segments *A*<sub>1</sub>*X*<sub>1</sub> and *A*<sub>2</sub>*X*<sub>2</sub> are an approximation to first order of the [parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595) of *A*<sub>0</sub>*X*<sub>0</sub> along the curve.

In the theory of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) more generally, **Schild's ladder** is a [first-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_approximation) method for *approximating* [parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595) of a vector along a curve using only [affinely parametrized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_of_connection#Geodesics_and_the_absorption_of_torsion) [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096). The method is named for [Alfred Schild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Schild), who introduced the method during lectures at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The idea is to identify a tangent vector *x* at a point $A_0$ with a geodesic segment of unit length $A_0X_0$, and to construct an approximate parallelogram with approximately parallel sides $A_0X_0$ and $A_1X_1$ as an approximation of the [Levi-Civita parallelogramoid](wikipedia://en/18771595); the new segment $A_1X_1$ thus corresponds to an approximately parallel translated tangent vector at $A_1.$

Formally, consider a curve γ through a point *A*<sub>0</sub> in a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) *M*, and let *x* be a [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) at *A*<sub>0</sub>. Then *x* can be identified with a geodesic segment *A*<sub>0</sub>*X*<sub>0</sub> via the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/276548). This geodesic σ satisfies

$$\sigma(0)=A_0\,$$

$$\sigma'(0) = x.\,$$

The steps of the Schild's ladder construction are:

- Let *X*<sub>0</sub> = σ(1), so the geodesic segment $A_0X_0$ has unit length.
- Now let *A*<sub>1</sub> be a point on γ close to *A*<sub>0</sub>, and construct the geodesic *X*<sub>0</sub>*A*<sub>1</sub>.
- Let *P*<sub>1</sub> be the midpoint of *X*<sub>0</sub>*A*<sub>1</sub> in the sense that the segments *X*<sub>0</sub>*P*<sub>1</sub> and *P*<sub>1</sub>*A*<sub>1</sub> take an equal affine parameter to traverse.
- Construct the geodesic *A*<sub>0</sub>*P*<sub>1</sub>, and extend it to a point *X*<sub>1</sub> so that the parameter length of *A*<sub>0</sub>*X*<sub>1</sub> is double that of *A*<sub>0</sub>*P*<sub>1</sub>.
- Finally construct the geodesic *A*<sub>1</sub>*X*<sub>1</sub>. The tangent to this geodesic *x*<sub>1</sub> is then the parallel transport of *X*<sub>0</sub> to *A*<sub>1</sub>, at least to first order.

:::

## Approximation {#Approximation}

This is a discrete approximation of the continuous process of parallel transport. If the ambient space is flat, this is exactly parallel transport, and the steps define [parallelograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallelograms), which agree with the [Levi-Civita parallelogramoid](wikipedia://en/18771595).

In a curved space, the error is given by [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) around the triangle $A_1A_0X_0,$ which is equal to the integral of the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) over the interior of the triangle, by the [Ambrose-Singer theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose-Singer_theorem); this is a form of [Green's theorem](wikipedia://en/243334) (integral around a curve related to integral over interior), and in the case of Levi-Civita connections on surfaces, of [Gauss–Bonnet theorem](wikipedia://en/139229#For_triangles).

## Notes {#Notes}

1. Schild's ladder requires not only geodesics but also relative distance along geodesics. Relative distance may be provided by affine parametrization of geodesics, from which the required midpoints may be determined.
1. The parallel transport which is constructed by Schild's ladder is necessarily [torsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_of_connection)-free.
1. A Riemannian metric is not required to generate the geodesics. But if the geodesics are generated from a Riemannian metric, the parallel transport which is constructed in the limit by Schild's ladder is the same as the [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176) because this connection is defined to be torsion-free.
