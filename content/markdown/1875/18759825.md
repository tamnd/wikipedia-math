# Maharam's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maharam's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Maharam's theorem** is a deep result about the decomposability of [measure spaces](wikipedia://en/45270), which plays an important role in the theory of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). In brief, it states that every [complete measure space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_measure_space) is decomposable into "non-atomic parts" (copies of products of the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) \[0,1\] on the [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438)), and "purely atomic parts," using the [counting measure](wikipedia://en/191786) on some discrete space.[^o2] The theorem is due to [Dorothy Maharam](wikipedia://en/13180716). It was extended to localizable measure spaces by [Irving Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Segal).[^o3]

:::

The result is important to classical Banach space theory, in that, when considering the Banach space given as an [L<sub>p</sub> space](wikipedia://en/45194) of [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055) over a general measurable space, it is sufficient to understand it in terms of its decomposition into non-atomic and atomic parts.

Maharam's theorem can also be translated into the language of [abelian von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/1496379). Every abelian von Neumann algebra is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a product of σ-finite abelian von Neumann algebras, and every σ-finite abelian von Neumann algebra is isomorphic to a spatial [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of discrete abelian von Neumann algebras; that is, algebras of [bounded functions](wikipedia://en/311509) on a [discrete set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_set).

A similar theorem was given by [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) for [Polish spaces](wikipedia://en/396622), stating that they are isomorphic, as [Borel spaces](wikipedia://en/43333), to either the reals, the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), or a finite set.
