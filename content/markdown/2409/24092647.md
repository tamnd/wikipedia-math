In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **[compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound) of four [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra)** can be constructed by four [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) in a number of different symmetry positions.

:::

## Uniform compounds {#Uniform_compounds}

A [uniform compound](wikipedia://en/8606809) of four tetrahedra can be constructed by rotating tetrahedra along an axis of symmetry *C<sub>2</sub>* (that is the middle of an edge) in multiples of $\pi/4$. It has dihedral symmetry, *D*<sub>8h</sub>, and the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) as the convex [octagonal prism](wikipedia://en/3026569).

This compound can also be seen as two compounds of [stella octangulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula) fit evenly on the same *C<sub>2</sub>* plane of symmetry, with one pair of tetrahedra shifted $\pi/4$. It is a special case of a *p/q*-gonal [prismatic compound&nbsp;of&nbsp;antiprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prismatic_compound&nbsp;of&nbsp;antiprisms), where in this case the component *p/q* = 2 is a digonal antiprism, or tetrahedron.

Four tetrahedra that are not spread equally in $\pi/4$ angles over *C*<sub>2</sub> can still hold uniform symmetry when allowed [rotational freedom](wikipedia://en/14890505). In this case, these tetrahedra share a symmetric arrangement over the common axis of symmetry *C*<sub>2</sub> that is rotated by equal and opposite angles. This compound is indexed as [UC<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/14890505), with parameters *p/q* = 2 and *n* = 4 as well.

## Other compounds {#Other_compounds}

A *nonuniform* compound can be generated by rotating tetrahedra about lines extending from the center of each face and through the centroid (as altitudes), with varying degrees of rotation.

A model for this compound polyhedron was first published by Robert Webb, using his program [Stella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_%28software%29), in 2004, following studies of polyhedron models:

*Image: Paper compound of four tetrahedra.jpg.* Compound of four tetrahedra rotated by $\pi/3$. This particular model was built by Robert Webb using *Stella*, a computer software for generating polyhedra.

With edge-length as a unit, it has a [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) equal to

$$S=\frac{1291}{210\cdot\sqrt3} \approx 3.5493.$$

This compound is self-dual, meaning its [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815#Self-dual_polyhedra) is the same compound polyhedron.
