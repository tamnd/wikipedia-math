# Double-star snark

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Double-star snark"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **double-star snark** is a [snark](wikipedia://en/727811) with 30 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 45 edges.

:::

In 1975, [Rufus Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Isaacs_%28game_theorist%29) introduced two infinite families of snarks—the [flower snark](wikipedia://en/23759337) and the [BDS snark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BDS_snark), a family that includes the two [Blanuša snarks](wikipedia://en/24054041), the [Descartes snark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes_snark) and the [Szekeres snark](wikipedia://en/4776914) (BDS stands for Blanuša Descartes Szekeres).[^o4] Isaacs also discovered one 30-vertex snark that does not belong to the BDS family and that is not a flower snark — the double-star snark.

As a snark, the double-star graph is a connected, bridgeless [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) with [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) equal to 4. The double-star snark is [non-planar](wikipedia://en/24314) but is [1-planar](wikipedia://en/38249632).[^o5] It is [non-hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) but is [hypohamiltonian](wikipedia://en/18301304). It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o7]
