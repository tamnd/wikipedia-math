# Fuzzy sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fuzzy sphere"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **fuzzy sphere** is one of the simplest and most canonical examples of [non-commutative geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_geometry). Ordinarily, the functions defined on a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) form a commuting algebra. A fuzzy sphere differs from an ordinary sphere because the algebra of functions on it is not commutative. It is generated by [spherical harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonic) whose spin *l* is at most equal to some *j*. The terms in the product of two spherical harmonics that involve spherical harmonics with spin exceeding *j* are simply omitted in the product. This truncation replaces an infinite-dimensional [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) by a $j^2$-dimensional non-commutative algebra.

:::

The simplest way to see this sphere is to realize this truncated algebra of functions as a matrix algebra on some finite-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). Take the three *j*-dimensional [square matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_matrices) $J_a,~ a=1,2,3$ that form a basis for the *j* dimensional irreducible representation of the Lie algebra *[su(2)](wikipedia://en/173997)*. They satisfy the relations $[J_a,J_b]=i\epsilon_{abc}J_c$, where $\epsilon_{abc}$ is the [totally antisymmetric symbol](wikipedia://en/195982) with $\epsilon_{123}=1$, and generate via the matrix product the algebra $M_j$ of *j* dimensional matrices. The value of the *su(2)* [Casimir operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_operator) in this representation is

$$J_1^2+J_2^2+J_3^2=\frac{1}{4}(j^2-1)I$$

where $I$ is the *j*-dimensional [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718). Thus, if we define the 'coordinates' $x_a=kr^{-1}J_a$ where *r* is the radius of the sphere and *k* is a parameter, related to *r* and *j* by $4r^4=k^2(j^2-1)$, then the above equation concerning the Casimir operator can be rewritten as

$$x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2=r^2,$$

which is the usual relation for the coordinates on a sphere of radius *r* embedded in three dimensional space.

One can define an integral on this space, by

$$\int_{S^2}fd\Omega:=2\pi k \, \text{Tr}(F)$$

where *F* is the matrix corresponding to the function *f*. For example, the integral of unity, which gives the surface of the sphere in the commutative case is here equal to

$$2\pi k \, \text{Tr}(I)=2\pi k j =4\pi r^2\frac{j}{\sqrt{j^2-1}}$$

which converges to the value of the surface of the sphere if one takes *j* to infinity.
