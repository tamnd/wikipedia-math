# Whittaker function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whittaker function"}

*Image: Plot of the Whittaker function M k,m(z) with k=2 and m=½ in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D.svg.* Plot of the Whittaker function M k,m(z) with k=2 and m=$\tfrac{1}{2}$ in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D

In mathematics, a **Whittaker function** is a special solution of **Whittaker's equation**, a modified form of the [confluent hypergeometric equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluent_hypergeometric_equation) introduced by (txt) to make the formulas involving the solutions more symmetric. More generally, (txt) introduced Whittaker [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) of [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386) over [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375), where the functions studied by Whittaker are essentially the case where the local field is the real numbers and the group is SL<sub>2</sub>(**R**).

:::

Whittaker's equation is

$$\frac{d^2w}{dz^2}+\left(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\kappa}{z}+\frac{1/4-\mu^2}{z^2}\right)w=0.$$

It has a [regular singular point](wikipedia://en/3166061) at 0 and an irregular singular point at ∞. Two solutions are given by the **Whittaker functions** *M*<sub>κ,μ</sub>(*z*), *W*<sub>κ,μ</sub>(*z*), defined in terms of Kummer's [confluent hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluent_hypergeometric_functions) *M* and *U* by

$$M_{\kappa,\mu}\left(z\right) = \exp\left(-z/2\right)z^{\mu+\tfrac{1}{2}}M\left(\mu-\kappa+\tfrac{1}{2}, 1+2\mu, z\right)$$

$$W_{\kappa,\mu}\left(z\right) = \exp\left(-z/2\right)z^{\mu+\tfrac{1}{2}}U\left(\mu-\kappa+\tfrac{1}{2}, 1+2\mu, z\right).$$

The Whittaker function $W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)$ is the same as those with opposite values of $\mu$, in other words considered as a function of $\mu$ at fixed $\kappa$ and $z$ it is [even functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_function). When $\kappa$ and $z$ are real, the functions give real values for real and imaginary values of $\mu$. These functions of $\mu$ play a role in so-called [Kummer spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kummer_space).[^o4]

Whittaker functions appear as coefficients of certain representations of the group SL<sub>2</sub>(**R**), called [Whittaker models](wikipedia://en/20679744).
