# Jan Śleszyński

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Jan Śleszyński"}

**Ivan Vladislavovich Sleshinsky[^o4] or Jan Śleszyński**[^o4][^o5][^o6] (Иван Владиславович Слешинский) (Polish: ś; 23 July 1854 – 9 March 1931) was a Polish-Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is regarded a pioneer of the study of [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) in Poland.[^o7]

:::

He worked as professor at the [Odesa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_University) and the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University), and was the co-founder of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791). His main areas of interest included [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199), [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) and [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). He is best known for introducing the [Śleszyński-Pringsheim theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śleszyński-Pringsheim_theorem).

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Śleszyński was born on 23 July 1853 in [Lysianka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysianka), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (present-day Ukraine) to Polish parents.[^o9] In 1864, Śleszyński attended [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in [Chișinău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chișinău). After four years, he transferred to the A. Richelieu Gymnasium in [Odesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa) finishing it with distinction.[^o10]

He matriculated at the [Odesa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_University) to study mathematics, graduating in 1875. In 1880, after having received a scholarship, Śleszyński continued his studies attending lectures by [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) and [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin)[^o10] where he earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1882. Upon his return to Odesa, he was awarded the title of professor at his *alma mater*, occupying the post from 1883 until 1909.[^o9] He taught [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), and the theory of [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) at the university. At the same time, he worked as a mathematics teacher at a local theological seminary, secondary schools in Odesa, and at the Higher Women’s Courses.[^o10] In the 1890s, Śleszyński became a leading figure among the mathematicians in Odesa influencing such scholars as [Samuil Shatunovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuil_Shatunovsky) and [Benjamin Kagan](wikipedia://en/9679792).[^o11]

In 1909, the publication of Śleszyński's translation of [Louis Couturat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Couturat)'s book *The Algebra of Logic* was released. His version went well beyond a simple translation as it was enriched with his own valuable commentary exerting a substantial impact on the development of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) in Russia and serving for many years as the principal textbook for students of the field.[^o9]

In 1911, he moved to Poland, where he became "extraordinary professor" at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków). Among his notable students was [Stanisław Zaremba](wikipedia://en/1028345). In 1919, Śleszyński was one of the founding members of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).[^o12] In the same year, he was appointed full Professor of Logic and Mathematics lecturing at the university until his retirement in 1924.[^o9] In 1925, he received the title of an honorary professor of the Jagiellonian University.[^o10] He was a member of the Kraków Philosophical Society ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Towarzystwo Filozoficzne w Krakowie*).[^o7]

He died on 9 March 1931 in Kraków[^o9] and was buried at the [Rakowicki Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakowicki_Cemetery).[^o13]

### Contributions {#Contributions}

In his 1892 paper, based on his doctoral thesis, Śleszyński revisited [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842)’s formulation of the [Central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) applying characteristic functions, and introducing several important corrections and refinements.[^o9]

Śleszyński's main work was on [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199), [least squares](wikipedia://en/82359) and axiomatic [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) based on [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). He and [Alfred Pringsheim](wikipedia://en/1068126),[^o15] working separately, proved what is now called the [Śleszyński–Pringsheim theorem](wikipedia://en/33062050).[^o16]

Śleszyński upheld the autonomy of logic with respect to mathematics, arguing that logic cannot be reduced to a mere methodological instrument of mathematical practice.[^o7]

His most important publications include: *Teoria dowodu* (The theory of proof) in two volumes (1925, 1929), and *Teoria wyznaczników* (The theory of determinants) (1926).[^o17]
