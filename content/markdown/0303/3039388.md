# List of topology topics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of topology topics"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **topology** (from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) words τόπος, and λόγος) is concerned with the properties of a [geometric object](wikipedia://en/19453961) that are preserved under [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [deformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory), such as [stretching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_factor), [twisting](wikipedia://en/40392210), crumpling and bending, but not tearing or [gluing](wikipedia://en/237213).

:::

A [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) endowed with a structure, called a *topology*, which allows defining continuous deformation of subspaces, and, more generally, all kinds of [continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28mathematics%29). [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697), and, more generally, [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) are examples of a topological space, as any distance or metric defines a topology. The deformations that are considered in topology are [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) and [homotopies](wikipedia://en/212250). A property that is invariant under such deformations is a [topological property](wikipedia://en/1337282). Basic examples of topological properties are: the [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_dimension), which allows distinguishing between a [line](wikipedia://en/946975) and a [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970); [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28topology%29), which allows distinguishing between a line and a circle; [connectedness](wikipedia://en/87141), which allows distinguishing a circle from two non-intersecting circles.

The ideas underlying topology go back to [Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz), who in the 17th century envisioned the geometria situs and analysis situs. [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902)'s [Seven Bridges of Königsberg](wikipedia://en/192753) problem and [polyhedron formula](wikipedia://en/148420#Polyhedra) are arguably the field's first theorems. The term *topology* was introduced by [Johann Benedict Listing](wikipedia://en/1525914) in the 19th century, although it was not until the first decades of the 20th century that the idea of a topological space was developed.
