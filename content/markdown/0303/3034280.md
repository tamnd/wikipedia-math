# Ciprian Manolescu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ciprian Manolescu"}

**Ciprian Manolescu** (; born December 24, 1978) is a [Romanian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian-American)[^o4] mathematician, working in [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155), [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), and [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). He is currently a professor of mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Manolescu completed his first eight classes at [School no. 11 Mihai Eminescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitești) and his secondary education at [Ion Brătianu High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Brătianu_National_College_%28Pitești%29) in [Pitești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitești).[^o6] He completed his undergraduate studies and PhD at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under the direction of [Peter B. Kronheimer](wikipedia://en/3845647). He was the winner of the [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157), awarded jointly by AMS-MAA-SIAM, in 2002. His undergraduate thesis was on *Finite dimensional approximation in [Seiberg–Witten theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_gauge_theory)*, and his [PhD thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD_thesis) topic was *A spectrum valued TQFT from the Seiberg–Witten equations*.

In early 2013, he released a paper detailing a disproof of the [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) conjecture for [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) of dimension 5 and higher.[^o7] For this paper, he received the [E. H. Moore Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._H._Moore_Prize) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He was among the recipients of the [Clay Research Fellowship](wikipedia://en/7655) (2004–2008).

In 2012, he was awarded one of the ten prizes of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) for his work on [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774), and particularly for his role in the development of combinatorial [Heegaard Floer homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heegaard_Floer_homology).[^o10]

He was elected as a member of the 2017 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the American Mathematical Society "for contributions to Floer homology and the topology of manifolds".[^o11]

In 2018, he was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro).

In 2020, he received a [Simons Investigator Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Investigator_Award).[^o12] The citation reads: "Ciprian Manolescu works in low-dimensional topology and gauge theory. His research is centered on constructing new versions of Floer homology and applying them to questions in topology. With collaborators, he showed that many Floer-theoretic invariants are algorithmically computable. He also developed a new variant of Seiberg-Witten Floer homology, which he used to prove the existence of non-triangulable manifolds in high dimensions."

## Competitions {#Competitions}

He has one of the best records ever in mathematical competitions:

- He holds the [sole distinction of writing three perfect papers](wikipedia://en/15712699#High-scoring_participants) at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208): Toronto, Canada (1995); Bombay, India (1996); Mar del Plata, Argentina (1997).[^o14]
- Manolescu is a three-time Putnam Fellow, having placed in the top five in the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) in 1997, 1998, and 2000.[^o15]
