# H tree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="H tree"}

*Image: h\_tree\_1600x1100f.gif.* The first ten levels of an H tree

In [fractal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_geometry), the **H tree** is a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) tree structure constructed from [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860), each smaller by a factor of the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682) from the next larger adjacent segment. It is so called because its repeating pattern resembles the letter "H". It has [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) 2, and comes arbitrarily close to every point in a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048). Its applications include [VLSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VLSI) design and microwave engineering.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

An H tree can be constructed by starting with a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) of arbitrary length, drawing two shorter segments at right angles to the first through its endpoints, and continuing in the same vein, reducing (dividing) the length of the line segments drawn at each stage by $\sqrt2$.[^n1] A variant of this construction could also be defined in which the length at each iteration is multiplied by a ratio less than $1/\sqrt2$, but for this variant the resulting shape covers only part of its bounding rectangle, with a fractal boundary.[^n2]

An alternative process that generates the same fractal set is to begin with a rectangle with sides in the ratio $1:\sqrt2$, and repeatedly bisect it into two smaller [silver](wikipedia://en/920526) rectangles, at each stage connecting the two [centroids](wikipedia://en/187926) of the two smaller rectangles by a line segment. A similar process can be performed with rectangles of any other shape, but the $1:\sqrt2$ rectangle leads to the line segment size decreasing uniformly by a $\sqrt2$ factor at each step while for other rectangles the length will decrease by different factors at odd and even levels of the recursive construction.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The H tree is a [self-similar](wikipedia://en/28782) [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913); its [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) is equal to 2.[^n2]

The points of the H tree come arbitrarily close to every point in a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) (the same as the starting rectangle in the constructing by centroids of subdivided rectangles).[^n3] It follows that its [topological closure](wikipedia://en/42309) is the entire rectangle. However, it does not include all points of the rectangle; for instance, the points on the perpendicular bisector of the initial line segment (other than the midpoint of this segment) are not included.

:::

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In [VLSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_Large_Scale_Integration) design, the H tree may be used as the layout for a [complete binary tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_binary_tree) using a total area that is proportional to the number of nodes of the tree.[^n4] Additionally, the H tree forms a space efficient layout for trees in [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813),[^n5] and as part of a construction of a point set for which the sum of squared edge lengths of the [traveling salesman tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_salesman_problem) is large.[^n6] It is commonly used as a [clock distribution network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_distribution_network) for routing [timing signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_signal) to all parts of a chip with equal propagation delays to each part,[^o15] and has also been used as an interconnection network for VLSI multiprocessors.[^o16]

*Image: 3D H-fractal.png.* 3-dimensional H tree

The planar H tree can be generalized to the three-dimensional structure via adding line segments on the direction perpendicular to the H tree plane.[^o17] The resultant three-dimensional H tree has [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) equal to 3. The planar H tree and its three-dimensional version have been found to constitute artificial electromagnetic atoms in [photonic crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photonic_crystals) and [metamaterials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamaterials) and might have potential applications in microwave engineering.[^o17]

:::

## Related sets {#Related_sets}

*Image: Fractal canopy1.gif.* 14 steps of the fractal canopy tree, animated.

The H tree is an example of a [fractal canopy](wikipedia://en/36903485), in which the angle between neighboring line segments is always 90 degrees. In its property of coming arbitrarily close to every point of its bounding rectangle, it also resembles a [space-filling curve](wikipedia://en/352398), although it is not itself a curve.[^n3]

Variations of the same tree structure with thickened polygonal branches in place of the line segments of the H tree have been defined by [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999), and are sometimes called the Mandelbrot tree. In these variations, to avoid overlaps between the leaves of the tree and their thickened branches, the scale factor by which the size is reduced at each level must be slightly greater than $\sqrt2$.[^n7]
