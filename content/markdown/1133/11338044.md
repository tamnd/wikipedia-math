# Regular Hadamard matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regular Hadamard matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) a **regular Hadamard matrix** is a [Hadamard matrix](wikipedia://en/507553) whose row and column sums are all equal. While the order of a Hadamard matrix must be 1, 2, or a multiple of 4, regular Hadamard matrices carry the further restriction that the order must be a [square number](wikipedia://en/213919). The **excess**, denoted *E*(*H*), of a Hadamard matrix *H* of order *n* is defined to be the sum of the entries of *H*. The excess satisfies the bound |*E*(*H*)| ≤ *n*<sup>3/2</sup>. A Hadamard matrix attains this bound [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is regular.

:::

## Parameters {#Parameters}

If *n* = 4*u*<sup>2</sup> is the order of a regular Hadamard matrix, then the excess is ±8*u*<sup>3</sup> and the row and column sums all equal ±2*u*. It follows that each row has 2*u*<sup>2</sup> ± *u* positive entries and 2*u*<sup>2</sup> ∓ *u* negative entries. The [orthogonality](wikipedia://en/102221) of rows implies that any two distinct rows have exactly *u*<sup>2</sup> ± *u* positive entries in common. If *H* is interpreted as the [incidence matrix](wikipedia://en/420919) of a [block design](wikipedia://en/1188184), with 1 representing incidence and −1 representing non-incidence, then *H* corresponds to a symmetric 2-(*v*,*k*,*λ*) design with parameters (4*u*<sup>2</sup>, 2*u*<sup>2</sup> ± *u*, *u*<sup>2</sup> ± *u*). A design with these parameters is called a **Menon design**.

## Construction {#Construction}

::: {#o5 .problem cue=unsolved}

Which square numbers can be the order of a regular Hadamard matrix?

:::

A number of methods for constructing regular Hadamard matrices are known, and some exhaustive computer searches have been done for regular Hadamard matrices with specified symmetry [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), but it is not known whether every [even](wikipedia://en/143135) perfect square is the order of a regular Hadamard matrix. **Bush-type Hadamard matrices** are regular Hadamard matrices of a special form, and are connected with [finite projective planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_projective_plane).

A regular Hadamard matrix that is also [circulant](wikipedia://en/889936) corresponds to a Menon design with a regular cyclic automorphism group. The existence of such circulant examples of order greater than 4 is the subject of [Ryser's conjecture on circulant Hadamard matrices](wikipedia://en/82859189), which conjectures that none exist.

:::

## History and naming {#History_and_naming}

Like Hadamard matrices more generally, regular Hadamard matrices are named after [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675). Menon designs are named after [P Kesava Menon](wikipedia://en/18537948), and Bush-type Hadamard matrices are named after Kenneth A. Bush.
