# Ursescu theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ursescu theorem"}

In mathematics, particularly in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201), the **Ursescu theorem** is a theorem that generalizes the [closed graph theorem](wikipedia://en/64620077), the [open mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/17395276), and the [uniform boundedness principle](wikipedia://en/247392).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Ursescu theorem"}

## Ursescu theorem {#Ursescu_theorem}

The following notation and notions are used, where $\mathcal{R} : X \rightrightarrows Y$ is a [set-valued function](wikipedia://en/12506030) and $S$ is a non-empty subset of a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) $X$:

- the [affine span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_span) of $S$ is denoted by $\operatorname{aff} S$ and the [linear span](wikipedia://en/56353) is denoted by $\operatorname{span} S.$
- $S^{i} := \operatorname{aint}_X S$ denotes the [algebraic interior](wikipedia://en/30823252) of $S$ in $X.$
- ${}^{i}S:= \operatorname{aint}_{\operatorname{aff}(S - S)} S$ denotes the [relative algebraic interior](wikipedia://en/30823252#Relative_algebraic_interior) of $S$ (i.e. the algebraic interior of $S$ in $\operatorname{aff}(S - S)$).
- ${}^{ib}S := {}^{i}S$ if $\operatorname{span} \left(S - s_0\right)$ is [barreled](wikipedia://en/1733592) for some/every $s_0 \in S$ while ${}^{ib}S := \varnothing$ otherwise.
  - If $S$ is convex then it can be shown that for any $x \in X,$ $x \in {}^{ib} S$ if and only if the cone generated by $S - x$ is a barreled linear subspace of $X$ or equivalently, if and only if $\cup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} n (S - x)$ is a barreled linear subspace of $X$
- The **domain of $\mathcal{R}$** is $\operatorname{Dom} \mathcal{R} := \{ x \in X : \mathcal{R}(x) \neq \varnothing \}.$
- The **image of $\mathcal{R}$** is $\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R} := \cup_{x \in X} \mathcal{R}(x).$ For any subset $A \subseteq X,$ $\mathcal{R}(A) := \cup_{x \in A} \mathcal{R}(x).$
- The **graph of $\mathcal{R}$** is $\operatorname{gr} \mathcal{R} := \{ (x, y) \in X \times Y : y \in \mathcal{R}(x) \}.$
- $\mathcal{R}$ is **closed** (respectively, **convex**) if the graph of $\mathcal{R}$ is closed (resp. convex) in $X \times Y.$
  - Note that $\mathcal{R}$ is convex if and only if for all $x_0, x_1 \in X$ and all $r \in [0, 1],$ $r \mathcal{R}\left(x_0\right) + (1 - r) \mathcal{R}\left(x_1\right) \subseteq \mathcal{R} \left(r x_0 + (1 - r) x_1\right).$
- The **inverse of $\mathcal{R}$** is the set-valued function $\mathcal{R}^{-1} : Y \rightrightarrows X$ defined by $\mathcal{R}^{-1}(y) := \{ x \in X : y \in \mathcal{R}(x) \}.$ For any subset $B \subseteq Y,$ $\mathcal{R}^{-1}(B) := \cup_{y \in B} \mathcal{R}^{-1}(y).$
  - If $f : X \to Y$ is a function, then its inverse is the set-valued function $f^{-1} : Y \rightrightarrows X$ obtained from canonically identifying $f$ with the set-valued function $f : X \rightrightarrows Y$ defined by $x \mapsto \{ f(x)\}.$
- $\operatorname{int}_T S$ is the [topological interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of $S$ with respect to $T,$ where $S \subseteq T.$
- $\operatorname{rint} S := \operatorname{int}_{\operatorname{aff} S} S$ is the [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of $S$ with respect to $\operatorname{aff} S.$

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem (Ursescu)"}

**Theorem[^n1] (**Ursescu**).**

Let $X$ be a [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856) [semi-metrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078) [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) and $\mathcal{R} : X \rightrightarrows Y$ be a [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_%28mathematics%29) [convex](wikipedia://en/245568) multifunction with non-empty domain. Assume that $\operatorname{span} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R} - y)$ is a [barrelled space](wikipedia://en/1733592) for some/every $y \in \operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}.$ Assume that $y_0 \in {}^{i}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R})$ and let $x_0 \in \mathcal{R}^{-1}\left(y_0\right)$ (so that $y_0 \in \mathcal{R}\left(x_0\right)$). Then for every neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ in $X,$ $y_0$ belongs to the relative interior of $\mathcal{R}(U)$ in $\operatorname{aff} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R})$ (that is, $y_0 \in \operatorname{int}_{\operatorname{aff} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R})} \mathcal{R}(U)$). In particular, if ${}^{ib}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}) \neq \varnothing$ then ${}^{ib}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}) = {}^{i}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}) = \operatorname{rint} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}).$

:::

## Corollaries {#Corollaries}

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Closed graph theorem"}

### Closed graph theorem {#Closed_graph_theorem}

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Closed graph theorem"}

**[Closed graph theorem](wikipedia://en/497834).**

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801) and $T : X \to Y$ be a linear map. Then $T$ is continuous if and only if the graph of $T$ is closed in $X \times Y.$

:::

::: {#o12 .proof cue=math-proof}

For the non-trivial direction, assume that the graph of $T$ is closed and let $\mathcal{R} := T^{-1} : Y \rightrightarrows X.$ It is easy to see that $\operatorname{gr} \mathcal{R}$ is closed and convex and that its image is $X.$ Given $x \in X,$ $(Tx, x)$ belongs to $Y \times X$ so that for every open neighborhood $V$ of $Tx$ in $Y,$ $\mathcal{R}(V) = T^{-1}(V)$ is a neighborhood of $x$ in $X.$ Thus $T$ is continuous at $x.$ [Q.E.D.](wikipedia://en/25228)

:::

:::

::: {#o14 .statement cue=named-heading name="Uniform boundedness principle"}

### Uniform boundedness principle {#Uniform_boundedness_principle}

::: {#o15 .statement cue=math-theorem name="Uniform boundedness principle"}

**[Uniform boundedness principle](wikipedia://en/247392).**

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801) and $T : X \to Y$ be a bijective linear map. Then $T$ is continuous if and only if $T^{-1} : Y \to X$ is continuous. Furthermore, if $T$ is continuous then $T$ is an isomorphism of [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801).

:::

::: {#o16 .proof cue=math-proof}

Apply the closed graph theorem to $T$ and $T^{-1}.$ Q.E.D.

:::

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Open mapping theorem"}

### Open mapping theorem {#Open_mapping_theorem}

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Open mapping theorem"}

**[Open mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/17395276).**

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801) and $T : X \to Y$ be a continuous surjective linear map. Then T is an [open map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_map).

:::

::: {#o20 .proof cue=math-proof}

Clearly, $T$ is a closed and convex relation whose image is $Y.$ Let $U$ be a non-empty open subset of $X,$ let $y$ be in $T(U),$ and let $x$ in $U$ be such that $y = Tx.$ From the Ursescu theorem it follows that $T(U)$ is a neighborhood of $y.$ Q.E.D.

:::

:::

### Additional corollaries {#Additional_corollaries}

The following notation and notions are used for these corollaries, where $\mathcal{R} : X \rightrightarrows Y$ is a set-valued function, $S$ is a non-empty subset of a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) $X$:

- a **[convex series](wikipedia://en/63558794) with elements of $S$** is a [series](wikipedia://en/15287) of the form $\sum_{i=1}^\infty r_i s_i$ where all $s_i \in S$ and $\sum_{i=1}^\infty r_i = 1$ is a series of non-negative numbers. If $\sum_{i=1}^\infty r_i s_i$ converges then the series is called **convergent** while if $\left(s_i\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}$ is bounded then the series is called **bounded** and **b-convex**.
- $S$ is **ideally convex** if any convergent b-convex series of elements of $S$ has its sum in $S.$
- $S$ is **lower ideally convex** if there exists a [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) $Y$ such that $S$ is equal to the projection onto $X$ of some ideally convex subset *B* of $X \times Y.$ Every ideally convex set is lower ideally convex.

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=math-theorem name="Corollary"}

**Corollary.**

Let $X$ be a barreled [first countable space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_countable_space) and let $C$ be a subset of $X.$ Then:

If $C$ is lower ideally convex then $C^{i} = \operatorname{int} C.$

If $C$ is ideally convex then $C^{i} = \operatorname{int} C = \operatorname{int} \left(\operatorname{cl} C\right) = \left(\operatorname{cl} C\right)^i.$

:::

### Simons' theorem {#Simons'_theorem}

::: {#o28 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Simons' theorem"}

**[Simons'](wikipedia://en/2430844) theorem[^n2].**

Let $X$ and $Y$ be [first countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_countable) with $X$ locally convex. Suppose that $\mathcal{R} : X \rightrightarrows Y$ is a multimap with non-empty domain that satisfies [condition (Hw*x*)](wikipedia://en/63558794#Conditions_%28Hx%29_and_%28Hwx%29) or else assume that $X$ is a [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) and that $\mathcal{R}$ is [lower ideally convex](wikipedia://en/63558794#Types_of_subsets). Assume that $\operatorname{span} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R} - y)$ is [barreled](wikipedia://en/1733592) for some/every $y \in \operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}.$ Assume that $y_0 \in {}^{i}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R})$ and let $x_0 \in \mathcal{R}^{-1}\left(y_0\right).$ Then for every neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ in $X,$ $y_0$ belongs to the relative interior of $\mathcal{R}(U)$ in $\operatorname{aff} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R})$ (i.e. $y_0 \in \operatorname{int}_{\operatorname{aff} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R})} \mathcal{R}(U)$). In particular, if ${}^{ib}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}) \neq \varnothing$ then ${}^{ib}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}) = {}^{i}(\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}) = \operatorname{rint} (\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}).$

:::

::: {#o30 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Robinson–Ursescu theorem"}

### Robinson–Ursescu theorem {#Robinson–Ursescu_theorem}

The implication (1) $\implies$ (2) in the following theorem is known as the Robinson–Ursescu theorem.[^n3]

::: {#o33 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Robinson–Ursescu theorem"}

**Robinson–Ursescu theorem[^n3].**

Let $(X, \|\,\cdot\,\|)$ and $(Y, \|\,\cdot\,\|)$ be [normed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) and $\mathcal{R} : X \rightrightarrows Y$ be a multimap with non-empty domain. Suppose that $Y$ is a [barreled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barreled_space), the graph of $\mathcal{R}$ verifies condition [condition (Hw*x*)](wikipedia://en/63558794#Conditions_%28Hx%29_and_%28Hwx%29), and that $(x_0, y_0) \in \operatorname{gr} \mathcal{R}.$ Let $C_X$ (resp. $C_Y$) denote the closed unit ball in $X$ (resp. $Y$) (so $C_X = \{ x \in X : \| x \| \leq 1 \}$). Then the following are equivalent:

$y_0$ belongs to the [algebraic interior](wikipedia://en/30823252) of $\operatorname{Im} \mathcal{R}.$

$$y_0 \in \operatorname{int} \mathcal{R}\left(x_0 + C_X\right).$$

There exists $B > 0$ such that for all $0 \leq r \leq 1,$ $y_0 + B r C_Y \subseteq \mathcal{R} \left(x_0 + r C_X\right).$

There exist $A > 0$ and $B > 0$ such that for all $x \in x_0 + A C_X$ and all $y \in y_0 + A C_Y,$ $d\left(x, \mathcal{R}^{-1}(y)\right) \leq B \cdot d(y, \mathcal{R}(x)).$

There exists $B > 0$ such that for all $x \in X$ and all $y \in y_0 + B C_Y,$ $d \left(x, \mathcal{R}^{-1}(y)\right) \leq \frac{1 + \left\|x - x_0\right\|}{B - \left\|y - y_0\right\|} \cdot d(y, \mathcal{R}(x)).$

:::
