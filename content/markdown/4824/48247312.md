# Daniel Bennequin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Bennequin"}

**Daniel Bennequin** (3 January 1952) is a French mathematician, known for the [Thurston–Bennequin number](wikipedia://en/7418819) (sometimes called the Bennequin number) introduced in his doctoral dissertation.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bennequin completed his secondary education at [Lycée Condorcet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Condorcet) and then graduated from the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure). He received his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) (Doctoral d'Etat) in 1982 from the [University of Paris VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VII) under [Alain Chenciner](wikipedia://en/48254929) with thesis *Entrelacements et équations de Pfaff*.[^o4] He was a professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) before becoming a professor at the University of Paris VII (Institut Mathématique de Jussieu).

Bennequin's dissertation was a major contribution to [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398), in which he gave the first example of an exotic contact structure embedded in Euclidean 3-space. On the basis of their work in the 1980s Bennequin and [Yakov Eliashberg](wikipedia://en/20652422) might be considered the founders of contact topology.[^o6] Bennequin also works on [motion planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_planning).[^o7] He was a member of [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394).[^o8]
