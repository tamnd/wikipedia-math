# Kleetope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kleetope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519), the **Kleetope** of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) or higher-dimensional [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) $P$ is another polyhedron or polytope $P^{K}$ formed by replacing each [facet](wikipedia://en/7186647) of $P$ with a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045).[^o2] In some cases, the pyramid is chosen to have regular sides, often producing a non-convex polytope; alternatively, by using sufficiently shallow pyramids, the results may remain convex. Kleetopes are named after [Victor Klee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Klee),[^o3] although the same concept was known under other names long before the work of Klee.[^n1]

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In each of these cases, the Kleetope is formed by attaching pyramids onto each face of the original polyhedron. These examples can be seen from the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905):

- The [triakis tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/722548) is the Kleetope of a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), the [triakis octahedron](wikipedia://en/722549) is the Kleetope of an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), and the [triakis icosahedron](wikipedia://en/722873) is the Kleetope of an [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084). These Kleetopes are formed by adding a triangular pyramid to each face of them.[^n1]
- The [tetrakis hexahedron](wikipedia://en/722554) is the Kleetope of the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), formed by adding a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) to each of its faces
- The [pentakis dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/723196) is the Kleetope of the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407), formed by adding a [pentagonal pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128461) to each face of the dodecahedron.[^n2]

The base polyhedron of a Kleetope does not need to be a Platonic solid. For instance, the [disdyakis dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/724752) is the Kleetope of the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), formed by replacing each [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) face of the dodecahedron with a rhombic pyramid, and the [disdyakis triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/725111) is the Kleetope of the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025). In fact, the base polyhedron of a Kleetope does not need to be [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral), as can be seen from the tripentakis icosidodecahedron above.

:::

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

One method of forming the Kleetope of a polytope $P$ is to place a new vertex outside $P$, near the centroid of each facet. If all of these new vertices are placed close enough to the corresponding centroids, then the only other vertices visible to them will be the vertices of the facets from which they are defined. In this case, the Kleetope of $P$ is the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the union of the vertices of $P$ and the set of new vertices.[^n3]

Alternatively, the Kleetope may be defined by [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) and its dual operation, [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429): the Kleetope of $P$ is the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of the truncation of the dual of $P$.

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties and applications"}

## Properties and applications {#Properties_and_applications}

If $P$ has enough vertices relative to its dimension, then the Kleetope of $P$ is *dimensionally unambiguous*: the graph formed by its edges and vertices is not the graph of a different polyhedron or polytope with a different dimension. More specifically, if the number of vertices of a $d$-dimensional polytope $P$ is at least $d^{2}/2$, then $P^{K}$ is dimensionally unambiguous.

If every $i$-dimensional face of a $d$-dimensional polytope $P$ is a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781), and if $i \le d - 2$, then every $(i + 1)$-dimensional face of $P^{K}$ is also a simplex. In particular, the Kleetope of any three-dimensional polyhedron is a [simplicial polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8853373), a polyhedron in which all facets are triangles.

*Image: Kleetope of a triangular bipyramid.svg.* The Kleetope of a triangular bipyramid

Kleetopes may be used to generate polyhedra that do not have any [Hamiltonian cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle): any path through one of the vertices added in the Kleetope construction must go into and out of the vertex through its neighbors in the original polyhedron, and if there are more new vertices than original vertices then there are not enough neighbors to go around. In particular, the [Goldner–Harary graph](wikipedia://en/24674758), the Kleetope of the triangular bipyramid, has six vertices added in the Kleetope construction and only five in the bipyramid from which it was formed, so it is non-Hamiltonian; it is the simplest possible non-Hamiltonian simplicial polyhedron. If a polyhedron with $n$ vertices is formed by repeating the Kleetope construction some number of times, starting from a tetrahedron, then its [longest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_path) has length $O(n^{\log_{3} 2})$; that is, the [shortness exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortness_exponent) of these graphs is $\log_{3} 2$, approximately 0.630930. The same technique shows that in any higher dimension $d$, there exist simplicial polytopes with shortness exponent $\log_{d} 2$.[^n4] Similarly, (Plummer 1992) used the Kleetope construction to provide an infinite family of examples of simplicial polyhedra with an even number of vertices that have no [perfect matching](wikipedia://en/44031).[^n5]

Kleetopes also have some extreme properties related to their [vertex degrees](wikipedia://en/1374448): if each edge in a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) is incident to at least seven other edges, then there must exist a vertex of degree at most five all but one of whose neighbors have degree 20 or more, and the Kleetope of the Kleetope of the icosahedron provides an example in which the high-degree vertices have degree exactly 20.[^n6]
