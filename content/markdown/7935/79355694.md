# Rank-finiteness

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rank-finiteness"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **rank-finiteness for fusion categories** is a collection of related [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) and [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) about structures related to [fusion categories](wikipedia://en/53762899). The question is whether or not a given categorical structure related to fusion categories has [finitely](wikipedia://en/11742) many or [infinitely](wikipedia://en/142679) many equivalence classes of a given rank. The original result in this direction was Ocneanu rigidity, which asserts that every fusion ring has finitely many [categorifications](wikipedia://en/1596969).[^o2] A particularly relevant example to [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics) is the rank-finiteness theorem for [modular tensor categories](wikipedia://en/79289899).[^o3][^o4]

:::

Here and throughout, the rank of a fusion category refers to the number of isomorphism classes of simple objects it has.

## Ocneanu rigidity {#Ocneanu_rigidity}

The **Ocneanu rigidity** theorem, named after mathematician Adrian Ocneanu, asserts that every fusion ring has finitely many [categorifications](wikipedia://en/1596969). Stated more directly, this means that are only finitely many equivalence classes of [fusion categories](wikipedia://en/53762899) with a given set of fusion rules. The braided version of Ocneanu rigidity states that there are only finitely many equivalence classes of [braided](wikipedia://en/1470466) fusion categories with a given set of fusion rules.[^o2]

The proof of Ocneanu rigidity relies on the demonstrating vanishing of the Davydov-Yetter cohomology groups, which classify certain equivalence classes of deformations of fusion categories.[^o6][^o7]

## Positive results on rank finiteness {#Positive_results_on_rank_finiteness}

There are several known cases of rank-finiteness. These

- The rank-finiteness theorem for [modular tensor category](wikipedia://en/79289899) is a theorem due to Paul Bruillard, Siu-Hung Ng, Eric Rowell, and [Zhenghan Wang](wikipedia://en/76425950).[^o4]
- The rank-finiteness theorem for [braided](wikipedia://en/1470466) fusion categories is a theorem due to Corey Jones, Scott Morrison, Dmitri Nikshych, and Eric Rowell.[^o9]
- The rank-finiteness theorem for G-crossed braided fusion categories is a theorem, also due to Jones et al.[^o9]
- The rank-finiteness theorem for super-modular tensor categories is a theorem, also due to Jones et al.[^o9]

## Unknown results on rank finiteness {#Unknown_results_on_rank_finiteness}

It is not known whether or not there are finitely many fusion categories of a given rank. Similarly, it is not known whether or not there are finitely many [pivotal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pivotal_category) fusion categories and [spherical](wikipedia://en/41038739) fusion categories of a given rank. This is related to the other open problem of determining whether or not it is true that every fusion category admits a pivotal (or even spherical) structure.[^o7]
