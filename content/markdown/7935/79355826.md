# Müger's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Müger's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Müger's theorem** asserts that the [Drinfeld center](wikipedia://en/11814370) of every [spherical](wikipedia://en/41038739) [fusion category](wikipedia://en/53762899) is a [modular tensor category](wikipedia://en/79289899). Müger's theorem was introduced in 2003 by mathematician Michael Müger.[^o2] Due to the connections between spherical fusion categories and modular tensor categories to the [algebraic theory of topological quantum information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_theory_of_topological_quantum_information), this theorem has found various uses within [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

:::

## Interpretation in terms of topological quantum field theory {#Interpretation_in_terms_of_topological_quantum_field_theory}

In terms of topological quantum field theory, Müger's theorem is useful for understanding the relationship between the Turaev–Viro construction and the [Reshetikhin–Turaev construction](wikipedia://en/60958267). The Reshetikhin–Turaev construction takes as input a modular tensor category and has as output a (2+1)-dimensional [topological quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/443757).[^o4] The Turaev–Viro construction takes as input a spherical fusion category and has as output a (2+1)-dimensional topological quantum field theory.[^o5][^o6] The relationship between these models is that the Turaev–Viro model with input spherical fusion category $\mathcal{C}$ is equivalent to the Reshetikhin-Turaev model with input modular tensor category $\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})$.[^o7] For this to be a valid equivalence, it is necessary for the Drinfeld center of a spherical fusion category to be a modular tensor category.

## Interpretation in terms of the Levin–Wen model {#Interpretation_in_terms_of_the_Levin–Wen_model}

In terms of [lattice model](wikipedia://en/291472), Müger's theorem is useful for understanding the Levin–Wen model. In [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics), the Levin–Wen model is a construction which takes as input a unitary fusion category $\mathcal{C}$ and has as output an [exactly solvable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exactly_solvable) lattice model for [topological order](wikipedia://en/3087602) whose [anyons](wikipedia://en/367654) are described by the category $\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})$.[^o9] For $\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})$ to be a valid description of anyons in a physical system, the [algebraic theory of topological quantum information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_theory_of_topological_quantum_information) asserts that $\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})$ would need to be a [unitary modular tensor category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_modular_tensor_category).

Every unitary fusion category $\mathcal{C}$ admits a canonical [spherical](wikipedia://en/41038739) structure.[^o10] As such, Müger's theorem applies and asserts that $\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})$ is a [modular tensor category](wikipedia://en/79289899), and the unitary structure on $\mathcal{C}$ endows $\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})$ with the structure of a [unitary modular tensor category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_modular_tensor_category). Thus, in this context, Müger's theorem encodes the fact that for any unitary fusion category $\mathcal{C}$ the quantum double $\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})$ is a valid choice of anyon theory for the Levin–Wen model.
