# Bruguières modularity theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bruguières modularity theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Bruguières modularity theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) about [modular tensor categories](wikipedia://en/79289899). It asserts that two different formulations of the modularity condition of a modular tensor category are equivalent. The Bruguières modularity theorem was introduced by mathematician Alain Bruguières in the year 2000.[^o2] The first notion of modularity used in the theorem statement is in terms of the non-degeneracy of the [braid statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_statistics) of the simple objects, and the other is in terms of the non-degeneracy of the [modular S-matrix](wikipedia://en/79355409). Historically, the non-degeneracy condition for modular tensor categories was originally stated in terms of the invertibility of the $S$-matrix.[^o3] Nowadays, it is common to define modular category in terms of the non-degeneracy of its braiding statistics, especially in the [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics) literature.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The Bruguières modularity theorem is stated in terms of **pre-modular tensor categories**, a notion introduced by Bruguières in the same paper in which he proved the modularity theorem.[^o2] A category $\mathcal{C}$ is called pre-modular if it is equipped with all of the structures of a [modular tensor category](wikipedia://en/79289899), and satisfies all of the axioms except possibly for non-degeneracy. Bruguières' modularity theorem asserts that a pre-modular tensor category has non-degenerate braiding if and only if its [modular S-matrix](wikipedia://en/79355409) is invertible.

:::

## Intuition {#Intuition}

Let $\mathcal{C}$ denote a pre-modular tensor category. One direction of the Bruguières modularity theorem is straightforward - if the braiding is degenerate, then there will be a simple object $A\in \mathcal{C}$ which braids trivially with all the other simple objects, and so the column of the modular $S$-matrix corresponding to the simple object $A\in \mathcal{C}$ will be proportional to the column of the modular $S$-matrix associated to the tensor unit ${\bf 1}$. Thus, the $S$-matrix will be degenerate. The content of the theorem is that if the braiding is non-degenerate, then the modular $S$-matrix will be invertible.

If [modular tensor categories](wikipedia://en/79289899) are defined in terms of the non-degeneracy of their braiding, then the Bruguières modularity theorem is a necessary ingredient for the existence of the [modular group representation](wikipedia://en/79355409). In the modular group representation the $S$-matrix is the image, up to scaling, of one of the generators of $\text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$. As such, for the modular group representation to be a valid representation it is necessary for the $S$-matrix to be invertible.[^o8]
