# Schauenburg–Ng theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schauenburg–Ng theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Schauenbug–Ng theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) about the [modular group representations](wikipedia://en/79355409) of [modular tensor categories](wikipedia://en/79289899) proved by Siu-Hung Ng and Peter Schauenburg in 2010. It asserts that that the kernels of the modular representations of all modular tensor categories are [congruence subgroups](wikipedia://en/382694) of $\text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$.[^o2] Since congruence subgroups all have finite index in $\text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, this implies in particular that the modular representations of all modular representations have finite image.

:::

On physical grounds coming from [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774), it has been conjectured since 1987 by [Greg Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Moore_%28physicist%29) and others that the kernel of the modular group representations should be congruence subgroups.[^o3][^o4][^o5] The proof by Schauenbug and Ng came after a series of partial results by other mathematicians, which proved the theorem in special cases.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

To prove their result Schauenbug and Ng introduced the notion of 'generalied Frobenius–Schur' indicators, which have since found separate applications to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).[^o9]
