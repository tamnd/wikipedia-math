# Wigner surmise

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wigner surmise"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the **Wigner surmise** is a statement about the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the spaces between points in the [spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy#Atoms) of nuclei of heavy atoms, which have many degrees of freedom, or quantum systems with few degrees of freedom but chaotic classical dynamics. It was proposed by [Eugene Wigner](wikipedia://en/10101) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o2] The surmise was a result of Wigner's introduction of [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices) in the field of [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics). The surmise consists of two postulates:

:::

- In a simple sequence ([spin](wikipedia://en/19593829) and [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_%28physics%29) are same), the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) for a spacing is given by,

$$p_w(s) = \frac{\pi s}{2} e^{-\pi s^2/4}.$$

Here, $s = \frac{S}{D}$ where *S* is a particular spacing and *D* is the mean distance between neighboring intervals.[^o3]

- In a mixed sequence (spin and parity are different), the probability density function can be obtained by randomly superimposing simple sequences.

The above result is exact for $2\times 2$ real symmetric matrices $M$, with elements that are independent standard gaussian random variables, with joint distribution proportional to

$$e^{-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}(M^2)}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\left(\begin{array}{cc}a & b \\b & c \\\end{array}\right)^2}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}a^2-\frac{1}{2}c^2-b^2}.$$

In practice, it is a good approximation for the actual distribution for real symmetric matrices of any dimension. The corresponding result for complex hermitian matrices (which is also exact in the $2\times 2$ case and a good approximation in general) with distribution proportional to $e^{-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}(MM^\dagger)}$, is given by

$$p_w(s) = \frac{32 s^2}{\pi^2} e^{-4s^2/\pi}.$$

## History {#History}

During the conference on [Neutron Physics by Time-of-Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_time-of-flight_scattering), held at [Gatlinburg, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatlinburg,_Tennessee), November 1 and 2, 1956, Wigner delivered a presentation on the theoretical arrangement of neighboring neutron resonances (with matching spin and parity) in heavy nuclei. In the presentation he gave the following guess:[^o5][^o6]At the time of statement, there was little data to prove the surmise. The situation quickly improved as people gathered more data. Firk et al. began a project in 1956, finishing in 1960, studying the first 100 resonances in ^{238}U + n at energies up to 8 keV. The data ruled out an exponential distribution and provided the best then-available evidence in support of Wigner’s surmise.[^o7]
