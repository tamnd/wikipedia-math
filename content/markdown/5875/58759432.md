# Edray Herber Goins

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edray Herber Goins"}

**Edray Herber Goins** (born June 29, 1972, Los Angeles) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3] He specializes in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). His interests include [Selmer groups](wikipedia://en/1970862) for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) using [class groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_group) of [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), [Belyi maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belyi_function) and [dessins d'enfant](wikipedia://en/1585922).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Goins was born in Los Angeles in 1972. His mother, Eddi Beatrice Brown, was a teacher. He attended public schools in [South Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Los_Angeles) and got his BSc in mathematics and physics in 1994 from [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), where he also received two prizes for mathematics. He completed his PhD in 1999 on “Elliptic Curves and Icosahedral Galois Representations” from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), under [Daniel Bump](wikipedia://en/50510967) and [Karl Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rubin).[^o7][^o9][^o10]

## Career {#Career}

He served for many years on the faculty of [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University).[^o12] He has also served as visiting scholar at both the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), and [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard).[^o9][^o13] Goins took a position at [Pomona College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona_College) in 2018.[^o14]

His summers have focused on engaging underrepresented students in research in the mathematical sciences. He currently runs the [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation)-funded [Research Experience for Undergraduates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Experience_for_Undergraduates) (REU) "Pomona Research in Mathematics Experience (PRiME)",[^o15] a program that Goins started in 2016 at Purdue University under the title "Purdue Research in Mathematics Experience (PRiME)".[^o16] He is noted for his 2018 essay, "Three Questions: The Journey of One Black Mathematician".[^o17] He was elected to the 2019 Class of Fellows of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o18]

From 2015 to 2020, Goins served as president of the [National Association of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/58449356) (NAM).[^o19]

## Mathematicians of the African Diaspora {#Mathematicians_of_the_African_Diaspora}

In 1997 [Scott W. Williams](wikipedia://en/10709770) of the [University at Buffalo, SUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo,_SUNY) created the website **[Mathematicians of the African Diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematicians_of_the_African_Diaspora)** (MAD) dedicated to promoting and highlighting the contributions of members of the African diaspora to mathematics, especially contributions to current mathematical research.[^o21] Williams retired in 2008 and it was left to others to continue the website he had spent 11 years building. After an initial town hall meeting about the future of the MAD Pages which took place at a Conference for African American Researchers in the Mathematical Sciences (CAARMS), an informal group of mathematicians decided to work together to preserve Williams’ work. In 2015, the National Association of Mathematicians (NAM) formed an ad hoc committee to update the MAD Pages, consisting of Edray Goins as NAM President, Committee Co-Chairs Don King ([Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University)) and Asamoah Nkwanta ([Morgan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_State_University)), and web developer John Weaver (Varsity Software).
