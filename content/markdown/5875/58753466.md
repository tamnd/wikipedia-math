# Bendixson's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bendixson's inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Bendixson's inequality** is a quantitative result in the field of [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) derived by [Ivar Bendixson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivar_Bendixson) in 1902.[^o2][^o3] The inequality puts limits on the imaginary and real parts of [characteristic roots](wikipedia://en/2161429) (eigenvalues) of real matrices.[^o4] A special case of this inequality leads to the result that characteristic roots of a real symmetric matrix are always real.

:::

The inequality relating to the imaginary parts of characteristic roots of real matrices is stated as:[^o2]

Let $A = \left ( a_{ij} \right )$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix and $\alpha = \max_{{1\leq i,j \leq n}} \frac{1}{2} \left | a_{ij} - a_{ji} \right |$. If $\lambda$ is any characteristic root of $A$, then

$\left | \operatorname{Im} (\lambda) \right | \le \alpha \sqrt{\frac{n(n-1)} 2 }.\,{}$[^o5]

If $A$ is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) then $\alpha = 0$ and consequently the inequality implies that $\lambda$ must be real.

The inequality relating to the real parts of characteristic roots of real matrices (Theorem II in [^o2]) is stated as:

Let $m$ and $M$ be the smallest and largest characteristic roots of $\tfrac{A+A^H}{2}$, then

$$m \leq\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \leq M.$$
