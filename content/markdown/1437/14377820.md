# Dirichlet energy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dirichlet energy"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Dirichlet energy** is a measure of how *variable* a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) is. More abstractly, it is a [quadratic](wikipedia://en/187240) [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) on the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $H^{1}$. The Dirichlet energy is intimately connected to [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941) and is named after the German mathematician [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) $\Omega \subseteq \mathbf{R}^{n}$ and a differentiable function $u : \Omega \to \mathbf{R}$, the **Dirichlet energy** of the function $u$ is the [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438)

$$E[u] = \frac 1 2 \int_\Omega \| \nabla u(x) \|^2 \, dx,$$

where $\nabla u : \Omega \to \mathbf{R}^{n}$ denotes the [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) of the function $u$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties and applications"}

## Properties and applications {#Properties_and_applications}

Since it is the integral of a non-negative quantity, the Dirichlet energy is itself non-negative, i.e. $E[u] \ge 0$ for every function $u$.

Solving Laplace's equation $-\Delta u(x) = 0$ for all $x \in \Omega$, subject to appropriate [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions), is equivalent to solving the [variational problem](wikipedia://en/171882) of finding a function $u$ that satisfies the boundary conditions and has minimal Dirichlet energy.

Such a solution is called a [harmonic function](wikipedia://en/55762) and such solutions are the topic of study in [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342).

In a more general setting, where $\Omega \subseteq \mathbf{R}^{n}$ is replaced by any [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $M$, and $u : \Omega \to \mathbf{R}$ is replaced by $u : M \to \Phi$ for another (different) Riemannian manifold $\Phi$, the Dirichlet energy is given by the [sigma model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_model). The solutions to the [Lagrange equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_equation) for the sigma model [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) are those functions $u$ that minimize/maximize the Dirichlet energy. Restricting this general case back to the specific case of $u : \Omega \to \mathbf{R}$ just shows that the Lagrange equations (or, equivalently, the [Hamilton–Jacobi equations](wikipedia://en/897539)) provide the basic tools for obtaining extremal solutions.
