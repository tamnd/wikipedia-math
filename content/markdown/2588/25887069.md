# Karlsruhe metric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karlsruhe metric"}

*Image: Karlsruhe Bodenehr 1721 koloriert.jpg.* View of Karlsruhe in 1721

In [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry), the **Karlsruhe metric** is a [measure of distance](wikipedia://en/20018) that assumes travel is only possible along rays through the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) and circular arcs centered at the origin. The name alludes to the layout of the city of [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe), which has radial streets and circular avenues around a central point. This metric is also called **Moscow metric**.[^o2][^o3]

:::

In this metric, there are two types of shortest paths. One possibility, when the two points are on nearby rays, combines a circular arc through the nearer to the origin of the two points and a segment of a ray through the farther of the two points. Alternatively, for points on rays that are nearly opposite, it is shorter to follow one ray all the way to the origin and then follow the other ray back out. Therefore, the Karlsruhe distance between two points $d_k(p_1,p_2)$ is the minimum of the two lengths that would be obtained for these two types of path. That is, it equals

$$d_k(p_1,p_2)= \begin{cases} \min(r_1,r_2) \cdot \delta(p_1,p_2) +|r_1-r_2|,&\text{if } 0\leq \delta(p_1,p_2)\leq 2\\ r_1+r_2,&\text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

where $(r_i,\varphi_i)$ are the [polar coordinates](wikipedia://en/25120) of $p_i$ and $\delta(p_1,p_2)=\min(|\varphi_1-\varphi_2|,2\pi-|\varphi_1-\varphi_2|)$ is the [angular distance](wikipedia://en/3720362) between the two points.
