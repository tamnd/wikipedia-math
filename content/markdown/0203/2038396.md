# Lens (geometry)

*Image: Geometric lens.gif.* A lens contained between two circular arcs of radius $R$, and centers at $O_{1}$ and $O_{2}$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lens"}

In 2-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **lens** is a [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) region bounded by two [circular arcs](wikipedia://en/5578920) joined to each other at their endpoints. In order for this shape to be convex, both arcs must bow outwards (convex-convex). This shape can be formed as the [intersection](wikipedia://en/41780044) of two [circular disks](wikipedia://en/329542). It can also be formed as the union of two [circular segments](wikipedia://en/404049) (regions between the [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) of a circle and the circle itself), joined along a common chord.

:::

## Types {#Types}

*Image: geometric\_lens\_examples.svg.* Example of two asymmetric lenses (left and right) and one symmetric lens (in the middle)

*Image: Vesica\_piscis\_circles.svg.* The [Vesica piscis](wikipedia://en/471830) is the intersection of two [disks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disk_%28geometry%29) with the same radius, R, and with the distance between centers also equal to R.

If the two arcs of a lens have equal radius, it is called a **symmetric lens**, otherwise is an **asymmetric lens**.

The [vesica piscis](wikipedia://en/471830) is one form of a symmetric lens, formed by arcs of two circles whose centers each lie on the opposite arc. The arcs meet at angles of 120° at their endpoints.

## Area {#Area}

**Symmetric**

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a symmetric lens can be expressed in terms of the radius *R* and arc lengths *θ* in radians:

$$A = R^2\left(\theta - \sin \theta \right).$$

**Asymmetric**

The area of an asymmetric lens formed from circles of radii *R* and *r* with distance *d* between their centers is

$$A=r^2 \cos^{-1} \left(\frac{d^2+r^2-R^2}{2dr}\right) +R^2\cos^{-1}\left( \frac{d^2+R^2-r^2}{2dR}\right) -2\Delta$$

where

$$\Delta = \frac{1}{4} \sqrt{(-d+r+R)(d-r+R)(d+r-R)(d+r+R)}$$

is the [area of a triangle](wikipedia://en/30654#Using_Heron's_formula) with sides *d*, *r*, and *R*.

The two circles overlap if $d<r+R$. For sufficiently large $d$, the coordinate $x$ of the lens centre lies between the coordinates of the two circle centers:

*Image: Two overlapping circles with large distance.svg.* A lens contained between two circular arcs of radii *R* and *r* at distance of *d*

For small $d$ the coordinate $x$ of the lens centre lies outside the line that connects the circle centres:

*Image: Two overlapping circles with small distance.svg.* A lens contained between two circular arcs of radii *R* and *r* at distance of *d*

By eliminating *y* from the circle equations $x^2+y^2=r^2$ and $(x-d)^2+y^2=R^2$ the [abscissa](wikipedia://en/60487) of the intersecting rims is

$$x=(d^2+r^2-R^2)/(2d).$$

The sign of *x*, i.e., $d^2$ being larger or smaller than $R^2-r^2$, distinguishes the two cases shown in the images.

The [ordinate](wikipedia://en/60487) of the intersection is

$$y=\sqrt{r^2-x^2} = \frac{\sqrt{[(R-d)^2-r^2][r^2-(R+d)^2]}}{2d}.$$

Negative values under the square root indicate that the rims of the two circles do not touch because the circles are too far apart or one circle lies entirely within the other.

The value under the square root is a biquadratic polynomial of *d*. The four roots of this polynomial are associated with *y=0* and with the four values of *d* where the two circles have only one point in common.

The angles in the blue triangle of sides *d*, *r* and *R* are

$$\sin a_r = y/r;\quad \sin a_R = y/R$$

where *y* is the ordinate of the intersection. The branch of the arcsin with $a_r>\pi/2$ is to be taken if $d^2<R^2-r^2$.

The [area](wikipedia://en/30654#Computing_the_area_of_a_triangle) of the triangle is $\Delta = \frac12 yd$.

The area of the asymmetric lens is $A=a_r r^2+a_R R^2-yd$, where the two angles are measured in radians. \[This is an application of the [Inclusion-exclusion principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion-exclusion_principle): the two circular sectors centered at (0,0) and (d,0) with central angles $2a_r$ and $2a_R$ have areas $2a_r r^2$ and $2a_R R^2$. Their union covers the triangle, the flipped triangle with corner at (x, −y), and twice the lens area.\]

## Applications {#Applications}

A lens with a different shape forms the answer to [Mrs. Miniver's problem](wikipedia://en/62047), on finding a lens with half the area of the union of the two circles.

Lenses are used to define [beta skeletons](wikipedia://en/26266763), geometric graphs defined on a set of points by connecting pairs of points by an edge whenever a lens determined by the two points is empty.
