# Çetin Kaya Koç

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Çetin Kaya Koç"}

**Çetin Kaya Koç** (born 23 January 1957, [Ağrı, Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ağrı)) is a Turkish-American [cryptographic engineer](wikipedia://en/18934432),[^o3] academic, and author known for his research and work in cryptographic engineering,[^o4] secure hardware design, [finite field arithmetic](wikipedia://en/576387), and side‑channel security.[^o3]

:::

He has retired from Computer Science Department at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) and has held permanent academic appointments in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), and visiting appointments in Turkey, Italy, and China.[^o5]

Koç is the co‑founder of the Conference on Cryptographic Hardware and Embedded Systems (CHES) and the founding *Editor‑in‑Chief* of the *Journal of Cryptographic Engineering*.[^o6]

## Biography & Education {#Biography_&_Education}

Koç was born in [Ağrı, Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ağrı), where he completed his early education before earning a B.S. degree (summa cum laude) in Electrical Engineering from [İstanbul Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/İstanbul_Technical_University).[^o8] He then received his Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) in 1988, focusing on [computer architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_architecture), parallel algorithms and hardware implementations for algebraic computation.[^o9][^o10][^o11]

## Career {#Career}

Following his doctoral studies, Koç began his academic career as an assistant professor at the [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston) from 1988 to 1992.[^o13] He then joined [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University), where he progressed from assistant to full professor (1992–2007) and established the *Information Security Laboratory*, serving as a central hub for research on secure hardware and embedded [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432).[^o14]

Koç has held faculty positions at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) and maintained international appointments, including visiting roles at [Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_University_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics) and at several universities in Turkey, supervising doctoral and master's candidates and directing collaborative research programs.[^o15]

In 1999, Koç co‑founded the Conference on Cryptographic Hardware and Embedded Systems (CHES) with [Christof Paar](wikipedia://en/74074951). CHES has become one of the premier international fora for research on cryptographic hardware, embedded security, and side‑channel analysis.[^o16][^o17]

Koç has been a foundational figure in cryptographic engineering. He co-founded the Workshop on Cryptographic Hardware and Embedded Systems (CHES) in 1999 and served as program co-chair for five consecutive years (1999–2003) andcontinuously served in the steering committee, establishing CHES as a leading conference in software and hardware realization of cryptography.[^o18][^o19]

He also founded and served as Editor-in-Chief of the *Journal of Cryptographic Engineering*, overseeing its inaugural publication and shaping it into a premier venue for research on cryptographic hardware and embedded systems.[^o6] Koç contributed to practical cryptographic software development, notably as one of the designers of the RSAREF software toolkit, a widely used [reference implementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_implementation) of RSA cryptography.[^o20][^o21]

## Research and works {#Research_and_works}

His work has been recognized with prestigious awards, including the ACM CCS Test of Time Award [^o23] and the USENIX 2024 Distinguished Paper Award.[^o24] An accomplished author, he has published books such as *Cryptographic Engineering* (2009),[^o30] *Cryptographic Algorithms on Reconfigurable Hardware*,[^o25] *Partially Homomorphic Encryption*,[^o26] and several monographs on finite field arithmetic and post-quantum cryptography.[^o27]

Koç is the first researcher who coined or invented the term "Cryptographic Engineering" and started using it actively: as the name of a course offered at EPFL, Switzerland every year since 2002,[^o5] the name of a book,[^o30] and the name of the journal he had founded.[^o28] Koç described the detailed definition and history of cryptographic engineering in an article published in 2011, entitled "Introduction to Cryptographic Engineering".[^o29]

Koç’s research contributions span multiple foundational areas in cryptographic engineering and secure computation.[^o28][^o30][^o31] His work in *cryptographic engineering* focuses on the design and optimization of secure hardware and software implementations of cryptographic systems, including practical realizations of public‑key and [symmetric algorithms](wikipedia://en/53042), secure embedded systems, and high‑performance cryptographic components for real‑world applications.[^o25][^o28]

He has played a leading role in developing architectures and algorithms for finite field and ring arithmetic, which underpin efficient modular multiplication and exponentiation used in public‑key protocols, elliptic‑curve [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), and error‑correcting codes.[^o32][^o4]

His work addresses challenges in random number generation, privacy‑preserving computation,[^o33] and the application of *homomorphic encryption to* *machine learning*.[^o34]

In more recent work, he has contributed to [post‑quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/26605226) and [homomorphic encryption](wikipedia://en/4939073), co‑authoring monographs on partially homomorphic schemes and exploring secure computation models that allow operations on encrypted data without decryption.[^o35][^o36][^o37]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Koç has been recognized by both academic institutions and professional societies for his contributions to cryptographic engineering and secure system design.

- 2001 – Award for Outstanding and Sustained Research Leadership, Oregon State University.[^o39]
- 2007 – Elected Fellow of the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) for contributions to cryptographic engineering.[^o42]
- 2020 – International Fellowship for Outstanding Researchers, Scientific and Technological Research Council of Türkiye (TÜBİTAK).[^o40]
- 2023 – Life Fellow of the IEEE for sustained contributions to cryptographic engineering.[^o41][^o42]
