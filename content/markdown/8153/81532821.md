# Margaret Evelyn Mauch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Margaret Evelyn Mauch"}

**Margaret Evelyn Mauch** (1897–1987) was an American mathematician and university professor. She was one of the few women to earn a PhD in mathematics before World War II in the United States.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Margaret Mauch was born in [De Smet, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Smet,_South_Dakota) on June 1, 1897 to Henry Mauch and Norway-born Rose Brekhus. She spent her early years living in the area around De Smet and the 1900 census recorded shows that her family was living in [Lake Preston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Preston,_South_Dakota).[^o3]

Mauch attended public elementary and high schools in South Dakota. She studied at Huron College (now closed) in [Huron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huron,_South_Dakota), about thirty miles from De Smet. After receiving her bachelor's degree in 1919, she taught for a year at [Winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winner,_South_Dakota) High School in the south-central part of South Dakota. Thereafter, she worked for a year as principal of Edgerton High School in Minnesota. From 1921 to 1923, she attended the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), where she received her master's degree in 1923 with a thesis in theoretical mechanics titled: *The Motion of a Certain Rotating Bar*.[^o3]

From 1924 to 1925, she taught at the high school in [Jacksonville, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida), and then served as an instructor and assistant professor at the Randolph-Macon Woman's College (now [Randolph College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_College)) in [Lynchburg, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynchburg,_Virginia), until 1929, when she returned to the University of Chicago to complete her PhD. In 1938, she received her doctorate under [Leonard Eugene Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247) with a dissertation titled *Extensions of [Waring's Theorem](wikipedia://en/33569) on Seventh Powers*.[^o5][^o6] Dickson had already published some of her findings in 1933, identifying her as M.A. Mauch.[^o7] According to Green:

> "Although Mauch's degree was not conferred until 1938, she had obtained some of her results on Waring's theorem for seventh powers considerably earlier. In Dickson's 1933 article, *Recent Progress on Waring's Theorem and its Generalizations*, he mentions her work on page 721 at the end of the section he calls "Remarkable Empirical Generalizations of Waring's Theorem."[^o3][^o7]

From 1934 to 1942, Mauch headed the mathematics department at the high school in [Brookings, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings,_South_Dakota), and then, until 1944, she was an instructor and assistant professor at [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College) in [Northfield, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northfield,_Minnesota). From 1944 to 1945, she was an instructor at Michigan State College (now [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University)), and in 1945 she became an assistant professor at the [University of Akron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Akron) in Ohio, where she was promoted to associate professor in 1950 and full professor in 1962. She retired from the university in 1963 but continued to teach there and was appointed professor [emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) in 1966.[^o3]

In 1966, Margaret Mauch and her younger sister, Ruby Mauch, moved back from Akron to Huron, South Dakota, to live with a brother. Margaret Mauch died in Huron at the Violet Tschetter Memorial Home at 90 on November 16, 1987. She was buried in the [Hetland, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetland,_South_Dakota), cemetery.[^o3]
