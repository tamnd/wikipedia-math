# Discrete-stable distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Discrete-stable distribution"}

**Discrete-stable distributions**[^o2] are a class of [probability distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_distributions) with the property that the sum of several random variables from such a distribution under appropriate scaling is distributed according to the same family. They are the discrete analogue of [continuous-stable distributions](wikipedia://en/1117869).

:::

Discrete-stable distributions have been used in numerous fields, in particular in [scale-free networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale-free_networks) such as the [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) and [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_networks)[^o3] or even [semantic networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_network).[^o4]

Both discrete and continuous classes of stable distribution have properties such as [infinite divisibility](wikipedia://en/13588803), [power law](wikipedia://en/24522) tails, and [unimodality](wikipedia://en/1588509).

The most well-known discrete stable distribution is the special case of the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144).[^o5] It is the only discrete-stable distribution for which the [mean](wikipedia://en/9653) and all [higher-order moments](wikipedia://en/368684) are finite.

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The discrete-stable distributions are defined[^o8] through their [probability-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability-generating_function)

$$G(s| \nu,a)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty P(N| \nu,a)(1-s)^N = \exp(-a s^\nu).$$

In the above, $a>0$ is a scale parameter and $0<\nu\le1$ describes the power-law behaviour such that when $0<\nu<1$,

$$\lim_{N \to \infty}P(N|\nu,a) \sim \frac{1}{N^{\nu+1}}.$$

When $\nu=1$, the distribution becomes the familiar [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with the mean $a$.

The [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) of a discrete-stable distribution has the form[^o9]

$\varphi(t; a, \nu) = \exp \left[a \left( e^{it} - 1 \right)^\nu \right]$, with $a>0$ and $0<\nu\le1$.

Again, when $\nu=1$, the distribution becomes the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with mean $a$.

The original distribution is recovered through repeated differentiation of the generating function:

$$P(N|\nu,a)= \left.\frac{(-1)^N}{N!}\frac{d^NG(s|\nu,a)}{ds^N}\right|_{s=1}.$$

A [closed-form expression](wikipedia://en/585143) using elementary functions for the probability distribution of the discrete-stable distributions is not known except for in the Poisson case in which

$$\!P(N| \nu=1, a)= \frac{a^N e^{-a}}{N!}.$$

Expressions exist, however, that use [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) for the case $\nu=1/2$[^o10] (in terms of [Bessel functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_functions)) and $\nu=1/3$[^o11] (in terms of [hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_functions)).

:::

## As compound probability distributions {#As_compound_probability_distributions}

The entire class of discrete-stable distributions can be formed as Poisson [compound probability distribution](wikipedia://en/28897820) where the mean, $\lambda$, of a Poisson distribution is defined as a random variable with a [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (PDF). When the PDF of the mean is a one-sided [continuous-stable distribution](wikipedia://en/1117869) with the stability parameter $0 < \alpha < 1$ and scale parameter $c$, the resultant distribution is[^o14] discrete-stable with index $\nu = \alpha$ and scale parameter $a = c \sec( \alpha \pi / 2)$.

Formally, this is written

$$P(N| \alpha, c \sec( \alpha \pi / 2)) = \int_0^\infty P(N| 1, \lambda)p(\lambda; \alpha, 1, c, 0) \, d\lambda$$

where $p(x; \alpha, 1, c, 0)$ is the pdf of a one-sided continuous-stable distribution with symmetry parameter $\beta=1$ and location parameter $\mu = 0$.

A more general result[^o11] states that forming a compound distribution from any discrete-stable distribution with index $\nu$ with a one-sided continuous-stable distribution with index $\alpha$ results in a discrete-stable distribution with index $\nu \cdot \alpha$ and reduces the power-law index of the original distribution by a factor of $\alpha$.

In other words,

$$P(N| \nu \cdot \alpha, c \sec(\pi \alpha / 2)) = \int_0^\infty P(N| \alpha, \lambda)p(\lambda; \nu, 1, c, 0) \, d\lambda.$$

## Poisson limit {#Poisson_limit}

In the limit $\nu \rightarrow 1$, the discrete-stable distributions behave[^o14] like a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with mean $a \sec(\nu \pi / 2)$ for small $N$, but for $N \gg 1$, the power-law tail dominates.

The convergence of i.i.d. random variates with power-law tails $P(N) \sim 1/N^{1 + \nu}$ to a discrete-stable distribution is extraordinarily slow[^o15] when $\nu \approx 1$, the limit being the Poisson distribution when $\nu > 1$ and $P(N| \nu, a)$ when $\nu \leq 1$.
