# Gordon Douglas Slade

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gordon Douglas Slade"}

**Gordon Douglas Slade** (born December 14, 1955, in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto)) is a Canadian mathematician, specializing in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Slade received in 1977 his bachelor's degree from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and in 1984 his PhD for research supervised by [Joel Feldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Feldman) and Lon Rosen at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia).

## Career and Research {#Career_and_Research}

As a [postdoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoc) he was a lecturer at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia). From 1986 he was at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University) and since 1999 he is a professor at the University of British Columbia.

He developed the technique of *lace expansion* (originally introduced by [David Brydges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Brydges) and [Thomas C. Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Spencer_%28mathematical_physicist%29) in 1985) with applications to probability theory and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), such as [self-avoiding random walks](wikipedia://en/11186496) and their enumeration, [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477), [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805), and branched polymers.

In 1989 Slade proved with Takashi Hara that the [Aizenman](wikipedia://en/22411175)–[Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_M._Newman) triangle condition at critical percolation is valid in sufficiently high dimension. The Hara–Slade result has important consequences in [mean field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_field_theory).[^o8]

In 1991 Slade and Hara used the lace expansion to prove that the average distance covered in self-avoiding random walks in 5 or more dimension grows as the square root of the number of steps and that the scaling limit is [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984).[^o9]

### Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Slade was an invited speaker in 1994 at the ICM in Zürich with lecture *The critical behaviour of random systems*.

Slade received in 1995 the [Coxeter–James Prize](wikipedia://en/18739444)[^o11] and in the 2010 the [CRM-Fields-PIMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRM-Fields-PIMS_Prize). He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Canada) (FRSC) in 2000,[^o12][^o13] in 2010 of the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768), and in 2012 of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics). He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2017.[^o14] In 2018 Slade was awarded the [Jeffery–Williams Prize](wikipedia://en/18739069).[^o15][^o16]

### Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- The lace expansion and its applications (École d’Eté de Probabilités de Saint-Flour XXXIV, 2004), Springer Verlag 2006[^o19]
