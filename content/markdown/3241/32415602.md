# Revolution Analytics

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Revolution Analytics"}

**Revolution Analytics** (formerly **REvolution Computing**) is a [statistical software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_software) company focused on developing open source and "open-core"[^o4] versions of the [free and open source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open_source_software) [R](wikipedia://en/376707) for enterprise, academic and analytics customers. Revolution Analytics was founded in 2007 as REvolution Computing providing support and services for R in a model similar to [Red Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hat)'s approach with Linux in the 1990s as well as bolt-on additions for parallel processing. In 2009 the company received nine million in [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital) from [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) along with a [private equity firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_equity_firm) and named [Norman H. Nie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_H._Nie) as their new CEO. In 2010 the company announced the name change as well as a change in focus. Their core product, Revolution R, would be offered free to academic users and their commercial software would focus on [big data](wikipedia://en/27051151), large scale multiprocessor (or "[high performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing)") computing, and multi-core functionality.

:::

[Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft) announced on January 23, 2015, that they had reached an agreement to [purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mergers_and_acquisitions_by_Microsoft) Revolution Analytics for an as yet undisclosed amount.[^o5][^o6] In 2021, Microsoft announced they would be retiring their R distribution they acquired from Revolution Analytics.[^o7] In 2023, Microsoft retired the Microsoft R Application Network, which was a proprietary package hosting service similar to the [Comprehensive R Archive Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_R_Archive_Network) for packages acquired from Revolution Analytics (like "ScaleR").[^o8]

## Founding and venture capital {#Founding_and_venture_capital}

REvolution Computing was founded in [New Haven, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut) in 2007 by Richard Schultz, Martin Schultz, Steve Weston and Kirk Mettler. At the time Martin Schultz was also the Watson Professor of Computer Science at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).[^o10][^o11] Adding [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing) to R allowed the company to net large gains in speed for many common analytics operations and early clients like [Pfizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizer) took advantage of REvolution R to see large performance gains using R on computing clusters.[^o12] While the improvements to core R were released under the [GNU General Public License](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_General_Public_License) (GPL), REvolution provides support and services to customers of their commercial product and had considerable early success with life sciences and pharmaceutical companies.[^o13][^o14] A year later the company opened an additional office in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle).[^o15]

In 2009 REvolution Computing accepted nine million dollars in venture capital from Intel and North Bridge Venture Partners, a private equity firm. Intel had previously supported REvolution Computing with venture capital in 2008.[^o16] A number of Intel employees also joined Revolution Analytics as employees or as advisors.[^o12] Concurrently, the company changed their name to Revolution Analytics and invited Norman Nie, founder of [SPSS](wikipedia://en/179088), to serve as CEO.[^o17][^o18] This change in management corresponded with a movement toward building a more complete set of software for commercial users; prior to 2009 Revolution had been focused on building parallel processing functionality into the then mostly single threaded R.[^o19] David Rich replaced Norman Nie as CEO in February 2012.[^o20]

## High performance computing, big data and the shift to analytics {#High_performance_computing,_big_data_and_the_shift_to_analytics}

Unlike analytics products offered by [SAS Institute](wikipedia://en/194872), R does not natively handle datasets larger than main memory. In 2010 Revolution Analytics introduced ScaleR, a package for Revolution R Enterprise designed to handle big data through a high-performance disk-based data store called XDF (not related to IBM's [Extensible Data Format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extensible_Data_Format)) and high performance computing across large clusters.[^o22] The release of ScaleR marked a push away from consulting and services alone to custom code and [a la carte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_la_carte) package pricing.[^o23] ScaleR also works with [Apache Hadoop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_Hadoop) and other distributed file systems and Revolution Analytics has partnered with IBM to further integrate Hadoop into Revolution R.[^o24][^o25] Packages to integrate Hadoop and MapReduce into open source R can also be found on the community package repository, CRAN.[^o26][^o27]

## Market position {#Market_position}

In comparison to developers of similar analytics tools, Revolution Analytics is a small company; in 2010 the company had a projected revenue of \$8–11 million, but no official records of revenue or profit were published in their projections.[^o29] According to Nie, the increased use of R - a fully fledged programming language, in contrast to other analytics packages - within academia is helping the company to grow quickly.[^o30][^o31][^o32][^o33][^o34] Community vice president David Smith suggested that movement away from "[black box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box)" analytics toward open source tools in general supported vendors like Revolution over solely proprietary tools.[^o35]

## Products {#Products}

Revolution Analytics' product Revolution R is available in three editions. Revolution R Open is a free and open source distribution of R with additional features for performance and reproducibility. Revolution R Plus provides technical support and open-source assurance (legal indemnification) subscriptions for Revolution R Open and other open-source components that work with R. (These products were first announced October 15, 2014.[^o38]) Revolution R Enterprise adds proprietary components to support statistical analysis of [Big Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Data), and is sold as subscriptions for workstations, servers, Hadoop and databases. (Single-user licenses are available free for academic users as well as users competing in [Kaggle](wikipedia://en/31663650) [data mining](wikipedia://en/42253) competitions.[^o39][^o40])

In January 2015 Microsoft rebranded and renewed several Revolution Analytics products and offerings for [Hadoop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadoop), [Teradata Database](wikipedia://en/168680), [SUSE Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUSE_Linux), [Red Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hat_Linux), and [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows). Microsoft made several of these R-based products free of charge for developers - these products included:

- **Microsoft R Server** which was previously called *Revolution R Enterprise for Hadoop, Linux and Teradata* and included new Microsoft enterprise support and purchasing options. Microsoft R Server was further made available to students through the Microsoft DreamSpark programme.
- **Microsoft R Server Developer Edition** a free version for developers that with a feature set akin to the commercial edition.
- **Microsoft Data Science Virtual Machine** an analytics tool developed by the Revolution Analytics division premiered in January 2015.
- **Microsoft R Open** a rebranded version of *Revolution R Open*.[^o41][^o42][^o43]
