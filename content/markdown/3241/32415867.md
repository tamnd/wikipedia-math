# Andrei Zelevinsky

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrei Zelevinsky"}

**Andrei Vladlenovich Zelevinsky** (Андрей Владленович Зелевинский; 30 January 1953 – 10 April 2013)[^o3] was a Russian-American mathematician who made important contributions to [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), among other areas.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Zelevinsky graduated in 1969 from the Moscow Mathematical School No. 2.[^o5] After winning a silver medal as a member of the USSR team at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208)[^o6] he was admitted without examination to the mathematics department of [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) where he obtained his PhD in 1978 under the mentorship of [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383), [Alexandre Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515) and [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643).[^o7]

He worked[^o8] in the mathematical laboratory of [Vladimir Keilis-Borok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Keilis-Borok) at the Institute of Earth Science (1977–85), and at the Council for Cybernetics of the Soviet Academy of Sciences (1985–90). In the early 1980s, at a great personal risk, he taught at the [Jewish People's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_People's_University),[^o9] an unofficial organization offering first-class mathematics education to talented students denied admission to Moscow State University's math department.

In 1990–91, Zelevinsky was a visiting professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), and from 1991 until his death was on faculty at [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University), Boston. With his wife, Galina, he had a son and a daughter; he also had several grandchildren.[^o10]

Zelevinsky is a relative of the physicists [Vladimir Zelevinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Zelevinsky) and [Tanya Zelevinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanya_Zelevinsky).

## Research {#Research}

Zelevinsky's most notable achievement is the discovery (with [Sergey Fomin](wikipedia://en/33046302)) of [cluster algebras](wikipedia://en/27038423). His other contributions include:

- [Bernstein–Zelevinsky classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein–Zelevinsky_classification) of representations of [*p*-adic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_group);
- Introduction (jointly with [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) and [Mikhail Kapranov](wikipedia://en/62272076)) of A-systems of hypergeometric equations (also known as GKZ-systems)[^o12] and development of the theory of [hyperdeterminants](wikipedia://en/15397886);[^o13][^o14]
- generalization of the [Littlewood–Richardson rule](wikipedia://en/16004359) and [Robinson–Schensted correspondence](wikipedia://en/1901026) using the combinatorics of ["pictures"](wikipedia://en/22358224);
- work (jointly with [Arkady Berenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkady_Berenstein) and [Sergey Fomin](wikipedia://en/33046302)) on [total positivity](wikipedia://en/2097524);
- work (with Sergey Fomin) on the [Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Alphonse_Laurent) phenomenon, including its applications to [Somos sequences](wikipedia://en/24612539).
