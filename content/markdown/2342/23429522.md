# Corner-point grid

*Image: TwoCells.png.* A trivial example of a Corner-point grid with only two cells.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Corner-point grid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **corner-point grid** is a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of a [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697) 3D volume, where the base [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) has 6 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) ([hexahedron](wikipedia://en/237209)).

:::

A set of straight lines defined by their end points define the *pillars* of the corner-point grid. The pillars have a [lexicographical ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicographical_ordering) that determines neighbouring pillars. On each pillar, a constant number of nodes (corner-points) is defined. A corner-point cell is now the volume between 4 neighbouring pillars and two neighbouring points on each pillar.

Each cell can be identified by integer coordinates $(i,j,k)$, where the $k$ coordinate runs along the pillars, and $i$ and $j$ span each layer. The cells are ordered naturally, where the index $i$ runs the fastest and $k$ the slowest.

Data within the interior of such cells can be computed by [trilinear interpolation](wikipedia://en/674582) from the boundary values at the 8 corners, 12 edges, and 6 faces.

In the special case of all pillars being vertical, the top and bottom face of each corner-point cell are described by [bilinear surfaces](wikipedia://en/674484) and the side faces are [planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29).

Corner-point grids are supported by most [reservoir simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_simulation) software, and has become an industry standard.

## Degeneracy {#Degeneracy}

A main feature of the format is the ability to define [erosion surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion_surface) in [geological modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_modelling), effectively done by collapsing nodes along each pillar. This means that the corner-point cells degenerate and may have less than 6 faces.

For the corner-point grids, non-neighboring connections are supported, meaning that grid cells that are not neighboring in ijk-space can be defined as neighboring. This feature allows for representation of faults with significant throw/displacement. Moreover, the neighboring grid cells do not need to have matching cell faces (just overlap).
