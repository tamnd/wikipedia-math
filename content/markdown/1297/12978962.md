# Plane table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plane table"}

A **plane table** (**plain table** prior to 1830)[^o4] is a device used in [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying), site mapping, exploration mapping, coastal navigation mapping, and related disciplines to provide a solid and level surface on which to make field drawings, charts and maps. The early use of the name *plain table* reflected its simplicity and plainness rather than its flatness.[^o8] "Plane" refers to the table being both flat and levelled ([horizontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_plane)).

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: US Army Corps of Engineers Map Making, World War I.jpg.* Using a plane table

The earliest mention of a plane table dates to 1551 in [Abel Foullon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Foullon)'s *"Usage et description de l'holomètre"*, published in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o3] However, since Foullon's description was of a complete, fully developed instrument, it must have been invented earlier.[^o8]

A brief description was also added to the 1591 edition of Digge's *Pantometria*.[^o3] The first mention of the device in English was by [Cyprian Lucar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprian_Lucar) in 1590.[^o4]

The Swiss [instrument maker](wikipedia://en/79892249) [Leonhard Zubler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Zubler) published an account of the plane table in *Novum instrumentum geometricum*, 1607. He used the term *Instrumentum Chorographicum* crediting its invention to [Philipp Eberhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Eberhard), a fellow Swiss instrument maker based in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich).[^o5]

Some have credited Johann Richter, also known as [Johannes Praetorius](wikipedia://en/14447712),[^o6] a [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) mathematician, in 1610[^o7] with the first plane table, but this appears to be incorrect.

The plane table became a popular instrument for [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying).[^o8] Its use was widely taught. Some considered it a [substandard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substandard) instrument compared to other devices such as the [theodolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodolite), since it was relatively easy to use.[^o4] By allowing the use of graphical methods rather than mathematical calculations, it could be used by those with less education than other instruments. The addition of a camera to the plane table, as was done from 1890 by [Sebastian Finsterwalder](wikipedia://en/41045441) in conjunction with a [phototheodolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phototheodolite), established [photogrammetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photogrammetry) in spatial and temporal surveying.

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Plane table cgs00426.jpg.* A plane table with part of the surface cut away to show the mounting on the tripod. The mount allows the table to be levelled. On the table, the [alidade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alidade) with telescopic sight is seen.

A plane table consists of a smooth table surface mounted on a sturdy base. The connection between the table top and the base permits one to level the table precisely, using [bubble levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_level), in a [horizontal plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_plane). The base, a tripod, is designed to support the table over a specific point on land. By adjusting the length of the legs, one can bring the table level regardless of the roughness of the terrain.

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

In use, a plane table is set over a point and brought to precise horizontal level. A drawing sheet is attached to the surface and an [alidade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alidade) is used to sight objects of interest. The alidade, in modern examples of the instrument a [rule](wikipedia://en/65593) with a [telescopic sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescopic_sight), can then be used to construct a line on the drawing that is in the direction of the object of interest.

By using the alidade as a [surveying level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_%28instrument%29), information on the [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) of the site can be directly recorded on the drawing as elevations. Distances to the objects can be measured directly or by the use of [stadia marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadia_mark) in the telescope of the alidade.
