# Alfred Goldie

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Goldie"}

**Alfred William Goldie** (10 December 1920, Coseley, [Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire) – 8 October 2005, [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow-in-Furness), [Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria)) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Goldie was educated at [Wolverhampton Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Grammar_School) and then read [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). His studies were interrupted by war work on [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics) with the Armament Research Department of the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply), eventually taking his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1942 and [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1946.

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Goldie became an assistant [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) in 1946. In 1948 he was appointed lecturer in [Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Mathematics) at what was then [King's College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleges_of_Durham_University) (and has been the [University of Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University) since 1963) where he was promoted to [senior lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_lecturer) in 1958 and [reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) in 1960.

In 1963 Goldie was appointed [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Mathematics) at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds). He retired from his chair in 1986 with the title [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor).[^o4]

Goldie won the 1970 [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Berwick_Prize) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), where he also served as vice-president from 1978 to 1980.

Goldie worked in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), where he introduced the notion of the [uniform dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_dimension) of a module, and the [reduced rank](wikipedia://en/31553078) of a module. He is well known for [Goldie's theorem](wikipedia://en/7738492), which characterizes [right Goldie rings](wikipedia://en/7738492). Indeed, his *[Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* obituary described him as the "Lord of the Rings".[^o5]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Goldie married Mary Kenyon in 1944. They had three children (from eldest to youngest): Isobel (Carlyle), Helen, and John. Both Isobel and Helen completed PhDs in Geography, while John completed a degree in music. Mary died in 1995 and in 2002 he married Margaret Turner, who survived him.[^o5]
