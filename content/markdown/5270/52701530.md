# Xu Yue (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Xu Yue"}

**Xu Yue** was a second-century mathematician born in [Donglai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donglai_Commandery), in present-day [Shandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong) province, [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China). Little is known of his life except that he was a student of [Liu Hong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Hong_%28astronomer%29), an astronomer, and mathematician in second-century China, and had frequent discussions with the Astronomer-Royal of the Astronomical Bureau.[^o2]

:::

## Works {#Works}

Xu Yue wrote a commentary on *[Nine Chapters on Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207)* and a treatise, *[Notes on Traditions of Arithmetic Methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shushu_Jiyi)*. The commentary has been lost, but his own work has survived with a commentary from [Zhen Luan](wikipedia://en/37332061).

*Notes on Traditions of Arithmetic Methods* mentions 14 old methods of calculation. This book was a prescribed mathematical text for the Imperial examinations in 656 and became one of *[The Ten Mathematical Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ten_Computational_Canons)* (算经十书) in 1084.[^o4]
