# Geometric quotient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric quotient"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **geometric quotient** of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *X* with the action of an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) *G* is a [morphism of varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_of_varieties) $\pi: X \to Y$ such that[^o2]

:::

(i) The map $\pi$ is surjective, and its fibers are exactly the G-orbits in X.

(ii) The topology of *Y* is the [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology): a subset $U \subset Y$ is open if and only if $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is open.

(iii) For any open subset $U \subset Y$, $\pi^{\#}: k[U] \to k[\pi^{-1}(U)]^G$ is an isomorphism. (Here, *k* is the base field.)

The notion appears in [geometric invariant theory](wikipedia://en/1639003). (i), (ii) say that *Y* is an [orbit space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_space) of *X* in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). (iii) may also be phrased as an isomorphism of sheaves $\mathcal{O}_Y \simeq \pi_*(\mathcal{O}_X^G)$. In particular, if *X* is irreducible, then so is *Y* and $k(Y) = k(X)^G$: rational functions on *Y* may be viewed as invariant rational functions on *X* (i.e., [rational-invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational-invariant) of *X*).

For example, if *H* is a closed subgroup of *G*, then $G/H$ is a geometric quotient. A [GIT quotient](wikipedia://en/41139312) may or may not be a geometric quotient: but both are categorical quotients, which is unique; in other words, one cannot have both types of quotients (without them being the same).

## Relation to other quotients {#Relation_to_other_quotients}

A geometric quotient is a [categorical quotient](wikipedia://en/39561038). This is proved in Mumford's geometric invariant theory.

A geometric quotient is precisely a [good quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_quotient) whose fibers are orbits of the group.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The canonical map $\mathbb{A}^{n+1} \setminus 0 \to \mathbb{P}^n$ is a geometric quotient.
- If *L* is a [linearized line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearized_line_bundle) on an algebraic *G*-variety *X*, then, writing $X^s_{(0)}$ for the set of [stable points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_point) with respect to *L*, the quotient

$$X^s_{(0)} \to X^s_{(0)}/G$$

is a geometric quotient.

:::
