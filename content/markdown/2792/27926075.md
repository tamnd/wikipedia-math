# Gauss–Bonnet gravity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gauss–Bonnet gravity"}

Gauss–Bonnet theorem In [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), **Gauss–Bonnet gravity**, also referred to as **Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet gravity**,[^o2] is a modification of the [Einstein–Hilbert action](wikipedia://en/855776) to include the [Gauss–Bonnet term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Gauss–Bonnet_theorem)[^o3] (named after [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) and [Pierre Ossian Bonnet](wikipedia://en/703244))

:::

$$\int d^Dx \sqrt{-g}\, G,$$

where

$$G= R^2 - 4R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} + R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.$$

This term is only nontrivial in 4+1D or greater, and as such, only applies to extra dimensional models. In 3+1D, it reduces to a topological [surface term](wikipedia://en/151864). This follows from the [generalized Gauss–Bonnet theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Gauss–Bonnet_theorem) on a 4D manifold

$$\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\, G = \chi(M).$$

In lower dimensions, it identically vanishes.

Despite being quadratic in the [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor) (and [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor)), terms containing more than 2 partial derivatives of the [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) cancel out, making the [Euler–Lagrange equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Lagrange_equations) [second order](wikipedia://en/52564#Linear_equations_of_second_order) [quasilinear](wikipedia://en/1424309) [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) in the metric. Consequently, there are no additional dynamical degrees of freedom, as in say [f(R) gravity](wikipedia://en/19989435).

Gauss–Bonnet gravity has also been shown to be connected to [classical electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/4603176) by means of complete gauge invariance with respect to [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159).[^o4]

More generally, we may consider a

$$\int d^Dx \sqrt{-g}\, f\left( G \right)$$

term for some function *f*. Nonlinearities in *f* render this coupling nontrivial even in 3+1D. Therefore, fourth order terms reappear with the nonlinearities.
