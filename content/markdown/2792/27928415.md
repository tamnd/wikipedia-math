# Felix Gantmacher

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Felix Gantmacher"}

**Felix Ruvimovich Gantmacher** (Феликс Рувимович Гантмахер) (23 February 1908 – 16 May 1964) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), professor at [Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology), well known for his contributions in mechanics, [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) theory. In 1925–1926 he participated in seminar guided by [Nikolai Chebotaryov](wikipedia://en/3033696) in Odessa and wrote his first research paper in 1926.

:::

His book *Theory of Matrices* (1953) is a standard reference of linear algebra. It has been translated into various languages including a two-volume version in English prepared by [Joel Lee Brenner](wikipedia://en/38091021), Donald W. Bushaw, and S. Evanusa.[^o3][^o4] [George Herbert Weiss](wikipedia://en/43489615) noted that "this book cannot be recommended too highly as it contains material otherwise unavailable in book form".[^o5]

Gantmacher collaborated with [Mark Krein](wikipedia://en/1190470) on *Oscillation Matrices and Kernels and Small Vibrations of Mechanical Systems*.[^o6]

In 1939 he contributed to the classification problem of the real [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).[^o7]

In the same year he wrote on [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of [complex Lie groups](wikipedia://en/2187847).[^o8]

His son [Vsevolod Gantmacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vsevolod_Gantmakher) was a noted physicist.
