# Dehn–Sommerville equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dehn–Sommerville equations"}

In mathematics, the **Dehn–Sommerville equations** are a complete set of linear relations between the numbers of faces of different dimension of a [simplicial polytope](wikipedia://en/8853373). For polytopes of dimension 4 and 5, they were found by [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602) in 1905. Their general form was established by [Duncan Sommerville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_MacLaren_Young_Sommerville) in 1927. The Dehn–Sommerville equations can be restated as a symmetry condition for the [*h*-vector](wikipedia://en/11503563) of the simplicial polytope and this has become the standard formulation in recent combinatorics literature. By duality, analogous equations hold for [simple polytopes](wikipedia://en/8853286).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *P* be a *d*-dimensional [simplicial polytope](wikipedia://en/8853373). For *i* = 0, 1, ..., *d* − 1, let *f*<sub>*i*</sub> denote the number of *i*-dimensional [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) of *P*. The sequence

$$f(P)=(f_0,f_1,\ldots,f_{d-1})$$

is called the ***f*-vector** of the polytope *P*. Additionally, set

$$f_{-1}=1, f_d=1.$$

Then for any *k* = −1, 0, ..., *d* − 2, the following **Dehn–Sommerville equation** holds:

$$\sum_{j=k}^{d-1} (-1)^j \binom{j+1}{k+1} f_j = (-1)^{d-1}f_k.$$

When *k* = −1, it expresses the fact that [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of a (*d* − 1)-dimensional [simplicial sphere](wikipedia://en/22359636) is equal to 1 + (−1)<sup>*d* − 1</sup>.

Dehn–Sommerville equations with different *k* are not independent. There are several ways to choose a maximal independent subset consisting of $\left[\frac{d+1}{2}\right]$ equations. If *d* is even then the equations with *k* = 0, 2, 4, ..., *d* − 2 are independent. Another independent set consists of the equations with *k* = −1, 1, 3, ..., *d* − 3. If *d* is odd then the equations with *k* = −1, 1, 3, ..., *d* − 2 form one independent set and the equations with *k* = −1, 0, 2, 4, ..., *d* − 3 form another.

:::

## Equivalent formulations {#Equivalent_formulations}

Sommerville found a different way to state these equations:

$$\sum_{i=-1}^{k-1}(-1)^{d+i}\binom{d-i-1}{d-k} f_i = \sum_{i=-1}^{d-k-1}(-1)^i \binom{d-i-1}{k} f_i,$$

where 0 ≤ k ≤ $\tfrac{1}{2}$(d−1). This can be further facilitated introducing the notion of *h*-vector of *P*. For *k* = 0, 1, ..., *d*, let

$$h_k = \sum_{i=0}^k (-1)^{k-i}\binom{d-i}{k-i}f_{i-1}.$$

The sequence

$$h(P)=(h_0,h_1,\ldots,h_d)$$

is called the [*h*-vector](wikipedia://en/11503563) of *P*. The *f*-vector and the *h*-vector uniquely determine each other through the relation

$$\sum_{i=0}^d f_{i-1}(t-1)^{d-i}=\sum_{k=0}^d h_k t^{d-k}.$$

Then the Dehn–Sommerville equations can be restated simply as

$$h_k = h_{d-k} \quad\text{ for } 0\leq k\leq d.$$

The equations with 0 ≤ k ≤ $\tfrac{1}{2}$(d−1) are independent, and the others are manifestly equivalent to them.

[Richard Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397) gave an interpretation of the components of the *h*-vector of a simplicial convex polytope *P* in terms of the [projective](wikipedia://en/320469) [toric variety](wikipedia://en/737087) *X* associated with (the dual of) *P*. Namely, they are the dimensions of the even [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology) groups of *X*:

$$h_k=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{IH}^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})$$

(the odd [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology) groups of *X* are all zero). In this language, the last form of the Dehn–Sommerville equations, the symmetry of the *h*-vector, is a manifestation of the [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) in the intersection cohomology of *X*.
