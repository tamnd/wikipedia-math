# Midpoint-stretching polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Midpoint-stretching polygon"}

*Image: Midpoint stretching polygon.svg.* A [cyclic polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon) (green), its [midpoint polygon](wikipedia://en/33456840) (red), and its midpoint-stretching polygon (pink)

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **midpoint-stretching polygon** of a [cyclic polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon) $P$ is another cyclic polygon inscribed in the same circle, the polygon whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) are the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the [circular arcs](wikipedia://en/5578920) between the vertices of $P$.[^o2] It may be derived from the [midpoint polygon](wikipedia://en/33456840) of $P$ (the polygon whose vertices are the edge midpoints) by placing the polygon in such a way that the circle's center coincides with the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432), and stretching or normalizing the vector representing each vertex of the midpoint polygon to make it have [unit length](wikipedia://en/167053).

:::

## Musical application {#Musical_application}

The midpoint-stretching polygon is also called the **shadow** of $P$; when the circle is used to describe a repetitive [time sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_sequence) and the polygon vertices on it represent the onsets of a [drum beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_beat), the shadow represents the set of times when the drummer's hands are highest, and has greater [rhythmic evenness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_evenness) than the original rhythm.[^o4]

## Convergence to regularity {#Convergence_to_regularity}

The midpoint-stretching polygon of a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) is itself regular, and iterating the midpoint-stretching operation on an arbitrary initial polygon results in a sequence of polygons whose shape converges to that of a regular polygon.[^o2][^o6]
