# David Masser

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Masser"}

**David William Masser** (born 8 November 1948) is [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) in the Department of Mathematics and Computer Science at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel).[^o3] He is known for his work in [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698), [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), and [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205). With [Joseph Oesterlé](wikipedia://en/1604631) in 1985, Masser formulated the [abc conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108), which has been called "the most important unsolved problem in [Diophantine analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_analysis)".[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Masser was born on 8 November 1948 in London, England.[^o6] He graduated from [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) with a B.A. (Hons) in 1970.[^o6] In 1974, he obtained his M.A. and Ph.D. at the University of Cambridge,[^o6] with a doctoral thesis under the supervision of [Alan Baker](wikipedia://en/18950800) titled *Elliptic Functions and Transcendence*.

## Career {#Career}

Masser was a Lecturer at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) from 1973 to 1975, before spending the 1975–1976 year as a Research Fellow of Trinity College at the University of Cambridge.[^o6] He returned to the University of Nottingham to serve as a Lecturer from 1976 to 1979 and then as a Reader from 1979 to 1983.[^o6] He was a professor at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) from 1983 to 1992.[^o6] He then moved to the Mathematics Institute at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel) and became emeritus there in 2014.[^o3][^o6][^o12]

## Research {#Research}

Masser's research focuses on [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698), [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), and [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205).[^o12] The *abc* conjecture originated as the outcome of attempts by Oesterlé and Masser to understand the [Szpiro conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szpiro_conjecture) about [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).[^o10]

## Awards {#Awards}

Masser was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in Warsaw in 1983.[^o6][^o12] In 1991, he received the [Humboldt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize).[^o6] He was elected as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2005.[^o6][^o12] In 2014, he was elected as a Member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea).[^o6][^o12]
