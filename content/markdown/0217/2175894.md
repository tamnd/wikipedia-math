# Vojtěch Jarník

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Vojtěch Jarník"}

**Vojtěch Jarník** (; 22 December 1897 – 22 September 1970) was a [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He worked for many years as a professor and administrator at [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University), and helped found the [Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Academy_of_Sciences). He is the namesake of [Jarník's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarník's_algorithm) for [minimum spanning trees](wikipedia://en/41795).

:::

Jarník worked in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), and [graph algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_algorithm). He has been called "probably the first Czechoslovak mathematician whose scientific works received wide and lasting international response".[^o25] As well as developing Jarník's algorithm, he found tight bounds on the number of [integer lattice points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_lattice_point) on [convex curves](wikipedia://en/29747989), studied the relationship between the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) of sets of real numbers and how well they can be [approximated by rational numbers](wikipedia://en/251558), and investigated the properties of [nowhere-differentiable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_function).

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Jarník was born on 22 December 1897. He was the son of Jan Urban Jarník, a professor of [Romance language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_language) [philology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology) at [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University),[^o4] and his older brother, Hertvík Jarník, also became a professor of linguistics.[^o5] Despite this background, Jarník learned no Latin at his gymnasium (the C.K. české vyšší reálné gymnasium, Ječná, Prague), so when he entered Charles University in 1915 he had to do so as an extraordinary student until he could pass a Latin examination three semesters later.[^o5]

He studied mathematics and physics at Charles University from 1915 to 1919, with [Karel Petr](wikipedia://en/4436275) as a mentor. After completing his studies, he became an assistant to Jan Vojtěch at the [Brno University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno_University_of_Technology), where he also met [Mathias Lerch](wikipedia://en/4436105).[^o5] In 1921 he completed a doctoral degree (RNDr.) at Charles University with a dissertation on [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700) supervised by Petr,[^o5] then returned to Charles University as Petr's assistant.[^o5][^o25]

While keeping his position at Charles University, he studied with [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) at the University of Göttingen from 1923 to 1925 and again from 1927 to 1929.[^o7] On his first return to Charles University he defended his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation), and on his return from the second visit, he was given a chair in mathematics as an extraordinary professor. He was promoted to full professor in 1935 and later served as Dean of Sciences (1947–1948) and Vice-Rector (1950–1953). He retired in 1968.[^o25][^o6]

Jarník supervised the dissertations of 16 doctoral students. Notable among these are [Miroslav Katětov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Katětov), a [chess master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_master) who became rector of Charles University, [Jaroslav Kurzweil](wikipedia://en/1485550), known for the [Henstock–Kurzweil integral](wikipedia://en/412708), Czech number theorist [Bohuslav Diviš](wikipedia://en/64704315), and Slovak mathematician [Tibor Šalát](wikipedia://en/9789033).[^o5][^o8]

He died on 22 September 1970, at the age of 72.[^o25]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Although Jarník's 1921 dissertation,[^o25] like some of his later publications, was in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), his main area of work was in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He studied the [Gauss circle problem](wikipedia://en/19474055) and proved a number of results on [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), [integer lattice point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_lattice_point) problems, and the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153).[^o6] He also made pioneering, but long-neglected, contributions to [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555).[^o21]

### Number theory {#Number_theory}

*Image: Convex curve through 13 grid points.svg.* A convex curve through 13 integer lattice points

The [Gauss circle problem](wikipedia://en/19474055) asks for the number of points of the [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402) enclosed by a given [circle](wikipedia://en/6220). One of Jarník's theorems (1926), related to this problem, is that any closed strictly [convex curve](wikipedia://en/29747989) with length $L$ passes through at most

$$\frac{3}{\sqrt[3]{2\pi}}L^{2/3}+O(L^{1/3})$$

points of the integer lattice. The $O$ in this formula is an instance of [Big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578). Neither the exponent of $L$ nor the leading constant of this bound can be improved, as there exist convex curves with this many grid points.[^o11][^o12]

Another theorem of Jarník in this area shows that, for any closed convex curve in the plane with a well-defined length, the [absolute difference](wikipedia://en/28021681) between the area it encloses and the number of integer points it encloses is at most its length.[^o13]

Jarník also published several results in [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), the study of the approximation of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) by [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). He proved (1928–1929) that the badly approximable real numbers (the ones with bounded terms in their [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199)) have [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) one. This is the same dimension as the set of all real numbers, intuitively suggesting that the set of badly approximable numbers is large. He also considered the numbers $x$ for which there exist infinitely many good rational approximations $p/q$, with

$$\left| x-\frac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{q^k}$$

for a given exponent $k > 2$, and proved (1929) that these have the smaller Hausdorff dimension $2/k$. The second of these results was later rediscovered by [Besicovitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abram_Samoilovitch_Besicovitch).[^o14] Besicovitch used different methods than Jarník to prove it, and the result has come to be known as the Jarník–Besicovitch theorem.[^o15]

### Mathematical analysis {#Mathematical_analysis}

Jarník's work in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478) was sparked by finding, in the unpublished works of [Bernard Bolzano](wikipedia://en/302185), a definition of a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) that was nowhere [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206). Bolzano's 1830 discovery predated the 1872 publication of the [Weierstrass function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_function), previously considered to be the first example of such a function. Based on his study of Bolzano's function, Jarník was led to a more general theorem: If a [real-valued function](wikipedia://en/22284647) of a [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) does not have [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) in any subinterval, then there is a dense subset of its domain on which at least one of its [Dini derivatives](wikipedia://en/2163404) is infinite. This applies in particular to the nowhere-differentiable functions, as they must have unbounded variation in all intervals. Later, after learning of a result by [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) and [Stefan Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203) that [generic functions](wikipedia://en/12886758) (that is, the members of a [residual set](wikipedia://en/226975) of functions) are nowhere differentiable, Jarník proved that at almost all points, all four Dini derivatives of such a function are infinite. Much of his later work in this area concerned extensions of these results to approximate derivatives.[^o17]

### Combinatorial optimization {#Combinatorial_optimization}

*Image: PrimAlgDemo.gif.* Animation of [Jarník's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarník's_algorithm) for [minimum spanning trees](wikipedia://en/41795)

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555), Jarník is known for an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) for constructing [minimum spanning trees](wikipedia://en/41795) that he published in 1930, in response to the publication of [Borůvka's algorithm](wikipedia://en/197253) by another Czech mathematician, [Otakar Borůvka](wikipedia://en/2923087).[^o19] [Jarník's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarník's_algorithm) builds a tree from a single starting vertex of a given [weighted graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_graph) by repeatedly adding the cheapest connection to any other vertex, until all vertices have been connected. The same algorithm was later rediscovered in the late 1950s by [Robert C. Prim](wikipedia://en/3162129) and [Edsger W. Dijkstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsger_W._Dijkstra). It is also known as Prim's algorithm or the Prim–Dijkstra algorithm.[^o20]

He also published a second, related, paper with Miloš Kössler (1934) on the Euclidean [Steiner tree problem](wikipedia://en/498304). In this problem, one must again form a tree connecting a given set of points, with edge costs given by the [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932). However, additional points that are not part of the input may be added to make the overall tree shorter. This paper is the first serious treatment of the general Steiner tree problem (although it appears earlier in a letter by [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)), and it already contains "virtually all general properties of Steiner trees" later attributed to other researchers.[^o21]

## Recognition and legacy {#Recognition_and_legacy}

Jarník was a member of the Czech Academy of Sciences and Arts, from 1934 as an extraordinary member and from 1946 as a regular member.[^o25] In 1952 he became one of the founding members of [Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o25][^o6] He was also awarded the Czechoslovak State Prize in 1952.[^o25]

*Image: Jarníkova koláž.jpg.* Jarníkova Street, the Jarníkova bus stop, and a commemorative sign honoring Jarník

The Vojtěch Jarník International Mathematical Competition, held each year since 1991 in [Ostrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrava), is named in his honor,[^o23] as is Jarníkova Street in the [Chodov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chodov_%28Prague%29) district of [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague). A series of [postage stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamp) published by Czechoslovakia in 1987 to honor the 125th anniversary of the [Union of Czechoslovak mathematicians and physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Czech_mathematicians_and_physicists) included one stamp featuring Jarník together with [Joseph Petzval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Petzval) and [Vincenc Strouhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenc_Strouhal).[^o24]

A conference was held in Prague, in March 1998, to honor the centennial of his birth.[^o25]

Since 2002, ceremonial *Jarník's lecture* is held every year at [Faculty of Mathematics and Physics, Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Mathematics_and_Physics,_Charles_University), in a lecture hall named after him.[^o26]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

Jarník published 90 papers in mathematics,[^o28] including:

- Jarník, Vojtěch. (1923). "O číslech derivovaných funkcí jedné reálné proměnné". *Časopis Pro Pěstování Matematiky a Fysiky*. 53. p. 98–101. doi:10.21136/CPMF.1924.109353. A function with unbounded variation in all intervals has a dense set of points where a Dini derivative is infinite.[^o17]
- Jarník, Vojtěch. (1926). "Über die Gitterpunkte auf konvexen Kurven". *Mathematische Zeitschrift*. 24 (1). p. 500–518. doi:10.1007/BF01216795. mr:1544776. Tight bounds on the number of integer points on a convex curve, as a function of its length.
- Jarník, Vojtĕch. (1928–1929). "Zur metrischen Theorie der diophantischen Approximationen". *Prace Matematyczno-Fizyczne*. 36. Warszawa. p. 91–106. <http://dml.cz/handle/10338.dmlcz/500716>. The badly-approximable numbers have Hausdorff dimension one.[^o14]
- Jarník, Vojtĕch. (1929). "Diophantische Approximationen und Hausdorffsches Maß". *Matematicheskii Sbornik*. 36. p. 371–382. <http://dml.cz/handle/10338.dmlcz/500710>. The well-approximable numbers have Hausdorff dimension less than one.[^o14]
- Jarník, Vojtěch. (1930). "O jistém problému minimálním. (Z dopisu panu O. Borůvkovi)". *Práce Moravské Přírodovědecké Společnosti*. 6. p. 57–63. <http://dml.cz/handle/10338.dmlcz/500725>. The original reference for [Jarnik's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarnik's_algorithm) for minimum spanning trees.[^o21]
- Jarník, Vojtěch. (1933). "Über die Differenzierbarkeit stetiger Funktionen". *Fundamenta Mathematicae*. 21. p. 48–58. doi:10.4064/fm-21-1-48-58. zbl:0007.40102. Generic functions have infinite Dini derivatives at almost all points.[^o17]
- Jarník, Vojtěch; Kössler, Miloš. (1934). "O minimálních grafech, obsahujících daných bodů". *Časopis pro Pěstování Matematiky a Fysiky*. 63 (8). p. 223–235. doi:10.21136/CPMF.1934.122548. zbl:0009.13106. The first serious treatment of the [Steiner tree problem](wikipedia://en/498304).[^o21]

He was also the author of ten textbooks in Czech, on [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), and [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).[^o28] These books "became classics for several generations of students".[^o36]
