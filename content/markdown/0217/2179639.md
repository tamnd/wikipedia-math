# Obstruction theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Obstruction theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **obstruction theory** is a name given to two different [mathematical theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_theory), both of which yield [cohomological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomological) [invariants](wikipedia://en/1126638).

:::

In the original work of [Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876) and [Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794), [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530) were defined as obstructions to the existence of certain fields of linear independent [vectors](wikipedia://en/62641). Obstruction theory turns out to be an application of cohomology theory to the problem of constructing a [section](wikipedia://en/367621) of a [bundle](wikipedia://en/276526).

## In homotopy theory {#In_homotopy_theory}

The older meaning for obstruction theory in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) relates to the procedure, inductive with respect to dimension, for extending a [continuous mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_mapping) defined on a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686), or [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022). It is traditionally called *Eilenberg obstruction theory*, after [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501). It involves [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) with coefficients in [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) to define obstructions to extensions. For example, with a mapping from a simplicial complex *X* to another, *Y*, defined initially on the [0-skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0-skeleton) of *X* (the vertices of *X*), an extension to the 1-skeleton will be possible whenever the image of the 0-skeleton will belong to the same [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected) component of *Y*. Extending from the 1-skeleton to the 2-skeleton means defining the mapping on each solid triangle from *X*, given the mapping already defined on its boundary edges. Likewise, then extending the mapping to the 3-skeleton involves extending the mapping to each solid 3-simplex of *X*, given the mapping already defined on its boundary.

At some point, say extending the mapping from the (n-1)-skeleton of *X* to the n-skeleton of *X*, this procedure might be impossible. In that case, one can assign to each n-simplex the homotopy class $\pi_{n-1}(Y)$ of the mapping already defined on its boundary, (at least one of which will be non-zero). These assignments define an [n-cochain](wikipedia://en/194060) with coefficients in $\pi_{n-1}(Y)$. Amazingly, this cochain turns out to be a **cocycle** and so defines a [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) class in the nth cohomology group of *X* with coefficients in $\pi_{n-1}(Y)$. When this cohomology class is equal to 0, it turns out that the mapping may be modified within its homotopy class on the (n-1)-skeleton of *X* so that the mapping may be extended to the n-skeleton of *X*. If the class is not equal to zero, it is called the obstruction to extending the mapping over the n-skeleton, given its homotopy class on the (n-1)-skeleton.

### Obstruction to extending a section of a principal bundle {#Obstruction_to_extending_a_section_of_a_principal_bundle}

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

#### Construction {#Construction}

Suppose that $B$ is a [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) simplicial complex and that $p : E \to B$ is a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) with fiber $F$. Furthermore, assume that we have a partially defined [section](wikipedia://en/367621) $\sigma_{n} : B_{n} \to E$ on the [$n$-skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910) of $B$.

For every $(n + 1)$-simplex $\Delta$ in $B$, $\sigma_{n}$ can be restricted to the boundary $\partial\Delta$ (which is a topological [$n$-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782)). Because $p$ sends each $\sigma_{n}(\partial\Delta)$ back to $\partial\Delta$, $\sigma_{n}$ defines a map from the $n$-sphere to $p^{-1}(\Delta)$. Because fibrations satisfy the homotopy lifting property, and $\Delta$ is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414); $p^{-1}(\Delta)$ is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to $F$. So this partially defined section assigns an element of $\pi_{n}(F)$ to every $(n + 1)$-simplex. This is precisely the data of a $\pi_{n}(F)$-valued [simplicial](wikipedia://en/1060236) [cochain](wikipedia://en/194060#definition) of degree $n + 1$ on $B$, i.e. an element of $C^{n + 1}(B; \pi_{n}(F))$. This cochain is called the **obstruction cochain** because it being the zero means that all of these elements of $\pi_{n}(F)$ are trivial, which means that our partially defined section can be extended to the $(n + 1)$-skeleton by using the homotopy between (the partially defined section on the boundary of each $\Delta$) and the constant map.

The fact that this cochain came from a partially defined section (as opposed to an arbitrary collection of maps from all the boundaries of all the $(n + 1)$-simplices) can be used to prove that this cochain is a cocycle. If one started with a different partially defined section $\sigma_{n}$ that agreed with the original on the $(n - 1)$-skeleton, then one can also prove that the resulting cocycle would differ from the first by a coboundary. Therefore we have a well-defined element of the cohomology group $H^{n + 1}(B; \pi_{n}(F))$ such that if a partially defined section on the $(n + 1)$-skeleton exists that agrees with the given choice on the $(n - 1)$-skeleton, then this cohomology class must be trivial.

The converse is also true if one allows such things as *homotopy sections*, i.e. a map $\sigma : B \to E$ such that $p \circ \sigma$ is homotopic (as opposed to equal) to the identity map on $B$. Thus it provides a complete invariant of the existence of sections up to homotopy on the $(n + 1)$-skeleton.

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

#### Applications {#Applications}

- By inducting over $n$, one can construct a *first obstruction to a section* as the first of the above cohomology classes that is non-zero.
- This can be used to find obstructions to trivializations of [principal bundles](wikipedia://en/310950#Trivializations_and_cross_sections).
- Because [any map can be turned into a fibration](wikipedia://en/19207725), this construction can be used to see if there are obstructions to the existence of a lift (up to homotopy) of a map into $B$ to a map into $E$ even if $p : E \to B$ is not a fibration.
- It is crucial to the construction of [Postnikov systems](wikipedia://en/15708202).

:::

## In geometric topology {#In_geometric_topology}

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), obstruction theory is concerned with when a [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193) has a [piecewise linear structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise_linear_structure), and when a piecewise linear manifold has a [differential structure](wikipedia://en/725272).

In dimension at most 2 (Rado), and 3 (Moise), the notions of topological manifolds and piecewise linear manifolds coincide. In dimension 4 they are not the same.

In dimensions at most 6 the notions of piecewise linear manifolds and differentiable manifolds coincide.

## In surgery theory {#In_surgery_theory}

The two basic questions of [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115) are whether a topological space with *n*-dimensional [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to an *n*-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), and also whether a [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) of *n*-dimensional manifolds is [homotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopic) to a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564). In both cases there are two obstructions for *n>9*, a primary [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) obstruction to the existence of a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524): if this vanishes there exists a [normal map](wikipedia://en/21298614), allowing the definition of the secondary [surgery obstruction](wikipedia://en/5961115) in [algebraic L-theory](wikipedia://en/696568) to performing surgery on the normal map to obtain a [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence).
