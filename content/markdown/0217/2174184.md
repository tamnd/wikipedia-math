# Piecewise linear manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Piecewise linear manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **piecewise linear manifold** (**PL manifold**) is a [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193) together with a **piecewise linear structure** on it. Such a structure can be defined by means of an [atlas](wikipedia://en/47436), such that one can pass from [chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chart_%28topology%29) to chart in it by [piecewise linear functions](wikipedia://en/444224). This is slightly stronger than the topological notion of a [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976). An [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of PL manifolds is called a **PL homeomorphism**.

:::

## Relation to other categories of manifolds {#Relation_to_other_categories_of_manifolds}

PL sits between DIFF (the category of [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold)) and TOP (the category of topological manifolds): it is categorically "better behaved" than DIFF — for example, the [Generalized Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/8536216) is true in PL (with the possible exception of dimension 4, where it is equivalent to DIFF), but is false generally in DIFF — but is "worse behaved" than TOP, as elaborated in [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115).

### Smooth manifolds {#Smooth_manifolds}

Every smooth manifold has a canonical PL structure — it is uniquely *triangulizable,* by Whitehead's theorem on [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) (Whitehead 1940)[^o5] — but a PL manifold might not have a [smooth structure](wikipedia://en/1542238) — it might not be *smoothable.* Smooth manifolds and PL manifolds are both contained in the *set* of [piecewise-smooth manifolds](wikipedia://en/10131478), but piecewise smooth functions are not closed under composition, so these do not form a category.

One way in which PL is better behaved than DIFF is that one can take [cones](wikipedia://en/782162) in PL, but not in DIFF — the cone point is acceptable in PL. A consequence is that the [Generalized Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/8536216) is true in PL for dimensions greater than four — the proof is to take a [homotopy sphere](wikipedia://en/1202074), remove two balls, apply the [*h*-cobordism](wikipedia://en/681049) theorem to conclude that this is a cylinder, and then attach cones to recover a sphere. This last step works in PL but not in DIFF, giving rise to [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931).

### Topological manifolds {#Topological_manifolds}

Not every topological manifold admits a PL structure, and of those that do, the PL structure need not be unique—it can have infinitely many. This is elaborated at [Hauptvermutung](wikipedia://en/2180532).

The obstruction to placing a PL structure on a topological manifold *M* is the [Kirby–Siebenmann class](wikipedia://en/2180593); to be precise, it is the [obstruction](wikipedia://en/2179639) to placing a PL-structure on *M* x **R** and in dimensions *n* > 4, the KS class vanishes if and only if *M* has at least one PL-structure.

### Real algebraic sets {#Real_algebraic_sets}

An A-structure on a PL manifold is a structure which gives an inductive way of resolving the PL manifold to a smooth manifold. Compact PL manifolds admit A-structures.[^o9][^o10] Compact PL manifolds are homeomorphic to [real-algebraic sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_algebraic_set).[^o11][^o12] Put another way, A-category sits over the PL-category as a richer category with no obstruction to lifting, that is BA → BPL is a product fibration with BA = BPL × PL/A, and PL manifolds are real algebraic sets because A-manifolds are real algebraic sets.

### Combinatorial manifolds and digital manifolds {#Combinatorial_manifolds_and_digital_manifolds}

- A [combinatorial manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_manifold) is a kind of manifold which is discretization of a manifold. It usually means a piecewise linear manifold made by [simplicial complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_complexes).
- A [digital manifold](wikipedia://en/27123668) is a special kind of combinatorial manifold which is defined in digital space. See [digital topology](wikipedia://en/5907688).
