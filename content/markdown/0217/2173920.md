# Johann Sturm

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Sturm"}

**Johann Christoph Sturm** (3 November 1635 – 26 December 1703)[^o3] was a German [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), professor at [University of Altdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Altdorf) and founder of a short-lived scientific academy known as the [Collegium Curiosum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Curiosum), based on the model of the Florentine [Accademia del Cimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_del_Cimento).[^o4] He edited two volumes of the academy's proceedings under the title *Collegium Experimentale* (1676 and 1685).[^o4] In 1670, he translated the works of Archimedes into German.[^o3]

:::

Sturm is the author of *Physica Electiva* (1697), a book that criticized [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) and prompted him to publish a [rebuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebuke). Sturm's critique was aimed at Leibniz's view that [Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature) and/or its constituent parts possess some creative force of their own. This criticism was partly [theological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theological), in that Sturm claimed Leibniz's view of Nature undermined the sovereignty of the [Christian God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_God).[^o5]
