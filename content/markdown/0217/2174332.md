# Loop space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Loop space"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **loop space** Ω*X* of a [pointed](wikipedia://en/1236666) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is the space of (based) loops in *X*, i.e. [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) pointed maps from the pointed [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) *S*<sup>1</sup> to *X*, equipped with the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335). Two loops can be multiplied by [concatenation](wikipedia://en/1189553#Path_composition). With this operation, the loop space is an [*A*<sub>∞</sub>-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-infinity_operad). That is, the multiplication is [homotopy-coherently](wikipedia://en/212250) [associative](wikipedia://en/1335).

:::

The [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [path components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_component) of Ω*X*, i.e. the set of based-homotopy [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of based loops in *X*, is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447), the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) *π*<sub>1</sub>(*X*).

The **iterated loop spaces** of *X* are formed by applying Ω a number of times.

There is an analogous construction for topological spaces without basepoint. The **free loop space** of a topological space *X* is the space of maps from the circle *S*<sup>1</sup> to *X* with the compact-open topology. The free loop space of *X* is often denoted by $\mathcal{L}X$.

As a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987), the free loop space construction is [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) with the circle, while the loop space construction is right adjoint to the [reduced suspension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_suspension). This adjunction accounts for much of the importance of loop spaces in [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655). (A related phenomenon in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) is [currying](wikipedia://en/6600), where the cartesian product is adjoint to the [hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037).) Informally this is referred to as [Eckmann–Hilton duality](wikipedia://en/23191477).

## Eckmann–Hilton duality {#Eckmann–Hilton_duality}

The loop space is dual to the [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702) of the same space; this duality is sometimes called [Eckmann–Hilton duality](wikipedia://en/23191477). The basic observation is that

$$[\Sigma Z,X] \approxeq [Z, \Omega X]$$

where $[A,B]$ is the set of homotopy classes of maps $A \rightarrow B$, and $\Sigma A$ is the suspension of A, and $\approxeq$ denotes the [natural](wikipedia://en/45022) [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660). This homeomorphism is essentially that of [currying](wikipedia://en/6600), modulo the quotients needed to convert the products to reduced products.

In general, $[A, B]$ does not have a group structure for arbitrary spaces $A$ and $B$. However, it can be shown that $[\Sigma Z,X]$ and $[Z, \Omega X]$ do have natural group structures when $Z$ and $X$ are [pointed](wikipedia://en/1236666), and the aforementioned isomorphism is of those groups.[^o3] Thus, setting $Z = S^{k-1}$ (the $k-1$ sphere) gives the relationship

$$\pi_k(X) \approxeq \pi_{k-1}(\Omega X).$$

This follows since the [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) is defined as $\pi_k(X)=[S^k,X]$ and the spheres can be obtained via suspensions of each-other, i.e. $S^k=\Sigma S^{k-1}$.[^o4]
