# Reduced product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reduced product"}

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a branch of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), and in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **reduced product** is a construction that generalizes both [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) and [ultraproduct](wikipedia://en/273642).

:::

Let {*S*<sub>*i*</sub> | *i* ∈ *I*} be a nonempty family of [structures](wikipedia://en/4055928) of the same [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156) σ indexed by a set *I*, and let *U* be a proper [filter](wikipedia://en/19719) on *I*. The domain of the reduced product is the [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_set) of the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095)

$$\prod_{i \in I} S_i$$

by a certain [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) ~: two elements (*a<sub>i</sub>*) and (*b<sub>i</sub>*) of the Cartesian product are equivalent if

$$\left\{ i \in I: a_i = b_i \right\}\in U$$

If *U* only contains *I* as an element, the equivalence relation is trivial, and the reduced product is just the direct product. If *U* is an [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911), the reduced product is an ultraproduct.

Operations from σ are interpreted on the reduced product by applying the operation pointwise. Relations are interpreted by

$$R((a^1_i)/{\sim},\dots,(a^n_i)/{\sim}) \iff \{i\in I\mid R^{S_i}(a^1_i,\dots,a^n_i)\}\in U.$$

For example, if each structure is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), then the reduced product is a vector space with addition defined as (*a* + *b*)<sub>*i*</sub> = *a<sub>i</sub>* + *b<sub>i</sub>* and multiplication by a scalar *c* as (*ca*)<sub>*i*</sub> = *c a<sub>i</sub>*.
