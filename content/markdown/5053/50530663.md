# Giacomo Candido

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giacomo Candido"}

**Giacomo Candido** (10 July 1871, in [Guagnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guagnano) – 30 December 1941, in [Galatina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatina)) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) mathematician and historian of mathematics.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

In 1897 Candido received his *Laurea* (teaching degree) from the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa) and started to teach mathematics: first, at the Liceo of Galatina, then at the Liceo of Campobasso and from 1927 at the Liceo of Brindisi.[^o4]

He was an editor and contributor for the *Periodico di Matematica per l'Insegnamento secondario* and was one of the founders of the journal *La Matematica elementare* (an intermediate-level journal for teachers, engineers and students).[^o5]

He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1928 in Bologna[^o6] and in 1932 in Zürich. In 1934 he founded the [Apulian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) branch of *Mathesis*, an Italian association of mathematics teachers.

He is also remembered for his work on the history of mathematics.

## Candido's identity {#Candido's_identity}

Candido devised his eponymous identity to prove that

$$(F_n^2 +F_{n+1}^2 +F_{n+2}^2 )^2= 2(F_n^4 +F_{n+1}^4 +F_{n+2}^4 )$$

where F<sub>n</sub> is the *n*th [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number). The identity of Candido is that, for all real numbers x and y,[^o9][^o10]

$$(x^2 +y^2 +(x+y)^2 )^2= 2(x^4 +y^4 +(x+y)^4 )$$

It is easy to prove that the identity holds in any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346).[^o9]

:::
