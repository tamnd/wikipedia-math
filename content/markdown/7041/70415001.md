# Peter Szüsz

*Image: PSz1974-BW.jpg.* Peter Szüsz (1974)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Szüsz"}

**Peter Szüsz** (11 November 1924 – 16 February 2008)[^o2] was a Serbian-[Hungarian](wikipedia://en/40842906)-American [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hungarian_Jews) known[^o3] for his proof (1961) of the Gauss-Kuzmin Theorem, his work in probabilistic [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and his book with Andrew M. Rockett on *Continued Fractions*.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Born in Novi Sad, Serbia, he grew up in Budapest, Hungary, attending the Eötvös József Gimnázium and beginning his life-long passions for chess,[^o7] music,[^o8] and mathematics. He was the son of Irma (née Oberson) and Felix Szusz. He had one brother, Adam (Allen Saunders). In 1944 he was drafted into forced [labour service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_service_in_Hungary_during_World_War_II) and sent to the Heidenau Lager at the copper mines near Bor,[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12] but escaped from the Nazi SS death march to Cservenka and was hidden by the Gyulai family near Kula until the end of the war.[^o13]

## Career {#Career}

After studying first electrical engineering and then mathematics at the University of Budapest, he became a Research Fellow at the Hungarian Academy of Science from 1950 to 1965, received his Ph.D. as a student of [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) in 1951,[^o15] and became a [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) at the Academy in 1962. He fled communist Hungary in 1965, became a Full Professor of Mathematics at the [State University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York) at Stony Brook (now Stony Brook University) in 1966 and retired in 1994.[^o16]
