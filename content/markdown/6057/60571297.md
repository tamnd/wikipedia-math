# Gary Antonick

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gary Antonick"}

**Gary Antonick** ( ;[^o2] born February 11, 1963) is an American journalist and recreational mathematician who for many years wrote a puzzle-based column called "Numberplay" for the [New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times).[^o8]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Antonick has a BS in Engineering from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and an MBA from [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School).[^o4] In 2019, he taught a course at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), titled *Math in the Wild: Using Mathematical Thinking to Solve Messy Real-World Problems*, alongside [Keith Devlin](wikipedia://en/574895).[^o5]

## Numberplay {#Numberplay}

From December 2009 to October 2016 Antonick wrote the puzzle themed "Numberplay" column for *The New York Times*.[^o7] The puzzles generally involved math or logic problems.[^o8] They came from many sources, and many were descended from columns by the celebrated [Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American) columnist [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435).[^o9] He often wrote about Gardner and considered him to be the leading popularizer of [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446).[^o10][^o11] Conferences called [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner) are held every two years to celebrate Gardner's legacy, and Antonick has twice spoken at these events.[^o12][^o13] He also supports the [Julia Robinson Mathematics Festival](wikipedia://en/57295923).[^o14]

Among the many classic problems of recreational mathematics featured in "Numberplay" are The [Prisoner's Dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner's_Dilemma), The [Two Child Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_child_problem), The [Monty Hall Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Hall_Problem), The [Monkey and the Coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey_and_the_coconuts), [The Two-cube Calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Two-cube_calendar), and [The Zebra Puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Zebra_Puzzle). Sometimes "Numberplay" was used to celebrate other mathematicians such as Paul Erdős,[^o15] or simply to report a breakthrough in mathematics or game theory.[^o16]

"Numberplay" columns led to five sequences originated by Antonick[^o17] being listed in the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004) (OEIS)[^o18]

## English Channel Swim {#English_Channel_Swim}

On August 8, 1988, Antonick swam the English Channel, starting from Dover, England, and finishing in France 8 hours and 46 minutes later. [^o20]
