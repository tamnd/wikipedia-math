# Gertrude Ehrlich

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gertrude Ehrlich"}

**Gertrude Ehrlich** (January 7, 1923 – October 18, 2025) was an Austrian-American mathematician, who specialized in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705). She was a professor [emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of mathematics at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park).

:::

## Background {#Background}

Ehrlich was born on January 7, 1923, in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), the daughter of Jewish lawyer Josef Ehrlich and his wife Charlotte, née Kobak. In the late 1930s, she became a student at the Chajes Gymnasium, a special high school in Vienna for Jewish honor students; her classmates included future Nobel laureate [Walter Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Kohn) and mathematicians Rodolfo Permutti and Karl Greger. She was able to escape [Nazi-occupied Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_within_Nazi_Germany) in 1939, traveling with her mother, her older sister Margarete Ehrlich (a philosophy student and later radiographer) and aunt Mathilde Ehrlich (a painter) to the US on the *[SS Statendam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statendam_%28ship%29)* in July 1939; her father rejoined them a year later. They lived for the next several years with her uncle Benedict Kobak in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta). She became a US citizen in 1945.

She graduated from the [Georgia State College for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_State_College_for_Women) in 1943, and earned a master's degree from the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina) in 1945. She completed her Ph.D. in 1953 at the [University of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee). Her dissertation, *The Structure of Continuous Rings*, was supervised by [Wallace Givens](wikipedia://en/8938904).

Ehrlich died in [Baltimore, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland), on October 18, 2025, at the age of 102.[^o3]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Ehrlich was the author of the book *Fundamental Concepts of Abstract Algebra* (PWS-Kent Publishing, 1991; Dover, 2011). She is the coauthor of *The Structure of the Real Number System* (with Leon Warren Cohen, [D. Van Nostrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Van_Nostrand), 1963) and of *Algebra* (with Jacob Goldhaber, Macmillan, 1970; Robert E. Krieger Publishing, 1980).

In 1964 she became editor of the "Classroom Notes" department of *[The American Mathematical Monthly](wikipedia://en/1179483)*. She was the first organizer of the University of Maryland High School Mathematics Competition, held annually for high school students in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland) and the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia), starting in 1979.

The concept of a morphic group comes from a 1976 research paper of Ehrlich, "Units and one-sided units in regular rings", in the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)*, and Ehrlich's theorem on the [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of morphic groups, from the same paper, is named for her.
