# Cohomological dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cohomological dimension"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), **cohomological dimension** is an invariant of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) which measures the homological complexity of its representations. It has important applications in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).

:::

## Cohomological dimension of a group {#Cohomological_dimension_of_a_group}

As most cohomological invariants, the cohomological dimension involves a choice of a "ring of coefficients" *R*, with a prominent special case given by $R=\mathbb{Z}$, the ring of [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers). Let *G* be a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536), *R* a non-zero [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) with a unit, and $RG$ the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223). The group *G* has **cohomological dimension less than or equal to *n***, denoted $\operatorname{cd}_R(G)\le n$, if the trivial $RG$-module *R* has a [projective resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_resolution) of length *n*, i.e. there are [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) $RG$-modules $P_0,\dots , P_n$ and $RG$-module homomorphisms $d_k\colon P_k\to P_{k-1} (k = 1,\dots, n)$ and $d_0\colon P_0\to R$, such that the image of $d_k$ coincides with the [kernel](wikipedia://en/1072915) of $d_{k-1}$ for $k = 1, \dots, n$ and the kernel of $d_n$ is trivial.

Equivalently, the cohomological dimension is less than or equal to *n* if for an arbitrary $RG$-module *M*, the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) of *G* with coefficients in *M* vanishes in degrees $k>n$, that is, $H^k(G,M) = 0$ whenever $k>n$. The *p*-cohomological dimension for prime *p* is similarly defined in terms of the *p*-torsion groups $H^k(G,M){p}$.[^o3]

The smallest *n* such that the cohomological dimension of *G* is less than or equal to *n* is the **cohomological dimension** of *G* (with coefficients *R*), which is denoted $n=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(G)$.

A free resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ can be obtained from a [free action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_action) of the group *G* on a [contractible topological space](wikipedia://en/1752414) *X*. In particular, if *X* is a contractible [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) of dimension *n* with a free action of a discrete group *G* that permutes the cells, then $\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)\le n$.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In the first group of examples, let the ring *R* of coefficients be $\mathbb{Z}$.

- A [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) has cohomological dimension one. As shown by [John Stallings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stallings) (for finitely generated group) and [Richard Swan](wikipedia://en/18384954) (in full generality), this property characterizes free groups. This result is known as the Stallings–Swan theorem.[^o6] The Stallings-Swan theorem for a group G says that G is free if and only if every [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) by G with abelian kernel is split.[^o7]
- The [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) other than the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) has cohomological dimension two.
- More generally, the fundamental group of a closed, connected, orientable [aspherical](wikipedia://en/7147287) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) of [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) *n* has cohomological dimension *n*. In particular, the fundamental group of a closed orientable hyperbolic *n*-manifold has cohomological dimension *n*.
- Nontrivial [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) have infinite cohomological dimension over $\mathbb{Z}$. More generally, the same is true for groups with nontrivial [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330).

Now consider the case of a general ring *R*.

- A group *G* has cohomological dimension 0 if and only if its group ring $RG$ is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/3876151). Thus a finite group has cohomological dimension 0 if and only if its order (or, equivalently, the orders of its elements) is invertible in *R*.
- Generalizing the Stallings–Swan theorem for $R=\mathbb{Z}$, [Martin Dunwoody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Dunwoody) proved that a group has cohomological dimension at most one over an arbitrary ring *R* if and only if it is the fundamental group of a connected [graph of finite groups](wikipedia://en/12281773) whose orders are invertible in *R*.

:::

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Cohomological dimension of a field"}

## Cohomological dimension of a field {#Cohomological_dimension_of_a_field}

The *p*-cohomological dimension of a field *K* is the *p*-cohomological dimension of the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of a [separable closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_closure) of *K*.[^o10] The cohomological dimension of *K* is the supremum of the *p*-cohomological dimension over all primes *p*.[^o11]

:::

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every field of non-zero [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_field) *p* has *p*-cohomological dimension at most 1.[^o14]
- Every finite field has [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) isomorphic to $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and so has cohomological dimension 1.[^o15]
- The field of formal [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213) $k((t))$ over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *k* of characteristic zero also has absolute Galois group isomorphic to $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and so cohomological dimension 1.[^o15]
