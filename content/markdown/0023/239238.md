# D. R. Fulkerson

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="D. R. Fulkerson"}

**Delbert Ray Fulkerson** (; August 14, 1924 – January 10, 1976) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who co-developed the [Ford{{Ndash}}Fulkerson algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford{{Ndash}}Fulkerson_algorithm), one of the best-known [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for solving the [maximum flow problem](wikipedia://en/403165) in [networks](wikipedia://en/645676).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

D. R. Fulkerson was born in [Tamms, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamms,_Illinois), the third of six children of Elbert and Emma Fulkerson. Fulkerson became an undergraduate at [Southern Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University). His academic career was interrupted by military service during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). Having returned to complete his degree after the war, he went on to do a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) under the supervision of [Cyrus MacDuffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Colton_MacDuffee), who was a student of [L. E. Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._E._Dickson). Fulkerson received his Ph.D. in 1951.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

After graduation, Fulkerson joined the mathematics department at the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation). In 1956, he and [L. R. Ford Jr.](wikipedia://en/7183185) described the [Ford–Fulkerson algorithm](wikipedia://en/53777).[^o7] In 1962 they produced a book-length description of their method.[^o8]

In 1971 he moved to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) as the Maxwell Upson Professor of Engineering. He was diagnosed with [Crohn's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crohn's_disease) and was limited in his teaching. In despair, he committed suicide in 1976.[^o9]

Fulkerson was the supervisor of [Jon Folkman](wikipedia://en/29238744) at RAND and [Tatsuo Oyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsuo_Oyama)[^o10] at [GRIPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Graduate_Institute_for_Policy_Studies). After Folkman committed suicide in 1969, Fulkerson blamed himself for failing to notice Folkman's suicidal behaviors.[^o11]

In 1979, the [Mathematical Programming Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Programming_Society) and the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) established the [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916), which is now awarded every three years for outstanding papers in [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492).[^o12]
