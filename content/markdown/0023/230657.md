# DigiCipher 2

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="DigiCipher 2"}

**DigiCipher 2**, or simply **DCII**, is a proprietary standard format of digital signal transmission and it doubles as an [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294) standard with MPEG-2/MPEG-4 signal [video compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_compression) used on many [communications satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_satellite) [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television) and audio signals. The DCII standard was originally developed in 1997 by [General Instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Instrument), which then became the Home and Network Mobility division of [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola), then bought by [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) in Aug 2011, and lastly became the Home portion of the division to [Arris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arris_Group).[^o2]

:::

The original attempt for a North American digital signal encryption and compression standard was DigiCipher 1, which was used most notably in the now-defunct [PrimeStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PrimeStar) medium-power [direct broadcast satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_broadcast_satellite) (DBS) system during the early 1990s. The DCII standard predates wide acceptance of [DVB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVB)-based digital terrestrial television compression (although not cable or satellite DVB) and therefore is incompatible with the DVB standard.

Approximately 70% of first-generation [digital cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_cable) networks in North America use the [4DTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4DTV)/DigiCipher 2 format.[^o3] The use of DCII is most prevalent in North American digital [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television) [set-top boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-top_box). DCII is also used on Motorola's 4DTV digital [satellite television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_television) [tuner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuner_%28television%29) and [Shaw Direct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Direct)'s DBS receiver.

The DigiCipher 2 encryption standard was reverse engineered in 2016.[^o4]

## Technical specifications {#Technical_specifications}

DigiCipher II uses QPSK and BPSK at the same time. The primary difference between DigiCipher 2 and DVB lies in how each standard handles SI [metadata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metadata), or System Information, where DVB reserves packet identifiers from 16 to 31 for metadata, DigiCipher reserves only packet identifier 8187 for its [master guide table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_and_System_Information_Protocol) which acts as a *look-up table* for all other metadata tables. DigiCipher 2 also extends the MPEG *program number* that is assigned for each service in a transport stream with the concept of a [virtual channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_channel) number, whereas the DVB system never defined this type of remapping preferring to use a registry of *network* identifiers to further differentiate *program numbers* from those used in other transport streams. There are also private non-standard additions to DVB that add virtual channel remapping using *logical channel numbers*. Also unlike DVB, all text used in descriptors can be compressed using standard [Huffman coding](wikipedia://en/13883) which saves on broadcast bandwidth and loading times. DigiCipher II uses [Dolby Digital AC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Digital) audio for all channels, although MPEG-1 Level 2 audio is not supported.[^o3]
