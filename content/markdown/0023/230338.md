# Robert Morris (cryptographer)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Morris"}

**Robert Morris** (July 25, 1932 – June 26, 2011) was an American [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist).[^o3][^o4] He is best known for his work on [Multics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multics) and [Unix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix) and for co-creating *[Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_%28video_game%29)*, the first [programming game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_game).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Robert Morris was born on July 25, 1932, in [Boston, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston,_Massachusetts), to Walter W. Morris, a salesman, and Helen Kelly Morris, a homemaker.[^o3] He received a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in mathematics in 1957 and a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in 1958 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

### Bell Labs {#Bell_Labs}

From 1960 until 1986, Morris was a researcher at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) and worked on [Multics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multics) and later [Unix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix).

Using the [TMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMG_%28language%29) compiler-compiler, Morris, together with [Douglas McIlroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_McIlroy), developed the early implementation of the [PL/I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL/I) compiler called EPL for the Multics project.[^o8][^o9] The pair also contributed a version of [runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TYPSET_and_RUNOFF) text-formatting program for Multics.[^o10]

Morris's contributions to [early versions of Unix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix) include the math library, the [dc programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dc_programming_language), the program [`crypt`](wikipedia://en/4179924), and the password encryption scheme used for user authentication.[^o11][^o12] The encryption scheme (invented by [Roger Needham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Needham)), was based on using a [trapdoor function](wikipedia://en/206753) (now called a [key derivation function](wikipedia://en/449781)) to compute hashes of user [passwords](wikipedia://en/24304) which were stored in the file `[[/etc/passwd]]`; analogous techniques, relying on different functions, are still in use today.[^o13]

In 1961, together with [Victor A. Vyssotsky](wikipedia://en/974955) and [Douglas McIlroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_McIlroy), Morris co-created *[Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_%28video_game%29)*, the first [programming game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_game), on an [IBM 7090](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_7090).[^o14]

### National Security Agency {#National_Security_Agency}

In 1986, Morris began work at the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) (NSA).[^o3] He served as chief scientist of the NSA's [National Computer Security Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Computer_Security_Center), where he was involved in the production of the [Rainbow Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Series) of computer security standards, and retired from the NSA in 1994.[^o16][^o17][^o18] He once told a reporter that, while at the NSA, he helped the FBI decode encrypted evidence.[^o3]

There is a description of Morris in [Clifford Stoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Stoll)'s book *[The Cuckoo's Egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cuckoo's_Egg)*. Many readers of Stoll's book remember Morris for giving Stoll a challenging mathematical [puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle) (originally due to [John H. Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Conway)) in the course of their discussions on [computer security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_security): *What is the next number in the sequence 1 11 21 1211 111221?* (known as the [look-and-say sequence](wikipedia://en/563980)). Stoll was unaware of the answer to this puzzle at the time and remained unaware when writing *The Cuckoo's Egg* and thus did not reveal the answer in his book.[^o19]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Robert Morris married Anne Farlow, and they had three children together: [Robert Tappan Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Tappan_Morris),[^o21] Meredith Morris, and Benjamin Morris.[^o22] Robert Tappan Morris created the [Morris worm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_worm) in 1988, which is considered the first [computer worm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_worm) on the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet).

Robert Morris died on June 26, 2011, in [Lebanon, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon,_New_Hampshire).
