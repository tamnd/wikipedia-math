# Questionable cause

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Questionable cause"}

The **questionable cause**—also known as **causal fallacy**, **false cause**, or ***non causa pro causa*** ("non-cause for cause" in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin))—is a category of [informal fallacies](wikipedia://en/1655450) in which the [cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality) or causes is/are incorrectly identified. In other words, it is a fallacy of reaching a conclusion that one thing caused another, simply because they are regularly associated.

:::

Questionable cause can be logically reduced to: "A is regularly associated with B; therefore, A causes B."[^o2]

For example: "Every time I score an A on the test it's a sunny day. Therefore the sunny day causes me to score well on the test." Here is the example the two events may coincide or correlate, but have no causal connection.[^o3]

Fallacies of questionable cause include:

- [Circular cause and consequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_cause_and_consequence)
- [Correlation implies causation](wikipedia://en/39834) (*cum hoc, ergo propter hoc*)
  - [Third-cause fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-cause_fallacy)
  - [Wrong direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrong_direction)
- [Fallacy of the single cause](wikipedia://en/599252)
- *[Post hoc ergo propter hoc](wikipedia://en/174725)*
- [Observational interpretation fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_interpretation_fallacy)
- [Regression fallacy](wikipedia://en/476228)
- [Texas sharpshooter fallacy](wikipedia://en/476197)
- [Jumping to conclusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumping_to_conclusions)
- [Association fallacy](wikipedia://en/235596)
- [Magical thinking](wikipedia://en/185307)
