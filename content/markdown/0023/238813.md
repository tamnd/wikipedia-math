# Correlative-based fallacies

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Correlative-based fallacies"}

In [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), **correlative-based fallacies** are [informal fallacies](wikipedia://en/1655450) based on [correlative conjunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlative_conjunction).

:::

## Correlative conjunctions {#Correlative_conjunctions}

A correlative conjunction is a relationship between two statements where one must be false and the other true. In formal logic this is known as the [exclusive or](wikipedia://en/105979) relationship; traditionally, terms between which this relationship exists have been called *contradictories*.

## Fallacies {#Fallacies}

Fallacies based on correlatives include:[^o6]

**[False dilemma](wikipedia://en/17687) or *false correlative*.**

Here something which is not a correlative is treated as a correlative, excluding some other possibility.

**[Denying the correlative](wikipedia://en/235558)**

where an attempt is made to introduce another option into a true correlative.

**[Suppressed correlative](wikipedia://en/238800)**

where the definitions of a correlative are changed so that one of the options includes the other, making one option impossible.
