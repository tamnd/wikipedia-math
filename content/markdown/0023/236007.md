# Unitary transformation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unitary transformation"}

In mathematics, a **unitary transformation** is a [linear isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_isomorphism) that preserves the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product): the inner product of two vectors before the transformation is equal to their inner product after the transformation.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

More precisely, a **unitary transformation** is an [isometric isomorphism](wikipedia://en/254777) between two [inner product spaces](wikipedia://en/14856) (such as [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932)). In other words, a *unitary transformation* is a [bijective function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective_function)

$$U : H_1 \to H_2$$

between two inner product spaces, $H_1$ and $H_2,$ such that

$$\langle Ux, Uy \rangle_{H_2} = \langle x, y \rangle_{H_1} \quad \text{ for all } x, y \in H_1.$$

It is a [linear isometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_isometry), as one can see by setting $x=y.$

:::

## Unitary operator {#Unitary_operator}

In the case when $H_1$ and $H_2$ are the same space, a unitary transformation is an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of that Hilbert space, and then it is also called a [unitary operator](wikipedia://en/225999).

## Relation to unitary matrices {#Relation_to_unitary_matrices}

In [complex coordinate space](wikipedia://en/39125683) $\mathbb{C}^n$ unitary transformations always have the shape

$$f \colon \mathbb{C}^n \to \mathbb{C}^n, \, x \mapsto U \cdot x,$$

where $U \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ is a [unitary matrix](wikipedia://en/129222), and the dot before the vector is the matrix-vector-product.

This matrix $U$ satisfies $UU^* = I$ [^o7].

## Antiunitary transformation {#Antiunitary_transformation}

A closely related notion is that of **[antiunitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiunitary) transformation**, which is a bijective function

$$U:H_1\to H_2\,$$

between two [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) Hilbert spaces such that

$$\langle Ux, Uy \rangle = \overline{\langle x, y \rangle}=\langle y, x \rangle$$

for all $x$ and $y$ in $H_1$, where the horizontal bar represents the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918).
