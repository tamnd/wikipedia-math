# David Chaum

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Chaum"}

**David Lee Chaum** (born 1955) is an American [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), [cryptographer](wikipedia://en/7088), and inventor. He is known as a pioneer in cryptography and privacy-preserving technologies and widely recognized as the inventor of [digital cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_currency). His 1982 dissertation, "Computer Systems Established, Maintained, and Trusted by Mutually Suspicious Groups," is the first known proposal for a [blockchain](wikipedia://en/44065971) protocol.[^o3] Complete with the code to implement the protocol, Chaum's dissertation proposed all but one element of the blockchain later detailed in the [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265) [whitepaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_paper). He has been referred to as "the father of online anonymity",[^o4] and "the godfather of cryptocurrency."[^o5]

:::

He is also known for developing [ecash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecash), an electronic cash application that aims to preserve a user's anonymity, and inventing many [cryptographic](wikipedia://en/18934432) protocols like the [blind signature](wikipedia://en/428273), [mix networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mix_networks), and the [Dining Cryptographers Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dining_cryptographers_protocol). In 1995, his company [DigiCash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigiCash) created the first digital currency with eCash.[^o6] His 1981 paper, "Untraceable Electronic Mail, Return Addresses, and Digital Pseudonyms," laid the groundwork for the field of anonymous communications research.[^o7]

More recently, in 2020, Chaum founded the [xx network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xx_network), a privacy-focused blockchain platform, and in 2021 launched [xx coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xx_network) (abbreviation XX), a cryptocurrency designed to enhance user privacy and provide quantum resistance.[^o8][^o9]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Chaum was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) family in [Los Angeles, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California).[^o11] He gained a doctorate in computer science from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), in 1982.[^o12][^o13] Also that year, he founded the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043) (IACR), which currently organizes academic conferences in cryptography research.[^o14] Subsequently, he taught at the [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) Graduate School of Business Administration and at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) (UCSB). He also formed a cryptography research group at [CWI](wikipedia://en/506063), the Dutch National Research Institute for Mathematics and Computer Science in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam).[^o15] He founded [DigiCash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigiCash), an electronic cash company, in 1990.[^o6]

Chaum received the Information Technology European Award for 1995.[^o16] In 2004, he was named an IACR Fellow.[^o17] In 2010, at the [RSA Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Conference), he was honored with the [RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Award_for_Excellence_in_Mathematics).[^o18] In 2019, he was awarded the honorary title of Dijkstra Fellow by CWI.[^o19] He received an honorary doctorate from the [University of Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_della_Svizzera_Italiana) in 2021.[^o20]

Chaum resides in [Sherman Oaks, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Oaks,_Los_Angeles).[^o21]

## Notable research contributions {#Notable_research_contributions}

### Vault systems {#Vault_systems}

Recently credited by [Alan Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Sherman)'s "On the Origins and Variations of Blockchain Technologies",[^o3] Chaum's 1982 Berkeley dissertation proposed every element of the [blockchain](wikipedia://en/44065971) found in [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265) except [proof of work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_work). The proposed vault system lays out a plan for achieving consensus state between nodes, chaining the history of consensus in blocks, and immutably time-stamping the chained data. The paper also lays out the specific code to implement such a protocol.

### Digital cash {#Digital_cash}

Chaum is credited as the inventor of secure digital cash for his 1983 paper, which also introduced the cryptographic primitive of a [blind signature](wikipedia://en/428273).[^o25] These ideas have been described as the technical roots of the vision of the [Cypherpunk](wikipedia://en/37314) movement that began in the late 1980s.[^o26] Chaum's proposal allowed users to obtain digital currency from a bank and spend it in a manner that is untraceable by the bank or any other party.[^o27] In 1988, he extended this idea (with [Amos Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Fiat) and [Moni Naor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moni_Naor)) to allow offline transactions that enable detection of double-spending.[^o28]

In 1990, he founded [DigiCash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigiCash), an electronic cash company, in Amsterdam to commercialize the ideas in his research.[^o6] The first electronic payment was sent in 1994.[^o29] In 1998, DigiCash filed for bankruptcy, and in 1999 Chaum sold off DigiCash and ended his involvement with the company.[^o15][^o30]

### Awards {#Awards}

In 2021, Chaum, [Claude Crépeau,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Crépeau) and [Ivan Damgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Damgård) received a [30-Year STOC Test of Time Award](https://sigact.org/prizes/stoc_tot.html) from the [ACM Special Interest Group on Algorithms and Computation Theory (SIGACT)](https://www.acm.org/special-interest-groups/sigs/sigact) for their 1988 paper, ["Multiparty Unconditionally Secure Protocols."](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/62212.62214) The paper was recognized as one of three seminal works on information-theoretically secure multiparty computation. <https://chaum.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/sigact-org-prizes-stoc-tot.pdf>

### New types of digital signatures {#New_types_of_digital_signatures}

In the same 1982 paper that proposed digital cash, Chaum introduced [blind signatures](wikipedia://en/428273).[^o25] This form of [digital signature](wikipedia://en/59644) blinds the content of a message before it is signed, so that the signer cannot determine the content. The resulting blind signature can be publicly verified against the original, unblinded message in the manner of a regular digital signature.[^o33]

In 1989, he (with Hans van Antwerpen) introduced [undeniable signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undeniable_signature).[^o34] This form of digital signature uses a verification process that is interactive, so that the signatory can limit who can verify the signature. Since signers may refuse to participate in the verification process, signatures are considered valid unless a signer specifically uses a disavowal protocol to prove that a given signature was not authentic.[^o35]

In 1991, he (with Eugene van Heyst) introduced [group signatures](wikipedia://en/3347478), which allow a member of a group to anonymously sign a message on behalf of the entire group.[^o36] However an appointed group manager holds the power to revoke the anonymity of any signer in the case of disputes.[^o37]

### Anonymous communication {#Anonymous_communication}

In 1981, Chaum proposed the idea of an anonymous communication network in a paper.[^o39] His proposal, called [mix networks](wikipedia://en/6200269), allows a group of senders to submit an encryption of a message and its recipient to a server. Once the server has a batch of messages, it will reorder and obfuscate the messages so that only this server knows which message came from which sender. The batch is then forwarded to another server who does the same process. Eventually, the messages reach the final server where they are fully decrypted and delivered to the recipient. A mechanism to allow return messages is also proposed. Mix networks are the basis of some [remailers](wikipedia://en/54666) and are the conceptual ancestor to modern anonymous web browsing tools like [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_%28anonymity_network%29) (based on [onion routing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion_routing)). Chaum has advocated that every router be made, effectively, a Tor node.

In 1988, Chaum introduced a different type of anonymous communication system called a DC-Net, which is a solution to his proposed [Dining Cryptographers Problem](wikipedia://en/17507355).[^o41] DC-Nets is the basis of the software tool Dissent.[^o42]

In 2017, Chaum published a description of a new variety of mix network.[^o43] A real-world implementation of this network, called cMix and running on the [xx network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xx_network), later became the [data transmission layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_transmission_protocol) for the [instant messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_messaging) platform [xx messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xx_messenger).[^o44]

### Trustworthy voting systems {#Trustworthy_voting_systems}

Chaum has made numerous contributions to secure voting systems, including the first proposal of a system that is [end-to-end verifiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End-to-end_auditable_voting_systems). This proposal, made in 1981,[^o39] was given as an application of mix networks. In this system, the individual ballots of voters were kept private which anyone could verify that the tally was counted correctly. This, and other early cryptographic voting systems, assumed that voters could reliably compute values with their personal computers. In 1991, Chaum introduced SureVote which allowed voters to cast a ballot from an untrustworthy voting system,[^o46] proposing a process now called "code voting" and used in remote voting systems like Remotegrity and DEMOS.[^o47][^o48]

In 1994, Chaum introduced the first in-person voting system in which voters cast ballots electronically at a polling station and cryptographically verify that the DRE did not modify their vote (or even learn what it was).[^o49] In the following years, Chaum proposed (often with others) a series a cryptographically verifiable voting systems that use conventional paper ballots: [Prêt à Voter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prêt_à_Voter),[^o50] [Punchscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punchscan),[^o51] and [Scantegrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scantegrity).[^o52] The city of [Takoma Park, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takoma_Park,_Maryland) used Scantegrity for its November, 2009 election.[^o53] This was the first time a public sector election was run using any cryptographically verifiable voting system.[^o54]

In 2011, Chaum proposed Random Sample Elections.[^o55] This electoral system allows a verifiably random selection of voters, who can maintain their anonymity, to cast votes on behalf the entire electorate.[^o56]

### Other contributions {#Other_contributions}

In a 1979 report published as Memorandum No. UCB/ERL M79/10[^o58] by the Electronics Research Laboratory at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), Chaum proposed a mechanism for splitting a cryptographic key into partial keys that could be distributed among mutually suspicious groups. This concept was a significant predecessor to what is now known as [secret sharing](wikipedia://en/458253).[^o59]

In 1985, Chaum proposed the original [anonymous credential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_credential) system,[^o27] which is sometimes also referred to as a pseudonym system.[^o60] This stems from the fact that the credentials of such a system are obtained from and shown to organizations using different pseudonyms that cannot be linked.

In 1988, Chaum with [Gilles Brassard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_Brassard) and [Claude Crépeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Crépeau) published a paper [^o61] that introduced [zero-knowledge arguments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-knowledge_proofs), as well as a security model using information-theoretic private channels, and also first formalized the concept of a [commitment scheme](wikipedia://en/439489).

1991, with Torben Pedersen, he demonstrated a well-cited [zero-knowledge proof](wikipedia://en/450541) of a [DDH tuple](wikipedia://en/1198015).[^o62] This proof is particularly useful as it can prove proper reencryption of an [ElGamal](wikipedia://en/59458) ciphertext.

Chaum contributed to an important [commitment scheme](wikipedia://en/439489) which is often attributed to Pedersen. In fact, Pedersen, in his 1991 paper,[^o63] cites a rump session talk on an unpublished paper by Jurjen Bos and Chaum for the scheme. It appeared even earlier in a paper by Chaum, Damgard, and Jeroen van de Graaf.[^o64]

In 1993 with [Stefan Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Brands), Chaum introduced the concept of a [distance-bounding protocol](wikipedia://en/9293361).[^o65]

In 2019, he was one of the speakers at the fifth [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum) developer conference, which was held in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan).[^o66]

In July 2024, Chaum was on a panel on the future of privacy at the Plasmacon conference at the [United Nations University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_University) in Tokyo, Japan, with [Vitalik Buterin](wikipedia://en/41729424), co-founder of [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum).[^o67]
