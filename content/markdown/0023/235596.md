# Association fallacy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Association fallacy"}

The **association fallacy** is a [formal fallacy](wikipedia://en/3637937) in which it is assumed that if a concept, whether physical or abstract, has certain properties, any other concept belonging to the same group as the first must also share these properties.

:::

When it is an attempt to win favor by exploiting the audience's preexisting spite or disdain for something else, it is called **guilt by association** or an **appeal to spite** (argumentum ad odium).[^o2] Guilt by association can be a component of [ad hominem](wikipedia://en/2226) arguments which attack the speaker rather than addressing the claims, but they are a distinct class of fallacious argument, and both are able to exist independently of the other.

## Set theory {#Set_theory}

*Image: Euler diagram.png.* An Euler diagram illustrating the association fallacy

Using [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the association fallacy may be written as follows:

**Premise**

A is in set S1

**Premise**

A is in set S2

**Premise**

B is also in set S2

**Conclusion**

Therefore, B is in set S1.

Using [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983), this fallacy may be expressed as$(S_1(A)\land S_2(A)\land S_2(B)) \rightarrow S_1(B)$.

The fallacy in the argument can be illustrated through the use of an [Euler diagram](wikipedia://en/2427526): A satisfies the requirement that it is part of both sets S1 and S2, but representing this as an Euler diagram makes it clear that B could be in S2 but not S1.

## Guilt by association {#Guilt_by_association}

This form of the argument is as follows:

- Group A makes a particular claim.
- Group B, which is currently viewed negatively by some, makes the same claim as Group A.
- Therefore, Group A is viewed as associated with Group B, and is now also viewed negatively.

An example of this fallacy would be "My opponent for office just received an endorsement from the Puppy Haters Association. Is that the sort of person you would want to vote for?"

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

Some [syllogistic](wikipedia://en/48046) examples of guilt by association:

- John is a con artist. John has black hair. Therefore, people with black hair are necessarily con artists.
- Lyle is a crooked salesman. Lyle proposes a monorail. Therefore, the proposed monorail is necessarily a folly.
- Country X is a dangerous country. Country X has a national [postal service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_service). Therefore, countries with national postal services are necessarily dangerous.
- Simon and Karl live in [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville), and they are both petty criminals. Jill lives in Nashville; therefore, Jill is necessarily a petty criminal.

Guilt by association can sometimes also be a type of *[ad hominem](wikipedia://en/2226)*, if the argument attacks a person because of the similarity between the views of someone making an argument and other proponents of the argument.[^o7][^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variations"}

## Variations {#Variations}

A form of the association fallacy often used by those denying a well-established scientific or historical proposition is the so-called **Galileo gambit** or **Galileo fallacy**.[^o11][^o12] The argument runs thus: [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo) was ridiculed in his time for his scientific observations, but was later acknowledged to be right; the proponent argues that since their non-mainstream views are provoking ridicule and rejection from other scientists, they will later be recognized as correct, like Galileo.[^o13] The gambit is flawed in that being ridiculed does not necessarily correlate with being right and that many people who have been ridiculed in history were, in fact, wrong.[^o11][^o14] Similarly, [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan) noted that people laughed at such geniuses as [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) and the [Wright brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers), but "they also laughed at [Bozo the Clown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_the_Clown)".[^o15][^o16]
