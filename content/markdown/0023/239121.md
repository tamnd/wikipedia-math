# Calculation

Computation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Calculation"}

A **calculation** is a deliberate [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) process that transforms a plurality of inputs into a singular or plurality of outputs, known also as a result or results. The term is used in a variety of senses, from the very definite [arithmetical](wikipedia://en/3118) calculation of using an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775), to the vague [heuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic) of calculating a strategy in a competition, or calculating the chance of a successful relationship between two people.

:::

For example, [multiplying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplying) 7 by 6 is a simple algorithmic calculation. Extracting the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) or the [cube root](wikipedia://en/292506) of a number using mathematical models is a more complex algorithmic calculation.

[Statistical estimations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_estimation) of the likely election results from opinion polls also involve algorithmic calculations, but produces ranges of possibilities rather than exact answers.

To *calculate* means to determine mathematically in the case of a number or amount, or in the case of an abstract problem to deduce the answer using [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [reason](wikipedia://en/42446) or [common sense](wikipedia://en/21478754).[^o2] The English word derives from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) calculus, which originally meant a [pebble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pebble) (from Latin calx), for instance the small stones used as a counters on an [abacus](wikipedia://en/655) (abacus, ἄβαξ). The abacus was an instrument used by Greeks and Romans for arithmetic calculations, preceding the [slide-rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide-rule) and the [electronic calculator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_calculator), and consisted of perforated pebbles sliding on iron bars.
