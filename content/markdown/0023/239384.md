# Population process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Population process"}

In [applied probability](wikipedia://en/366719), a **population process** is a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) in which the state of the chain is analogous to the number of individuals in a population (0, 1, 2, etc.), and changes to the state are analogous to the addition or removal of individuals from the population. Typical population processes include [birth–death processes](wikipedia://en/2276797) and [birth, death and catastrophe processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth,_death_and_catastrophe_process).[^o2]

:::

Although named by analogy to [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) populations from [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466), population processes find application in a much wider range of fields than just [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) and other biological sciences. These other applications include [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications) and [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), [chemical kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_kinetics) and [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics), and hence the *population* could be of [packets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packet_switching) in a [computer network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network), of [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule) in a [chemical reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction), or even of units in a [financial index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_index).

Population processes are typically characterized by processes of birth and immigration, and of death, emigration and catastrophe, which correspond to the basic [demographic](wikipedia://en/45642) processes and broad [environmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment) effects to which a population is subject. However, population processes are also often equivalent to other processes that may typically be characterized under other [paradigms](wikipedia://en/175308) (in the literal sense of "patterns"). [Queues](wikipedia://en/50578), for example, are often characterized by an arrivals process, a service process, and the number of servers. In appropriate circumstances, however, arrivals at a queue are functionally equivalent to births or immigration and the service of waiting "customers" is equivalent to death or emigration.
