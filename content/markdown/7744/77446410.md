# Mathematics on stamps

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematics on stamps"}

*Image: 450th birth anniversary of Copernicus Poland 1923.jpg.* [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus), Poland, 1923

*Image: Franklin SC1 1847.jpg.* [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin), USA, 1847

*Image: DR 1926 395 Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz.jpg.* [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281), Germany, 1926

The depiction of **mathematics on stamps** began in 1923 with the issue of a set of three Polish stamps commemorating the 450th birth anniversary of the astronomer and mathematician [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus). As the definition of what constitutes a valid mathematical stamp is not universally agreed, some collectors make the case that the first mathematical stamp was the 1847 United States issue depicting the polymath [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin) even though he was not primarily a mathematician;[^o2] others prefer to recognize the 1926 German [Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz) stamp as the first depicting a mathematician.[^o3]

:::

The total number of mathematical stamps that have been issued is approximately 1,600 as of 2024.[^o4][^o5]

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A mathematical stamp has one or more of the following characteristics:

- It depicts a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), or a [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) with a significant mathematical output[^o8]
- It depicts a [mathematical concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_concept) or [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961)
- It depicts a [mathematical symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_symbol), [formula](wikipedia://en/164040) or [notation](wikipedia://en/277184)
- It commemorates a mathematical event such as a national or [international congress](wikipedia://en/627842), or an [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208)
- It depicts the process of [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471),[^o2] or a building used primarily for mathematical research or education

Stamps illustrating [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) such as [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics), [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography), [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), [mathematical art](wikipedia://en/22468661), [mathematical games](wikipedia://en/26446), [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation), [perspective in art](wikipedia://en/380405) and [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) may or may not be included in a mathematical stamp collection depending on the breadth of view of the collector. Typically such stamps may depict a mathematically related object or instrument, e.g. an [astrolabe](wikipedia://en/73664) or a [slide rule](wikipedia://en/28743).

The following types of material are excluded (although they may also be collected by mathematical stamp enthusiasts):

- [Postal stationery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_stationery),[^o9] e.g. a postcard depicting a mathematician with a non-mathematical stamp affixed
- [Cinderella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_stamp), [local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_post), private or personal issues, i.e. unofficial stamps
- Non-postal stamps, e.g. [revenue stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_stamp)[^o10]
- Stamps issued by non-existing/unrecognized countries and/or in excess of actual postal requirements

## Examples {#Examples}

### Mathematicians {#Mathematicians}

Stamps depicting individual mathematicians are often issued by countries to commemorate the birth or death anniversaries of their significant national mathematicians, for example stamps issued by Norway celebrating [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573).[^o14][^o15] Examples are illustrated in the gallery above. Some countries have also issued stamps depicting internationally famous mathematicians such as [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)[^o16] or [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736).[^o17]

### Mathematical concepts and objects {#Mathematical_concepts_and_objects}

Stamps depicting a mathematical concept or object, sometimes combined with a portrait of the mathematician responsible for inventing the concept or object, are generally issued as [commemorative stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_stamp) rather than [definitive stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definitive_stamp). Examples are shown in the gallery above: a 1955 Greek stamp illustrating the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034), a 1967 Brazilian stamp illustrating a [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) and also commemorating the 6th Brazilian mathematical congress, a 1977 West German stamp illustrating the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) and also commemorating the 200th birth anniversary of [Carl Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gauss), and a 1998 German stamp illustrating [squaring a rectangle](wikipedia://en/67903) and also commemorating an International Mathematical Congress.

### Mathematical symbols and formulae {#Mathematical_symbols_and_formulae}

Stamps depicting a mathematical symbol or formula are frequently depicted together with the mathematician they are primarily associated with.[^o20] Examples are shown in the gallery above: a 1966 Soviet stamp illustrating the [integral](wikipedia://en/3171907), [summation](wikipedia://en/246160) and [union](wikipedia://en/47949) symbols and also commemorating an International Mathematical Congress, a 2000 Russian stamp illustrating the [pi](wikipedia://en/23601),[^o21] [function](wikipedia://en/185427) and [summation](wikipedia://en/246160) symbols and commemorating the life of [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228), a 2000 Czech stamp illustrating [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) and commemorating [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027)'s 1995 proof, and a 2023 Serbian stamp illustrating the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918) and commemorating the 75th anniversary of the Mathematical Society of Serbia.

:::

## Publications {#Publications}

[Robin J. Wilson](wikipedia://en/2715277) is the leading writer in the field having published a well-reviewed[^o23][^o24][^o25] book entitled *Stamping through mathematics* in 2001,[^o2] a paper on European mathematical history through stamps,[^o26] and also contributing the Stamp Corner column to *[The Mathematical Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Intelligencer)* starting in 1984.[^o4]

William L. Schaaf published a paper,[^o27] later expanded into a book entitled *Mathematics and science: an adventure in postage stamps* in 1978.[^o28] From 1979 until at least 2011 the Mathematical Study Unit of the [American Topical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Topical_Association) published *Philamat: A journal of mathematical philately* but this organization is no longer part of the ATA as of 2024. A sample issue is available online.[^o29]

Listings of new issues of mathematical stamps are included in the monthly [Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_catalogue) Stamp magazine and in [Linn's Stamp News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn's_Stamp_News); they are also available online from October 2010 to date in the Computers and Mathematics section.[^o30]
