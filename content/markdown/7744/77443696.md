# Bing double

*Image: Bing double of unknot.svg.* The [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) (left) and its Bing double (right).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bing double"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), a field of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Bing double** of a knot is a link with two components which follow the pattern of the knot and "hook together". Bing doubles were introduced in (Bing 1952) by their namesake, the American mathematician [R. H. Bing](wikipedia://en/967353).[^n1] The Bing double of a [slice knot](wikipedia://en/4158126) is a [slice link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slice_link), though it is unknown whether the converse is true.[^n2] The components of a Bing double bound disjoint [Seifert surfaces](wikipedia://en/1343951).[^n2]

:::

*Image: Solid torus surrounding Bing double of unknot.svg.* A [solid torus](wikipedia://en/1251473) encasing the Bing double of the unknot.

The Bing double of a knot $K$ is defined by placing the Bing double of the unknot in the [solid torus](wikipedia://en/1251473) surrounding it, as shown in the figure, and then twisting that solid torus into the shape of $K$.[^n2] This definition is similar to that for [Whitehead doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead_double). The Bing double of the unknot is also called the **Bing link**.[^n3]
