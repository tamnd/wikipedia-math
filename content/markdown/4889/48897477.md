# Quotient of an abelian category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quotient of an abelian category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **quotient** (also called **Serre quotient** or **Gabriel quotient**) of an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) $\mathcal{A}$ by a [Serre subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_subcategory) $\mathcal{B}$ is the abelian category $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{B}$ which, intuitively, is obtained from $\mathcal{A}$ by ignoring (i.e. treating as [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-object)) all [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) from $\mathcal{B}$. There is a canonical [exact](wikipedia://en/453945) [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $Q \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}/\mathcal{B}$ whose kernel is $\mathcal B$, and $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{B}$ is in a certain sense the most general abelian category with this property.

:::

Forming Serre quotients of abelian categories is thus formally akin to forming [quotients of groups](wikipedia://en/11526). Serre quotients are somewhat similar to [quotient categories](wikipedia://en/4004632), the difference being that with Serre quotients all involved categories are abelian and all functors are exact. Serre quotients also often have the character of [localizations of categories](wikipedia://en/611460), especially if the Serre subcategory is [localizing](wikipedia://en/44974120).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Formally, $\mathcal A/\mathcal B$ is the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) whose objects are those of $\mathcal A$ and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) from *X* to *Y* are given by the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) (of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974))

$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal A/\mathcal B}(X,Y):=\varinjlim \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal A(X', Y/Y')$$

where the limit is taken over [subobjects](wikipedia://en/540476) $X' \subseteq X$ and $Y' \subseteq Y$ such that $X/X'\in \cal{B}$ and $Y' \in \cal{B}$. (Here, $X/X'$ and $Y/Y'$ denote [quotient objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_object) computed in $\mathcal A$.) These pairs of subobjects are ordered by $(X',Y')\preccurlyeq(X'',Y'') \Longleftrightarrow X''\subseteq X' \text{ and } Y'\subseteq Y''$.

Composition of morphisms in $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{B}$ is induced by the [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) of the direct limit.

The canonical functor $Q \colon \mathcal A \to \mathcal A/\mathcal B$ sends an object *X* to itself and a morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ to the corresponding element of the direct limit with *X′* = X and *Y′* = 0.

An alternative, equivalent construction of the quotient category uses what is called a "[calculus of fractions](wikipedia://en/611460)" to define the morphisms of $\mathcal A/\mathcal B$. Here, one starts with the class $S$ of those morphisms in $\mathcal A$ whose kernel and cokernel both belong to $\mathcal{B}$. This is a multiplicative system in the sense of Gabriel-Zisman, and one can localize the category $\mathcal A$ at the system $S$ to obtain $\mathcal A/\mathcal B:=\mathcal A[S^{-1}]$.[^o4]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Let $k$ be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) and consider the abelian category ${\rm Mod}(k)$ of all [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over $k$. Then the full subcategory ${\rm mod}(k)$ of finite-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) vector spaces is a Serre-subcategory of ${\rm Mod}(k)$. The Serre quotient $\cal{C} = {\rm Mod}(k)/{\rm mod}(k)$ has as objects the $k$-vector spaces, and the set of morphisms from $X$ to $Y$ in $\cal{C}$ is

$$\{k\text{-linear maps from } X \text{ to } Y\}/\{k\text{-linear maps from } X \text{ to } Y \text{ with finite-dimensional image}\}$$

(which is a [quotient of vector spaces](wikipedia://en/898784)). This has the effect of identifying all finite-dimensional vector spaces with 0, and of identifying two [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) whenever their difference has finite-dimensional [image](wikipedia://en/579311). This example shows that the Serre quotient can behave like a [quotient category](wikipedia://en/4004632).

For another example, take the [abelian category **Ab** of all abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748) and the Serre subcategory of all [torsion abelian groups](wikipedia://en/46340931). The Serre quotient here is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category $\operatorname{Mod}(\mathbb{Q})$ of all vector spaces over the rationals, with the canonical functor $\mathbf{Ab}\to\operatorname{Mod}(\mathbb{Q})$ given by tensoring with $\mathbb{Q}$. Similarly, the Serre quotient of the category of finitely generated abelian groups by the subcategory of finitely generated torsion groups is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional vectorspaces over $\mathbb{Q}$.[^o7] Here, the Serre quotient behaves like a [localization](wikipedia://en/611460).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The Serre quotient $\mathcal A/\mathcal B$ is an abelian category, and the canonical functor $Q \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}/\mathcal{B}$ is [exact](wikipedia://en/453945) and surjective on objects. The kernel of $Q$ is $\mathcal B$, i.e., $Q(X)$ is [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-object) in $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{B}$ if and only if $X$ belongs to $\mathcal{B}$.

The Serre quotient and canonical functor are characterized by the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245): if $\mathcal C$ is any abelian category and $F \colon \mathcal A \to \mathcal C$ is an exact functor such that $F(X)$ is a zero in *$\mathcal C$* for each object $X \in \mathcal B$, then there is a unique exact functor $\overline{F} \colon \mathcal A/\mathcal B \to \mathcal C$ such that $F = \overline{F} \circ Q$.[^o10]

Given three abelian categories $\mathcal{A}$, $\mathcal{B}$, $\mathcal{C}$, we have

$$\mathcal A/\mathcal B \cong \mathcal C$$

if and only if

there exists an exact and [essentially surjective](wikipedia://en/4557668) functor $F \colon \mathcal A \to \mathcal C$ whose kernel is $\mathcal{B}$ and such that for every morphism $f:FX\to FY$ in $\mathcal C$ there exist morphisms $\phi:W\to X$ and $\psi:W\to Y$ in $\mathcal A$ so that $F\phi$ is an isomorphism and $f=(F\psi)\circ(F\phi)^{-1}$.

## Theorems involving Serre quotients {#Theorems_involving_Serre_quotients}

### Serre's description of coherent sheaves on a projective scheme {#Serre's_description_of_coherent_sheaves_on_a_projective_scheme}

According to a theorem by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), the category $\operatorname{coh}(X)$ of [coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/603780) on a projective scheme $X=\operatorname{Proj}(R)$ (where $R$ is a commutative [noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094), graded by the non-negative integers and generated by degree-0 and finitely many degree-1 elements, and $\operatorname{Proj}(R)$ refers to the [Proj construction](wikipedia://en/2285000)) can be described as the Serre quotient

$$\operatorname{coh}(X) \cong \operatorname{mod}^\mathbb{Z}(R)\ / \ \operatorname{mod}_{\mathrm{tor}}^\mathbb{Z}(R)$$

where $\operatorname{mod}^\mathbb{Z}(R)$ denotes the category of finitely-generated graded modules over $R$ and $\operatorname{mod}_{\mathrm{tor}}^\mathbb{Z}(R)$ is the Serre subcategory consisting of all those graded modules $M$ which are 0 in all degrees that are high enough, i.e. for which there exists $n_0\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $M_n=0$ for all $n\geq n_0$.[^o14][^o15]

A similar description exists for the category of [quasi-coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaves) on $X=\operatorname{Proj}(R)$, even if $R$ is not noetherian.

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Gabriel–Popescu theorem"}

### Gabriel–Popescu theorem {#Gabriel–Popescu_theorem}

The [Gabriel–Popescu theorem](wikipedia://en/35884241) states that any [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538) $\mathcal{A}$ is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to a Serre quotient of the form $\operatorname{Mod}(R)/\cal{B}$, where $\operatorname{Mod}(R)$ denotes the abelian category of right [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over some [unital ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_ring) $R$, and $\cal{B}$ is some [localizing subcategory](wikipedia://en/44974120) of $\operatorname{Mod}(R)$.[^o18]

:::

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Quillen's localization theorem"}

### Quillen's localization theorem {#Quillen's_localization_theorem}

[Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519)'s [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) assigns to each [exact category](wikipedia://en/10987985) $\mathcal{C}$ a sequence of abelian groups $K_n(\mathcal C),\ n\geq 0$, and this assignment is functorial in $\mathcal{C}$. Quillen proved that, if $\mathcal{B}$ is a Serre subcategory of the abelian category $\mathcal{A}$, there is a [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence) of the form[^o21]

$$\cdots \to K_n(\mathcal B) \to K_n(\mathcal A) \to K_n(\mathcal {A/B})\to K_{n-1}(\mathcal B)\to\cdots\to K_0(\mathcal {A/B})\to 0$$
