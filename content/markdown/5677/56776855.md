# Jane Wadsworth

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jane Wadsworth"}

**Jane Wadsworth** (1942–1997)[^o3] was a medical [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and a pioneer in academic sexual health research.[^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Wadsworth was born in 1942, during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), as the eldest of four children. Her family moved to [Sevenoaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevenoaks), where her father established a GP practice after his demobilisation.

## Career {#Career}

Following an education at [West Heath Girls' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Heath_Girls'_School), Wadsworth went on to study mathematics at [St. Andrews University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), Scotland.[^o7] She met her husband, Michael Wadsworth, in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) and the couple moved to [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh), where they started their young family. Following their return to London, Jane began working part-time at the Institute for Social Sciences in Medical Care.

When her youngest child began school, Wadsworth returned to education and completed an MSc in Medical Statistics at the [London School of Hygiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_&_Tropical_Medicine). This was followed by a series of research positions in London, [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol) and [Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter), before she took on the position of Lecturer in Medical Statistics at [St Mary's Hospital Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Hospital_Medical_School) in 1983. While with the hospital, Wadsworth contributed to several clinical studies, inclining a study of pelvic pain syndrome with Professor Richard Beard and the Nation Childhood Encephalopathy Study with Professor David Miller.

When the AIDS epidemic arrived, Wadsworth became involved in determining the pattern of the [HIV infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS) throughout Britain. This was the first attempt to conduct a study about sexual behaviour in the UK, and gave her the opportunity to take the leading role in initiating her own research programme for the first time. During this period, Wadsworth's personal life became more strained, however, and her marriage broke up in the late 1980s.[^o7]

After several years of laying the foundations for sex research, Wadsworth, together with Julia Field, Anne Johnson and Kaye Wellings, embarked on a national study, during which they interviewed 18,876 men and women about their sex lives.[^o8] The study was filmed by *[Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_%28British_TV_programme%29)* for television, and the women briefly found themselves in the eye of the media.[^o8][^o7] In 1994, Wadsworth and her fellow researchers published *Sexual Attitudes and Lifestyle*, detailing the results of the survey. A version of this, *Sexual Behaviour in Britain*, was serialised in the [*Independent* on Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent). As the first of its kind, the [National Survey of Sexual Attitudes and Lifestyle](wikipedia://en/11211918) (NATSAL) became both the gold standard and the model for subsequent studies for a number of countries around the world.[^o7]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

The Jane Wadsworth clinic for Sexual Health[^o10] of the Jefferiss wing at [St. Mary's Hospital, Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Hospital,_London), was named for her.
