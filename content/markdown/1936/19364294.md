# Calibrated geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Calibrated geometry"}

article In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **calibrated manifold** is a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) (*M*,*g*) of dimension *n* equipped with a [differential *p*-form](wikipedia://en/221519) *φ* (for some 0 ≤ *p* ≤ *n*) which is a **calibration,** meaning that:

:::

- *φ* is [closed](wikipedia://en/404181), that is, d*φ* = 0, where d is the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782).
- *φ* has [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722) at most 1. That is, for any *x* ∈ *M* and any [*p*-vector](wikipedia://en/2727288) $\xi \in \Lambda^p T_x M$, we have *φ*(*ξ*) ≤ vol(*ξ*), with volume defined with respect to the Riemannian metric *g*.

A main reason for defining a calibration is that it creates a distinguished set of "directions" (i.e. *p*-planes) in which *φ* is actually equal to the volume form, that is, the inequality above is an equality. For *x* in *M*, set *G*<sub>*x*</sub>(*φ*) to be the subset of such planes in the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of *p*-planes in T*<sub>x</sub>M*. In cases of interest, *G*<sub>*x*</sub>(*φ*) is always nonempty. Let *G*(*φ*) be the union of *G*<sub>*x*</sub>(*φ*) for all $x \in M$, viewed as a subspace of the bundle of *p*-planes in T*M*.

## History {#History}

[Harvey](wikipedia://en/65653589) and [Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson) introduced the term *calibration* and developed the theory in 1982,[^o3] but the subject has a long prehistory.[^o4]

The first motivating example, that of Kähler manifolds, is due implicitly to [Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921) in 1936[^o5] and explicitly to [de Rham](wikipedia://en/398699) in 1957.[^o6] In 1965, [Federer](wikipedia://en/12185345) used this to construct the first examples of singular minimal submanifolds.[^o7]

Soon afterwards, the other main examples were introduced. [Edmond Bonan](wikipedia://en/39597334) studied [G<sub>2</sub>-manifolds](wikipedia://en/648062) and [Spin(7)-manifolds](wikipedia://en/3330144) in 1966,[^o8] constructing all the parallel forms and showing that such manifolds must be [Ricci-flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci-flat), although examples of either would not be constructed for another 20 years until the work of [Robert Bryant](wikipedia://en/24204031). [Quaternion-Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/2640559) were studied simultaneously in 1965 by [Edmond Bonan](wikipedia://en/39597334)[^o9] and Vivian Yoh Kraines,[^o10] each of whom constructed the parallel 4-form. Finally, in 1970, [Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363) gave the general argument that calibrated submanifolds are minimal and applied it to these cases.[^o11]

## Calibrated submanifolds {#Calibrated_submanifolds}

A *p*-dimensional submanifold *Σ* of *M* is said to be a **calibrated submanifold** with respect to *φ* (or simply *φ*-calibrated) if *φ*|<sub>*Σ*</sub> = vol<sub>*Σ*</sub>. Equivalently, T*Σ* lies in *G*(*φ*).

A famous one-line argument shows that calibrated closed submanifolds minimize volume within their [homology class](wikipedia://en/142432). Indeed, suppose that *Σ* is calibrated, and *Σ*′ is a submanifold in the same homology class. Then

$$\int_\Sigma \mathrm{vol}_\Sigma = \int_\Sigma \varphi = \int_{\Sigma'} \varphi \leq \int_{\Sigma'} \mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma'},$$

where the first equality holds because *Σ* is calibrated, the second equality is [Stokes' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Stokes'_theorem) (as *φ* is closed), and the inequality holds because *φ* has operator norm 1.

The same argument shows that even a noncompact calibrated submanifold is a [minimal submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_submanifold) in the variational sense, and therefore has zero [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210).

In particular, [affine complex algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/320468) are locally area-minimizing. Federer used this to give some of the first examples of singular minimal submanifolds, such as the [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) $\{w^2=z^3\} \subset \mathbb{C}^2$.[^o7][^o4]

## Examples {#Examples}

- On a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538), suitably normalized powers of the [Kähler form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_form) are calibrations, and the calibrated submanifolds are the [complex submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_submanifold). This follows from the [Wirtinger inequality](wikipedia://en/12096417).
- On a [Calabi–Yau manifold](wikipedia://en/188932), the real part of a holomorphic volume form (suitably normalized) is a calibration, and the calibrated submanifolds are [special Lagrangian submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Lagrangian_submanifold).
- On a [G<sub>2</sub>-manifold](wikipedia://en/648062), both the parallel 3-form and its Hodge dual 4-form define calibrations. The corresponding calibrated submanifolds are called associative and coassociative submanifolds.
- On a [Spin(7)-manifold](wikipedia://en/3330144), the defining 4-form, known as the Cayley form, is a calibration. The corresponding calibrated submanifolds are called Cayley submanifolds.

:::
