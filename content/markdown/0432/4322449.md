# Filtered category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Filtered category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), **filtered categories** generalize the notion of [directed set](wikipedia://en/9165) understood as a category (hence called a directed category; while some use directed category as a synonym for a filtered category). There is a dual notion of **cofiltered** category, which will be recalled below.

:::

## Filtered categories {#Filtered_categories}

A [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $J$ is **filtered** when

- it is not empty,
- for every two objects $j$ and $j'$ in $J$ there exists an object $k$ and two arrows $f:j\to k$ and $f':j'\to k$ in $J$,
- for every two parallel arrows $u,v:i\to j$ in $J$, there exists an object $k$ and an arrow $w:j\to k$ such that $wu=wv$.

A **filtered colimit** is a [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) of a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $F:J\to C$ where $J$ is a filtered category.

## Cofiltered categories {#Cofiltered_categories}

A category $J$ is cofiltered if the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) $J^{\mathrm{op}}$ is filtered. In detail, a category is cofiltered when

- it is not empty,
- for every two objects $j$ and $j'$ in $J$ there exists an object $k$ and two arrows $f:k\to j$ and $f':k \to j'$ in $J$,
- for every two parallel arrows $u,v:j\to i$ in $J$, there exists an object $k$ and an arrow $w:k\to j$ such that $uw=vw$.

A **cofiltered limit** is a [limit](wikipedia://en/39248) of a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $F:J \to C$ where $J$ is a cofiltered category.

## Ind-objects and pro-objects {#Ind-objects_and_pro-objects}

Given a [small category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category) $C$, a [presheaf](wikipedia://en/4840944) of sets $C^{op}\to Set$ that is a small filtered colimit of representable presheaves, is called an **ind-object** of the category $C$. Ind-objects of a category $C$ form a full subcategory $Ind(C)$ in the category of functors (presheaves) $C^{op}\to Set$. The category $Pro(C)=Ind(C^{op})^{op}$ of pro-objects in $C$ is the opposite of the category of ind-objects in the opposite category $C^{op}$.

## κ-filtered categories {#κ-filtered_categories}

There is a variant of "filtered category" known as a "κ-filtered category", defined as follows. This begins with the following observation: the three conditions in the definition of filtered category above say respectively that there exists a [cocone](wikipedia://en/7533645) over any diagram in $J$ of the form $\{\ \ \}\rightarrow J$, $\{j\ \ \ j'\}\rightarrow J$, or $\{i\rightrightarrows j\}\rightarrow J$. The existence of cocones for these three shapes of diagrams turns out to imply that cocones exist for *any* finite diagram; in other words, a category $J$ is filtered (according to the above definition) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) there is a cocone over any *finite* diagram $d: D\to J$.

Extending this, given a [regular cardinal](wikipedia://en/374128) κ, a category $J$ is defined to be κ-filtered if there is a cocone over every diagram $d$ in $J$ of [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) smaller than κ. (A small [diagram](wikipedia://en/7651625) is of cardinality κ if the [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) set of its domain is of cardinality κ.)

A κ-filtered colimit is a colimit of a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $F:J\to C$ where $J$ is a κ-filtered category.
