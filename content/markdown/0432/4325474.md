# Geometric analysis

*Image: Saddle Tower Minimal Surfaces.png.* [Saddle tower](wikipedia://en/36763480) minimal surface. [Minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) are among the objects of study in geometric analysis.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric analysis"}

**Geometric analysis** is a [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) discipline where tools from [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), especially [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787) (PDEs), are used to establish new results in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562). The use of [linear](wikipedia://en/379868) elliptic PDEs dates at least as far back as [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). More recently, it refers largely to the use of [nonlinear partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_partial_differential_equations) to study geometric and topological properties of spaces, such as [submanifolds](wikipedia://en/525095) of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652), and [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356). This approach dates back to the work by [Tibor Radó](wikipedia://en/1490393) and [Jesse Douglas](wikipedia://en/387571) on [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734), [John Forbes Nash Jr.](wikipedia://en/102567) on [isometric](wikipedia://en/254777) [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) of Riemannian manifolds into Euclidean space, work by [Louis Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725) on the [Minkowski problem](wikipedia://en/27966551) and the Weyl problem, and work by [Aleksandr Danilovich Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Danilovich_Aleksandrov) and [Aleksei Pogorelov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Pogorelov) on [convex](wikipedia://en/12677098) [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101). In the 1980s fundamental contributions by [Karen Uhlenbeck](wikipedia://en/4384796),[^o2] [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920), [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137), [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen), and [Richard Hamilton](wikipedia://en/758911) launched a particularly exciting and productive era of geometric analysis that continues to this day. A celebrated achievement was the solution to the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798) by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266), completing a program initiated and largely carried out by Richard Hamilton.

:::

## Scope {#Scope}

The scope of geometric analysis includes both the use of [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) methods in the study of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) (when it is also known as "geometric PDE"), and the application of the theory of partial differential equations to geometry. It incorporates problems involving curves and surfaces, or domains with curved boundaries, but also the study of [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) in arbitrary dimension. The [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) is sometimes regarded as part of geometric analysis, because differential equations arising from [variational principles](wikipedia://en/491097) have a strong geometric content. Geometric analysis also includes [global analysis](wikipedia://en/42027498), which concerns the study of differential equations on [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), and the relationship between differential equations and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

The following is a partial list of major topics within geometric analysis:

- [Gauge theory](wikipedia://en/64324611)
- [Harmonic maps](wikipedia://en/4577484)
- [Kähler–Einstein metrics](wikipedia://en/7335270)
- [Mean curvature flow](wikipedia://en/13110176)
- [Minimal submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_submanifold)
- [Positive energy theorems](wikipedia://en/2608111)
- [Pseudoholomorphic curves](wikipedia://en/2294542)
- [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291)
- [Yamabe problem](wikipedia://en/11757994)
- [Yang–Mills equations](wikipedia://en/35544664)
