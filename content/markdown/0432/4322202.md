# Kulkarni–Nomizu product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kulkarni–Nomizu product"}

In the mathematical field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Kulkarni–Nomizu product** (named for [Ravindra Shripad Kulkarni](wikipedia://en/50081918) and [Katsumi Nomizu](wikipedia://en/37009604)) is defined for two (0, 2)-[tensors](wikipedia://en/29965) and gives as a result a (0, 4)-tensor.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If *h* and *k* are symmetric (0, 2)-tensors, then the product is defined via:[^o4]

$$\begin{aligned} (h {~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~} k)(X_1, X_2, X_3, X_4) :={} &h(X_1, X_3)k(X_2, X_4) + h(X_2, X_4)k(X_1, X_3) \\ &{}- h(X_1, X_4)k(X_2, X_3) - h(X_2, X_3)k(X_1, X_4) \\[3pt] {}={} &\begin{vmatrix} h(X_1, X_3) &h (X_1, X_4)\\ k(X_2, X_3) &k (X_2, X_4) \end{vmatrix} + \begin{vmatrix} k(X_1, X_3) &k (X_1, X_4)\\ h(X_2, X_3) &h (X_2, X_4) \end{vmatrix} \end{aligned}$$

where the *X*<sub>*j*</sub> are tangent vectors and $|\cdot|$ is the [matrix determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_determinant). Note that $h {~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~} k = k {~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~} h$, as it is clear from the second expression.

With respect to a basis $\{\partial_i\}$ of the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807), it takes the compact form

$$(h~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~k)_{ijlm} = (h{~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~}k )(\partial_i, \partial_j, \partial_l,\partial_m) = 2h_{i [l}k_{m]j} + 2h_{j [m}k_{l]i}\,,$$

where $[\dots]$ denotes the [total antisymmetrisation symbol](wikipedia://en/696955#Notation).

The Kulkarni–Nomizu product is a special case of the product in the graded algebra

$$\bigoplus_{p=1}^n S^2\left(\Omega^p M\right),$$

where, on simple elements,

$$(\alpha\cdot\beta) {~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~} (\gamma\cdot\delta) = (\alpha\wedge\gamma)\odot(\beta\wedge\delta)$$

($\odot$ denotes the [symmetric product](wikipedia://en/23087738#Symmetric_product)).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Kulkarni–Nomizu product of a pair of symmetric tensors has the algebraic symmetries of the [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor).[^o7] For instance, on [space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009) (i.e. spaces of constant [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618)) and two-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifolds, the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) has a simple expression in terms of the Kulkarni–Nomizu product of the [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) $g=g_{ij}dx^i\otimes dx^j$ with itself; namely, if we denote by

$$\operatorname{R}(\partial_i, \partial_j) \partial_k = {R^l}_{ijk} \partial_l$$

the (1, 3)-curvature tensor and by

$$\operatorname{Rm}=R_{ijkl} dx^i\otimes dx^j\otimes dx^k\otimes dx^l$$

the Riemann curvature tensor with $R_{ijkl}= g_{im} {R^m}_{jkl}$, then

$$\operatorname{Rm}=\frac{\operatorname{Scal}}{4} g~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~g,$$

where $\operatorname{Scal}=\operatorname{tr}_g\operatorname{Ric}={R^i}_i$ is the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) and

$$\operatorname{Ric}(Y,Z) = \operatorname{tr}_g\lbrace X\mapsto\operatorname{R}(X,Y)Z\rbrace$$

is the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor), which in components reads $R_{ij}={R^k}_{ikj}$. Expanding the Kulkarni–Nomizu product $g~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~g$ using the definition from above, one obtains

$$R_{ijkl} = \frac{\operatorname{Scal}}{4} g_{i[k} g_{l]j} = \frac{\operatorname{Scal}}{2} ( g_{ik} g_{jl} - g_{il} g_{jk} )\,.$$

This is the same expression as stated in the article on the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526).

For this very reason, it is commonly used to express the contribution that the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) (or rather, the [Schouten tensor](wikipedia://en/6129873)) and the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848) each makes to the [curvature](wikipedia://en/651361) of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652). This so-called [Ricci decomposition](wikipedia://en/2427587) is useful in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

When there is a [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) *g*, the Kulkarni–Nomizu product of *g* with itself is the identity endomorphism of the space of 2-forms, Ω<sup>2</sup>(*M*), under the identification (using the metric) of the [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) End(Ω<sup>2</sup>(*M*)) with the tensor product Ω<sup>2</sup>(*M*) ⊗ Ω<sup>2</sup>(*M*).

A Riemannian manifold has constant [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) *k* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the Riemann tensor has the form

$$R = \frac{k}{2}g {~\wedge\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;\bigcirc~} g$$

where *g* is the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795).
