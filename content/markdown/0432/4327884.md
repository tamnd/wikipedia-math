# Bethe ansatz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bethe ansatz"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **Bethe ansatz** is used to calculate the exact [wave functions](wikipedia://en/145343) of certain quantum-mechanical many-body systems, most commonly in one spatial dimension. This [ansatz](wikipedia://en/1359360) was introduced by [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe) in 1931 to obtain the exact [eigenvalues and eigenvectors](wikipedia://en/2161429) of the one-dimensional [antiferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiferromagnetism) isotropic (XXX) [Heisenberg model](wikipedia://en/4899779).[^o2]

:::

The approach was later generalized into the [quantum inverse scattering method](wikipedia://en/25672752) (QISM) and the [algebraic Bethe ansatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_Bethe_ansatz), forming the basis of modern [integrable system](wikipedia://en/2573213) theory.[^o3][^o4]

Since then, the method has been extended to other [spin chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_chain), statistical [lattice models](wikipedia://en/291472) and [generalized hydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/82737261).[^o5]

"Bethe ansatz problems" were one of the topics featuring in the "To learn" section of [Richard Feynman](wikipedia://en/25523)'s blackboard at the time of his death.[^o6]

## Discussion {#Discussion}

In the framework of many-body [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), models solvable by the Bethe ansatz can be contrasted with free [fermion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) models. The dynamics of a free model is one-body reducible: its many-body wave function for [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) ([bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson)) is the antisymmetrized (symmetrized) product of one-body wave functions. Models solvable by the Bethe ansatz are interacting systems: their two-body sector has a nontrivial [scattering matrix](wikipedia://en/622053) that depends on the particle momenta.

By contrast, such models are two-body reducible: the many-body scattering matrix factorizes into a product of two-body scattering matrices. Many-body collisions occur as sequences of pairwise interactions, and the total wave function can be represented entirely in terms of two-body scattering states. The overall scattering matrix equals the ordered product of these pairwise matrices.

$$\Psi_M(j_1, \cdots, j_M) = \prod_{M \geq a > b \geq 1} \operatorname{sgn}(j_a - j_b) \sum_{P \in \mathfrak S_M} (-1)^{[P]} \exp \left(i \sum_{a=1}^M k_{P_a} j_a + \frac{i}{2}\sum_{M \geq a > b \geq 1} \operatorname{sgn}(j_a - j_b) \phi(k_{P_a}, k_{P_b})\right),$$

where $M$ is the number of particles, $j_a\ (a = 1, \cdots M)$ are their position, $\mathfrak S_M$ is the set of all permutations of the integers $1, \cdots, M$; $(-1)^{[P]} = \pm 1$ is the parity of the permutation $P$; $k_a$ is the (quasi-)momentum of the $a$-th particle, $\phi$ is the scattering phase shift function and $\operatorname{sgn}$ is the [sign function](wikipedia://en/505449). This form is universal (at least for non-nested systems), with the momentum and scattering functions being model-dependent.

The [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810) guarantees consistency of the construction.[^o8] The [Pauli exclusion principle](wikipedia://en/24669) is valid for models solvable by the Bethe ansatz, even for models of interacting [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosons).

The [ground state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state) is a [Fermi sphere](wikipedia://en/986096). [Periodic boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/9667552) lead to the Bethe ansatz equations or simply Bethe equations. In logarithmic form the Bethe ansatz equations can be generated by the [Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen-Ning_Yang) action. The square of the norm of Bethe wave function is equal to the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the [Hessian](wikipedia://en/412108) of the Yang action.[^o9]

A substantial generalization is the [quantum inverse scattering method](wikipedia://en/25672752), or algebraic Bethe ansatz, which gives an ansatz for the underlying [operator algebra](wikipedia://en/455987) that "has allowed a wide class of nonlinear evolution equations to be solved".[^o10]

The exact solutions of the so-called *s-d* model (by P. B. Wiegmann[^o11] in 1980 and independently by N. Andrei,[^o12] also in 1980) and the Anderson model (by P. B. Wiegmann[^o13] in 1981, and by N. Kawakami and A. Okiji[^o14] in 1981) are also both based on the Bethe ansatz. There exist multi-channel generalizations of these two models also amenable to exact solutions (by N. Andrei and C. Destri[^o15] and by C. J. Bolech and N. Andrei[^o16]). Recently several models solvable by Bethe ansatz were realized experimentally in solid states and optical lattices. An important role in the theoretical description of these experiments was played by Jean-Sébastien Caux and [Alexei Tsvelik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Tsvelik).

## Examples {#Examples}

### Heisenberg antiferromagnetic chain {#Heisenberg_antiferromagnetic_chain}

The Heisenberg antiferromagnetic chain is defined by the Hamiltonian (assuming periodic boundary conditions)

$$H = J \sum_{j=1}^N \mathbf{S}_j \cdot \mathbf{S}_{j+1}, \quad \mathbf{S}_{j+N} \equiv \mathbf{S}_j.$$

This model is solvable using the (coordinate) Bethe ansatz. The scattering phase shift function is $\phi\big(k_a(\lambda_a), k_b(\lambda_b)\big) = \theta_2(\lambda_a - \lambda_b)$ with $\theta_n(\lambda) \equiv 2 \arctan(2\lambda/n),$ in which the momentum has been conveniently reparametrized as $k(\lambda) = \pi - 2 \arctan 2\lambda$ in terms of the *rapidity* $\lambda.$ The boundary conditions (periodic here) impose the *Bethe equations*

$$\left[\frac{\lambda_a + i/2}{\lambda_a - i/2}\right]^N = \prod_{b \neq a}^M \frac{\lambda_a - \lambda_b + i}{\lambda_a - \lambda_b - i}, \quad a = 1, \dots, M,$$

or more conveniently in logarithmic form

$$\theta_1(\lambda_a) - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{b = 1}^M \theta_2(\lambda_a - \lambda_b) = 2\pi \frac{I_a}{N},$$

where the quantum numbers $I_j$ are distinct half-odd integers for $N - M$ even, integers for $N - M$ odd (with $I_j$ defined $\bmod N$).

:::

## Applicability {#Applicability}

The following systems can be solved using the Bethe ansatz

- [Anderson impurity model](wikipedia://en/12305030)
- [Gaudin model](wikipedia://en/71851317)
- XXX and XXZ [Heisenberg spin chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_spin_chain) for arbitrary spin $s$
- [Hubbard model](wikipedia://en/1702398)
- [Kondo model](wikipedia://en/33331939)
- [Lieb–Liniger model](wikipedia://en/24952147)
- [Six-vertex model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-vertex_model) and [Eight-vertex model](wikipedia://en/20095931) (through Heisenberg spin chain)

## Chronology {#Chronology}

section

- 1928: [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg) publishes [his model](wikipedia://en/4899779).[^o29]
- 1930: [Felix Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Bloch) proposes an oversimplified ansatz which miscounts the number of solutions to the Schrödinger equation for the Heisenberg chain.[^o30]
- 1931: [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe) proposes the correct ansatz and carefully shows that it yields the correct number of eigenfunctions.[^o2]
- 1938: Lamek Hulthén obtains the exact ground-state energy of the Heisenberg model.[^o31]
- 1958: [Raymond Lee Orbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Lee_Orbach) uses the Bethe ansatz to solve the Heisenberg model with anisotropic interactions.[^o32]
- 1962: J. des Cloizeaux and J. J. Pearson obtain the correct spectrum of the Heisenberg antiferromagnet (spinon dispersion relation),[^o33] showing that it differs from Anderson's spin-wave theory predictions[^o34] (the constant prefactor is different).
- 1963: [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb) and Werner Liniger provide the exact solution of the 1d δ-function interacting Bose gas[^o35] (now known as the [Lieb-Liniger model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieb-Liniger_model)). Lieb studies the spectrum and defines two basic types of excitations.[^o36]
- 1964: [Robert B. Griffiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_B._Griffiths) obtains the magnetization curve of the Heisenberg model at zero temperature.[^o37]
- 1966: [C. N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning) and [C. P. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._P._Yang) rigorously prove that the ground-state of the Heisenberg chain is given by the Bethe ansatz.[^o38] They study properties and applications in[^o39] and.[^o40]
- 1967: [C. N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning) generalizes Lieb and Liniger's solution of the δ-function interacting Bose gas to arbitrary permutation symmetry of the wavefunction, giving birth to the nested Bethe ansatz.[^o41]
- 1968: [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb) and [F. Y. Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fa-Yueh_Wu) solve the 1d Hubbard model.[^o42]
- 1969: [C. N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning) and [C. P. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._P._Yang) obtain the thermodynamics of the Lieb-Liniger model,[^o43] providing the basis of the thermodynamic Bethe ansatz (TBA).
