# Benedict Gross

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Benedict Gross"}

**Benedict Hyman Gross**[^o10] (June 22, 1950 – December 19, 2025) was an American mathematician who was a professor at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego),[^o4] the George Vasmer Leverett Professor of Mathematics Emeritus at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), and [Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) of [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College).[^o5]

:::

Gross is known for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), particularly the [Gross–Zagier theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross–Zagier_theorem) on [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225), and related topics in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200). He received a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program) in 1986 and a [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in 1987. He was a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), a fellow of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society), and an elected member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Benedict Hyman Gross was born on June 22, 1950, in [South Orange, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey), to Joel and Terry Gross.[^o10] His father, Joel Gross, was born in [Jersey City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City) to Austrian immigrants who had moved to the United States as children.[^o10] Joel Gross was a lawyer in [Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey) and was active in Jewish civic and philanthropic organizations.[^o8] Terry Gross (née Stavisky) was born in New York City to Polish immigrants who had also moved to the United States as teenagers.[^o10] Benedict Gross had two siblings, Ruth Gross Picker and [Avrum Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avrum_Gross).[^o8] [Al Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gross_%28politician%29) was his nephew.[^o9]

Gross and his family moved to [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California), at the age of 5, before returning to New Jersey during second grade.[^o10] He attended [West Orange High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Orange_High_School_%28New_Jersey%29), but transferred out after his freshman year. Gross graduated from The [Pingry School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingry_School), a leading independent school in New Jersey, in 1967 as the valedictorian.[^o10]

He initially studied physics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), deterred from mathematics because he thought that his classmates were better prepared than he was, but switched to mathematics in his sophomore year after enjoying the [Math 55](wikipedia://en/22008131) course that was taught by [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871).[^o28] In 1971, he graduated [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) from Harvard University.[^o28]

Gross studied music in Africa and Asia, and then moved to [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) as a [Marshall Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Scholar) where he studied history, sociology, and mathematics.[^o28] He received an M.Sc. from Oxford University in 1974, and then returned to Harvard, where he received his Ph.D. in mathematics in 1978 under the supervision of [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933).[^o5]

### Career {#Career}

Gross was an assistant professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1978 to 1982, a [maître de conférences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maître_de_conférences) at [Université de Paris VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) in 1980, and an associate professor at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) from 1982 to 1985.[^o5] Gross became a tenured professor at Harvard University in 1985 and became the George Vasmer Leverett Professor at Harvard University in 1988.[^o5] He served as Dean of Undergraduate Education from 2002 to 2003 and as the Dean of [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) from 2003 to 2007.[^o13] He retired from Harvard University as a professor emeritus and joined the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), as a professor in 2016.[^o26][^o14]

He was the mathematical consultant for the 1980 film [*It's My Turn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_My_Turn_%28film%29) containing the scene[^o15] in which actress [Jill Clayburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Clayburgh), portraying a mathematics professor, impeccably proves the [snake lemma](wikipedia://en/87089).[^o16][^o17] Gross was a member of the Board of Trustees of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 2012 to 2017[^o18][^o19] and the executive committee of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) from 2015 to 2019.[^o20] He joined the Board of Directors of [Scripps Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research) in 2018.[^o14]

## Research {#Research}

Gross's work spanned topics in number theory, [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), and [group representations](wikipedia://en/12695).[^o26][^o22] His work with [Don Zagier](wikipedia://en/1408557) on the [Gross–Zagier formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross–Zagier_formula) in 1986 has been influential in modern [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o27] It describes the [height](wikipedia://en/9236652) of Heegner points in terms of a derivative of the [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283) of elliptic curves[^o23] and led to breakthroughs on the [class number problem](wikipedia://en/596282) of [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125).[^o27][^o22]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Gross first met his wife [Jill P. Mesirov](wikipedia://en/45477104) at a party hosted by [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986).[^o10] They married in 1982 and had two sons.[^o10]

He was diagnosed with [testicular cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testicular_cancer) when he was an undergraduate at Harvard University, a likely effect of his mother having been prescribed [diethylstilbestrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diethylstilbestrol) during pregnancy to prevent [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage).[^o10] He received radiation therapy to prevent recurrence and had several major abdominal surgeries to deal with issues caused by this radiation over his lifetime.[^o10] Gross died on December 19, 2025, at the age of 75, after a long illness.[^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Gross received a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program) in 1986.[^o22][^o26] Gross, Zagier, and [Dorian M. Goldfeld](wikipedia://en/7536770) won the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 1987 for their work on the Gross–Zagier formula and the Gauss class number problem.[^o30][^o31] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o32]

He was elected as a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1992[^o33] and as a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2004.[^o34] He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 2017.[^o35]
