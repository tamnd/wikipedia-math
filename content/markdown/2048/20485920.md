# Paul F. Velleman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paul F. Velleman"}

**Paul F. Velleman** (born April 16, 1949) is an American academic who is a professor of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Velleman earned a Bachelor of Arts degree in mathematics and social science from [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), followed by Master of Arts and PhD from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). Velleman's thesis was written on the topic of non-linear [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data) [smoothing](wikipedia://en/4155662).[^o5][^o6]

## Career {#Career}

He is the author and designer of the multimedia statistics [CD-ROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD-ROM), [ActivStats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ActivStats), for which he was awarded the EDUCOM Medal for innovative uses of computers in teaching statistics, and the ICTCM Award for Innovation in Using Technology in College Mathematics.[^o8] He developed the statistics program, [Data Desk](wikipedia://en/19098686) and the Internet site Data and Story Library (DASL),[^o9] which provides datasets for teaching statistics. In 1987, he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o10]

### Books {#Books}

He is co-author (with Richard De Veaux and David Bock) of *Intro Stats, Stats: Modeling the World, and Stats: Data* and *Models, Business Statistics and Business Statistics: A First Course* (with [Norean Sharpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norean_R._Sharpe) and Richard De Veaux) and the co-author (with David Hoaglin) of *ABCs of Exploratory Data Analysis*. Velleman taught statistics at Cornell University from 1975 to 2018. He is a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) and of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).
