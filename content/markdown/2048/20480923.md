# Experimental event rate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Experimental event rate"}

In [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) and [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878), the **experimental event rate (EER)** is a measure of [how often](wikipedia://en/4839019) a particular statistical event (such as response to a drug, [adverse event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_event) or death) occurs within the experimental group (non-control group) of an experiment.[^o2]

:::

This value is very useful in determining the therapeutic benefit or risk to patients in experimental groups, in comparison to patients in [placebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo) or traditionally treated control groups.

Three statistical terms rely on EER for their calculation: [absolute risk reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_risk_reduction), [relative risk reduction](wikipedia://en/9795530) and [number needed to treat](wikipedia://en/1964571).

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Control Event Rate"}

## Control event rate {#Control_event_rate}

The *control event rate (CER)* is identical to the experimental event rate except that is measured within the [scientific control group](wikipedia://en/1640288) of an experiment.[^o5]

:::
