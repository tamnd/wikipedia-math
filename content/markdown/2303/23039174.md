# Eigengap

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eigengap"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), the **eigengap** of a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) is the difference between two successive [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues), where eigenvalues are sorted in ascending order.

:::

The [Davis–Kahan theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis–Kahan_theorem), named after [Chandler Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Davis) and [William Kahan](wikipedia://en/303413), uses the eigengap to show how eigenspaces of an operator change under [perturbation](wikipedia://en/102140).[^o2] Essentially, a larger eigengap indicates that the corresponding eigenvectors are more robust and less sensitive to small changes or noise in the data.

In [spectral clustering](wikipedia://en/13651683), the eigengap is often referred to as the **[spectral gap](wikipedia://en/7999250)**; although the spectral gap may often be defined in a broader sense than that of the eigengap. In this context, a significant jump or gap between successive eigenvalues often suggests a natural division in the underlying data structure, helping to determine the ideal number of clusters.
