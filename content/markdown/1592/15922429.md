# Scattered order

order theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scattered order"}

In mathematical [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924), a **scattered order** is a [linear order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_order) that contains no [densely ordered](wikipedia://en/6320997) [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) with more than one element.[^o2]

:::

A characterization due to [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989) states that the class of all scattered orders is the smallest class of linear orders that contains the singleton orders and is closed under [well-ordered](wikipedia://en/33456) and reverse well-ordered [sums](wikipedia://en/30330#Sums_of_orders).

[Laver's theorem](wikipedia://en/63406456) (generalizing a conjecture of [Roland Fraïssé](wikipedia://en/4234672) on [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) orders) states that the embedding relation on the class of countable unions of scattered orders is a [well-quasi-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-quasi-order).[^o3]

The [order topology](wikipedia://en/213650) of a scattered order is [scattered](wikipedia://en/40260318). The converse implication does not hold, as witnessed by the [lexicographic order](wikipedia://en/567667) on $\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Z$.
