# Circular surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Circular surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and, in particular, [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) a **circular surface** is the image of a [map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) *ƒ* : *I* × *S*<sup>1</sup> → **R**<sup>3</sup>, where *I* ⊂ **R** is an [open interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval) and *S*<sup>1</sup> is the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071), defined by

:::

$$f(t,\theta) := \gamma(t) + r(t){\mathbf u}(t)\cos\theta + r(t){\mathbf v}(t)\sin\theta, \,$$

where γ, **u**, **v** : *I* → **R**<sup>3</sup> and *r* : *I* → **R**<sub>\>0</sub>, when **R**<sub>\>0</sub> := { *x* ∈ **R** : *x* > 0 }. Moreover, it is usually assumed that **u · u** = **v · v** = 1 and **u · v** = 0, where dot denotes the canonical [scalar product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_product) on **R**<sup>3</sup>, i.e. **u** and **v** are [unit length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_length) and mutually [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944). The map γ : *I* → **R**<sup>3</sup> is called the **base curve** for the circular surface and the two maps **u**, **v** : *I* → **R**<sup>3</sup> are called the **direction frame** for the circular surface. For a fixed *t*<sub>0</sub> ∈ *I* the image of *ƒ*(*t*<sub>0</sub>, *θ*) is called a **generating [circle](wikipedia://en/6220)** of the circular surface.[^o2]

Circular surfaces are an analogue of [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158). In the case of circular surfaces the generators are circles; called the generating circles. In the case of ruled surface the generators are straight lines; called rulings.
