# David Heath (probabilist)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Heath"}

**David Clay Heath** (~1943 – 11 August 2011) was an American [probabilist](wikipedia://en/23542) known for co-inventing the [Heath–Jarrow–Morton framework](wikipedia://en/2876834) to model the evolution of the [interest rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate) curve.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Oak Park, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Park,_Illinois), to W. Curtis and Margaret Wasson Heath. He graduated from [Elkhart High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elkhart_High_School) in 1960 and earned his bachelor's degree from [Kalamazoo College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamazoo_College) in 1964.

### Scientific career {#Scientific_career}

Heath obtained his PhD in mathematics in 1969 under the supervision of [Frank Bardsley Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Bardsley_Knight) at the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) with a dissertation titled 'Probabilistic Analysis of Hyperbolic Systems of Partial Differential Equations'. After graduation, he became an assistant professor at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota). In the late 1970s, he moved to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) where he joined the School of Operations Research and Industrial Engineering. There he founded the [financial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_engineering) program, becoming the Merrill Lynch Professor of Financial Engineering. In the late 1990s Heath moved to [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) in Pittsburgh, where he became the Orion Hoch Professor of Mathematical Sciences, and where he stayed until his retirement in 2006.
