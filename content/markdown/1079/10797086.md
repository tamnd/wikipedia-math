# Janko group J2

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Janko group J2"}

In the area of modern algebra known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Janko group** *J<sub>2</sub>* or the **Hall-Janko group** *HJ* is a [sporadic simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527)

:::

604,800 = 2<sup>7</sup>3<sup>3</sup>5<sup>2</sup>7

≈ 6.

## History and properties {#History_and_properties}

*J<sub>2</sub>* is one of the 26 [Sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806) and is also called **Hall–Janko–Wales group**. In 1969 [Zvonimir Janko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvonimir_Janko) predicted J<sub>2</sub> as one of two new simple groups having 2<sup>1+4</sup>:A<sub>5</sub> as a centralizer of an involution (the other is the [Janko group J3](wikipedia://en/10796938)). It was constructed by (txt) as a [rank 3 permutation group](wikipedia://en/29763000) on 100 points.

Both the [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) and the [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) have order 2. As a permutation group on 100 points J<sub>2</sub> has [involutions](wikipedia://en/293450#group_theory) moving all 100 points and involutions moving just 80 points. The former involutions are products of 25 double transportions, an odd number, and hence lift to 4-elements in the [double cover](wikipedia://en/25118486) 2.A<sub>100</sub>. The double cover 2.J<sub>2</sub> occurs as a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/345511#Suzuki_chain_of_product_groups) of the Conway group Co<sub>0</sub>.

J<sub>2</sub> is the only one of the 4 Janko groups that is a [subquotient](wikipedia://en/1639294) of the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422); it is thus part of what [Robert Griess](wikipedia://en/5034215) calls the Happy Family. Since it is also found in the [Conway group Co1](wikipedia://en/36237614), it is therefore part of the second generation of the Happy Family.

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Representations"}

## Representations {#Representations}

It is a subgroup of [index](wikipedia://en/230581) two of the group of automorphisms of the [Hall–Janko graph](wikipedia://en/1110667), leading to a [permutation representation](wikipedia://en/59487764) of degree 100. It is also a subgroup of index two of the group of automorphisms of the Hall–Janko [Near Octagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_Polygon),[^o6] leading to a permutation representation of degree 315.

It has a [modular representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_representation) of dimension six over the field of four elements; if in [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) two we have *w*<sup>2</sup> + *w* + 1 = 0, then J<sub>2</sub> is generated by the two matrices

$${\mathbf A} = \begin{pmatrix} w^2 & w^2 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & w^2 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & w^2 & w^2 & 0 & 0 \\ w & 1 & 1 & w^2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & w^2 & w^2 & w^2 & 0 & w \\ w^2 & 1 & w^2 & 0 & w^2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

and

$${\mathbf B} = \begin{pmatrix} w & 1 & w^2 & 1 & w^2 & w^2 \\ w & 1 & w & 1 & 1 & w \\ w & w & w^2 & w^2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\ w^2 & 1 & w^2 & w^2 & w & w^2 \\ w^2 & 1 & w^2 & w & w^2 & w \end{pmatrix}.$$

These matrices satisfy the equations

$${\mathbf A}^2 = {\mathbf B}^3 = ({\mathbf A}{\mathbf B})^7 = ({\mathbf A}{\mathbf B}{\mathbf A}{\mathbf B}{\mathbf B})^{12} = 1.$$

(Note that matrix multiplication on a finite field of order 4 is defined slightly differently from ordinary matrix multiplication. See Finite field for the specific addition and multiplication tables, with *w* the same as *a* and *w*${}^{2}$ the same as *1 + a*.)

J<sub>2</sub> is thus a [Hurwitz group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz_group), a finite homomorphic image of the [(2,3,7) triangle group](wikipedia://en/12659803).

The matrix representation given above constitutes an embedding into [Dickson's](wikipedia://en/1790247) group [*G*<sub>2</sub>(4)](wikipedia://en/1691490). There is only one conjugacy class of J<sub>2</sub> in *G*<sub>2</sub>(4). Every subgroup J<sub>2</sub> contained in *G*<sub>2</sub>(4) extends to a subgroup J<sub>2</sub>:2 = Aut(J<sub>2</sub>) in *G*<sub>2</sub>(4):2 = Aut(*G*<sub>2</sub>(4)) (*G*<sub>2</sub>(4) extended by the field automorphisms of **F**<sub>4</sub>). *G*<sub>2</sub>(4) is in turn isomorphic to a subgroup of the [Conway group](wikipedia://en/345511) Co<sub>1</sub>.
