# Karamata's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karamata's inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Karamata's inequality**,[^o2] named after [Jovan Karamata](wikipedia://en/3130497),[^o3] also known as the **majorization inequality**, is a theorem in [elementary algebra](wikipedia://en/9710) for convex and concave real-valued functions, defined on an interval of the real line. It generalizes the discrete form of [Jensen's inequality](wikipedia://en/297811), and generalizes in turn to the concept of [Schur-convex functions](wikipedia://en/3897244).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=named-heading name="Statement of the inequality"}

## Statement of the inequality {#Statement_of_the_inequality}

Let $I$ be an [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) and let $f$ denote a real-valued, [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) defined on $I$. If $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n}$ are numbers in $I$ such that $(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n})$ [majorizes](wikipedia://en/2864495) $(y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n})$, then

::: {#o6 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$f(x_1)+\cdots+f(x_n) \ge f(y_1)+\cdots+f(y_n). \tag{1}$$

:::

Here majorization means that $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n}$ satisfies

::: {#o7 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

x_1\ge x_2\ge\cdots\ge x_n$ and $y_1\ge y_2\ge\cdots\ge y_n, (2)

:::

and we have the inequalities

::: {#o8 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

x_1+\cdots+x_i\ge y_1+\cdots+y_i$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$. (3)

:::

and the equality

::: {#o9 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$x_1+\cdots+x_n=y_1+\cdots+y_n \tag{4}$$

:::

If $f$ is a [strictly convex function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_convex_function), then the inequality ((1)) holds with equality if and only if $y$ is a permutation of $x$.

### Weak majorization case {#Weak_majorization_case}

$x \preceq_w y$ if and only if $\sum g\left(x_i\right) \leq \sum g\left(y_i\right)$ for any continuous increasing convex function $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$.[^o11]

:::

## Remarks {#Remarks}

If the convex function $f$ is [non-decreasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-decreasing_function), then the proof of ((1)) below and the discussion of equality in case of strict convexity shows that the equality ((4)) can be relaxed to

::: {#o13 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$x_1+\cdots+x_n\ge y_1+\cdots+y_n. \tag{5}$$

:::

The inequality ((1)) is reversed if $f$ is [concave](wikipedia://en/263321), since in this case the function $-f$ is convex.

::: {#o15 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The finite form of [Jensen's inequality](wikipedia://en/297811) is a special case of this result. Consider the real numbers $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n} \in I$ and let

$$a := \frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n}$$

denote their [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612). Then $(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n})$ majorizes the $n$-tuple $(a, a, \ldots, a)$, since the arithmetic mean of the $i$ largest numbers of $(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n})$ is at least as large as the arithmetic mean $a$ of all the $n$ numbers, for every $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$. By Karamata's inequality ((1)) for the convex function $f$,

$$f(x_1)+f(x_2)+ \cdots +f(x_n) \ge f(a)+f(a)+\cdots+f(a) = nf(a).$$

Dividing by $n$ gives Jensen's inequality. The sign is reversed if $f$ is concave.

:::

::: {#o17 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of the inequality"}

## Proof of the inequality {#Proof_of_the_inequality}

We may assume that the numbers are in decreasing order as specified in ((2)).

If $x_{i} = y_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, then the inequality ((1)) holds with equality, hence we may assume in the following that $x_{i} \ne y_{i}$ for at least one $i$.

If $x_{i} = y_{i}$ for an $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, then the inequality ((1)) and the majorization properties ((3)) and ((4)) are not affected if we remove $x_{i}$ and $y_{i}$. Hence we may assume that $x_{i} \ne y_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$.

It is a [property of convex functions](wikipedia://en/245568#Properties) that for two numbers $x \ne y$ in the interval $I$ the [slope](wikipedia://en/29368)

$$\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}$$

of the [secant line](wikipedia://en/241886) through the points $(x, f\,(x))$ and $(y, f\,(y))$ of the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of $f$ is a [monotonically non-decreasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonically_non-decreasing) function in $x$ for $y$ fixed (and [vice versa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Latin_phrases:_V#vice_versa)). This implies that

::: {#o18 .equation cue=numblk name="6"}

$$c_{i+1}:=\frac{f(x_{i+1})-f(y_{i+1})}{x_{i+1}-y_{i+1}}\le\frac{f(x_i)-f(y_i)}{x_i-y_i}=:c_i \tag{6}$$

:::

for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$. Define $A_{0} = B_{0} = 0$ and

$$A_i=x_1+\cdots+x_i,\qquad B_i=y_1+\cdots+y_i$$

for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$. By the majorization property ((3)), $A_{i} \ge B_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$ and by ((4)), $A_{n} = B_{n}$. Hence,

::: {#o19 .equation cue=numblk name="7"}

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^n \bigl(f(x_i) - f(y_i)\bigr) &=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i (x_i - y_i)\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i \bigl(\underbrace{A_i - A_{i-1}}_{=\,x_i}{} - (\underbrace{B_i - B_{i-1}}_{=\,y_i})\bigr)\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i (A_i - B_i) - \sum_{i=1}^n c_i (A_{i-1} - B_{i-1})\\ &=c_n (\underbrace{A_n-B_n}_{=\,0}) + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\underbrace{c_i - c_{i + 1}}_{\ge\,0})(\underbrace{A_i - B_i}_{\ge\,0}) - c_1(\underbrace{A_0-B_0}_{=\,0})\\ &\ge0, \end{aligned} \tag{7}$$

:::

which proves Karamata's inequality ((1)).

To discuss the case of equality in ((1)), note that $x_{1} > y_{1}$ by ((3)) and our assumption $x_{i} \ne y_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$. Let $i$ be the smallest index such that $(x_{i}, y_{i}) \ne (x_{i+1}, y_{i+1})$, which exists due to ((4)). Then $A_{i} > B_{i}$. If $f$ is strictly convex, then there is strict inequality in ((6)), meaning that $c_{i+1} < c_{i}$. Hence there is a strictly positive term in the sum on the right hand side of ((7)) and equality in ((1)) cannot hold.

If the convex function $f$ is non-decreasing, then $c_{n} \ge 0$. The relaxed condition ((5)) means that $A_{n} \ge B_{n}$, which is enough to conclude that $c_{n}(A_{n}-B_{n}) \ge 0$ in the last step of ((7)).

If the function $f$ is strictly convex and non-decreasing, then $c_{n} > 0$. It only remains to discuss the case $A_{n} > B_{n}$. However, then there is a strictly positive term on the right hand side of ((7)) and equality in ((1)) cannot hold.

:::
