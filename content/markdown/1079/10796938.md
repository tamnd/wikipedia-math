# Janko group J3

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Janko group J3"}

In the area of modern algebra known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Janko group** *J<sub>3</sub>* or the **Higman-Janko-McKay group** *HJM* is a [sporadic simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527)

:::

50,232,960 = 2<sup>7</sup>3<sup>5</sup>51719.

## History and properties {#History_and_properties}

*J<sub>3</sub>* is one of the 26 [Sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806) and was predicted by [Zvonimir Janko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvonimir_Janko) in 1969 as one of two new simple groups having 2<sup>1+4</sup>:A<sub>5</sub> as a centralizer of an involution (the other is the Janko group [*J<sub>2</sub>*](wikipedia://en/10797086)). *J<sub>3</sub>* was shown to exist by (txt).

In 1982 [R. L. Griess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._L._Griess) showed that *J<sub>3</sub>* cannot be a [subquotient](wikipedia://en/1639294) of the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422).[^o4] Thus it is one of the 6 sporadic groups called the [pariahs](wikipedia://en/16569533).

J<sub>3</sub> has an [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) of order 2 and a [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) of order 3, and its triple cover has a unitary 9-dimensional [representation](wikipedia://en/1110685) over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with 4 elements. (Weiss 1982) constructed it via an underlying geometry. It has a modular representation of dimension eighteen over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with 9 elements. It has a complex projective representation of dimension eighteen.

The degrees of [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) of the Janko group *J<sub>3</sub>* are 1, 85, 85, 323, 323, 324, ... .

### Using the subgroup PSL(2,16) {#Using_the_subgroup_PSL(2,16)}

The automorphism group *J*<sub>3</sub>:2 can be constructed by starting with the subgroup PSL(2,16):4 and adjoining 120 involutions, which are identified with the [Sylow](wikipedia://en/53993) 17-subgroups. Note that these 120 involutions are outer elements of *J*<sub>3</sub>:2. One then defines the following relation:

$$\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\1&0\end{matrix}\sigma t_{(\nu,\nu7)}\right)^5=1$$

where $\sigma$ is the [Frobenius automorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270) of order 4, and $t_{(\nu,\nu7)}$ is the unique 17-cycle that sends

$$\infty\rightarrow0\rightarrow1\rightarrow7$$

Curtis showed, using a computer, that this relation is sufficient to define *J*<sub>3</sub>:2.[^o11]

### Using a presentation {#Using_a_presentation}

In terms of generators *a*, *b*, *c*, and *d* its automorphism group J<sub>3</sub>:2 can be [presented](wikipedia://en/99494) as $a^{17} = b^8 = a^ba^{-2} = c^2 = b^cb^3 = (abc)^4 = (ac)^{17} = d^2 = [d, a] = [d, b] = (a^3b^{-3}cd)^5 = 1.$

A presentation for J<sub>3</sub> in terms of (different) generators *a*, *b*, *c*, *d* is $a^{19} = b^9 = a^ba^2 = c^2 = d^2 = (bc)^2 = (bd)^2 = (ac)^3 = (ad)^3 = (a^2ca^{-3}d)^3 = 1.$
