# Combinatorics and physics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Combinatorics and physics"}

**Combinatorial physics** or **physical combinatorics** is the area of interaction between [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

"Combinatorial Physics is an emerging area which unites combinatorial and discrete mathematical techniques applied to theoretical physics, especially Quantum Theory."[^o3]

"Physical combinatorics might be defined naively as combinatorics guided by ideas or insights from physics"[^o4]

Combinatorics has always played an important role in [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) and [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics).[^o5] However, combinatorial physics only emerged as a specific field after a seminal work by [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340) and [Dirk Kreimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Kreimer),[^o6] showing that the [renormalization](wikipedia://en/291453) of [Feynman diagrams](wikipedia://en/11617) can be described by a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714).

Combinatorial physics can be characterized by the use of algebraic concepts to interpret and solve physical problems involving combinatorics. It gives rise to a particularly harmonious collaboration between mathematicians and physicists.

Among the significant physical results of combinatorial physics, we may mention the reinterpretation of renormalization as a [Riemann–Hilbert problem](wikipedia://en/9710396),[^o7] the fact that the [Slavnov–Taylor identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavnov–Taylor_identities) of [gauge theories](wikipedia://en/23912155) generate a Hopf ideal,[^o8] the [quantization of fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_quantization)[^o9] and [strings](wikipedia://en/28305),[^o10] and a completely algebraic description of the combinatorics of quantum field theory.[^o11] An important example of applying combinatorics to physics is the enumeration of [alternating sign matrix](wikipedia://en/1740155) in the solution of [ice-type models](wikipedia://en/20096022). The corresponding ice-type model is the six vertex model with domain wall boundary conditions.
