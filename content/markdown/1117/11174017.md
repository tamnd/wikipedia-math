# Fred Galvin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fred Galvin"}

**Frederick William Galvin** is a mathematician, currently a professor at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas). His research interests include [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

His notable combinatorial work includes the proof of the [Dinitz theorem](wikipedia://en/920110). In set theory, he proved with [András Hajnal](wikipedia://en/21765779) that if ℵ<sub>ω<sub>1</sub></sub> is a [strong limit cardinal](wikipedia://en/374002), then

$$2^{\aleph_{\omega_1}}<\aleph_{(2^{\aleph_1})^+}$$

holds. The research on extending this result led [Saharon Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) to the invention of [PCF theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCF_theory). Galvin gave an elementary proof of the [Baumgartner&ndash;Hajnal theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baumgartner&ndash;Hajnal_theorem) $\omega_1\to(\alpha)^2_k$ ($\alpha<\omega_1, k<\omega$). The original proof by [Baumgartner](wikipedia://en/5825279) and Hajnal used [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) and absoluteness. Galvin and Shelah also proved the square bracket partition relations $\aleph_1\not\to[\aleph_1]^2_4$ and $2^{\aleph_0}\not\to[2^{\aleph_0}]^2_{\aleph_0}$. Galvin also proved the partition relation $\eta\to[\eta]^2_3$ where η denotes the [order type](wikipedia://en/4499021) of the set of rational numbers. Galvin and [Karel Prikry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Prikry) proved that every [Borel set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_algebra) is Ramsey. Galvin and [Komjáth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Péter_Komjáth) showed that the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) is equivalent to the statement that every [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) has a [chromatic number](wikipedia://en/426743#Chromatic_number).

Galvin received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1967 from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota).

He also invented [Marseillais chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseillais_chess) in 1957 (already published by others earlier in 1925), and Push Chess in 1967.
