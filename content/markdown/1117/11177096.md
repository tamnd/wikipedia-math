# W. B. R. Lickorish

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="W. B. R. Lickorish"}

**William Bernard Raymond Lickorish** (born 19 February 1938) is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) professor of [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732) in the [Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Pure_Mathematics_and_Mathematical_Statistics), [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and also an emeritus fellow of [Pembroke College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_College,_Cambridge). His research interests include [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008). He was one of the discoverers of the [HOMFLY polynomial](wikipedia://en/1039260) invariant of links, and proved the [Lickorish-Wallace theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lickorish-Wallace_theorem) which states that all closed orientable 3-manifolds can be obtained by [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) on a link.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Lickorish received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from Cambridge in 1964; his thesis was written under the supervision of [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415).

## Recognition and awards {#Recognition_and_awards}

In 1991, Lickorish received the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o6] Lickorish and [Kenneth Millett](wikipedia://en/45353475) won the 1991 [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for their paper "The New Polynomial Invariants of Knots and Links".[^o7] Lickorish was included in the 2019 class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to knot theory and low-dimensional topology".[^o8]
