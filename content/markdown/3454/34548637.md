# Helmut H. Schaefer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helmut H. Schaefer"}

**Helmut Heinrich Schaefer** (February 14, 1925 in [Großenhain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Großenhain), [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) – December 16, 2005 in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen), Germany) was a German mathematician, who worked primarily in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). His two best known scientific monographs are titled *[Topological Vector Spaces](wikipedia://en/45752)* (1966) and *Banach Lattices* *and Positive Operators* (1974). The first of these was subsequently translated into Spanish and Russian. The second made him an internationally recognized and leading scholar in this particular field of mathematics. (Roquette & Wolff, 2006)

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

As teenager, Helmut Schaefer attended the [Sankt Afra boarding school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sächsisches_Landesgymnasium_Sankt_Afra_zu_Meißen) for gifted children in [Meissen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meissen), Germany on a merit-based scholarship. In 1943, then 18, he was recruited to serve as interpreter of Anglo-American intelligence. After the war he studied mathematics at [TU Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dresden) and [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig), where he earned his doctorate in 1951 and his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1954. Prof. [Ernst Hölder](wikipedia://en/31518948) served as his academic advisor in Leipzig. In 1956 he accepted an offer from the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle) as professor of mathematics.

In 1957, Schaefer, his wife and two children escaped from [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) to the Federal Republic. For one year, he worked under Prof. [Gottfried Köthe](wikipedia://en/27641787) at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz). In 1958 he became associate professor at [Washington State University at Pullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University_at_Pullman) and a few years later he, his wife and now three children moved on to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) at [Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor). Then in 1963 he accepted an offer from the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen) in Germany where he remained until his retirement in 1990. In Tübingen he served two terms as department head.

Interrupting this period on several occasions and following retirement in Tübingen he spent a number of one-year terms or semesters as visiting or full professor at various American universities, including the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign), the [University of Maryland at College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland_at_College_Park), the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in Pasadena, Texas A&M University at College Station, and [Florida Atlantic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_University) at Boca Raton. He remained active in mathematical research until the year 1999, at which point he completely dedicated himself to his lifelong hobby of astronomy, especially [astrophotography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophotography).

In 1978, Helmut Schaefer was accepted as full member of the Mathematics and Natural Sciences Class of the [Heidelberg Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities). Earlier, he was admitted to the Academy of Sciences in Zaragoza (Spain). Over the years, he was able to attract many students to functional analysis, combining an expectation of high achievement with a tolerant, humorous, and factual attitude. Ten of his doctoral students went on to become professors at various universities in Germany and the U.S. (Roquette & Wolff, 2006) His doctoral students include Wolfgang Arendt, Rainer Nagel, and [Bertram John Walsh](wikipedia://en/61889118).
