# Julia Stadlmann

Academics

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Julia Stadlmann"}

**Julia Stadlmann** (born 2 July 1998) is an Austrian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and [sieve theory](wikipedia://en/1035915). In August 2026 she improved the best known unconditional bound on gaps between consecutive [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) from 246 to 240, the first advance on the problem in twelve years.[^o18][^o15]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Stadlmann was born in [Unzmarkt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unzmarkt-Frauenburg) in [Upper Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Styria) and attended the BG/BRG Judenburg secondary school, competing successfully in [mathematical olympiads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_olympiad) in 2015 and 2016. At the age of 16 she was invited to the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) for a summer teaching programme, and in 2016 she received a Dr. Hans Riegel prize for outstanding pre-university research. Her parents have no particular background in mathematics.[^o18][^o19]

She went on to study at Oxford, where she completed her [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) under the supervision of [James Maynard](wikipedia://en/42512102), winner of the 2022 [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for work on gaps between primes.[^o18] In 2023 *[Quanta Magazine](wikipedia://en/51804976)* named her one of three early-career researchers contributing new results on the distribution of primes.[^o17]

## Career {#Career}

After a postdoctoral year at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), Stadlmann became a J. L. Doob Research Assistant Professor at the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign).[^o13][^o18] Her stated research interests include the properties of primes, [sums of two squares](wikipedia://en/46248976), [Harman's sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harman's_sieve) and the [GPY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldston–Pintz–Yıldırım) method, and large values of [Dirichlet polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_polynomial) and [exponential sums](wikipedia://en/468937).[^o13]

## Research {#Research}

### Primes in arithmetic progressions {#Primes_in_arithmetic_progressions}

In 2023 Stadlmann proved that the primes below $x$ are on average equidistributed in [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389) to [smooth](wikipedia://en/1035039) moduli of size up to $x^{1/2+1/40-\epsilon}$, raising the [exponent of distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent_of_distribution) from $\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{7}{300}$, obtained earlier by the [Polymath](wikipedia://en/34858344) collaboration, to $\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{40}$. The main new ingredient was a modification of the *q*-van der Corput process.[^o14]

### Bounded gaps between primes {#Bounded_gaps_between_primes}

Building on her doctoral work on gaps between three or more primes, Stadlmann spent two years on the case of consecutive primes. In the 34-page preprint *Bounded gaps between primes*, posted to [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) on 31 August 2026, she showed how the [Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem](wikipedia://en/4632596) can be combined with newer equidistribution estimates for smooth moduli to obtain $H_1 = \liminf (p_{n+1}-p_n) \leq 240$.[^o15] This improved on the bound of 246 established in 2014 by Maynard and the Polymath8b project, which had itself followed [Yitang Zhang](wikipedia://en/39441844)'s 2013 proof that the smallest infinitely recurring gap is at most 70 million.[^o18]

Stadlmann had obtained a first proof some months earlier but continued working towards a sharper result; on hearing a rumour that OpenAI was about to publish on the same problem, she uploaded the version she had, saying she could not compete with such a company's computing power. The preprint has not yet undergone formal [peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review).[^o18]

### Reception and concurrent AI results {#Reception_and_concurrent_AI_results}

[Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) called the paper "a remarkable effort in the spirit of the original Polymath8b argument", noting that it was carried out by a single mathematician using conventional numerical computation rather than modern [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) tools.[^o16] Stadlmann confirmed that she "did not use any AI at all", neither for the mathematical ideas nor for the code or the write-up.[^o18] Maynard told *[Der Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Standard)* that computational methods were among her particular strengths and were important to the result.[^o18]
