# Statistical area

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical area"}

The [United States federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States) defines and delineates the nation's [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) for statistical purposes, using a set of standard **statistical area** definitions. the U.S. [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget) (OMB) defined and delineated 393 [metropolitan statistical areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) (MSAs) and 542 [micropolitan statistical areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micropolitan_statistical_area) (μSAs) in the United States and Puerto Rico.[^o2] Many of these 935 MSAs and μSAs are, in turn, components of larger [combined statistical areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) (CSAs) consisting of adjacent MSAs and μSAs that are linked by [commuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuting) ties; 582 metropolitan and micropolitan areas are components of the 184 defined CSAs.

:::

Metropolitan and micropolitan statistical areas are defined as consisting of one or more adjacent [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_%28United_States%29) or [county equivalents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-equivalent) with at least one [urban core area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_urban_areas) meeting relevant population thresholds, plus adjacent territory that has a high degree of social and [economic integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_integration) with the core, as measured by commuting ties. A metropolitan statistical area has at least one [core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) with a population of at least 50,000. In a micropolitan statistical area, the largest core has a population of at least 10,000 but less than 50,000.
