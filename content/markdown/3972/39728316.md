# List of polygons

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of polygons"}

*Image: Regular polygon 5 annotated.svg.* A pentagon is a five-sided polygon. A regular pentagon has 5 equal edges and 5 equal angles.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) is traditionally a [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) [figure](wikipedia://en/169191) that is bounded by a finite chain of straight [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) closing in a loop to form a [closed chain](wikipedia://en/12056032). These segments are called its *edges* or *sides*, and the points where two of the edges meet are the polygon's *[vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417)* (singular: vertex) or *corners*.

:::

The word *polygon* comes from [Late Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Latin) polygōnum (a noun), from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) πολύγωνον (polygōnon/polugōnon), noun use of neuter of πολύγωνος (polygōnos/polugōnos, the masculine adjective), meaning "many-angled". Individual polygons are named (and sometimes classified) according to the number of sides, combining a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language)-derived [numerical prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_prefix) with the suffix *\-gon*, e.g. *[pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014)*, *[dodecagon](wikipedia://en/508486)*. The [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) and [nonagon](wikipedia://en/414824) are exceptions, although the regular forms *trigon*, *tetragon*, and *enneagon* are sometimes encountered as well.
