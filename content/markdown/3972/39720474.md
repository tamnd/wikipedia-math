# Poincaré separation theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poincaré separation theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Poincaré separation theorem**, also known as the **Cauchy interlacing theorem**, gives some [upper and lower bounds](wikipedia://en/42693) of [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of a real [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474) $\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{AB}$ that can be considered as the [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of a larger real symmetric matrix **A** onto a [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) spanned by the columns of **B**. The theorem is named after [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740).

:::

More specifically, let **A** be an *n* × *n* real symmetric matrix and **B** an *n* × *r* [semi-orthogonal matrix](wikipedia://en/34417095) such that $\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B}$ = $I_{r}$. Denote by $\lambda_i$, *i* = 1, 2, ..., *n* and $\mu_i$, *i* = 1, 2, ..., *r* the eigenvalues of **A** and $\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{AB}$, respectively (in descending order). We have

$$\lambda_i \geq \mu_i \geq \lambda_{n-r+i},$$

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

An algebraic proof, based on the [variational interpretation of eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/694952), has been published in Magnus' *Matrix Differential Calculus with Applications in Statistics and Econometrics*.[^o4] From the geometric point of view, $\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{AB}$ can be considered as the [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of **A** onto the linear subspace spanned by **B**, so the above results follow immediately.[^o5]

An alternative proof can be made for the case where **B** is a principal submatrix of **A**, demonstrated by Steve Fisk.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

When considering two mechanical systems, each described by an [equation of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equation_of_motion), that differ by exactly one constraint (such that $n-r =1$), the [natural frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_frequency) of the two systems interlace.

This has an important consequence when considering the [frequency response](wikipedia://en/302033) of a complicated system such as a [large room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_acoustics). Even though there may be many modes, each with unpredictable modes shapes that will vary as details change such as furniture being moved, the interlacing theorem implies that the modal density (average number of modes per frequency interval) remains predictable and approximately constant. This allows for the technique of [modal density analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_energy_analysis).

:::
