# Anna Barbara Reinhart

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anna Barbara Reinhart"}

*Image: Anna Barbara Reinhart, Schweizer Mathematikerin (1730-1796).jpg.* Copper engraving of Anna Reinhart

*Image: Anna Barbara Reinhart, Schweizer Mathematikerin (1730-1796), vier Ansichten.jpg.* 4 images of Anna Reinhart, unknown artist

**Anna Barbara Reinhart** (12 July 1730 – 5 January 1796), was a Swiss mathematician. She was considered an internationally respected mathematician of her era.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Anna Barbara Reinhart was the third child and first daughter of Councilor Salomon Reinhart (1693 - 1761) and Anna Steiner.[^o3]

Her childhood was overshadowed by an accident when she fell off her horse at a wedding party, which caused her to be confined her to her bed for significant periods of time.[^o4] Her physician, Dr. Johann Heinrich Hegner, however, noticed her aptitude for mathematics and began to teach her.[^o5] Henceforth, she studied mathematics using the books of [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), [Gabriel Cramer](wikipedia://en/499331), [Pieter van Musschenbroek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_van_Musschenbroek) and [Jérôme Lalande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jérôme_Lalande).

Reinhart corresponded with several mathematicians of the period, such as [Christoph Jezler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Jezler), and also received them as guests. She was active as a teacher of mathematics and was the instructor of [Ulrich Hegner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Hegner) and [Heinrich Bosshard von Rümikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Bosshard_von_Rümikon) among others. It is said that she edited the works of several of her contemporaries and wrote a manuscript commenting on the *[Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophiae_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica)* by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627),[^o6] however her comments were lost after her death, but her letters to Christoph Jezler were preserved.[^o5]

Several contemporaries commended Reinhart in their work, such as [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784) who praised her for expanding and improving the [pursuit curve](wikipedia://en/587650) as discussed by [Pierre Louis Maupertuis](wikipedia://en/196221).

Reinhart died in 1796 at the age of 66 from gout and the consequences of her childhood accident, from which never fully recovered.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

In 2003, a street was named after her in her hometown [Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterthur) in [Zurich, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich).
