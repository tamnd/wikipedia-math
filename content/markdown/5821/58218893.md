# Robert J. Plemmons

*Image: Robert Plemmons.jpg.* Plemmons in 2007

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert J. Plemmons"}

**Robert James Plemmons** (December 18, 1938 – February 6, 2026) was an American mathematician who specialized in [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833).[^o2] He was the emeritus Z. Smith Reynolds Professor of Mathematics and Computer Science at [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University).[^o3] In 1979, Plemmons co-authored the book *Nonnegative Matrices in the Mathematical Sciences*.[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Plemmons was born on December 18, 1938, in the small town of [Old Fort, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Fort,_North_Carolina), and grew up in rural Appalachia.[^o2] He attended Old Fort High School and graduated in 1957, having been the star athlete in both baseball and football.[^o6]

He attended [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) (WFU) on a full baseball scholarship.[^o7] Former athletic director [Gene Hooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Hooks) was his baseball coach.[^o8] In 1959, he held the record for [earned run average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earned_run_average).[^o9] During the years 1959–61, he held the record for victories, [innings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innings) pitched, [strikeouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikeout), and complete games, and made All-Conference Pitcher all three years.[^o9] In the academic year 1960–61, he was awarded the WFU ACC Scholar-Athlete of the Year.[^o10][^o11] Plemmons graduated from Wake Forest in 1961 with a Bachelor of Science degree in mathematics.[^o2]

During the early 1960s, Plemmons played professional baseball for four years with the [Baltimore Orioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Orioles)' minor league clubs.[^o12][^o13] He played with the [Tri-City Atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-City_Atoms), [Aberdeen Pheasants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Pheasants), and [Elmira Pioneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmira_Pioneers).[^o14]

In December 1963, he married Mary Jo Harris, also from Old Fort and a graduate of Old Fort High School.[^o4][^o6]

Plemmons attended graduate school at [Auburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University) from 1961 to 1965, receiving his PhD in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in 1965.[^o2] He then held research positions with [Martin Marietta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Marietta) in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida), and the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) in [Ft. Meade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_George_G._Meade).[^o2] He served as a faculty member at the [University of Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mississippi) from 1966 to 1967,[^o13] before moving to the [University of Tennessee Knoxville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee) in 1967.[^o2][^o13] In 1981, Plemmons moved to [Raleigh, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh,_North_Carolina), where he taught at [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) until 1990.[^o2][^o13] While there, he founded the University of North Carolina System's Center for Research in Scientific Computation.[^o15] Plemmons joined the faculty of [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) in 1990.[^o2] In 2013, he retired from teaching,[^o16] but still conducted research at WFU.[^o17][^o18] He was also the professor and mentor of former NBA and WFU basketball player [Rusty LaRue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusty_LaRue).[^o13][^o19]

## Academic work {#Academic_work}

Plemmons focused his work on applied [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833).[^o2] At [Auburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University) in the early 1960s, Plemmons' work with PhD advisors Richard Ball and Emilie Haynsworth[^o2] was focused on [finite semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_Semigroup_%28mathematics%29) theory.[^o21][^o22] He continued this research until the early 1980s at the [University of Tennessee Knoxville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee).[^o23][^o24] In 1979, he co-authored the book Nonnegative Matrices in the Mathematical Sciences along with Abraham Berman.[^o4] The book has been cited over 7,500 times.[^o25] In 1994, it was revised and republished by the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM).[^o26]

In the mid-to-late 1980s until mid-1990s, his research focused on numerical linear algebra,[^o27][^o28][^o29] specifically in [Matrix Theory](wikipedia://en/432648) with applications in [Markov](wikipedia://en/60876) chains and nonnegative matrices.[^o30][^o31] Plemmons has been recognized internationally for his significant contributions to the field, celebrated at the Linear Algebra: Theory, Applications, and Computations Conference held at [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) in 1999 in honor of Plemmons' 60th birthday,[^o2] and the International Workshop on Numerical Linear Algebra with Applications held in Hong Kong in 2013 in honor of his 75th birthday.[^o32]

Plemmons conducted research in imaging physics and applied optics at [Wake Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) from the late 1990s.[^o2][^o33] He focused initially in [biometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometrics), including [iris recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_recognition).[^o34][^o35][^o36] His later research was in space situational awareness, tracking [space debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_debris) using optical methods to prevent damage to space assets.[^o33]

Over the years, he received funding from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939), the [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency), the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO), the [Air Force Office of Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Office_of_Scientific_Research), the [Army Research Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Research_Office), the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy), the [Intelligence Advanced Research Projects Activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Advanced_Research_Projects_Activity), and the [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geospatial-Intelligence_Agency).[^o16] His research was continuously funded by the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense) from 1973.[^o37]

In 1996, Plemmons delivered a congressional testimony to the [U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) Committee on National Security,[^o37][^o38] in support of appropriations to the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense) on behalf of the [Joint Policy Board for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/4675178), including the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376).[^o38]

The mathematics lineage of Plemmons can be traced back to the German mathematicians in the 17th century.[^o39] Among his peers, he was a Fellow of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376).[^o40]

He was the author of more than 250 publications on [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833),[^o25] and served on the [editorial board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_board) of six journals.[^o7]

## Death {#Death}

Plemmons died from cancer at his home in [Winston-Salem, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem,_North_Carolina), on February 6, 2026, at the age of 87.[^o42]
