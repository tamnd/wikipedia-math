# Hermine Agavni Kalustyan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hermine Agavni Kalustyan"}

**Hermine Agavni Kalustyan** (Հերմինէ Աղաւնի Գալուստեան, 1914 – 3 September 1989) was a [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people)-[Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians) mathematician, educator, and politician.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Kalustyan was born in 1914 in [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul), Turkey. She graduated from Paris High School Teacher Training School and from Istanbul University Mathematics Department. From 1932 to 1936, she was at Ecole Normale Superieure to study math. In 1941, she became the first woman in Turkey to obtain a Ph.D. degree in mathematics.[^o3] She wrote her dissertation titled "Conformal depiction and the movement of an object" in the [Istanbul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_University) under [Richard von Mises](wikipedia://en/821517) and [William Prager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Prager).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Between 1948 and 1973, Kalustyan was appointed principal at Esayan Armenian High School. She also taught mathematics at the Getronagan and Galatasaray lyceums in Istanbul, Turkey.[^o7] In 1961, she became the republic's first non-Muslim minority woman to serve in parliament[^o8] with her appointment to the transitional parliament (1960–1961) that constructed the [1961 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Constitution_of_1961).[^o9] Afterwards, she became a member of the CHP on February 18, 1961.[^o10] In 1975, she published an Armenian book titled, "Towards the Past and Now: Towards Fezaya", and also moved to France.
