# Susan Hermiller

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susan Hermiller"}

**Susan Marie Hermiller** is an American mathematician specializing in the [computational](wikipedia://en/618584), [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/979834), and [geometric](wikipedia://en/955164) theory of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447). She is a Willa Cather Professor of Mathematics and a former Graduate Chair for Mathematics at the [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hermiller earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics and physics from [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) in 1984. She went to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) for graduate study in mathematics, earning a master's degree in 1987 and completing her Ph.D. in 1992. Her [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) was [Kenneth Brown](wikipedia://en/41104429), and her dissertation was *[Rewriting Systems](wikipedia://en/23947591) for [Coxeter Groups](wikipedia://en/297004)*.

After postdoctoral research at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) and the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), she became an assistant professor of mathematics at [New Mexico State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_State_University) in 1994. She moved to the University of Nebraska–Lincoln in 1999.

In 2021, her joint research with Mark Brittenham disproved the longstanding Bernhard-Jablan conjecture in knot theory, demonstrating that a proposed algorithm for determining the unknotting number of a knot was not viable. [^o3]

In 2025, the pair gave a counterexample to another long-standing conjecture, showing that the [unknotting number](wikipedia://en/26711383) is not additive under the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134). They proved that the connected sum of the [Septoil knot](wikipedia://en/26657115) with its mirror image, $7_1 \# \overline{7_1}$, has unknotting number 5, although each individual knot has unknotting number 3.[^o4]

## Service {#Service}

Hermiller was a founding member of the Committee on Women in Mathematics of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), in 2013. She also served as the American Mathematical Society representative on the Joint Committee on Women in the Mathematical Sciences for 2011 through 2013. She was also a former AMS Council member at large.[^o6]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Hermiller became the Willa Cather Professor in 2017. She was included in the 2019 class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to combinatorial and geometric group theory and for service to the profession, particularly in support of underrepresented groups".
