# ANNNI model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="ANNNI model"}

In [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), the **axial** (or **anisotropic**) **next-nearest neighbor Ising model**, usually known as the **ANNNI model**, is a variant of the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744). In the ANNNI model, competing [ferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism) and [antiferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiferromagnetism) [exchange interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_interaction) couple [spins](wikipedia://en/19593829) at nearest and next-nearest neighbor sites along one of the crystallographic axes of the [lattice](wikipedia://en/661808).

:::

The model is a prototype for complicated spatially modulated magnetic [superstructures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstructure_%28condensed_matter%29) in [crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal).

To describe experimental results on magnetic orderings in [erbium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erbium), the model was introduced in 1961 by [Roger Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Elliott_%28physicist%29) from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o2] The model has given its name in 1980 by [Michael E. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_E._Fisher) and [Walter Selke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Selke),[^o3] who analysed it first by [Monte Carlo methods](wikipedia://en/56098), and then by [low temperature series expansions](wikipedia://en/5072098), showing the fascinating complexity of its phase diagram, including [devil's staircases](wikipedia://en/320819) and a [Lifshitz point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicritical_point). Indeed, it provides, for two- and three-dimensional systems, a theoretical basis for understanding numerous experimental observations on [commensurate](wikipedia://en/753944) and [incommensurate](wikipedia://en/753944) structures, as well as accompanying [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423), in various [magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet), [alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy), [adsorbates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adsorbate), [polytypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymorphism_%28materials_science%29), [multiferroics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiferroics), and other [solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid). [^o4] [^o5] Further possible applications range from modeling of [cerebral cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_cortex) to [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274).
