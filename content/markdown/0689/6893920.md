# Clifford Taubes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clifford Taubes"}

**Clifford Henry Taubes** (born February 21, 1954)[^o3] is the William Petschek Professor of Mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and works in [gauge field theory](wikipedia://en/23912155), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and low-dimensional [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). His brother is the journalist [Gary Taubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Taubes).

:::

## Early career {#Early_career}

Taubes received his B.A. from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1975 and his Ph.D. in physics in 1980 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under the direction of [Arthur Jaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Jaffe),[^o3] having proven results collected in () about the existence of solutions to the [Landau–Ginzburg](wikipedia://en/396286) [vortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vortex) equations and the Bogomol'nyi [monopole](wikipedia://en/2415128) equations.

Soon, he began applying his gauge-theoretic expertise to pure mathematics. His work on the boundary of the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of solutions to the [Yang-Mills equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang-Mills_equation) was used by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305) in his proof of [Donaldson's theorem](wikipedia://en/2180754) on diagonizability of [intersection forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form). He proved in (Taubes 1987) that **R**<sup>4</sup> has an uncountable number of [smooth structures](wikipedia://en/1542238) (see also [exotic **R**<sup>4</sup>](wikipedia://en/2180637)), and (with [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457) in (Bott Taubes 1989)) proved Witten's rigidity theorem on the [elliptic genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_genus).

## Work based on Seiberg–Witten theory {#Work_based_on_Seiberg–Witten_theory}

In a series of four long papers in the 1990s (collected in (Taubes 2000)), Taubes proved that, on a closed [symplectic](wikipedia://en/294298) four-manifold, the (gauge-theoretic) [Seiberg–Witten invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_invariant) is equal to an invariant which enumerates certain [pseudoholomorphic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoholomorphic_curves) and is now known as [Taubes's Gromov invariant](wikipedia://en/3008788). This fact improved mathematicians' understanding of the topology of symplectic four-manifolds.

More recently (in (Taubes 2007)), by using Seiberg–Witten [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856) as developed by [Peter Kronheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kronheimer) and [Tomasz Mrowka](wikipedia://en/20652006) together with some new estimates on the spectral flow of [Dirac operators](wikipedia://en/1079448) and some methods from (Taubes 2000), Taubes proved the longstanding [Weinstein conjecture](wikipedia://en/4120135) for all three-dimensional [contact manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold), thus establishing that the Reeb vector field on such a manifold always has a closed orbit. Expanding both on this and on the equivalence of the Seiberg–Witten and Gromov invariants, Taubes has also proven (in a long series of preprints, beginning with (Taubes 2008)) that a contact 3-manifold's embedded contact homology is isomorphic to a version of its Seiberg–Witten Floer cohomology. More recently, Taubes, C. Kutluhan and Y-J. Lee proved that Seiberg–Witten Floer homology is isomorphic to Heegaard Floer homology.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- Four-time speaker at [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (1986, 1994 (plenary), 1998,[^o14] 2010 (plenary; selected, but did not speak))
- [Veblen Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veblen_Prize) (AMS) (1991)
- [Elie Cartan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Cartan_Prize) (Académie des Sciences) (1993)
- Elected as a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1995.
- Elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1996.
- [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) (2008)
- [NAS Award in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Award_in_Mathematics) (2008) from the National Academy of Sciences.[^o15][^o16]
- [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematics (2009) jointly with [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305)
