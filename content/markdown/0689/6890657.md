# Edward Belbruno

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Belbruno"}

**Edward Belbruno** (born August 2, 1951) is an American mathematician. His research areas are in [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182), dynamical systems, dynamical astronomy, and aerospace engineering.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Belbruno was born in the USAG Army Base in [Heidelberg, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg,_Germany). He received his associate degree from [Mitchell College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_College), his Bachelor of Science degree in mathematics from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) and his PhD in mathematics from New York University's [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) in 1981, where his mentor was mathematician [Jürgen Moser](wikipedia://en/1752316).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

He worked as mathematician at Boston University until 1985.[^o5] He was employed by the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory) from 1985 to 1990 as an orbital analyst on such missions as [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_%28spacecraft%29), [Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_%28spacecraft%29), [Cassini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassini_%28spacecraft%29), [Ulysses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_%28spacecraft%29), [Mars Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Observer), and others. During that time, he laid the foundations for the first systematic application of [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295) to space flight originally called [fuzzy boundary theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_travel#Fuzzy_orbits), which allows for the construction of very low energy paths for spacecraft.[^o6]

Belbruno had first proposed using a low-energy transfer orbit for a JPL probe in 1988. However, he faced a great deal of skepticism, and found himself in conflict with engineers. He had also expected to make no progress on *Hiten*, but the Japanese proved receptive to his ideas and called ballistic capture an "amazing result." He left JPL in fall of 1990 and took a position at [Pomona College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona_College).[^o7]

Belbruno is president and founder of the company Innovative Orbital Design, Inc., based in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) and holds patents on routes in space. He consulted on the rescue of the [Asiasat-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAS-22) communications satellite for Hughes, although a different trajectory was ultimately used for the rescue.

Belbruno's books include *Fly Me to the Moon* and *Capture Dynamics and Chaotic Motions in Celestial Mechanics*. He is a consultant with the [National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aeronautics_and_Space_Administration), and has made appearances on NBC's [*Today Show*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_%28NBC_program%29) and [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR)'s *[Studio 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_360)* entitled "Propelled to Paint".[^o8]

The abstract expressionist paintings of Ed Belbruno reflect both inner and outer realities. Belbruno is self-taught and draws inspiration from his subconscious and his personal life, but is also deeply influenced by his research in math, science, and astrophysics.[^o9] Belbruno was featured at the Shanghai Art Fair, November 2–5, 2017[^o10] and the West Contemporary Arts Appreciation Society exhibition, in Weihai in 2018.[^o11] An exhibition of his work at Agora Gallery (530 West 25th Street, New York) on Thursday, Nov. 14, 2019 included a screening of the documentary "Painting the Way to the Moon" and special guest [Neil deGrasse Tyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_deGrasse_Tyson).[^o12]

A documentary by Jacob Okada called "Painting the Way to the Moon" explores the life, art, and science of NASA-JPL mathematician, Ed Belbruno, and includes a discussion on the nature of scientific creativity with Neil deGrasse Tyson. It won Best Feature Documentary at the [Philip K. Dick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_K._Dick), Boston Sci-Fi, and NYLA Film Festivals.[^o13]

Belbruno was awarded the [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize) in November, 2017 in recognition for his “Accomplishments in Research and Teaching in Mathematics as Applied to Celestial Mechanics, Astrodynamics and AstroPhysics”.[^o14] The Humboldt Research Award is Germany's most prestigious award in mathematics and sciences which supported Belbruno in a yearlong stay in Germany at the University of Ausburg.[^o15]

Currently, Belbruno is a Clinical Professor of Mathematics at [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University)[^o16] and a Visiting Research Collaborator at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o17]
