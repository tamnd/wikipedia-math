# Feller process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Feller process"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) relating to [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes), a **Feller process** is a particular kind of [Markov process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $X$ be a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) with a [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) [base](wikipedia://en/59220). Let $C_0(X)$ denote the space of all real-valued [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on $X$ that [vanish at infinity](wikipedia://en/461986), equipped with the [sup-norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sup_norms) $\|f\|$. From analysis, we know that $C_0(X)$ with the sup norm is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989).

A **Feller semigroup** on $C_0(X)$ is a [contraction C<sub>0</sub>-semigroup](wikipedia://en/1644938#Contraction_semigroups) of [positive operators](wikipedia://en/64045357) on $C_0(X)$. Concretely, it is a collection $(T_t)_{t \ge 0}$ of [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) from $C_0(X)$ to itself with the following properties[^o4]:

- $T_t f \ge 0$ for all $t \ge 0$ and $f \in C_0(X)$ where $f \ge 0$, i.e., each $T_t$ is a [positive operator](wikipedia://en/64045357);
- $\|T_t f\| \le \|f\|$ for all $t \ge 0$ and $f \in C_0(X)$, i.e., it is a [contraction](wikipedia://en/6239) (in the weak sense);
- $T_0 = \operatorname{Id}$ and $T_{t+s} = T_t \circ T_s$ for all $s,t \ge 0$, i.e., it is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799);
- $\lim_{t \to 0} \|T_t f - f\| = 0$ for every $f \in C_0(X)$. Using the semigroup property, this is equivalent to the map $T_t f$ from $t \in [0,\infty)$ to $C_0(X)$ being [right continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_continuous) for every $f$.

**Warning**: This terminology is not uniform across the literature. In particular, the assumption that $T_t$ maps $C_0(X)$ into itself is replaced by some authors by the condition that it maps $C_b(X)$, the space of bounded continuous functions, into itself. The reason for this is twofold: first, it allows including processes that enter "from infinity" in finite time. Second, it is more suitable to the treatment of spaces that are not locally compact and for which the notion of "vanishing at infinity" makes no sense.

A **Feller transition function** is a probability transition function associated with a Feller semigroup.

A **Feller process** is a [Markov process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process) with a Feller transition function.

:::

## Generator {#Generator}

Feller processes (or transition semigroups) can be described by their [infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/12193437). A function $f$ in $C_0(X)$ is said to be in the domain of the generator if the uniform limit

$$Af = \lim_{t\rightarrow 0} \frac{T_tf - f}{t},$$

exists. The operator $A$ is the generator of $(T_t)_{t \ge 0}$, and the space of functions on which it is defined is written as $D(A)$.

A characterization of operators that can occur as the infinitesimal generator of Feller processes is given by the [Hille–Yosida theorem](wikipedia://en/1578212). This uses the resolvent of the Feller semigroup, defined below.

## Resolvent {#Resolvent}

The **[resolvent](wikipedia://en/3378540)** of a Feller process (or semigroup) is a collection of maps $(R_\lambda)_{\lambda > 0}$ from $C_0(X)$ to itself defined by

$$R_\lambda f = \int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}T_t f\,dt.$$

It can be shown that it satisfies the identity

$$R_\lambda R_\mu = R_\mu R_\lambda = (R_\mu-R_\lambda)/(\lambda-\mu).$$

Furthermore, for any fixed $\lambda > 0$, the image of $R_\lambda$ is equal to the domain $D(A)$ of the generator $A$, and

$$\begin{aligned} & R_\lambda = (\lambda - A)^{-1}, \\ & A = \lambda - R_\lambda^{-1}. \end{aligned}$$

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) and the [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) are examples of Feller processes (with Feller semigroups given by $T_{t}f := \mathbb{E}[f(X_{t})]$). More generally, every [Lévy process](wikipedia://en/1025748) is a Feller process.
- [Bessel processes](wikipedia://en/7646799) are Feller processes.
- Solutions to [stochastic differential equations](wikipedia://en/1361454) with [Lipschitz continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_continuous) coefficients are Feller processes.
- Every [adapted](wikipedia://en/6583420) right continuous Feller process on a [filtered probability space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_probability_space) $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\geq 0})$ satisfies the [strong Markov property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Markov_property) with respect to the filtration $(\mathcal{F}_{t^+})_{t\geq 0}$, i.e., for each $(\mathcal{F}_{t^+})_{t\geq 0}$-[stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193) $\tau$, conditioned on the event $\{\tau < \infty\}$, we have that for each $t\ge 0$, $X_{\tau + t}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_{\tau^+}$ given $X_\tau$.[^o9]
