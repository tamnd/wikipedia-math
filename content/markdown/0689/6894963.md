# Michael Golomb

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Golomb"}

**Michael Golomb** (May 3, 1909 in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) – April 9, 2008) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [educator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educator) who was affiliated with [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) for over half a century. He was a student of [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191) and Adolf Hammerstein, and received his doctorate from the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) in 1933. However, as a Jew, he had to leave Germany shortly afterwards to avoid Nazi persecution.[^o3] After a short period in [Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb) in the former [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia), Michael Golomb arrived in the [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1939, when he turned to [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969). He was one of the first mathematicians to apply [normed vector spaces](wikipedia://en/21538) in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). He taught mathematics <http://www.math.purdue.edu> at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) from 1942 until his retirement in 1975, at times holding joint appointment with the Schools of Engineering. He continued to teach as Professor Emeritus.

:::

In 1998 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), Michael Golomb was honored as part of a special exhibition entitled "Terror and Exile: Persecuted and expelled Berlin mathematicians in the time of the Nazi regime."[^o4] The exhibition was organized by the city of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) to coincide with the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) there.

Golomb co-authored a paper with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284). [^o5]
