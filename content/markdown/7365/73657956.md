# How Data Happened

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="How Data Happened"}

***How Data Happened: A History from the Age of Reason to the Age of Algorithms*** is a 2023 non-fiction book written by [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) professors [Chris Wiggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wiggins_%28data_scientist%29) and [Matthew L. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_L._Jones). The book explores the history of data and statistics from the end of the 18th century to the present day.[^o3]

:::

## Content {#Content}

The book starts at the end of the 18th century, when European states began tabulating physical resources, and ends at the present day, when algorithms manipulate our personal information as a commodity. It looks at the rise of data and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), and how early statistical methods were used to justify [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics), quantify supposed racial differences, and develop military and industrial applications. The authors also discuss the impact of the internet and e-commerce on data collection, the rise of [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904), and the consequences of government-run [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance) systems collecting vast amounts of personal data for customized, [targeted advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_advertising). They emphasize the importance of [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy) and [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) and propose remedies to the problems caused by mass data collection, including stronger regulation of the tech industry and [collective action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_action) by its employees. The book is a historical analysis that provides context for understanding the debates surrounding data and its control.[^o3][^o5][^o6]

The book has 336 pages and was published in 2023 by [W. W. Norton & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._W._Norton_&_Company).[^o3]
