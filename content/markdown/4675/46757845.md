# John David Crawford

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John David Crawford"}

**John David Crawford** (1954–1998)[^o3] was an American physicist and professor at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh).[^o3] He obtained his undergraduate degree with honors from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1977 and his Ph.D. from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1983.[^o3]

:::

An internationally recognized researcher in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), he specialized in [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics) and [nonlinear dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_dynamics).[^o4] His broad physical insight and deep knowledge of mathematics enabled him to make profound contributions to [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).[^o4] He published more than 80 research papers and wrote a landmark review on [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042).[^o5] Additionally, he was also a codirector of the "Research Experiences for Undergraduates" program, which exposed undergraduates to scientific study, in 1997.[^o3]

Apart from his research, he was a passionate mountain climber. He died on August 23, 1998, at the [Montefiore Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPMC_Montefiore) in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh) of [Burkitt's lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkitt's_lymphoma), a form of lymph cancer.[^o3]

In 2001, [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376)'s [Activity Group in Dynamical Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activity_Group_in_Dynamical_Systems) established the [J.D. Crawford Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.D._Crawford_Prize), which is now the world's top award in dynamical systems.[^o6]
