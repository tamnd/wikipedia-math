# Logjam (computer security)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Logjam"}

**Logjam** is a [security vulnerability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability_%28computing%29) in systems that use [Diffie–Hellman key exchange](wikipedia://en/7903) with the same prime number. It was discovered by a team of computer scientists and publicly reported on May 20, 2015.[^o2] The discoverers were able to demonstrate their attack on 512-bit ([US export-grade](wikipedia://en/826003)) DH systems. They estimated that a state-level attacker could do so for 1024-bit systems, then widely used, thereby allowing decryption of a significant fraction of Internet traffic. They recommended upgrading to at least 2048 bits for shared prime systems.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Details {#Details}

Diffie–Hellman key exchange depends for its security on the presumed difficulty of solving the [discrete logarithm problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_logarithm_problem). The authors took advantage of the fact that the [number field sieve](wikipedia://en/152734) algorithm, which is generally the most effective method for finding discrete logarithms, consists of four large computational steps, of which the first three depend only on the order of the group G, not on the specific number whose finite log is desired. If the results of the first three steps are [precomputed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precomputed) and saved, they can be used to solve any discrete log problem for that prime group in relatively short time. This vulnerability was known as early as 1992.[^o7] It turns out that much Internet traffic only uses one of a handful of groups that are of order 1024 bits or less.

One approach enabled by this vulnerability that the authors demonstrated was using a [man-in-the-middle network attacker](wikipedia://en/144676) to downgrade a [Transport Layer Security](wikipedia://en/187813) (TLS) connection to use 512-bit DH [export-grade](wikipedia://en/826003) cryptography, allowing them to read the exchanged data and inject data into the connection. It affects the [HTTPS](wikipedia://en/13586), [SMTPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMTPS), and [IMAPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAPS) protocols, among others. The authors needed several thousand [CPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU) cores for a week to precompute data for a single 512-bit prime. Once that was done, however, individual logarithms could be solved in about a minute using two 18-core [Intel Xeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Xeon) CPUs.[^o8] Its CVE ID is 2015-4000.[^o9]

The authors also estimated the feasibility of the attack against 1024-bit Diffie–Hellman primes. By design, many Diffie–Hellman implementations use the same pre-generated [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) for their field. This was considered secure, since the [discrete logarithm problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_logarithm_problem) is still considered hard for big enough primes even if the group is known and reused. The researchers calculated the cost of creating logjam precomputation for one 1024-bit prime at hundreds of millions of USD, and noted that this was well within range of the FY2012 \$10.5 billion [U.S. Consolidated Cryptologic Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Consolidated_Cryptologic_Program) (which includes [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA)). Because of the reuse of primes, generating precomputation for just one prime would break two-thirds of [VPNs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VPN) and a quarter of all [SSH](wikipedia://en/28814) servers globally. The researchers noted that this attack fits claims in leaked NSA papers that NSA is able to break much current cryptography. They recommend using primes of 2048 bits or more as a defense or switching to [elliptic-curve Diffie–Hellman](wikipedia://en/3357958) (ECDH).[^o2] Claims on the practical implications of the attack were however disputed by security researchers Eyal Ronen and [Adi Shamir](wikipedia://en/57314) in their paper "Critical Review of Imperfect Forward Secrecy".[^o10]

## Responses {#Responses}

- On May 12, 2015, Microsoft released a patch for [Internet Explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Explorer).[^o12]
- On June 16, 2015, the [Tor Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_Project) provided a patch for Logjam to the [Tor Browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_Browser).[^o13]
- On June 30, 2015, [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc.) released a patch for both [OS X Yosemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OS_X_Yosemite) and [iOS 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_8) operating system.[^o14][^o15]
- On June 30, 2015, the [Mozilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla) project released a fix for the [Firefox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefox) browser.[^o16]
- On September 1, 2015, Google released a fix for the [Chrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chrome) browser.[^o17]
- On December 6, 2017, [IETF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Engineering_Task_Force) published 8270 called "Increase the Secure Shell Minimum Recommended Diffie-Hellman Modulus Size to 2048 Bits".
