# Harold Rosenberg (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harold Rosenberg"}

**Harold William Rosenberg** (born February 19, 1941 in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City)) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).[^o2] Rosenberg has worked at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), and at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). He currently works at the [IMPA](wikipedia://en/2334708), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil).[^o2] He earned his Ph.D. at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1963 under the supervision of Stephen P. L. Diliberto.

:::

In 2004 he was elected to the [Brazilian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o2] His students include [Norbert A'Campo](wikipedia://en/34945743), [Christian Bonatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Bonatti), and [Michael Herman](wikipedia://en/28451358).[^o3]

In 1993, he studied the hypersurfaces in Euclidean space with a given constant value of an [elementary symmetric polynomial](wikipedia://en/1440695) of the [shape operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_operator), known as a *higher-order mean curvature*. His primary result was to obtain some control of the height of such a surface over a plane containing its boundary. As an application, he was able to derive some rigidity results for complete surfaces with constant higher-order mean curvature.

In 2004, he and Uwe Abresch extended the classical *Hopf differential*, discovered by [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082) in the 1950s, from the setting of surfaces in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) to the setting of surfaces in products of two-dimensional [space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009) with the real line. They showed that, if the surface has constant mean curvature, then their Hopf differential is holomorphic relative to the natural [complex structure](wikipedia://en/514258) on the surface. As an application, they were able to show that any immersed sphere of constant mean curvature must be rotationally symmetric, thereby extending a classical theorem of [Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Danilovich_Aleksandrov).
