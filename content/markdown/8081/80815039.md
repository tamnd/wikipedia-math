# −2

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="−2"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **negative two** or **minus two** is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) two units from the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432),[^o3] denoted as −2[^o4] or <sup>−</sup>2.[^o5] It is the [additive inverse](wikipedia://en/228312) of [2](wikipedia://en/64516), following −3 and preceding [−1](wikipedia://en/449568), and is the largest negative even integer. Except in rare cases exploring [integral ring](wikipedia://en/15462) [prime elements](wikipedia://en/60692), negative two is generally not considered a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666).[^o7]

:::

Negative two is sometimes used to denote the square reciprocal in the notation of [SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) base units, such as [m·s<sup>−2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_per_second_squared). Additionally, in fields like [software design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_design), [−1](wikipedia://en/449568) is often used as an invalid return value for functions,[^o9] and similarly, negative two may indicate other invalid conditions beyond negative one.[^o10] For example, in the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004), negative one denotes non-existence, while negative two indicates an infinite solution.

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Negative two is a complementary [Bell number](wikipedia://en/201022) (also known as Rao Uppuluri-Carpenter numbers),[^o15][^o16] and a [Hermite number](wikipedia://en/18543082).
- Negative two makes the [class number](wikipedia://en/150907) of the [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) $\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{d}]$ equal to 1, meaning its [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) is a [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546).[^o17][^o18] According to the [Stark–Heegner theorem](wikipedia://en/391251), only nine negative numbers have this property,[^o19][^o20][^o21] corresponding to [Heegner numbers](wikipedia://en/522147).
  - Negative two also makes the quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{d}]$ a simple Euclidean field (or norm-Euclidean field).[^o23] Only five negative numbers have this property: −11, −7, −3, −2, −1.[^o24][^o25] If relaxed, −15 is included.[^o26]
- Negative two is the largest negative number unreachable from 1 in two steps using addition, subtraction, or multiplication. The largest in one step is −1, and in three steps, −4.[^o28] This relates to the [straight-line program](wikipedia://en/45235652) combined with addition, subtraction, and multiplication,[^o29] exploring the algebraic complexity of integers in relation to NP = P.[^o30]
- Negative two is the second-order i.e., $H_2 = H_2(0) = -2$.[^o32]
- Negative two makes $k(k+1)(k+2)$ a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915).[^o33] Only nine integers have this property, with negative two being the smallest: −2, −1, 0, 1, 4, 5, 9, [56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/56_%28number%29), and [636](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/636_%28number%29).

### Divisors of negative two {#Divisors_of_negative_two}

The divisors of negative two, including negative [divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisors), are identical to those of two: −2, −1, 1, 2. By definition, negative numbers are not typically subjected to [prime factorization](wikipedia://en/23666#Unique_factorization), though $-1$ can be factored out as $-1 \times 2$,[^o36] making 2 a [prime factor](wikipedia://en/23666) but not the result of prime factorization. As a [Gaussian integer](wikipedia://en/48628), negative two can be factored as $i \times (1+i)^2$, where $1+i$ is a [Gaussian prime](wikipedia://en/26969485) and $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358).[^o37]

### Powers of negative two {#Powers_of_negative_two}

The first few powers of negative two are −2, 4, −8, 16, −32, 64, −128, oscillating between positive and negative.[^o39] The positive terms are powers of four, and the negative terms differ from powers of four by a factor of negative two. This property makes negative two the largest negative number that can represent all real numbers as a base without using a negative sign or [two's complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two's_complement).[^o39][^o41][^o42] In 1957, some computers used a base-negative-two numeral system for calculations.[^o44] Similarly, using $2i$ can represent complex numbers.[^o45]

The [sum of the powers of negative two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Series_of_−2) is a [divergent geometric series](wikipedia://en/9777020). Although divergent, its generalized sum is $\frac{1}{3}$.[^o46][^o47]

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} (-2)^k = 1 - 2 + 4 - 8 + \dots$$

Using the [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630) formula,[^o48]

$$\sum_{k=0}^\infty a r^k = \frac{a}{1-r}$$

with the first term $a=1$ and common ratio $r=-2$, the result is $\frac{1}{3}$. However, the series is divergent, with partial sums:

1, −1, 3, −5, 11, −21, 43, −85, 171, −341...[^o49]

Though divergent, [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler) assigned the value $\frac{1}{3}$ to this series,[^o50] known as [Euler summation](wikipedia://en/9780066).[^o51]

### Negative second power {#Negative_second_power}

The negative second power of a number is its square reciprocal, applicable to functions as well.[^o53] In daily life, it is occasionally used to denote units without a division sign, such as acceleration, typically written as m/s<sup>2</sup> but also as m·s<sup>−2</sup> in [SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) notation.[^o8]

Common topics related to the square reciprocal include: for any real number $n$, its square reciprocal $n^{-2}$ is always positive; the [inverse-square law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse-square_law);[^o54] grid turbulence decay;[^o55] and the [Basel problem](wikipedia://en/490303).[^o56] The Basel problem states that the sum of the square reciprocals of natural numbers converges to $\frac{\pi^2}{6}$:[^o57][^o56]

$$\sum^{\infty}_{n=1} {n^{-2}} = {1^{-2} }+{2^{-2} }+{3^{-2} }+\cdots = {1 \over 1^2 }+{1 \over 2^2 }+{1 \over 3^2 }+\cdots = { \pi^2 \over 6 }$$

This value equals the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) at 2.[^o58][^o59]

For any real number, the square reciprocal is positive. For negative two, it is $\frac{1}{4}$. The square reciprocals of the first few [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) are:

### Square root of negative two {#Square_root_of_negative_two}

The square root of negative two, defined with the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358) $i$ satisfying $i^2 = -1$, is derived from $\sqrt{-x} = \pm i \sqrt{x}$. For negative two, it is $\sqrt{-2} = \pm i \sqrt{2}$.[^o63][^o64][^o65][^o66][^o67] The principal value is $\sqrt{-2} = i \sqrt{2}$.[^o68][^o64][^o65][^o66][^o67]

:::

## Representation {#Representation}

Negative two is typically represented by adding a negative sign before 2,[^o71] commonly called "negative two" or "minus two" in English.[^o72]

In [binary](wikipedia://en/238686), especially in computing, negative numbers are often represented using [two's complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two's_complement).[^o73] Negative two is represented as "...11111110<sub>(2)</sub>", specifically "1110<sub>(2)</sub>" for 4-bit, "11111110<sub>(2)</sub>" for 8-bit, and "1111111111111110<sub>(2)</sub>" for 16-bit integers.[^o74] In signed notation, it is "−10<sub>(2)</sub>".[^o75]

:::

## Plus or minus two {#Plus_or_minus_two}

Plus or minus two ($\pm 2$) is expressed using the [plus-minus sign](wikipedia://en/367225), representing both positive and negative two. It denotes the [square roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_roots) of 4 or the solutions to the [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175) $x^2 = 4$, i.e., $\sqrt{4} = \pm{2}$. Plus or minus two appears more frequently in cultural contexts, such as [music compositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_composition)[^o77] or the documentary ±2 °C, which explores the environmental impact of a [global temperature change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) of two degrees.[^o78][^o79]
