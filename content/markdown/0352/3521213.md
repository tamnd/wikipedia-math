# Trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron

*Image: Trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron** or **rhombo-trapezoidal dodecahedron** is a [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) with 6 [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) and 6 [trapezoidal](wikipedia://en/201826) faces. It has $D_{3h}$ symmetry. A concave form can be constructed with an identical net, seen as excavating [trigonal trapezohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_trapezohedra) from the top and bottom. It is also called the **trapezoidal dodecahedron**.

:::

## Description {#Description}

The trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron is a polyhedron with six trapezoidal and six rhombic faces. This polyhedron can be obtained from a [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) by cutting it in half through the hexagonal cross-section and rotating the halves 60° with respect to each other. It is the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of a [triangular orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1196250), a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) whose faces are squares and equilateral triangles.

The trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron is a [plesiohedron](wikipedia://en/53396858), a [special kind of polyhedron that can tile a space](wikipedia://en/58607583) with its copy, representing the three-dimensional [Voronoi cell](wikipedia://en/177668) of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) in a [hexagonal close packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-packing_of_spheres); this and the face-centered cubic packing are the densest ways to [pack spheres](wikipedia://en/368621). It is therefore related to the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), as suggested from the construction, which is a Voronoi cell of the other optimal way to pack spheres. The two shapes differ in their combinatorial structure as well as in their geometry: in the rhombic dodecahedron, every edge connects a degree-three vertex to a degree-four vertex, whereas the trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron has six edges that connect vertices of equal degrees.

*Image: Trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron honeycomb.png.* Copies of trapezo-rhombic dodecahedra as [plesiohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesiohedra), creating a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)

A trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron has two different edges lengths, $\frac{2}{3}a$ and $\frac{4}{3}a$. Its surface area $A$ and volume $V$ are given by

$$A = 8\sqrt{2} a^2 \approx 11.314a^2, \quad V = \frac{16}{9}\sqrt{3} a^3 \approx 3.079a^3.$$

## Variations {#Variations}

The trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron can be seen as an elongation of another dodecahedron, which can be called a **rhombo-triangular dodecahedron**, with 6 rhombi (or squares) and 6 triangles. It also has $D_{3h}$ symmetry and is space-filling. It has 21 edges and 11 vertices. With square faces, it can be seen as a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) split across the 3-fold axis, separated with the two halves rotated 180°, and filling the gaps with triangles. When used as a space-filler, connecting dodecahedra on their triangles leaves two cubical step surfaces on the top and bottom, which can connect with complementary steps.
