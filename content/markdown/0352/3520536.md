# Hyperexponential distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperexponential distribution"}

*Image: Hyperexponential.svg.* Diagram showing queueing system equivalent of a hyperexponential distribution

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **hyperexponential distribution** is a [continuous probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_probability_distribution) whose [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) *X* is given by

:::

$$f_X(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n f_{Y_i}(x)\;p_i,$$

where each *Y*<sub>*i*</sub> is an [exponentially distributed](wikipedia://en/45906) random variable with rate parameter *λ*<sub>*i*</sub>, and *p*<sub>*i*</sub> is the probability that *X* will take on the form of the exponential distribution with rate *λ*<sub>*i*</sub>.[^o2] It is named the *hyper*exponential distribution since its [coefficient of variation](wikipedia://en/1012687) is greater than that of the exponential distribution, whose coefficient of variation is 1, and the [hypoexponential distribution](wikipedia://en/1873318), which has a coefficient of variation smaller than one. While the [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906) is the continuous analogue of the [geometric distribution](wikipedia://en/45922), the hyperexponential distribution is not analogous to the [hypergeometric distribution](wikipedia://en/180841). The hyperexponential distribution is an example of a [mixture density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixture_density).

An example of a hyperexponential random variable can be seen in the context of [telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephony), where, if someone has a modem and a phone, their phone line usage could be modeled as a hyperexponential distribution where there is probability *p* of them talking on the phone with rate *λ*<sub>1</sub> and probability *q* of them using their internet connection with rate *λ*<sub>2</sub>.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Since the expected value of a sum is the sum of the expected values, the expected value of a hyperexponential random variable can be shown as

$$E[X] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x f(x) \, dx= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i\int_0^\infty x\lambda_i e^{-\lambda_ix} \, dx = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i}{\lambda_i}$$

and

$$E\!\left[X^2\right] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x^2 f(x) \, dx = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i\int_0^\infty x^2\lambda_i e^{-\lambda_ix} \, dx = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{2}{\lambda_i^2}p_i,$$

from which we can derive the variance:[^o5]

$$\operatorname{Var}[X] = E\!\left[X^2\right] - E\!\left[X\right]^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{2}{\lambda_i^2}p_i - \left[\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i}{\lambda_i}\right]^2 = \left[\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i}{\lambda_i}\right]^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n p_i p_j \left(\frac{1}{\lambda_i} - \frac{1}{\lambda_j} \right)^2.$$

The standard deviation exceeds the mean in general (except for the degenerate case of all the *λ*s being equal), so the [coefficient of variation](wikipedia://en/1012687) is greater than 1.

The [moment-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function) is given by

$$E\!\left[e^{tx}\right] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{tx} f(x) \, dx= \sum_{i=1}^n p_i \int_0^\infty e^{tx}\lambda_i e^{-\lambda_i x} \, dx = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_i - t}p_i.$$

:::

## Fitting {#Fitting}

A given [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543), including a [heavy-tailed distribution](wikipedia://en/8092200), can be approximated by a hyperexponential distribution by fitting recursively to different time scales using [Prony's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prony's_method).[^o7]
