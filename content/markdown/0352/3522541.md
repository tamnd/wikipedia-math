# Elongated hexagonal bipyramid

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Elongated hexagonal bipyramid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **elongated hexagonal bipyramid** is constructed by elongating a [hexagonal bipyramid](wikipedia://en/2081012) (by inserting a [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668) between its congruent halves).

:::

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

This polyhedron is in the family of [elongated bipyramids](wikipedia://en/3522878), of which the first three can be [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034): [J<sub>14</sub>](wikipedia://en/1177950), [J<sub>15</sub>](wikipedia://en/1177952), and [J<sub>16</sub>](wikipedia://en/1177953). The hexagonal form can be constructed by all regular faces but is not a *Johnson solid* because 6 equilateral triangles would form six co-planar faces (in a regular [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733)).

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

- A quartz crystal is an example of an elongated hexagonal bipyramid. Because it has 18 faces, it can be called an [octadecahedron](wikipedia://en/35942599). Other chemicals also have this shape.[^o6]
- The *edge-first orthogonal projection* of a [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485#Projections) is an elongated hexagonal bipyramid.
- Used as the shape of [Fruit Gushers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Gushers) candy.
- Used as a physical manifestation for assisting various branches of three-dimensional [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).

:::
