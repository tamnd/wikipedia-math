# Richard H. Price

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard H. Price"}

**Richard H. Price** (born March 1, 1943) is an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) specializing in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). He is known for Price’s theorem and contributions to [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) perturbation theory.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Price graduated from [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) in 1960, and went on to earn a dual degree in physics and engineering from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1965. He earned his PhD in 1971 from [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech) under the supervision of [Kip Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne).[^n1]

## Career {#Career}

Price spent his career from 1971 to 2004 at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah), where he holds the title of emeritus professor.[^o5] In 2004, he joined the [Center for Gravitational Wave Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Gravitational_Wave_Astronomy) at the [University of Texas at Brownsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Brownsville). As of 2015, he became senior lecturer in physics at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT). He is also on the adjunct faculty at the [University of Massachusetts Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Dartmouth).[^o6]

In 2017, Price became the editor of the *[American Journal of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Physics)*.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Price’s theorem"}

### Price’s theorem {#Price’s_theorem}

Price is probably best known for a 1972 theorem that is usually informally stated as follows: *any inhomogeneities in the spacetime geometry outside a black hole will be radiated away*.[^o11] (Any such inhomogeneities can be quantified as nonzero higher [multipole moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipole_moment).) Price's theorem explains how the [no hair theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_hair_theorem) is enforced.

:::

### Numerical relativity {#Numerical_relativity}

Price also made pioneering numerical simulations which established (nonrigorously) a precise scenario for the emission of gravitational radiation during the merger of two compact objects (such as two [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650)).[^o13] Subsequent work has largely confirmed the scenario which was first developed in his work. These simulations have provided a major impetus for the development of [gravitational wave](wikipedia://en/8111079) detectors such as [LIGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO).[^o14] He has also produced relativity visualizations.[^o15]

## Teaching and honors {#Teaching_and_honors}

Price has done much in development of pedagogical techniques in physics at the undergraduate, graduate, and postdoc levels;[^o17] a significant portion at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah) in [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), Utah.\<ref name="URI"[^o18]

Price is the coauthor of three well known books in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). He is a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) and the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o7]
