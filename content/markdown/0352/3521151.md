# Holditch's theorem

*Image: Holditch s theorem.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Holditch's theorem"}

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), **Holditch's theorem** states that if a [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) of fixed length is allowed to rotate inside a convex closed curve, then the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of a point on the chord a distance *p* from one end and a distance *q* from the other is a closed curve whose enclosed area is less than that of the original curve by $\pi pq$. The theorem was published in 1858 by Rev. [Hamnet Holditch](wikipedia://en/41808008).[^o5][^o2] While not mentioned by Holditch, the proof of the theorem requires an assumption that the chord be short enough that the traced locus is a simple closed curve.[^o3]

:::

## Observations {#Observations}

The theorem is included as one of [Clifford Pickover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Pickover)'s 250 milestones in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).[^o5] Some peculiarities of the theorem include that the area formula $\pi pq$ is independent of both the shape and the size of the original curve, and that the area formula is the same as for that of the area of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) with semi-axes *p* and *q*. The theorem's author was a president of [Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caius_College,_Cambridge).

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

## Extensions {#Extensions}

Broman[^o3] gives a more precise statement of the theorem, along with a generalization. The generalization allows, for example, consideration of the case in which the outer curve is a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), so that the conditions of the precise statement of Holditch's theorem do not hold because the paths of the endpoints of the chord have [retrograde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrograde_motion) portions (portions that retrace themselves) whenever an [acute angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_angle) is traversed. Nevertheless, the generalization shows that if the chord is shorter than any of the triangle's [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241), and is short enough that the traced locus is a simple curve, Holditch's formula for the in-between area is still correct (and remains so if the triangle is replaced by any [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019) with a short enough chord). However, other cases result in different formulas.

:::
