# Peter Shalen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Shalen"}

**Peter B. Shalen** (born c. 1946) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working primarily in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). He is the "S" in [JSJ decomposition](wikipedia://en/228599).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He graduated from [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) in 1962,[^o4] before earning a BA from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) in 1966 and his PhD from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1972.[^o5] Following academic appointments at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University), and the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute), he joined the faculty of the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago).

Shalen was a [Sloan Foundation Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in mathematics (1977–1979).[^o6] In 1986 he was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).[^o7] He was elected as a member of the 2017 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to three-dimensional topology and for exposition".[^o8]

## Work {#Work}

His work with [Marc Culler](wikipedia://en/29258614) related properties of representation varieties of [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1589135) [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) [groups](wikipedia://en/11004) to decompositions of 3-manifolds. Based on this work, Culler, [Cameron Gordon](wikipedia://en/4695115), [John Luecke](wikipedia://en/29631637), and Shalen proved the [cyclic surgery theorem](wikipedia://en/29580519). An important corollary of the theorem is that at most one nontrivial [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) (+1 or −1) on a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) can result in a [simply-connected](wikipedia://en/523879) 3-manifold. This was an important piece of the [Gordon–Luecke theorem](wikipedia://en/10208241) that knots are determined by their complements. This paper is often referred to as "CGLS".

With [John Morgan](wikipedia://en/8487966), he generalized his work with Culler, and reproved several foundational results of [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190).
