# David Harbater

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Harbater"}

**David Harbater** (born December 19, 1952) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), well known for his work in [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Harbater was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) and attended [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School), where he was on the math team. After graduating in 1970, he entered [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).

After graduating [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude) in 1974, Harbater earned a master's degree from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) and then a Ph.D. in 1978 from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), where he wrote a dissertation (Deformation Theory and the Fundamental Group in Algebraic Geometry) under the direction of [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821).

## Research {#Research}

He solved the [inverse Galois problem](wikipedia://en/739166) over $\mathbb{Q}_p(t)$, and made many other significant contributions to the field of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316).

Harbater's recent work on patching over fields, together with Julia Hartmann and Daniel Krashen, has had applications in such varied fields as [quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_forms), [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082) and [local-global principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local-global_principle).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1995, Harbater was awarded the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) for his solution, with [Michel Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928), of the long outstanding [Abhyankar conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhyankar_conjecture).

In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6]
