# Exhaustion by compact sets

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exhaustion by compact sets"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), an **exhaustion by compact sets**[^n1] of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is a nested [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [compact subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_subset) $K_i$ of $X$ (i.e. $K_1\subseteq K_2\subseteq K_3\subseteq\cdots$), such that each $K_i$ is contained in the [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of $K_{i+1}$, i.e. $K_i\subset\text{int}(K_{i+1})$, and $X=\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty K_i$.

:::

A space admitting an exhaustion by compact sets is called **exhaustible by compact sets**.[^n2]

As an example, for the space $X=\mathbb{R}^n$, the sequence of [closed balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_ball) $K_i = \{ x : |x| \le i \}$ forms an exhaustion of the space by compact sets.

There is a weaker condition that drops the requirement that $K_i$ is in the interior of $K_{i+1}$, meaning the space is [σ-compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Σ-compact) (i.e., a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of compact subsets.)

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

If there is an exhaustion by compact sets, the space is necessarily locally compact (if Hausdorff). The converse is also often true. For example, for a locally compact Hausdorff space $X$ that is a countable union of compact subsets, we can construct an exhaustion as follows. We write $X = \bigcup_1^{\infty} K_n$ as a union of compact sets $K_n$. Then inductively choose open sets $V_n \supset \overline{V_{n-1}} \cup K_n$ with compact closures, where $V_0 = \emptyset$. Then $\overline{V_n}$ is a required exhaustion.

For a locally compact Hausdorff space that is second-countable, a similar argument can be used to construct an exhaustion.

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

For a Hausdorff space $X$, an exhaustion by compact sets can be used to show the space is paracompact.[^o8] Indeed, suppose we have an increasing sequence $V_1 \subset V_2 \subset \cdots$ of open subsets such that $X = \bigcup V_n$ and each $\overline{V_n}$ is compact and is contained in $V_{n+1}$. Let $\mathcal{U}$ be an open cover of $X$. We also let $V_n = \emptyset, \, n \le 0$. Then, for each $n \ge 1$, $\{ (V_{n + 1} - \overline{V_{n-2}}) \cap U \mid U \in \mathcal{U} \}$ is an open cover of the compact set $\overline{V_n} - V_{n-1}$ and thus admits a finite subcover $\mathcal{V}_n$. Then $\mathcal{V} := \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathcal{V}_n$ is a locally finite refinement of $\mathcal{U}.$

Remark: The proof in fact shows that each open cover admits a countable refinement consisting of open sets with compact closures and each of whose members intersects only finitely many others.[^o8]

The following type of converse also holds. A paracompact locally compact Hausdorff space with countably many open connected components is a countable union of compact sets[^o9] and thus admits an exhaustion by compact subsets.

:::

## Relation to other properties {#Relation_to_other_properties}

The following are equivalent for a topological space $X$:[^o11]

1. $X$ is exhaustible by compact sets.
1. $X$ is [σ-compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Σ-compact) and [weakly locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_locally_compact).
1. $X$ is [Lindelöf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindelöf_%28topology%29) and weakly locally compact.

(where *weakly locally compact* means [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) in the weak sense that each point has a compact [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29)).

The [hemicompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicompact) property is intermediate between exhaustible by compact sets and σ-compact. Every space exhaustible by compact sets is hemicompact[^o12] and every hemicompact space is σ-compact, but the [reverse implications](wikipedia://en/155941) do not hold. For example, the [Arens-Fort space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arens-Fort_space) and the [Appert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appert_space) are hemicompact, but not exhaustible by compact sets (because not weakly locally compact),[^o13] and the set $\mathbb{Q}$ of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) with the usual [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) is σ-compact, but not hemicompact.[^o14]

Every [regular Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Hausdorff_space) that is a countable union of compact sets is [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact).
