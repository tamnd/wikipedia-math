# Gelfond–Schneider theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gelfond–Schneider theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Gelfond–Schneider theorem** establishes the [transcendence](wikipedia://en/30325) of a large class of numbers.

:::

## History {#History}

It was originally proved independently in 1934 by [Aleksandr Gelfond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Gelfond) and [Theodor Schneider](wikipedia://en/10312621).[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

> If $a$ and $b$ are [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) with $a \not \in \{ 0,1 \}$ and $b$ [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689), then any value of $a^b$ is a [transcendental number](wikipedia://en/30325).

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Sqrt(2)^sqrt(2)"}

### Comments {#Comments}

The values of *$a$* and *$b$* are not restricted to [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438); [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) are allowed (here complex numbers are not regarded as rational when they have an imaginary part not equal to 0, even if both the real and imaginary parts are rational).

In general, $a^b = \text{exp} (b \ln a)$ is [multivalued](wikipedia://en/222367). This accounts for the phrase "any value of" in the theorem's statement.

An equivalent formulation of the theorem is the following: if *$\alpha$* and *$\gamma$* are nonzero algebraic numbers, and we take any non-zero logarithm of *$\alpha$*, then $(\ln \gamma )/( \ln \alpha)$ is either [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) or transcendental. This may be expressed as saying that if $\ln \alpha$, $\ln \gamma$ are [linearly independent](wikipedia://en/101863) over the rationals, then they are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers. The generalization of this statement to more general [linear forms in logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_forms_in_logarithms) of several algebraic numbers is in the domain of [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698).

If the restriction that *a* and *b* be algebraic is removed, the statement does not remain true in general. For example,

$${\left(\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}\right)}^{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}^2 = 2.$$

Here, $a$ is $\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}$, which (as proven by the theorem itself) is transcendental rather than algebraic. Similarly, if $a=3$ and $b = (\ln 2)/(\ln 3)$, which is transcendental, then $a^b = 2$ is algebraic. A characterization of the values for *$a$* and *$b$* which yield a transcendental $a^b$ is not known.

[Kurt Mahler](wikipedia://en/4421496) proved the [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) analogue of the theorem: if *$a$* and *$b$* are in $\mathbf{C}_p$, the [completion](wikipedia://en/7832#Completion) of the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of $\mathbf{Q}_p$, and they are algebraic over **$\mathbf{Q}$**, and if $|a-1|_p<1$ and $|b-1|_p<1,$ then $(\log_p a)/(\log_p b)$ is either rational or transcendental, where $\log_p$ is the [*p*-adic logarithm function](wikipedia://en/28760028#p-adic_logarithm_function).

:::

:::

## Corollaries {#Corollaries}

The transcendence of the following numbers follows immediately from the theorem:

- [Gelfond–Schneider constant](wikipedia://en/886866) $2^{\sqrt{2}}=2.665144142\ldots$ and its square root $\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{2^{\sqrt{2}}}= 1.632526919\ldots$
- [Gelfond's constant](wikipedia://en/1984963) $e^{\pi} = \left( e^{i \pi} \right)^{-i} = (-1)^{-i} = 23.14069263 \ldots$
- $$i^i = \left( e^{\frac{i \pi}{2}} \right)^i = e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{e^{\pi}}} = 0.207879576 \ldots$$

::: {#o13 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Gelfond–Schneider theorem answers affirmatively [Hilbert's seventh problem](wikipedia://en/392579).

:::
