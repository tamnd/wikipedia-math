# Charles Ehresmann

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Ehresmann"}

**Charles Ehresmann** (19 April 1905 – 22 September 1979) was a German-born French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869).

:::

He was an early member of the [Bourbaki group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbaki_group), and is known for his work on the differential geometry of smooth [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526), notably the introduction of the concepts of [Ehresmann connection](wikipedia://en/5731754) and of [jet bundles](wikipedia://en/928060), and for his seminar on category theory.

## Life {#Life}

Ehresmann was born in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) (at the time part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire)) to an [Alsatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsatian_language)-speaking family; his father was a gardener. After [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace) returned part of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) and Ehresmann was taught in French at [Lycée Kléber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Kléber).[^o5]

Between 1924 and 1927 he studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) (ENS) in Paris and obtained [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in mathematics. After one year of military service, in 1928-29 he taught at a French school in [Rabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabat), Morocco. He studied further at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) during the years 1930–31, and at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1932–34.[^o4][^o5]

He completed his [PhD thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) entitled *Sur la topologie de certains espaces homogènes* (On the topology of certain [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325)) at ENS in 1934 under the supervision of [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274).

From 1935 to 1939 he was a researcher with the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) and he contributed to the [seminar](wikipedia://en/3862616) of [Gaston Julia](wikipedia://en/249107), which was a forerunner of the [Bourbaki seminar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbaki_seminar). In 1939 Ehresmann became a lecturer at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), but one year later the whole faculty was evacuated to [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand) due to the [German occupation of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_France). When Germany withdrew in 1945, he returned to Strasbourg.[^o4][^o5]

From 1955 he was Professor of Topology at [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), and after it split into multiple universities in 1969 he ended up at [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) (Paris 7).

Ehresmann was President of the [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France) in 1965.[^o7] He was awarded in 1940 the [Prix Francoeur](wikipedia://en/57185095) for young researchers in mathematics[^o8] and in 1967 an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Doctorate) by the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna).[^o9] He also held visiting chairs at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), in Brazil ([São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Paulo), [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro)), [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires), Mexico City, [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal), and the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research) in [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai).[^o10]

After his retirement in 1975 and until 1978 he gave lectures at the [University of Picardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Picardy_Jules_Verne) at [Amiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens), where he moved because his second wife, [Andrée Charles-Ehresmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrée_Charles-Ehresmann), was a professor of mathematics there. He died at Amiens in 1979.[^o4][^o5]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

In the first part of his career Ehresmann introduced many new mathematical objects in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [topology](wikipedia://en/8562), which gave rise to entire new fields, often developed later by his students.[^o12][^o13][^o5]

In his first works he investigated the topology and homology of manifolds associated with classical [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_groups), such as [Grassmann manifolds](wikipedia://en/373810) and other [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325).[^o14][^o15]

He developed the concept of [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526), and the related notions of [Ehresmann connection](wikipedia://en/5731754) and [solder form](wikipedia://en/9792297),[^o16][^o17][^o18][^o19] building on the works by [Herbert Seifert](wikipedia://en/637024)[^o20] and [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794)[^o21] in the 1930s. [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod) was working in the same direction from a topological point of view,[^o22] but Ehresmann, influenced by Cartan's ideas, was particularly interested in differentiable (smooth) fiber bundles, and in the differential-geometric aspects of these.[^o5] This approach led him also to the notion of [almost complex structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost-complex_structure),[^o23] which was introduced independently also by [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082).[^o24]

In order to obtain a more conceptual understanding of completely integrable systems of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), in 1944 Ehresmann inaugurated the theory of [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227),[^o25][^o26] which will be later developed by his student [Georges Reeb](wikipedia://en/20126484).[^o27] With the same perspective, he pioneered the notions of [jet](wikipedia://en/1521924) and of [Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923).[^o28][^o29][^o30][^o31]

Since the 1960s, Ehresmann's research interests moved to [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), where he introduced the concepts of [sketch](wikipedia://en/20494787), of [multiplicative graph](wikipedia://en/83649526), and of [strict 2-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict_2-category).[^o32][^o33]

His collected works, edited by his wife, appeared in seven volumes in 1980–1983 (four volumes published by Imprimerie Evrard, Amiens, and the rest in the journal *[Cahiers de Topologie et Géométrie Différentielle Catégoriques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahiers_de_Topologie_et_Géométrie_Différentielle_Catégoriques)*, which he had founded in 1957).[^o34] His publications include also the books *Catégories et structures* (Dunod, Paris, 1965) and *Algèbre* (1969).

[Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084) described Ehresmann's personality as "*... distinguished by forthrightness, simplicity, and total absence of conceit or careerism. As a teacher he was outstanding, not so much for the brilliance of his lectures as for the inspiration and tireless guidance he generously gave to his research students ...*"[^o5]

He had 76 PhD students, including [Georges Reeb](wikipedia://en/20126484), [Wu Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Wenjun) (吴文俊), [André Haefliger](wikipedia://en/20280849), [Valentin Poénaru](wikipedia://en/8527892), and Daniel Tanré.[^o6] His first student was [Jacques Feldbau](wikipedia://en/34779188).[^o35][^o36]
