# Kronecker's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kronecker's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Kronecker's theorem** is a theorem about diophantine approximation, introduced by (txt).

:::

Kronecker's approximation theorem had been firstly proved by L. Kronecker in the end of the 19th century. It has been now revealed to relate to the idea of [n-torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-torus) and [Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425) since the later half of the 20th century. In terms of physical systems, it has the consequence that planets in circular orbits moving uniformly around a star will, over time, assume all alignments, unless there is an exact dependency between their orbital periods.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

**Kronecker's theorem** is a result about [Diophantine approximations](wikipedia://en/251558) that generalizes [Dirichlet's approximation theorem](wikipedia://en/2770230) to multiple variables.

The Kronecker approximation theorem is classically formulated as follows.

*Given real*n*\-[tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) $\alpha_i=(\alpha_{i 1},\dots,\alpha_{i n})\in\mathbb{R}^n, i=1,\dots,m$ and $\beta=(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n)\in \mathbb{R}^n$ , the condition:*

$$\forall \epsilon > 0 \, \exists q_i, p_j \in \mathbb Z : \biggl| \sum^m_{i=1}q_i\alpha_{ij}-p_j-\beta_j\biggr|<\epsilon, 1\le j\le n$$

*holds if and only if for any $r_1,\dots,r_n\in\mathbb{Z},\ i=1,\dots,m$ with*

$$\sum^n_{j=1}\alpha_{ij}r_j\in\mathbb{Z}, \ \ i=1,\dots,m\ ,$$

*the number $\sum^n_{j=1}\beta_jr_j$ is also an integer.*

In plainer language, the first condition states that the tuple $\beta = (\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_n)$ can be approximated arbitrarily well by linear combinations of the $\alpha_i$s (with integer coefficients) and integer vectors.

For the case of a $m=1$ and $n=1$, Kronecker's theorem can be stated as follows.[^o5] For any $\alpha, \beta, \epsilon \in \mathbb{R}$ with $\alpha$ irrational and $\epsilon > 0$ there exist integers $p$ and $q$ with $q>0$, such that

$$|\alpha q - p - \beta| < \epsilon.$$

:::

## Relation to tori {#Relation_to_tori}

In the case of *N* numbers, taken as a single *N*-[tuple](wikipedia://en/132729) and point *P* of the [torus](wikipedia://en/74800)

*T* = *R<sup>N</sup>/Z<sup>N</sup>*,

the [closure](wikipedia://en/293802) of the subgroup \<*P*> generated by *P* will be finite, or some torus *T′* contained in *T*. The original **Kronecker's theorem** ([Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450), 1884) stated that the [necessary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition) for

*T′* = *T*,

which is that the numbers *x<sub>i</sub>* together with 1 should be [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), is also [sufficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition). Here it is easy to see that if some [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of the *x<sub>i</sub>* and 1 with non-zero rational number coefficients is zero, then the coefficients may be taken as integers, and a [character](wikipedia://en/293418) χ of the group *T* other than the [trivial character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_character) takes the value 1 on *P*. By [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) we have *T′* contained in the [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28group_theory%29) of χ, and therefore not equal to *T*.

In fact a thorough use of Pontryagin duality here shows that the whole Kronecker theorem describes the closure of \<*P*> as the intersection of the kernels of the χ with

χ(*P*) = 1.

This gives an ([antitone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antitone)) [Galois connection](wikipedia://en/156411) between [monogenic](wikipedia://en/18071162) closed subgroups of *T* (those with a single generator, in the topological sense), and sets of characters with kernel containing a given point. Not all closed subgroups occur as monogenic; in particular, the (necessarily finite) quotient of a monogenic subgroup by the connected component of the identity element must be cyclic.

The theorem leaves open the question of how well (uniformly) the multiples *mP* of *P* fill up the closure. In the one-dimensional case, the distribution is uniform by the [equidistribution theorem](wikipedia://en/2541664).
