# Concave polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Concave polygon"}

*Image: Simple polygon.svg.* An example of a concave polygon.

A [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530) that is not [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) is called **concave**,[^o2] **non-convex**[^o3] or **reentrant**.[^o4] A concave polygon will always have at least one [reflex interior angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflex_angle)—that is, an angle with a measure that is between 180° degrees and 360° degrees exclusive.[^o5]

:::

## Polygon {#Polygon}

Some lines containing interior points of a concave polygon intersect its boundary at more than two points.[^o5] Some [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251#Polygons) of a concave polygon lie partly or wholly outside the polygon.[^o5] Some [sidelines](wikipedia://en/43958459) of a concave polygon fail to divide the plane into two half-planes one of which entirely contains the polygon. None of these three statements holds for a convex polygon.

As with any simple polygon, the sum of the [internal angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) of a concave polygon is $\pi$(*n* − 2) [radians](wikipedia://en/26003), equivalently 180°(*n* − 2) degrees, where *n* is the number of sides.

It is always possible to [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) a concave polygon into a set of convex polygons. A [polynomial-time algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial-time) for finding a decomposition into as few convex polygons as possible is described by (Chazelle Dobkin 1985).[^o8]

According to Euclidean geometry, a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) can never be concave, but there exist concave polygons with *n* sides for any *n* > 3. An example of a concave [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) is the [dart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dart_%28geometry%29).

At least one interior angle does not contain all other vertices in its edges and interior.

The [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the concave polygon's vertices, and that of its edges, contains points that are exterior to the polygon.
