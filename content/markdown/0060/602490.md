# Sober space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sober space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **sober space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* such that every (nonempty) [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_space) [closed subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subset) of *X* is the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of exactly one point of *X*: that is, every nonempty irreducible closed subset has a unique [generic point](wikipedia://en/3043978).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Sober spaces have a variety of [cryptomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptomorphic) definitions, which are documented in this section.[^o4][^o5] In each case below, replacing "unique" with "at most one" gives an equivalent formulation of the [T<sub>0</sub> axiom](wikipedia://en/68121). Replacing it with "at least one" is equivalent to the property that the T<sub>0</sub> [quotient](wikipedia://en/237213) of the space is sober, which is sometimes referred to as having "enough points" in the literature.

### With irreducible closed sets {#With_irreducible_closed_sets}

A closed set is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/7556651) if it cannot be written as the union of two proper closed subsets. A space is **sober** if every nonempty irreducible closed subset is the closure of a unique point.

### In terms of morphisms of [frames and locales](wikipedia://en/650751) {#In_terms_of_morphisms_of_frames_and_locales}

A topological space *X* is sober if every map from its [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) of open subsets to $\{0, 1\}$ that preserves all [joins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_%28order_theory%29) and all finite meets is the inverse image of a unique [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) from the one-point space to *X*.

This may be viewed as a correspondence between the notion of a point in a locale and a point in a topological space, which is the motivating definition.

### Using completely prime filters {#Using_completely_prime_filters}

A [filter](wikipedia://en/47516955) *F* of open sets is said to be *completely prime* if for any family $O_i$ of open sets such that $\bigcup_i O_i \in F$, we have that $O_i \in F$ for some *i*. A space *X* is sober if each completely prime filter is the [neighbourhood filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_filter) of a unique point in *X*.

### In terms of nets {#In_terms_of_nets}

A [net](wikipedia://en/22170) $x_{\bullet}$ is *self-convergent* if it converges to every point $x_i$ in $x_{\bullet}$, or equivalently if its eventuality filter is completely prime. A net $x_{\bullet}$ that converges to $x$ *converges strongly* if it can only converge to points in the closure of $x$. A space is sober if every self-convergent net $x_{\bullet}$ converges strongly to a unique point $x$.[^o5]

In particular, a space is T<sub>1</sub> and sober precisely if every self-convergent net is constant.

### As a property of sheaves on the space {#As_a_property_of_sheaves_on_the_space}

A space *X* is sober if every [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from the category of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) *Sh*(*X*) to *Set* that preserves all [finite limits](wikipedia://en/39248) and all [small colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_colimit) must be the [stalk](wikipedia://en/15879621) functor of a unique point *x*.

:::

## Properties and examples {#Properties_and_examples}

Any [Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T2_space) (T<sub>2</sub>) space is sober (the only irreducible subsets being singletons), and all sober spaces are [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space) (T<sub>0</sub>), and both implications are strict.[^o12]

Sobriety is not [comparable](wikipedia://en/3834818) to the [T<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/148721) condition:

- an example of a T<sub>1</sub> space that is not sober is an infinite set with the [cofinite topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite_topology), the whole space being an irreducible closed subset with no generic point;
- an example of a sober space that is not T<sub>1</sub> is the [Sierpinski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_space).

Moreover, T<sub>2</sub> is strictly stronger than T<sub>1</sub> *and* sober, i.e., while every T<sub>2</sub> space is at once T<sub>1</sub> and sober, there exist spaces that are simultaneously T<sub>1</sub> and sober, but not T<sub>2</sub>. One such example is the following: let *X* be the set of real numbers, with a new point *p* adjoined; the open sets being all real open sets, and all cofinite sets containing *p*.

Sobriety of *X* is precisely a condition that forces the [lattice of open subsets](wikipedia://en/469304) of *X* to determine *X* [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660), which is relevant to [pointless topology](wikipedia://en/24764).

Sobriety makes the [specialization preorder](wikipedia://en/542347) a [directed complete partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_complete_partial_order).

Every [continuous directed complete poset](wikipedia://en/325077) equipped with the [Scott topology](wikipedia://en/743106) is sober.

Finite T<sub>0</sub> spaces are sober.[^o13]

The [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum) Spec(*R*) of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* with the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) sober space.[^o12] In fact, every [spectral space](wikipedia://en/544273) (i.e. a compact sober space for which the collection of compact open subsets is closed under finite intersections and forms a base for the topology) is homeomorphic to Spec(*R*) for some commutative ring *R*. This is a theorem of [Melvin Hochster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Hochster).[^o14] More generally, the underlying topological space of any [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) is a sober space.

The subset of Spec(*R*) consisting only of the [maximal ideals](wikipedia://en/48164), where *R* is a commutative ring, is not sober in general.
