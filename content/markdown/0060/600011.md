## Conjectures with resolved and unresolved cases {#Conjectures_with_resolved_and_unresolved_cases}

The following conjectures are treated in reliable sources as having distinct recognized cases, settings, or parameter regimes with different resolution statuses. At least one such case must have been completely resolved, either affirmatively or negatively, while at least one other remains open. Results concerning only isolated special cases, individual instances, or weaker or conditional forms do not by themselves qualify. The column "cites" lists the number of results for a [Google Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Scholar) search for the term, in double quotes .

## Refutations of conventional wisdom {#Refutations_of_conventional_wisdom}

In contemporary mathematics, ideas are supposedly not accepted absent [rigorous proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigorous_proof). Historically, some statements that were generally accepted were then shown to be false.

::: {#o197 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pythagoreans"}

- The idea of the [Pythagoreans](wikipedia://en/291170) that all numbers can be expressed as a ratio of two [whole numbers](wikipedia://en/14563). This was disproved by one of [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275)' own disciples, [Hippasus](wikipedia://en/920575), who showed that the square root of two is what we today call an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689). One story claims that he was thrown off the ship in which he and some other Pythagoreans were sailing because his discovery was too heretical.[^o198]

:::

::: {#o199 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euclid"}

- [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [parallel postulate](wikipedia://en/33731493) stated that if two lines cross a third in a [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) in such a way that the sum of the "interior angles" is not 180° then the two lines meet. Furthermore, he implicitly assumed that two separate intersecting lines meet at only one point. These assumptions were believed to be true for more than 2000 years, but in light of [General Relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Relativity) at least the second can no longer be considered true. In fact the very notion of a straight line in four-dimensional curved [space-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-time) has to be redefined, which one can do as a [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096). (But the notion of a plane does not carry over.) It is now recognized that [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) can be studied as a mathematical abstraction, but that the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe) is [non-Euclidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Euclidean).

:::

::: {#o200 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fermat"}

- [Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat) conjectured that all numbers of the form $2^{2^m}+1$ (known as [Fermat numbers](wikipedia://en/91127)) were prime. However, this conjecture was disproved by [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler), who found that $2^{2^5}+1=4,294,967,297 = 641 \times 6,700,417.$[^o201]

:::

::: {#o202 .entry cue=list-entry name="Transcendental number"}

- The idea that [transcendental numbers](wikipedia://en/30325) were unusual. Disproved by [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216) who [showed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor's_first_uncountability_proof) that there are so many transcendental numbers that it is impossible to make a one-to-one mapping between them and the [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158). In other words, the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the set of transcendentals (denoted $\beth_1$) is greater than that of the set of algebraic numbers ($\aleph_0$).[^o203]

:::

::: {#o204 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bernhard Riemann"}

- [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980), at the end of his famous 1859 paper "[On the Number of Primes Less Than a Given Magnitude](wikipedia://en/536817)", stated (based on his results) that the [logarithmic integral](wikipedia://en/47137) gives a somewhat too high estimate of the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826). The evidence also seemed to indicate this. However, in 1914 [J. E. Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Littlewood) proved that this was not always the case, and in fact it is now known that the first *x* for which $\pi(x) > \mathrm{li}(x)$ occurs somewhere before 10<sup>317</sup>. See [Skewes' number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skewes'_number) for more detail.

:::

::: {#o205 .entry cue=list-entry name="Karl Weierstrass"}

- Naïvely it might be expected that a continuous function must have a derivative or else that the set of points where it is not differentiable should be "small" in some sense. This was disproved in 1872 by [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), and in fact examples had been found earlier of functions that were nowhere differentiable (see [Weierstrass function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_function)). According to Weierstrass in his paper, earlier mathematicians including [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss) had often assumed that such functions did not exist.

:::

::: {#o206 .entry cue=list-entry name="George Pólya"}

- It was conjectured in 1919 by [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya), based on the evidence, that most numbers less than any particular limit have an odd number of [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor). However, this [Pólya conjecture](wikipedia://en/4582047) was disproved in 1958. It turns out that for some values of the limit (such as values a bit more than 906 million),[^o207][^o208] most numbers less than the limit have an even number of prime factors.

:::
