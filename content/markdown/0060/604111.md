# Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras** states that every [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a certain [field of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676). The theorem is fundamental to the deeper understanding of [Boolean algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_logic) that emerged in the first half of the 20th century. The theorem was first proved by [Marshall H. Stone](wikipedia://en/758331).[^o2] Stone was led to it by his study of the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of [operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).[^o3]

:::

## Stone spaces {#Stone_spaces}

Each [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959) *B* has an associated [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), denoted here *S*(*B*), called its **[Stone space](wikipedia://en/380219)**. The points in *S*(*B*) are the [ultrafilters](wikipedia://en/31911) on *B*, or equivalently the [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) from *B* to the [two-element Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/2584449). The topology on *S*(*B*) is generated by a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) consisting of all sets of the form

$$\{ x \in S(B) \mid b \in x\},$$

where *b* is an element of *B*. These sets are also [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) and so are [clopen](wikipedia://en/233398) (both closed and open). This is the [topology of pointwise convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_of_pointwise_convergence) of [nets](wikipedia://en/22170) of homomorphisms into the two-element Boolean algebra.

For every Boolean algebra *B*, *S*(*B*) is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637); such spaces are called **Stone spaces** (also *profinite spaces*). Conversely, given any Stone space *X*, the collection of subsets of *X* that are clopen is a Boolean algebra.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Representation theorem"}

## Representation theorem {#Representation_theorem}

A simple version of **Stone's representation theorem** states that every Boolean algebra *B* is isomorphic to the algebra of clopen subsets of its Stone space *S*(*B*). The isomorphism sends an element $b \in B$ to the set of all ultrafilters that contain *b*. This is a clopen set because of the choice of topology on *S*(*B*) and because *B* is a Boolean algebra.

Restating the theorem using the language of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869); the theorem states that there is a [duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_of_categories) between the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) of [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959) and the category of Stone spaces. This duality means that in addition to the correspondence between Boolean algebras and their Stone spaces, each [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) from a Boolean algebra *A* to a Boolean algebra *B* corresponds in a natural way to a continuous function from *S*(*B*) to *S*(*A*). In other words, there is a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) that gives an [equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_%28category_theory%29) between the categories. The inverse functor's action on morphisms maps a continuous map between Stone spaces $f : S(B_1) \to S(B_2)$ to the morphism of Boolean algebras $B_2 \to B_1$ which sends an element $x$ of $B_2$, identified to a clopen subset of $S(B_2)$, to the inverse image $f^{-1}(x)$, a clopen subset of $S(B_1)$ identified to an element of $B_1$. This was an early example of a nontrivial duality of categories.

The theorem is a special case of [Stone duality](wikipedia://en/379619), a more general framework for dualities between [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) and [partially ordered sets](wikipedia://en/23572).

The proof requires either the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) or a weakened form of it. Specifically, the theorem is equivalent to the [Boolean prime ideal theorem](wikipedia://en/314919), a weakened choice principle that states that every Boolean algebra has a prime ideal.

An extension of the classical Stone duality to the category of Boolean spaces (that is, [zero-dimensional](wikipedia://en/3173612) [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) Hausdorff spaces) and continuous maps (respectively, perfect maps) was obtained by G. D. Dimov (respectively, by H. P. Doctor).[^o7][^o8]
