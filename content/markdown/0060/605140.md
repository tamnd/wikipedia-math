# Robert Daniel Carmichael

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Daniel Carmichael"}

**Robert Daniel Carmichael** (March 1, 1879 – May 2, 1967) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Carmichael was born in [Goodwater, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwater,_Alabama). He attended [Lineville College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineville_College) in [Clay County, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_County,_Alabama), briefly, and he earned his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in 1898, while he was studying towards his [Ph.D. degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D._degree) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). Carmichael completed the requirements for his Ph.D. in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1911. Carmichael's Ph.D. research in mathematics was done under the guidance of the noted American mathematician [G. David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906), and it is considered to be the first significant American contribution to the knowledge of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) in mathematics.

Carmichael next taught at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington) from 1911 to 1915. Then he moved on to the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign), where he remained from 1915 until his retirement in 1947.

Carmichael is known for his research in what are now called the [Carmichael numbers](wikipedia://en/7723) (a subset of [Fermat pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/45891), numbers satisfying properties of primes described by [Fermat's Little Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat's_Little_Theorem) although they are not primes), [Carmichael's totient function conjecture](wikipedia://en/19103379), [Carmichael's theorem](wikipedia://en/932711), and the [Carmichael function](wikipedia://en/1181756), all significant in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and in the study of the [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). He found the smallest Carmichael number, 561, and over 50 years later, it was proven that there are infinitely many of them. Carmichael also described the [Steiner system](wikipedia://en/28016) S(5,8,24) in his 1931 paper *Tactical Configurations of Rank 2* and his 1937 book *Introduction to the [Theory of Groups](wikipedia://en/41890) of Finite Order*, but the structure is often named after [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911), who rediscovered it in 1938.

While at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington), Carmichael was involved with the [special theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_theory_of_relativity).[^o4]

## Relatives {#Relatives}

Carmichael's younger brother was university administrator [Oliver Carmichael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Carmichael).[^o6]
