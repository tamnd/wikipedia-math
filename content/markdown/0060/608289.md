# Eduard Heine

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eduard Heine"}

**Heinrich Eduard Heine** (16 March 1821 – 21 October 1881) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Heine became known for results on [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function) and in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478). In particular, he authored an important treatise on [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056) and [Legendre functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendre_functions) (*Handbuch der Kugelfunctionen*). He also investigated [basic hypergeometric series](wikipedia://en/2233526). He introduced the [Mehler–Heine formula](wikipedia://en/32798823).

## Biography {#Biography}

Heinrich Eduard Heine was born on 16 March 1821 in Berlin, as the eighth child of banker Karl Heine and his wife Henriette Märtens. Eduard was initially [home schooled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_schooled), then studied at the Friedrichswerdersche [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) and Köllnische Gymnasium in Berlin.[^o4] In 1838, after graduating from gymnasium, he enrolled at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin), but transferred to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) to attend the mathematics lectures of [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) and [Moritz Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Stern). In 1840 Heine returned to Berlin, where he studied mathematics under [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002), while also attending classes of [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) and [Johann Franz Encke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Franz_Encke). In 1842 he was awarded a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) by the University of Berlin for a thesis on [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) submitted with [Enno Dirksen](wikipedia://en/62908345) and [Martin Ohm](wikipedia://en/4336770) as advisors. Heine dedicated the doctoral thesis to his professor Gustav Dirichlet. Next he went to the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) to participate in the mathematical seminar of [Carl Gustav Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340), while also following [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) classes of [Franz Ernst Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Ernst_Neumann). In [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) Heine got in contact with fellow students [Gustav Kirchhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Kirchhoff) and [Philipp Ludwig von Seidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Ludwig_von_Seidel).[^o5]

In 1844 Heine went for a teaching position at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), passing his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) and starting as a [privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent). He continued his research in mathematics in Bonn and, in 1848, was promoted to [extraordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_professor). In 1850 he married Sophie Wolff, the daughter of a Berlin merchant; the couple had five children, four daughters and one son. In 1856 Heine moved as a full professor to the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle), where he remained for the rest of his life. From 1864 to 1865, he served as a rector of the university. In 1875, the University of Göttingen offered Heine a mathematics chair but he decided to reject the offer and remain in Halle. In 1877, at the centenary of Gauss's birth, he was awarded the Gauss Medal for his research.[^o5] Eduard Heine died on 21 October 1881 in Halle.[^o5]
