# Thierry Coquand

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thierry Coquand"}

**Thierry Coquand** (; born 18 April 1961) is a French computer scientist and mathematician who is since 1996 a professor of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at the [University of Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Gothenburg),[^o3] having formerly worked at [INRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIA). He is known for his work in [constructive mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_mathematics), especially the [calculus of constructions](wikipedia://en/613557).

:::

He received his Ph.D. under the supervision of [Gérard Huet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_Huet), another academic who has experience in both mathematics and computer science. According to the [ACM Digital Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery#Portal_and_Digital_Library), his first published article was a 1985 collaboration with Huet titled "Constructions: A Higher Order Proof System for Mechanizing Mathematics".[^o4] Coquand and Huet published another joint article in September of that year which further expanded on their ideas regarding constructive mathematics.[^o5] In the following year, 1986, Coquand published a noteworthy paper about [Girard's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girard's_paradox) in the System U logic system.[^o6] Since then, Coquand has written a wide variety of papers in both French and English.

In addition to his contributions to theoretical computer science, Coquand is also known as the co-creator of the [Rocq](wikipedia://en/581974) (formerly named *Coq*, the name is a reference partly to Coquand's surname) [proof assistant](wikipedia://en/1258607), which he began developing in 1984 while working at INRIA (a French national research institute for computer science and mathematics), and which was released officially in 1989.[^o7] Coq won the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) (ACM) [SIGPLAN Programming Languages Software Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGPLAN_Programming_Languages_Software_Award) in 2013, for "provid\[ing\] a rich environment for interactive development of machine-checked formal reasoning".[^o8][^o9] Rocq has been used to provide novel solutions for mathematical problems, especially for those that have a [non-surveyable proof](wikipedia://en/48740815), such as the [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949). It has also been used in software development, such as with the [CompCert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CompCert) [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) compiler.[^o10]

Coquand often gives talks about the subjects that he specializes in, such as his description of the work of University of Nottingham professor [Thorsten Altenkirch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorsten_Altenkirch).[^o11]
