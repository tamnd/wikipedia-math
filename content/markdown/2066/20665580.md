# Hesse normal form

*Image: Hesse normalenform.svg.* Distance from the origin O to the line E calculated with the Hesse normal form. Normal vector in red, line in green, point O shown in blue.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hesse normal form"}

In [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202), the **Hesse normal form** (named after [Otto Hesse](wikipedia://en/740995)) is an equation used to describe a [line](wikipedia://en/946975) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) $\mathbb{R}^2$, a plane in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^3$, or a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) in [higher dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_dimension).[^o2][^o3] It is primarily used for calculating distances (see [point-plane distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-plane_distance) and [point-line distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-line_distance)).

:::

It is written in vector notation as

$$\vec r \cdot \vec n_0 - d = 0.\,$$

The dot $\cdot$ indicates the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) (or scalar product). Vector $\vec r$ points from the origin of the coordinate system, *O*, to any point *P* that lies precisely in plane or on line *E*. The vector $\vec n_0$ represents the [unit](wikipedia://en/167053) [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) of plane or line *E*. The distance $d \ge 0$ is the shortest distance from the origin *O* to the plane or line.

## Derivation/Calculation from the normal form {#Derivation/Calculation_from_the_normal_form}

Note: For simplicity, the following derivation discusses the 3D case. However, it is also applicable in 2D.

In the normal form,

$$(\vec r -\vec a)\cdot \vec n = 0\,$$

a plane is given by a normal vector $\vec n$ as well as an arbitrary position vector $\vec a$ of a point $A \in E$. The direction of $\vec n$ is chosen to satisfy the following inequality

$$\vec a\cdot \vec n \geq 0\,$$

By dividing the normal vector $\vec n$ by its [magnitude](wikipedia://en/32533#Length) $| \vec n |$, we obtain the unit (or normalized) normal vector

$$\vec n_0 = {{\vec n} \over {| \vec n |}}\,$$

and the above equation can be rewritten as

$$(\vec r -\vec a)\cdot \vec n_0 = 0.\,$$

Substituting

$$d = \vec a\cdot \vec n_0 \geq 0\,$$

we obtain the Hesse normal form

$$\vec r \cdot \vec n_0 - d = 0.\,$$

*Image: Ebene Hessesche Normalform.PNG.*

In this diagram, *d* is the distance from the origin. Because $\vec r \cdot \vec n_0 = d$ holds for every point in the plane, it is also true at point *Q* (the point where the vector from the origin meets the plane E), with $\vec r = \vec r_s$, per the definition of the [Scalar product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_product)

$$d = \vec r_s \cdot \vec n_0 = |\vec r_s| \cdot |\vec n_0| \cdot \cos(0^\circ) = |\vec r_s| \cdot 1 = |\vec r_s|.\,$$

The magnitude $|\vec r_s|$ of ${\vec r_s}$ is the shortest distance from the origin to the plane.

## Distance to a line {#Distance_to_a_line}

The [Quadrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrance) (distance squared) from a line $ax + by + c = 0$ to a point $(x, y)$ is

$$\frac{(ax+by+c)^2}{a^2 + b^2}.$$

If $(a, b)$ has unit length then this becomes $(ax+by+c)^2.$
