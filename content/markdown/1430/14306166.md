::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biplot"}

**Biplots** are a type of exploratory graph used in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a generalization of the simple two-variable [scatterplot](wikipedia://en/412544). A biplot overlays a *score plot* with a *loading plot*. A biplot allows information on both [samples](wikipedia://en/160361) and variables of a [data matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_matrix_%28statistics%29) to be displayed graphically. Samples are displayed as points while variables are displayed either as vectors, linear [axes](wikipedia://en/7706) or nonlinear trajectories. In the case of categorical variables, *category level points* may be used to represent the levels of a categorical variable. A *generalised* biplot displays information on both continuous and categorical variables.

:::

The biplot was introduced by [K. Ruben Gabriel](wikipedia://en/43203588) (1971).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

A biplot is constructed by using the [singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207) (SVD) to obtain a [low-rank approximation](wikipedia://en/34327576) to a transformed version of the data matrix **X**, whose *n* rows are the samples (also called the cases, or objects), and whose *p* columns are the variables. The transformed data matrix **Y** is obtained from the original matrix **X** by centering and optionally standardizing the columns (the variables). Using the SVD, we can write **Y** = Σ<sub>*k*=1,...*p*</sub>*d*<sub>*k*</sub>**u**<sub>*k*</sub>**v**<sub>*k*</sub><sup>T</sup>;, where the **u**<sub>*k*</sub> are *n*-dimensional column vectors, the **v**<sub>*k*</sub> are *p*-dimensional column vectors, and the *d*<sub>*k*</sub> are a non-increasing sequence of non-negative [scalars](wikipedia://en/3588331). The biplot is formed from two scatterplots that share a common set of axes and have a between-set [scalar product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_product) interpretation. The first scatterplot is formed from the points (*d*<sub>1</sub><sup>α</sup>*u*<sub>1*i*</sub>, *d*<sub>2</sub><sup>α</sup>*u*<sub>2*i*</sub>), for *i* = 1,...,*n*. The second plot is formed from the points (*d*<sub>1</sub><sup>1−α</sup>*v*<sub>1*j*</sub>, *d*<sub>2</sub><sup>1−α</sup>*v*<sub>2*j*</sub>), for *j* = 1,...,*p*. This is the biplot formed by the dominant two terms of the SVD, which can then be represented in a two-dimensional display. Typical choices of α are 1 (to give a distance interpretation to the row display) and 0 (to give a distance interpretation to the column display), and in some rare cases α=1/2 to obtain a symmetrically scaled biplot (which gives no distance interpretation to the rows or the columns, but only the scalar product interpretation). The set of points depicting the variables can be drawn as arrows from the origin to reinforce the idea that they represent biplot axes onto which the samples can be projected to approximate the original data.

:::
