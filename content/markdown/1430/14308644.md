# Vertex arrangement

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vertex arrangement"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **vertex arrangement** is a set of [points](wikipedia://en/593693) in space described by their relative positions. They can be described by their use in [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471).

:::

For example, a *[square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) vertex arrangement* is understood to mean four points in a plane, equal distance and angles from a center point.

Two polytopes share the same *vertex arrangement* if they share the same [0-skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910).

A group of polytopes that shares a vertex arrangement is called an *army*.

## Vertex arrangement {#Vertex_arrangement}

The same set of vertices can be connected by edges in different ways. For example, the *pentagon* and *pentagram* have the same *vertex arrangement*, while the second connects alternate vertices.

## Edge arrangement {#Edge_arrangement}

[Polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) can also share an *edge arrangement* while differing in their faces.

For example, the self-intersecting *great dodecahedron* shares its edge arrangement with the convex *icosahedron*:

A group polytopes that share both a *vertex arrangement* and an *edge arrangement* are called a *regiment*.

## Face arrangement {#Face_arrangement}

[4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychoron) can also have the same *face arrangement* which means they have similar vertex, edge, and face arrangements, but may differ in their cells.

For example, of the ten nonconvex regular [Schläfli-Hess polychora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli-Hess_polychoron), there are only 7 unique face arrangements.

For example, the [grand stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604676) and [great stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604610), both with [pentagrammic](wikipedia://en/197544) faces, appear visually indistinguishable without a representation of their [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29):

## Classes of similar polytopes {#Classes_of_similar_polytopes}

George Olshevsky advocates the term *regiment* for a set of polytopes that share an edge arrangement, and more generally *n-regiment* for a set of polytopes that share elements up to dimension *n*. Synonyms for special cases include *company* for a 2-regiment (sharing faces) and *army* for a 0-regiment (sharing vertices).
