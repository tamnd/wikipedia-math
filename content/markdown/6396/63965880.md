# Zero dynamics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zero dynamics"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), **zero dynamics** describes the internal behavior of a dynamic system when its outputs are constrained to [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero) through control inputs (e.g., maintaining a robot arm at a fixed position while internal states continue to evolve).[^o2] Even when outputs are held at zero, the system's internal dynamics may persist, influencing stability and performance.

:::

Understanding zero dynamics helps design robust controllers in applications like [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics), [feedback linearization](wikipedia://en/5708736), and [control engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_engineering).

## History {#History}

The idea was introduced thirty years ago as the [nonlinear](wikipedia://en/21723) approach to the concept of transmission of zeros. The original purpose of introducing the concept was to develop an [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/3107) stabilization with a set of guaranteed regions of attraction ([semi-global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-global_matching) [stabilizability](wikipedia://en/100638)), to make the overall system stable.[^o4]

## Initial working {#Initial_working}

Given the internal dynamics of any system, zero dynamics refers to the control action chosen in which the output variables of the system are kept identically zero.[^o6] While various systems have an equally distinctive set of zeros, such as decoupling zeros, invariant zeros, and transmission zeros, the reason for developing this concept was to control the non-minimum phase and nonlinear systems effectively.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The concept is widely utilized in SISO mechanical systems, whereby applying a few [heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic) approaches, zeros can be identified for various linear systems.[^o10] Zero dynamics adds an essential feature to the overall system’s analysis and the design of the controllers. Mainly its behavior plays a significant role in measuring the performance limitations of specific feedback systems. In a [Single Input Single Output system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-input_single-output_system), the zero dynamics can be identified by using junction structure patterns. In other words, using concepts like [bond graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_graph) models can help to point out the potential direction of the SISO systems.[^o11]

Apart from its application in nonlinear standardized systems, similar controlled results can be obtained by using zero dynamics on nonlinear discrete-time systems. In this scenario, the application of zero dynamics can be an interesting tool to measure the performance of nonlinear digital design systems (nonlinear discrete-time systems).[^o12]

Before the advent of zero dynamics, the problem of acquiring non-interacting control systems by using internal stability was not specifically discussed. However, with the [asymptotic stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_stability) present within the zero dynamics of a system, static feedback can be ensured. Such results make zero dynamics an interesting tool to guarantee the internal stability of non-interacting control systems.[^o13]
