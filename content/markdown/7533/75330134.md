# Griffiths' theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Griffiths' theorem"}

*Image: Pedal circle griffith point2 schar.svg.* Griffiths' theorem: every red circle is a pedal circle of a blue point on the line through the circumcenter *O*, and *G* is the Griffiths point

**Griffiths' theorem**, named after [John Griffiths](wikipedia://en/18543566) (1837-1916), is a theorem in [elementary geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_geometry). It states that all the [pedal circles](wikipedia://en/75329877) for a points located on a line through the center of the triangle's [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) share a common (fixed) point. Such a point defined for a triangle and a line through its circumcenter is called a [Griffiths point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffiths_point).

:::

Griffiths published the theorem in the *Educational Times* in 1857. Its later rediscoveries include works by M. Weil in *Nouvelles Annales de Mathématiques*, 1880, and by W. S. McCay in *Transactions of the Royal Irish Academy*, 1889.[^o3][^o4] Additionally, in 1906, Georges Fontené refound the theorem.[^o5] So the theorem is also called the Fontené's (Second) theorem.
