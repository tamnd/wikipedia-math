# Wall's finiteness obstruction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wall's finiteness obstruction"}

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), a field within mathematics, the [obstruction](wikipedia://en/2179639) to a finitely dominated space *X* being [homotopy-equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) to a finite [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex) is its **Wall finiteness obstruction** *w(X)* which is an element in the reduced zeroth [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) $\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1(X)])$ of the integral [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) $\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1(X)]$. It is named after the mathematician [C. T. C. Wall](wikipedia://en/2613478).

:::

By work of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632)[^o2] on finitely dominated spaces, no generality is lost in letting *X* be a CW-complex. A *finite domination* of *X* is a finite CW-complex *K* together with maps $r:K\to X$ and $i\colon X\to K$ such that $r\circ i\simeq 1_X$. By a construction due to Milnor it is possible to extend *r* to a homotopy equivalence $\bar{r}\colon \bar{K}\to X$ where $\bar{K}$ is a CW-complex obtained from *K* by attaching cells to kill the relative homotopy groups $\pi_n(r)$.

The space $\bar{K}$ will be *finite* if all relative homotopy groups are finitely generated. Wall showed that this will be the case if and only if his finiteness obstruction vanishes. More precisely, using covering space theory and the [Hurewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/591587) one can identify $\pi_n(r)$ with $H_n(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{K})$. Wall then showed that the cellular chain complex $C_*(\widetilde{X})$ is chain-homotopy equivalent to a chain complex $A_*$ of finite type of [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) $\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1(X)]$-modules, and that $H_n(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{K})\cong H_n(A_*)$ will be finitely generated if and only if these modules are [stably-free](wikipedia://en/19745945). Stably-free modules vanish in reduced K-theory. This motivates the definition

$$w(X)=\sum_i(-1)^i[A_i]\in\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1(X)]).$$
