# Marc Krasner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marc Krasner"}

**Marc Krasner** (1912 – 13 May 1985, in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989)) was a Russian-born French mathematician, who worked on [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Krasner emigrated from the Soviet Union to France and received in 1935 his PhD from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) under [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) with thesis *Sur la théorie de la ramification des idéaux de corps non-galoisiens de nombres algébriques*. From 1937 to 1960 he was a scientist at [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) and from 1960 professor at the [University of Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Clermont-Ferrand). From 1965 he was a professor at the [University of Paris VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VI) (Pierre et Marie Curie), where he retired in 1980 as professor emeritus.

Krasner did research on [p-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327). In 1944 he introduced the concept of [ultrametric spaces](wikipedia://en/333996),[^o4] to which p-adic numbers belong. In 1951, alongside [Lev Kaluznin](wikipedia://en/59686732), he proved the [Krasner-Kaloujnine universal embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/61229758), which states that every extension of one group by another is isomorphic to a subgroup of the wreath product. A well-known Krasner's theorem, everywhere known as [Krasner's lemma](wikipedia://en/23007058), relies the topological structure and the algebraic structure of vector spaces over local fields.

In 1958 he received the [Prix Paul Doistau–Émile Blutet](wikipedia://en/63735602) of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences).
