# Matroid rank

*Image: Free matroid.svg.* The [graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714) of a [forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_%28graph_theory%29) with 4 edges, which is the [free matroid](wikipedia://en/65782984) with a ground set of size 4 (also the [uniform matroid](wikipedia://en/27967069) $U{}^4_4$). The **rank** of the given matroid is 4, the size of its largest independent set. As this matroid is a member of the graphic, free, and uniform matroid families, **rank functions** can be used to calculate the rank of the matroid, specifically $r(B) = |B|$ as a free matroid, $r(B) = \min(k, |B|)$ as a uniform matroid, and the number of vertices in the graph, minus the number of [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28graph_theory%29) of $B$ (including single-vertex components), as a graphic matroid.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matroid rank"}

In the mathematical theory of [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321), the **rank** of a matroid is the maximum size of an independent set in the matroid. The rank of a subset *S* of elements of the matroid is, similarly, the maximum size of an independent subset of *S*, and the **rank function** of the matroid maps sets of elements to their ranks.

:::

The rank function is one of the fundamental concepts of matroid theory via which matroids may be axiomatized. **Matroid rank functions** form an important subclass of the [submodular set functions](wikipedia://en/33273315). The rank functions of matroids defined from certain other types of [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961) such as [undirected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph), [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859), and [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634) are important within the study of those objects.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In all examples, *E* is the base set of the matroid, and *B* is some subset of *E*.

- Let *M* be the [free matroid](wikipedia://en/65782984), where the independent sets are all subsets of *E*. Then the rank function of *M* is simply: *r*(*B*) = |*B*|.
- Let *M* be a [uniform matroid](wikipedia://en/27967069), where the independent sets are the subsets of *E* with at most *k* elements, for some integer *k*. Then the rank function of *M* is: *r*(*B*) = min(*k*, |*B*|).
- Let *M* be a [partition matroid](wikipedia://en/36780298): the elements of *E* are partitioned into categories, each category *c* has capacity *k<sub>c</sub>*, and the independent sets are those containing at most *k<sub>c</sub>* elements of category *c*. Then the rank function of *M* is: *r*(*B*) = sum<sub>*c*</sub> min(*k<sub>c</sub>*, |*B<sub>c</sub>*|) where *B<sub>c</sub>* is the subset *B* contained in category *c*.
- Let *M* be a [graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714), where the independent sets are all the acyclic edge-sets ([forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_%28graph_theory%29)) of some fixed [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) *G*. Then the rank function *r*(*B*) is the number of vertices in the graph, minus the number of [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28graph_theory%29) of *B* (including single-vertex components).

:::

## Properties and axiomatization {#Properties_and_axiomatization}

The rank function of a matroid obeys the following properties.

(R1) The value of the rank function is always a non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) and the rank of the empty set is 0.

(R2) For any two subsets $A$ and $B$ of $E$, $r(A\cup B)+r(A\cap B)\le r(A)+r(B)$. That is, the rank is a [submodular set function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodular_function).

(R3) For any set $A$ and element $x$, $r(A)\le r(A\cup\{x\})\le r(A)+1$.

These properties may be used as axioms to characterize the rank function of matroids: every integer-valued submodular set function on the subsets of a finite set that obeys the inequalities $r(A)\le r(A\cup\{x\})\le r(A)+1$ for all $A$ and $x$ is the rank function of a matroid.[^o5][^o6]

The above properties imply additional properties:

- If $A\subset B\subset E$, then $r(A)\leq r(B)\leq r(E)$. That is, the rank is a [monotonic function](wikipedia://en/48260).
- $$r(A)\leq |A|.$$

## Other matroid properties from rank {#Other_matroid_properties_from_rank}

The rank function may be used to determine the other important properties of a matroid:

- A set is independent [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its rank equals its cardinality, and dependent if and only if it has greater cardinality than rank.[^o8]
- A nonempty set is a circuit if its cardinality equals one plus its rank and every subset formed by removing one element from the set has equal rank.[^o8]
- A set is a basis if its rank equals both its cardinality and the rank of the matroid.[^o8]
- A set is closed if it is [maximal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) for its rank, in the sense that there does not exist another element that can be added to it while maintaining the same rank.
- The difference $|A|-r(A)$ is called the **nullity** of the subset $A$. It is the minimum number of elements that must be removed from $A$ to obtain an independent set.[^o9]
- The **corank** of a subset $A$ can refer to at least two different quantities: some authors use it to refer to the rank of $A$ in the [dual matroid](wikipedia://en/23515837), $r^*(A) = |A| + r(E \setminus A) - r(E)$, while other authors use corank to refer to the difference $r(E)- r(A)$.

## Ranks of special matroids {#Ranks_of_special_matroids}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the [cyclomatic number](wikipedia://en/1483049) of a graph is the corank of the associated [graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714); it measures the minimum number of edges that must be removed from the graph to make the remaining edges form a forest.[^o11] Several authors have studied the [parameterized complexity](wikipedia://en/603026) of graph algorithms parameterized by this number.[^o12][^o13]

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), the rank of a [linear matroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_matroid) defined by [linear independence](wikipedia://en/101863) from the columns of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) is the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of the matrix,[^o14] and it is also the [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) of the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) spanned by the columns.

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the rank of a matroid defined from sets of elements in a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *L*/*K* by [algebraic independence](wikipedia://en/287808) is known as the [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree).[^o15]

## Matroid rank functions as utility functions {#Matroid_rank_functions_as_utility_functions}

Matroid rank functions (MRF) has been used to represent utility functions of agents in problems of [fair item allocation](wikipedia://en/46974282). If the utility function of the agent is an MRF, it means that:

- The agent's utility has [diminishing returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishing_returns) (this follows from the fact that the MRF is a submodular function);
- The agent's [marginal utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_utility) for each item is dichotomous (binary) - either 0 or 1. That is, adding an item to a bundle either adds no utility, or adds a utility of 1.

The following solutions are known for this setting:

- **Babaioff, Ezra and Feige[^o17]** design a deterministic polynomial-time [truthful mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truthful_mechanism) called Prioritized Egalitarian, that outputs a Lorenz dominating allocation, which is consequently also EFX<sub>0</sub>, maximizes the product of utilities, attains 1/2-fraction [maximin share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximin_share), and attains the full maximin share when the valuations are additive. With random priorities, this mechanism is also ex-ante [envy-free](wikipedia://en/51991437). They also study *e*-dichotomous valuations, in which the marginal utility is either non-positive or in the range \[1,1+*e*\].
- **Benabbou, Chakraborty, Igarashi and Zick[^o18]** show that, in this setting, every [Pareto-optimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto-optimal) allocation maximizes the sum of utilities (the *utilitarian welfare*), the set of allocations that maximize a symmetric strictly-[concave function](wikipedia://en/263321) *f* over all max-sum allocations does not depend on the choice of *f*, and all these *f*-maximizing allocations are EF1. This implies that the max-product allocations are the [leximin-optimal](wikipedia://en/65470806) allocations, and they are all max-sum and EF1. They also present a polynomial-time algorithm that computes a max-sum and EF1 allocation (which does not necessarily maximize a concave function), and a polynomial-time algorithm that maximizes a concave function for the special case of MRFs based on [maximum-cardinality matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_cardinality_matching) in bipartite graphs.

The matroid-rank functions are a subclass of the [gross substitute valuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_substitutes_%28indivisible_items%29).
