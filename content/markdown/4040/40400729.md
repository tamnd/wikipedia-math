# Limiting case (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Limiting case"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **limiting case** of a [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961) is a [special case](wikipedia://en/31308010) that arises when one or more components of the object take on their most extreme possible values.[^o2] For example:

:::

- In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the limiting case of the [binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/3876) is the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144). As the number of events tends to infinity in the binomial distribution, the random variable changes from the binomial to the Poisson distribution.
- A [circle is a limiting case](wikipedia://en/6220#Limiting_case_of_other_figures) of various other figures, including the [Cartesian oval](wikipedia://en/29131481), the [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), the [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211), and the [Cassini oval](wikipedia://en/2040906). Each type of figure is a circle for certain values of the defining parameters, and the generic figure appears more like a circle as the limiting values are approached.
- [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) calculated an approximate value of [π](wikipedia://en/23601) by treating the circle as the limiting case of a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) with 3 × 2<sup>*n*</sup> sides, as *n* gets large.
- In [electricity and magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_and_magnetism), the [long wavelength limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_wavelength_limit) is the limiting case when the [wavelength](wikipedia://en/33125) is much larger than the system size.
- In [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), two limiting cases of a [demand curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand_curve) or [supply curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_curve) are those in which the [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28economics%29) is zero (the totally inelastic case) or infinity (the infinitely elastic case).
- In [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [continuous compounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_compounding) is the limiting case of compound interest in which the compounding period becomes infinitesimally small, achieved by taking the limit as the number of compounding periods per year goes to infinity.

A limiting case is sometimes a [degenerate case](wikipedia://en/232952) in which some qualitative properties differ from the corresponding [properties of the generic case](wikipedia://en/12886758). For example:

- A [point](wikipedia://en/593693) is a degenerate [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), whose [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) is zero.
- A [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) can degenerate into two distinct or coinciding [parallel lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_lines).
- An [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) can degenerate into a single point or a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860).
- A [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) can degenerate into two [intersecting lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersecting_lines).
