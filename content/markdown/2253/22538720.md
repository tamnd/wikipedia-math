# Luca Valerio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luca Valerio"}

**Luca Valerio** (1553 – 16 January 1618) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He developed ways to find [volumes](wikipedia://en/32498) and [centers of gravity](wikipedia://en/173961) of solid [bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) using the methods of [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844). He corresponded with [Galileo Galilei](wikipedia://en/29688374) and was a member of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Luca Valerio was born in [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples) in 1553[^o3] He entered in the [Jesuit order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) in 1570. He studied [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) at the [Roman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_College) as a student of [Christopher Clavius](wikipedia://en/633645), and left the Jesuits in 1580. He later taught [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447) and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) at the [Collegio Pontifico Greco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Colleges#Collegio_Pontifico_Greco) and mathematics and ethics at the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome). In 1611 Valerio obtained a position in the [Vatican Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_Library) in addition to his post at Sapienza giving him close connection with the top people in the [Roman Catholic church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_church).

### Galileo and Copernicus {#Galileo_and_Copernicus}

Valerio met Galileo on a visit to [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) in 1584. He corresponded with Galileo from 1609 until 1616 and in 1612 he became a member of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei), a group which also included Galileo as a member. On 5 March 1616 [Cardinal Robert Bellarmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Robert_Bellarmine), chief theologian of the Roman Catholic Church, issued a [decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree) that the idea of a Sun centred [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System), the [Copernican system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus#Copernican_system), a theory supported by Galileo, was *false and erroneous*.[^o5] The prospect of being called before the [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquisition) caused Valerio to end all correspondence with Galileo and resign from the *Accademia dei Lincei*. The members of the Academy looked on Valerio's actions as aligning himself with Galileo's opponents and accusing the Academy itself of committing a crime.[^o6] His resignation was rejected by the Accademia, but they took away his right to participate in Accademia meetings. [Federico Cesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Cesi), the Accademia dei Lincei's founder, still hoped however that Valerio could re-enter the ranks of the academics, but the mathematician died in January 1618.

Among the mathematicians who studied him and spoke highly of him were [Cavalieri](wikipedia://en/412561), [Torricelli](wikipedia://en/210413) and [J.-C. de la Faille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Charles_della_Faille). He also had a direct influence on [Guldin](wikipedia://en/233695), [Gregorius Saint Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grégoire_de_Saint-Vincent), and [Tacquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Tacquet).[^o7]
