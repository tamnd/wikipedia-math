# John Wrench

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Wrench"}

**John William Wrench, Jr.** (October 13, 1911 – February 27, 2009) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked primarily in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). He was a pioneer in using [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) for mathematical calculations, and is noted for work done with [Daniel Shanks](wikipedia://en/18171362) to calculate the [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) to 100,000 decimal places.

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Wrench was born on October 13, 1911, in [Westfield, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield,_New_York), and grew up in [Hamburg, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg,_New_York). He received a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1933 and an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1935, both from the [University at Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics in 1938 from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).[^o4] His thesis was titled *The derivation of arctangent relations*.[^o5]

Wrench died on February 27, 2009, of [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia) in [Frederick, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick,_Maryland).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Wrench started his career teaching at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University), but switched to doing research for the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). His specialty for the Navy was developing high-speed computational methods, and he was a pioneer in using computers for mathematical calculations. He worked on projects involving [underwater sound waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_acoustics), [underwater explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_explosion), [structural design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_design), [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics), [aerodynamics](wikipedia://en/2819), and [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954). He became deputy head of the Applied Mathematics Laboratory at the Navy's [David Taylor Model Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Taylor_Model_Basin) in 1953, and retired in 1974 as the head of the laboratory. He also had academic appointments at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University), [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), and [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University).[^o6]

Wrench had a particular interest in computing the decimal digits of [π](wikipedia://en/23601), and performed some lengthy calculations even before the availability of computers.[^o6] During the period 1945–1956 Wrench and Levi B. Smith used a [desk calculator](wikipedia://en/432897) to produce more and more digits of π, ending with 1160 places.[^o8] In 1961, Wrench and [Daniel Shanks](wikipedia://en/18171362) used an [IBM 7090](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_7090) computer to calculate π to 100,000 digits.[^o9] Harry Polachek had a printout of the 100,000 digits specially bound, inscribed in gold letters, and donated to the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution).[^o10]

Wrench also calculated a number of other [mathematical constants](wikipedia://en/48779551) to high precision, for example the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant) γ to 328 decimal places[^o11] and [Khinchin's constant](wikipedia://en/435315) to 65 places.[^o12]

He was the editor of the *Journal of Mathematics of Computation* from 1959 to 1978.[^o13] Wrench was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council). He published more than 150 scientific papers.
