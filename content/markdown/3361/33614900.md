# Mixed Poisson process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mixed Poisson process"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **mixed Poisson process** is a special [point process](wikipedia://en/2147685) that is a generalization of a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process). Mixed Poisson processes are simple example for [Cox processes](wikipedia://en/6196078).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mu$ be a [locally finite measure](wikipedia://en/6621919) on $S$ and let $X$ be a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with $X \geq 0$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908).

Then a [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846) $\xi$ on $S$ is called a mixed Poisson process based on $\mu$ and $X$ iff $\xi$ conditionally on $X=x$ is a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) on $S$ with [intensity measure](wikipedia://en/56214154) $x\mu$.

:::

## Comment {#Comment}

Mixed Poisson processes are doubly stochastic in the sense that in a first step, the value of the random variable $X$ is determined. This value then determines the "second order stochasticity" by increasing or decreasing the original intensity measure $\mu$.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Conditional on $X=x$ mixed Poisson processes have the [intensity measure](wikipedia://en/56214154) $x \mu$ and the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610)

$$\mathcal L(f)=\exp \left(- \int 1-\exp(-f(y))\; (x \mu)(\mathrm dy)\right).$$

:::
