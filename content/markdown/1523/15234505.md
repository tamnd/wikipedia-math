# Wallman compactification

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wallman compactification"}

In mathematics, the **Wallman compactification**, generally called **Wallman–Shanin compactification** is a [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) of [T<sub>1</sub> topological spaces](wikipedia://en/148721) that was constructed by (Wallman 1938).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The points of the Wallman compactification ω*X* of a space *X* are the maximal proper [filters](wikipedia://en/19719) in the [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) of closed subsets of *X*. Explicitly, a point of ω*X* is a family $\mathcal F$ of closed nonempty subsets of *X* such that $\mathcal F$ is closed under finite intersections, and is maximal among those families that have these properties. For every closed subset *F* of *X*, the class Φ<sub>*F*</sub> of points of ω*X* containing *F* is closed in ω*X*. The topology of ω*X* is generated by these closed classes.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

For [normal spaces](wikipedia://en/48629), the Wallman compactification is essentially the same as the [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897).

:::
