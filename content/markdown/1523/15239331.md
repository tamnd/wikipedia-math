# Arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more precisely in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), **Arithmetic Kleinian groups** are a special class of [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652) constructed using [orders](wikipedia://en/1114171) in [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890). They are particular instances of [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741). An **arithmetic hyperbolic three-manifold** is the quotient of [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) $\mathbb H^3$ by an arithmetic Kleinian group.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and examples"}

## Definition and examples {#Definition_and_examples}

### Quaternion algebras {#Quaternion_algebras}

A quaternion algebra over a field $F$ is a four-dimensional [central simple](wikipedia://en/404082) $F$-algebra. A quaternion algebra has a basis $1, i, j, ij$ where $i^2, j^2 \in F^\times$ and $ij = -ji$.

A quaternion algebra is said to be split over $F$ if it is isomorphic as an $F$-algebra to the algebra of matrices $M_2(F)$; a quaternion algebra over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) is always split.

If $\sigma$ is an embedding of $F$ into a field $E$ we shall denote by $A \otimes_\sigma E$ the algebra obtained by [extending scalars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_of_scalars) from $F$ to $E$ where we view $F$ as a subfield of $E$ via $\sigma$.

### Arithmetic Kleinian groups {#Arithmetic_Kleinian_groups}

A subgroup of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is said to be *derived from a quaternion algebra* if it can be obtained through the following construction. Let $F$ be a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) which has exactly two embeddings into $\mathbb{C}$ whose image is not contained in $\mathbb{R}$ (one conjugate to the other). Let $A$ be a quaternion algebra over $F$ such that for any embedding $\tau: F \to \mathbb{R}$ the algebra $A \otimes_\tau \mathbb{R}$ is isomorphic to the [Hamilton quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_quaternions). Next we need an order $\mathcal O$ in $A$. Let $\mathcal O^1$ be the group of elements in $\mathcal O$ of reduced norm 1 and let $\Gamma$ be its image in $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ via $\phi$. We then consider the Kleinian group obtained as the image in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ of $\phi(\mathcal O^1)$.

The main fact about these groups is that they are discrete subgroups and they have finite covolume for the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$. Moreover, the construction above yields a cocompact subgroup [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the algebra $A$ is not split over $F$. The discreteness is a rather immediate consequence of the fact that $A$ is only split at its complex embeddings. The finiteness of covolume is harder to prove.[^n1]

An *arithmetic Kleinian group* is any subgroup of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ which is [commensurable](wikipedia://en/29343178) to a group derived from a quaternion algebra. It follows immediately from this definition that arithmetic Kleinian groups are discrete and of finite covolume (this means that they are [lattices](wikipedia://en/15782871) in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$).

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

Examples are provided by taking $F$ to be an [imaginary quadratic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field), $A = M_2(F)$ and $\mathcal O = M_2(O_F)$ where $O_F$ is the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of $F$ (for example $F = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ and $O_F = \mathbb{Z}[i]$). The groups thus obtained are the [Bianchi groups](wikipedia://en/3238478). They are not cocompact, and any arithmetic Kleinian group which is not commensurable to a conjugate of a Bianchi group is cocompact.

If $A$ is any quaternion algebra over an imaginary quadratic number field $F$ which is not isomorphic to a matrix algebra then the unit groups of orders in $A$ are cocompact.

:::

## Trace field of arithmetic manifolds {#Trace_field_of_arithmetic_manifolds}

The invariant [trace field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_field) of a Kleinian group (or, through the monodromy image of the fundamental group, of an hyperbolic manifold) is the field generated by the traces of the squares of its elements. In the case of an arithmetic manifold whose fundamental groups is commensurable with that of a manifold derived from a quaternion algebra over a number field $F$ the invariant trace field equals $F$.

One can in fact characterise arithmetic manifolds through the traces of the elements of their fundamental group. A Kleinian group is an arithmetic group if and only if the following three conditions are realised:

- Its invariant trace field $F$ is a number field with exactly one complex place;
- The traces of its elements are [algebraic integers](wikipedia://en/98981);
- For any $\gamma$ in the group, $t=\mathrm{Trace}(\gamma^2)$ and any embedding $\sigma: F \to \mathbb{R}$ we have $|\sigma(t)| \le 2$.

## Geometry and spectrum of arithmetic hyperbolic three-manifolds {#Geometry_and_spectrum_of_arithmetic_hyperbolic_three-manifolds}

::: {#o12 .statement cue=named-heading name="Volume formula"}

### Volume formula {#Volume_formula}

For the volume of an arithmetic three manifold $M = \Gamma_{\mathcal O} \backslash \mathbb H^3$ derived from a maximal order in a quaternion algebra $A$ over a number field $F$, we have this formula:[^n2]

$$\mathrm{vol}(M) = \frac {2 |D_F|^{\frac 3 2} \cdot \zeta_F(2)} {2^{2r + 1} \cdot \pi^{2r}} \cdot \prod_{\mathfrak p | D_A} (N(\mathfrak p) - 1).$$

where $D_A,D_F$ are the [discriminants](wikipedia://en/55607) of $A, F$ respectively; $\zeta_F$ is the [Dedekind zeta function](wikipedia://en/1131243) of $F$; and $r = [F:\mathbb{Q}]$.

:::

### Remarkable arithmetic hyperbolic three-manifolds {#Remarkable_arithmetic_hyperbolic_three-manifolds}

The [Weeks manifold](wikipedia://en/4201216) is the hyperbolic three-manifold of smallest volume[^o16] and the [Meyerhoff manifold](wikipedia://en/31265515) is the one of next smallest volume.

The complement in the three-sphere of the [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565) is an arithmetic hyperbolic three-manifold[^o17] and attains the smallest volume among all cusped hyperbolic three-manifolds.[^o18]

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Spectrum and Ramanujan conjectures"}

### Spectrum and Ramanujan conjectures {#Spectrum_and_Ramanujan_conjectures}

The [Ramanujan conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan_conjecture) for automorphic forms on $\mathrm{GL}(2)$ over a number field would imply that for any congruence cover of an arithmetic three-manifold (derived from a quaternion algebra) the spectrum of the Laplace operator is contained in $[1, +\infty)$.

:::

## Arithmetic manifolds in three-dimensional topology {#Arithmetic_manifolds_in_three-dimensional_topology}

Many of Thurston's conjectures (for example the [virtually Haken conjecture](wikipedia://en/4692183)), now all known to be true following the work of [Ian Agol](wikipedia://en/32084718),[^o22] were checked first for arithmetic manifolds by using specific methods.[^o23] In some arithmetic cases the Virtual Haken conjecture is known by general means but it is not known if its solution can be arrived at by purely arithmetic means (for instance, by finding a congruence subgroup with positive first Betti number).

Arithmetic manifolds can be used to give examples of manifolds with large injectivity radius whose first Betti number vanishes.[^o24][^o25]

A remark by [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) is that arithmetic manifolds "...often seem to have special beauty."[^o26] This can be substantiated by results showing that the relation between topology and geometry for these manifolds is much more predictable than in general. For example:

- For a given genus *g* there are at most finitely many arithmetic (congruence) hyperbolic 3-manifolds which fiber over the circle with a fiber of genus *g*.[^o27]
- There are at most finitely many arithmetic (congruence) hyperbolic 3-manifolds with a given Heegaard genus.[^o28]
