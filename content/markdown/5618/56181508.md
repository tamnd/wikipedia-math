# Chaim Goodman-Strauss

*Image: Chaim Goodman-Strauss.jpg.* Chaim Goodman-Strauss assembling sculpture at Gathering 4 Gardner, Atlanta, GA, 2008.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chaim Goodman-Strauss"}

**Chaim Goodman-Strauss** (born June 22, 1967 in Austin, Texas) is an American mathematician who works in [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/4165882), especially [aperiodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145). He retired from the faculty of the [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas) and currently serves as outreach mathematician for the [National Museum of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mathematics). He is co-author with [John H. Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Conway) and Heidi Burgiel of *[The Symmetries of Things](wikipedia://en/69062204)*, a comprehensive book surveying the mathematical theory of patterns.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Goodman-Strauss received both his B.S. (1988) and Ph.D. (1994) in mathematics from the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin).[^o4] His doctoral advisor was [John Edwin Luecke](wikipedia://en/29631637).[^o5] He joined the faculty at the [University of Arkansas, Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas,_Fayetteville) (UA) in 1994 and served as departmental chair from 2008 to 2015. He held visiting positions at the [National Autonomous University of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Autonomous_University_of_Mexico) and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o4][^o6]

During 1995 he did research at [The Geometry Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Geometry_Center), a mathematics research and education center at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), where he investigated aperiodic tilings of the plane.[^o7]

Goodman-Strauss has been fascinated by patterns and mathematical paradoxes for as long as he can remember. He attended a lecture about the mathematician [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216) when he was 17 and says, "I was already doomed to be a mathematician, but that lecture sealed my fate."[^o8] He became a mathematics writer and popularizer. From 2004 to 2012, in conjunction with [KUAF 91.3 FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KUAF), the University of Arkansas [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR) affiliate, he presented "[The Math Factor](http://mathfactor.uark.edu/)," a podcast website dealing with recreational mathematics.[^o9] He is an admirer of [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) and is on the advisory council of [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner), an organization that celebrates the legacy of the famed mathematics popularizer and [Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American) columnist,[^o10] and is active in the associated [Celebration of Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebration_of_Mind) events.[^o11][^o12] In 2022 Goodman-Strauss was awarded the National Museum of Mathematics' [Rosenthal Prize](https://momath.org/rosenthal-prize/), which recognizes innovation and inspiration in math teaching.[^o13]

## Aperiodic monotiles {#Aperiodic_monotiles}

*Image: Smith\_aperiodic\_monotiling.svg.* A tiling that does not repeat and uses only one shape, discovered by [David Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Smith_%28hobbyist%29).

On Mar 20, 2023 Strauss, together with [David Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Smith_%28hobbyist%29), Joseph Samuel Myers and [Craig S. Kaplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_S._Kaplan), announced the proof that the tile discovered by David Smith is an aperiodic monotile,[^o15] i.e., a solution to a longstanding open [einstein problem](wikipedia://en/43542926).[^o16] The team continues to refine this work.[^o17]

## Mathematical artist {#Mathematical_artist}

In 2008 Goodman-Strauss teamed up with [J. H. Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._H._Conway) and Heidi Burgiel to write *[The Symmetries of Things](wikipedia://en/69062204)*, an exhaustive and reader-accessible overview of the mathematical theory of patterns. He produced hundreds of full-color images for this book using software that he developed for the purpose.[^o19] The Mathematical Association of America said, "The first thing one notices when one picks up a copy … is that it is a beautiful book … filled with gorgeous color pictures … many of which were generated by Goodman-Strauss. Unlike some books which add in illustrations to keep the reader's attention, the pictures are genuinely essential to the topic of this book."[^o20]

He also creates large-scale sculptures inspired by mathematics, and some of these have been featured at Gathering 4 Gardner conferences.[^o21]
