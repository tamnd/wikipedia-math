# DBAR problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="DBAR problem"}

The **DBAR problem**, or the $\bar{\partial}$-problem, is the problem of solving the [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309)

$$\bar{\partial} f (z, \bar{z}) = g(z)$$

for the function $f(z,\bar{z})$, where $g(z)$ is assumed to be known and $z = x + iy$ is a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) in a [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) $R\subseteq \mathbb{C}$. The operator $\bar{\partial}$ is called the DBAR operator:[^n1]

$$\bar{\partial} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + i \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \right)$$

The DBAR operator is nothing other than the complex conjugate of the operator

$$\partial=\frac{\partial}{\partial z} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x} - i \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \right)$$

denoting the usual differentiation in the complex $z$-plane.

:::

The DBAR problem is of key importance in the theory of [integrable systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_systems), [Schrödinger operators](wikipedia://en/59874) and (together with a jump condition) generalizes the [Riemann–Hilbert problem](wikipedia://en/9710396).[^n1][^n2][^n3]
