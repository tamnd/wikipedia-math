# Stephen Fienberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stephen Fienberg"}

**Stephen Elliott Fienberg** (27 November 1942 – 14 December 2016) was a professor emeritus[^o3] (formerly the Maurice Falk University Professor of [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and Social Science) in the Department of Statistics, the [Machine Learning](wikipedia://en/233488) Department, [Heinz College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_College), and Cylab at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University).[^o4][^o11] Fienberg was the founding [co-editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of the *[Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Review_of_Statistics_and_Its_Application)*[^o17] and of the *[Journal of Privacy and Confidentiality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Privacy_and_Confidentiality)*.[^o15]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario), Fienberg earned a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree in Mathematics and Statistics from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1964, a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree in statistics in 1965, and a Ph.D. in statistics in 1968 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) for research supervised by [Frederick Mosteller](wikipedia://en/6125615).[^o9][^o6]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Fienberg was on the Carnegie Mellon University faculty from 1980 and served as Dean of the [Dietrich College of Humanities and Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietrich_College_of_Humanities_and_Social_Sciences).[^o11][^o4] He became a U.S. citizen in 1998.

Fienberg was one of the foremost social statisticians in the world, and was well known for his work in [log-linear modeling](wikipedia://en/15689191) for [categorical data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_data), the statistical analysis of network data, and methodology for disclosure limitation. He was also an expert on forensic science, the only statistician to serve on the National Commission on Forensic Science.[^o8]

He authored more than 400 publications, including six books, advised more than 30 Ph.D. students, and could claim more than 105 descendants in his mathematical genealogy. His publications included books on discrete multivariate analysis[^o10] [categorical data analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_data_analysis),[^o11] US census adjustment,[^o12][^o13] and forensic science.[^o14]

He was a founder and editor-in-chief of the *Journal of Privacy and Confidentiality*.[^o15][^o16] and of the *[Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Review_of_Statistics_and_Its_Application)*.[^o17]

### Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Fienberg was an elected member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o19] an elected fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada), an elected fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences),[^o20] a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science),[^o21] a fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988)[^o22] and a fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o23]

He was a recipient of the [Committee of Presidents of Statistical Societies](wikipedia://en/44155344) (COPSS) [Presidents' Award](wikipedia://en/11690483) in 1982.[^o24][^o25] In 2002, Fienberg received the [Samuel S. Wilks Award](wikipedia://en/15966180) from the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) for his distinguished career in statistics.[^o8] He received the inaugural [Statistical Society of Canada](wikipedia://en/15572327)'s [Lise Manchester Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Manchester_Award) in 2008 in recognition of his application of statistics to problems of public interest.[^o8] In 2015, he received the Jerome Sacks Award for Cross-Disciplinary Research from the [National Institute of Statistical Sciences](wikipedia://en/56043115),[^o26] and the [R. A. Fisher Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher_Lectureship) from COPSS in 2015.[^o27] He was awarded the Zellner Medal by the [International Society for Bayesian Analysis](wikipedia://en/6038312) (ISBA) in 2016.[^o28] In 2009 Fienberg was recipient of the [American Statistical Association's Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224).[^o29]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Stephen Fienberg was married to Joyce Fienberg and had two sons, Anthony and Howard, and six grandchildren. He died on 14 December 2016.[^o34][^o11] Joyce Fienberg died on 27 October 2018. She was one of eleven worshippers murdered during the [Pittsburgh synagogue shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_synagogue_shooting) at the [Tree of Life – Or L'Simcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_Life_–_Or_L'Simcha_Congregation) synagogue in the [Squirrel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel_Hill) neighborhood of [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh), Pennsylvania.[^o35]
