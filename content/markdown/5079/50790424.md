# Heptagonal triangle

*Image: Heptagrams.svg.* solid red 2px solid green 2px solid blue 2px Each of the fourteen [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) **heptagonal triangles** has one green side, one blue side, and one red side.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heptagonal triangle"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **heptagonal triangle** is an [obtuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_triangle), [scalene triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalene_triangle) whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) coincide with the first, second, and fourth vertices of a regular [heptagon](wikipedia://en/365349) (from an arbitrary starting vertex). Thus its sides coincide with one side and the adjacent shorter and longer [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251#Polygons) of the regular heptagon. All heptagonal triangles are [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) (have the same shape), and so they are collectively known as *the* heptagonal triangle. Its angles have measures $\pi/7, 2\pi/7,$ and $4\pi/7,$ and it is the only triangle with angles in the ratios 1:2:4. The heptagonal triangle has various remarkable properties.

:::

## Key points {#Key_points}

The heptagonal triangle's [nine-point center](wikipedia://en/4517570) is also its first [Brocard point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_point).[^o3]

The second Brocard point lies on the nine-point circle.[^o4]

The [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and the [Fermat points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_points) of a heptagonal triangle form an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285).[^o3]

The distance between the circumcenter *O* and the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) *H* is given by[^o4]

$$OH=R\sqrt{2},$$

where *R* is the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius). The squared distance from the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) *I* to the orthocenter is[^o4]

$$IH^2=\frac{R^2+4r^2}{2},$$

where *r* is the [inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius).

The two tangents from the orthocenter to the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) are mutually [perpendicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendicular_lines).[^o4]

## Relations of distances {#Relations_of_distances}

### Sides {#Sides}

The heptagonal triangle's sides *a* \< *b* \< *c* coincide respectively with the regular heptagon's side, shorter diagonal, and longer diagonal. They satisfy[^o7]

$$\begin{aligned} a^2 & =c(c-b), \\[5pt] b^2 & =a(c+a), \\[5pt] c^2 & =b(a+b), \\[5pt] \frac 1 a & =\frac 1 b + \frac 1 c \end{aligned}$$

(the latter[^o4] being the [optic equation](wikipedia://en/45342125)) and hence

$$ab+ac=bc,$$

and[^o7]

$$b^3+2b^2c-bc^2-c^3=0,$$

$$c^3-2c^2a-ca^2+a^3=0,$$

$$a^3-2a^2b-ab^2+b^3=0.$$

Thus –*b*/*c*, *c*/*a*, and *a*/*b* all satisfy the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787)

$$t^3-2t^2-t + 1=0.$$

However, no [algebraic expressions](wikipedia://en/4283745) with purely real terms exist for the solutions of this equation, because it is an example of [casus irreducibilis](wikipedia://en/11153189).

The approximate relation of the sides is

$$b\approx 1.80193\cdot a, \qquad c\approx 2.24698\cdot a.$$

We also have[^o8][^o9]

$$\frac{a^2}{bc}, \quad -\frac{b^2}{ca}, \quad -\frac{c^2}{ab}$$

satisfy the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787)

$$t^3+4t^2+3t-1=0.$$

We also have[^o8]

$$\frac{a^3}{bc^2}, \quad -\frac{b^3}{ca^2}, \quad \frac{c^3}{ab^2}$$

satisfy the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787)

$$t^3-t^2-9t+1=0.$$

We also have[^o8]

$$\frac{a^3}{b^2c}, \quad \frac{b^3}{c^2a}, \quad -\frac{c^3}{a^2b}$$

satisfy the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787)

$$t^3+5t^2-8t+1=0.$$

We also have[^o4]

$$b^2-a^2=ac,$$

$$c^2-b^2=ab,$$

$$a^2-c^2=-bc,$$

and[^o4]

$$\frac{b^2}{a^2}+\frac{c^2}{b^2}+\frac{a^2}{c^2}=5.$$

We also have[^o8]

$$ab-bc+ca=0,$$

$$a^{3}b-b^{3}c+c^{3}a=0,$$

$$a^{4}b+b^{4}c-c^{4}a=0,$$

$$a^{11}b^{3}-b^{11}c^{3}+c^{11}a^{3}=0.$$

### Altitudes {#Altitudes}

The altitudes *h*<sub>*a*</sub>, *h*<sub>*b*</sub>, and *h*<sub>*c*</sub> satisfy

$h_a=h_b+h_c$[^o4]

and

$h_a^2+h_b^2+h_c^2=\frac{a^2+b^2+c^2}{2}.$[^o4]

The altitude from side *b* (opposite angle *B*) is half the internal angle bisector $w_A$ of *A*:[^o4]

$$2h_b=w_A.$$

Here angle *A* is the smallest angle, and *B* is the second smallest.

### Internal angle bisectors {#Internal_angle_bisectors}

We have these properties of the [internal angle bisectors](wikipedia://en/152547#Angle_bisector) $w_A, w_B,$ and $w_C$ of angles *A, B*, and *C* respectively:[^o4]

$$w_A=b+c,$$

$$w_B=c-a,$$

$$w_C=b-a.$$

### Circumradius, inradius, and exradius {#Circumradius,_inradius,_and_exradius}

The triangle's area is[^o18]

$$A=\frac{\sqrt{7}}{4}R^2,$$

where *R* is the triangle's [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius).

We have[^o4]

$$a^2+b^2+c^2=7R^2.$$

We also have[^o13]

$$a^4+b^4+c^4=21R^4.$$

$$a^6+b^6+c^6=70R^6.$$

The ratio *r* /*R* of the [inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius) to the circumradius is the positive solution of the cubic equation[^o18]

$$8x^3+28x^2+14x-7=0.$$

In addition,[^o4]

$$\frac{1}{a^2}+\frac{1}{b^2}+\frac{1}{c^2}=\frac{2}{R^2}.$$

We also have[^o13]

$$\frac{1}{a^4}+\frac{1}{b^4}+\frac{1}{c^4}=\frac{2}{R^4}.$$

$$\frac{1}{a^6}+\frac{1}{b^6}+\frac{1}{c^6}=\frac{17}{7R^6}.$$

In general for all integer *n*,

$$a^{2n}+b^{2n}+c^{2n}=g(n)(2R)^{2n}$$

where

$$g(-1) = 8, \quad g(0)=3, \quad g(1)=7$$

and

$$g(n)=7g(n-1)-14g(n-2)+7g(n-3).$$

We also have[^o13]

$$2b^2-a^2=\sqrt{7}bR, \quad 2c^2-b^2=\sqrt{7}cR, \quad 2a^2-c^2=-\sqrt{7}aR.$$

We also have[^o8]

$$a^{3}c + b^{3}a - c^{3}b = -7R^{4},$$

$$a^{4}c - b^{4}a + c^{4}b = 7\sqrt{7}R^{5},$$

$$a^{11}c^{3}+b^{11}a^{3} - c^{11}b^{3} = -7^{3}17R^{14}.$$

The [exradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) *r*<sub>*a*</sub> corresponding to side *a* equals the radius of the [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243) of the heptagonal triangle.[^o4]

## Orthic triangle {#Orthic_triangle}

The heptagonal triangle's [orthic triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthic_triangle), with vertices at the feet of the [altitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_%28geometry%29), is [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) to the heptagonal triangle, with similarity ratio 1:2. The heptagonal triangle is the only obtuse triangle that is similar to its orthic triangle (the [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) being the only acute one).[^o4]

## Hyperbola {#Hyperbola}

The [rectangular hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) through $A,B,C,G=X(2),H=X(4)$ has the following properties:

- first focus $F_1 = X(5)$
- center $U$ is on Euler circle (general property) and on circle $(O, F_1)$
- second focus $F_2$ is on the circumcircle

### Cubic polynomials {#Cubic_polynomials}

The [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787) $64y^3-112y^2+56y-7=0$ has solutions[^o4] $\sin^2\! A,\ \sin^2\! B,\ \sin^2\! C.$

The positive solution of the cubic equation $x^3+x^2-2x-1=0$ equals $2\cos B.$[^o22]

The [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) of the cubic equation $x^3 - \tfrac{\sqrt 7}{2}x^2 + \tfrac{\sqrt 7}{8} = 0$ are[^o8] $\sin 2A,\ \sin 2B,\ \sin 2C.$

The roots of the cubic equation $x^3 - \tfrac{\sqrt 7}{2} x^2 + \tfrac{\sqrt 7}{8} = 0$ are $-\sin A,\ \sin B,\ \sin C.$

The roots of the cubic equation $x^3 + \tfrac{1}{2}x^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}x - \tfrac{1}{8} = 0$ are $-\cos A,\ \cos B,\ \cos C.$

The roots of the cubic equation $x^3 + \sqrt{7}x^2 - 7x + \sqrt{7} = 0$ are $\tan A,\ \tan B,\ \tan C.$

The roots of the cubic equation $x^3 - 21x^2 + 35x - 7 = 0$ are $\tan^2\! A,\ \tan^2\! B,\ \tan^2\! C.$
