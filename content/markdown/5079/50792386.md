# Günter Harder

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Günter Harder"}

**Günter Harder** (14 March 1938 – 10 June 2025) was a German mathematician, specializing in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Harder studied mathematics and physics in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). Simultaneously with the *[Staatsexamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsexamen)* in 1964 in Hamburg, he received his doctoral degree (Dr. rer. nat.) under [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911) with a thesis *Über die Galoiskohomologie der Tori*. Two years later, he completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation).

## Career {#Career}

After a one-year postdoctorate position at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and a position as an assistant professor at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg), he became a professor ordinarius at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn). With the exception of a six-year stay at the former [Universität-Gesamthochschule Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergische_Universität_Wuppertal), Harder remained at the University of Bonn until his retirement in 2003. From 1995 to 2006, he was one of the directors of the [Max-Planck-Institut für Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Mathematik) in Bonn.

He was a visiting professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), at Princeton's [Institute for Advanced Study (IAS)](wikipedia://en/184179) (for the academic years 1966–1967, 1972–1973, 1986–1987, autumn of 1983, autumn of 2006),[^o6] at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) (I.H.É.S.) in Paris, at the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research) in Mumbai, and at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

For decades, Harder was known to German mathematicians as the *Spiritus Rector* for a mathematical workshop held for one week in spring and one week in autumn; the workshop, sponsored by the [Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach), introduced young mathematicians and scientists to important new developments in pure mathematics and mathematical sciences.

Harder's doctoral students include [Kai Behrend](wikipedia://en/31872403), [Jörg Bewersdorff](wikipedia://en/37203806), [Joachim Schwermer](wikipedia://en/56118470), and [Maria Heep-Altiner](wikipedia://en/60048114).[^o4]

## Death {#Death}

Harder died on 10 June 2025, at the age of 87.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

His research deals with arithmetic geometry, [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), [Shimura varieties](wikipedia://en/10389861), [motives](wikipedia://en/430680), and [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705). He made foundational contributions to the [Waldspurger formula](wikipedia://en/55941264).

With [Ina Kersten](wikipedia://en/62766198), he was a co-editor of the collected works of [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911).

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Harder was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1970 and gave a talk titled *Semisimple group schemes over curves and automorphic functions*[^o11] and in 1990 with a talk titled *Eisenstein cohomology of arithmetic groups and its applications to number theory*.[^o12] In 1988, he was awarded the [Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize) by the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft).

In 2004, Harder received, with [Friedhelm Waldhausen](wikipedia://en/20163961), the [von Staudt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Staudt_Prize).[^o13]
