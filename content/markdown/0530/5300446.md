# Perseus (geometer)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perseus"}

**Perseus** (Περσεύς; c. 150 BC) was an [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer), who invented the concept of [spiric sections](wikipedia://en/5300614), in analogy to the [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673) studied by [Apollonius of Perga](wikipedia://en/257242).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Few details of Perseus' life are known, as he is mentioned only by [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus) and [Geminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geminus); none of his own works have survived.

## Spiric sections {#Spiric_sections}

The spiric sections result from the intersection of a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) with a [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) that is parallel to the rotational symmetry axis of the torus. Consequently, spiric sections are fourth-order ([quartic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_curve)) [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691), whereas the [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673) are second-order ([quadratic](wikipedia://en/187240)) [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691). Spiric sections are a special case of a [toric section](wikipedia://en/5350369), and were the first toric sections to be described.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

The most famous spiric section is the [Cassini oval](wikipedia://en/2040906), which is the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of points having a constant *product* of distances to two foci. For comparison, an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) has a constant sum of focal distances, a [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) has a constant difference of focal distances, and a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) has a constant ratio of focal distances.

:::
