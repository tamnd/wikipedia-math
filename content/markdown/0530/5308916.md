# Random compact set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Random compact set"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **random compact set** is essentially a [compact set](wikipedia://en/6042)-valued [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685). Random compact sets are useful in the study of attractors for [random dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/7185428).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(M, d)$ be a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). Let $\mathcal{K}$ denote the set of all compact subsets of $M$. The Hausdorff metric $h$ on $\mathcal{K}$ is defined by

$$h(K_{1}, K_{2}) := \max \left\{ \sup_{a \in K_{1}} \inf_{b \in K_{2}} d(a, b), \sup_{b \in K_{2}} \inf_{a \in K_{1}} d(a, b) \right\}.$$

$(\mathcal{K}, h)$ is also а complete separable metric space. The corresponding open subsets generate a [σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_algebra) on $\mathcal{K}$, the [Borel sigma algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_sigma_algebra) $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})$ of $\mathcal{K}$.

A **random compact set** is а [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) $K$ from а [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ into $(\mathcal{K}, \mathcal{B} (\mathcal{K}) )$.

Put another way, a random compact set is a measurable function $K \colon \Omega \to 2^{M}$ such that $K(\omega)$ is [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) compact and

$$\omega \mapsto \inf_{b \in K(\omega)} d(x, b)$$

is a measurable function for every $x \in M$.

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

Random compact sets in this sense are also [random closed sets](wikipedia://en/79229206) as in [Matheron](wikipedia://en/4415402) (1975). Consequently, under the additional assumption that the carrier space is locally compact, their distribution is given by the probabilities

$\mathbb{P} (X \cap K = \emptyset)$ for $K \in \mathcal{K}.$

(The distribution of а random compact convex set is also given by the system of all inclusion probabilities $\mathbb{P}(X \subset K).$)

For $K = \{ x \}$, the probability $\mathbb{P} (x \in X)$ is obtained, which satisfies

$$\mathbb{P}(x \in X) = 1 - \mathbb{P}(x \not\in X).$$

Thus the **covering function** $p_{X}$ is given by

$p_{X} (x) = \mathbb{P} (x \in X)$ for $x \in M.$

Of course, $p_{X}$ can also be interpreted as the mean of the indicator function $\mathbf{1}_{X}$:

$$p_{X} (x) = \mathbb{E} \mathbf{1}_{X} (x).$$

The covering function takes values between $0$ and $1$. The set $b_{X}$ of all $x \in M$ with $p_{X} (x) > 0$ is called the **support** of $X$. The set $k_X$, of all $x \in M$ with $p_X(x)=1$ is called the **kernel**, the set of **fixed points**, or **essential minimum** $e(X)$. If $X_1, X_2, \ldots$, is а sequence of [i.i.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.i.d.) random compact sets, then almost surely

$$\bigcap_{i=1}^\infty X_i = e(X)$$

and $\bigcap_{i=1}^\infty X_i$ converges almost surely to $e(X).$
