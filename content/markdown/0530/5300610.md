# Boolean delay equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boolean delay equation"}

A **Boolean Delay Equation** (BDE) is an evolution rule for the state of dynamical variables whose values may be represented by a finite discrete numbers or states, such as 0 and 1. As a novel type of semi-discrete [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), **Boolean delay equations (BDEs)** are models with Boolean-valued variables that evolve in continuous time. Since at the present time, most phenomena are too complex to be modeled by [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) (as continuous infinite-dimensional systems), BDEs are intended as a ([heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic)) first step on the challenging road to further understanding and [modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_model) them. For instance, one can mention complex problems in [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) dynamics, solid-earth [geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics), and many problems elsewhere in [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences) where much of the discourse is still conceptual.

:::

One example of a BDE is the [Ring oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_oscillator) equation: $X(t-\tau) = \overline{X}(t)$, which produces periodic oscillations. More complex equations can display richer behavior, such as nonperiodic and chaotic (deterministic) behavior.[^o2]
