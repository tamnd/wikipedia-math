# List of Euclidean uniform tilings

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of Euclidean uniform tilings"}

*Image: Semi-regular-floor-3464.JPG.* An example of uniform tiling in the [Archeological Museum of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeological_Museum_of_Seville), [Sevilla, Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla,_Spain): [rhombitrihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864311)

*Image: Brückner Vielflache Fig. 96b - 98.jpg.* Uniform tilings and their duals drawn by [Max Brückner](wikipedia://en/48703370) in *Vielecke und Vielflache* (1900)

This table shows the 11 convex [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635) (regular and semiregular) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/9417), and their dual tilings.

:::

There are three regular and eight semiregular [tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_by_regular_polygons) in the plane. The semiregular tilings form new tilings from their duals, each made from one type of irregular face.

[John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) called these uniform duals ***Catalan tilings***, in parallel to the [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) polyhedra.

Uniform tilings are listed by their [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745), the sequence of faces that exist on each vertex. For example *\4.8.8* means one square and two octagons on a vertex.

These 11 uniform tilings have 32 different *[uniform colorings](wikipedia://en/18624814)*. A uniform coloring allows identical sided polygons at a vertex to be colored differently, while still maintaining vertex-uniformity and transformational congruence between vertices. (Note: Some of the tiling images shown below are *not* color-uniform.)

In addition to the 11 convex uniform tilings, there are also [14 known nonconvex tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635#Expanded_lists_of_uniform_tilings), using [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303), and reverse orientation vertex configurations. A further 28 uniform tilings are known using [apeirogons](wikipedia://en/5658261). If zigzags are also allowed, there are 23 more known uniform tilings and 10 more known families depending on a parameter: in 8 cases the parameter is continuous, and in the other 2 it is discrete. The set is not known to be complete.

## Convex uniform tilings of the Euclidean plane {#Convex_uniform_tilings_of_the_Euclidean_plane}

All reflectional forms can be made by [Wythoff constructions](wikipedia://en/2950777), represented by [Wythoff symbols](wikipedia://en/3691548), or [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), each operating upon one of three [Schwarz triangle](wikipedia://en/2069325#Triangles_for_the_Euclidean_plane) (4,4,2), (6,3,2), or (3,3,3), with symmetry represented by [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004): \[4,4\], \[6,3\], or \[3<sup>\[3\]</sup>\]. [Alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) forms such as the snub can also be represented by special markups within each system. Only one uniform tiling can't be constructed by a Wythoff process, but can be made by an [elongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29) of the triangular tiling. An orthogonal mirror construction \[∞,2,∞\] also exists, seen as two sets of parallel mirrors making a rectangular fundamental domain. If the domain is square, this symmetry can be doubled by a diagonal mirror into the \[4,4\] family.

## Laves tilings {#Laves_tilings}

In the 1987 book, *[Tilings and patterns](wikipedia://en/76301624)*, [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) calls the vertex-uniform tilings ***Archimedean***, in parallel to the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847). Their [dual tilings](wikipedia://en/2536864) are called ***Laves tilings*** in honor of [crystallographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) [Fritz Laves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Laves).[^o25][^o26] They're also called **Shubnikov–Laves tilings** after [Aleksei Shubnikov](wikipedia://en/70215429).[^o27] [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) called the uniform duals ***Catalan tilings***, in parallel to the [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) polyhedra.

The Laves tilings have vertices at the centers of the regular polygons, and edges connecting centers of regular polygons that share an edge. The [tiles](wikipedia://en/39372) of the Laves tilings are called *[planigons](wikipedia://en/53413131)*. This includes the 3 regular tiles (triangle, square and hexagon) and 8 irregular ones. Each vertex has edges evenly spaced around it. Three dimensional analogues of the *planigons* are called [stereohedrons](wikipedia://en/53413889).

These dual tilings are listed by their [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration), the number of faces at each vertex of a face. For example *V4.8.8* means isosceles triangle tiles with one corner with four triangles, and two corners containing eight triangles. The orientations of the vertex planigons (up to [D<sub>12</sub>](wikipedia://en/100267)) are consistent with the vertex diagrams in the below sections.
