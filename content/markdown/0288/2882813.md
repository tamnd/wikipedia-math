# Parameter space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parameter space"}

The **parameter space** is the [space](wikipedia://en/5308894) of all possible parameter values that define a particular [mathematical model](wikipedia://en/20590). It is also sometimes called **weight space**, and is often a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of finite-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697).

:::

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), parameter spaces are particularly useful for describing [parametric families](wikipedia://en/19653337) of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543). They also form the background for [parameter estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parameter_estimation). In the case of [extremum estimators](wikipedia://en/22286047) for [parametric models](wikipedia://en/2883137), a certain [objective function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objective_function) is maximized or minimized over the parameter space.[^o2] Theorems of [existence](wikipedia://en/251177) and [consistency](wikipedia://en/1497569) of such estimators require some assumptions about the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of the parameter space. For instance, [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness) of the parameter space, together with [continuity](wikipedia://en/6122) of the objective function, suffices for the existence of an extremum estimator.[^o2]

Sometimes, parameters are analyzed to view how they affect their statistical model. In that context, they can be viewed as inputs of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427), in which case the technical term for the parameter space is [domain of a function](wikipedia://en/50263). The ranges of values of the parameters may form the axes of a [plot](wikipedia://en/19774918), and particular outcomes of the model may be plotted against these axes to illustrate how different regions of the parameter space produce different types of behavior in the model.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A simple model of health deterioration after developing [lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer) could include the two parameters gender[^o5] and smoker/non-smoker, in which case the parameter space is the following set of four possibilities: $\{(\operatorname{Male}, \operatorname{Smoker}), (\operatorname{Male}, \operatorname{Non}-\operatorname{smoker}), (\operatorname{Female}, \operatorname{Smoker}), (\operatorname{Female}, \operatorname{Non}-\operatorname{smoker})\}$.
- The [logistic map](wikipedia://en/18137) $x_{n+1} = r x_n (1-x_n)$ has one parameter, *r*, which can take any positive value. The parameter space is therefore [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571).

For some values of *r*, this function ends up cycling around a few values or becomes fixed on one value. These long-term values can be plotted against *r* in a [bifurcation diagram](wikipedia://en/79173) to show the different behaviours of the function for different values of *r*.

- In a [sine wave](wikipedia://en/324749) model $y(t) = A \cdot \sin(\omega t + \phi),$ the parameters are [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude) *A* > 0, [angular frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency) ω > 0, and [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) φ ∈ S<sup>1</sup>. Thus the parameter space is $R^+ \times R^+ \times S^1 .$
- In [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518), the parameter space is the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) **C** = { *z* = *x* + *y* i : x, y ∈ **R** }, where i<sup>2</sup> = −1.

The famous [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562) is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of this parameter space, consisting of the points $c$ in the complex plane which give a [bounded set](wikipedia://en/48258) of numbers when a [iterated function](wikipedia://en/1448702) $z_{n+1} = f_c(z_n) = z^2+c$ is repeatedly applied from that starting point $z_0 = 0$. The remaining points, which are not in the set, give an unbounded set of numbers (they tend to infinity) when this function is repeatedly applied from that $z_0$.

- In [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), [hyperparameters](wikipedia://en/32402755) are used to describe models. In [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning), the parameters of a deep network are called **weights.** Due to the layered structure of deep networks, their weight space has a complex structure and geometry.[^o6][^o7] For example, in [multilayer perceptrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilayer_perceptrons), the same function is preserved when [permuting](wikipedia://en/44027) the nodes of a hidden layer, amounting to permuting weight matrices of the network. This property is known as *[equivariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariance) to permutation* of deep weight spaces.[^o6] The study seeks [hyperparameter optimization](wikipedia://en/54361643).

:::

## History {#History}

Parameter space contributed to the liberation of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) from the confines of [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853). For instance, the parameter space of [spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_%28geometry%29) in three dimensions, has four dimensions—three for the sphere center and another for the radius. According to [Dirk Struik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Struik), it was the book *Neue Geometrie des Raumes* (1849) by [Julius Plücker](wikipedia://en/52146) that showed

> ...geometry need not solely be based on points as basic elements. Lines, planes, circles, spheres can all be used as the elements (*Raumelemente*) on which a geometry can be based. This fertile conception threw new light on both synthetic and algebraic geometry and created new forms of duality. The number of dimensions of a particular form of geometry could now be any positive number, depending on the number of parameters necessary to define the "element".[^o9]

The requirement for higher dimensions is illustrated by [Plücker's line geometry](wikipedia://en/1168486). Struik writes

> \[Plücker's\] geometry of lines in three-space could be considered as a four-dimensional geometry, or, as [Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) has stressed, as the geometry of a four-dimensional [quadric](wikipedia://en/145570) in a five-dimensional space.[^o9]

Thus the [Klein quadric](wikipedia://en/2933091) describes the parameters of lines in space.
