# Apodicticity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apodicticity"}

"**Apodictic**", also spelled "**apodeictic**" (ἀποδεικτικός, "capable of demonstration"), is an [adjectival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective) expression from [Aristotelian logic](wikipedia://en/48046) that refers to [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094) that are demonstrably, necessarily or [self-evidently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-evident) true.[^o2] **Apodicticity** or apodixis is the corresponding [abstract noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_noun), referring to [logical certainty](wikipedia://en/3986852).

:::

Apodictic propositions contrast with [assertoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assertoric) propositions, which merely assert that something is (or is not) true, and with problematic propositions, which assert only the possibility of something's being true. Apodictic judgments are clearly provable or logically certain. For instance, "Three plus one equals four" is apodictic, because it is true by definition. "Chicago is larger than Omaha" is assertoric. "A corporation could be wealthier than a country" is problematic. In [Aristotelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelianism) logic, "apodictic" is opposed to "[dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813)", as [scientific proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_proof) is opposed to philosophical [reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning). [Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant) contrasted "apodictic" with "problematic" and "assertoric" in the *[Critique of Pure Reason](wikipedia://en/347543)* (A70/B95 - A76/B101).[^o3]

## Apodictic a priorism {#Apodictic_a_priorism}

[Hans Reichenbach](wikipedia://en/1546863), one of the founders of [logical positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism), offered a modified version of [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant)'s [a priorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_priorism) by distinguishing between *apodictic a priorism* and *constitutive a priorism*.[^o5]
