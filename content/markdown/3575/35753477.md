# Pseudo-uniform polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pseudo-uniform polyhedron"}

A **pseudo-[uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223)** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) which has [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) as faces and has the same [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) at all [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) but is not [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive): it is not true that for any two vertices, there exists a [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) of the polyhedron mapping the first [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) onto the second. Thus, although all the vertices of a pseudo-uniform polyhedron appear the same, it is not [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560). They are called pseudo-uniform polyhedra due to their resemblance to some true [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223).

:::

There are two known pseudo-uniform polyhedra: the [pseudorhombicuboctahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudorhombicuboctahedron) and the [pseudo-great rhombicuboctahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-great_rhombicuboctahedron). It is not known if there are any others; [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) conjectured that there are not, but thought that a proof would be "probably quite complicated".[^o2] They both have [D<sub>4d</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry) symmetry, the same symmetry as a [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045). They can both be constructed from a [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223) by twisting one [cupola](wikipedia://en/689743)-shaped cap.

## The pseudo-uniform polyhedra {#The_pseudo-uniform_polyhedra}

### Pseudorhombicuboctahedron {#Pseudorhombicuboctahedron}

The pseudorhombicuboctahedron is the only convex pseudo-uniform polyhedron. It is also a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) (J<sub>37</sub>) and can also be called the [elongated square gyrobicupola](wikipedia://en/1128551). Its dual is the pseudo-deltoidal icositetrahedron. As the name suggests, it can be constructed by elongating a [square gyrobicupola](wikipedia://en/1128615) (*J*<sub>29</sub>) and inserting an [octagonal](wikipedia://en/314575) [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) between its two halves. The resulting solid is locally vertex-regular — the arrangement of the four faces incident on any vertex is the same for all vertices; this is unique among the Johnson solids. However, it is not [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), and consequently not one of the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847), as there are pairs of vertices such that there is no isometry of the solid which maps one into the other. Essentially, the two types of vertices can be distinguished by their "neighbors of neighbors." Another way to see that the polyhedron is not vertex-regular is to note that there is exactly one belt of eight squares around its equator, which distinguishes vertices on the belt from vertices on either side.

The solid can also be seen as the result of twisting one of the [square cupolae](wikipedia://en/1128479) (*J*<sub>4</sub>) on a [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213) (one of the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847); a.k.a. the elongated square orthobicupola) by 45 degrees. Its similarity to the rhombicuboctahedron gives it the alternative name **pseudorhombicuboctahedron**. It has occasionally been referred to as "the fourteenth Archimedean solid".

### Pseudo-great rhombicuboctahedron {#Pseudo-great_rhombicuboctahedron}

The uniform [nonconvex great rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/4157120) may be seen as an [octagrammic prism](wikipedia://en/24232091) with the octagrams excavated with crossed square cupolae, similarly to how the [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213) may be seen as an [octagonal prism](wikipedia://en/3026569) with the octagons augmented with square cupolae. Rotating one of the cupolae in this construction results in the [pseudo-great rhombicuboctahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-great_rhombicuboctahedron).

The pseudo great rhombicuboctahedron has a single "belt" of squares around its equator, and can be constructed by twisting one of the [crossed square cupolae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossed_square_cupola) on a nonconvex great rhombicuboctahedron by 45 degrees. This is analogous to the pseudorhombicuboctahedron.

## Duals of the pseudo-uniform polyhedra {#Duals_of_the_pseudo-uniform_polyhedra}

The [duals](wikipedia://en/8815) of the pseudo-uniform polyhedra have all faces [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330), but not transitive: their faces do not all lie within the same [symmetry orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_orbit) and they are thus not [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120). This is a consequence of the pseudo-uniform polyhedra having the same [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) at every vertex, but not being [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive). This is demonstrated by the different colours used for the faces in the images of the dual pseudo-uniform polyhedra in this article, denoting different types of faces.
