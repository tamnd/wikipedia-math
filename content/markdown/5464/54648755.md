# Mark Braverman (mathematician)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Mark Braverman"}

**Mark Braverman** (מארק ברוורמן; born 1984) is an Israeli mathematician and theoretical computer scientist. He was awarded an [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize) in 2016 as well as [Presburger Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presburger_Award) in the same year.[^o4][^o5] In 2019, he was awarded the [Alan T. Waterman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_T._Waterman_Award).[^o6] In 2022, he won the [IMU Abacus Medal](wikipedia://en/21459).[^o7]

:::

He earned his doctorate from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 2008, under the supervision of [Stephen Cook](wikipedia://en/39432). After this, he did post-doctoral research at [Microsoft Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research) and then joined the faculty at University of Toronto. In 2011, he joined the [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) department of computer science.[^o8] In 2014, he was an Invited Speaker with talk *Interactive information and [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869)* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul).[^o9]

Braverman is the son of mathematician [Elena Braverman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Braverman)[^o10] and, through her, the grandson of his co-author, mathematical statistician Yan Petrovich Lumel'skii.[^o11]
