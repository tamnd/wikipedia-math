# Self-linking number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Self-linking number"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **self-linking number** is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) of [framed knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_knot). It is related to the [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999) of curves.

:::

A **framing** of a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) is a choice of a non-zero non-tangent vector at each point of the knot. More precisely, a *framing* is a choice of a non-zero section in the normal bundle of the knot, i.e. a (non-zero) normal vector field. Given a framed knot *C*, the self-linking number is defined to be the [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999) of *C* with a new curve obtained by pushing points of *C* along the framing vectors.

Given a [Seifert surface](wikipedia://en/1343951) for a knot, the associated **Seifert framing** is obtained by taking a tangent vector to the surface pointing inwards and perpendicular to the knot. The self-linking number obtained from a Seifert framing is always zero.[^o2]

The **blackboard framing** of a knot is the framing where each of the vectors points in the vertical (*z*) direction. The self-linking number obtained from the blackboard framing is called the **Kauffman self-linking number** of the knot. This is not a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) because it is only well-defined up to [regular isotopy](wikipedia://en/7404505).
