# Liouville's theorem (conformal mappings)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Liouville's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Liouville's theorem**, proved by [Joseph Liouville](wikipedia://en/341810) in 1850,[^o2] is a [rigidity](wikipedia://en/1458875) theorem about [conformal mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). It states that every [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) conformal mapping on a domain of **R**${}^{n}$, where *n* > 2, can be expressed as a composition of [translations](wikipedia://en/204682), [homotheties](wikipedia://en/386138), [orthogonal transformations](wikipedia://en/105620) and [inversions](wikipedia://en/295844#In_higher_dimensions): they are [Möbius transformations](wikipedia://en/314493#Higher_dimensions) (in *n* dimensions).[^o3][^o4] This theorem severely limits the variety of possible conformal mappings in **R**${}^{3}$ and higher-dimensional spaces. By contrast, conformal mappings in **R**${}^{2}$ can be much more complicated – for example, all nonempty proper [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) planar domains are [conformally equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformally_equivalent), by the [Riemann mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/26215).

:::

Generalizations of the theorem hold for transformations that are only [weakly differentiable](wikipedia://en/1411087) (Iwaniec Martin 2001, Chapter 5). The focus of such a study is the non-linear [Cauchy–Riemann system](wikipedia://en/7583) that is a necessary and sufficient condition for a smooth mapping *f* : Ω → **R**${}^{n}$ to be conformal:

$$Df^\mathrm{T} Df = \left|\det Df\right|^{2/n} I$$

where *Df* is the [Jacobian derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_derivative), *T* is the [matrix transpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_transpose), and *I* is the identity matrix. A weak solution of this system is defined to be an element *f* of the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) *W*${}^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}$(Ω, **R**${}^{n}$) with non-negative Jacobian determinant [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688), such that the Cauchy–Riemann system holds at almost every point of Ω. Liouville's theorem is then that every weak solution (in this sense) is a Möbius transformation, meaning that it has the form

$$f(x) = b + \frac{\alpha A (x-a)}{|x-a|^\varepsilon},\qquad Df = \frac{\alpha A}{|x-a|^\varepsilon}\left(I-\varepsilon\frac{x-a}{|x-a|}\frac{(x-a)^\mathrm{T}}{|x-a|}\right),$$

where *a* is a vector in **R**${}^{n}$ \\ Ω, *b* is a vector in **R**${}^{n}$, *α* is a non-zero scalar, *A* is a [rotation matrix](wikipedia://en/856005), *ε* = 0 or 2, and the matrix in parentheses is *I* or a [Householder matrix](wikipedia://en/485424#Householder_matrix) (so, orthogonal). Equivalently stated, any [quasiconformal map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiconformal_map) of a domain in Euclidean space that is also conformal is a Möbius transformation. This equivalent statement justifies using the Sobolev space *W*${}^{1,n}$, since *f* ∈ *W*${}^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}$(*Ω*, **R**${}^{n}$) then follows from the geometrical condition of conformality and the [ACL characterization](wikipedia://en/611964#Absolutely_continuous_on_lines_%28ACL%29_characterization_of_Sobolev_functions) of Sobolev space. The result is not optimal however: in even dimensions *n* = 2*k*, the theorem also holds for solutions that are only assumed to be in the space *W*${}^{1,k}_{\operatorname{loc}}$, and this result is sharp in the sense that there are weak solutions of the Cauchy–Riemann system in *W*${}^{1,p}$ for any *p* \< *k* that are not Möbius transformations. In odd dimensions, it is known that *W*${}^{1,n}$ is not optimal, but a sharp result is not known.

Similar rigidity results (in the smooth case) hold on any [conformal manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_manifold). The group of conformal isometries of an *n*-dimensional conformal [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) always has dimension that cannot exceed that of the full conformal group SO(*n* + 1, 1). Equality of the two dimensions holds exactly when the conformal manifold is isometric with the [*n*-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_sphere) or [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135). Local versions of the result also hold: The [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of [conformal Killing fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_Killing_equation) in an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) has dimension less than or equal to that of the conformal group, with equality holding [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the open set is locally conformally flat.
