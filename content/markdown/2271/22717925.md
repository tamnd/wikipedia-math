# János Aczél (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="János Aczél"}

Aczél János

**János Dezső Aczél** (; 26 December 1924 – 1 January 2020),[^o11][^o3] also known as **John Aczel**,[^o4] was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people)-[Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) mathematician, who specialized in [functional equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_equations) and [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773).

:::

## Professional career {#Professional_career}

Aczél earned a doctorate in mathematical analysis from the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Budapest), and held positions at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne), [Kossuth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Debrecen), [University of Miskolc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miskolc), and [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged).[^o6] He joined the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) faculty in 1965, eventually becoming Distinguished Professor in the Department of Pure Mathematics.[^o7]

He was the founder of the journal *[Aequationes Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/54589646)*, first published in 1968, and remained its honorary editor-in-chief.[^o8]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Aczél held honorary degrees from the [University of Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology), the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz), and the [University of Silesia in Katowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Silesia).[^o6] In 1971, he was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada).[^o10] He was the 1988 winner of the [Santiago Ramón y Cajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Ramón_y_Cajal) Medal.[^o6] In 1990, he became an external member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o11] and, in 2004, he was honored by the academy as one of the "big five" - five distinguished Hungarian mathematicians born in 1924 and graduating/obtaining a doctorate in 1947. The other honorees were [John Horvath](wikipedia://en/65971885), [Steven Gaal](wikipedia://en/24774553), [Ákos Császár](wikipedia://en/22603037) and [László Fuchs](wikipedia://en/34809998).[^o12] In 2008, he became an honorary member of the [Hamburg Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Mathematical_Society), the oldest active mathematical society in the world.[^o13]

In 2004, he won the Kampé de Fériet Award of the annual Information Processing and Management of Uncertainty conference, "for his pioneering work on the theory of functional equations, with applications in many fields, such as information measures, index numbers, group decision making, aggregation, production functions, laws of science, theory of measurement and utility theory."[^o14]

Issues of the journal *Aequationes Mathematicae* were dedicated to Aczél in 1999, 2005, and 2010, in honor of his 75th, 80th, and 85th birthdays. He died at the age of 95 on 1 January 2020.[^o15][^o16][^o17]
