# Semigroup with two elements

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Semigroup with two elements"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **semigroup with two elements** is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) for which the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the [underlying set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underlying_set) is two. There are exactly five [nonisomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) semigroups having two elements:

:::

- O<sub>2</sub>, the [null semigroup](wikipedia://en/22694038) of order two.
- LO<sub>2</sub>, the [left zero semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_zero_semigroup) of order two.
- RO<sub>2</sub>, the [right zero semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_zero_semigroup) of order two.
- ({0,1}, ∧) (where "∧" is the [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) "[and](wikipedia://en/18152)"), or equivalently the set {0,1} under multiplication: the only [semilattice](wikipedia://en/755647) with two elements and the only non-null semigroup with [zero](wikipedia://en/22694038#Zero_element_in_a_semigroup) of order two, also a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652), and ultimately the [two-element Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/2584449); this is also isomorphic to (Z<sub>2</sub>, ·<sub>2</sub>), the multiplicative group of {0,1} modulo 2.
- (Z<sub>2</sub>, +<sub>2</sub>) (where Z<sub>2</sub> = {0,1} and "+<sub>2</sub>" is "addition modulo 2"), or equivalently ({0,1}, ⊕) (where "⊕" is the logical connective "[xor](wikipedia://en/105979)"), or equivalently the set {−1,1} under multiplication: the only [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of order two.

The semigroups LO<sub>2</sub> and RO<sub>2</sub> are [antiisomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiisomorphic). O<sub>2</sub>, ({0,1}, ∧) and (Z<sub>2</sub>, +<sub>2</sub>) are [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity), and LO<sub>2</sub> and RO<sub>2</sub> are noncommutative. LO<sub>2</sub>, RO<sub>2</sub> and ({0,1}, ∧) are [bands](wikipedia://en/5848270).

## Determination of semigroups with two elements {#Determination_of_semigroups_with_two_elements}

Choosing the set *A* = $\{1, 2\}$ as the underlying set having two elements, sixteen [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948) can be defined in *A*. These operations are shown in the table below. In the table, a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) of the form

::: {#o3 .table cue=table}

| *x* | *y* |
|---|---|
| *z* | *t* |

:::

indicates a binary operation on *A* having the following [Cayley table](wikipedia://en/1802723).

::: {#o4 .table cue=table}

|  | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *x* | *y* |
| **2** | *z* | *t* |

:::

*List of binary operations in $\{1, 2\}$*

| 1 1 1 1 | 1 1 1 2 | 1 1 2 1 | 1 1 2 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Null semigroup** O<sub>2</sub> | ≡ **Semigroup** ($\{0,1\}$, $\wedge$) | 2·(1·2) = 2, (2·1)·2 = 1 | **Left zero semigroup** LO<sub>2</sub> |
| 1 2 1 1 | 1 2 1 2 | 1 2 2 1 | 1 2 2 2 |
| 2·(1·2) = 1, (2·1)·2 = 2 | **Right zero semigroup** RO<sub>2</sub> | ≡ **Group** (Z<sub>2</sub>, ·<sub>2</sub>) | ≡ **Semigroup** ($\{0,1\}$, $\wedge$) |
| 2 1 1 1 | 2 1 1 2 | 2 1 2 1 | 2 1 2 2 |
| 1·(1·2) = 2, (1·1)·2 = 1 | ≡ **Group** (Z<sub>2</sub>, +<sub>2</sub>) | 1·(1·1) = 1, (1·1)·1 = 2 | 1·(2·1) = 1, (1·2)·1 = 2 |
| 2 2 1 1 | 2 2 1 2 | 2 2 2 1 | 2 2 2 2 |
| 1·(1·1) = 2, (1·1)·1 = 1 | 1·(2·1) = 2, (1·2)·1 = 1 | 1·(1·2) = 2, (1·1)·2 = 1 | **Null semigroup** O<sub>2</sub> |

In this table:

- The semigroup ({0,1}, $\wedge$) denotes the two-element semigroup containing the [zero element](wikipedia://en/22694038#Zero_element_in_a_semigroup) 0 and the [unit element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_element) 1. The two binary operations defined by matrices in a green background are associative and pairing either with *A* creates a semigroup isomorphic to the semigroup ({0,1}, $\wedge$). Every element is [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent) in this semigroup, so it is a [band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_%28mathematics%29). Furthermore, it is commutative (abelian) and thus a [semilattice](wikipedia://en/755647). The [order induced](wikipedia://en/755647#Connection_between_the_two_definitions) is a [linear order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_order), and so it is in fact a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) and it is also a [distributive](wikipedia://en/310883) and [complemented lattice](wikipedia://en/753521), i.e. it is actually the [two-element Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/2584449).
- The two binary operations defined by matrices in a blue background are associative and pairing either with *A* creates a semigroup isomorphic to the [null semigroup](wikipedia://en/22694038) O<sub>2</sub> with two elements.
- The binary operation defined by the matrix in an orange background is associative and pairing it with *A* creates a semigroup. This is the [left zero semigroup](wikipedia://en/22694038#Left_zero_semigroup) LO<sub>2</sub>. It is not commutative.
- The binary operation defined by the matrix in a purple background is associative and pairing it with *A* creates a semigroup. This is the [right zero semigroup](wikipedia://en/22694038#Right_zero_semigroup) RO<sub>2</sub>. It is also not commutative.
- The two binary operations defined by matrices in a red background are associative and pairing either with *A* creates a semigroup isomorphic to the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) (Z<sub>2</sub>, +<sub>2</sub>).
- The remaining eight [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948) defined by matrices in a white background are not [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) and hence none of them create a semigroup when paired with *A*.

## The two-element semigroup ({0,1}, ∧) {#The_two-element_semigroup_({0,1},_∧)}

The [Cayley table](wikipedia://en/1802723) for the semigroup ({0,1}, $\wedge$) is given below:

::: {#o6 .table cue=table}

| $\wedge$ | 0 | 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | 0 | 0 |
| **1** | 0 | 1 |

:::

This is the simplest non-trivial example of a semigroup that is not a group. This semigroup has an identity element, 1, making it a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652). It is also commutative. It is not a group because the element 0 does not have an inverse, and is not even a cancellative semigroup because we cannot cancel the 0 in the equation 1·0 = 0·0.

This semigroup arises in various contexts. For instance, if we choose 1 to be the [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) "[true](wikipedia://en/29920)" and 0 to be the [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) "[false](wikipedia://en/229823)" and the operation to be the [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) "[and](wikipedia://en/18152)", we obtain this semigroup in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065). It is isomorphic to the monoid {0,1} under multiplication. It is also isomorphic to the semigroup

$$S = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \right\}$$

under [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280).

## The two-element semigroup (Z<sub>2</sub>, +<sub>2</sub>) {#The_two-element_semigroup_(Z2,_+2)}

The [Cayley table](wikipedia://en/1802723) for the semigroup (Z<sub>2</sub>, +<sub>2</sub>) is given below:

::: {#o8 .table cue=table}

| \+<sub>2</sub> | 0 | 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | 0 | 1 |
| **1** | 1 | 0 |

:::

This group is isomorphic to the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) Z<sub>2</sub> and the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) S<sub>2</sub>.

## Semigroups of order 3 {#Semigroups_of_order_3}

Let *A* be the three-element set $\{1, 2, 3\}$. Altogether, a total of 3<sup>9</sup> = 19683 different binary operations can be defined on *A*. 113 of the 19683 binary operations determine 24 nonisomorphic semigroups, or 18 non-equivalent semigroups (with equivalence being isomorphism or anti-isomorphism). [^o10] With the exception of the [group with three elements](wikipedia://en/52327), each of these has one (or more) of the above two-element semigroups as subsemigroups.[^o11] For example, the set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ under multiplication is a semigroup of order 3, and contains both $\{0, 1\}$ and $\{-1, 1\}$ as subsemigroups.

## Finite semigroups of higher orders {#Finite_semigroups_of_higher_orders}

Algorithms and computer programs have been developed for determining nonisomorphic finite semigroups of a given order. These have been applied to determine the nonisomorphic semigroups of small order.[^o11][^o13][^o14] The number of nonisomorphic semigroups with *n* elements, for *n* a nonnegative integer, is listed under in the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004). lists the number of non-equivalent semigroups, and the number of associative binary operations, out of a total of *n*<sup>*n*<sup>2</sup></sup>, determining a semigroup.
