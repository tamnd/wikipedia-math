# Raynaud surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raynaud surface"}

In mathematics, a **Raynaud surface** is a particular kind of [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) that was introduced in (txt) and named for (txt). To be precise, a Raynaud surface is a [quasi-elliptic surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-elliptic_surface) over an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g* greater than 1, such that all fibers are irreducible and the fibration has a section. The [Kodaira vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/2988093) fails for such surfaces; in other words the Kodaira theorem, valid in algebraic geometry over the complex numbers, has such surfaces as counterexamples, and these can only exist in [characteristic *p*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_p).

:::

**Generalized Raynaud surfaces** were introduced in (Lang 1983), and give examples of surfaces of general type with global vector fields.
