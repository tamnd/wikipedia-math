# Leah Keshet

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Leah Keshet"}

**Leah Edelstein-Keshet** (לאה אדלשטיין-קשת) is an Israeli-Canadian [mathematical biologist](wikipedia://en/662088).

:::

Edelstein-Keshet is known for her contributions to the field of mathematical biology and biophysics.<https://www.smb.org/leah-edelstein-keshet-prize/> Her research spans many topics including sub-cellular biology, ecology, and biomedical research,[^o3] with particular focus on [cell motility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_motility) and the [cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeleton), modeling of physiology and diseases, such as autoimmune diabetes, and swarming and aggregation behavior in social organisms.[^o4]

She is a full-time professor at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) in Vancouver, Canada.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Edelstein-Keshet was born in Israel, and moved to Canada with her parents when she was 12.[^o6] She earned her Bachelor of Science and Master of Science in Mathematics from [Dalhousie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalhousie_University) and received in 1982 her doctorate in Applied Mathematics from the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science) in Israel, where she was supervised by [Lee Segel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Segel).[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Edelstein-Keshet held teaching positions at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) and [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) before joining the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) as an associate professor in 1989, where she is now Associate Head (Faculty Affairs).[^o9] She has authored three books, including *Mathematical Models in Biology* in the SIAM Series and *Classics in Applied Mathematics*. In 1995 she became the first female president of the [Society for Mathematical Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Mathematical_Biology).[^o10]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

In 2003 she was awarded the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055).[^o12] She became a Fellow of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) in 2014 "for contributions to the mathematics and modeling of the cell, the immune system, and biological swarms, as well as to applied mathematics education".[^o13] She was also awarded a Faculty of Science Award for Leadership from the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia). She is the 2022 SIAM John von Neumann Prize Lecturer.[^o14]
