# Nilsemigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nilsemigroup"}

In mathematics, and more precisely in [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) theory, a **nilsemigroup** or **nilpotent semigroup** is a semigroup whose every element is [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Formally, a semigroup *S* is a nilsemigroup if:

- *S* contains *0* and
- for each element *a*∈*S*, there exists a positive integer *k* such that *a*<sup>*k*</sup>=*0*.

### Finite nilsemigroups {#Finite_nilsemigroups}

Equivalent definitions exists for finite semigroup. A finite semigroup *S* is nilpotent if, equivalently:

- $x_1\dots x_n=y_1\dots y_n$ for each $x_i,y_i\in S$, where $n$ is the cardinality of *S*.
- The zero is the only idempotent of *S*.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The trivial semigroup of a single element is trivially a nilsemigroup.

The set of [strictly upper triangular matrix](wikipedia://en/374222#Strictly_triangular_matrix), with matrix multiplication is nilpotent.

Let $I_n=[a,n]$ a bounded interval of positive real numbers. For *x*, *y* belonging to *I*, define $x\star_n y$ as $\min(x+y,n)$. We now show that $\langle I,\star_n\rangle$ is a nilsemigroup whose zero is *n*. For each natural number *k*, *kx* is equal to $\min(kx,n)$. For *k* at least equal to $\left\lceil\frac{n-x}{x}\right\rceil$, *kx* equals *n*. This example generalize for any bounded interval of an [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264158) ordered semigroup.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A non-trivial nilsemigroup does not contain an identity element. It follows that the only nilpotent monoid is the trivial monoid.

The class of nilsemigroups is:

- closed under taking subsemigroups
- closed under taking [quotients](wikipedia://en/27799#Quotients_and_divisions)
- closed under finite products
- but is *not* closed under arbitrary [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887#Direct_product_in_universal_algebra). Indeed, take the semigroup $S=\prod_{n\in\mathbb N}\langle I_n,\star_n\rangle$, where $\langle I_n,\star_n\rangle$ is defined as above. The semigroup *S* is a direct product of nilsemigroups, however its contains no nilpotent element.

It follows that the class of nilsemigroups is not a [variety of universal algebra](wikipedia://en/1021753). However, the set of finite nilsemigroups is a [variety of finite semigroups](wikipedia://en/57151064). The variety of finite nilsemigroups is defined by the profinite equalities $x^\omega y=x^\omega=yx^\omega$.

:::
