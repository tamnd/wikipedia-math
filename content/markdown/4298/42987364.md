# Bott–Samelson resolution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bott–Samelson resolution"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Bott–Samelson resolution** of a [Schubert variety](wikipedia://en/10469987) is a [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115). It was introduced by (Bott Samelson 1958) in the context of [compact Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group).[^n1] The algebraic formulation is independently due to (Hansen 1973) and (Demazure 1974).

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *G* be a connected [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386) complex [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924), *B* a [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) and *T* a [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136) contained in *B*.

Let $w \in W = N_G(T)/T.$ Any such *w* can be written as a product of reflections by simple roots. Fix minimal such an expression:

$$\underline{w} = (s_{i_1}, s_{i_2}, \ldots, s_{i_\ell})$$

so that $w = s_{i_1} s_{i_2} \cdots s_{i_\ell}$. (*ℓ* is the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length_of_a_Weyl_element) of *w*.) Let $P_{i_j} \subset G$ be the subgroup generated by *B* and a representative of $s_{i_j}$. Let $Z_{\underline{w}}$ be the quotient:

$$Z_{\underline{w}} = P_{i_1} \times \cdots \times P_{i_\ell}/B^\ell$$

with respect to the action of $B^\ell$ by

$$(b_1, \ldots, b_\ell) \cdot (p_1, \ldots, p_\ell) = (p_1 b_1^{-1}, b_1 p_2 b_2^{-1}, \ldots, b_{\ell-1} p_\ell b_\ell^{-1}).$$

It is a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469). Writing $X_w = \overline{BwB} / B = (P_{i_1} \cdots P_{i_\ell})/B$ for the Schubert variety for *w*, the multiplication map

$$\pi: Z_{\underline{w}} \to X_w$$

is a [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115) called the Bott–Samelson resolution. $\pi$ has the property: $\pi_* \mathcal{O}_{Z_{\underline{w}}} = \mathcal{O}_{X_w}$ and $R^i \pi_* \mathcal{O}_{Z_{\underline{w}}} = 0, \, i \ge 1.$ In other words, $X_w$ has [rational singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_singularities).[^o8]

There are also some other constructions; see, for example, (Vakil 2006).

:::
