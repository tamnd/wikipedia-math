# Planar projection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Planar projection"}

**Planar projections** are the subset of [3D graphical projections](wikipedia://en/313741) constructed by [linearly mapping](wikipedia://en/18102) points in [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853) to points on a [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional) **projection plane**.[^o2] The projected point on the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) is chosen such that it is [collinear](wikipedia://en/946975) with the corresponding three-dimensional point and the **centre of projection**. The lines connecting these points are commonly referred to as **projectors**.

:::

The centre of projection can be thought of as the location of the observer, while the plane of projection is the surface on which the two dimensional projected [image](wikipedia://en/71925) of the scene is recorded or from which it is viewed (e.g., photographic negative, photographic print, computer monitor). When the centre of projection is at a finite distance from the projection plane, a [perspective](wikipedia://en/380405) projection is obtained. When the centre of projection is at infinity, all the projectors are parallel, and the corresponding subset of planar projections are referred to as **parallel projections**.

## Mathematical formulation {#Mathematical_formulation}

Mathematically, planar projections are [linear transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformations) acting on a point in three-dimensional space $\mathbf{a}_{x,y,z}$ to give a point $\mathbf{b}_{u,v}$ on the projection plane. These transformations consist of various [compositions](wikipedia://en/195947) of the five transformations: [orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052), [rotation](wikipedia://en/410009), [shear](wikipedia://en/799405), [translation](wikipedia://en/204682) and [perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation).

## Map uses {#Map_uses}

It is also used in maps to show the planet Earth and other planets or objects in space. This is good for maps of close-up areas.
