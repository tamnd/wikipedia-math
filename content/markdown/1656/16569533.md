# Pariah group

*Image: SporadicGroups.svg.* Relationships among the sporadic simple groups. The monster group M is at the top, and the groups which are descended from it are the happy family.The six which are not connected by an upward path to M (white ellipses) are the pariahs.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pariah group"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the term **pariah** was introduced by [Robert Griess](wikipedia://en/5034215) in (Griess 1982) to refer to the six [sporadic simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_groups) which are not [subquotients](wikipedia://en/1639294) of the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422).

:::

The twenty groups which are subquotients, including the monster group itself, he dubbed the **happy family**.

For example, the orders of *J*<sub>4</sub> and the Lyons Group *Ly* are divisible by 37. Since 37 does not divide the order of the monster, these cannot be subquotients of it; thus *J*<sub>4</sub> and *Ly* are pariahs. Three other sporadic groups were also shown to be pariahs by Griess in 1982, and the [Janko Group J<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/10796779) was shown to be the final pariah by [Robert A. Wilson](wikipedia://en/4582220) in 1986.

### Lyons group {#Lyons_group}

The [Lyons group](wikipedia://en/1110773), $Ly$, is the unique group (up to [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)) that has in [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) $t$ where $C_G(t)$ is the [covering group](wikipedia://en/1378699) of the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) $A_{11}$, and $t$ is not [weakly closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_closed) in $C_G(t)$. [Richard Lyons](wikipedia://en/35256697), the namesake of these groups, was the first to consider their properties, including their [order](wikipedia://en/493527), and [Charles Sims](wikipedia://en/20022166) proved with machine calculation that such a group must exist and be unique. The group has an order of $2^8 \cdot 3^7 \cdot 5^6 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 \cdot 31 \cdot 37 \cdot 67$.[^o12]

### Rudvalis group {#Rudvalis_group}

The [Rudvalis group](wikipedia://en/1196760) is a finite simple group $R$ that is a rank 3 [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634) on 4060 letters where the [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28group_theory%29) of a point is the [Ree group](wikipedia://en/1739637). The group was described by [Arunas Rudvalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunas_Rudvalis), who proved the existence of such a group. This group has order of $145,926,144,000=2^{14} \cdot 3^3 \cdot 5^3 \cdot 7 \cdot 13 \cdot 29$.[^o15]
