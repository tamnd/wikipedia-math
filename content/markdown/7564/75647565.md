# Modal clausal form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modal clausal form"}

**Modal clausal form**, also known as **separated normal form by modal levels** (SNF<sub>*ml*</sub>)[^o2] and **Mints normal form**,[^o3] is a normal form for [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) formulae.

:::

Such a normal form is commonly used for [automated theorem proving](wikipedia://en/2546) using [tableau calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tableau_calculi) and [resolution calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_calculi) techniques due to its benefits of better space bounds and improved decision procedures. In [normal modal logic](wikipedia://en/911820), any set of formulae can be transformed into an equisatisfiable set of formulae in this normal form.[^o4]

In [multimodal logic](wikipedia://en/11233252) where *a* represents an agent corresponding to an accessibility relation function in [Kripke semantics](wikipedia://en/911960), a formula in this normal form is a conjunction of clauses labelled by the modal level (i.e., the number of nested modalities). Each modal level consists of three forms as follows.

- Literal clause: a disjunction of propositional literals $\bigvee_{b=1}^r l_b$.
- Positive *a*-clause: $l' \to \Box_a l$ where $l'$ and $l$ are propositional literals.
- Negative *a*-clause: $l' \to \Diamond_a l$ where $l'$ and $l$ are propositional literals.

These three forms are also called *cpl*-clauses, *box*-clauses and *dia*-clauses respectively.[^o5] Note that any clause in [conjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73342) (CNF) is also a literal clause in this normal form.
