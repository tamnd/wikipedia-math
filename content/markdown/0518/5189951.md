# Elena Cornaro Piscopia

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elena Cornaro Piscopia"}

**Elena Lucrezia Cornaro Piscopia** (,[^o3] ; 5 June 1646 – 26 July 1684) or **Elena Lucrezia Corner** (, ), also known in English as **Helen Cornaro**, was an Italian [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) of noble descent in the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) who in 1678 became [one of the first women to receive an academic degree from a university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_women_who_obtained_doctoral_degrees_before_1800) and the first appointed a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Elena Cornaro Piscopia was born in the [Palazzo Loredan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca'_Loredan), in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice), on 5 June 1646. She was the third child of Gianbattista Cornaro-Piscopia, a member of the [Cornaro family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornaro_family); and his mistress Zanetta Boni. Her mother was a commoner and her parents were not married at the time of her birth.[^o5][^o6]

In 1664, Elena’s father was chosen to become [Over-Procurator of Saint Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurators_of_Saint_Mark), the treasurer of [St Mark's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mark's_Basilica), a coveted position among [Venetian nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_nobility). That made Gianbattista second only to the [Doge of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge_of_Venice) in terms of precedence.[^o7] Because of this connection, Elena was prominent in the [Marriage of the Sea ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_of_the_Sea_ceremony) held in the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) on the [Feast of the Ascension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Ascension), even though she was born illegitimate. Her father tried to arrange betrothals for her several times, but she rebuffed each man's advances. Early biographers' suggestion that she took a [vow of chastity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_chastity) at age 11 is disputed by Francesco Ludovico Maschietto.[^o8]

In 1665 she took the [religious habit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_habit) of an [oblate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblate) of the [Benedictines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictines), but did not become a [nun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nun).[^o5]

## Education {#Education}

As a young girl Elena was seen as a prodigy. On the advice of Giovanni Fabris, a priest who was a friend of the family, she began a classical education. She studied [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) under distinguished instructors and became proficient in those languages, as well as [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language), by the age of seven.[^o5]

Elena came to be an expert musician, mastering the [harpsichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpsichord), the [clavichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clavichord), the harp and the violin. Her skills were shown by the music that she composed in her lifetime. In her late teens and early twenties, she became interested in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics). [Carlo Rinaldini](wikipedia://en/79882142), her tutor in philosophy and at that point, the Chairman of Philosophy at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), published a book in 1668 written in Latin and centred on geometry. The book was dedicated to a twenty-two-year-old Elena. After the death of her main tutor, Fabris, she became even closer to Rinaldini, who took over her studies.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

In 1669 she translated the *Colloquy of Christ* by [Carthusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthusian) monk [Lanspergius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanspergius) from Spanish into Italian. The translation was dedicated to [Gian Paolo Oliva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Paolo_Oliva), her close friend and [confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessor). The volume was issued in five editions in the Republic from 1669 to 1672. She was invited to be a part of many scholarly societies when her fame spread and in 1670 she became president of the Venetian society Accademia dei Pacifici.[^o11][^o12]

*Image: Cornaro Window.jpg.* [Thompson Memorial Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_Memorial_Library)'s window depicting Cornaro's conferral.

On the recommendation of Carlo Rinaldini, her tutor in philosophy, Felice Rotondi petitioned the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) to grant Cornaro the *[laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea#Former_status_of_the_Laurea_degree)* in theology.[^o13] When Cardinal [Gregorio Barbarigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorio_Barbarigo), the bishop of Padua, learned that she was pursuing a degree in theology, he refused on the grounds that she was a woman.[^o13] However he did allow her to study for a degree in philosophy and after a course of study she received the *laurea* in Philosophy.[^o13] The degree was conferred on 25 June 1678 in [Padua Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua_Cathedral) in the presence of the university authorities, the professors of all the faculties, the students and most of the [Venetian Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Senate), together with many guests from the Universities of [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), [Perugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Perugia), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University) and [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples). Elena spoke for an hour in Classical Latin, explaining difficult passages selected at random from the works of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308): one from the *[Posterior Analytics](wikipedia://en/1692809)* and the other from the *[Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_%28Aristotle%29)*.[^o34] She was listened to with great attention and when she had finished she received plaudits as Professor Rinaldini proceeded to award her the insignia of the laurea: a book of philosophy, a [laurel wreath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_wreath) on her head, a ring on her finger and over her shoulders an ermine [mozzetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozzetta). She was proclaimed Magistra et Doctrix Philosophiae \[teacher and doctor of philosophy\],[^o14] thus becoming [one of the first women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_women_who_obtained_doctoral_degrees_before_1800) to receive an [academic degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree) from a university[^o15].

The last seven years of her life were devoted to study and charity. She died in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) in 1684 of tuberculosis and was buried in the [church of Santa Giustina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_Santa_Giustina).[^o16]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

A few months after Elena's conferral, [Charles Patin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Patin), lecturer in medicine at Padua, applied for his daughter [Gabrielle-Charlotte \[Carla Gabriella\] Patin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrielle-Charlotte_Patin) to begin a degree.[^o18] The university, supported by Gianbattista Cornaro-Piscopia, changed its statutes to prohibit women from graduating. The next female doctorate was granted by the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) in 1732 to [Laura Bassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bassi).[^o19]

Cornaro's death was marked by memorial services in Venice, Padua, [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena) and Rome. The Accademia degli Infecondi published two memorial volumes of tributes by members: one to mark her degree,[^o20] and the other her death.[^o21] Padua's [Accademia dei Ricovrati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Ricovrati) also produced a volume at her death.[^o22]

In 1895 Abbess Mathilda Pynsent of the [English Benedictine Nuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Benedictine_Congregation) in Rome had Cornaro's tomb opened, the remains placed in a new casket, and a suitable tablet inscribed to her memory. Her graduation ceremony is depicted in the Cornaro Window, installed in 1906 in the West Wing of the [Thompson Memorial Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_Memorial_Library) at [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College).[^o23] At the suggestion of [Ruth Crawford Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Crawford_Mitchell), Cornaro is depicted in [Giovanni Romagnoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Romagnoli)'s 1949 mural in the [Italian Nationality Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality_Rooms#Italian) at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh).[^o23] On 5 June 2019, Google celebrated her 373rd birthday with a [Google Doodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Doodle).[^o24]

Earlier biographies of Elena Cornaro include Massimiliano Dezza's *Vita di Helena Lucretia Cornara Piscopia* (Venice: Bosio, 1686) and [Antonio Lupis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Lupis)' *L'eroina veneta* (Venice: Curti, 1689). Her collected works, with a biography, were published four years after her death by [Benedetto Bacchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedetto_Bacchini).[^o25]

In 2022, the Italian authorities refused to add her statue to the 78 statues of famous male scientists in [Prato della Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato_della_Valle) in Padua, arguing that a statue of the scientist already exists elsewhere on the university campus.[^o26] In 2024, the Padua municipality organized a poll for a new female statue, which was won by Elena Cornaro Piscopia with 47.98% of the votes. The statue will be placed in a very central spot in the city.[^o27]

In 2026, the Municipality of Padua issued a call for tenders for the creation of the statue. The location is the historic centre of the city. The fee established for the creation of the work is €65,000.[^o28]

The Piscopia Initiative, named after Cornaro, was founded in Edinburgh in 2019 to tackle the participation crisis of women and non-binary people in mathematics research in the United Kingdom. The Piscopia Initiative encourages *women and non-binary students to pursue a PhD in mathematics*. As of 2023, it has local committees at 19 UK universities and 250 members ranking from undergraduates to Professors.[^o29]
