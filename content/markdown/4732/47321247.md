# Glaeser's continuity theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glaeser's continuity theorem"}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), **Glaeser's continuity theorem** is a characterization of the [continuity](wikipedia://en/6122) of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of the [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208) of functions of class $C^2$. It was introduced in 1963 by [Georges Glaeser](wikipedia://en/37595171),[^o2] and was later simplified by [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084).[^o3]

:::

The theorem states: Let $f\ :\ U \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{+}_0$ be a function of class $C^2$ in an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) *U* contained in $\mathbb{R}^n$, then $\sqrt{f\,}$ is of class $C^1$ in *U* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) of first and second order vanish in the [zeros](wikipedia://en/264210) of *f*.
