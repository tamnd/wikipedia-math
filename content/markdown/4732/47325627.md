# John Watt Butters

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Watt Butters"}

*Image: J Watt Butters.jpg.*

**John Watt Butters** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) FRSGS (1863–1946) was a Scottish mathematician who served as Rector of [Ardrossan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardrossan_Academy) from 1899 to 1928.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

He was born in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) in 1863, the son of Isabella Watt and John Butters, a tailor.[^o3] His early education was at the Old High School in Edinburgh.

Attending [George Heriot’s School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot’s_School) from 1871 to 1876, his headmaster engendered a strong love of mathematics in him, and in later school years allowed him to act as a pupil-teacher, to younger pupils.

Initially studying to be a minister at the Established Free Church College, in conjunction with studies at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), his interests returned fully to mathematics and he graduated MA with First Class Honours in Mathematics and Natural Philosophy in 1894.

## Career and later life {#Career_and_later_life}

After initial teaching roles in [Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth) and [James Gillespies High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gillespies_High_School) he returning as a full-time teacher to [George Heriot’s School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot’s_School) in 1888. He left in 1899 to take the role of Rector (headmaster) at [Ardrossan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardrossan_Academy) where he remained until retiral in 1928.

On retiral he returned to Edinburgh. Here he mixed a highly erudite circle of societies ([Royal Scottish Geographical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Scottish_Geographical_Society), [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh), the [Educational Institute of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Institute_of_Scotland), and the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940)) with more individual and leisurely activities focussing on his love of hill-walking. He was also an active member of the Business Committee of the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).

He died in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 11 January 1946.[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in April 1896, his proposers were [George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803), [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait), [David Fowler Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fowler_Lowe) and [John Sturgeon Mackay](wikipedia://en/54492344).[^o7]

He served as president of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) from 1900 to 1901.[^o8]

## Family {#Family}

He was married to the sister of [Dr Arthur Crichton Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Crichton_Mitchell). They had two daughters and a son.
