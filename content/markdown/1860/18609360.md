# Ultralimit

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ultralimit"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **ultralimit** is a geometric construction that assigns a limit [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) to a sequence of metric spaces $X_n$. The concept captures the limiting behavior of finite configurations in the $X_n$ spaces employing an [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911) to bypass the need for repeated consideration of subsequences to ensure convergence. Ultralimits generalize [Gromov–Hausdorff convergence](wikipedia://en/482366) in metric spaces.

:::

## Ultrafilters {#Ultrafilters}

An [ultrafilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_%28set_theory%29), denoted as **ω**, on the set of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) $\mathbb{N}$ is a set of nonempty subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ (whose [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) can be thought of as a measure) which is closed under finite intersection, upwards-closed, and also which, given any subset *X* of $\mathbb{N}$, contains either *X* or $\mathbb{N} \setminus X.$ An ultrafilter on $\mathbb{N}$ is *non-principal* if it contains no [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742).

## Limit of a sequence of points with respect to an ultrafilter {#Limit_of_a_sequence_of_points_with_respect_to_an_ultrafilter}

In the following, *ω* is a non-principal ultrafilter on $\mathbb N$.

If $(x_n)_{n\in \mathbb N}$ is a sequence of points in a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) (*X*,*d*) and *x*∈ *X*, then the point *x* is called an *ω*-*limit* of *x*<sub>*n*</sub>, denoted as $x=\lim_\omega x_n$, if for every $\epsilon>0$ it holds that

$$\{n: d(x_n,x)\le \epsilon \}\in\omega.$$

It is observed that,

- If an *ω*-[limit of a sequence](wikipedia://en/285773) of points exists, it is unique.
- If $x=\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n$ in the standard sense, $x=\lim_\omega x_n$. (For this property to hold, it is crucial that the ultrafilter should be non-principal.)

A fundamental fact[^o11] states that, if (*X*,*d*) is compact and *ω* is a non-principal ultrafilter on $\mathbb N$, the *ω*-limit of any sequence of points in *X* exists (and is necessarily unique). In particular, any [bounded sequence](wikipedia://en/311509) of real numbers has a well-defined *ω*-limit in $\mathbb R$, as closed intervals are [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

## Ultralimit of metric spaces with specified base-points {#Ultralimit_of_metric_spaces_with_specified_base-points}

Let *ω* be a non-principal ultrafilter on $\mathbb N$. Let (*X*<sub>*n*</sub> ,*d*<sub>*n*</sub>) be a sequence of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) with specified base-points *p*<sub>*n*</sub> ∈ *X*<sub>*n*</sub>.

A sequence $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$, where *x*<sub>*n*</sub> ∈ *X*<sub>*n*</sub>, is *admissible* if the sequence of real numbers (*d*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x<sub>n</sub>* ,*p<sub>n</sub>*))<sub>*n*</sub> is bounded, that is, if there exists a positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) *C* such that $d_n(x_n,p_n)\le C$ for all *n*. Denote the set of all admissible sequences by $\mathcal A$.

It follows from the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941) that for any two admissible sequences $\mathbf x=(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ and $\mathbf y=(y_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ the sequence (*d*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x<sub>n</sub>*,*y<sub>n</sub>*))<sub>*n*</sub> is bounded and hence there exists an *ω*-limit $\hat d_\infty(\mathbf x, \mathbf y):=\lim_\omega d_n(x_n,y_n)$. We define an equivalence relation $\sim$ on the set $\mathcal A$ of all admissible sequences as follows: for $\mathbf x, \mathbf y\in \mathcal A$, one has $\mathbf x\sim\mathbf y$ iff $\hat d_\infty(\mathbf x, \mathbf y)=0.$

The **ultralimit** $\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n, p_n)$ with respect to *ω* of the sequence (*X*<sub>*n*</sub>,*d*<sub>*n*</sub>, *p*<sub>*n*</sub>) is a metric space $(X_\infty, d_\infty)$ defined as follows.[^o5]

As a set, we take $X_\infty=\mathcal A/{\sim}$ .

For two $\sim$-equivalence classes $[\mathbf x], [\mathbf y]$ of admissible sequences $\mathbf x=(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ and $\mathbf y=(y_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$, we define $d_\infty([\mathbf x], [\mathbf y]):=\hat d_\infty(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=\lim_\omega d_n(x_n,y_n).$ This definition is independent of the choice of representative elements. Then $d_\infty$ is a [metric](wikipedia://en/20018) on $X_\infty$.

## On base points in the case of uniformly bounded spaces {#On_base_points_in_the_case_of_uniformly_bounded_spaces}

Suppose that (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) is a sequence of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) of uniformly bounded diameter, that is, there exists a real number *C* > 0 such that diam(*X*<sub>*n*</sub>) ≤ *C* for every $n\in \mathbb N$. Then for any choice *p<sub>n</sub>* of base-points in *X<sub>n</sub>* *every* sequence $(x_n)_n, x_n\in X_n$ is admissible. Therefore, in this situation the choice of base-points does not have to be specified when defining an ultralimit, and the ultralimit $(X_\infty, d_\infty)$ depends only on (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) and on *ω* but does not depend on the choice of a base-point sequence $p_n\in X_n$. In this case one writes $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n)$.

## Basic properties of ultralimits {#Basic_properties_of_ultralimits}

1. If (*X<sub>n</sub>*,*d<sub>n</sub>*) are [geodesic metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space) then $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n, d_n, p_n)$ is also a geodesic metric space.[^o11]
1. If (*X<sub>n</sub>*,*d<sub>n</sub>*) are [complete metric spaces](wikipedia://en/7832) then $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n, p_n)$ is also a complete metric space.[^o8][^o9]

Actually, by construction, the limit space is always complete, even when (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) is a repeating sequence of a space (*X*, *d*) which is not complete.[^o10]

1. If (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) are compact metric spaces that converge to a compact metric space (*X*, *d*) in the [Gromov–Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/482366) sense (this automatically implies that the spaces (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) have uniformly bounded diameter), then the ultralimit $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n)$ is isometric to (*X*, *d*).
1. Suppose that (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) are [proper metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_metric_space) and that $p_n\in X_n$ are base-points such that the pointed sequence (*X*<sub>*n*</sub>, *d<sub>n</sub>*, *p<sub>n</sub>*) converges to a proper metric space (*X*, *d*) in the [Gromov–Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/482366) sense. Then the ultralimit $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n,p_n)$ is isometric to (*X*, *d*).[^o11]
1. Let *κ* ≤ 0 and let (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) be a sequence of [CAT(*κ*)-metric spaces](wikipedia://en/12941912). Then the ultralimit $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n, p_n)$ is also a CAT(*κ*)-space.[^o11]
1. Let (*X<sub>n</sub>*, *d<sub>n</sub>*) be a sequence of [CAT(*κ<sub>n</sub>*)-metric spaces](wikipedia://en/12941912) where $\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa_n=-\infty.$ Then the ultralimit $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n, p_n)$ is [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914).[^o11]

## Asymptotic cones {#Asymptotic_cones}

An important class of ultralimits are the so-called *asymptotic cones* of metric spaces. Let (*X*,*d*) be a metric space, let *ω* be a non-principal ultrafilter on $\mathbb N$ and let *p<sub>n</sub>* ∈ *X* be a sequence of base-points. Then the *ω*–ultralimit of the sequence $(X, \frac{d}{n}, p_n)$ is called the asymptotic cone of *X* with respect to *ω* and $(p_n)_n\,$ and is denoted $Cone_\omega(X,d, (p_n)_n)\,$. One often takes the base-point sequence to be constant, *p<sub>n</sub>* = *p* for some *p ∈ X*; in this case the asymptotic cone does not depend on the choice of *p ∈ X* and is denoted by $Cone_\omega(X,d)\,$ or just $Cone_\omega(X)\,$.

The notion of an asymptotic cone plays an important role in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) since asymptotic cones (or, more precisely, their [topological types](wikipedia://en/13660) and [bi-Lipschitz types](wikipedia://en/51731)) provide [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259) invariants of metric spaces in general and of finitely generated groups in particular.[^o14] Asymptotic cones also turn out to be a useful tool in the study of [relatively hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/21993809) and their generalizations.[^o15]

## Examples {#Examples}

1. Let (*X*,*d*) be a compact metric space and put (*X*<sub>*n*</sub>,*d*<sub>*n*</sub>)=(*X*,*d*) for every $n\in \mathbb N$. Then the ultralimit $(X_\infty, d_\infty)=\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n)$ is isometric to (*X*,*d*).
1. Let (*X*,*d<sub>X</sub>*) and (*Y*,*d<sub>Y</sub>*) be two distinct compact metric spaces and let (*X<sub>n</sub>*,*d<sub>n</sub>*) be a sequence of metric spaces such that for each *n* either (*X<sub>n</sub>*,*d<sub>n</sub>*)=(*X*,*d<sub>X</sub>*) or (*X<sub>n</sub>*,*d<sub>n</sub>*)=(*Y*,*d<sub>Y</sub>*). Let $A_1=\{n | (X_n,d_n)=(X,d_X)\}\,$ and $A_2=\{n | (X_n,d_n)=(Y,d_Y)\}\,$. Thus *A*<sub>1</sub>, *A*<sub>2</sub> are disjoint and $A_1\cup A_2=\mathbb N.$ Therefore, one of *A*<sub>1</sub>, *A*<sub>2</sub> has *ω*-measure 1 and the other has *ω*-measure 0. Hence $\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n)$ is isometric to (*X*,*d<sub>X</sub>*) if *ω*(*A*<sub>1</sub>)=1 and $\lim_\omega(X_n,d_n)$ is isometric to (*Y*,*d<sub>Y</sub>*) if *ω*(*A*<sub>2</sub>)=1. This shows that the ultralimit can depend on the choice of an ultrafilter *ω*.
1. Let (*M*,*g*) be a compact connected [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) of dimension *m*, where *g* is a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on *M*. Let *d* be the metric on *M* corresponding to *g*, so that (*M*,*d*) is a [geodesic metric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space). Choose a base point *p*∈*M*. Then the ultralimit (and even the ordinary [Gromov-Hausdorff limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov-Hausdorff_limit)) $\lim_\omega(M,n d, p)$ is isometric to the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) *T<sub>p</sub>M* of *M* at *p* with the distance function on *T<sub>p</sub>M* given by the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) *g(p)*. Therefore, the ultralimit $\lim_\omega(M,n d, p)$ is isometric to the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb R^m$ with the standard [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric).[^o18]
1. Let $(\mathbb R^m, d)$ be the standard *m*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) with the standard Euclidean metric. Then the asymptotic cone $Cone_\omega(\mathbb R^m, d)$ is isometric to $(\mathbb R^m, d)$.
1. Let $(\mathbb Z^2,d)$ be the 2-dimensional [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402) where the distance between two lattice points is given by the length of the shortest edge-path between them in the grid. Then the asymptotic cone $Cone_\omega(\mathbb Z^2, d)$ is isometric to $(\mathbb R^2, d_1)$ where $d_1\,$ is the [Taxicab metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab_metric) (or **L**<sup>1</sup>-metric) on $\mathbb R^2$.
1. Let (*X*,*d*) be a [*δ*-hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space) geodesic metric space for some *δ*≥0. Then the asymptotic cone $Cone_\omega(X)\,$ is a [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914).[^o11][^o19]
1. Let (*X*,*d*) be a metric space of finite diameter. Then the asymptotic cone $Cone_\omega(X)\,$ is a single point.
1. Let (*X*,*d*) be a [CAT(0)-metric space](wikipedia://en/12941912). Then the asymptotic cone $Cone_\omega(X)\,$ is also a CAT(0)-space.[^o11]

:::
