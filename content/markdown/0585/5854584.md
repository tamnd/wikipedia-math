# Richard Aaron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Aaron"}

**Richard Ithamar Aaron**, (6 November 1901 – 29 March 1987), was a [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people) philosopher who became an authority on the work of [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke). He also wrote a [history of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy#Historical_overview) in the [Welsh language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in [Blaendulais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sisters,_Wales), [Glamorgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamorgan), Aaron was the son of a Welsh [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist) [draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper), William Aaron, and his wife, Margaret Griffith.[^o4] He was educated at [Ystalyfera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ystalyfera) Grammar School,[^o5] then at the [University of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wales) from 1918, where he studied history and philosophy. In 1923 he was elected a Fellow of the university, allowing him to attend [Oriel College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford), where he gained a DPhil in 1928 for a [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) on "The History and Value of the Distinction between Intellect and Intuition".[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

In 1926 Aaron was appointed a lecturer in the Department of Philosophy at the [University College of Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_of_Swansea).[^o6] After the retirement of W. Jenkyn Jones in 1932, Aaron was appointed to the chair of philosophy at [University College of Wales, Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_of_Wales,_Aberystwyth) where he settled, initially on the nearby hill of [Bryn Hir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhos_Bryn_Hir) and later at [Garth Celyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garth_Celyn).[^n1][^o4]

Although his early publications focused on [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology) and the [history of ideas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ideas), Aaron became fascinated with the work and life of [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke). The interest was sparked by his discovery of unexamined information in the Lovelace Collection: notes and drafts left by John Locke to his cousin [Peter King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_King,_1st_Baron_King). There he found letters, notebooks, catalogues, and most pertinently, an early draft of Locke's "[An Essay Concerning Human Understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Essay_Concerning_Human_Understanding)", hitherto presumed missing. Aaron's research led to the 1937 publication of a book on the life and work of Locke that subsequently became an accepted standard work.[^o4] The proofs were read by Rhiannon Morgan, whom Aaron married in 1937. They had five children.[^o4] One of their children is [Welsh literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh-language_literature) specialist [Jane Aaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Aaron_%28educator%29), born in 1951.[^o9][^o10]

Aaron produced several more books and articles, including a book in [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) on the history of philosophy, *Hanes athroniaeth—o [Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes) i [Hegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegel)* in 1932.[^o4] His attempts to boost interest in philosophy in Wales included establishing in that year a philosophy section at the University of Wales Guild of Graduates, which still conducts its proceedings in Welsh.[^o4] From 1938 to 1968 he was the editor of the Welsh journal *[Efrydiau Athronyddol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efrydiau_Athronyddol)* *('Philosophical Studies').*[^o11]

Other notable publications of Aaron's include "Two Senses of the Word Universal" (1939)[^o12] and "Our Knowledge of Universals", a study read to the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy) in 1945 and published in its *[Proceedings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_British_Academy)* and as a separate monograph.[^o13][^o14] Aaron's fascination with the idea of a [universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_%28metaphysics%29) culminated in *The Theory of Universals* (1952).[^o15] Here, he attacks the notion of universals as [Platonic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_realism), but is as critical of [Aristotelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle's_theory_of_universals) realism on [essences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essences) as he is of [nominalism](wikipedia://en/21176) and [conceptualism](wikipedia://en/633037) as theories of universals.[^n2]

In 1952–1953, Aaron was a visiting professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). In 1956, he was able to study the third draft of Locke's *[An Essay Concerning Human Understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Essay_Concerning_Human_Understanding)* at the [Pierpont Morgan Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierpont_Morgan_Library), which led to a substantial addition to the second edition of *John Locke*, published in 1955. He became a [Fellow of the British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_British_Academy) (FBA) and president of the [Mind Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_Association) in the same year. In 1956, the Joint Session of the [Aristotelian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_Society) and Mind Association (publisher of the journal *[Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_%28journal%29)*) was held in [Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth), and Aaron gave the inaugural lecture.[^n3] In 1957 he was elected President of the Aristotelian Society.[^o4]

In 1967, Aaron published a second edition of *The Theory of Universals* with a new preface and several additions and rewritten chapters. In 1971, he published a third edition of his Locke biography and the book *Knowing and the Function of Reason*, which includes broad discussion of the laws of [non-contradiction](wikipedia://en/17636), [excluded middle](wikipedia://en/17637) and [identity](wikipedia://en/1403190), of the use of [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) in [speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech) and [thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought), and of [substance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_theory) and [causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality).[^o4]

After retiring in 1969, he taught for a semester at [Carlton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_College), [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota), before returning to Wales, where he helped to write articles for the 1974 edition of the *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica)*.[^o4] He began to feel the effects of [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease) and died at his home in [Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth) on 29 March 1987.[^o4][^o18]
