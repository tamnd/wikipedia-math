# Romanovski polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Romanovski polynomials"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Romanovski polynomials** are one of three finite subsets of real [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) discovered by [Vsevolod Romanovsky](wikipedia://en/50679801)[^o2] (Romanovski in French transcription) within the context of probability distribution functions in statistics. They form an orthogonal subset of a more general family of little-known **Routh polynomials** introduced by [Edward John Routh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Routh)[^o3] in 1884. The term **Romanovski polynomials** was put forward by Raposo,[^o4] with reference to the so-called 'pseudo-Jacobi polynomials in Lesky's classification scheme.[^o5] It seems more consistent to refer to them as **Romanovski–Routh polynomials**, by analogy with the terms **Romanovski–Bessel** and **Romanovski–Jacobi** used by Lesky for two other sets of orthogonal polynomials.

:::

In some contrast to the standard classical orthogonal polynomials, the polynomials under consideration differ, in so far as for arbitrary parameters only *a finite number of them are orthogonal*, as discussed in more detail below.

## The differential equation for the Romanovski polynomials {#The_differential_equation_for_the_Romanovski_polynomials}

The Romanovski polynomials solve the following version of the [hypergeometric differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_differential_equation)

::: {#o7 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\begin{aligned} &s(x) {R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_n}''(x) + t^{(\alpha,\beta)}_1(x) {R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_n}'(x) + \lambda_n R^{(\alpha,\beta) }_n(x) = 0,\\[4pt] & \qquad x\in (-\infty, +\infty ),\quad s(x)=\left(1+x^2\right), \quad t^{(\alpha,\beta)}_1(x)=2\beta x +\alpha, \quad \lambda_n =-n(2\beta +n-1). \end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

:::

Curiously, they have been omitted from the standard textbooks on [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) in mathematical physics[^o8][^o9] and in mathematics[^o10][^o11] and have only a relatively scarce presence elsewhere in the mathematical literature.[^o12][^o13][^o14]

The [weight functions](wikipedia://en/490990) are

::: {#o15 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x) =\left(1+x^2\right)^{\beta-1} \exp\left(-\alpha \operatorname{arccot} x\right); \tag{2}$$

:::

they solve Pearson's differential equation

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$[s(x) w(x)]' = t(x) w(x), \quad s(x)=1+x^2, \tag{3}$$

:::

that assures the [self-adjointness](wikipedia://en/187408) of the differential operator of the hypergeometric [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753).

For $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta < 0$, the weight function of the Romanovski polynomials takes the shape of the [Cauchy distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_distribution), whence the associated polynomials are also denoted as Cauchy polynomials[^o17] in their applications in random matrix theory.[^o18]

The Rodrigues formula specifies the polynomial $R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)$ as

::: {#o19 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_n(x)=N_n \frac{1}{w^{(\alpha,\beta )}(x)} \frac{\mathrm{d}^n}{\mathrm{d}x^n} \left(w^{(\alpha,\beta) }(x)s(x)^n \right), \quad 0\leq n, \tag{4}$$

:::

where $N_{n}$ is a normalization constant. This constant is related to the coefficient $c_{n}$ of the term of degree $n$ in the polynomial $R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)$ by the expression

::: {#o20 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$N_{n}= \frac{(-1)^{n} n! \, c_n}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \lambda_n^{(k)}},\quad \lambda_n=-n\left({t^{(\alpha,\beta)}_n}' + \tfrac{1}{2}(n-1)s''(x)\right), \tag{5}$$

:::

which holds for $n \ge 1$.

## Relationship between the polynomials of Romanovski and Jacobi {#Relationship_between_the_polynomials_of_Romanovski_and_Jacobi}

As shown by Askey this finite sequence of real orthogonal polynomials can be expressed in terms of [Jacobi polynomials](wikipedia://en/30863587) of imaginary argument and thereby is frequently referred to as complexified Jacobi polynomials.[^o22] Namely, the Romanovski equation ((1)) can be formally obtained from the Jacobi equation,

::: {#o24 .equation cue=numblk name="6"}

$$\begin{aligned} &\left(1-x^2\right) {P_n^{(\gamma,\delta)}}''(x) + t^{(\gamma,\delta)}_1(x) {P_n^{(\gamma,\delta )}}'(x) + \lambda_n P^{(\gamma, \delta)}_n(x) = 0, \\[4pt] &\qquad t^{(\gamma, \delta)}_1(x)=\delta -\gamma -(\gamma +\delta +2)x, \quad \lambda_n= n(n+\gamma +\delta +1),\quad x\in \left[-1,1 \right], \end{aligned} \tag{6}$$

:::

via the replacements, for real $x$,

::: {#o25 .equation cue=numblk name="7"}

$$x\to ix, \quad \frac{\mathrm d}{{\mathrm d}x}\to -i\frac{\mathrm d}{{\mathrm d}x}, \quad \gamma=\delta^\ast =\beta -1 +\frac{\alpha i}{2}, \tag{7}$$

:::

in which case one finds

::: {#o26 .equation cue=numblk name="8"}

$$R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_n(x) = i^n P^{\left(\beta - 1 + \frac{i}{2}\alpha,\beta -1 - \frac{i}{2}\alpha\right)}_n(ix), \tag{8}$$

:::

(with suitably chosen normalization constants for the Jacobi polynomials). The complex Jacobi polynomials on the right are defined via (1.1) in Kuijlaars *et al.* (2003)[^o27] which assures that ((8)) are real polynomials in x. Since the cited authors discuss the non-hermitian (complex) orthogonality conditions only for real Jacobi indexes the overlap between their analysis and definition ((8)) of Romanovski polynomials exists only if α = 0. However examination of this peculiar case requires more scrutiny beyond the limits of this article. Notice the invertibility of ((8)) according to

::: {#o28 .equation cue=numblk name="9"}

$$P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_n(x) = (-i)^n R^{\left(i(\alpha-\beta), \frac{1}{2}(\alpha+\beta)+1)\right)}_n(-ix), \tag{9}$$

:::

where, now, $P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)$ is a real Jacobi polynomial and

$$R^{\left(i(\alpha-\beta), \frac{1}{2}(\alpha+\beta)+1)\right)}_n(-ix)$$

would be a complex Romanovski polynomial.

::: {#o30 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties of Romanovski polynomials"}

## Properties of Romanovski polynomials {#Properties_of_Romanovski_polynomials}

### Explicit construction {#Explicit_construction}

For real $\alpha, \beta$ and $n = 0, 1, 2, ...$, a function $R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)$ can be defined by the Rodrigues formula in Equation ((4)) as

::: {#o32 .equation cue=numblk name="10"}

$$R_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x) \equiv \frac{1}{w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)} \frac{{\rm d}^n}{{\rm d}x^n}\left( w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x) s(x)^n \right), \tag{10}$$

:::

where $w^{(\alpha,\beta)}$ is the same weight function as in ((2)), and $s(x) = 1 + x^{2}$ is the coefficient of the second derivative of the [hypergeometric differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_differential_equation) as in ((1)).

Note that we have chosen the normalization constants $N_{n} = 1$, which is equivalent to making a choice of the coefficient of highest degree in the polynomial, as given by equation ((5)). It takes the form

::: {#o33 .equation cue=numblk name="11"}

$$c_n = \frac{1}{n!} \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \bigl ( 2 \beta (n-k) + n(n-1) - k(k-1)\bigr ), \quad n\geq 1. \tag{11}$$

:::

Also note that the coefficient $c_{n}$ does not depend on the parameter $\alpha$, but only on $\beta$ and, for particular values of $\beta$, $c_{n}$ vanishes (i.e., for all the values

$$\beta=\frac{k(k-1) - n(n-1)}{2(n-k)}$$

where $k = 0, ..., n - 1$). This observation poses a problem addressed below.

For later reference, we write explicitly the polynomials of degree 0, 1, and 2,

$$\begin{aligned} R_0^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x) & = 1, \\[6pt] R^{(\alpha ,\beta )}_1(x) & = \frac{1}{w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)} \left(w'^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)s(x)+s'(x)w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\right)\\[6pt] & = t^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=2\beta x+\alpha,\\[6pt] R^{(\alpha ,\beta )}_2(x) & = \frac{1}{w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)}\frac{\mathrm d}{{\mathrm d}x} \left(s^2(x) w'^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)+2s(x)s'(x)w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\right)\\ & = \frac{1}{w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)}\frac{\mathrm d}{{\mathrm d}x}\left(s(x)w^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x) \left(t^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)+s'(x)\right)\right)\\[6pt] & = \left(2x+t^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\right) t^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)+\left(2+t'^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\right)s(x)\\[6pt] & = (2\beta+1)(2\beta+2) x^2 + 2(2\beta+1)\alpha x + \left(2\beta + \alpha^2 +2\right), \end{aligned}$$

which derive from the Rodrigues formula ((10)) in conjunction with Pearson's ODE ((3)).

### Orthogonality {#Orthogonality}

The two polynomials, $R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{m}(x)$ and $R^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)$ with $m \ne n$, are orthogonal,[^o4]

::: {#o35 .equation cue=numblk name="12"}

$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} w^{(\alpha, \beta )}(x)R_m^{(\alpha,\beta )}(x)R_n^{(\alpha,\beta )}(x)=0 \tag{12}$$

:::

if and only if,

::: {#o36 .equation cue=numblk name="13"}

$$m+n< 1-2\beta. \tag{13}$$

:::

In other words, for arbitrary parameters, only a finite number of Romanovski polynomials are orthogonal. This property is referred to as *finite orthogonality*. However, for some special cases in which the parameters depend in a particular way on the polynomial degree infinite orthogonality can be achieved.

This is the case of a version of equation ((1)) that has been independently encountered anew within the context of the exact solubility of the quantum mechanical problem of the [trigonometric Rosen–Morse potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_Rosen–Morse_potential) and reported in Compean & Kirchbach (2006).[^o37] There, the polynomial parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are no longer arbitrary but are expressed in terms of the potential parameters, $a$ and $b$, and the degree $n$ of the polynomial according to the relations,

::: {#o38 .equation cue=numblk name="14"}

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha\to \alpha_n=\frac{2b}{n+1+a}, \quad \beta \to \beta_n= -(a+n+1) +1, \quad n=0,1,2,\ldots, \infty. \end{aligned} \tag{14}$$

:::

Correspondingly, $\lambda_{n}$ emerges as $\lambda_{n} = -n(2a + n - 1)$, while the weight function takes the shape

$$\left(1+x^2\right)^{-(a+n+1) }\exp\left(-\frac{2b}{n+a+1} \operatorname{arccot} x\right).$$

Finally, the one-dimensional variable, $x$, in Compean & Kirchbach (2006)[^o37] has been taken as

$$x=\cot\left( \frac{r}{d}\right),$$

where $r$ is the radial distance, while $d$ is an appropriate length parameter. In Compean & Kirchbach[^o37] it has been shown that the family of Romanovski polynomials corresponding to the infinite sequence of parameter pairs,

::: {#o39 .equation cue=numblk name="15"}

$$\left(\alpha_1,\beta_1\right),\left(\alpha_2\beta_2\right),\ldots, \left(\alpha_n\beta_n\right),\ldots, \quad n \longrightarrow \infty , \tag{15}$$

:::

is orthogonal.

### Generating function {#Generating_function}

In Weber (2007)[^o41] polynomials $Q^{(\alpha_{n}, \beta_{n} + n)}_{\nu}(x)$, with $\beta_{n} + n = -a$, and complementary to $R^{(\alpha_{n}, \beta_{n})}_{n}(x)$ have been studied, generated in the following way:

::: {#o42 .equation cue=numblk name="16"}

$$Q_\nu^{\left(\alpha_n, \beta_n+n\right)}(x)= \frac{1}{w^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_n +n-\nu \right)}} \frac{{\mathrm d}^\nu}{{\mathrm d}x^\nu} w^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_n\right)}(x)\left(1+x^2\right)^n. \tag{16}$$

:::

In taking into account the relation,

::: {#o43 .equation cue=numblk name="17"}

$$w^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_n\right)}(x)\left(1+x^2\right)^\delta =w^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_n+\delta \right) }(x), \tag{17}$$

:::

Equation ((16)) becomes equivalent to

::: {#o44 .equation cue=numblk name="18"}

$$\begin{aligned} Q_\nu^{\left(\alpha_n, \beta_n+n\right)}(x)& = \frac{1}{w^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_n +n-\nu\right)}} \frac{{\mathrm d}^\nu}{{\mathrm d}x^\nu} w^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_n +n-\nu\right)}(x)\left(1+x^2\right)^\nu\\[4pt] &=R_\nu^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_n+n-\nu\right)}(x), \end{aligned} \tag{18}$$

:::

and thus links the complementary to the principal Romanovski polynomials.

The main attraction of the complementary polynomials is that their [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) can be calculated in closed form.[^o45] Such a [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993), written for the Romanovski polynomials based on Equation ((18)) with the parameters in ((14)) and therefore referring to infinite orthogonality, has been introduced as

::: {#o46 .equation cue=numblk name="19"}

$$G^{\left(\alpha_n, \beta_n\right)}(x,y) =\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}R_\nu^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_{n}+n-\nu \right)}(x)\frac{y^\nu}{\nu !}. \tag{19}$$

:::

The notational differences between Weber[^o41] and those used here are summarized as follows:

- $G^{(\alpha_{n}, \beta_{n})}(x,y)$ here versus $Q(x,y;\alpha,-a)$ there, $\alpha$ there in place of $\alpha_{n}$ here,
- $a = -\beta_{n} - n$, and
- $Q^{(\alpha,-a)}_{\nu}(x)$ in Equation (15) in Weber[^o41] corresponding to $R^{(\alpha_{n}, \beta_{n} + n - \nu)}_{\nu}(x)$ here.

The generating function under discussion obtained in Weber[^o41] now reads:

::: {#o47 .equation cue=numblk name="20"}

$$G^{(\alpha_n, \beta_n)}(x,y)= \left(1+x^2\right)^{-\beta_n -n +1}\exp\left(\alpha_n\operatorname{arccot} x\right) \left( 1+\left(x+y\left(1+x^2\right)\right)^2\right)^{-\left(-\beta_{n}-n +1\right)}\exp\left(-\alpha_n\operatorname{arccot} \left(x+y\left(1+x^2\right)\right)\right. \tag{20}$$

:::

:::

## Recurrence relations {#Recurrence_relations}

[Recurrence relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurrence_relations) between the infinite orthogonal series of Romanovski polynomials with the parameters in the above equations ((14)) follow from the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993),[^o41]

::: {#o49 .equation cue=numblk name="21"}

$$\nu\bigl(\nu+1-2(\beta_n+n)\bigr)R_{\nu-1}^{\left(\alpha_n, \beta_{n}+n-\nu+1\right)}(x)+\frac{\mathrm d}{{\mathrm d}x}R_\nu^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_{n}+n-\nu\right)}(x)=0, \tag{21}$$

:::

and

::: {#o50 .equation cue=numblk name="22"}

$$R^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_{n}+n-\nu -1\right)}_{\nu+ 1}(x) = \bigl(\alpha_n -2x(-\beta_n-n+\nu +1) \bigr) R_\nu^{\left(\alpha_n, \beta_{n}+n-\nu\right)} -\nu \left(1+x^2\right)\bigl(2(-\beta_n -n) +\nu +1\bigr) R_{\nu -1} ^{\left(\alpha_n,\beta_{n}+n -\nu +1\right)}, \tag{22}$$

:::

as Equations (10) and (23) of Weber (2007)[^o41] respectively.
