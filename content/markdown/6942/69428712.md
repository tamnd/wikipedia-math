# Susanne Dierolf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susanne Dierolf"}

**Susanne Dierolf** (16 July 1942 – 24 April 2009) was a German mathematician specializing in the theory of [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752). She was a professor for many years at the [University of Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Trier).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Dierolf was born on 16 July 1942 in [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava), at the time under German occupation and administered as part of [Lower Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Austria).

She completed her doctorate in 1974 at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich), with the dissertation *Über Vererbbarkeitseigenschaften in topologischen Vektorräumen* supervised by Walter Roelcke. She continued at LMU Munich as an assistant, earning her habilitation there in 1985. She became a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at Trier in 1985, and [außerplanmäßiger Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Außerplanmäßiger_Professor) in 1991.

She died on 24 April 2009.

## Research {#Research}

Dierolf published 71 mathematics papers and was the advisor to ten doctoral students. Highlights of her research contributions include the solution of four problems of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) and of a conjecture of Dmitriĭ A. Raĭkov. Her work often involved the construction of [counterexamples](wikipedia://en/143151), for which she became known as "Mrs. Counterexample".

Beyond the main part of her work on [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752), she was also a coauthor of a book on [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) theory, *Uniform structures on topological groups and their quotients* (with Walter Roelcke, McGraw-Hill, 1981).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

A special volume of the journal *Functiones et Approximatio Commentarii Mathematici* was published in Dierolf's memory in 2011.
