# Veeravalli S. Varadarajan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Veeravalli S. Varadarajan"}

**Veeravalli Seshadri[^o3] Varadarajan** (18 May 1937 – 25 April 2019)[^o4][^o5][^o6] was an Indian mathematician at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), who worked in many areas of mathematics, including probability, [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and their [representations](wikipedia://en/12695), quantum mechanics, differential equations, and supersymmetry.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Varadarajan's father, Seshadri, was an Inspector of Schools in the Department of Education. He was transferred to Madras where the medium of instruction was generally English. After Varadarajan completed his high school studies, he joined Intermediate for two years at [Loyola College, Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_College,_Chennai) where he was taught mathematics by K.A. Adivarahan, a very strict disciplinarian who made a strong impression on him.[^o8][^o9] Varadarajan received his undergraduate degree in 1957 from [Presidency College, Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_College,_Chennai) and his doctorate in 1960 from the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418) in Calcutta, under the supervision of [C. R. Rao](wikipedia://en/1418009).[^o10] He was one of the "famous four"[^o11] at the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418) during 1956-1963 (the others being [Ramaswamy Ranga Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramaswamy_Ranga_Rao), [K. R. Parthasarathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._R._Parthasarathy_%28probabilist%29), and [S. R. Srinivasa Varadhan](wikipedia://en/9638038)).

In 1960 Varadarajan went to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as a [post-doctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-doctoral_fellow) and in the Fall of 1960 he went to the [University of Washington, Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington,_Seattle) where he spent the academic year, followed by a year at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) at NYU. He returned to the Indian Statistical Institute in 1962.[^o12]

He joined the Department of Mathematics at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) in 1965. Varadarajan was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) during the periods September 1968 until December 1968 and January 1976 until June 1976.[^o13]

In March 2019, it was announced by UCLA that Varadarajan and his wife had donated \$1 million to the Department of Mathematics at UCLA to establish the Ramanujan Visiting Professorship.[^o14]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Varadarajan's early work, including his doctoral thesis, was in the area of probability theory. He then moved into [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) where he has done some of his best known work. He has also done work in mathematical physics, in particular quantum theory and p-adic themes in physics. In the 1980s, he wrote a series of papers with [Donald Babbitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Babbitt) on the theory of differential equations with irregular singularities. His latest work has been in supersymmetry.

He introduced [Kostant–Parthasarathy–Ranga Rao–Varadarajan determinants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostant–Parthasarathy–Ranga_Rao–Varadarajan_determinant) along with [Bertram Kostant](wikipedia://en/3520590), [K. R. Parthasarathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._R._Parthasarathy_%28probabilist%29) and [R. Ranga Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Ranga_Rao_%28mathematician%29) in 1967,[^o16] the [Trombi–Varadarajan theorem](wikipedia://en/34666424)[^o17] in 1972 and the [Enright–Varadarajan modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enright–Varadarajan_module)[^o18] in 1975.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

He was awarded the [Onsager Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsager_Medal) in 1998 for his work. He was recognized along with 23 Indian and Indian American members "who have made outstanding contributions to the creation, exposition advancement, communication, and utilization of mathematics" by the Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) program on 1 November 2012.[^o20]
