# Vasily Vladimirov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vasily Vladimirov"}

**Vasily Sergeyevich Vladimirov** (Васи́лий Серге́евич Влади́миров; 9 January 1923 – 3 November 2012) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the fields of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), [generalized functions](wikipedia://en/1580246), [several complex variables](wikipedia://en/360243), [p-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), [multidimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Tauberian theorems](wikipedia://en/411990).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Vladimirov was born to a peasant family of 5 children, in 1923, [Petrograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrograd). Under the impact of food shortage and poverty, he began schooling in 1930. He then went to a 7-year school in 1934, but transferred to the Leningrad Technical School of Hydrology and Meteorology in 1937. In 1939, at the age of sixteen, he enrolled into a night preparatory school for workers, and finally successfully progressed to Leningrad University to study physics.

During the Second World War, Vladimirov took part in defence of Leningrad against German invasion, building defences, working as a tractor driver and as meteorologist in Air Force after training. He served in several different units, mainly as part of air-defense system of Leningrad. He was given the rank of sergeant major in the reserves after the war and permitted to return to his study.

When he returned to university, Vladimirov shifted his focus of interest from physics to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Under the advice of Boris Alekseevich Venkov (1900-1962), an expert on [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478), he started undertaking research in number theory and attained a master's degree in 1948. In the first thesis of his master study in Leningrad, he confirmed the existence of non-extreme perfect quadratic form in six variables in Georgy Fedoseevich Voronoy's conjecture. In his second thesis, he approached packing problems for convex bodies initiated by [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537). Upon graduation, he was appointed as a junior researcher in the Leningrad Branch of the [Steklov Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Mathematical_Institute) of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences).

As the Soviet atomic bomb programme ran, Vladimirov was assigned to assist with the development of the bomb, in joint force with many top scientists and industrialists. He worked with Vitalevich Kantorovich calculating critical parameters of certain simple nuclear systems. In 1950, when he was sent to [Arzamas-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arzamas_16), he worked under the direction of [Nikolai Nikolaevich Bogolyubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Nikolaevich_Bogolyubov), who later became a long-term collaborator with Vladimirov. In Arzamas-16, Vladimirov worked on finding mathematical solutions for problems raised by physicists. He developed new techniques for the numerical solution of boundary value problems, especially for solving the kinetic equation of neutron transfer in nuclear reactors in 1952, which is now known as [Vladimirov method](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Vladimirov_method).

After the success of the bomb project, Vladimirov was awarded the Stalin Prize in for his contribution 1953. He continued working on mathematics for atomic bomb in the Central Scientific Research Institute for Artillery Armaments, where he served as Senior Researcher in 1955. Vladimirov moved to Steklov Mathematical Institute, Moscow, in 1956, under the supervision of [Nikolay Nikolaevich Bogolyubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Nikolaevich_Bogolyubov).[^o4] There he started working on new mathematical branches for solving problems in quantum field theory. He defended his doctoral thesis in 1958, which contains the renowned 'Vladimirov variational principle'.[^o5]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- Vladimirov, V. S. (1966). "Methods of the theory of functions of several complex variables. With a foreword of N.N. Bogolyubov". The M.I.T. Press. p. XII+353. mr:0201669. zbl:0125.31904 ([Zentralblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentralblatt) review of the original [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) edition). One of the first modern monographs on the theory of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), being different from other ones of the same period due to the extensive use of [generalized functions](wikipedia://en/1580246).
- Vladimirov, V. S. (1979). "Generalized functions in mathematical physics". Mir Publishers. p. 362. isbn:978-0-8285-0001-2. mr:0564116. zbl:0515.46034. A textbook on the theory of [generalized functions](wikipedia://en/1580246) and their applications to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).
- Vladimirov, V.S. (1983). "Equations of mathematical physics". Mir Publishers. p. 464. mr:0764399. zbl:0207.09101 ([Zentralblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentralblatt) review of the first [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) edition).
- Vladimirov, V.S.; Drozzinov, Yu.N.; Zavialov, B.I. (1988). "Tauberian theorems for generalized functions". 10. Kluwer Academic Publishers. p. XV+293. isbn:978-90-277-2383-3. mr:0947960. zbl:0636.40003.
- Vladimirov, V.S. (2002). "Methods of the theory of generalized functions". 6. Taylor & Francis. p. XII+353. isbn:978-0-415-27356-5. mr:2012831. zbl:1078.46029. <https://books.google.com/books?id=hlumB8fkX0UC&q=Methods+of+the+theory+of+generalized+functions&pg=PR1>. A monograph on the theory of [generalized functions](wikipedia://en/1580246) written with an eye towards their applications to [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), as is customary for the Author: it is a substantial revision of the [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) (Vladimirov 1979).
