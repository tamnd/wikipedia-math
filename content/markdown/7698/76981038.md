# Louis Bertrand (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Louis Bertrand"}

*Image: Louis Bertrand (1731-1812).png.* Louis Bertrand

**Louis Bertrand** (; 3 October 1731 – 15 May 1812) was a Genevan [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bertrand was born on 3 October 1731 in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Republic of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Geneva), the son of Antoine Bertrand, a merchant and banker, and Madeleine Lafont.[^o3] He studied in Geneva and under [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin).[^o3] Bertrand was a professor of mathematics at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) from 1761 to 1795, and was its [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) in 1783.[^o3] He became a member of Geneva's [Council of Two Hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Two_Hundred) in 1784, and served in the National Assembly in 1795 during the Genevan revolutionary period.[^o3]

In 1778, Bertrand published the work *Developpement nouveau de la partie elementaire des mathematiques*, which included a demonstration of [Euclid's postulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_postulates) that gained fame before the rise of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) and influenced most of the [elementary geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_geometry) treatises of the 19th century.[^o3] In 1774, he published the *De l'instruction publique* in open opposition to [Horace Bénédict de Saussure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Bénédict_de_Saussure)'s *Projet de réforme pour le [Collège de Genève](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_Genève)*.[^o3] He worked, besides in Geneva, also in Berlin, [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London).[^o4] Bertrand died on 15 May 1812 in Geneva, aged 80.[^o3]
