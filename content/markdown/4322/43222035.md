# Collapsing algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Collapsing algebra"}

In mathematics, a **collapsing algebra** is a type of [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959) sometimes used in [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) to reduce ("collapse") the size of [cardinals](wikipedia://en/6173). The [posets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) used to generate collapsing algebras were introduced by [Azriel Lévy](wikipedia://en/18329294) in 1963.[^n1]

:::

The collapsing algebra of λ<sup>ω</sup> is a [complete Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/1839944) with at least λ elements but generated by a countable number of elements. As the size of countably generated complete Boolean algebras is unbounded, this shows that there is no [free](wikipedia://en/494669) complete Boolean algebra on a countable number of elements.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

There are several slightly different sorts of collapsing algebras.

If κ and λ are cardinals, then the Boolean algebra of [regular open sets](wikipedia://en/47306) of the [product space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_space) κ<sup>λ</sup> is a collapsing algebra. Here κ and λ are both given the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology). There are several different options for the topology of κ<sup>λ</sup>. The simplest option is to take the usual product topology. Another option is to take the topology generated by open sets consisting of functions whose value is specified on less than λ elements of λ.

:::
