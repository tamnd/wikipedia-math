# Alfred Aeppli

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Aeppli"}

**Alfred Aeppli** was a Swiss mathematician. The [Pólya–Aeppli distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya–Aeppli_distribution) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) is named after him and his doctoral advisor [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Alfred Aeppli was born in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) on 15 July 1894 to Alfred Aeppli and Rosa Aeppli-Gehring. He went to a primary school in Zürich and the [canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zurich)'s Industrial School, where he received his [matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura) in the summer of 1913. Afterwards, Aeppli studied at the [Eidgenössische Technische Hochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) (ETH Zürich) at the department for higher teachers of mathematics and physics. In the winter semester of 1914–1915 he was on leave for military service. After receiving his [Diplom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom), he worked at a private school in Germany for a year and returned to the ETH in the spring of 1919 as a research assistant of [Arthur Hirsch](wikipedia://en/60442250).

Aeppli earned his doctorate in 1924 under the supervision of George Pólya and [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544). He came up with the Pólya–Aeppli distribution in his doctoral dissertation.[^o5] This discovery was published by Pólya in 1930, and he credited its discovery to his student Aeppli.[^o6] The Pólya–Aeppli distribution, now also known as the geometric Poisson distribution,[^o7] is a particular case of the [compound Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/949562), and is used to describe objects that come in clusters, where the number of clusters follows a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) and the number of objects within a cluster follows a [geometric distribution](wikipedia://en/45922).[^o8]
