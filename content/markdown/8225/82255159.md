# Universal hypothesis testing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Universal hypothesis testing"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **universal hypothesis testing** is a special case of binary [simple hypothesis testing](wikipedia://en/30284). The universal problem is to distinguish between a simple null hypothesis $H_0: Q = P$, and the most general composite alternative $H_1: Q \neq P$, using [independent and identically distributed](wikipedia://en/30876902) samples from $Q$. The setting is sometimes referred to as [goodness of fit](wikipedia://en/2474821) testing, or one-sample testing.

:::

A simple binary hypothesis testing problem involves distinguishing between $H_0: Q = P_0$ and $H_1: Q = P_1$, using samples $X_1, \dots, X_n \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} Q$. In the traditional setting of hypothesis testing $P_0, P_1$ are known apriori. A composite version of this problem involves sets of probability distributions $\Omega_0, \Omega_1$, and asks to distinguish between $H_0: Q \in \Omega_0$ and $H_1: Q \in \Omega_1$. In contrast, the universal setting corresponds to the special case of composite hypothesis testing, where the null hypothesis is simple, $\Omega_0 = P$ and the alternative hypothesis is the set of all distributions other than $P$, $\Omega_1 = \{F: F \neq P\}$. For example, someone might want to know if a particular coin was fair, i.e. $\mathbb{P}[X = H] = \frac{1}{2} = \mathbb{P}[X = T]$ or not, i.e. $\mathbb{P}[X = H] \neq \mathbb{P}[X=T]$, where $H,T$ denote the coin coming up heads or tails.

The [asymptotics](wikipedia://en/60531312) of universal hypothesis testing were first discussed in Hoeffding's work on optimal tests for multinomial distributions.[^o2] There have been many subsequent works on the topic[^o3][^o4][^o5] in many directions. While Hoeffding's initial results were restricted to distributions with finite supports, later results developed solutions for continuous distributions using extensions of the [Kullback-Leibler Divergence](wikipedia://en/1115052),[^o6] or [kernel methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_embedding_of_distributions#Measuring_distance_between_distributions).[^o7][^o8]
