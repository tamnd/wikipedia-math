# Szegő limit theorems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Szegő limit theorems"}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), the **Szegő limit theorems** describe the asymptotic behaviour of the [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468) of large [Toeplitz matrices](wikipedia://en/151569).[^o2][^o4] They were first proved by [Gábor Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065).

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

Let $w$ be a [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) with Fourier coefficients $c_k$, relating to each other as

$$w(\theta) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} c_k e^{i k \theta}, \qquad \theta \in [0,2\pi],$$

$$c_k = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} w(\theta) e^{-ik\theta} \, d\theta,$$

such that the $n\times n$ Toeplitz matrices $T_n(w) = \left(c_{k-l}\right)_{0\leq k,l \leq n-1}$ are [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682), i.e., $T_n(w)=T_n(w)^\ast$, or equivalently $c_{-k}=\overline{c_k}$. Then both $w$ and the eigenvalues $(\lambda_m^{(n)})_{0\leq m \leq n-1}$ of $T_n(w)$ are real-valued and the determinant of $T_n(w)$ is given by

$$\det T_n(w) = \prod_{m=1}^{n-1} \lambda_m^{(n)}.$$

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Szegő theorem"}

## Szegő theorem {#Szegő_theorem}

Under suitable assumptions the Szegő theorem states that

$$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty}\frac{1}{n} \sum_{m=0}^{n-1}F(\lambda_m^{(n)}) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} F(w(\theta))\, d\theta$$

for any function $F$ that is continuous on the range of $w$. In particular

::: {#o9 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty}\frac{1}{n} \sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\lambda_m^{(n)} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} w(\theta)\, d\theta < \infty \tag{1}$$

:::

i.e. the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) of $\lambda^{(n)}$ converges to the integral of $w$, i.e. $c_0$.[^o10]

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="First Szegő theorem"}

### First Szegő theorem {#First_Szegő_theorem}

The first Szegő theorem[^o2][^o4][^o13] states that, if right-hand side of ((1)) holds and $w \geq 0$, then

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\det T_n(w)\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\det T_n(w)}{\det T_{n-1}(w)} = \exp \left( \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \log w(\theta) \, d\theta \right) \tag{2}$$

:::

holds for $w > 0$ and $w\in L^1$. The RHS of ((2)) is the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of $w$ (well-defined by the [arithmetic-geometric mean inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic-geometric_mean_inequality)).

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Second Szegő theorem"}

### Second Szegő theorem {#Second_Szegő_theorem}

Let $\widehat c_k$ be the Fourier coefficient of $\log w \in L^{1}$, written as

$$\widehat c_k = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \log (w(\theta)) e^{-ik\theta} \, d\theta$$

The second (or strong) Szegő theorem[^o2][^o17] states that, if $w \geq 0$, then

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\det T_n(w)}{e^{(n+1) \widehat c_0}} = \exp \left( \sum_{k=1}^\infty k \left| \widehat c_k\right|^2 \right).$$

:::
