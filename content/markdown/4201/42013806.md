# Littlewood's Tauberian theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Littlewood's Tauberian theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Littlewood's Tauberian theorem** is a strengthening of [Tauber's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauber's_theorem) introduced by (txt).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Littlewood showed the following: If *a*<sub>*n*</sub> = [O](wikipedia://en/44578)(1/*n* ), and as *x* ↑ 1 we have

$$\sum a_n x^n \to s,$$

then

$$\sum a_n = s.$$

Hardy and Littlewood later showed that the hypothesis on *a*<sub>*n*</sub> could be weakened to the "one-sided" condition *a*<sub>*n*</sub> ≥ –*C*/*n* for some constant *C*. However in some sense the condition is optimal: Littlewood showed that if *c*<sub>*n*</sub> is any unbounded sequence then there is a series with |*a*<sub>*n*</sub>| ≤ |*c*<sub>*n*</sub>|/*n* which is divergent but Abel summable.

:::

## History {#History}

(Littlewood 1953) described his discovery of the proof of his Tauberian theorem. [Alfred Tauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Tauber)'s original theorem was similar to Littlewood's, but with the stronger hypothesis that *a*<sub>*n*</sub>=[o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_O)(1/*n*). Hardy had proved a similar theorem for Cesàro summation with the weaker hypothesis *a*<sub>*n*</sub>=O(1/*n*), and suggested to Littlewood that the same weaker hypothesis might also be enough for Tauber's theorem. In spite of the fact that the hypothesis in Littlewood's theorem seems only slightly weaker than the hypothesis in Tauber's theorem, Littlewood's proof was far harder than Tauber's, though [Jovan Karamata](wikipedia://en/3130497) later found an easier proof.

Littlewood's theorem follows from the later [Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem](wikipedia://en/22846993), which is in turn a special case of [Wiener's Tauberian theorem](wikipedia://en/4106793), which itself is a special case of various abstract Tauberian theorems about [Banach algebras](wikipedia://en/4665).
