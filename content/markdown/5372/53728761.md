# Kathryn Hess

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kathryn Hess"}

**Kathryn Pamela Hess** (born 1967) is an American mathematician who has served as professor of mathematics at [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (EPFL) since 1999. She is known for her work on [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), both pure and applied. In particular, she applies the methods of algebraic topology to the study of [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience), cancer biology, and materials science. She is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Kathryn Hess was born 21 September 1967 in [Bryn Mawr, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr,_Pennsylvania). She began to accelerate in mathematics in 1979, thanks to the Mathematical Talent Development Project (MTDP) set up in [Eau Claire, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin), by her parents, through the Association for High Potential Children, which they also founded. Both programs are currently defunct. Hess earned a BSc with honors in mathematics from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1985. She received her doctorate in mathematics from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1989 under the direction of David J. Anick. Her dissertation was entitled *A Proof of Ganea's Conjecture for Rational Spaces*.H91

## Work {#Work}

Hess has worked and written extensively on topics in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) including [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/212250), [model categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_categories)H02 and [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500).HS She has also used the methods of algebraic topology and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) to investigate homotopical generalizations of [descent theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent_theory)H10 and Hopf–Galois extensions.H09 In particular, she has studied generalizations of these structures for [ring spectra](wikipedia://en/21118494) and [differential graded algebras](wikipedia://en/2415863).

She has more recently used algebraic topology to understand structures in [neurology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology)KDSDHL and [materials science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science).LBD

As of March 2025, she has been the principal academic advisor for 21 mathematics PhDs at EPFL, including 9 women.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In addition to her strong publication record, Hess has been widely recognized for her pedagogical abilities. She received the Agepoly prize for best teacher in the Basic Sciences Faculty in 2005, the Credit Suisse prize for best EPFL teacher in 2012, and the "Polysphere d'Or" Agepoly prize for best teacher at EPFL in 2013.[^o6]

She was named an individual member of the Swiss Academy of Engineering Sciences in 2016 and a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for "contributions to homotopy theory, applications of topology to the analysis of biological data, and service to the mathematical community" in 2017. In 2017, she received an award as a distinguished speaker of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470). She delivered one of the public lectures during the Eighth European Congress of Mathematics on 21 June 2021. Hess was named a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the class of 2024 "for her support of women in mathematics via innovative and impactful programs, including her role in founding and sustaining the Women in Topology program; for her exceptional mentoring; and for her commitment to gender diversity throughout her many leadership roles in the mathematics profession."[^o7] She was a visiting member of the [IBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Basic_Science) Center for Geometry and Physics.[^o8]
