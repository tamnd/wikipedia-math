# Shihoko Ishii

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shihoko Ishii"}

**Shihoko Ishii** (石井志保子, born 1950)[^o3] is a Japanese mathematician and professor at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo). Her research area is [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Ishii received her bachelor's degree from [Tokyo Women's Christian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Women's_Christian_University) in 1973 and her master's degree from [Waseda University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waseda_University) in 1975. She later earned her PhD from [Tokyo Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_University) in 1983.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Ishii's research focuses on [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763). She studies arc spaces, a mathematical concept related to [jets](wikipedia://en/1521924): arc spaces are [varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) encapsulating information about curves on another variety.[^o6]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Ishii received the [Saruhashi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saruhashi_Prize) for accomplishments by a Japanese woman researcher in the natural sciences in 1995.[^o9] As a postdoc, Ishii was inspired by reading a profile of [Fumiko Yonezawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumiko_Yonezawa), a physicist and former winner of the Saruhashi prize.[^o6]

Ishii received the Algebra Prize from the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786) in 2011.[^o10]

In 2022, she became a laureate of the [Asian Scientist 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist_100) by the *[Asian Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist)*.[^o11]
