# Deanna Haunsperger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Deanna Haunsperger"}

**Deanna Haunsperger** (born 1964) is an American mathematician. She was the president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for the 2017–2018 term.[^o12] She is a professor of mathematics at [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College).[^o8]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Haunsperger received her [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) from [Simpson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson_College) in 1986. She then received her master's degree in mathematics in 1989 and her Ph.D. in mathematics in 1991 from [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University).[^o4] Her dissertation was entitled *Projection and Aggregation Paradoxes in Nonparametrical Statistical Tests* and her advisor was [Donald Gene Saari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Gene_Saari).[^o5][^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Haunsperger was an assistant professor of mathematics at [St. Olaf College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Olaf_College) from 1991 to 1994.[^o4] Since 1994, she has been a faculty member in the mathematics department at [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College).[^o8][^o4]

From 1995 to 2014, Haunsperger and mathematician Stephen Kennedy directed the Carleton College Summer Mathematics Program for Women. This program worked to prepare undergraduate women to pursue a Ph.D. in mathematics.[^o9][^o10]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

From 1999 to 2003, Haunsperger and Kennedy were co-editors of *[Math Horizons](wikipedia://en/21340642)*, a magazine aimed at undergraduate students who are interested in mathematics.[^o12]

Haunsperger was the second vice-president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) from 2006 to 2008. She was elected president of the MAA for the 2017–2018 term.[^o12]

Haunsperger has won several awards from the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM). In 2012, she was selected for the [M. Gweneth Humphreys Award](wikipedia://en/51370132), which recognizes mathematics educators who have exhibited outstanding mentorship.[^o13] She was presented with the second annual AWM Presidential Award in 2017.[^o14] In 2017, she was selected as a fellow of the AWM in the inaugural class.[^o15] She is the 2021 winner of the Yueh-Gin Gung and Dr. Charles Y. Hu Award for Distinguished Service to Mathematics of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) "for her prolific service to mathematics, including with the Mathematical Association of America; for her influential leadership of women in mathematics; for her long focus on inclusion and on building inclusive mathematical communities; and for a laudable career that has been rich in mathematical research, mathematical education, and mathematical exposition".[^o16]
