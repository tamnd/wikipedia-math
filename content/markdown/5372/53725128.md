# Joan Ferrini-Mundy

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Joan Ferrini-Mundy"}

**Joan Ferrini-Mundy** (born July 14, 1954)[^o4] is an American academic. Her research interests include [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) teaching and learning, mathematics teacher learning, and [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663) education policy. She is currently the president of the [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine).

:::

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Ferrini-Mundy earned a Ph.D. in mathematics education from the [University of New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Hampshire) (UNH) in 1980 and spent two years there as a postdoctoral associate. After one year at [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College), she returned to UNH as a faculty member in mathematics until joining the faculty of [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) in 1999. One year later, she chaired the writing group for *Standards 2000*, a publication from the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691).[^o6]

Ferrini-Mundy served as director of the Mathematical Sciences Education Board at the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences), and subsequently joined the mathematics and teacher education faculty of [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) and served as associate dean for science and mathematics education in the College of Natural Science and director of the Division of Science and Mathematics Education.

In 2007, Ferrini-Mundy joined the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (NSF) as the director of the new Division of Research on Learning in Formal and Informal Settings in the Directorate for Education and Human Resources; she remained a faculty member at Michigan State until 2010. From 2007 to 2009, she served on the education subcommittee of the [National Science and Technology Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_and_Technology_Council).[^o7]

In February 2011, Ferrini-Mundy became the assistant director of the National Science Foundation's Directorate for Education and Human Resources.[^o8] In this strategic role, she set the NSF's direction for scientific education.[^o8] In 2014 she was elected to the executive committee of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) for a 2-year term.[^o9]

In June 2017, she was appointed the [chief operating officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer) of the NSF. One year later, she left the NSF to become the 21st president of the [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Machias).[^o10]

After becoming the president of the [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Machias) in 2018, in 2021 she was also appointed University of Maine System (UMS) vice chancellor for research and innovation where she leads a formalized effort to make the University of Maine’s research infrastructure accessible to and supportive of all universities and faculty in the system.[^o10]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

In 2000, Ferrini-Mundy was the recipient of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698)' [Louise Hay Award](wikipedia://en/51270001).[^o6][^o12]

In 2011, Ferrini-Mundy was elected as a Fellow at the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science)[^o13] She was elected to the 2018 class of [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14]

In 2022, Ferrini-Mundy was appointed to the President’s Committee on the National Medal of Science[^o15]

In 2023, Ferrini-Mundy received the Lifetime Achievement Award from the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691).[^o16] The award recognizes her service to the mathematics education community spanning multiple levels, state, regional, national, and international and go well beyond the responsibilities of her jobs.
