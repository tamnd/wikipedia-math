# Animal Math

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Animal Math"}

***Animal Math*** is an educational book series featuring baby animals that introduces reader to mathematic principles. The series consists of five books: *Tiger Math* (2000), *Chimp Math* (2002), *Polar Bear Math* (2004), *Panda Math* (2005), and *Cheetah Math* (2007). On the right-hand pages, the books tell stories of baby animals cared for at either the [Denver Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Zoo) or [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo) who needed extra care when they were young. The left-hand pages discuss basic mathematic concepts using the animals as examples in problems.

:::

Published by [Henry Holt and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Holt_and_Company), the books are authored by Ann Whitehead Nagda, with support from Cindy Bickel, an assistant at the Denver Zoo, or in collaboration with the San Diego Zoo.

## Books {#Books}

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Tiger Math"}

### *Tiger Math* (2000) {#Tiger_Math_(2000)}

*Tiger Math: Learning to Graph from a Baby Tiger* (2000), co-written by Ann Whitehead Nagda and Cindy Bickel, teaches students about different types of graphs, including [bar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_graph), [line graphs](wikipedia://en/675231), picture graphs, and [pie charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pie_charts).[^o6] The book features the [Siberian tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_tiger) cub T. J., who was born at the Denver Zoo, then struggled to eat after his mother died.[^o7]

*Tiger Math* is a [Junior Library Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Library_Guild) book.[^o8] *Kirkus Reviews* said the book provided "a delightful way to learn math".[^o7]

:::

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Chimp Math"}

### *Chimp Math* (2002) {#Chimp_Math_(2002)}

*Chimp Math: Learning about Time from a Baby Chimpanzee* (2002), co-written by Ann Whitehead Nagda and Cindy Bickel, teaches students about time, such as timelines and telling time on a clock.[^o11] The book features Jiggs, a baby [chimpanzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimpanzee) at the Denver Zoo who was ignored by his mother. Jiggs is kept in the zoo's nursery during the day and stays with Bickel in the evenings. While in the nursery, he spends time with a [jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar) cub named Giorgio, before returning to the chimpanzee enclosure.[^o12]

*Kirkus Reviews* praised *Chimp Math*, highlighting her strength of "combining multiple disciplines and teaching in a non-threatening, as-you-need-it manner".[^o12]

:::

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Polar Bear Math"}

### *Polar Bear Math* (2004) {#Polar_Bear_Math_(2004)}

*Polar Bear Math: Learning about Fractions from Klondike and Snow* (2004), co-written by Ann Whitehead Nagda and Cindy Bickel, teaches students about [fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractions).[^o15][^o16] The book features the polar bear cubs Klondike and Snow, who were cared for at the [Denver Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Zoo_Conservation_Alliance) after being abandoned. Along with a vet and another assistant at the zoo, Bickel learns how to care for the polar bears, who eventually thrive.[^o17]

*Polar Bear Math* is a Junior Library Guild book.[^o18]

:::

::: {#o20 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Panda Math"}

### *Panda Math* (2005) {#Panda_Math_(2005)}

*Panda Math: Learning about Subtraction from Hua Mei and Mei Sheng* (2005), written by Ann Whitehead Nagda in collaboration with the [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo), teaches students about [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141). The mathematical text discusses different ways to subtract,[^o21] including "regrouping, subtracting each place value, thinking about doubles and adding up".*[^o22]* The story features Hua Mei and Mei Sheng, panda cubs at the San Diego Zoo.*[^o22]*

According to *[Kirkus Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews)*, Nagda explains the different approaches to solving subtraction problems well, though they concede that "children will need additional support".*[^o22]*

:::

### *Cheetah Math* (2007) {#Cheetah_Math_(2007)}

*Cheetah Math: Learning about Division from Baby Cheetahs* (2007), written by Ann Whitehead Nagda in collaboration with the [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo), teaches students about [division](wikipedia://en/53696). The book features the baby [cheetahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheetah) Majani and Kubali, who were raised at the San Diego Zoo.[^o24]

*Cheetah Math* is a Junior Library Guild book.[^o25] Sally Woolsey called the book "well done" and it is a popular item in many elementary school libraries. *[Kirkus Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews)* called the book "a great addition to both the math and wild-animal conservation bookshelves".[^o24] The *[School Library Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_Library_Journal)* also gave a favorable review, saying *Cheetah Math* "is a wonderful cross-curricular book and an appealing way to introduce math".[^o26]
