# Rodney Hill

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rodney Hill"}

**Rodney Hill** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o11] (11 June 1921 – 2 February 2011)[^o3] was an [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and a former Professor of Mechanics of Solids at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge).

:::

## Career {#Career}

In 1953 he was appointed Professor of Applied Mathematics at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham). His 1950 *The Mathematical Theory of Plasticity* work[^o5] forms the foundation of [plasticity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasticity_theory). Hill is widely regarded as among the foremost contributors to the foundations of solid mechanics over the second half of the 20th century. His early work was central to founding the mathematical theory of plasticity. This deep interest led eventually to general studies of uniqueness and stability in nonlinear [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918), work which has had a profound influence on the field of solid mechanics—theoretical, computational and experimental alike—over the past decades. Hill was the founding editor of the *[Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_Mechanics_and_Physics_of_Solids)*, still among the principal journals in the field.[^o6]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Hill's work is recognized worldwide for its concise style of presentation and exemplary standards of scholarship. Publisher [Elsevier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsevier), in collaboration with [IUTAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUTAM), established a quadrennial award in the field of solid mechanics, known as the Rodney Hill Prize, first presented at ICTAM in [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide) in August 2008. The prize consists of a plaque and a cheque for US\$25,000. Its first recipient is Michael Ortiz, for his contribution to nonconvex plasticity and deformation microstructures (California Institute of Technology, USA).[^o8][^o9]

Hill won the [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal) in 1993 for his contribution to the theoretical [mechanics of soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_mechanics) and the [plasticity](wikipedia://en/240123) of solids.[^o10] He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1961).[^o11][^o12] He was awarded an Honorary Degree (Doctor of Science) by the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) in 1978.

## Death {#Death}

He died on 2 February 2011.[^o11]
