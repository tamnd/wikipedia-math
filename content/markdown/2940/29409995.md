# Jordan–Schur theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jordan–Schur theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Jordan–Schur theorem** also known as **Jordan's theorem on finite linear groups** is a theorem in its original form due to [Camille Jordan](wikipedia://en/355931). In that form, it states that there is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) *ƒ*(*n*) such that given a finite [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *G* of the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) GL(*n*, **C**) of [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) *n*-by-*n* [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859), there is a subgroup *H* of *G* with the following properties:

:::

- *H* is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974).
- *H* is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*.
- The index of *H* in *G* satisfies (*G* : *H*) ≤ *ƒ*(*n*).

[Schur](wikipedia://en/617522) proved a more general result that applies when *G* is not assumed to be finite, but just [periodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_group). Schur showed that *ƒ*(*n*) may be taken to be

((8*n*)<sup>1/2</sup> + 1)<sup>2*n*<sup>2</sup></sup> − ((8*n*)<sup>1/2</sup> − 1)<sup>2*n*<sup>2</sup></sup>.[^o2]

A tighter bound (for *n* ≥ 3) is due to [Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Speiser), who showed that as long as *G* is finite, one can take

*ƒ*(*n*) = *n*! 12<sup>*n*(*π*(*n*+1)+1)</sup>

where *π*(*n*) is the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826).[^o2][^o3] This was subsequently improved by [Hans Frederick Blichfeldt](wikipedia://en/33390154) who replaced the 12 with a 6. Unpublished work on the finite case was also done by [Boris Weisfeiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Weisfeiler).[^o4] Subsequently, [Michael Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Collins), using the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), showed that in the finite case, one can take *ƒ*(*n*) = (*n* + 1)! when *n* is at least 71, and gave near complete descriptions of the behavior for smaller *n*.

## Generalization to arbitrary fields {#Generalization_to_arbitrary_fields}

The theorem has also been extended to finite subgroups of general linear groups over arbitrary fields - with a more complicated albeit analogous statement - by Collins[^o6] and separately Larsen and Pink.[^o7] Collins's paper includes exact bounds and depends on the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445); Larsen and Pink used a different approach that avoids depending on the CFSG.
