# Herbert Enderton

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Herbert Enderton"}

**Herbert Bruce Enderton** (April 15, 1936 – October 20, 2010)[^o3] was an American mathematician. He was a [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) and a former member of the faculties of Mathematics and of Logic and the Methodology of Science at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

:::

Enderton also contributed to [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory), the theory of definability, models of analysis, [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/7543), and the [history of logic](wikipedia://en/59945).[^o4]

He earned his Ph.D. at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1962.[^o5] He was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1961 until his death.[^o3]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He lived in [Santa Monica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica). He married his wife, Cathy, in 1961 and they had two sons; Eric and Bert.[^o7]

## Death {#Death}

He died from [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia) in 2010.[^o7]
