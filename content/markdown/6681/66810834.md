# Extended natural numbers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Extended natural numbers"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **extended natural numbers** is a set which contains the values $0, 1, 2, \dots$ and $\infty$ (infinity). That is, it is the result of adding a [maximum element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_element) $\infty$ to the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474). Addition and multiplication work as normal for finite values, and are extended by the rules $n+\infty=\infty+n=\infty$ ($n\in\mathbb{N}\cup \{\infty\}$), $0\times \infty=\infty \times 0=0$ and $m\times \infty=\infty\times m=\infty$ for $m\neq 0$.

:::

With addition and multiplication, $\mathbb{N}\cup \{\infty\}$ is a [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468) but not a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), as $\infty$ lacks an [additive inverse](wikipedia://en/228312).[^n1] The set can be denoted by $\overline{\mathbb{N}}$, $\mathbb{N}_\infty$ or $\mathbb{N}^\infty$.[^n2][^n3][^n4] It is a subset of the [extended real number line](wikipedia://en/51698), which extends the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) by adding $-\infty$ and $+\infty$.[^n2]

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the extended natural numbers are used to define distances in [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), with $\infty$ being the distance between two [unconnected](wikipedia://en/1614492) vertices.[^n2] They can be used to show the extension of some results, such as the [max-flow min-cut theorem](wikipedia://en/78130), to infinite graphs.[^n5]

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the [Grothendieck topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_topos) of right [actions](wikipedia://en/1058218) on the extended natural numbers is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) **PRO** of [projection](wikipedia://en/3017382) algebras.[^n4]

In [constructive mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_mathematics), the extended natural numbers $\mathbb{N}_\infty$ are a [one-point compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-point_compactification) of the natural numbers, yielding the set of [non-increasing](wikipedia://en/27838#Increasing_and_decreasing) [binary sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_sequence) i.e. $(x_0,x_1,\dots)\in 2^\mathbb{N}$ such that $\forall i\in\mathbb{N}: x_i\ge x_{i+1}$. The sequence $1^n 0^\omega$ represents $n$, while the sequence $1^\omega$ represents $\infty$. It is a [retract](wikipedia://en/2120001) of $2^\mathbb{N}$ and the claim that $\mathbb{N}\cup \{\infty\}\subseteq \mathbb{N}_\infty$ implies the [limited principle of omniscience](wikipedia://en/37440876).[^n3]

:::
