# Anderson's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anderson's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Anderson's theorem** is a result in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) which says that the [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) of an integrable, symmetric, unimodal, non-negative function *f* over an *n*-dimensional [convex body](wikipedia://en/8652020) *K* does not decrease if *K* is translated inwards towards the origin. This is a natural statement, since the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of *f* can be thought of as a hill with a single peak over the origin; however, for *n* ≥ 2, the proof is not entirely obvious, as there may be points *x* of the body *K* where the value *f*(*x*) is larger than at the corresponding translate of *x*.

:::

Anderson's theorem, named after [Theodore Wilbur Anderson](wikipedia://en/22021377), also has an interesting application to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *K* be a convex body in *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**${}^{n}$ that is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/53741) with respect to reflection in the origin, i.e. *K* = −*K*. Let *f* : **R**${}^{n}$ → **R** be a non-[negative](wikipedia://en/154616), symmetric, globally integrable function; i.e.

- *f*(*x*) ≥ 0 for all *x* ∈ **R**${}^{n}$;
- *f*(*x*) = *f*(−*x*) for all *x* ∈ **R**<sup>*n*</sup>;
- $$\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}} f(x) \, \mathrm{d} x < + \infty.$$

Suppose also that the super-[level sets](wikipedia://en/559622) *L*(*f*, *t*) of *f*, defined by

$$L(f, t) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{n} | f(x) \geq t \},$$

are [convex subsets](wikipedia://en/6292) of **R**<sup>*n*</sup> for every *t* ≥ 0. (This property is sometimes referred to as being **unimodal**.) Then, for any 0 ≤ *c* ≤ 1 and *y* ∈ **R**<sup>*n*</sup>,

$$\int_{K} f(x + c y) \, \mathrm{d} x \geq \int_{K} f(x + y) \, \mathrm{d} x.$$

:::

## Application to probability theory {#Application_to_probability_theory}

Given a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) (Ω, Σ, Pr), suppose that *X* : Ω → **R**${}^{n}$ is an **R**${}^{n}$-valued [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) *f* : **R**<sup>*n*</sup> → \[0, +∞) and that *Y* : Ω → **R**${}^{n}$ is an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) random variable. The probability density functions of many well-known probability distributions are *p*-[concave](wikipedia://en/263321) for some *p*, and hence unimodal. If they are also symmetric (e.g. the [Laplace](wikipedia://en/1106213) and [normal distributions](wikipedia://en/21462)), then Anderson's theorem applies, in which case

$$\Pr ( X \in K ) \geq \Pr ( X + Y \in K )$$

for any origin-symmetric convex body *K* ⊆ **R**<sup>*n*</sup>.
