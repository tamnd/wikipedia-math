# Alexander Pfänder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Pfänder"}

**Alexander Pfänder** (7 February 1870 – 18 March 1941) was a German philosopher who was a member of the [Munich phenomenological school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_phenomenological_school).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Pfänder was born in [Iserlohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iserlohn) and spent his entire academic career at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), where he was a student of [Theodor Lipps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Lipps) and one of the founding members of the [Munich circle of phenomenologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_phenomenology). As a professor, Pfänder was also influential in conveying and promoting a version of phenomenology that differed from [Edmund Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl)'s "[transcendental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenology_%28philosophy%29#Transcendental_phenomenology_after_the_Ideen_.281913.29)" orientation. His early phenomenological analysis of [willing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_%28philosophy%29) (1900) in fact predated Husserl's breakthrough in phenomenology (*Logical Investigations*, vol. II (1901)).

In spite of his talents as a writer and a teacher, Pfänder did not come into prominence as did [Heidegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidegger) with *[Being and Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_and_Time)* (1927) and has consequently been overshadowed by subsequent developments out of Heideggerian and Husserlian orientations. Nevertheless, his detailed analyses of various phenomena, such as willing and attitudes (*Gesinnungen*), have been undeservedly ignored. Moreover, his development of the concept of an "understanding psychology" also merits attention, which has not received its due to its treatment in a work with an unfashionable title (*The Soul of Man*, 1933).

He died in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich).

## Works {#Works}

- *Phänomenologie des Wollens: Eine psychologische Analyse* (1900).
- *Logik* (1921); English translation: *Logic* Frankfurt, Ontos Verlag (2009).
- *Die Seele des Menschen* (1933).
