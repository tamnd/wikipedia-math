# Mittag-Leffler star

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mittag-Leffler star"}

*Image: ML-star.png.* Illustration of the Mittag-Leffler star (the region bounded by the blue contour). The original disk *U* is centered at *a*.

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Mittag-Leffler star** of a [complex-analytic function](wikipedia://en/14110) is a set in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) obtained by attempting to extend that function along [rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28mathematics%29) emanating from a given point. This concept is named after [Gösta Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/1438792).

:::

## Definition and elementary properties {#Definition_and_elementary_properties}

Formally, the Mittag-Leffler star of a complex-analytic function *ƒ* defined on an [open disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_disk) *U* in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) centered at a point *a* is the set of all points *z* in the complex plane such that *ƒ* can be continued analytically along the [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) joining *a* and *z* (see [analytic continuation along a curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_continuation_along_a_curve)).

It follows from the definition that the Mittag-Leffler star is an open [star-convex set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star-convex_set) (with respect to the point *a*) and that it contains the disk *U*. Moreover, *ƒ* admits a single-valued [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) to the Mittag-Leffler star.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The Mittag-Leffler star of the complex [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) defined in a neighborhood of *a* = 0 is the entire complex plane.
- The Mittag-Leffler star of the [complex logarithm](wikipedia://en/4870290) defined in the neighborhood of point *a* = 1 is the entire complex plane without the origin and the negative real axis. In general, given the complex logarithm defined in the neighborhood of a point *a* ≠ 0 in the complex plane, this function can be extended all the way to infinity on any ray starting at *a*, except on the ray which goes from *a* to the origin, one cannot extend the complex logarithm beyond the origin along that ray.
- Any open star-convex set is the Mittag-Leffler star of some complex-analytic function, since any open set in the complex plane is a [domain of holomorphy](wikipedia://en/3236377).

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

*Image: ML expansion illustration.png.* An illustration of the regions of convergence of the Mittag-Leffler expansion and the Taylor series expansion around *a* (the regions bounded by the blue curve and red circle respectively).

Any complex-analytic function *ƒ* defined around a point *a* in the complex plane can be expanded in a [series](wikipedia://en/15287) of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) which is convergent in the entire Mittag-Leffler star of *ƒ* at *a*. Each polynomial in this series is a linear combination of the first several terms in the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) expansion of *ƒ* around *a*.

Such a series expansion of *ƒ*, called the **Mittag-Leffler expansion**, is convergent in a larger set than the Taylor series expansion of *ƒ* at *a*. Indeed, the largest open set on which the latter series is convergent is a disk centered at *a* and contained within the Mittag-Leffler star of *ƒ* at *a*.

:::
