# Shephard's problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shephard's problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Shephard's problem**, is the following geometrical question asked by [Geoffrey Colin Shephard](wikipedia://en/33354532) in 1964: if *K* and *L* are centrally symmetric [convex bodies](wikipedia://en/8652020) in *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) such that whenever *K* and *L* are [projected](wikipedia://en/3909097) onto a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862), the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of the projection of *K* is smaller than the volume of the projection of *L*, then does it follow that the volume of *K* is smaller than that of *L*?[^n1]

:::

In this case, "centrally symmetric" means that the [reflection](wikipedia://en/1390873) of *K* in the origin, *−K*, is a translate of *K*, and similarly for *L*. If $\pi$<sub>*k*</sub> : **R**<sup>*n*</sup> → Π<sub>*k*</sub> is a [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) of **R**<sup>*n*</sup> onto some *k*-dimensional [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) Π<sub>*k*</sub> (not necessarily a coordinate hyperplane) and *V*<sub>*k*</sub> denotes *k*-dimensional volume, Shephard's problem is to determine the truth or falsity of the implication

$$V_{k} (\pi_{k} (K)) \leq V_{k} (\pi_{k} (L)) \mbox{ for all } 1 \leq k < n \implies V_{n} (K) \leq V_{n} (L).$$

*V*<sub>*k*</sub>($\pi$<sub>*k*</sub>(*K*)) is sometimes known as the **brightness** of *K* and the function *V*<sub>*k*</sub> o $\pi$<sub>*k*</sub> as a (*k*-dimensional) **brightness function**.

In dimensions *n* = 1 and 2, the answer to Shephard's problem is "yes". In 1967, however, Petty and Schneider showed that the answer is "no" for every *n* ≥ 3.[^n2][^n3] The solution of Shephard's problem requires [Minkowski's first inequality for convex bodies](wikipedia://en/10614602) and the notion of [projection bodies](wikipedia://en/33350956) of convex bodies.
