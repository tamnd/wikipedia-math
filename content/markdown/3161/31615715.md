# Deligne cohomology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deligne cohomology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Deligne cohomology**, sometimes called **Deligne-Beilinson cohomology**, is the [hypercohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercohomology) of the **Deligne complex** of a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742). It was introduced by [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) in unpublished work in about 1972 as a cohomology theory for [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties) that includes both ordinary cohomology and [intermediate Jacobians](wikipedia://en/3092994).

:::

For introductory accounts of Deligne cohomology, see (Brylinski 2008, section 1.5), (Esnault Viehweg 1988), and (Gomi 2009, section 2).

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The analytic Deligne complex **Z**(*p*)<sub>D, an</sub> on a complex analytic manifold *X* is

> $$0\rightarrow \mathbf Z(p)\rightarrow \Omega^0_X\rightarrow \Omega^1_X\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow \Omega_X^{p-1} \rightarrow 0 \rightarrow \dots$$

where **Z**(*p*) = (2π i)<sup>*p*</sup>**Z**. Depending on the context, $\Omega^*_X$ is either the complex of smooth (i.e., *C*<sup>∞</sup>) [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) or of holomorphic forms, respectively. The Deligne cohomology *H*(*X*,**Z**(*p*)) is the *q*-th hypercohomology of the Deligne complex. An alternative definition of this complex is given as the homotopy limit[^o7] of the diagram

> $$\begin{matrix} & & \mathbb{Z} \\ & & \downarrow \\ \Omega_X^{\bullet \geq p} & \to & \Omega_X^\bullet \end{matrix}$$

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Deligne cohomology groups *H*(*X*,**Z**(*p*)) can be described geometrically, especially in low degrees. For *p* = 0, it agrees with the *q*-th singular cohomology group (with **Z**-coefficients), by definition. For *q* = 2 and *p* = 1, it is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the group of isomorphism classes of smooth (or holomorphic, depending on the context) [principal **C**<sup>×</sup>-bundles](wikipedia://en/2794417) over *X*. For *p* = *q* = 2, it is the group of isomorphism classes of **C**<sup>×</sup>-bundles with [connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connection_%28fiber_bundle%29). For *q* = 3 and *p* = 2 or 3, descriptions in terms of [gerbes](wikipedia://en/822043) are available ((Brylinski 2008)). This has been generalized to a description in higher degrees in terms of iterated [classifying spaces](wikipedia://en/923556) and connections on them ((Gajer 1997)).

### Relation with Hodge classes {#Relation_with_Hodge_classes}

Recall there is a subgroup $\text{Hdg}^p(X) \subset H^{p,p}(X)$ of integral cohomology classes in $H^{2p}(X)$ called the group of Hodge classes. There is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) relating Deligne-cohomology, their [intermediate Jacobians](wikipedia://en/3092994), and this group of Hodge classes, namely

> $$0 \to J^{2p-1}(X) \to H^{2p}_\mathcal{D}(X,\mathbb{Z}(p)) \to \text{Hdg}^{2p}(X) \to 0.$$

:::

::: {#o14 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Deligne cohomology is used to formulate [Beilinson conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beilinson_conjectures) on [special values of *L*-functions](wikipedia://en/25305882).

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

## Extensions {#Extensions}

There is an extension of Deligne-cohomology defined for any [symmetric spectrum](wikipedia://en/40785236) $E$[^o7] where $\pi_i(E)\otimes \mathbb{C} = 0$ for $i$ odd which can be compared with ordinary Deligne cohomology on complex analytic varieties.

:::
