# Dickson's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dickson's conjecture"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Dickson's conjecture** is the statement that for a finite set of linear [forms](wikipedia://en/1698977) $a_1+b_1n, a_2+b_2n, \dots, a_k+b_kn$ with each $b_i\geq 1$, there are infinitely many positive integers $n$ for which they are all [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), unless there is a [congruence](wikipedia://en/20087) condition preventing this.[^o2] The conjecture is named after [Leonard Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247), who first proposed it in 1904.[^o3]

:::

The case $k=1$ is [Dirichlet's theorem](wikipedia://en/101453). Two other special cases are well-known conjectures: that there are infinitely many [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997) ($n$ and $n+2$ are primes), and that there are infinitely many [Sophie Germain primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Germain_prime) ($n$ and $2n+1$ are primes).

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Generalized Dickson's conjecture"}

## Generalized Dickson's conjecture {#Generalized_Dickson's_conjecture}

Given $n$ polynomials with positive degrees and integer coefficients ($n$ can be any natural number) that each satisfy all three conditions in the [Bunyakovsky conjecture](wikipedia://en/12511846), and for any prime $p$ there is an integer $x$ such that the values of all $n$ polynomials at $x$ are not divisible by $p$, then there are infinitely many positive integers $x$ such that all values of these $n$ polynomials at $x$ are prime. For example, if the conjecture is true then there are infinitely many positive integers $x$ such that $x^2+1$, $3x-1$, and $x^2+x+41$ are all prime. When all the polynomials have degree 1, this is the original Dickson's conjecture. This generalization is equivalent to the [generalized Bunyakovsky conjecture](wikipedia://en/12511846#Generalized_Bunyakovsky_conjecture) and [Schinzel's hypothesis H](wikipedia://en/604052).

:::
