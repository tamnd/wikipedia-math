# Special group (algebraic group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Special group"}

In the theory of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924), a **special group** is a [linear algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) *G* with the property that every [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) is locally trivial in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744). Special groups include the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564), the [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823), and the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977). Special groups are necessarily [connected](wikipedia://en/6233). Products of special groups are special. The [projective linear group](wikipedia://en/382667) is not special because there exist [Azumaya algebras](wikipedia://en/3125089), which are trivial over a finite [separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340), but not over the base field.

:::

Special groups were defined in 1958 by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483)[^o2] and classified soon thereafter by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042).[^o3]
