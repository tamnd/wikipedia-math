# George Anderson (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Anderson"}

**George Anderson** (born ca. 1720)[^o2] was an English[^o2] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), about whom nothing is known beyond what is contained in eight letters addressed by him to the celebrated mathematician [William Jones](wikipedia://en/239126) (father of the [orientalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_studies) [Sir William Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Jones)), which were printed from the [Macclesfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Macclesfield) papers in 1841.[^o3] They give proof of singular ability in treating the most advanced mathematical problems of the time, and by many indications show the writer (contrary to an editorial surmise)[^o4] to have occupied a respectable position in life.[^o5] The first three are dated from [Twickenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham), August to October 1736; the two from 1739 were sent from [Hothfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hothfield) and Newbottle, respectively; the last was written 27 September 1740, at [Leyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyden), where the writer, now aged 20 and enrolled as a law student since 12 September,[^o2][^o6] had just entered upon a "train of studies and exercises"[^o7] at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University). He expressed in 1739 a strong desire to be admitted to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), but his name does not appear upon the list of its members.[^o8]

:::
