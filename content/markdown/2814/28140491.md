# Randall Dougherty

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Randall Dougherty"}

**Randall Dougherty** (born 1961) is an American mathematician. Dougherty has made contributions in widely varying areas of mathematics, including [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492), [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), and [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869).[^o3]

:::

Dougherty is a three-time winner of the [U.S.A. Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) (1976, 1977, 1978) and a three-time medalist in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208). He is also a three-time [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) (1978, 1979, 1980).[^o5] Dougherty earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1985 at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) under the direction of [Jack Silver](wikipedia://en/21626512).

With [Matthew Foreman](wikipedia://en/3332703) he showed that the [Banach-Tarski decomposition](wikipedia://en/19759220) is possible with pieces with the [Baire property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baire_property), solving a problem of [Marczewski](wikipedia://en/1028149) that remained unsolved for more than 60 years.[^o7] With [Chris Freiling](wikipedia://en/162265) and Ken Zeger, he showed that linear codes are insufficient to gain the full advantages of [network coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_coding).[^o8]
