# Erich Rothe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erich Rothe"}

**Erich Hans Rothe** (July 21, 1895, [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) – February 19, 1988, [Ann Arbor, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan)) was a German-born American mathematician, who did research in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234), and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).[^o4] He is known for the Rothe method (also known as the method of lines or the method of semidiscretization) used for solving [evolution equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_equation).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Rothe, whose father was a lawyer, attended Berlin's [Royal Wilhelm Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Wilhelm_Gymnasium) and passed his *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur)* in October 1913. After completing two semesters at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), he volunteered to join the German Army in a field artillery regiment. He was wounded in the [Battle of Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Verdun) and was discharged from the German Army in December 1918. In 1919, he continued his mathematical studies for one semester at [Technische Hochschule Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_Berlin) and then transferred to the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin). There he studied mathematics, physics, and philosophy and in 1923 passed the *Lehramtsexamen* qualifying him to become a *[Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29)* teacher. From 1923 to 1926, he taught at Berlin's *Mommsen-Gymnasium*.[^o3] In 1927, he received his [*Promotion*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_%28Germany%29) from Technische Hochschule Berlin. His dissertation *Über einige Analogien zwischen linearen partiellen und linearen gewöhnlichen Differentialgleichungen* (About some analogies between linear partial and linear ordinary differential equations) was supervised by [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191) and [Richard von Mises](wikipedia://en/821517). Rothe worked from 1926 to 1927 at the Institute of Applied Mathematics of the Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin. In 1928, he married the mathematician [Hildegard Ille](wikipedia://en/70781180) (1899–1942).[^o3] From 1928 to 1931, he was a *[Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent)* and *Assistent* under [Fritz Noether](wikipedia://en/7522370) at the *[Technische Hochschule Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_Breslau)*. There he received his *[Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation)* in 1928. From 1931 to 1935, he was a *Privatdozent* at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau). There he received his *Umhabilitation* in 1931).[^o9][^o10] During the time he held positions in Breslau, he took study leave for a year at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). In Breslau in April 1931, Hildegard Rothe gave birth to Erhard W. Rothe.[^o3]

After being dismissed in 1935 from the German civil service because he was a Jew, Rothe with his wife and son escaped to Zurich and emigrated in 1937 to the USA. From 1937 to 1943, he taught mathematics (with a very small salary) at [William Penn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn_College) in [Oskaloosa, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskaloosa,_Iowa).[^o3][^o11] His wife died of cancer in December 1942. At the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), Rothe was an assistant professor from 1944 to 1949, an associate professor from 1949 to 1955, and a full professor from 1955 to 1964, when he retired as professor emeritus.[^o3] In retirement, he taught at the [University of Michigan–Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan–Dearborn)[^o12] and in the academic year from 1967 to 1968 at [Western Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Michigan_University) (WMU).[^o3] During his year at WMU, Rothe helped to develop the PhD program for WMU's mathematics department, which awarded its first PhD in December 1969.[^o13]

Rothe published more than 50 mathematical papers.[^o12] He was a co-author, with [Hans Rademacher](wikipedia://en/1864916), of chapter 19 of the 7th edition of *Die Differential- und Integralgleichungen der Mechanik und Physik*.[^o14] In 1986 at the age of 91, Rothe published the 242-page book *Introduction to Various Aspects of Degree Theory in Banach Spaces*.[^o3]

In addition to the Rothe Method, he is also known for his theorem, proven in 1937, that a functional in a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) is weakly continuous if and only if its [Fréchet derivative](wikipedia://en/2735727) is a [completely continuous operator](wikipedia://en/577441#Completely_continuous_operators)[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] and for Rothe's fixed point theorem, proven in 1937.[^o19][^o20] In 1978 a collection of papers was published in his honor.[^o21] His doctoral students include [Jane Cronin Scanlon](wikipedia://en/44843265) and [George J. Minty](wikipedia://en/67693763).[^o22]

Upon his death, Erich Rothe was survived by his son and two granddaughters.[^o12]
