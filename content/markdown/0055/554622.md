# Negation normal form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Negation normal form"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), a formula is in **negation normal form** (**NNF**) if the [negation](wikipedia://en/161019) operator ($\lnot$, not) is only applied to variables and the only other allowed [Boolean operators](wikipedia://en/54476844) are [conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152) ($\land$, and) and [disjunction](wikipedia://en/7962) ($\lor$, or).

:::

Negation normal form is not a [canonical form](wikipedia://en/515096): for example, $a \land (b\lor \lnot c)$ and $(a \land b) \lor (a \land \lnot c)$ are equivalent, and are both in negation normal form.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The following is a [context-free grammar](wikipedia://en/6759) for *NNF*:

{|

\|- | *NNF* || $\to$ || *Literal* |- | || || 1$\mid \quad$( *NNF* $\, \land \,$ *NNF* 1) |- | || || 1$\mid \quad$( *NNF* $\, \lor \,$ *NNF* 1) |- | |- | *Literal* || $\to$ || *Variable* |- | || || 1$\mid \quad \neg \,$ *Variable* |} where *Variable* is any variable.

:::

## Examples and counterexamples {#Examples_and_counterexamples}

{|style="float:right; margin: 1em;"

\|

```
         ∨
       /   \
      ∧     D
    /   \
   ∧     ¬
 /   \   |
A     ∨  C
     /  \
    ¬    C
    |
    B         
```

\|} The following formulae are all in negation normal form:

$$\begin{aligned} &((A \lor B) \land C) \\ &(A \lor \lnot B) \\ &(A \land \lnot B) \\ &((A \land (\lnot B \lor C)) \land \lnot C) \end{aligned}$$

The first example is also in [conjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73342), the next two are in both [conjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73342) and [disjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73341), but the last example is in neither.

The following formulae are not in negation normal form:

$$\begin{aligned} (A &\to B) \\ \lnot (A &\lor B) \\ \lnot (A &\land B) \\ \lnot (A &\lor \lnot C) \end{aligned}$$

They are however respectively equivalent to the following formulae in negation normal form:

$$\begin{aligned} (\lnot A &\lor B) \\ (\lnot A &\land \lnot B) \\ (\lnot A &\lor \lnot B) \\ (\lnot A &\land C) \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Conversion to NNF {#Conversion_to_NNF}

In [classical logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic) and many [modal logics](wikipedia://en/333365), every formula can be brought into this form by replacing [implications](wikipedia://en/658808) ($\to$) and [equivalences](wikipedia://en/14922) ($\leftrightarrow$) by their definitions, using [De Morgan's laws](wikipedia://en/64669) to push negation inwards, and eliminating double negations. This process can be represented using the following [rewrite rules](wikipedia://en/415847):[^n1]

$$\begin{aligned} A \to B &~\rightsquigarrow~ \lnot A \lor B \\ A \leftrightarrow B &~\rightsquigarrow~ (\lnot A \lor B) \land (A \lor \lnot B) \\ \lnot (A \lor B) &~\rightsquigarrow~ \lnot A \land \lnot B \\ \lnot (A \land B) &~\rightsquigarrow~ \lnot A \lor \lnot B \\ \lnot \lnot A &~\rightsquigarrow~ A \\ \lnot \exists x A &~\rightsquigarrow~ \forall x \lnot A \\ \lnot \forall x A &~\rightsquigarrow~ \exists x \lnot A \end{aligned}$$

Transformation into negation normal form can increase the size of a formula only linearly: the number of occurrences of atomic formulas remains the same, the total number of occurrences of $\land$ and $\lor$ is unchanged, and the number of occurrences of $\lnot$ in the normal form is bounded by the length of the original formula.

A formula in negation normal form can be put into the stronger [conjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73342) or [disjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73341) by applying [distributivity](wikipedia://en/103118). Repeated application of distributivity may exponentially increase the size of a formula. In the classical [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154), transformation to negation normal form does not impact computational properties: the [satisfiability problem](wikipedia://en/4715) continues to be [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete), and the validity problem continues to be [co-NP-complete](wikipedia://en/54680). For formulas in conjunctive normal form, the validity problem is solvable in polynomial time, and for formulas in disjunctive normal form, the satisfiability problem is solvable in polynomial time.
