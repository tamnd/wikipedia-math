# Leonid I. Vainerman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Leonid I. Vainerman"}

**Leonid Iosifovich Vainerman** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Леонiд Йосипович Вайнерман; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Леонид Иосифович Вайнерман; alternative spelling: Leonid Iosifovich Vaynerman; born November 15, 1946, in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv), Ukraine) is a Ukrainian and French mathematician, professor emeritus at [University of Caen Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Caen_Normandy).[^o3][^o4] Vainerman's research results are in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equations), [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), [topological groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_groups), [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_groups), and [abstract harmonic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_harmonic_analysis).[^n1] In the 1970s, he co-developed [Pontryagin-style dualities for non-commutative topological groups](wikipedia://en/366136#Dualities_for_non-commutative_topological_groups), a set of results that served as a precursor for the modern theory of [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups).[^o25][^o26]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

### Degrees and appointments in Ukraine {#Degrees_and_appointments_in_Ukraine}

Vainerman studied mathematics at the [Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv) and graduated in 1969. He completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) ([Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) in the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR)) in 1974 at [Institute of Mathematics of National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](wikipedia://en/36753361) under the direction of [Myroslav Horbachuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myroslav_Horbachuk) (Gorbachuk).[^o7][^o8] Vainerman was a professor at [Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv)[^o9][^o10] until 1992. He was a professor at [International Solomon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Solomon_University) from 1992 to 2002.[^o28][^o11]

### Visiting France, Belgium and Germany {#Visiting_France,_Belgium_and_Germany}

- From 1992 to 1995, Vainerman was a visiting researcher at [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) (Paris VI). During this appointment, he had fruitful collaborations with [Michel Enock](wikipedia://en/74785890)[^o13] and [Richard Kerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kerner).[^o14]
- In 1999, Vainerman was a visiting researcher at [Katholieke Universiteit Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven), where he had a fruitful collaboration with [Stefaan Vaes](wikipedia://en/58028722).[^o30]
- From 1998 to 2002, Vainerman was a visiting researcher at [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn), Germany.[^o15]
- From 2000 to 2002, Vainerman was a visiting professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) in France, where he organized a prominent meeting of theoretical physicists and mathematicians.[^o35] While there, he also collaborated with Dmitri Nikshych and [Vladimir Turaev](wikipedia://en/56815253).[^o16]

### Permanent appointment in Caen {#Permanent_appointment_in_Caen}

Vainerman joined the [University of Caen Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Caen_Normandy) as an associate professor in [Nicolas Oresme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Oresme) Mathematics Laboratory,[^o33] becoming a full professor in 2005. He directed three Ph.D. dissertations there (those of Pierre Fima, Camille Mével and Frank Taipe).[^o7][^o18] He has been a professor emeritus at [University of Caen Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Caen_Normandy) since 2015.[^o3] While at Caen, Vainerman collaborated with Dmitri Nikshych[^o29] and [Jean-Michel Vallin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Michel_Vallin).[^o31]

## Scientific contributions {#Scientific_contributions}

In the 1970s, Vainerman collaborated with George I. Kac (Georgii Isaakovich Kac)[^o20] on generalizations of [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) to non-commutative groups and developed the concept now known as Kac algebras[^o21][^o22][^o23][^o24] (distinct from [Kac-Moody algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kac-Moody_algebras)).

According to the French mathematician [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340),[^o25] The theory of Kac algebras and their duality, \[was\] elaborated independently by M. Enock and J. -M. Schwartz, and by G. I. Kac and L. I. Vainermann in the seventies. The subject has now reached a state of maturity The two teams independently developed a general [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) theory for all [locally compact groups](wikipedia://en/1651204). The contributions of both teams are covered in the 1992 book by [Michel Enock](wikipedia://en/74785890) and Jean-Marie Schwartz on Kac algebras.[^o26] Per Alain Connes,[^o25] these results form "a general theory to characterize [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups) among [Hopf algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_algebras), similar to the characterization of [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_groups) among [locally compact groups](wikipedia://en/1651204)." As mentioned in the postface by Adrian Ocneanu to the book by Enock and Schwartz,[^o26] Kac algebras and their actions on [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) naturally arise in the theory of [subfactors](wikipedia://en/2780098) developed by [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628).[^o22][^o27]

In his subsequent research, Vainerman obtained results on [C\*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebras), [Hopf algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_algebras) and [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups), as well as quantum [hypergroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergroup) and [quantum groupoids](wikipedia://en/55131254).[^o28][^o29][^o30][^o31] Vainerman's research advanced the theory of quantum groups, including structural results on C\*-algebraic quantum groups and their actions on operator algebras.[^o30] He has made notable contributions to the structure and classification of weak Hopf C\*-algebras (sometimes described as quantum groupoids), with applications to topics such as subfactors, dynamical quantum groups, and the construction of [knot invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_invariants) and [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifolds).[^o29][^o31] His collaborative works include surveys and original research articles detailing the foundational theory and applications of finite quantum groupoids.[^o29]

Vainerman is credited as a co-author or editor in more than 70 mathematics publications.[^o32][^o33]

## Scientific leadership {#Scientific_leadership}

Vainerman organized a meeting at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) February 21–23, 2002 that assembled theoretical physicists and mathematicians specializing in [quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678) and [quantum groupoid](wikipedia://en/55131254) applications in quantum theories beyond the [Standard Model](wikipedia://en/47641). Vainerman edited the proceedings of the meeting and had them published as a book in 2003.[^o35]
