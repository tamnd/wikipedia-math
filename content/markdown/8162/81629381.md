# Hallard Croft

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hallard Croft"}

**Hallard Thomas Croft** (15 April 1936 – 15 September 2025) was an English mathematician.[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Born in [Bromley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromley), he was educated at [City of London School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London_School)[^o6] and earned a PhD from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1961, under [John Edensor Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371)'s supervision.[^o5] A fellow of [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge), from 1963 to 2003,[^o6] in 1967 he invented a geometric shape called Croft's Tortoise and in 1991 he co-authored *Unsolved Problems in Geometry* with [Kenneth Falconer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Falconer_%28mathematician%29) and [Richard K. Guy](wikipedia://en/816308).[^o7]

Croft clashed with [Hugh Trevor-Roper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Trevor-Roper) after the latter became Master of Peterhouse in 1980 when Croft was Director of Studies in mathematics and Senior Fellow;[^o6][^o8] Croft's conservatism led him to deeply dislike Trevor-Roper, and the upshot was that he was re-elected as a Fellow in 1983 without his seniority. Friends suggested that Trevor-Roper disliked Croft because of his homosexuality.[^o6][^o8]
