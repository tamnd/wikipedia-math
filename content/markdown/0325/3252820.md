# Bruno Abakanowicz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bruno Abakanowicz"}

**Bruno [Abdank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdank_coat_of_arms)-Abakanowicz** (6 October 1852 – 29 August 1900) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [inventor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventor), and [electrical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineer). He is best known for inventing the [integraph](wikipedia://en/14230769) and contributing to the development of mathematical instruments and early electrification systems in France.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Abakanowicz was born in 1852 in [Ukmergė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukmergė), then part of the [Vilna Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilna_Governorate) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (now [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania)).[^o4]

After graduating from the [Riga Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga_Technical_University), he completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation)[^o5] and began working as an assistant at the [Technical University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Polytechnic). In 1881, he moved to [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France),[^o5] where he purchased a villa in [Parc Saint-Maur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Maur-des-Fossés) on the outskirts of [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).

Earlier, Abakanowicz invented the [integraph](wikipedia://en/14230769), a mechanical device for drawing the integral of a curve, which was patented in 1880.[^o6] It was later produced commercially by the Swiss firm *Coradi*.[^o7] Among his other inventions were the [parabolagraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolagraph), the [spirograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirograph), an electric bell used in trains, and a form of [electric arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_arc) lamp.[^o8]

He also published several works on [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), mathematical instruments, and practical applications of integration, including *Les intégraphes* (1886).[^o6] In France, he served as a government expert on electrification projects and worked on systems in cities such as [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon).[^o5] His patents and innovations brought him wealth and recognition, and in 1889 he was awarded the [Légion d'honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour).

*Image: Le château de Costaeres à Trégastel - PSIMG 4117.jpg.* The Château de Costaérès built by Abakanowicz in Trégastel

In the 1890s, Abakanowicz retired to a small island in [Trégastel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trégastel), [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany), where between 1892 and 1896 he built the [Château de Costaérès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Château_de_Costaérès), a [neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival) manor.[^o9] Although the construction was not completed during his lifetime, the castle became a meeting place for the Polish émigré community in France. Frequent guests included [Aleksander Gierymski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksander_Gierymski), [Władysław Mickiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Władysław_Mickiewicz), [Leon Wyczółkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Wyczółkowski), and [Henryk Sienkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Sienkiewicz). Sienkiewicz completed *[The Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Teutonic_Knights_%28novel%29)* and *[The Polaniecki Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Polaniecki_Family)* at Abakanowicz's villa in Saint-Maur and wrote *[Quo Vadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quo_Vadis_%28novel%29)* at the Château de Costaérès.[^o5]

Abakanowicz died suddenly on 29 August 1900. In his will, he appointed Sienkiewicz as the guardian of his only daughter, [Zofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zofia_Pstrokońska), who later graduated from the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) and the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), and was murdered during World War II at the [Auschwitz concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp).

## Legacy and identity {#Legacy_and_identity}

Abakanowicz’s nationality has been described in different ways. He was born in the territory of the former [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Lithuanian_Commonwealth), which by 1852 was part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). Some contemporary documents list him as Russian due to political boundaries, while *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica)* refers to him as Lithuanian. However, his education, language, and social circle were predominantly Polish, and he is generally regarded as a Polish scientist and inventor.[^o5]

The surname *Abakanowicz*, of [Lipka Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipka_Tatar) origin, derives from a noble family of the [szlachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta) associated with the [Abdank coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdank_coat_of_arms).[^o12][^o13][^o14]

:::
