# Georg Rasch

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Georg Rasch"}

**Georg William Rasch** () (21 September 1901 – 19 October 1980) was a Danish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), and [psychometrician](wikipedia://en/24982), most famous for the development of a class of [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022) models known as [Rasch models](wikipedia://en/2161615). He studied with [R.A. Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824) and also briefly with [Ragnar Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar_Frisch), and was elected a member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) in 1948.

:::

In 1919, Rasch began studying mathematics at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen). He completed a master's degree in 1925 and received a doctorate in science with thesis director [Niels Erik Nørlund](wikipedia://en/17132688) in 1930. Rasch married in 1928. Unable to find work as a mathematician in the 1930s, he turned to work as a statistical consultant. In this capacity, he worked on a range of problems, including problems of biological growth.

## Contributions to psychometrics {#Contributions_to_psychometrics}

Georg Rasch is best known for his contributions to [psychometrics](wikipedia://en/24982). His work in this field began when he used the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) to model the number of errors made by students when reading texts. He referred to the model as the multiplicative Poisson model.

He later developed the [Rasch model](wikipedia://en/2161615) for dichotomous data, which he applied to response data derived from intelligence and attainment tests including data collected by the Danish military. At the same epoch, [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) scientists independently developed [item response theory](wikipedia://en/420159) (IRT). Within IRT, the Rasch model is one of the most simple response models. In contrast to other simple models, the Rasch model has a distinctive mathematical property: the model parameters (item difficulties, examinee ability) are [sufficient statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_statistics). Rasch demonstrated that his approach met criteria for measurement deduced from an analysis of measurement in the physical sciences. He also proposed generalizations of his model (Rasch, 1960/1980, 1977).

Today, the Rasch model is used extensively in assessment in education and [educational psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_psychology), particularly for attainment and cognitive assessments.
