# Hans Langmaack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Langmaack"}

**Hans Langmaack** (born 7 May 1934) is a German mathematician and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist).[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Hans Langmaack attended the Bismarck School in [Elmshorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmshorn), where he received a prize in natural sciences (the Ernst Hermann Koelln Prize, 1951) and, before graduating from high school in 1954, completed an [analog computer](wikipedia://en/2428) for [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405) as a mathematical year's work. After an internship in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering), he studied mathematics, physics, and logic at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) (and one semester in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg)) from the winter semester of 1954. In 1957, he passed the teaching examination, became a scholarship holder of the German Academic Scholarship Foundation, and received his doctorate in 1960 under [Heinrich Behnke](wikipedia://en/22922678) with a dissertation on analysis in several [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable) (construction of [holomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic) hulls of unramified regions over $\mathbb C^n$).

From 1960, Langmaack was an assistant to [Klaus Samelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Samelson) at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz) and turned to computer science. From 1960 to 1962, he developed with [Ursula Hill-Samelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Hill-Samelson) the [Algol 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algol_60) *Alcor Mainz 2002* [compiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler) for [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), further developed from 1962 to 1964 at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) (where Samelson moved in 1963 and where Langmaack followed him as an assistant and later senior assistant) to *Alcor Munich 2002*. From 1966 to 1967, he was an assistant professor of Computer Science at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), and in 1967, he qualified as a professor at the Technical University of Munich (on Lidskii's theorem, concerning [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of sums of [Hermitian matrices](wikipedia://en/189682)). He was then a lecturer and scientific advisor at the Technical University of Munich and, from 1970, a full professor at the [University of Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saarland). In 1974, he moved to the [Christian Albrecht University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Albrecht_University_of_Kiel) (Chair of Programming Languages and Compiler Construction). In 1999, he became a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus).

Langmaack was involved in various industrial compiler projects (including [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29), [BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC), and [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_%28programming_language%29)) and [expert systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expert_system) and worked on verified compilers and automated [software verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_verification). From 1989 to 1995, Langaack participated in the EU [ESPRIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPRIT) [ProCoS project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProCoS_project) on *Provably Correct Systems*, as the site leader at Kiel.[^o6][^o7] He has published on [Chomsky grammars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chomsky_grammar), among other subjects.

In 1973, Langmaack was a [visiting scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scientist) at the [Federal University of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Rio_de_Janeiro), in 1974 in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo), and in 1981 at the [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Madison). In 1980, he was a co-initiator (with [Friedrich L. Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_L._Bauer) and Klaus Indermark) of the biennial colloquium series *Programmiersprachen und Grundlagen der Programmierung* (KPS, Programming Languages and Fundamentals of Programming).[^o8] In 1998, he was awarded an honorary doctorate by the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich). A [Festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) volume was published for his retirement in 1999.[^o9]
