# Farsightedness (game theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Farsightedness"}

In [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), **farsightness** refers to players’ ability to consider the long-term consequences of their strategies, beyond immediate payoffs, often formalized as **farsighted stability** where players anticipate future moves and stable outcomes.[^o2]

:::

In static games, players optimize payoffs based on current information, as in the [Nash equilibrium](wikipedia://en/45337),[^o3] but farsightedness involves anticipating [dynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_game) or [repeated](wikipedia://en/3143089) interactions, such as in coalition games like [hedonic games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedonic_games) where preferences shape long-term alliances.[^o4] For example, in a repeated [Prisoner's Dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner's_Dilemma), a farsighted player might cooperate to encourage future cooperation, unlike the one-shot case where defection prevails.[^o5] Similarly, a player might refuse a small immediate payoff to build a more valuable alliance later.[^o2]

Farsightedness assumes significant foresight and computational ability, which may be unrealistic in complex scenarios. In evolutionary settings, myopic strategies might dominate if immediate survival outweighs long-term planning.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In [evolutionary game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_game_theory), farsightedness contrasts with myopic adaptation, where strategies adjust based on immediate fitness.[^o9] A farsighted strategy might aim for an [evolutionarily stable strategy](wikipedia://en/9616) (ESS) that withstands long-term mutant challenges. In coalition settings, farsighted players assess how current choices affect future stability, rejecting short-term gains for long-term benefits, as seen in hedonic games.[^o4] Farsighted stability captures this by modeling chains of responses predicting stable configurations.[^o2]

:::
