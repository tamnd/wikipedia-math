# Finite difference coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Finite difference coefficient"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), to approximate a [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) to an arbitrary order of accuracy, it is possible to use the [finite difference](wikipedia://en/39147). A finite difference can be **central**, **forward** or **backward**.

:::

For example, the third derivative with a second-order accuracy is

$$f'''(x_{0}) \approx \frac{-\frac{1}{2}f(x_{-2}) + f(x_{-1}) -f(x_{+1}) + \frac{1}{2}f(x_{+2})}{h^3_x} + O\left(h_x^2 \right),$$

where $h_x$ represents a uniform grid spacing between each finite difference interval, and $x_n = x_0 + n h_x$.

For the $m$-th derivative with accuracy $n$, there are $2p + 1 = 2 \left\lfloor \frac{m+1}{2} \right\rfloor - 1 + n$ central coefficients $a_{-p}, a_{-p+1}, ..., a_{p-1}, a_p$. These are given by the solution of the linear equation system

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & ... & 1 & 1 \\ -p & -p+1 & ... & p-1 & p \\ (-p)^2 & (-p+1)^2 &... & (p-1)^2 & p^2 \\ ... & ... &...&...&... \\ ... & ... &...&...&... \\ ... & ... &...&...&... \\ (-p)^{2p} & (-p+1)^{2p} & ... & (p-1)^{2p} & p^{2p} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a_{-p} \\ a_{-p+1} \\ a_{-p+2} \\ ... \\ ... \\ ... \\ a_p \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ ... \\ m! \\ ...\\ 0 \end{pmatrix},$$

where the only non-zero value on the right hand side is in the $(m+1)$-th row.

An open source implementation for calculating finite difference coefficients of arbitrary derivates and accuracy order in one dimension is available.[^o5] Given that the left-hand side matrix $\mathbf{J}^{T}$ is a transposed [Vandermonde matrix](wikipedia://en/348641), a rearrangement reveals that the coefficients are basically computed by fitting and deriving a $2p$-th order polynomial to a window of $2p + 1$ points. Consequently, the coefficients can also be computed as the $m$-th order derivative of a fully determined [Savitzky–Golay filter](wikipedia://en/1988157) with polynomial degree $2p$ and a window size of $2p + 1$. For this, open source implementations are also available.[^o6] There are two possible definitions which differ in the ordering of the coefficients: a filter for filtering via [discrete convolution](wikipedia://en/7519#Discrete_convolution) or via a [matrix-vector-product](wikipedia://en/125280). The coefficients given in the table above correspond to the latter definition.

The theory of [Lagrange polynomials](wikipedia://en/217523) provides explicit formulas for the finite difference coefficients.[^o7] For the first six derivatives we have the following:

::: {#o8 .table cue=table}

| Derivative | $a_0$ | $a_p (p \neq 0)$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $0$ | $\frac{1!(-1)^{p+1}(n!)^2}{p (n-p)!(n+p)!}$ |
| 2 | $- 2 H_{n,2}$ | $\frac{2!(-1)^{p+1}(n!)^2}{p^2 (n-p)!(n+p)!}$ |
| 3 | $0$ | $\frac{3!(-1)^{p+1}(n!)^2}{p^3 (n-p)!(n+p)!}(1 - p^2 H_{n,2})$ |
| 4 | $12 (H_{n,2}^2 - H_{n,4})$ | $\frac{4!(-1)^{p+1}(n!)^2}{p^4 (n-p)!(n+p)!}(1 - p^2 H_{n,2})$ |
| 5 | $0$ | $\frac{5!(-1)^{p+1}(n!)^2}{p^5 (n-p)!(n+p)!}\left(1 - p^2 H_{n,2}+\frac{p^4}{2}(H_{n,2}^2-H_{n,4})\right)$ |
| 6 | $- 120 H_{n,2}^3 + 360 H_{n,2}H_{n,4} - 120 H_{n,6}$ | $\frac{6!(-1)^{p+1}(n!)^2}{p^6 (n-p)!(n+p)!}\left(1 - p^2 H_{n,2}+\frac{p^4}{2}(H_{n,2}^2-H_{n,4})\right)$ |

:::

where $H_{n,m}$ are [generalized harmonic numbers](wikipedia://en/214729).

## Arbitrary stencil points {#Arbitrary_stencil_points}

For $\displaystyle N$ arbitrary [stencil](wikipedia://en/18558187) points $\displaystyle s$ and any derivative of order $\displaystyle d < N$ up to one less than the number of stencil points, the finite difference coefficients can be obtained by solving the linear equations [^o15]

$$\begin{pmatrix} s_1^0 & \cdots & s_N^0 \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ s_1^{N-1} & \cdots & s_N^{N-1} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ \vdots \\ a_N \end{pmatrix} = d! \begin{pmatrix} \delta_{0,d} \\ \vdots\\ \delta_{i,d}\\ \vdots\\ \delta_{N-1,d} \end{pmatrix},$$

where $\delta_{i,j}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890), equal to one if $i = j$, and zero otherwise.

Example, for $s = [-3, -2, -1, 0, 1]$, order of differentiation $d = 4$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a_{1} \\ a_{2} \\ a_{3} \\ a_4 \\ a_5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ -3 & -2 & -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 9 & 4 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\ -27 & -8 & -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 81 & 16 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\ \end{pmatrix}^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 24 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -4 \\ 6 \\ -4\\ 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The order of accuracy of the approximation takes the usual form $O\left(h_{x}^{(N-d)}\right)$ (or better in the case of central finite difference).
