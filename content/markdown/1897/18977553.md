# N! conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="N! conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the ***n*! conjecture** is the [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) that the [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) of a certain [bi-graded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi-graded) [module](wikipedia://en/276410) of [diagonal harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_harmonic) is *n*!. It was made by [A. M. Garsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._M._Garsia) and [M. Haiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Haiman) and later [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by [M. Haiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Haiman). It implies [Macdonald](wikipedia://en/3100378)'s [positivity conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macdonald_polynomial#The_Macdonald_positivity_conjecture) about the [Macdonald polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macdonald_polynomial).

:::

## Formulation and background {#Formulation_and_background}

The Macdonald polynomials $P_\lambda$ are a two-parameter family of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) indexed by a positive weight λ of a [root system](wikipedia://en/277087), introduced by [Ian G. Macdonald](wikipedia://en/3100378) (1987). They generalize several other families of orthogonal polynomials, such as [Jack polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_polynomial) and [Hall–Littlewood polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall–Littlewood_polynomial). They are known to have deep relationships with [affine Hecke algebras](wikipedia://en/10361630) and [Hilbert schemes](wikipedia://en/6612581), which were used to prove several conjectures made by Macdonald about them.

(Macdonald 1988) introduced a new basis for the space of [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359), which specializes to many of the well-known bases for the symmetric functions, by suitable substitutions for the parameters *q* and *t*.

In fact, we can obtain in this manner the [Schur functions](wikipedia://en/3147062), the Hall–Littlewood symmetric functions, the Jack symmetric functions, the [zonal symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zonal_symmetric_function), the [zonal spherical functions](wikipedia://en/17708915), and the elementary and monomial symmetric functions.

The so-called *q*,*t*-[Kostka polynomials](wikipedia://en/22630866) are the coefficients of a resulting [transition matrix](wikipedia://en/217313). Macdonald conjectured that they are polynomials in *q* and *t*, with non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) coefficients.

It was [Adriano Garsia](wikipedia://en/18857510)'s idea to construct an appropriate [module](wikipedia://en/276410) in order to prove positivity (as was done in his previous joint work with [Procesi](wikipedia://en/35219117) on Schur positivity of [Kostka–Foulkes polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostka–Foulkes_polynomials)).

In an attempt to prove Macdonald's conjecture, (Garsia Haiman 1993) introduced the bi-graded module $H_\mu$ of [diagonal harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_harmonics) and conjectured that the (modified) Macdonald polynomials are the Frobenius image of the character generating function of *H*<sub>μ</sub>, under the diagonal action of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901).

The proof of Macdonald's conjecture was then reduced to the *n*! conjecture; i.e., to prove that the dimension of *H*<sub>μ</sub> is *n*!. In 2001, Haiman proved that the dimension is indeed *n*! (see \[4\]).

This breakthrough led to the discovery of many hidden connections and new aspects of [symmetric group representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_group_representation_theory), as well as combinatorial objects (e.g., insertion tableaux, Haglund's inversion numbers, and the role of parking functions in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200)).
