# Tensor decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tensor decomposition"}

In [multilinear algebra](wikipedia://en/243946), a **tensor decomposition** is any scheme for expressing a ["data tensor"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_%28machine_learning%29) (M-way array) as a sequence of elementary operations acting on other, often simpler tensors.[^o2][^o3][^o4] Many tensor decompositions generalize some [matrix decompositions](wikipedia://en/253873).[^o5]

:::

[Tensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensors) are generalizations of matrices to higher dimensions (or rather to higher orders, i.e. the higher number of dimensions) and can consequently be treated as multidimensional fields.[^o2][^o6] The main tensor decompositions are:

- [Tensor rank decomposition](wikipedia://en/18961138);[^o7]
- [Higher-order singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/20148816);[^o8]
- [Tucker decomposition](wikipedia://en/18964319);
- [matrix product states](wikipedia://en/42099889), and operators or tensor trains;
- [Online Tensor Decompositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Tensor_Decompositions)[^o9][^o10][^o11]
- [hierarchical Tucker decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical_Tucker_decomposition);[^o12]
- [block term decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_term_decomposition)[^o13][^o14][^o12][^o15]

## Introduction {#Introduction}

A multi-way graph with K perspectives is a collection of K matrices ${X_1,X_2.....X_K}$ with dimensions I × J (where I, J are the number of nodes). This collection of matrices is naturally represented as a tensor X of size I × J × K. In order to avoid overloading the term “dimension”, we call an I × J × K tensor a three “mode” tensor, where “modes” are the numbers of indices used to index the tensor.
