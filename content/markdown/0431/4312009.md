# Erdős conjecture on arithmetic progressions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős conjecture on arithmetic progressions"}

**Erdős' conjecture on arithmetic progressions**, often referred to as the **Erdős–Turán conjecture**, is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) in [arithmetic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243381). It states that if the sum of the reciprocals of the members of a set *A* of positive integers diverges, then *A* contains arbitrarily long [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389).

:::

Formally, the conjecture states that if *A* is a [large set](wikipedia://en/3152856) in the sense that

$$\sum_{n\in A} \frac{1}{n} \ =\ \infty,$$

then *A* contains arithmetic progressions of any given length, meaning that for every positive integer *k* there are an integer *a* and a non-zero integer *c* such that $\{a,a{+}c,a{+}2c,\ldots,a{+}kc\}\subset A$.

## History {#History}

In 1936, [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) made the weaker conjecture that any set of integers with positive [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) contains infinitely many three-term arithmetic progressions.[^o3] This was proven by [Klaus Roth](wikipedia://en/787827) in 1952, and generalized to arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions by [Szemerédi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szemerédi) in 1975 in what is now known as [Szemerédi's theorem](wikipedia://en/591703).

In a 1976 talk titled "To the memory of my lifelong friend and collaborator Paul Turán," Erdős offered a prize of US\$3000 for a proof of this conjecture.[^o4] In 1996 he raised the prize to US\$5000.[^o5]

## Progress and related results {#Progress_and_related_results}

::: {#o7 .problem cue=unsolved}

Does every large set of natural numbers contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions?

:::

Erdős' conjecture on arithmetic progressions can be viewed as a stronger version of Szemerédi's theorem. Because the sum of the reciprocals of the primes diverges, the [Green&ndash;Tao theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green&ndash;Tao_theorem) on arithmetic progressions is a special case of the conjecture.

The [weaker claim](wikipedia://en/591703#Quantitative_bounds) that *A* must contain infinitely many arithmetic progressions of length 3 is a consequence of an improved bound in [Roth's theorem](wikipedia://en/62455443). A 2016 paper by [Bloom](wikipedia://en/71404791)[^o8] proved that if $A\subset \{1,...,N\}$ contains no non-trivial three-term arithmetic progressions then $|A|\ll N(\log{\log{N}})/\log{N}$.

In 2020 a preprint by Bloom and Sisask[^o9] improved the bound to $|A|\ll N/(\log{N})^{1+c}$ for some absolute constant $c>0$ .

In 2023 a new bound of $\exp(-c(\log N)^{1/12})N$[^o10][^o11][^o12] was found by computer scientists Kelley and Meka, with an exposition given in more familiar mathematical language by [Bloom](wikipedia://en/71404791) and Sisask,[^o13][^o14] who have since improved the exponent of the Kelly-Meka bound to $\beta=1/9$, and conjectured $\beta=5/41$, in a preprint.[^o15]
