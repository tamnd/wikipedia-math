# Fill device

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fill device"}

*Image: Ky-57.jpg.* KY-57 voice encryptor. Note fill port in center.

*Image: KYK-13.nsa.jpg.* A KYK-13 fill device.

A **fill device** or **key loader** is a module used to load [cryptographic keys](wikipedia://en/53039) into electronic [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294) machines. Fill devices are usually hand held and electronic ones are battery operated.

:::

Older mechanical encryption systems, such as [rotor machines](wikipedia://en/196223), were keyed by setting the positions of wheels and plugs from a printed keying list. Electronic systems required some way to load the necessary cryptovariable data. In the 1950s and 1960s, systems such as the [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) [KW-26](wikipedia://en/1006090) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)'s [Fialka](wikipedia://en/1194986) used [punched cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punched_card) for this purpose. Later [NSA encryption systems](wikipedia://en/1042273) incorporated a [serial port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_port) fill connector and developed several **common fill devices** (CFDs) that could be used with multiple systems. A CFD was plugged in when new keys were to be loaded. Newer [NSA](wikipedia://en/21939) systems allow "[over the air rekeying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Air_Rekeying)" (OTAR), but a master key often must still be loaded using a fill device.

NSA uses two serial protocols for key fill, **DS-101** and **DS-102**. Both employ the same [U-229](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-229) 6-pin connector type used for U.S. military audio [handsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handset), with the DS-101 being the newer of the two serial fill protocols. The DS-101 protocol can also be used to load cryptographic algorithms and software updates for crypto modules.

Besides encryption devices, systems that can require key fill include [IFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_Friend_or_Foe), [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) and [frequency hopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_hopping) radios such as [Have Quick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Have_Quick) and [SINCGARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SINCGARS).

Common fill devices employed by NSA include:

- Next Generation Load Device-Medium (NGLD-M) - replacement for the Simple Key Loader.[^o2]
- [AN/PYQ-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/PYQ-10) Simple Key Loader (SKL) - originated in 2006 as a replacement for the DTD.
- [KIK-30](wikipedia://en/38878690), a more recent fill device, is trademarked as the "Really Simple Key Loader" (RASKL) with "single button key-squirt." It supports a wide variety of devices and keys.[^o3]
- KYK-28 pin gun used with the [NESTOR (encryption)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NESTOR_%28encryption%29) system
- [KYK-13](wikipedia://en/4013761) Electronic Transfer Device
- KYX-15 Net Control Device[^o4]
- MX-10579 ECCM Fill Device ([SINCGARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SINCGARS))[^o5]
- KOI-18 [paper tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_tape) reader. Can read 8-level paper or [PET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PET_film_%28biaxially_oriented%29) tape, which is manually pulled through the reader slot by the operator. It is battery powered and has no internal storage, so it can load keys of different lengths, including the 128-bit keys used by more modern systems. The KOI-18 can also be used to load keys into other fill devices that do have internal storage, such as the [KYK-13](wikipedia://en/4013761) and [AN/CYZ-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/CYZ-10). The KOI-18 only supports the DS-102 interface.
- AN/CYZ-10 Data Transfer Device (DTD) - a small [PDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_digital_assistant)-like unit that can store up to 1000 keys, maintains an automatic internal [audit trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audit_trail) of all security-relevant events that can be uploaded to the [LMD/KP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EKMS#Tier_2), encrypts key for storage, and is programmable. It is capable of keying multiple information systems security ([INFOSEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFOSEC)) devices and is compatible with such [COMSEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMSEC) equipment as [SINCGARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SINCGARS) radios, KY-57 VINSON, [KG-84](wikipedia://en/1637220), and others that are keyed by common fill devices (CFDs). The AN/CYZ-10 supports both the DS-101 and DS-102 interfaces. It was developed in the early 1990s, weighs about 4 lb (1.8 kg), and was designed to be fully compatible with future INFOSEC equipment meeting DS-101 signaling and benign fill standards. It will eventually replace the legacy family of CFDs, including the [KYK-13](wikipedia://en/4013761), KYX-15 electronic storage devices, and the KOI-18 [paper tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_tape) reader. Only the DTD and the KOI-18 support newer, [128-bit keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_length).
- [Secure DTD2000 System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_DTD2000_System) (SDS) - Named KIK-20, this was the next generation common fill device replacement for the DTD when it started production in 2006. It employs the [Windows CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_CE) operating system.[^o6]
- [KSD-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSD-64) Crypto ignition key (CIK)

The older KYK-13,[^o7] KYX-15 and MX-10579 are limited to certain key types.
