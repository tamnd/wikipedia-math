# Finite measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Finite measure"}

In [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **finite measure** or **totally finite measure**[^o15] is a special [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) that always takes on finite values. Among finite measures are [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437). The finite measures are often easier to handle than more general measures and show a variety of different properties depending on the [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) they are defined on.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $\mu$ on [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) $(X, \mathcal A)$ is called a finite measure if it satisfies

$$\mu(X) < \infty.$$

By the monotonicity of measures, this implies

$$\mu(A) < \infty \text{ for all } A \in \mathcal A.$$

If $\mu$ is a finite measure, the [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) $(X, \mathcal A, \mu)$ is called a **finite measure space** or a **totally finite measure space**.[^o15]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### General case {#General_case}

For any measurable space, the finite measures form a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) in the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) of [signed measures](wikipedia://en/2985811) with the [total variation](wikipedia://en/683561) norm. Important subsets of the finite measures are the sub-probability measures, which form a [convex subset](wikipedia://en/6292), and the probability measures, which are the intersection of the [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) in the normed space of signed measures and the finite measures.

### Topological spaces {#Topological_spaces}

If $X$ is a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) and $\mathcal A$ contains the [Borel $\sigma$-algebra](wikipedia://en/43327) then every finite measure is also a [locally finite](wikipedia://en/6621919) [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322).

### Metric spaces {#Metric_spaces}

If $X$ is a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) and the $\mathcal A$ is again the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, the [weak convergence of measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures) can be defined. The corresponding topology is called weak topology and is the [initial topology](wikipedia://en/1197123) of all bounded continuous functions on $X$. The weak topology corresponds to the [weak\* topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak*_topology) in functional analysis. If $X$ is also [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), the weak convergence is metricized by the [Lévy–Prokhorov metric](wikipedia://en/6789891).[^o14]

### Polish spaces {#Polish_spaces}

If $X$ is a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622) and $\mathcal A$ is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, then every finite measure is a [regular measure](wikipedia://en/3390453) and therefore a [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933).[^o13] If $X$ is Polish, then the set of all finite measures with the weak topology is Polish too.[^o12]
