# Paul Koebe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Koebe"}

**Paul Koebe** (15 February 1882 – 6 August 1945) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His work dealt primarily with [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), his best known results being on the [uniformization](wikipedia://en/288270) of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Paul Koebe was born 15 February 1882 in Luckenwalde to Otto Hermann Koebe (1852–1932), a successful firefighting equipment manufacturer, and Karoline Emma Krämer. After elementary school in Luckenwalde, he attended the [Joachimsthal Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachimsthal_Gymnasium). He studied mathematics at [Kiel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel_University) and [Charlottenburg Technische Hochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin) before completing his doctorate in mathematics at [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) under [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834) in 1904.

Koebe completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) and was subsequently a lecturer at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) from 1907 to 1910. While there he obtained his famous results on the [uniformization](wikipedia://en/288270#history) of [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surfaces) in a series of papers,[^n1][^n2][^n3][^n4][^n5] representing major progress on the [Twenty Second](wikipedia://en/2336114) of [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s [Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_Problems).[^o9]

Koebe was an [extraordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_professor) at [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) from 1910 to 1914, then an [ordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_professor) at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) before returning to Leipzig in 1926 as an ordinary professor.

Koebe's relationships with his colleagues were contentious. In one incident he publicly trivialized the results of [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) as simple corollaries to his own work.[^o10] In another incident he stole and published results from the thesis of [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238) as his own.[^o11] In a similar incident with [L. E. J. Brouwer](wikipedia://en/172896), Koebe supposedly went as far as to interfere with the printing of Brouwer's paper.[^o12][^o13]

In November 1933, Koebe signed the [Vow of Allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State) to Hitler and the Nazi party.

Koebe never married. He died from stomach cancer, 6 August 1945 in Leipzig, and is buried in Luckenwalde.

He conjectured the [Koebe quarter theorem](wikipedia://en/16758771) on the radii of disks in the images of injective functions, in 1907. His conjecture became a theorem when it was proven by [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) in 1916, and the function $f(z)=z/(1-z)^2$ providing a tight example for this theorem became known as the [Koebe function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koebe_function).[^o14]

Koebe was a member of several German scientific societies including [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o15] [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities), and [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities),[^o16] as well as the [Royal Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences)[^o17] and the [Finnish Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters). Koebe received several mathematical prizes for his work on uniformization: In 1910, he was awarded the Berlin Academy prize;[^o18] in 1922, the [Ackermann–Teubner Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/19181338);[^o19] and in 1927, Koebe received the international mathematics prize of the King of Sweden.[^o20]
