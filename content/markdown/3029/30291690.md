# Sunzi Suanjing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sunzi Suanjing"}

*Image: Sun Zi Suanjing.JPG.* Facsimile of Qing dynasty edition of *The Mathematical Classic of Sun Zi*

***Sunzi Suanjing*** () was a mathematical [treatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise) written during 3rd to 5th centuries CE which was listed as one of the [Ten Computational Canons](wikipedia://en/30269292) during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty). The specific identity of its author Sunzi (lit. "Master Sun") is still unknown but he lived much later than his namesake [Sun Tzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Tzu), author of *[The Art of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_War)*. From the textual evidence in the book, some scholars concluded that the work was completed during the [Southern and Northern Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_and_Northern_Dynasties). Besides describing arithmetic methods and investigating [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109), the treatise touches upon [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_astronomy) and attempts to develop a [calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calendar).

:::

The book is divided into three chapters.

### Chapter 1 {#Chapter_1}

Chapter 1 discusses measurement units of length, weight and capacity, and the rules of [counting rods](wikipedia://en/26661047). Although counting rods were in use in the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period) and there were many ancient books on mathematics such as *[Book on Numbers and Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_on_Numbers_and_Computation)* and *[The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207)*, no detailed account of the rules was given. For the first time, *The Mathematical Classic of Sun Zi* provided a detail description of the rules of counting rods: "one must know the position of the counting rods, the units are vertical, the tens horizontal, the hundreds stand, the thousands prostrate",[^o4] followed by the detailed layout and rules for manipulation of the counting rods in addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with ample examples.

### Chapter 2 {#Chapter_2}

Chapter 2 deals with operational rules for fractions with rod numerals: the reduction, addition, subtraction, and division of fractions, followed by mechanical algorithm for the [extraction of square roots](wikipedia://en/11919629#Extraction_of_Square_root).[^o6]

### Chapter 3 {#Chapter_3}

Chapter 3 contains the earliest example of the [Chinese remainder theorem](wikipedia://en/7713), a key tool to understanding and resolving [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109).
