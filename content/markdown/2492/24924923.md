# Phillip Colella

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Phillip Colella"}

**Phillip Colella** is an American applied mathematician and a member of the Applied Numerical Algorithms Group at the [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory). He has also worked at [Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Livermore_National_Laboratory). He is known for his fundamental contributions in the development of mathematical methods and numerical tools used to solve [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), including [high-resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-resolution_scheme) and [adaptive mesh refinement](wikipedia://en/1822182) schemes. Colella is a member of the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Colella received his bachelor's degree in 1974, Master's degree in 1976, and Ph.D. in 1979 degree from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), all in applied mathematics.[^o5] He received the Ph.D. degree under the supervision of [Alexandre Chorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Chorin). He began his research career at [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory), University of California, California. His primary area of research involves the development of high-resolution schemes and adaptive mesh refinement methods for the solution of partial differential equations. He has also applied [computational methods](wikipedia://en/1678715) in a variety of scientific and engineering fields, including low-speed [incompressible flows](wikipedia://en/461477), [shock wave theory](wikipedia://en/146253), [combustion](wikipedia://en/5638), [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310), and astrophysical flows.[^o6] Colella has also been the leader of a project in [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)'s Computational Technologies for Earth and Space Sciences, called "Block-Structured Adaptive Mesh Refinement Methods for Multiphase Microgravity Flows and Star Formation".[^o3]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Colella is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) since 2004 and Fellow of [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM).[^o8] He is the recipient of many honors, including the [Sidney Fernbach Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Fernbach_Award) from the [IEEE Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Computer_Society) in 1998, given each year to one person who has made "an outstanding contribution in the application of high performance computers using innovative approaches."[^o9] He has also received the [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376)/[ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) prize (with [John Bell](wikipedia://en/31128208)) for computational science and engineering in 2003.[^o10]
