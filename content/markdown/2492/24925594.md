# Fermi–Pustyl'nikov model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fermi–Pustyl'nikov model"}

The **Fermi–Pustyl'nikov model**, named after [Enrico Fermi](wikipedia://en/10264) and [Lev Pustyl'nikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Pustyl'nikov), is a model of the Fermi acceleration mechanism.

:::

A point mass falls with a constant [acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443) vertically on the infinitely heavy horizontal wall, which moves vertically in accordance with analytic periodic law in time. The point interacts with the wall by the law of [elastic collision](wikipedia://en/65907). For this model it was proved that under some general conditions the [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) and [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) of the point at the moments of collisions with the wall tend to infinity for an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) of initial data having the infinite [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198).[^o2] This model was introduced in 1968 in,[^o3] and studied in,[^o2][^o3] by L. D. Pustyl'nikov in connection with the justification of the Fermi acceleration mechanism.

(See also [^o4] and references therein).
