# Red/black concept

*Image: RedBlack.png.* Red/black box

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Red/black concept"}

The **red/black concept**, named in comparison to a [typewriter ribbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typewriter_ribbon) and sometimes called the **red–black architecture**[^o2] or **red/black engineering**,[^o3][^o4] refers to the careful segregation in [cryptographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic) systems of signals that contain sensitive or [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classified_information) [plaintext](wikipedia://en/157935) information (**red signals**) from those that carry encrypted information, or [ciphertext](wikipedia://en/175560) (**black signals**). Therefore, the **red** side is usually considered the internal side, and the **black** side the more public side, with often some sort of [guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_%28information_security%29), [firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_%28computing%29) or [data-diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidirectional_network) between the two.

:::

In [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA) jargon, encryption devices are often called [blackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacker_%28security%29), because they convert red signals to black. [TEMPEST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempest_%28codename%29) standards spelled out in Tempest/[2-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSTISSAM_TEMPEST/2-95) specify shielding or a minimum physical distance between wires or equipment carrying or processing red and black signals.[^o5]

Different organizations have differing requirements for the separation of red and black [fiber-optic cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber-optic_cable).

Red/black terminology is also applied to cryptographic [keys](wikipedia://en/53039). Black keys have themselves been encrypted with a "key encryption key" (KEK) and are therefore benign. Red keys are not encrypted and must be treated as highly sensitive material.[^o6]

## Red/Gray/Black {#Red/Gray/Black}

*Image: NSA Red-Gray-Black diagram.png.* NSA Red-Gray-Black diagram

The NSA's Commercial Solutions for Classified (CSfC) program, which uses two layers of independent, commercial off-the-shelf cryptographic products to protect classified information, includes a red/gray/black concept. In this extension of the red/black concept, the separated gray compartment handles data that has been encrypted only once, which happens at the red/gray boundary. The gray/black interface adds or removes a second layer of encryption.[^o8]
