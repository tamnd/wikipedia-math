# Hilbert–Speiser theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert–Speiser theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hilbert–Speiser theorem** is a result on [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462), characterising those with a [normal integral basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_integral_basis). More generally, it applies to any finite [abelian extension](wikipedia://en/174448) of $\mathbf{Q}$, which by the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](wikipedia://en/1082550) are isomorphic to subfields of cyclotomic fields.

:::

**Hilbert–Speiser Theorem.** A finite abelian extension $K/\mathbf{Q}$ has a normal integral basis if and only if it is [tamely ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamely_ramified) over $\mathbf{Q}$.

This is the condition that it should be a [subfield](wikipedia://en/11634) of $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{n})$ where $n$ is a [squarefree](wikipedia://en/29525) [odd number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_number). This result was introduced by (txt) in his [Zahlbericht](wikipedia://en/33462303) and by (txt, corollary to proposition 8.1).

In cases where the theorem states that a normal integral basis does exist, such a basis may be constructed by means of [Gaussian periods](wikipedia://en/456085). For example if we take $n$ a prime number $p > 2$, $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{p})$ has a normal integral basis consisting of all the $p$-th [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) other than $1$. For a field $K$ contained in it, the [field trace](wikipedia://en/457105) can be used to construct such a basis in $K$ also (see the article on [Gaussian periods](wikipedia://en/456085)). Then in the case of $n$ squarefree and odd, $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{n})$ is a [compositum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositum) of subfields of this type for the primes $p$ dividing $n$ (this follows from a simple argument on ramification). This decomposition can be used to treat any of its subfields.

(txt) proved a converse to the Hilbert–Speiser theorem:

Each finite [tamely ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamely_ramified) [abelian extension](wikipedia://en/174448) $K$ of a fixed [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $J$ has a relative normal integral basis if and only if $J =\mathbf{Q}$.

There is an elliptic analogue of the theorem proven by (txt). It is now called the Srivastav-Taylor theorem (txt).
