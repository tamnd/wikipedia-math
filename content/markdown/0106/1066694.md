# Salem number

*Image: Salem numbers.svg.* Plot of the roots of Lehmer's [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000), with the corresponding Salem number near $x=1.17628$ in gold.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Salem number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Salem number** is a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981) $\alpha > 1$ whose [conjugate roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_roots) all have [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) no greater than 1, and at least one of which has absolute value exactly 1. Salem numbers are of interest in [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558) and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). They are named after [Raphaël Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphaël_Salem).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Because it has a root of absolute value 1, the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) for a Salem number must be a [reciprocal polynomial](wikipedia://en/1106042). This implies that $1/\alpha$ is also a root, and that all other roots have absolute value exactly one. As a consequence α must be a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666) in the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of algebraic integers, being of [norm](wikipedia://en/450555) 1.

Every Salem number is a [Perron number](wikipedia://en/5362893) (a real algebraic number greater than one all of whose conjugates have smaller absolute value).

:::

## Relation with Pisot–Vijayaraghavan numbers {#Relation_with_Pisot–Vijayaraghavan_numbers}

The smallest known Salem number is the largest real root of **Lehmer's polynomial** (named after [Derrick Henry Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Henry_Lehmer))

$$P(x) = x^{10} + x^9 - x^7 - x^6 - x^5 - x^4 - x^3 + x + 1,$$

which is about $x = 1.17628$: it is [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that it is indeed the smallest Salem number, and the smallest possible [Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425) of an [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) non-[cyclotomic](wikipedia://en/171992) polynomial.[^o5]

Lehmer's polynomial is a factor of the shorter [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652)-12 polynomial,

$$Q(x) = x^{12} - x^7 - x^6 - x^5 + 1,$$

all twelve roots of which satisfy the relation[^o6]

$$x^{630}-1 = \frac{(x^{315}-1)(x^{210}-1)(x^{126}-1)^2(x^{90}-1)(x^{3}-1)^3(x^{2}-1)^5(x-1)^3 }{(x^{35}-1)(x^{15}-1)^2(x^{14}-1)^2(x^{5}-1)^6\,x^{68}}$$

Salem numbers can be constructed from [Pisot–Vijayaraghavan numbers](wikipedia://en/519303). To recall, the smallest of the latter is the unique real root of the [cubic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_polynomial),

$$x^3 - x - 1,$$

known as the *[plastic ratio](wikipedia://en/2148532)* and approximately equal to 1.324718. This can be used to generate a family of Salem numbers including the smallest one found so far. The general approach is to take the minimal polynomial $P(x)$ of a Pisot–Vijayaraghavan number and its [reciprocal polynomial](wikipedia://en/1106042), $P^*(x)$, and solve the equation,

$$x^n P(x) = \pm P^{*}(x)$$

for [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ above a bound. Subtracting one side from the other, factoring, and disregarding trivial factors will then yield the minimal polynomial of certain Salem numbers. For example, using the negative case of the above,

$$x^n(x^3 - x - 1) = -(x^3 + x^2 - 1)$$

then for $n=8$, this factors as,

$$(x-1)(x^{10} + x^9 -x^7 -x^6 -x^5 -x^4 -x^3 +x +1) = 0$$

where the [decic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decic_equation) is Lehmer's polynomial. Using higher $n$ will yield a family with a root approaching the plastic ratio. This can be better understood by taking $n$th roots of both sides,

$$x(x^3 - x - 1)^{1/n} = \pm (x^3 + x^2 - 1)^{1/n}$$

so as $n$ goes higher, $x$ will approach the solution of $x^3 - x - 1=0$. If the positive case is used, then $x$ approaches the plastic ratio from the opposite direction. Using the minimal polynomial of the next smallest Pisot–Vijayaraghavan number gives

$$x^n(x^4 - x^3 - 1) = -(x^4 + x - 1),$$

which for $n=7$ factors as

$$(x-1)(x^{10} - x^6 - x^5 - x^4 + 1) = 0,$$

a decic not generated in the previous and has the root $x=1.216391\ldots$ which is the 5th smallest known Salem number. As $n\to\infty$, this family in turn tends towards the larger real root of $x^4 - x^3 - 1 = 0$.
