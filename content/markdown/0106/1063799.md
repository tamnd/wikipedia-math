# Categorical logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Categorical logic"}

**Categorical logic** is the branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in which tools and concepts from [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) are applied to the study of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). It is also notable for its connections to [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).[^o2] In broad terms, categorical logic represents both syntax and semantics by a [category](wikipedia://en/53759), and an [interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) by a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987). The categorical framework provides a rich conceptual background for logical and [type-theoretic](wikipedia://en/40282) constructions. The subject has been recognisable in these terms since around 1970.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

There are three important themes in the categorical approach to logic:

**Categorical semantics**

Categorical logic introduces the notion of *structure valued in a category* **C** with the classical [model theoretic](wikipedia://en/19858) notion of a structure appearing in the particular case where **C** is the [category of sets and functions](wikipedia://en/26829). This notion has proven useful when the [set-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-theoretic) notion of a model lacks generality and/or is inconvenient. [R.A.G. Seely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.A.G._Seely)'s modeling of various [impredicative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impredicative) theories, such as [System F](wikipedia://en/767637), is an example of the usefulness of categorical semantics.

It was found that the [connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) of pre-categorical logic were more clearly understood using the concept of [adjoint functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor), and that the [quantifiers](wikipedia://en/43507260) were also best understood using adjoint functors.[^o4]

**Internal languages**

This can be seen as a formalization and generalization of proof by [diagram chasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagram_chasing). One defines a suitable internal language naming relevant constituents of a category, and then applies categorical semantics to turn assertions in a logic over the internal language into corresponding categorical statements. This has been most successful in the theory of [toposes](wikipedia://en/21391464), where the internal language of a topos together with the semantics of [intuitionistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitionistic) [higher-order logic](wikipedia://en/562883) in a topos enables one to reason about the objects and morphisms of a topos as if they were sets and functions.[^o5] This has been successful in dealing with toposes that have "sets" with properties incompatible with [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868). A prime example is [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875)'s model of [untyped lambda calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untyped_lambda_calculus) in terms of objects that [retract](wikipedia://en/10630303) onto their own [function space](wikipedia://en/340630). Another is the [Moggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Moggi)–Hyland model of [system F](wikipedia://en/767637) by an internal [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) of the [effective topos](wikipedia://en/30939514) of [Martin Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Hyland).

**Term model constructions**

In many cases, the categorical semantics of a logic provide a basis for establishing a correspondence between [theories](wikipedia://en/3512103) in the logic and instances of an appropriate kind of category. A classic example is the correspondence between theories of [βη](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_reduction)-[equational logic](wikipedia://en/33522862) over [simply typed lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/1986011) and [Cartesian closed categories](wikipedia://en/237037). Categories arising from theories via term model constructions can usually be characterized up to [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) by a suitable [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245). This has enabled proofs of [meta-theoretical](wikipedia://en/387934) properties of some logics by means of an appropriate . For instance, [Freyd](wikipedia://en/4593361) gave a proof of the [disjunction and existence properties](wikipedia://en/1010522) of [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) this way.

These three themes are related. The categorical semantics of a logic consists in describing a category of structured categories that is related to the category of theories in that logic by an adjunction, where the two functors in the adjunction give the internal language of a structured category on the one hand, and the term model of a theory on the other.
