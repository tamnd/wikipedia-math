# Mark Kac

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mark Kac"}

**Mark Kac** ( ; [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Language): *Marek Kac*; August 3, 1914 – October 26, 1984) was a [Polish-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His main interest was [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). His question, "[Can one hear the shape of a drum](wikipedia://en/1072325)?" set off research into [spectral geometry](wikipedia://en/20635289), the idea of understanding the extent to which the [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum) allows one to read back the geometry. In the end, the answer was generally "No," although certain shapes, such as a circular drum, can be inferred from the spectrum of the Laplacian.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

He was born to a [Polish-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Jewish) family; their town, [Kremenets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenets) ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Language): "Krzemieniec"), changed hands from the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (by then [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine)) to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) after the [Peace of Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Riga), when Kac was a child.[^o4]

Kac completed his Ph.D. in mathematics at the Polish [University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lwów) in 1937 under the direction of [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735). While there, he was a member of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328).

After receiving his degree, he began to look for a position abroad, and in 1938 was granted a scholarship from the Parnas Foundation, which enabled him to go work in the United States. He arrived in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) in November 1938.[^o6]

With the onset of [World War II in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Europe), Kac was able to stay in the United States, while his parents and brother, who had remained in Kremenets, were [murdered by the Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust) in [mass executions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_execution) in August 1942.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

### Cornell University {#Cornell_University}

From 1939 to 1961, Kac taught at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), an [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) university in [Ithaca, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca,_New_York), where he was first an instructor. In 1943, he was appointed an assistant professor, and he became a full professor in 1947.

While a professor at Cornell, he became a [naturalized US citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_US_citizen) in 1943. From 1943 to 1945, he also worked with [George Uhlenbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Uhlenbeck) at the [MIT Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory).[^o6] During the 1951–1952 academic year, Kac was on [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o10]

In 1952, Kac, with [Theodore H. Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_H._Berlin), introduced the spherical model of a [ferromagnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnet), a variant of the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744),[^o11] and, with [J. C. Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Clive_Ward), found an exact solution of the Ising model using a combinatorial method.[^o12]

In 1956, he introduced a simplified mathematical model known as the [Kac ring](wikipedia://en/74576446), which features the emergence of [macroscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic_scale) irreversibility from completely [time-symmetric](wikipedia://en/151013) [microscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopic_scale) laws. Using the model as an analogy to molecular motion, he provided an explanation for [Loschmidt's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loschmidt's_paradox).[^o13]

### Rockefeller University {#Rockefeller_University}

In 1961, Kac left Cornell and went to [The Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rockefeller_University) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City).

He worked with [George Uhlenbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Uhlenbeck) and [P. C. Hemmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Christian_Hemmer) on the mathematics of a [van der Waals gas](wikipedia://en/206064).[^o15] After twenty years at Rockefeller, he moved to the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) where he spent the rest of his career.

In his 1966 article *[Can one hear the shape of the drum](wikipedia://en/1072325)*, Kac asked whether the [geometric shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_shape) of a drum is uniquely defined by its sound. The answer was negative, meaning two different [resonators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonator) can have identical set of [eigenfrequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenfrequency).

### Human rights {#Human_rights}

Kac was the co-chair of the [Committee of Concerned Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Concerned_Scientists).[^o17] He co-authored a letter which publicized the case of the scientist Vladimir Samuilovich Kislik[^o18] and a letter which publicized the case of the applied mathematician [Yosif Begun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosif_Begun).[^o19]

- 1959 – member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences)[^o22]
- 1965 – member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences)[^o23]

- 1971 – Solvay Lecturer at Brussels
