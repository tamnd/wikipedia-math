# Louis Bachelier

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Louis Bachelier"}

**Louis Jean-Baptiste Alphonse Bachelier** (; 11 March 1870 – 28 April 1946)[^o3] was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is credited with being the first person to model the [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) now called [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), as part of his doctoral thesis *The Theory of Speculation* (*Théorie de la spéculation*, defended in 1900).

:::

Bachelier's doctoral thesis, which introduced the first mathematical model of Brownian motion and its use for valuing [stock options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_options), was the first paper to use advanced mathematics in the study of [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance). His [Bachelier model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelier_model) has been influential in the development of other widely used models, including the [Black-Scholes model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-Scholes_model).

Bachelier is considered as the forefather of [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680) and a pioneer in the study of stochastic processes.

## Early years {#Early_years}

Bachelier was born in [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre), in [Seine-Maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Maritime). His father was a [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine) merchant and [amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur) [scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist), and the vice-consul of [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela) at Le Havre. His mother was the daughter of an important banker (who was also a writer of [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry) books). Both of Louis's parents died just after he completed his high school diploma ("baccalauréat" in French), forcing him to take care of his sister and three-year-old brother and to assume the family business, which effectively put his graduate studies on hold. During this time Bachelier gained a practical acquaintance with the financial markets. His studies were further delayed by [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) service. Bachelier arrived in Paris in 1892 to study at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), where his grades were less than ideal.

## The doctoral thesis {#The_doctoral_thesis}

Defended on 29 March 1900 at the University of Paris,[^o6] Bachelier's thesis was not well received because it attempted to apply mathematics to an area mathematicians found unfamiliar.[^o7] "Too much on finance," wrote [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462).[^o8] However, his instructor, [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740), is recorded as having given some positive feedback (though insufficient to secure Bachelier an immediate teaching position in France at that time). For example, Poincaré called his approach to deriving [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)'s law of errors

very original, and all the more interesting in that [Fourier's](wikipedia://en/25767401) reasoning can be extended with a few changes to the theory of errors. ... It is regrettable that M. Bachelier did not develop this part of his thesis further.

The thesis received a grade of *honorable,* and was accepted for publication in the prestigious *Annales Scientifiques de l’École Normale Supérieure*. While it did not receive a mark of *très honorable*, despite its ultimate importance, the grade assigned is still interpreted as an appreciation for his contribution. [Jean-Michel Courtault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Michel_Courtault) et al. point out in "On the Centenary of *Théorie de la spéculation*"[^o9] that *honorable* was "the highest note which could be awarded for a thesis that was essentially outside mathematics and that had a number of arguments far from being rigorous".

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

For several years following the successful defense of his thesis, Bachelier further developed the theory of [diffusion processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_processes), and was published in prestigious journals. In 1909 he became a "free professor" at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). In 1914, he published a book, *Le Jeu, la Chance, et le Hasard* (Games, Chance, and Randomness), that sold over six thousand copies. With the support of the Council of the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), Bachelier was given a permanent professorship at the Sorbonne, but [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) intervened and he was drafted into the French army as a private. His army service ended on December 31, 1918.[^o11] In 1919, he found a position as an assistant professor in [Besançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Besançon), replacing a regular professor on leave.[^o11] He married Augustine Jeanne Maillot in September 1920 but was soon widowed.[^o11] When the professor returned in 1922, Bachelier replaced another professor at [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon).[^o11] He moved to [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes) in 1925, but was finally awarded a permanent professorship in 1927 at the [University of Besançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Franche-Comté), where he worked for 10 years until his retirement.[^o11]

Besides the setback that the war had caused him, Bachelier was blackballed in 1926 when he attempted to receive a permanent position at Dijon. This was due to a "misinterpretation" of one of Bachelier's papers by Professor [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462), who—to Bachelier's understandable fury—knew nothing of Bachelier's work, nor of the candidate that Lévy recommended above him. Lévy later learned of his error, and reconciled himself with Bachelier.[^o12]

Although Bachelier's work on [random walks](wikipedia://en/235451) predated [Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s celebrated study of [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) by five years, the pioneering nature of his work was recognized only after several decades, first by [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) who pointed out his work to [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462), then by [Leonard Jimmie Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Jimmie_Savage) who translated Bachelier's thesis into English and brought the work of Bachelier to the attention of [Paul Samuelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Samuelson). The arguments Bachelier used in his thesis also predate [Eugene Fama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Fama)'s [efficient-market hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient-market_hypothesis), which is very closely related, as the idea of a random walk is suited to predict the random future in a stock market where everyone has all the available information. His work in finance is recognized as one of the foundations for the [Black–Scholes model](wikipedia://en/113515).
