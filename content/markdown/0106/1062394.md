# Stefan Kaczmarz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stefan Kaczmarz"}

**Stefan Marian Kaczmarz** (Polish: '; 20 March 1895 – 1939) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a representative of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328). He is known for introducing the [Kaczmarz method](wikipedia://en/2407650), which provided the basis for many modern imaging technologies, including the [CAT scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT_scan).[^o4]

:::

Kaczmarz was a professor of mathematics in the faculty of [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) of [Jan Kazimierz University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University) from 1923 to 1939, where he collaborated with [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646).[^o3]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in 1895 in [Sambor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambir), [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria) in [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (now [Sambir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambir), Ukraine), to father Mikołaj, who worked as a court clerk, and mother Emilia (née Smyczyńska). He had three siblings: sister Helena, and two brothers, Roman and Eustachy.[^o3] In 1901–1905, he went to primary school in [Kęty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kęty). He then attended state [gymnasiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in [Wadowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadowice) (1905–1907) and [Tarnów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnów) (1907–1913).[^o7] After graduating with honours, Kaczmarz enrolled at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) to pursue studies in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry). His professors included some prominent figures of the time, such as [Stanisław Zaremba](wikipedia://en/1028345), [Marian Smoluchowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Smoluchowski), [Jan Śleszyński](wikipedia://en/3033762) and [Kazimierz Żorawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Żorawski).[^o3]

### World War I {#World_War_I}

His studies were interrupted by the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I). Kaczmarz enlisted in the [Polish Legions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Legions_in_World_War_I) where he reached the rank of sergeant and participated in the Carpathian Campaign against the Russian forces until 1917. He was subsequently transferred to the School of Artillery in [Walawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walawa).[^o3] Kaczmarz was interned by the Austrian authorities following his refusal to take an oath of allegiance to the emperor. In 1918, he was released and resumed his studies at the university, which he completed in 1922. In 1921–23, he was employed as an assistant in the Department of Mathematics of the [Academy of Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGH_University_of_Kraków) in Kraków. At that time, Kaczmarz additionally worked as a maths teacher at a private school for girls in Kraków.[^o3]

### Lviv period {#Lviv_period}

*Image: Lwowscy matematycy1930.jpg.* Kaczmarz (first row, far right) with fellow Lviv mathematicians in 1930.

In 1923, Kaczmarz relocated to [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) after having received an offer from the [John Casimir University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Casimir_University) arranged for him personally by [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), an extraordinary professor at the university, who was strongly committed to the idea of establishing Lwów as a major research centre in mathematics.[^o3] He taught at the Faculty of Mechanical Engineering and was influenced in his first years in Lwów by his collaborations with [Stanisław Saks](wikipedia://en/362649) and [Stanisław Ruziewicz](wikipedia://en/1821290). He later worked with [Józef Marcinkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Marcinkiewicz) and produced a joint paper on multipliers of orthongonal systems.[^o10] In 1924, he obtained his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) based on his thesis entitled *The relationships between certain functional and differential equations*.[^o11] His findings from the thesis were published in *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)* in the same year.[^o3] In 1925, he was promoted to the rank of second lieutenant in the [Polish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Armed_Forces).[^o12] In 1929, he received his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation). Kaczmarz also took part in the famous discussions at the [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651) attended by renowned mathematicians including [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162), [Władysław Orlicz](wikipedia://en/362718), [Stanisław Ulam](wikipedia://en/41531) and [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425).[^o13]

In 1928, he married teacher Helena Firlcińcka in [Nowy Sącz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowy_Sącz) with whom he had two daughters. In 1932, after having been granted a scholarship by the National Culture Fund, he travelled to [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) where he met [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [Raymond Paley](wikipedia://en/2788568) and participated in lectures on [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) delivered by [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185). He then spent brief time in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) and came back to Lwów. In 1933, he became the recipient of the [Cross of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Independence).[^o14] In 1935, he was appointed an assistant professor at the [Lviv Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Polytechnic). The following year, he became a member of the [Lwów Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_Scientific_Society). In 1937, he was awarded the [Golden Cross of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Merit_%28Poland%29).[^o15]

### Death {#Death}

The circumstances of Kaczmarz's death are unclear. In early September 1939, after [World War II broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), he was called up for Polish military service as a reserve lieutenant. He sent a letter to his wife on 4 September and was not heard from afterwards. Theories for his death include the possibility that he died soon after near [Nisko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nisko) (the source of his last letter to his wife) in a German bombing raid on a train he was traveling in, that he died later that month in combat against the Germans near [Umiastów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umiastów), or that he was murdered by the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD) in April or May 1940 as part of the [Katyń massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre).[^o3]

## Work {#Work}

Kaczmarz belonged to a group of collaborators of [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) and is regarded as a representative of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328).[^o18] He mainly worked in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038), and [orthogonal functions](wikipedia://en/453041). He is perhaps most well-known for introducing the [Kaczmarz method](wikipedia://en/2407650), an [iterative algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterative_algorithm) for solving [linear equation systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equation_system), which laid the foundation for developing many modern imaging technologies, including the [CAT scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT_scan).[^o19] [^o20] It was originally published in his paper entitled *Angenäherte Auflösung von Systemen linearer Gleichungen*[^o21] and appeared in the *Bulletin International de l'Académie Polonaise des Sciences et des Lettres* in 1937 after having been presented to the journal by [Tadeusz Banachiewicz](wikipedia://en/1018238).[^o3]
