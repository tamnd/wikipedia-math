# Pattern theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pattern theory"}

**Pattern theory**, formulated by [Ulf Grenander](wikipedia://en/27178142), is a mathematical [formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formalism_%28mathematics%29) to describe knowledge of the world as [patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern). It differs from other approaches to [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) in that it does not begin by prescribing [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) and machinery to recognize and classify patterns; rather, it prescribes a vocabulary to articulate and recast the pattern concepts in precise language. Broad in its mathematical coverage, Pattern Theory spans [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), as well as local topological and global entropic properties.

:::

In addition to the new algebraic vocabulary, its [statistical](wikipedia://en/26685) approach is novel in its aim to:

- Identify the [hidden variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_variable) of a [data set](wikipedia://en/8495) using real world data rather than artificial stimuli, which was previously commonplace.
- Formulate prior distributions for hidden variables and models for the observed variables that form the vertices of a [Gibbs](wikipedia://en/509709)-like graph.
- Study the randomness and variability of these graphs.
- Create the basic classes of [stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222) models applied by listing the deformations of the patterns.
- Synthesize (sample) from the models, not just analyze signals with them.

The [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) Pattern Theory Group was formed in 1972 by Ulf Grenander.[^o2] Many mathematicians are currently working in this group, noteworthy among them being the [Fields Medalist](wikipedia://en/10859) [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594).[^o3] Mumford regards Grenander as his "guru"[^o4] in Pattern Theory.
