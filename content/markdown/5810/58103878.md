# Peano kernel theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peano kernel theorem"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), the **Peano kernel theorem** is a general result on error bounds for a wide class of numerical approximations (such as [numerical quadratures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_quadrature)), defined in terms of [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functionals). It is attributed to [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $\mathcal{V}[a,b]$ be the space of all functions $f$ that are [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) on $(a,b)$ that are of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) on $[a,b]$, and let $L$ be a [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) on $\mathcal{V}[a,b]$. Assume that that $L$ *annihilates* all polynomials of degree $\leq \nu$, i.e.

$$Lp=0,\qquad \forall p\in\mathbb{P}_\nu[x].$$

Suppose further that for any [bivariate function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivariate_function) $g(x,\theta)$ with $g(x,\cdot),\,g(\cdot,\theta)\in C^{\nu+1}[a,b]$, the following is valid:

$$L\int_a^bg(x,\theta)\,d\theta=\int_a^bLg(x,\theta)\,d\theta,$$

and define the **Peano kernel** of $L$ as

$$k(\theta)=L[(x-\theta)^\nu_+],\qquad\theta\in[a,b],$$

using the notation

$$(x-\theta)^\nu_+ = \begin{cases} (x-\theta)^\nu, & x\geq\theta, \\ 0, & x\leq\theta. \end{cases}$$

The *Peano kernel theorem[^o2][^o5]* states that, if $k\in\mathcal{V}[a,b]$, then for every function $f$ that is $\nu+1$ times [continuously differentiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_differentiable), we have

$$Lf=\frac{1}{\nu!}\int_a^bk(\theta)f^{(\nu+1)}(\theta)\,d\theta.$$

### Bounds {#Bounds}

Several bounds on the value of $Lf$ follow from this result:

$$\begin{aligned} |Lf|&\leq\frac{1}{\nu!}\|k\|_1\|f^{(\nu+1)}\|_\infty\\[5pt] |Lf|&\leq\frac{1}{\nu!}\|k\|_\infty\|f^{(\nu+1)}\|_1\\[5pt] |Lf|&\leq\frac{1}{\nu!}\|k\|_2\|f^{(\nu+1)}\|_2 \end{aligned}$$

where $\|\cdot\|_1$, $\|\cdot\|_2$ and $\|\cdot\|_\infty$are the [taxicab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab_norm), [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/53932) and [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_norm) [norms](wikipedia://en/990534) respectively.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

In practice, the main application of the Peano kernel theorem is to bound the error of an approximation that is exact for all $f\in\mathbb{P}_\nu$. The theorem above follows from the [Taylor polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_polynomial) for $f$ with integral remainder:

$$\begin{aligned} f(x)=f(a) + {} & (x-a)f'(a) + \frac{(x-a)^2}{2}f''(a)+\cdots \\[6pt] & \cdots+\frac{(x-a)^\nu}{\nu!}f^{(\nu)}(a)+ \frac{1}{\nu!}\int_a^x(x-\theta)^\nu f^{(\nu+1)}(\theta)\,d\theta, \end{aligned}$$

defining $L(f)$ as the error of the approximation, using the [linearity](wikipedia://en/18102) of $L$ together with exactness for $f\in\mathbb{P}_\nu$ to annihilate all but the final term on the right-hand side, and using the $(\cdot)_+$ notation to remove the $x$-dependence from the integral limits.[^o9]
