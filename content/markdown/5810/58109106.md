# Layered permutation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Layered permutation"}

In the mathematics of [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027), a **layered permutation** is a permutation that reverses contiguous blocks of elements. Equivalently, it is the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/36187558) of decreasing permutations.

:::

One of the earlier works establishing the significance of layered permutations was (Bóna 1999), which established the [Stanley–Wilf conjecture](wikipedia://en/7892663) for classes of permutations forbidding a layered permutation, before the conjecture was proven more generally.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

For instance, the layered permutations of length four, with the reversed blocks separated by spaces, are the eight permutations

1 2 3 4

1 2 43

1 32 4

1 432

21 3 4

21 43

321 4

4321

:::

## Characterization by forbidden patterns {#Characterization_by_forbidden_patterns}

The layered permutations can also be equivalently described as the permutations that do not contain the [permutation patterns](wikipedia://en/24360911) 231 or 312. That is, no three elements in the permutation (regardless of whether they are consecutive) have the same ordering as either of these forbidden triples.

::: {#o7 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Enumeration"}

## Enumeration {#Enumeration}

A layered permutation on the numbers from $1$ to $n$ can be uniquely described by the subset of the numbers from $1$ to $n-1$ that are the first element in a reversed block. (The number $n$ is always the first element in its reversed block, so it is redundant for this description.) Because there are $2^{n-1}$ subsets of the numbers from $1$ to $n-1$, there are also $2^{n-1}$ layered permutation of length $n$.

The layered permutations are [Wilf equivalent](wikipedia://en/44185220) to other permutation classes, meaning that the numbers of permutations of each length are the same. For instance, the [Gilbreath permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbreath_permutation) are counted by the same function $2^{n-1}$.

:::

## Superpatterns {#Superpatterns}

The shortest [superpattern](wikipedia://en/7371807) of the layered permutations of length $n$ is itself a layered permutation. Its length is a [sorting number](wikipedia://en/58123003), the number of comparisons needed for [binary insertion sort](wikipedia://en/15205) to sort $n+1$ elements. For $n=1,2,3,\dots$ these numbers are

1, 3, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 21, 25, 29, 33, 37, ...

and in general they are given by the formula

$$(n+1)\bigl\lceil\log_2 (n+1)\bigr\rceil - 2^{\left\lceil\log_2 (n+1)\right\rceil} + 1.$$

## Related permutation classes {#Related_permutation_classes}

Every layered permutation is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450). They are exactly the 231-avoiding involutions, and they are also exactly the 312-avoiding involutions.

The layered permutations are a subset of the [stack-sortable permutations](wikipedia://en/39287529), which forbid the pattern 231 but not the pattern 312. Like the stack-sortable permutations, they are also a subset of the [separable permutations](wikipedia://en/24360777), the permutations formed by recursive combinations of direct and skew sums.
