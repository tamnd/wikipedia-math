# Hanner's inequalities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hanner's inequalities"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Hanner's inequalities** are results in the theory of [*L*<sup>*p*</sup> spaces](wikipedia://en/45194). Their proof was published in 1956 by [Olof Hanner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olof_Hanner). They provide a simpler way of proving the [uniform convexity](wikipedia://en/9133418) of *L*<sup>*p*</sup> spaces for *p* ∈ (1, +∞) than the approach proposed by [James A. Clarkson](wikipedia://en/38164836) in 1936.

:::

## Statement of the inequalities {#Statement_of_the_inequalities}

Let *f*, *g* ∈ *L*<sup>*p*</sup>(*E*), where *E* is any [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270). If *p* ∈ \[1, 2\], then

$$\|f+g\|_p^p + \|f-g\|_p^p \geq \big( \|f\|_p + \|g\|_p \big)^p + \big| \|f\|_p-\|g\|_p \big|^p.$$

The substitutions *F* = *f* + *g* and *G* = *f* − *g* yield the second of Hanner's inequalities:

$$2^p \big( \|F\|_p^p + \|G\|_p^p \big) \geq \big( \|F+G\|_p + \|F-G\|_p \big)^p + \big| \|F+G\|_p-\|F-G\|_p \big|^p.$$

For *p* ∈ \[2, +∞) the inequalities are reversed (they remain non-strict).

Note that for $p = 2$ the inequalities become equalities which are both the [parallelogram rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallelogram_rule).
