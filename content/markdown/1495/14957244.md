# Trisected perimeter point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trisected perimeter point"}

*Image: Trisected perimeter point 3-4-5.svg.* The trisected perimeter point of a 3-4-5 right triangle. For this triangle, *C´B* = *A´C* and *BA´* = *CB´*, but that is not the case for triangles of other shapes.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), given a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) *ABC*, there exist unique [points](wikipedia://en/593693) *A´*, *B´*, and *C´* on the sides *BC*, *CA*, *AB* respectively, such that:

:::

- *A´*, *B´*, and *C´* partition the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the triangle into three equal-length pieces. That is,

*C´B* + *BA´* = *B´A* + *AC´* = *A´C* + *CB´*.

- The three lines *AA´*, *BB´*, and *CC´* meet in a point, the **trisected perimeter point**.

This is point *X*<sub>369</sub> in Clark Kimberling's *Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers*.[^o3] Uniqueness and a formula for the [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of *X*<sub>369</sub> were shown by Peter Yff late in the twentieth century. The formula involves the unique real root of a [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787).[^o3]
