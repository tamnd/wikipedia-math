# Rating scales for depression

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rating scales for depression"}

A **depression rating scale** is a [psychometric](wikipedia://en/24982) instrument (tool), usually a [questionnaire](wikipedia://en/956483) whose wording has been [validated](wikipedia://en/21526661) with [experimental evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_practice), having descriptive words and phrases that indicate the severity of [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder) for a time period.[^o3] When used, an observer may make judgements and rate a person at a specified scale level with respect to identified characteristics. Rather than being used to diagnose depression, a depression rating scale may be used to assign a score to a person's behaviour where that score may be used to determine whether that person should be evaluated more thoroughly for a [depressive disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_disorder) diagnosis.[^o3] Several [rating scales](wikipedia://en/5216974) are used for this purpose.[^o3]

:::

## Scales completed by clinicians, researchers, and workers {#Scales_completed_by_clinicians,_researchers,_and_workers}

Some depression rating scales are completed by clinicians or researchers. The [Hamilton Depression Rating Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Depression_Rating_Scale) includes twenty-one questions, each having three to five possible responses that reflect increasing or decreasing severity. The clinician must choose the possible responses to each question by interviewing the patient and by observing the patient's symptoms. Designed by psychiatrist [Max Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Hamilton) in 1960, the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale is one of the two most commonly used among those completed by clinicians and researchers in assessing the effects of drug therapy.[^o5][^o6] Alternatively, the [Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery-Åsberg_Depression_Rating_Scale) (MADRS) features ten items to be completed for the purpose of assessing the effects of drug therapy,[^o5][^o7] Another scale is the [Raskin Depression Rating Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raskin_Depression_Rating_Scale) rating the severity of the patients' symptoms in three areas: verbal reports, behavior, and secondary symptoms of depression.[^o8] Finally, the [Occupational Depression Inventory](wikipedia://en/73590677)[^o9][^o10] specifically focuses on depressive symptoms that people attribute to their jobs.[^o11][^o12]

## Scales completed by patients {#Scales_completed_by_patients}

**The two questions on the Patient Health Questionnaire-2 (PHQ-2):**[^o20] ---- During the past month, have you often been bothered by feeling down, depressed, or hopeless? ---- During the past month, have you often been bothered by little interest or pleasure in doing things?

Some depression rating scales are completed by patients. The [Beck Depression Inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck_Depression_Inventory), for example, is a 21-question [self-report inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-report_inventory) that covers symptoms such as irritability, fatigue, weight loss, lack of interest in sex, and feelings of guilt, hopelessness or fear of being punished.[^o14] The scale is completed by patients to identify the presence and severity of symptoms consistent with the [DSM-IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnostic_and_Statistical_Manual_of_Mental_Disorders) diagnostic criteria.[^o15] The Beck Depression Inventory was originally designed by psychiatrist [Aaron T. Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Temkin_Beck) in 1961.[^o14]

The [Geriatric Depression Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geriatric_Depression_Scale) (GDS) is another self-administered scale, but in this case it is used for older patients, and for patients with mild to moderate [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia). Instead of presenting a five-category response set, the GDS questions are answered with a simple "yes" or "no".[^o16][^o17] The [Zung Self-Rating Depression Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zung_Self-Rating_Depression_Scale) is similar to the Geriatric Depression Scale in that the answers are preformatted. In the Zung Self-Rating Depression Scale, there are 20 items: ten positively worded and ten negatively worded. Each question is rated on a scale of 1 through 4 based on four possible answers: "a little of the time", "some of the time", "good part of the time", and "most of the time".[^o18]

The [Patient Health Questionnaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Health_Questionnaire) (PHQ) sets are self-reported depression rating scales. For example, the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 ([PHQ-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHQ-9)) is a self-reported, 9-question version of the Primary Care Evaluation of Mental Disorders.[^o19] The Patient Health Questionnaire-2 (PHQ-2) is a shorter version of the PHQ-9 with two screening questions to assess the presence of a [depressed mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depressed_mood) and a [loss of interest or pleasure in routine activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhedonia); a positive response to either question indicates further testing is required.[^o20]

## Scales completed by patients and researchers {#Scales_completed_by_patients_and_researchers}

The **Prim**ary Care **E**valuation of **M**ental **D**isorders (PRIME-MD) is completed by the patient and a researcher. This depression rating scale includes a 27-item screening questionnaire and follow-up clinician interview designed to facilitate the diagnosis of common mental disorders in primary care. Its lengthy administration time has limited its clinical usefulness; it has been replaced by the [Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Health_Questionnaire).[^o19]

## Usefulness {#Usefulness}

[Screening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screening_%28medicine%29) programs using rating scales to search for candidates for a more in-depth evaluation have been advocated to improve detection of depression, but there is evidence that they do not improve detection rates, treatment, or outcome.[^o23] There is also evidence that a consensus on the interpretation of rating scales, in particular the Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression, is largely missing, leading to misdiagnosis of the severity of a patient's depression.[^o24] However, there is evidence that portions of rating scales, such as the [somatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_symptom_disorder) section of the PHQ-9, can be useful in predicting outcomes for subgroups of patients like [coronary heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_heart_disease) patients.[^o25]

## Mathematical models of the distributions on rating scales for depression {#Mathematical_models_of_the_distributions_on_rating_scales_for_depression}

Because the majority of individuals in the general population have a few or no depressive symptoms, the item score and total score distributions on rating scales for depression are usually skewed to the right.[^o19] Recent research has revealed that item score and total score distributions on such scales exhibit a common mathematical pattern in general population samples.[^o27] Specifically, total scores on these scales follow an exponential distribution, except for the lower end of the distribution.[^o28] Furthermore, responses to all depressive symptom items show a proportional relationship between response options, except for the lower end option.[^o29] These findings have been confirmed repeatedly by analyzing large-scale data collected from dozens of nationally representative surveys in the US, EU, and Japan.[^o30][^o31][^o32]

## Copyrighted vs. public domain scales {#Copyrighted_vs._public_domain_scales}

The Beck Depression Inventory is [copyrighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright), a fee must be paid for each copy used, and [photocopying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photocopier) it is a violation of copyright. There is no evidence that the BDI-II is more valid or reliable than other depression scales,[^o34] and [public domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_domain) scales such as the Major Depression Inventory, the revised Center for Epidemiological Studies Depression Scale (CES-D-R), the Zung Depression scale and [Patient Health Questionnaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Health_Questionnaire) – Nine Item (PHQ-9) have been studied as a useful tools.[^o35][^o36][^o37]

Other copyrighted scales allow individual clinicians and researchers to make copies for their own use, but require licenses for electronic versions or large-scale redistribution.
