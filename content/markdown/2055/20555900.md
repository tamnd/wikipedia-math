# Traffic light rating system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Traffic light rating system"}

A **traffic light rating system** is a system for indicating the status of a variable using the red, amber or green of [traffic lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_light).

:::

## Product labelling {#Product_labelling}

### Food and drink {#Food_and_drink}

Food may be labelled with a **traffic light label** showing how much [fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat), [saturated fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_fat), [sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar) and [salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt) are in that food by using the [traffic light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_light) signals for high (*red*), medium (*amber*) and low (*green*) percentages for each of these ingredients. Foods with 'green' indicators are healthier and to be preferred over those with 'red' ones.[^o4] The label is on the front of the package and easier to spot and interpret than [Guideline Daily Amount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guideline_Daily_Amount) (GDA) labelling which will continue. The GDA is difficult to understand for many, including children, and does not lend itself to quick comparisons. The use of traffic light labelling is supported by many physician groups including the British Medical Association and welcomed by consumers.[^o5] Despite worries from some in the food industry that red foods would be shunned, the [British Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Medical_Association), [Food Standards Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_Standards_Agency) and others agree that consumers interpret the labels sensibly and realise they can have red foods as a treat, and these labels are easier to understand than lists of percentages.[^o5]

*Food[^o7]*

::: {#o6 .table cue=table name="Food"}

| Substance | Green (low) per 100g | Amber (medium) per 100g | Red (high) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fat** | less than 3g | between 3g and 17.5g | more than 17.5g per 100g **or** 21g per portion |
| **Saturated fats** | less than 1.5g | between 1.5g and 5g | more than 5g per 100g **or** 6g per portion |
| **Sugar** | less than 5g | between 5g and 22.5g | more than 22.5g per 100g **or** 27g per portion |
| **Salt** | less than 0.3g | between 0.3g and 1.5g | more than 1.5g per 100g **or** 1.8g per portion |

:::

*Drinks[^o7]*

::: {#o8 .table cue=table name="Drinks"}

| Substance | Green (low) per 100ml | Amber (medium) per 100ml | Red (high) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fat** | less than 1.5g | between 1.5g and 8.75g | more than 8.75g per 100ml **or** 10.5g per portion |
| **Saturated fats** | less than 0.75g | between 0.75g and 2.5g | more than 2.5g per 100ml **or** 3g per portion |
| **Sugar** | less than 2.5g | between 2.5g and 11.25g | more than 11.25g per 100ml **or** 13.5g per portion |
| **Salt** | less than 0.3g | between 0.3g and 0.75g | more than 0.75g per 100ml **or** 0.9g per portion |

:::

Currently the traffic light label is used in some European countries on a voluntary basis.

### Other labelling {#Other_labelling}

[White goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_goods) must be labelled with a [label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_energy_label) indicating energy consumption. This is not strictly a 'traffic light' but by analogy a scale of seven values that run from red to green through intermediate colours.

The system may also be used for the European Game Information System ([PEGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGI)),[^o10] which puts age ratings onto [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game). Again this is not strictly a 'traffic light' since there are likely to be more than three colours.

## Performance monitoring {#Performance_monitoring}

In many factories, different stations on the production line(s) are equipped with factory monitoring and control systems; attached to such systems is a 'traffic light' status indicator which is generally visible from many places within the factory. Green typically indicates normal levels of production; amber indicates that production has slowed (or attention is otherwise warranted); red indicates that production has stopped or the line is down.

In the [British Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Civil_Service) and other [departments of the United Kingdom government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_the_United_Kingdom_government), traffic light colours are used as a coding system for good or bad performance, usually known as a 'RAG rating'—Red, Amber, Green. For example, a red workload performance would mean inadequate, amber would mean reasonable, and green would mean good. The letters R, A and G are used in addition to swatches of colour, so that the system can be used by [colour-blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colour_blindness) readers.

### Project management {#Project_management}

When status reporting how well a [milestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestone), [project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project), [program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_management) or [portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_portfolio_management) is performing or being delivered, [project managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_manager) often use a RAG rating to indicate how [on track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management_triangle) or [at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management) is the project, its [deliverables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deliverable) or [tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_%28project_management%29).[^o13]

In some cases, additional colours are adopted to communicate nuances of status. For example, blue to show where milestones or components of a milestone, project, programme, or portfolio are complete—referred to as BRAG (Creator: Sam Rafferty).[^o14]

## Behavioural {#Behavioural}

Some organisations, such as the [U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Navy), use traffic light terminology for [sexual harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_harassment) education. Green light behaviour is normal discussions or actions, such as discussing work or assisting someone on stairs. Amber light behaviour is potentially offensive behavior, such as sexist jokes or patting someone on the behind. Red light behaviour is obvious sexual harassment such as sexual requests.

Various [school cafeterias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafeteria) in the United States also use a traffic light like device to monitor noise levels among students. A green light means that students are talking quietly, an amber light means that noise is increasing and that the pupils need to quiet down. At a red light, pupils voices are too loud and the device will emit a loud beeping sound to warn the students to stop talking. A red light can also result in disciplinary action from teachers such as reduced [recess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recess_%28break%29) time.[^o16]
