# Perron's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perron's formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more particularly in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), **Perron's formula** is a formula discovered by [Oskar Perron](wikipedia://en/1485469) to calculate the sum of an [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170), by means of an inverse [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $\{a(n)\}$ be an [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170), and let

$$g(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}$$

be the corresponding [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258). Presume the Dirichlet series to be [uniformly convergent](wikipedia://en/50652) for $\Re(s)>\sigma$. Then Perron's formula is

$$A(x) = {\sum_{n\le x}}' a(n) =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty} g(z)\frac{x^{z}}{z} \,dz.$$

Here, the prime on the summation indicates that the last term of the sum must be multiplied by 1/2 when *x* is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). The integral is not a convergent [Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288); it is understood as the [Cauchy principal value](wikipedia://en/408195). The formula requires that *c* > 0, *c* > σ, and *x* > 0.

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

An easy sketch of the proof comes from taking [Abel's sum formula](wikipedia://en/9747155)

$$g(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a(n)}{n^{s} }=s\int_{1}^{\infty} A(x)x^{-(s+1) } dx.$$

This is nothing but a [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) under the variable change $x = e^t.$ Inverting it one gets Perron's formula.

Proofs of Perron's formula have been published by [Tom M. Apostol](wikipedia://en/2196529)[^o7] and by [Gérald Tenenbaum](wikipedia://en/36258646).[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Because of its general relationship to Dirichlet series, the formula is commonly applied to many number-theoretic sums. Thus, for example, one has the famous integral representation for the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809):

$$\zeta(s)=s\int_1^\infty \frac{\lfloor x\rfloor}{x^{s+1}}\,dx$$

and a similar formula for [Dirichlet *L*-functions](wikipedia://en/400092):

$$L(s,\chi)=s\int_1^\infty \frac{A(x)}{x^{s+1}}\,dx$$

where

$$A(x)=\sum_{n\le x} \chi(n)$$

and $\chi(n)$ is a [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515). Other examples appear in the articles on the [Mertens function](wikipedia://en/435639) and the [von Mangoldt function](wikipedia://en/1928862).

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Perron's formula is a special case of the formula

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a(n)f(n/x)= \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}F(s)G(s)x^{s}ds$$

where

$$G(s)= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}$$

and

$$F(s)= \int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)x^{s-1}dx$$

the Mellin transform. The Perron formula is the special case of the test function $f(1/x)=\theta (x-1),$ for $\theta(x)$ the [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299).

:::
