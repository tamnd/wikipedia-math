# Sridhara

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sridhara"}

**Śrīdhara** or **Śrīdharācārya** (8th–9th century) was an [Indian mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_mathematician), known for two extant treatises about [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) and practical mathematics, *[Pātīgaṇita](wikipedia://en/77782419)* and *Pāṭīgaṇita-sāra*, and a now-lost treatise about [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), *Bījagaṇita*.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Very little is known about Śrīdhara's life beyond mentions of his mathematical work by later mathematicians and the content of his extant treatises, which do not contain biographical details such as his parents, teachers, or birthplace.[^n1] Various scholars have suggested he came from the [Bengal region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_region) or from [South India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India).[^o4] But at the end of his own composition Nyāyakaṇḍalī he mentions himself as a native resident of [Bhurishreshtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhurshut) village in [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), which is currently located in [Hooghly district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooghly_district) of [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal). His father's name was Baladev Acharya and his mother's name was Achhoka Devi.[^o5] Based on example problems in his works mentioning [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva), and a dedication in *Pāṭīgaṇita-sāra*, he was probably a [Shaivite Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaivism).[^n1]

He was mentioned by [Bhāskara II](wikipedia://en/480509) (12th century), and made apparent reference to [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206) (7th century). [Govindasvāmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govindasvāmin) (9th century) quoted a passage also found in *Pāṭīgaṇita-sāra*, and overlapping material is found in the work of [Mahāvīra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahāvīra_%28mathematician%29) (9th century), from which historians estimate Śrīdhara to have lived in the 8th or early 9th century.[^o7]

He has sometimes been conflated with other medieval Indian scholars also named Śrīdhara.[^n1]

## Works {#Works}

Śrīdhara wrote two extant mathematical treatises. The first, *Pāṭīgaṇita*, also called *Bṛhat-Pāṭi* ("Bigger *Pāṭi*") and *Navaśatī* ("Having 900"), extensively covered the practical mathematics of the time including [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) and [mensuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mensuration_%28mathematics%29) (the part of geometry concerned with calculating sizes, lengths, areas, and volumes).[^n1] It is believed to have originally included 900 stanzas, but only 251 are extant, and many topics mentioned in the table of contents have been lost. The second, *Pāṭīgaṇita-sāra*, also called *Triśatikā* ("Having 300") because it was written in three hundred verses, is an abridged summary of *Pāṭīgaṇita*.[^n1] It discusses counting of numbers, natural number, zero, measures, multiplication, fraction, division, squares, cubes, [rule of three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_%28mathematics%29), interest-calculation, joint business or partnership, and mensuration.

He also wrote a work on algebra, *Bījagaṇita*, which has been lost, but some quotations remain in the works of later mathematicians. Some historians believe that Śrīdhara may have authored another mathematical treatise called *Gaṇita-pan̄caviṁśī*.[^o12]

His notable works include–[^n2]

- He gave an exposition on the zero. He wrote, "If zero is added to any number, the sum is the same number; if zero is subtracted from any number, the number remains unchanged; if zero is multiplied by any number, the product is zero".
- In the case of dividing a fraction he has found out the method of multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the divisor.
- He presented a method of [completing the square](wikipedia://en/303500) to solve [quadratic equations](wikipedia://en/25175), sometimes called *Śrīdhara's method* or the *Hindu method*. Because the [quadratic formula](wikipedia://en/59217) can be derived by completing the square for a generic quadratic equation with symbolic coefficients, it is called *Śrīdharācārya's formula* in some places.
