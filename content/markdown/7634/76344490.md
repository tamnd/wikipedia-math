# Geometric symmetry (book)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric symmetry"}

***Geometric symmetry*** is a book by mathematician E.H. Lockwood and design engineer R.H. Macmillan published by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) in 1978. The subject matter of the book is [symmetry](wikipedia://en/44362809) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

## Structure and topics {#Structure_and_topics}

The book is divided into two parts. The first part (chapters 1-13) is largely descriptive and written for the non-mathematical reader. The second part (chapters 14-27) is more mathematical, but only elementary geometrical knowledge is assumed.

In the first part the authors describe and illustrate the following topics: [symmetry elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_element), [frieze patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieze_pattern), [wallpaper patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallpaper_pattern), and [rod](wikipedia://en/32186921), [layer](wikipedia://en/32187203) and [space](wikipedia://en/469246) patterns. The first part also introduces the concepts of [continuous](wikipedia://en/3667668), [dilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_invariance), [dichromatic](wikipedia://en/73498718) and [polychromatic symmetry](wikipedia://en/73619197).

In the second part the authors revisit all of the topics from the first part; but in more detail, and with greater mathematical rigour. [Group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) are the foundations of the material in the second part of the book. A detailed analysis of the subject matter is given in the appendix below.

The book is printed in two colours, red and black, to facilitate the identification of colour symmetry in patterns.

## Audience {#Audience}

In the preface the authors state: "In this book we attempt to provide a fairly comprehensive account of symmetry in a form acceptable to readers without much mathematical knowledge \[...\] The treatment is geometrical which should appeal to art students and to readers whose mathematical interests are that way inclined." However, Joseph H. Gehringer in a review in *[The Mathematics Teacher](wikipedia://en/1561691#Journals)* commented "Clearly not intended as a popular treatment of symmetry, the style of the authors is both concise and technical \[...\] this volume will appeal primarily to those devoting special attention to this field,"[^o5]

## Reception {#Reception}

The reception of the book was mixed.

- [William J. Firey](wikipedia://en/57935108) in his review for [zbMATH Open](wikipedia://en/1701961) praised the book for its "beautiful and appropriate figures" but criticised "a certain imprecision about such fundamental notions as pattern, ornament, crystal."[^o7]
- [H.S.M. Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) in [Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646) also praised "this beautifully illustrated book", but took issue with the authors' artificially restricted approach to colour symmetry, describing it as "unfortunate" in comparison to that of [A.V. Shubnikov](wikipedia://en/70215429) and [V.A. Koptsik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Alexandrovich_Koptsik) in *[Symmetry in Science and Art](wikipedia://en/76514470)*, and [C.H. MacGillavry's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Henriette_MacGillavry) analysis of [M.C. Escher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.C._Escher)'s work in *[Symmetry aspects of M. C. Escher's periodic drawings](wikipedia://en/76352984)*.[^o8]
- [H. Martyn Cundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Martyn_Cundy) in [The Mathematical Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Gazette) wrote positively about the book: "It assumes little beyond the most elementary knowledge, develops the mathematics it needs as it goes along \[...\] and conveys the reader on a fascinating journey from the human face to polychromatic symmetry groups in three dimensions." and concluded by saying "Altogether this is a wholly delightful volume, obviously destined to be a classic work of reference which every school and college library will need and want to have, and to be treasured as a thing of beauty and a possession for ever by all lovers of geometry." However, Cundy also had some criticisms of the selection of material, and of the use of some non-standard terminology.[^o9]
- In 1987, [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) and [Geoffrey Colin Shephard](wikipedia://en/33354532) writing in *[Tilings and patterns](wikipedia://en/76301624)* criticised the authors and their Russian predecessor [N.V.Belov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Belov_%28geochemist%29) by saying that it is "clear that the groups of colour symmetries of chromatic plane patterns were not in the authors' minds."[^o10]

- Reprint edition published by Cambridge University Press in 2008[^o13]
