# The Drunkard's Walk

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="The Drunkard's Walk"}

***The Drunkard's Walk: How Randomness Rules Our Lives*** is a 2008 popular science book by American physicist and author [Leonard Mlodinow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Mlodinow), which became a *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* bestseller and a *New York Times* notable book.[^o4]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

*The Drunkard's Walk* discusses the role of randomness in everyday events, and the [cognitive biases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_bias) that lead people to misinterpret random events and [stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222) processes. The title refers to a certain type of [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451), a mathematical process in which one or more variables change value under a series of random steps. Mlodinow discusses the contributions of mathematical heavyweights [Jacob Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/64740), [Pierre-Simon Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783), and [Blaise Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068), and introduces basic statistical concepts such as [regression toward the mean](wikipedia://en/186028) and the [law of large numbers](wikipedia://en/157055), while discussing the role of probability in examples from wine ratings and school grades to political polls.[^o4]

## Reception {#Reception}

In 2008 the [Committee for Skeptical Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_Skeptical_Inquiry) (CSICOP) awarded Mlodinow the Robert P. Balles Prize for Critical Thinking for the book.[^o6]
