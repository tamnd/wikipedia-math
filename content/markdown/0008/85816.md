# Complete graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complete graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **complete graph** is a [simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph) [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) in which every pair of distinct [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) is connected by a unique [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29). A **complete digraph** is a [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) in which every pair of distinct vertices is connected by a pair of unique edges (one in each direction).[^o3]

:::

Graph theory itself is typically dated as beginning with [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902)'s 1736 work on the [Seven Bridges of Königsberg](wikipedia://en/192753). However, [drawings](wikipedia://en/325813) of complete graphs, with their vertices placed on the points of a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306), had already appeared in the 13th century, in the work of [Ramon Llull](wikipedia://en/69677).[^o4] Such a drawing is sometimes referred to as a **mystic rose**.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The complete graph on $n$ vertices is denoted by $K_{n}$. Some sources claim that the letter $K$ in this notation stands for the German word komplett,[^o8] but the German name for a complete graph, vollständiger Graph, does not contain the letter $K$, and other sources state that the notation honors the contributions of [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) to graph theory.[^o9]

$K_{n}$ has $n(n - 1)/2$ edges (a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915)), and is a [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) of [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) $n - 1$. All complete graphs are their own [maximal cliques](wikipedia://en/524466). They are maximally [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) as the only [vertex cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_cut) which disconnects the graph is the complete set of vertices. The [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649) of a complete graph is an [empty graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_graph).

If the edges of a complete graph are each given an [orientation](wikipedia://en/36619818), the resulting [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) is called a [tournament](wikipedia://en/1427502).

$K_{n}$ can be decomposed into $n$ trees $T_{i}$ such that $T_{i}$ has $i$ vertices.[^o10] Ringel's conjecture asks if the complete graph $K_{2n+1}$ can be decomposed into copies of any tree with $n$ edges.[^o11] This is known to be true for sufficiently large $n$.[^o12][^o13]

The number of all distinct [paths](wikipedia://en/638889) between a specific pair of vertices in $K_{n+2}$ is given[^o14] by

$$w_{n+2} = n! e_n = \lfloor en!\rfloor,$$

where $e$ refers to [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/9633), and

$$e_n = \sum_{k=0}^n\frac{1}{k!}.$$

The number of [matchings](wikipedia://en/581797) of the complete graphs are given by the [telephone numbers](wikipedia://en/35258497)

1, 1, 2, 4, 10, 26, 76, 232, 764, 2620, 9496, 35696, 140152, 568504, 2390480, 10349536, 46206736, ... .

These numbers give the largest possible value of the [Hosoya index](wikipedia://en/14221581) for an $n$-vertex graph.[^o15] The number of [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031) of the complete graph $K_{n}$ (with $n$ even) is given by the [double factorial](wikipedia://en/507208) $(n - 1)!!$.[^o16]

The [crossing numbers](wikipedia://en/18298594) up to $K_{27}$ are known, with $K_{28}$ requiring either 7233 or 7234 crossings. Further values are collected by the Rectilinear Crossing Number project.[^o17] Rectilinear Crossing numbers for $K_{n}$ are

0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 3, 9, 19, 36, 62, 102, 153, 229, 324, 447, 603, 798, 1029, 1318, 1657, 2055, 2528, 3077, 3699, 4430, 5250, 6180, ... .

:::

## Geometry and topology {#Geometry_and_topology}

*Image: Csaszar\_polyhedron\_3D\_model.svg.* Interactive [Csaszar polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csaszar_polyhedron) model with vertices representing nodes. In [the SVG image](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/Csaszar_polyhedron_3D_model.svg), move the mouse to rotate it.[^o19]

A complete graph with $n$ nodes is the [edge graph](wikipedia://en/74718534) of an $(n - 1)$-dimensional [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781). Geometrically $K_{3}$ forms the edge set of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), $K_{4}$ a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), etc. The [Császár polyhedron](wikipedia://en/11731171), a nonconvex polyhedron with the topology of a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800), has the complete graph $K_{7}$ as its [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_%28topology%29).[^o20] Every [neighborly polytope](wikipedia://en/26212072) in four or more dimensions also has a complete skeleton.

$K_{1}$ through $K_{4}$ are all [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314). However, every planar drawing of a complete graph with five or more vertices must contain a crossing, and the nonplanar complete graph $K_{5}$ plays a key role in the characterizations of planar graphs: by [Kuratowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/54493), a graph is planar if and only if it contains neither $K_{5}$ nor the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{3,3}$ as a subdivision, and by [Wagner's theorem](wikipedia://en/3126130) the same result holds for [graph minors](wikipedia://en/353042) in place of subdivisions. As part of the [Petersen family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersen_family), $K_{6}$ plays a similar role as one of the [forbidden minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_minor) for [linkless embedding](wikipedia://en/772031).[^o21] In other words, and as Conway and Gordon[^o22] proved, every embedding of $K_{6}$ into three-dimensional space is intrinsically linked, with at least one pair of linked triangles. Conway and Gordon also showed that any three-dimensional embedding of $K_{7}$ contains a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) that is embedded in space as a [nontrivial knot](wikipedia://en/366808).
