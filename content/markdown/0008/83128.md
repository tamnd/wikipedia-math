# Georg Mohr

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Georg Mohr"}

*Image: Georg Mohr.png.*

**Jørgen Mohr** (Latinised *Georg(ius) Mohr*; 1 April 1640 – 26 January 1697)[^o3] was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for being the first to prove the [Mohr–Mascheroni theorem](wikipedia://en/1196909), which states that any geometric construction which can be done with [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge) can also be done with compasses alone.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Mohr was born in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen), the son of a tradesman named David Mohrendal. Beginning in 1662 he traveled to the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), to study mathematics with [Christiaan Huygens](wikipedia://en/42127).[^o3][^o4] In 1672 he published his first book, *[Euclides Danicus](wikipedia://en/43156677)*, simultaneously in Copenhagen and Amsterdam, in Danish and Dutch respectively. This book, proving the Mohr–Mascheroni theorem 125 years earlier than [Lorenzo Mascheroni](wikipedia://en/88116), would languish in obscurity until its rediscovery in 1928.[^o5] Mohr served in [Franco-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Dutch_War) in 1672–1673, and was taken prisoner by the French.[^o3][^o4] By 1673, he had published his second book, *Compendium Euclidis Curiosi*.[^o4] A third book was later mentioned by Mohr's son; for many years this was believed to be the *Gegenübung auf Compendium Euclidis Curiosi* but (Andersen Meyer 1985) argue that it must be a different book, and that the *Gegenübung* has a different author.[^o3][^o7] As well as his work on geometry, Mohr contributed to the theory of [nested radicals](wikipedia://en/2503693), with the aim of simplifying [Cardano's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardano's_formula) for the roots of a [cubic polynomial](wikipedia://en/243709).[^o7]

While in the Netherlands, Mohr became a friend of [Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus](wikipedia://en/26456397). The two visited [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) in France and [John Collins](wikipedia://en/2361164) in England together. Mohr returned to Denmark in 1681; he had dedicated *Euclides Danicus* to [Christian V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_V) and hoped for a position in exchange, but was offered only a position as a shipyard supervisor, which he declined. He married Elizabeth von der Linde of Copenhagen on 19 July 1687, and soon after returned to Holland; their son, Peter Georg Mohrenthal, eventually settled in [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden) as a bookseller and publisher.[^o4][^o8] In 1695 he took a job with Tschirnhaus,[^o4] and spent his last few years as a guest in Tschirnhaus's house. He died in [Kieslingswalde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sławnikowice,_Lower_Silesian_Voivodeship) near [Görlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Görlitz), Germany.[^o3]

The Georg Mohr Competition, which is used to select Danish participants to the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), is named after Mohr.[^o9]
