# Lorenzo Mascheroni

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lorenzo Mascheroni"}

**Lorenzo Mascheroni** (; 13 May 1750 – 14 July 1800) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for proving that all Euclidean constructions achievable with a compass and straightedge can also be done using only a compass ([Mohr–Mascheroni theorem](wikipedia://en/1196909)). He also calculated the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant) to 32 decimal places.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Lorenzo Mascheroni was born on 13 May 1750 in Castagneta, near [Bergamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamo), [Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy), to a wealthy merchant family. After [seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) studies in Bergamo, he was ordained a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) priest in 1774, at the age of 17. His early interest in literary writing, in both Italian and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), was increasingly displaced by scientific – especially mathematical – studies. At first a professor of [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447), from 1778 he began teaching [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the seminary in Bergamo.

In 1786, Mascheroni succeeded [Pietro Paoli](wikipedia://en/47868477) as professor of mathematics at the [University of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia) and in 1789, he became the rector of the university, a position he held for the next four years. In 1790 and 1792, respectively, Mascheroni published two very important memoirs in which he was able to rectify the value of [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/148594), reaching the calculus of thirty-two decimal digits.[^n1]

Though a priest, Mascheroni was sympathetic to the ideals of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution).[^o6] He enthusiastically supported the French armies that invaded Italy in 1796–99 and played a major role in the government of the [Cisalpine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic). [Napoleon](wikipedia://en/69880) personally knew and admired Mascheroni and was instrumental in bringing his work to the attention of the learned circles of France.[^n2] According to [Howard Eves](wikipedia://en/4304796), the [theorem](wikipedia://en/2728914) and a [construction problem](wikipedia://en/2941110) bearing Napoleon's name were discovered by Mascheroni, who let the Emperor claim them for himself.[^o8]

In 1797, Mascheroni was invited to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) as a member of the international commission that created the [metric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system).[^n3] During his stay in Paris, Mascheroni taught at various schools and made the acquaintance of [Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793), [Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783), and [Monge](wikipedia://en/77840). On the occasion of the death of the mathematician and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Jean-Charles de Borda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Charles_de_Borda), he composed an [elegy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elegy) in Latin in his honour (1799). Mascheroni was unable to return to Italy due to the Austro-Russian invasion of Milan in 1799. He died in Paris the following year.[^n4] On his death, his friend [Vincenzo Monti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Monti) dedicated to him the poem *In morte di Lorenzo Mascheroni* (three cantos published in 1801).

Mascheroni was a member of the *[Accademia Galileiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Galileiana)* of Padua, the [Royal Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_Virgiliana_di_Scienze_Lettere_ed_Arti) of Mantua and the [Accademia nazionale delle scienze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_nazionale_delle_scienze). During his life, he published a substantial number of mathematical writings, the best known of which was his *Geometria del Compasso* (Geometry of the Compass, 1797).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Mohr–Mascheroni Theorem"}

### Mohr–Mascheroni Theorem {#Mohr–Mascheroni_Theorem}

*Image: Mascheroni - Geometria del compasso, anno V della Repubblica francese 1797 - 1415055.jpg.* *La geometria del compasso*, 1797

In his work, *Geometria del Compasso* (Pavia, 1797), Mascheroni proved that any geometrical construction which can be done with [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge), can also be done with compasses alone. Mascheroni's *Geometria del Compasso* had a huge impact on the scientific community. It was soon translated into [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) by Antoine-Michel Carette (Paris, 1798), and into [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) by Johann Philipp Gruson (Berlin, 1825). A revised and augmented edition of the French translation was published in Paris and Brussels in 1828.

However, the priority for this result (now known as the [Mohr–Mascheroni theorem](wikipedia://en/1196909)) belongs to the [Dane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [Georg Mohr](wikipedia://en/83128), who had previously published a proof in 1672 in an obscure book, *[Euclides Danicus](wikipedia://en/43156677)*. Mohr's book was overlooked by European mathematicians, and Mascheroni, like the rest of the [scientific community](wikipedia://en/338705), was unaware of it, so it is Mascheroni whose name is generally associated with this result.[^n5]

:::

### Euler–Mascheroni constant {#Euler–Mascheroni_constant}

In his *Adnotationes ad calculum integralem Euleri* (1790), Mascheroni extended several of [Euler](wikipedia://en/17902)'s results, especially those involving the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant), usually denoted as $\gamma$ (gamma). Mascheroni was able to rectify Euler's value for $\gamma$ and attempted to calculate the constant to 32 decimal places, but made errors in the 20th–22nd and 31st–32nd decimal places; starting from the 20th digit, he calculated ...**181**12090082**39** when the correct value is ...**065**12090082**40**. The figure was corrected by [Johann Georg von Soldner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_von_Soldner) in 1809. Mascheroni's *Adnotationes* have been reprinted as an appendix in Euler's *Opera Omnia.*[^o16]
