# Composite number

*Image: Primes-vs-composites.svg.* Composite numbers can be arranged into [rectangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangles) but prime numbers cannot.

*Image: Composite number Cuisenaire rods 10.svg.* Demonstration, with [Cuisenaire rods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisenaire_rods), of the divisors of the composite number 10

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Composite number"}

A **composite number** is a [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) that can be formed by multiplying two smaller positive integers. Accordingly, it is a positive integer that has at least one [divisor](wikipedia://en/49492) other than 1 and itself.[^n1][^n2] Every positive integer is composite, [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), or the [unit](wikipedia://en/289666) 1, so the composite numbers are exactly the natural numbers that are not prime and not a unit.[^n3][^n4] For example, the integer 14 is a composite number because it is the product of the two smaller integers 2 and 7; however, the integers 2 and 3 are not because each can only be divided by one and itself.

Every composite number can be written as the product of two or more (not necessarily distinct) primes.[^n2] For example, the composite number [299](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/299_%28number%29) can be written as 13 × 23, and the composite number [360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/360_%28number%29) can be written as 2<sup>3</sup> × 3<sup>2</sup> × 5; furthermore, this representation is unique [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) the order of the factors. This fact is called the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556).[^n5][^n6][^n7][^n8]

There are several known [primality tests](wikipedia://en/183751) that can determine whether a number is prime or composite, which do not necessarily reveal the [factorization](wikipedia://en/82341) of a composite input.

[Grimm's conjecture](wikipedia://en/6278350) states that, for every set of consecutive composite numbers, there is an equally sized set of prime numbers, and a bijection mapping each composite in the former set to a prime in the latter set that it is divisible by.

## Types {#Types}

One way to classify composite numbers is by counting the number of prime factors. A composite number with two prime factors is a [semiprime](wikipedia://en/339542) or 2-almost prime (the factors need not be distinct, hence squares of primes are included). A composite number with three distinct prime factors is a [sphenic number](wikipedia://en/245203). In some applications, it is necessary to differentiate between composite numbers with an odd number of distinct prime factors and those with an even number of distinct prime factors. For the latter,

$$\mu(n) = (-1)^{2x} = 1$$

(where μ is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) and x is half the total of prime factors), while for the former,

$$\mu(n) = (-1)^{2x + 1} = -1.$$

However, for prime numbers, the function also returns −1 and $\mu(1) = 1$. For a number n with one or more repeated prime factors,

$\mu(n) = 0$.[^n9]

If all the prime factors of a number are repeated it is called a [powerful number](wikipedia://en/462499) (too, all [perfect powers](wikipedia://en/4023591) are powerful numbers). If none of its prime factors are repeated, it is called [squarefree](wikipedia://en/29525). (All prime numbers and 1 are squarefree.)

For example, [72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72_%28number%29) = 2<sup>3</sup> × 3<sup>2</sup>, all the prime factors are repeated, so 72 is a powerful number. [42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42_%28number%29) = 2 × 3 × 7, none of the prime factors are repeated, so 42 is squarefree.

Another way to classify composite numbers is by counting the number of divisors. All composite numbers have at least three divisors. In the case of squares of primes, those divisors are $\{1, p, p^2\}$. A number n that has more divisors than any x \< n is a [highly composite number](wikipedia://en/208732) (though the first two such numbers are 1 and 2).

Composite numbers have also been called "rectangular numbers", but that name can also refer to the [pronic numbers](wikipedia://en/652733), numbers that are the product of two consecutive integers.

Yet another way to classify composite numbers is to determine whether all prime factors are either all below or all above some fixed (prime) number. Such numbers are called [smooth numbers](wikipedia://en/1035039) and [rough numbers](wikipedia://en/7375449), respectively.
