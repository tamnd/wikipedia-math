# Newton line

*Image: Newton line.svg.* $E, K, F$ lie on a common line, the Newton line

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Newton line"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **Newton line** is the line that connects the midpoints of the two [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251) in a [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) other than a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) $\overline{GH}$ and $\overline{IJ}$ that connect the midpoints of opposite sides (the [bimedians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimedian)) of a convex quadrilateral intersect in a point that lies on the Newton line. This point $K$ bisects the line segment $\overline{EF}$ that connects the diagonal midpoints.

By [Anne's theorem](wikipedia://en/42084564) and its converse, any interior point *P* on the Newton line of a quadrilateral $ABCD$ has the property that

$$[\triangle ABP] + [\triangle CDP] = [\triangle ADP] + [\triangle BCP],$$

where $[\triangle ABP]$ denotes the area of triangle $\triangle ABP$.[^n1]

If the quadrilateral is a [tangential quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/9176412), then its [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) also lies on this line.
