# Meier Eidelheit

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Meier Eidelheit"}

**Meier "Maks" Eidelheit** (6 July 1910 – March 1943) was a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) belonging to the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328) who worked in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) and was murdered in the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Meier Eidelheit left the Lwów [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in 1929 and then studied mathematics at the scientific faculty in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów), completing his study in 1933 with a thesis on the theory of [summation](wikipedia://en/246160). In 1938, with [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) as supervisor, he gained a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from the Jan-Kazimierz-[University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University) with a [Dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) *über die Auflösbarkeit eines linearen Gleichungssystems mit unendlich vielen Unbekannten*.[^o4] From 1933 to 1939 he gave private lectures; from 31 January 1939 onwards he was an Assistant Professor of [Analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), from 21 March 1941 he was candidate for a professorship.[^o5] He worked mainly on [Functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). On the basis of his 1936 paper on [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) in linear [normed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space), geometric versions of the [hyperplane separation theorem](wikipedia://en/4739827) are also known (in German) as *Trennungssatz von Eidelheit* (Eidelheit separation theorem).[^o8][^o6] A theorem on the solubility of certain infinite systems of equations in [Fréchet spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fréchet_spaces) is also named after him.[^o7]

Eidelheit published six papers in *[Studia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studia_Mathematica)* from 1936 to 1940;[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13] a seventh was printed posthumously.[^o14] Eidelheit was an active contributor to the *[Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115)*, posing problems 172, 173, 174, 176 and 188[^o15] and answering problem 26 ([Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162)), 64 (Mazur),[^o8][^o16] 162 ([Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735)), and 176 (Eidelheit).

Meier Eidelheit was murdered in the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust) in March 1943. His posthumously published article *Quelques remarques sur les fonctionelles linéaires* in volume 10 of the [Studia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studia_Mathematica) was prefaced with the following lines: "L’auteur de ce travail a été assassiné par les Allemands en mars de 1943. Le manuscrit qu’il fut parvenir à la Rédaction en 1941 a été retrouvé récemment entre les papiers laissés par S. Banach." (in English: The author of this work was murdered in March 1943 by the Germans. The manuscript, which reached the editors in 1941, was recently found among the writings left by S. Banach.)[^o14]
