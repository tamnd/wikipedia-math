# Kostant polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kostant polynomial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kostant polynomials**, named after [Bertram Kostant](wikipedia://en/3520590), provide an explicit basis of the [ring of polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_polynomials) over the ring of polynomials invariant under the [finite reflection group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_reflection_group) of a [root system](wikipedia://en/277087).

:::

## Background {#Background}

If the reflection group *W* corresponds to the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) of a compact [semisimple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_group) *K* with [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136) *T*, then the Kostant polynomials describe the structure of the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) of the generalized [flag manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_manifold) *K*/*T*, also isomorphic to *G*/*B* where *G* is the [complexification](wikipedia://en/2187847) of *K* and *B* is the corresponding [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356). [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) showed that its [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) is isomorphic to the quotient of the ring of polynomials by the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the invariant homogeneous polynomials of positive degree. This ring had already been considered by [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040) in establishing the foundations of the cohomology of [compact Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) and their [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325) with [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), [Jean-Louis Koszul](wikipedia://en/5147910) and [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536); the existence of such a basis was used by Chevalley to prove that the ring of invariants was itself a polynomial ring. A detailed account of Kostant polynomials was given by (Bernstein Gelfand Gelfand 1973) and independently (Demazure 1973) as a tool to understand the [Schubert calculus](wikipedia://en/3061740) of the flag manifold. The Kostant polynomials are related to the [Schubert polynomials](wikipedia://en/31925388) defined combinatorially by (Lascoux Schützenberger 1982) for the classical flag manifold, when *G* = SL(n,**C**). Their structure is governed by [difference operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_operator) associated to the corresponding [root system](wikipedia://en/277087).

(Steinberg 1975) defined an analogous basis when the polynomial ring is replaced by the [ring of exponentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_exponentials) of the [weight lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_lattice). If *K* is [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected), this ring can be identified with the [representation ring](wikipedia://en/16673816) *R*(*T*) and the *W*-invariant [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) with *R*(*K*). Steinberg's basis was again motivated by a problem on the topology of homogeneous spaces; the basis arises in describing the *T*-[equivariant K-theory](wikipedia://en/18887838) of *K*/*T*.

## Definition {#Definition}

Let Φ be a [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) in a finite-dimensional real inner product space *V* with [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) *W*. Let Φ<sup>\+</sup> be a set of positive roots and Δ the corresponding set of simple roots. If α is a root, then *s*<sub>α</sub> denotes the corresponding reflection operator. Roots are regarded as linear polynomials on *V* using the inner product α(*v*) = (α,*v*). The choice of Δ gives rise to a [Bruhat order](wikipedia://en/10163003) on the Weyl group determined by the ways of writing elements minimally as products of simple root reflection. The minimal length for an element *s* is denoted $\ell(s)$. Pick an element *v* in *V* such that *α*(*v*) > 0 for every positive root.

If α<sub>*i*</sub> is a simple root with reflection operator *s*<sub>*i*</sub>

$$s_i x= x- 2{(x,\alpha_i)\over (\alpha_i,\alpha_i)}\alpha_i,$$

then the corresponding **divided difference operator** is defined by

$$\delta_i f = {f-f\circ s_i\over \alpha_i}.$$

If $\ell(s)=m$ and *s* has reduced expression

$$s=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_m},$$

then

$$\delta_s=\delta_{i_1}\cdots \delta_{i_m}$$

is independent of the reduced expression. Moreover

$$\delta_s\delta_t=\delta_{st}$$

if $\ell(st)=\ell(s)+\ell(t)$ and 0 otherwise.

If *w*<sub>0</sub> is the [longest element](wikipedia://en/5355571) of *W*, the element of greatest length or equivalently the element sending Φ<sup>\+</sup> to −Φ<sup>\+</sup>, then

$$\delta_{w_0}f= {\sum_{s \in W} \det s \, f\circ s\over \prod_{\alpha>0} \alpha}.$$

More generally

$$\delta_{s}f={\det s \, f\circ s + \sum_{t<s} a_{s,t} \,f\circ t\over \prod_{\alpha>0, \, s^{-1}\alpha<0} \alpha}$$

for some constants *a*<sub>*s*,*t*</sub>.

Set

$$d= |W|^{-1}\prod_{\alpha>0} \alpha.$$

and

$$P_s=\delta_{s^{-1}w_0} d.$$

Then *P*<sub>s</sub> is a [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) of degree $\ell(s)$.

These polynomials are the **Kostant polynomials**.

## Properties {#Properties}

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**. *The Kostant polynomials form a free basis of the ring of polynomials over the W-invariant polynomials.*

In fact the matrix

$$N_{st} =\delta_s (P_t)$$

is unitriangular for any [total order](wikipedia://en/30330) such that *s* ≥ *t* implies $\ell(s)\ge \ell(t)$.

Hence

$$\det N=1.$$

Thus if

$$f = \sum_s a_s P_s$$

with *a*<sub>*s*</sub> invariant under *W*, then

$$\delta_t(f) = \sum_s \delta_t(P_s) a_s.$$

Thus

$$a_s = \sum_t M_{s,t} \delta_t(f),$$

where

$$M=N^{-1}$$

another unitriangular matrix with polynomial entries. It can be checked directly that *a*<sub>*s*</sub> is invariant under *W*.

In fact δ<sub>*i*</sub> satisfies the [derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29) property

$$\delta_i(fg)=\delta_i(f)g + (f\circ s_i)\delta_i(g).$$

Hence

$$\delta_i\delta_s(f) = \sum_t \delta_i( \delta_s(P_t))a_t) = \sum_t (\delta_s(P_t)\circ s_i)\delta_i(a_t) + \sum_t \delta_i\delta_s(P_t)a_t.$$

Since

$$\delta_i\delta_s=\delta_{s_is}$$

or 0, it follows that

$$\sum_t \delta_s(P_t)\,\delta_i(a_t)\circ s_i=0$$

so that by the invertibility of *N*

$$\delta_i(a_t)=0$$

for all *i*, i.e. *a*<sub>*t*</sub> is invariant under *W*.

:::

## Steinberg basis {#Steinberg_basis}

As above let Φ be a [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) in a real inner product space *V*, and Φ<sup>\+</sup> a subset of positive roots. From these data we obtain the subset Δ = {*α*<sub>1</sub>, *α*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *α*<sub>*n*</sub>} of the simple roots, the coroots

$$\alpha_i^\vee=2(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)^{-1}\alpha_i,$$

and the fundamental weights λ<sub>1</sub>, λ<sub>2</sub>, ..., λ<sub>*n*</sub> as the dual basis of the coroots.

For each element *s* in *W*, let Δ<sub>*s*</sub> be the subset of Δ consisting of the simple roots satisfying *s*<sup>−1</sup>*α* \< 0, and put

$$\lambda_s = s^{-1}\sum_{\alpha_i \in \Delta_s} \lambda_i,$$

where the sum is calculated in the weight lattice *P*.

The set of linear combinations of the exponentials *e*<sup>μ</sup> with integer coefficients for μ in *P* becomes a ring over **Z** isomorphic to the group algebra of *P*, or equivalently to the representation ring *R*(*T*) of *T*, where *T* is a maximal torus in *K*, the simply connected, connected compact semisimple [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) with root system Φ. If *W* is the Weyl group of Φ, then the representation ring *R*(*K*) of *K* can be identified with *R*(*T*)<sup>*W*</sup>.

**Steinberg's theorem**. *The exponentials* λ<sub>*s*</sub> (*s* *in* *W*) *form a free basis for the ring of exponentials over the subring of* *W*-*invariant exponentials.*

Let ρ denote the half sum of the positive roots, and *A* denote the antisymmetrisation operator

$$A(\psi)=\sum_{s\in W} (-1)^{\ell(s)} s\cdot \psi.$$

The positive roots β with *s*β positive can be seen as a set of positive roots for a root system on a subspace of *V*; the roots are the ones orthogonal to s.λ<sub>*s*</sub>. The corresponding Weyl group equals the stabilizer of *λ*<sub>*s*</sub> in *W*. It is generated by the simple reflections *s*<sub>*j*</sub> for which *s*α<sub>*j*</sub> is a positive root.

Let *M* and *N* be the matrices

$$M_{ts}=t(\lambda_s),\,\,N_{st}= (-1)^{\ell(t)}\cdot t(\psi_s),$$

where ψ<sub>*s*</sub> is given by the weight *s*<sup>−1</sup>ρ - λ<sub>*s*</sub>. Then the matrix

$$B_{s,s'}=\Omega^{-1}(NM)_{s,s'}={A(\psi_s\lambda_{s'})\over \Omega}$$

is triangular with respect to any total order on *W* such that *s* ≥ *t* implies $\ell(s)\ge \ell(t)$. Steinberg proved that the entries of *B* are *W*-invariant exponential sums. Moreover, its diagonal entries all equal 1, so it has determinant 1. Hence its inverse *C* has the same form. Define

$$\varphi_s=\sum C_{s,t}\psi_t.$$

If χ is an arbitrary [exponential sum](wikipedia://en/468937), then it follows that

$$\chi=\sum_{s\in W} a_s \lambda_s$$

with *a*<sub>*s*</sub> the *W*-invariant exponential sum

$$a_s ={A(\varphi_s\chi)\over \Omega}.$$

Indeed, this is the unique solution of the system of equations

$$t\chi=\sum_{s\in W} t(\lambda_s)\,\,a_s=\sum_s M_{t,s}a_s.$$
