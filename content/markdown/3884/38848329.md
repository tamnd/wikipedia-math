# Rectangular cuboid

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rectangular cuboid"}

A **rectangular cuboid** is a special case of a [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802) with [rectangular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangular) faces in which all of its [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046) are [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956). This shape is also called **rectangular parallelepiped** or **orthogonal parallelepiped**.

:::

Many writers just call these "cuboids", without qualifying them as being rectangular, but others use [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802) to refer to a more general class of polyhedra with six [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) faces.[^n1]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A rectangular cuboid is a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) with six [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) faces. The [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046) of a rectangular cuboid are all [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956), and its opposite faces are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330).[^o6] Because of the faces' [orthogonality](wikipedia://en/102221), the rectangular cuboid is classified as [convex orthogonal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/69902387).[^n2] By definition, this makes it a *right rectangular [prism](wikipedia://en/283007)*. Rectangular cuboids may be referred to colloquially as "boxes" (after the [physical object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box)). If two opposite faces become [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), the resulting one may obtain another special case of rectangular prism, known as **square rectangular cuboid**. They can be represented as the [prism graph](wikipedia://en/45012199) $\Pi_4$.[^n3] In the case that all six faces are squares, the result is a [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_%28geometry%29).[^n4]

If a rectangular cuboid has length $a$, width $b$, and height $c$, then:[^o10]

- Its volume is the product of the rectangular area and its height: $$V=abc.$$
- its surface area is the sum of the area of all faces: $$A=2(ab+ac+bc).$$
- its [space diagonal](wikipedia://en/1337505) can be found by constructing a right triangle of height $c$ with its base as the diagonal of the $a$-by-$b$ rectangular face, then calculating the hypotenuse's length using the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034): $$d=\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}.$$

:::

## Appearance {#Appearance}

Rectangular cuboid shapes are often used for boxes, cupboards, rooms, buildings, containers, cabinets, books, sturdy computer chassis, printing devices, electronic calling touchscreen devices, washing and drying machines, etc. They are among those solids that can [tessellate three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/6008654). The shape is fairly versatile in being able to contain multiple smaller rectangular cuboids, e.g., [sugar cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cube) in a box, boxes in a cupboard, cupboards in a room, and rooms in a building.

American psychologist [Joy Paul Guilford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Paul_Guilford) modelled a three-dimensional 5×4×6 cube, called "Guilford's cube", displaying 120 possible ways of thinking apropos of the intelligence structure. Each dimension represents the mental factors, which includes five operations ([cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition), [memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory), [convergent thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_thinking), [divergent thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergent_thinking), and [evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaluation)); four contents (figural, symbolic, [semantic](wikipedia://en/29107), and [behavioral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavior)); and six products (units, classes, relations, systems, transformations, and implications).[^n5] An extension into a 5×5×6 cube by adding two-factor contents, which replace figural with visual and auditory, yielding 150 possible ways.[^o13]

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

A rectangular cuboid with integer edges, as well as integer face diagonals, is called an [Euler brick](wikipedia://en/1278374); for example, with sides 44, 117, and 240. A [perfect cuboid](wikipedia://en/1278374#Perfect_cuboid) is an Euler brick whose space diagonal is also an integer. It is currently unknown whether a perfect cuboid actually exists.[^n6]

The number of different [nets](wikipedia://en/1148129) for a [simple cube is 11](wikipedia://en/6285#Constructions). However, this number increases significantly to at least 54 for a rectangular cuboid of three different lengths.[^o16]
