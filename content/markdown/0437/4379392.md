# Julian Coolidge

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Julian Coolidge"}

**Julian Lowell Coolidge** (September 28, 1873 – March 5, 1954) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian), a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) and chairman of the [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) Mathematics Department.[^o6]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Brookline, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline,_Massachusetts), he graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University)[^o6] and [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford).[^o4]

Between 1897 and 1899, Julian Coolidge taught at the [Groton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groton_School), where one of his students was [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt).[^o6] He left Groton to accept a teaching position at Harvard and in 1902 was given an assistant professorship, but took two years off to further his education with studies in [Turin, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin,_Italy)[^o6] before receiving his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn).[^o6] Julian Coolidge then returned to teach at Harvard where he remained for his entire academic career, interrupted only by a year at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in Paris as an exchange professor.[^o6]

During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), he served with the [U.S. Army's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army) Overseas Expeditionary Force in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), rising to the rank of [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_%28United_States%29). In 1919, he was awarded a [Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight) of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France)'s [Legion of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honor).[^o6]

Coolidge returned to teach at Harvard where he was awarded a full professorship. In 1927 he was appointed chairman of the Mathematics Department at Harvard,[^o6] a position he held until his retirement in 1940. A Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences),[^o7] Coolidge served as president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) and vice-president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6][^o8] He authored several books on mathematics and on the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220). He was Master of Lowell House (one of Harvard's undergraduate residences) from 1930 to 1940.

Coolidge died in 1954 in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts), aged 80.[^o6]

- J. L. Coolidge (1916) *[A Treatise on the Circle and the Sphere](wikipedia://en/64439520)*, [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press).[^o10]
- J. L. Coolidge (1924) *The geometry of the complex domain*, [The Clarendon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clarendon_Press).
- J. L. Coolidge (1925) *An introduction to mathematical probability*, [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press).
- J. L. Coolidge (1931) *A Treatise on Algebraic Plane Curves*, [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) ([Dover Publications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Publications) 2004).
