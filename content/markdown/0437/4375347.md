# Sphere theorem (3-manifolds)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphere theorem"}

In mathematics, in the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257), the **sphere theorem** of (txt) gives conditions for elements of the second homotopy group of a 3-manifold to be represented by embedded spheres.

:::

One example is the following:

Let $M$ be an [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) 3-manifold such that $\pi_2(M)$ is not the trivial group. Then there exists a non-zero element of $\pi_2(M)$ having a representative that is an [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) $S^2\to M$. This statement may be strengthened to show that the embedding is piecewise linear (Lickorish 1997, 133).

The proof of this version of the theorem can be based on [transversality](wikipedia://en/22667049) methods, see (txt).

Another more general version (also called the projective plane theorem, and due to [David B. A. Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_B._A._Epstein)) is:

Let $M$ be any 3-manifold and $N$ a $\pi_1(M)$-[invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) subgroup of $\pi_2(M)$. If $f\colon S^2\to M$ is a [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) map such that $[f]\notin N$ and $U$ is any neighborhood of the singular set $\Sigma(f)$, then there is a map $g\colon S^2\to M$ satisfying

1. $$[g]\notin N,$$
1. $$g(S^2)\subset f(S^2)\cup U,$$
1. $g\colon S^2\to g(S^2)$ is a [covering map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_map), and
1. $g(S^2)$ is a [2-sided](wikipedia://en/4785316) submanifold ([2-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-sphere) or [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350)) of $M$.

quoted in (Hempel 1976, 54).
