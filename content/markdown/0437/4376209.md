# Masaki Kashiwara

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Masaki Kashiwara"}

**Masaki Kashiwara** is a Japanese mathematician and professor at the [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) Institute for Advanced Study (KUIAS). He is known for his contributions to [algebraic analysis](wikipedia://en/20846707), [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841), [*D*-module](wikipedia://en/3440755) theory, [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).[^o3] He was awarded the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2025,[^o4] and is the award's first recipient from Japan.[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kashiwara was born in [Yūki, Ibaraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yūki,_Ibaraki) on January 30, 1947. One of his early mathematical fascinations was the tsurukamezan problem, which asks the number of cranes and turtles given a set number of legs and heads.[^o14]

Kashiwara spent his undergraduate years at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) (UTokyo), earning his bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1969. He then went on to study at the same institution for his master's degree, which he completed in 1971.[^o7] At UTokyo, Kashiwara was a student of [Mikio Sato](wikipedia://en/373336). His master's thesis, written in Japanese, laid the foundations for the study of D-modules.[^o13][^o11] He continued studying under Sato at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) after Sato moved to the [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1978962) (RIMS), earning his doctorate in 1974.[^o11][^o8]

He was a [plenary speaker at International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1978 and an invited speaker in 1990.[^o9] He is a foreign associate of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences)[^o10] and a member of the [Japan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy).[^o11] He has been involved in [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1978962) (RIMS) since 1978 as a professor and later director.[^o12][^o11]

Kashiwara was awarded Japan's [Order of the Sacred Treasure, Gold and Silver Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Sacred_Treasure#2nd_class,_Gold_and_Silver_Star) in 2020.[^o13]

He received the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2025,[^o4] the first Japanese national to do so.[^o14] The chairman of the Prize Committee, [Helge Holden](wikipedia://en/24884384), described Kashiwara's contributions as "very important in many different areas of mathematics,” and explained that Kashiwara "has solved some open conjectures — hard problems that have been around \[and has\] opened new avenues, connecting areas that were not known to be connected before.”[^o15] The award ceremony is planned to be held at [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo), Norway on May 20, 2025.[^o14]

## Research {#Research}

Kashiwara and Sato established the foundations of the theory of [systems of linear equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_of_linear_equations) [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) with [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) coefficients, introducing the idea of applying [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) to [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).[^o17] Kashiwara's master thesis states the foundations of [*D*-module](wikipedia://en/3440755) theory.[^o17] Kashiwara developed the analytic theory of *D*-modules, while [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383) introduced a similar approach in the algebraic case.[^o17]

Kashiwara's PhD thesis proves the rationality of the roots of b-functions ([Bernstein–Sato polynomials](wikipedia://en/1636721)), using *D*-module theory and [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115).[^o3][^o17]

Kashiwara's 1973 paper with Sato and [Takahiro Kawai](wikipedia://en/57758222) on the involutivity of characteristics of microdifferential systems and classification at generic points of microdifferential systems was described by [Pierre Schapira](wikipedia://en/41082949) as having "an enormous influence on the analysis of partial differential equations".[^o17]
