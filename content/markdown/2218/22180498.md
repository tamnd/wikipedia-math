# Birman–Wenzl algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Birman–Wenzl algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Birman–Murakami–Wenzl (BMW) algebra**, introduced by (txt) and (txt), is a two-parameter family of [algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) $\mathrm{C}_n(\ell,m)$ of dimension $1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots (2n-1)$ having the [Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/55907272) of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) as a [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635). It is related to the [Kauffman polynomial](wikipedia://en/9565831) of a [link](wikipedia://en/1161784). It is a deformation of the [Brauer algebra](wikipedia://en/3875858) in much the same way that Hecke algebras are deformations of the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) of the symmetric group.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For each [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n*, the BMW algebra $\mathrm{C}_n(\ell,m)$ is generated by $G_1^{\pm1},G_2^{\pm1},\dots, G_{n-1}^{\pm1},E_1,E_2,\dots,E_{n-1}$ and relations:

$$G_iG_j = G_jG_i, \mathrm{if} \left\vert i-j \right\vert \geq 2,$$

$G_i G_{i+1} G_i = G_{i+1} G_i G_{i+1},$ $E_i E_{i\pm1} E_i = E_i,$

$$G_i + {G_i}^{-1} = m(1+E_i),$$

$G_{i\pm1} G_i E_{i\pm1} = E_i G_{i\pm1} G_i = E_i E_{i\pm1},$ $G_{i\pm1} E_i G_{i\pm1} ={G_i}^{-1} E_{i\pm1} {G_i}^{-1},$

$G_{i\pm1} E_i E_{i\pm1} = {G_i}^{-1} E_{i\pm1},$ $E_{i\pm1} E_i G_{i\pm1} =E_{i\pm1} {G_i}^{-1},$

$G_i E_i= E_i G_i = l^{-1} E_i,$ $E_i G_{i\pm1} E_i = l E_i.$

These relations imply the further relations:

$$E_i E_j=E_j E_i, \mathrm{if} \left\vert i-j \right\vert \geq 2,$$

$$(E_i)^2 = (m^{-1}(l+l^{-1})-1) E_i,$$

$${G_i}^2 = m(G_i+l^{-1}E_i)-1.$$

This is the original definition given by Birman and Wenzl. However a slight change by the introduction of some minus signs is sometimes made, in accordance with Kauffman's 'Dubrovnik' version of his link invariant. In that way, the fourth relation in Birman & Wenzl's original version is changed to

1. (Kauffman [skein relation](wikipedia://en/314902))

$$G_i - {G_i}^{-1}=m(1-E_i),$$

Given invertibility of *m*, the rest of the relations in Birman & Wenzl's original version can be reduced to

1. (Idempotent relation)

$$(E_i)^2 = (m^{-1}(l-l^{-1})+1) E_i,$$

1. (Braid relations)

$$G_iG_j=G_jG_i, \text{if } \left\vert i-j \right\vert \geqslant 2, \text{ and } G_i G_{i+1} G_i=G_{i+1} G_i G_{i+1},$$

1. (Tangle relations)

$$E_i E_{i\pm1} E_i=E_i \text{ and } G_i G_{i\pm1} E_i = E_{i\pm1} E_i,$$

1. (Delooping relations)

$$G_i E_i= E_i G_i = l^{-1} E_i \text{ and } E_i G_{i\pm1} E_i =l E_i.$$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- The dimension of $\mathrm{C}_n(\ell,m)$ is $(2n)!/(2^nn! )$.
- The [Iwahori–Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/521808) associated with the symmetric group $S_n$ is a quotient of the Birman–Murakami–Wenzl algebra $\mathrm{C}_n$.
- The Artin [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138) embeds in the BMW algebra: $B_n \hookrightarrow \mathrm{C}_n$.

:::

## Baxterisation of Birman–Murakami–Wenzl algebra {#Baxterisation_of_Birman–Murakami–Wenzl_algebra}

Define the face operator as

$$U_i(u) = 1- \frac{i\sin u}{\sin \lambda \sin \mu}(e^{i(u-\lambda)} G_i -e^{-i(u-\lambda)}{G_i}^{-1}),$$

where $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are determined by

$$2\cos \lambda = 1+(l-l^{-1})/m$$

and

$$2\cos \lambda = 1+(l-l^{-1})/(\lambda \sin \mu).$$

Then the face operator satisfies the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810).

$$U_{i+1}(v) U_i(u+v) U_{i+1}(u) = U_i(u) U_{i+1}(u+v) U_i(v)$$

Now $E_i=U_i(\lambda)$ with

$$\rho(u)=\frac{\sin (\lambda-u) \sin (\mu+u)}{\sin \lambda \sin \mu}.$$

In the [limits](wikipedia://en/26551602) $u \to \pm i \infty$, the [braids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_theory) ${G_j}^{\pm}$ can be recovered [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) a [scale factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_factor).

## History {#History}

In 1984, [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628) introduced a new polynomial invariant of link isotopy types which is called the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642). The invariants are related to the traces of irreducible representations of [Hecke algebras](wikipedia://en/55907272) associated with the symmetric groups. (Murakami 1987) showed that the [Kauffman polynomial](wikipedia://en/9565831) can also be interpreted as a function $F$ on a certain associative algebra. In 1989, (Birman Wenzl 1989) constructed a two-parameter family of algebras $\mathrm{C}_n(\ell,m)$ with the Kauffman polynomial $K_n(\ell,m)$ as trace after appropriate renormalization.
