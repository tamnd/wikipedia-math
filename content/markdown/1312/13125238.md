# Writer invariant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Writer invariant"}

**Writer invariant**, also called **authorial invariant** or **author's invariant**, is a property of a text which is invariant of its [author](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Author), that is, it will be similar in all texts of a given author and different in texts of different authors. It can be used to find [plagiarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagiarism) or discover who is real author of [anonymously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_work) published text. Writer invariant is also an author's pattern of writing a letter in [handwritten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handwriting) [text recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_recognition).[^o2]

:::

While it is generally recognised that writer invariants exist,[^o3] it is not agreed what properties of a text should be used.[^o3][^o4] Among the first ones used was distribution of [word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word) lengths;[^o3] other proposed invariants include average [sentence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentence_%28linguistics%29) length,[^o3][^o4] average word length,[^o3][^o4] [noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun), [verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verb) or [adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective) usage frequency,[^o4] [vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocabulary) richness,[^o3] and frequency of [function words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_word),[^o3][^o4] or specific function words.[^o4]

Of these, average sentence lengths can be very similar in works of different authors[^o3][^o4] or vary significantly even within a single work;[^o4] average word lengths likewise turn out to be very similar in works of different authors.[^o4] Analysis of function words shows promise because they are used by authors unconsciously.[^o3][^o4][^o5]
