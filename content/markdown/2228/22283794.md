# Hitchin–Thorpe inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hitchin–Thorpe inequality"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) the **Hitchin–Thorpe inequality** is a relation which restricts the topology of [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795) that carry an [Einstein metric](wikipedia://en/644814).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the Hitchin–Thorpe inequality"}

## Statement of the Hitchin–Thorpe inequality {#Statement_of_the_Hitchin–Thorpe_inequality}

Let *M* be a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475), oriented, four-dimensional [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold). If there exists a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on *M* which is an [Einstein metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_metric), then

$$\chi(M) \geq \frac{3}{2}|\tau(M)|,$$

where $\chi(M)$ is the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of $M$ and $\tau(M)$ is the [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) of $M$.

This inequality was first stated by John Thorpe in a footnote to a 1969 paper focusing on manifolds of higher dimension.[^o4] [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164) then rediscovered the inequality, and gave a complete characterization of the equality case in 1974;[^o5] he found that if $(M, g)$ is an Einstein manifold for which equality in the Hitchin-Thorpe inequality is obtained, then the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) of $g$ is zero; if the sectional curvature is not identically equal to zero, then $(M, g)$ is a [Calabi–Yau manifold](wikipedia://en/188932) whose [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) is a [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116).

Already in 1961, [Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363) showed that the Euler characteristic is always non-negative.[^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Let $(M, g)$ be a four-dimensional smooth [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) which is Einstein. Given any point $p$ of $M$, there exists a $g_{p}$-orthonormal basis $e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{3}, e_{4}$ of the tangent space $T_{p}M$ such that the curvature operator $\operatorname{Rm}_{p}$, which is a symmetric linear map of $\land^{2}T_{p}M$ into itself, has matrix

$$\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1&0&0&\mu_1&0&0\\ 0&\lambda_2&0&0&\mu_2&0\\ 0&0&\lambda_3&0&0&\mu_3\\ \mu_1&0&0&\lambda_1&0&0\\ 0&\mu_2&0&0&\lambda_2&0\\ 0&0&\mu_3&0&0&\lambda_3\end{pmatrix}$$

relative to the basis $e_{1} \land e_{2}, e_{1} \land e_{3}, e_{1} \land e_{4}, e_{3} \land e_{4}, e_{4} \land e_{2}, e_{2} \land e_{3}$. One has that $\mu_{1} + \mu_{2} + \mu_{3}$ is zero and that $\lambda_{1} + \lambda_{2} + \lambda_{3}$ is one-fourth of the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) of $g$ at $p$. Furthermore, under the conditions $\lambda_{1} \le \lambda_{2} \le \lambda_{3}$ and $\mu_{1} \le \mu_{2} \le \mu_{3}$, each of these six functions is uniquely determined and defines a continuous real-valued function on $M$.

According to [Chern-Weil theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern-Weil_theory), if $M$ is oriented then the Euler characteristic and signature of $M$ can be computed by

$$\begin{aligned} \chi(M)&=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_M\big(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2+\lambda_3^2+\mu_1^2+\mu_2^2+\mu_3^2\big)\,d\mu_g\\ \tau(M)&=\frac{1}{3\pi^2}\int_M\big(\lambda_1\mu_1+\lambda_2\mu_2+\lambda_3\mu_3\big)\,d\mu_g. \end{aligned}$$

Equipped with these tools, the Hitchin-Thorpe inequality amounts to the elementary observation

$$\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2+\lambda_3^2+\mu_1^2+\mu_2^2+\mu_3^2=\underbrace{(\lambda_1-\mu_1)^2+(\lambda_2-\mu_2)^2+(\lambda_3-\mu_3)^2}_{\geq 0}+2\big(\lambda_1\mu_1+\lambda_2\mu_2+\lambda_3\mu_3\big).$$

:::

## Failure of the converse {#Failure_of_the_converse}

A natural question to ask is whether the Hitchin–Thorpe inequality provides a [sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition) for the existence of Einstein metrics. In 1995, [Claude LeBrun](wikipedia://en/38381127) and Andrea Sambusetti independently showed that the answer is no: there exist infinitely many non-homeomorphic compact, smooth, oriented 4-manifolds $M$ that carry no Einstein metrics but nevertheless satisfy

$$\chi(M) > \frac{3}{2}|\tau(M)|.$$

LeBrun's examples are actually simply connected, and the relevant obstruction depends on the smooth structure of the manifold.[^o11] By contrast, Sambusetti's obstruction only applies to 4-manifolds with infinite fundamental group, but the volume-entropy estimate he uses to prove non-existence only depends on the homotopy type of the manifold.[^o12]
