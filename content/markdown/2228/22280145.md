# Richard Laver

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Laver"}

*Image: Richard Laver.jpg.* Richard Laver

**Richard Joseph Laver** (October 20, 1942 – September 19, 2012) was an American mathematician, working in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Laver received his PhD at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1969, under the supervision of [Ralph McKenzie](wikipedia://en/49943169),[^o3] with a thesis on *Order Types and Well-Quasi-Orderings*. The largest part of his career he spent as Professor and later Emeritus Professor at the [University of Colorado at Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder).

Richard Laver died in [Boulder, CO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulder,_CO), on September 19, 2012 after a long illness.[^o4]

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

Among Laver's notable achievements some are the following.

- Using the theory of [better-quasi-orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better-quasi-order), introduced by [Nash-Williams](wikipedia://en/16550517), (an extension of the notion of [well-quasi-ordering](wikipedia://en/739775)), he proved[^o7] [Fraïssé's](wikipedia://en/4234672) conjecture (now [Laver's theorem](wikipedia://en/63406456)): if (*A*<sub>0</sub>,≤),(*A*<sub>1</sub>,≤),...,(*A*<sub>*i*</sub>,≤), are countable ordered sets, then for some *i*\<*j* (*A*<sub>i</sub>,≤) isomorphically embeds into (*A*<sub>*j*</sub>,≤). This also holds if the ordered sets are countable unions of [scattered](wikipedia://en/15922429) ordered sets.[^o8]
- He proved[^o9] the consistency of the [Borel conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_conjecture_%28set_theory%29), i.e., the statement that every [strong measure zero set](wikipedia://en/45200516) is countable. This important independence result was the first when a [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) (see [Laver forcing](wikipedia://en/15550368#Laver_forcing)), adding a real, was iterated with countable support iteration. This method was later used by [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) to introduce proper and semiproper forcing.
- He proved[^o10] the existence of a [Laver function](wikipedia://en/19089448) for [supercompact cardinals](wikipedia://en/248116). With the help of this, he proved the following result. If κ is supercompact, there is a κ-[c.c.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_condition) [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) notion (*P*, ≤) such that after forcing with (*P*, ≤) the following holds: κ is supercompact and remains supercompact in any forcing extension via a κ-directed closed forcing. This statement, known as the **indestructibility result**,[^o11] is used, for example, in the proof of the consistency of the [proper forcing axiom](wikipedia://en/6250799) and variants.
- Laver and [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) proved[^o12] that it is consistent that the continuum hypothesis holds and there are no ℵ<sub>2</sub>-[Suslin trees](wikipedia://en/5028195).
- Laver proved[^o13] that the perfect subtree version of the [Halpern–Läuchli theorem](wikipedia://en/4358338) holds for the product of infinitely many trees. This solved a longstanding open question.
- Laver started[^o14][^o15][^o16] investigating the algebra that *j* generates where *j*:*V*<sub>λ</sub>→*V*<sub>λ</sub> is some elementary embedding. This algebra is the free left-distributive algebra on one generator. For this he introduced [Laver tables](wikipedia://en/1050125).
- He also showed[^o17] that if *V*\[*G*\] is a (set-)[forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) extension of [*V*](wikipedia://en/445980), then *V* is a [class](wikipedia://en/20696) in *V*\[*G*\].
