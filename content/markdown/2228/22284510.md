# Hilbert's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert's lemma"}

**Hilbert's lemma** was proposed at the end of the 19th century by mathematician [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). The lemma describes a property of the [principal curvatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_curvatures) of surfaces. It may be used to prove [Liebmann's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebmann's_theorem) that a [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) surface with constant [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) must be a sphere.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Statement of the lemma"}

## Statement of the lemma {#Statement_of_the_lemma}

Given a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) in three dimensions that is [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) and [differentiable](wikipedia://en/2119219) over a patch containing the point *p*, where *k* and *m* are defined as the principal curvatures and *K*(*x*) is the [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) at a point *x*, if *k* has a max at *p*, *m* has a min at *p*, and *k* is strictly greater than *m* at *p*, then *K*(*p*) is a non-positive real number.[^o5]

:::
