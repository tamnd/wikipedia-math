# Arithmetical ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetical ring"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a [ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$ is said to be **arithmetical** (or **arithmetic**) if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

:::

1. The [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) $R_\mathfrak{m}$ of $R$ at $\mathfrak{m}$ is a [uniserial ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniserial_ring) for every [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) $\mathfrak{m}$ of $R$.
1. For all [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$, and $\mathfrak{c}$,

$$\mathfrak{a} \cap (\mathfrak{b} + \mathfrak{c}) = (\mathfrak{a} \cap \mathfrak{b}) + (\mathfrak{a} \cap \mathfrak{c})$$

1. For all ideals $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$, and $\mathfrak{c}$,

$$\mathfrak{a} + (\mathfrak{b} \cap \mathfrak{c}) = (\mathfrak{a} + \mathfrak{b}) \cap (\mathfrak{a} + \mathfrak{c})$$

The last two conditions both say that the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) of all ideals of $R$ is [distributive](wikipedia://en/310883).

An arithmetical [domain](wikipedia://en/487627) is the same thing as a [Prüfer domain](wikipedia://en/12367835).
