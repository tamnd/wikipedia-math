# Real-valued function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real-valued function"}

*Image: Weights 20mg~500g.jpg.* [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) measured in [grams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram) is a function from this collection of weight to [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) real numbers. The term "[weight function](wikipedia://en/245523)", an allusion to this example, is used in pure and applied mathematics.

In mathematics, a **real-valued function** is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) whose [values](wikipedia://en/50264) are [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). In other words, it is a function that assigns a real number to each member of its [domain](wikipedia://en/50263).

:::

Real-valued [functions of a real variable](wikipedia://en/1268958) (commonly called *real functions*) and real-valued [functions of several real variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_of_several_real_variables) are the main object of study of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and, more generally, [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478). In particular, many [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630) consist of real-valued functions.

## Algebraic structure {#Algebraic_structure}

Let ${\mathcal F}(X,{\mathbb R})$ be the set of all functions from a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $X$ to real numbers $\mathbb R$. Because $\mathbb R$ is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), ${\mathcal F}(X,{\mathbb R})$ may be turned into a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) and a [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) over the reals with the following operations:

- $f+g: x \mapsto f(x) + g(x)$ – [vector addition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_addition)
- $\mathbf{0}: x \mapsto 0$ – [additive identity](wikipedia://en/4178225)
- $c f: x \mapsto c f(x),\quad c \in \mathbb R$ – [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373)
- $f g: x \mapsto f(x)g(x)$ – [pointwise](wikipedia://en/4786797) multiplication

These operations extend to [partial functions](wikipedia://en/23577) from $X$ to $\mathbb R,$ with the restriction that the partial functions $f + g$ and $f g$ are defined only if the [domains](wikipedia://en/50263) of $f$ and $g$ have a nonempty intersection; in this case, their domain is the intersection of the domains of $f$ and $g$.

Also, since $\mathbb R$ is an ordered set, there is a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order)

- $$\ f \le g \quad\iff\quad \forall x: f(x) \le g(x),$$

on ${\mathcal F}(X,{\mathbb R}),$ which makes ${\mathcal F}(X,{\mathbb R})$ a [partially ordered ring](wikipedia://en/22211384).

## Measurable {#Measurable}

The [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586) of [Borel sets](wikipedia://en/43327) is an important structure on real numbers. If $X$ has its σ-algebra and a function $f$ is such that the [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) $f\,^{-1}(B)$ of any Borel set $B$ belongs to that σ-algebra, then $f$ is said to be [measurable](wikipedia://en/44055). Measurable functions also form a vector space and an algebra as explained above in .

Moreover, a set (family) of real-valued functions on $X$ can actually *define* a σ-algebra on $X$ generated by all preimages of all Borel sets (or of [intervals](wikipedia://en/49172) only, it is not important). This is the way how σ-algebras arise in ([Kolmogorov's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov's_axioms)) [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), where real-valued functions on the [sample space](wikipedia://en/22958) $\Omega$ are real-valued [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685).

## Continuous {#Continuous}

Real numbers form a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) and a [complete metric space](wikipedia://en/7832). [Continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) real-valued functions (which implies that $X$ is a topological space) are important in theories [of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/178649) and [of metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry). The [extreme value theorem](wikipedia://en/276881) states that for any real continuous function on a [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042) its global [maximum and minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima) exist.

The concept of [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) itself is defined with a real-valued function of two variables, the *[metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29)*, which is continuous. The space of [continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_functions_on_a_compact_Hausdorff_space) has a particular importance. [Convergent sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_sequence) also can be considered as real-valued continuous functions on a special topological space.

Continuous functions also form a vector space and an algebra as explained above in , and are a subclass of measurable functions because any topological space has the σ-algebra generated by open (or closed) sets.

## Smooth {#Smooth}

Real numbers are used as the codomain to define smooth functions. A domain of a real smooth function can be the [real coordinate space](wikipedia://en/1482083) (which yields a [real multivariable function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_multivariable_function)), a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752),[^o6] an [open subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) of them, or a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold).

Spaces of smooth functions also are vector spaces and algebras as explained above in and are subspaces of the space of continuous functions.

## Appearances in measure theory {#Appearances_in_measure_theory}

A [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on a set is a [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) real-valued functional on a σ-algebra of subsets.[^o8] [L<sup>*p*</sup> spaces](wikipedia://en/45194) on sets with a measure are defined from aforementioned real-valued measurable functions, although they are actually [quotient spaces](wikipedia://en/237213). More precisely, whereas a function satisfying an appropriate [summability condition](wikipedia://en/15532) defines an element of L<sup>*p*</sup> space, in the opposite direction for any $f \in L^{p}(X)$ and $x \in X$ which is not an [atom](wikipedia://en/3058373), the value $f(x)$ is [undefined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-definition). Though, real-valued L<sup>*p*</sup> spaces still have some of the structure described above in . Each of L<sup>*p*</sup> spaces is a vector space and have a partial order, and there exists a pointwise multiplication of "functions" which changes $p$, namely

$$\cdot: L^{1/\alpha} \times L^{1/\beta} \to L^{1/(\alpha+\beta)},\quad 0 \le \alpha,\beta \le 1,\quad\alpha+\beta \le 1.$$

For example, pointwise product of two L<sup>2</sup> functions belongs to L<sup>1</sup>.

## Other appearances {#Other_appearances}

Other contexts where real-valued functions and their special properties are used include [monotonic functions](wikipedia://en/48260) (on [ordered sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_set)), [convex functions](wikipedia://en/245568) (on vector and [affine spaces](wikipedia://en/298834)), [harmonic](wikipedia://en/55762) and [subharmonic](wikipedia://en/3237359) functions (on [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652)), [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) (usually of one or more real variables), [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169) (on real [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808)), and [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) (of one or more real variables).
