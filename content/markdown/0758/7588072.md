# Dispersion point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dispersion point"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **dispersion point**[^n1][^n2] of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is a point $p\in X$ such that $X$ is [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_%28topology%29) and removing the point leaves $X\setminus\{p\}$ [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected) (meaning that its [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29) are all singletons, i.e., $X\setminus\{p\}$ contains no connected subset of size at least two).

:::

Examples of spaces with a dispersion point include:

- the [Knaster–Kuratowski fan](wikipedia://en/14988066),[^n3]
- spaces with a [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200),
- spaces with an [excluded point topology](wikipedia://en/5539197).

A connected space $X$ with $|X|\ge 3$ can have at most one dispersion point.[^o5] The case $|X|=2$ (covering [Sierpiński space](wikipedia://en/387651) and an [indiscrete space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_space) of size two) is a degenerate case, where every point of $X$ is a dispersion point (because removing a point leaves a singleton, which is totally disconnected).

## Explosion point {#Explosion_point}

An **explosion point**[^n2] of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is a point $p\in X$ such that $X$ is [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_%28topology%29) and removing the point leaves $X\setminus\{p\}$ [totally separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_separated) (meaning that for any two distinct points $x$ and $y$ there is a [clopen set](wikipedia://en/233398) containing $x$ and not $y$).

Every explosion point is a dispersion point (because totally separated spaces are totally disconnected).

Examples of spaces with an explosion point include spaces with a [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200) and spaces with an [excluded point topology](wikipedia://en/5539197). The [Knaster–Kuratowski fan](wikipedia://en/14988066) has a dispersion point which is not an explosion point.[^n4]

A space of size at least three can have at most one explosion point.

If *p* is an explosion point for a space *X*, then the totally separated space $X\setminus \{p\}$ is said to be *pulverized*.
