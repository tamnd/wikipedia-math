# P-space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="P-space"}

In the mathematical field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), there are various notions of a ***P*-space** and of a ***p*-space**.

:::

## Generic use {#Generic_use}

The expression *P-space* might be used generically to denote a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) satisfying some given and previously introduced [topological invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_invariant) *P*.[^o3] This might apply also to [spaces](wikipedia://en/5308894) of a different kind, i.e. non-topological spaces with additional structure.

## *P-spaces* in the sense of Gillman–Henriksen {#P-spaces_in_the_sense_of_Gillman–Henriksen}

A *P-space* in the sense of [Gillman](wikipedia://en/15123686)–[Henriksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Henriksen) is a topological space in which every [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) is open. An equivalent condition is that countable [unions](wikipedia://en/47949) of [closed sets](wikipedia://en/47279) are closed. In other words, [G<sub>δ</sub> sets](wikipedia://en/252076) are open and [F<sub>σ</sub> sets](wikipedia://en/396575) are closed. The letter *P* stands for both *pseudo-discrete* and *prime*. Gillman and Henriksen also define a *P-point* as a point at which any [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) of the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [real-valued](wikipedia://en/20646438) [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) is [maximal](wikipedia://en/48164), and a P-space is a space in which every point is a P-point.[^o5]

Different authors restrict their attention to topological spaces that satisfy various [separation axioms](wikipedia://en/40117302). With the right axioms, one may characterize *P*-spaces in terms of their rings of continuous [real-valued functions](wikipedia://en/22284647).[^o5]

Special kinds of *P*-spaces include [Alexandrov-discrete spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrov-discrete_space), in which arbitrary intersections of open sets are open. These in turn include [locally finite spaces](wikipedia://en/17267863), which include [finite spaces](wikipedia://en/14682596) and [discrete spaces](wikipedia://en/56061).

## *P-spaces* in the sense of Morita {#P-spaces_in_the_sense_of_Morita}

A different notion of a *P-space* has been introduced by [Kiiti Morita](wikipedia://en/12034472) in 1964, in connection with [his (now solved) conjectures](wikipedia://en/12034549) (see the relevant entry for more information). Spaces satisfying the covering property introduced by Morita are sometimes also called *Morita P-spaces* or *normal P-spaces*.

## *p-spaces* {#p-spaces}

A notion of a *p-space* has been introduced by [Alexander Arhangelskii](wikipedia://en/36284117).[^o8]
