# L(h, k)-coloring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="L(h, k)-coloring"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **$L(h, k)$-labelling**, **$L(h, k)$-coloring** or sometimes **$L(p, q)$-coloring** is a [(proper) vertex coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) in which every pair of adjacent [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) has [color numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) that differ by at least $h$, and any nodes connected by a 2 length path have their colors differ by at least $k$.[^o2] The parameters $h, k$ are understood to be non-negative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

:::

The problem originated from a channel assignment problem in radio networks. The **span** of an $L(h, k)$-labelling, $\rho_{h,k}(G)$ is the difference between the largest and the smallest assigned frequency. The goal of the $L(h, k)$-labelling problem is usually to find a labelling with minimum span.[^o3] For a given graph, the minimum span over all possible labelling functions is the $\lambda_{h,k}$-number of $G$, denoted by $\lambda_{h,k}(G)$.

When $h = 1$ and $k = 0$, it is the usual [(proper) vertex coloring](wikipedia://en/426743).

There is a very large number of articles concerning $L(h, k)$-labelling, with different $h$ and $k$ parameters and different classes of graphs.

In some variants, the goal is to minimize the number of used colors (the *order*).
