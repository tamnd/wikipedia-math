# Less-than sign

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Less-than sign"}

\<

The **less-than sign** is a mathematical symbol that denotes an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) between two values. The widely adopted form of two equal-length strokes connecting in an acute angle at the left, \<, has been found in documents dated as far back as the 1560s. In mathematical writing, the less-than sign is typically placed between two values being compared and signifies that the first number is less than the second number. Examples of typical usage include $1 < 4$ and $-2 < 0$.

:::

Since the development of computer [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015), the less-than sign and the greater-than sign have been repurposed for a range of uses and operations.

## Computing {#Computing}

The **less-than sign**, \<, is an original [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) character (hex 3C, decimal 60).[^o4]

### Programming {#Programming}

In [BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC), [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29)-family languages, and [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29)-family languages (including [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29) and [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++)), [comparison operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28computing%29) `<` means "less than".

In [Coldfusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldfusion), operator `.lt.` means "less than".

In [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran), operator `.LT.` means "less than"; later versions allow `<`.

#### Shell scripts {#Shell_scripts}

In [Bourne shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourne_shell) (and many other shells), operator `-lt` means "less than". Less-than sign is used to [redirect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redirection_%28computing%29) input from a file. Less-than plus ampersand (\<&) is used to redirect from a [file descriptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File_descriptor).

#### Double less-than sign {#Double_less-than_sign}

The **double less-than sign**, \<\<, may be used for an approximation of the *much-less-than sign* (≪) or of the opening [guillemet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillemet) («). ASCII does not encode either of these signs, though they are both included in [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode).

In [Bash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bash_%28Unix_shell%29), [Perl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perl), and [Ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_%28programming_language%29), operator \<\<EOF (where "EOF" is an arbitrary string, but commonly "EOF" denoting "end of file") is used to denote the beginning of a [here document](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_document).

In [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) and [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++), operator \<\< represents a [binary left shift](wikipedia://en/264399#Shifts_in_C,_C++,_C#,_Python).

In the [C++ Standard Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++_Standard_Library), operator \<\<, when applied on an output stream, acts as *insertion operator* and performs an output operation on the stream.

In [Ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_%28programming_language%29), operator \<\< acts as *append operator* when used between an array and the value to be appended.

In [XPath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XPath) the \<\< operator returns true if the left operand precedes the right operand in document order; otherwise it returns false.[^o8]

#### Triple less-than sign {#Triple_less-than_sign}

In [PHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHP), operator \<\<\<OUTPUT is used to denote the beginning of a [heredoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_document) statement (where `OUTPUT` is an arbitrary named variable.)

In Bash, \<\<\<word is used as a "here string", where word is expanded and supplied to the command on its standard input, similar to a heredoc.

#### Less-than sign with equals sign {#Less-than_sign_with_equals_sign}

The less-than sign with the equals sign, \<=, may be used for an approximation of the [less-than-or-equal-to sign](wikipedia://en/89489), ≤. ASCII does not have a less-than-or-equal-to sign, but [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) defines it at code point 2264.

In [BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC), [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29)-family languages, and [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29)-family languages (including [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29) and [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++)), operator \<= means "less than or equal to". In [Sinclair BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinclair_BASIC) it is encoded as a single-byte code point token.

In [Prolog](wikipedia://en/23485), =\< means "less than or equal to" (as distinct from the arrow \<=).

In [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran), operators .LE. and \<= both mean "less than or equal to".

In [Bourne shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourne_shell) and [Windows PowerShell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_PowerShell), the operator -le means "less than or equal to".

#### Less-than sign with hyphen-minus {#Less-than_sign_with_hyphen-minus}

In the [R programming language](wikipedia://en/376707), the less-than sign is used in conjunction with a [hyphen-minus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen-minus) to create an arrow (\<-), this can be used as the left assignment operator.

#### Spaceship operator {#Spaceship_operator}

The less-than sign is used in the [spaceship operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceship_operator).

#### HTML {#HTML}

In [HTML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML) (and [SGML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Generalized_Markup_Language) and [XML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XML)), the less-than sign is used at the beginning of tags. The less-than sign may be included with `&lt;`. The less-than-or-equal-to sign, ≤, may be included with `&le;`.

The less-than sign may be seen for an approximation of the opening [angle bracket](wikipedia://en/59338#Angle_brackets), ⟨. True angle bracket characters, as required in [linguistics notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notation#Linguistics), are expected in formal texts.

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

In an inequality, the less-than sign and greater-than sign always "point" to the smaller number. Put another way, the "jaws" (the wider section of the symbol) always direct to the larger number.

The less-than-sign is sometimes used to represent a [total order](wikipedia://en/30330), [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) or [preorder](wikipedia://en/23582). However, the symbol $\prec$ is often used when it would be confusing or not convenient to use \<. In mathematical writing using [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX), the [TeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX) command is \\prec . The Unicode [code point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) is 227A.
