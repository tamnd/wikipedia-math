# Trigonometric moment problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trigonometric moment problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **trigonometric [moment problem](wikipedia://en/1244992)** is formulated as follows: given a sequence $\{c_k\}_{k \in \mathbb{N}_{0}}$, does there exist a [distribution function](wikipedia://en/58117270) $\sigma$ on the interval $[0,2\pi]$ such that:[^n1][^n2]

$$c_k = \frac{1}{2 \pi}\int_0 ^{2 \pi} e^{-ik\theta}\,d \sigma(\theta),$$

with $c_{-k} = \overline{c}_k$ for $k \geq 1$. An affirmative answer to the problem means that $\{c_k\}_{k \in \mathbb{N}_{0}}$ are the [Fourier-Stieltjes coefficients](wikipedia://en/59038#Fourier-Stieltjes_series) for some (consequently positive) unique [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933) $\mu$ on $[0,2\pi]$ as distribution function.[^n3][^n4][^n5][^n6]

:::

In case the sequence is finite, i.e., $\{c_k\}_{k = 0}^{n < \infty}$, it is referred to as the **truncated trigonometric moment problem**.[^n7]

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

The trigonometric moment problem is solvable, that is, $\{c_k\}_{k=0}^{n}$ is a sequence of Fourier coefficients, if and only if the $(n + 1) \times (n + 1)$ [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682) [Toeplitz matrix](wikipedia://en/151569)

$$T = \left(\begin{matrix} c_0 & c_1 & \cdots & c_n \\ c_{-1} & c_0 & \cdots & c_{n-1} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ c_{-n} & c_{-n+1} & \cdots & c_0 \\ \end{matrix}\right)$$

with $c_{-k}=\overline{c_{k}}$ for $k \geq 1$, is [positive semi-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-semidefinite_matrix).[^n6]

The "only if" part of the claims can be verified by a direct calculation. We sketch an argument for the converse. The positive semidefinite matrix $T$ defines a [sesquilinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesquilinear) product on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$, resulting in a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932)

$$(\mathcal{H}, \langle \;,\; \rangle)$$

of dimensional at most $n + 1$. The Toeplitz structure of $T$ means that a "truncated" shift is a [partial isometry](wikipedia://en/1348079) on $\mathcal{H}$. More specifically, let $\{e_0,\dotsc,e_n\}$ be the standard basis of $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$. Let $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ be subspaces generated by the [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) $\{[e_0],\dotsc,[e_{n-1}]\}$ respectively $\{[e_1],\dotsc,[e_{n}]\}$. Define an operator

$$V: \mathcal{E} \rightarrow \mathcal{F}$$

by

$$V[e_k] = [e_{k+1}] \quad \mbox{for} \quad k = 0 \ldots n-1.$$

Since

$$\langle V[e_j], V[e_k] \rangle = \langle [e_{j+1}], [e_{k+1}] \rangle = T_{j+1, k+1} = T_{j, k} = \langle [e_{j}], [e_{k}] \rangle,$$

$V$ can be extended to a partial isometry acting on all of $\mathcal{H}$. Take a minimal [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999) extension $U$ of $V$, on a possibly larger space (this always exists). According to the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495),[^n8][^n9] there exists a Borel measure $m$ on the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/508177) $\mathbb{T}$ such that for all integer $k$

$$\langle (U^*)^k [ e_ {n+1} ], [ e_ {n+1} ] \rangle = \int_{\mathbb{T}} z^{k} dm .$$

For $k = 0,\dotsc,n$, the left hand side is

$$\langle (U^*)^k [ e_ {n+1} ], [ e_ {n+1} ] \rangle = \langle (V^*)^k [ e_ {n+1} ], [ e_{n+1} ] \rangle = \langle [e_{n+1-k}], [ e_{n+1} ] \rangle = T_{n+1, n+1-k} = c_{-k}=\overline{c_k}.$$

As such, there is a $j$-[atomic measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_measure) $m$ on $\mathbb{T}$, with $j \leq 2n + 1 < \infty$ (i.e. the set is finite), such that[^n10]

$$c_k = \int_{\mathbb{T}} z^{-k} dm = \int_{\mathbb{T}} \bar{z}^k dm,$$

which is equivalent to

$$c_k = \frac{1}{2 \pi} \int_0 ^{2 \pi} e^{-ik\theta} d\mu(\theta).$$

for some suitable measure $\mu$.

### Parametrization of solutions {#Parametrization_of_solutions}

The above discussion shows that the truncated trigonometric moment problem has infinitely many solutions if the Toeplitz matrix $T$ is invertible.[^n11][^n12] In that case, the solutions to the problem are in bijective correspondence with minimal unitary extensions of the [partial isometry](wikipedia://en/1348079) $V$.
