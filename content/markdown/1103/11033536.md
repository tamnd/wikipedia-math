# Greater-than sign

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Greater-than sign"}

The **greater-than sign** is a mathematical symbol that denotes an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) between two values. The widely adopted form of two equal-length strokes connecting in an acute angle at the right, **\>**, has been found in documents dated as far back as 1631.[^o4] In mathematical writing, the greater-than sign is typically placed between two values being compared and signifies that the first number is greater than the second number. Examples of typical usage include $1.5 > 1$ and $1 > -2$. The less-than sign and greater-than sign always "point" to the smaller number. Since the development of computer [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015), the greater-than sign and the [less-than sign](wikipedia://en/11033535) have been repurposed for a range of uses and operations.

:::

## History {#History}

The earliest known use of the symbols \< and > is found in Artis Analyticae Praxis ad Aequationes Algebraicas Resolvendas (*The Analytical Arts Applied to Solving Algebraic Equations*) by [Thomas Harriot](wikipedia://en/780445), published posthumously in 1631.[^o4] The text states "Signum majoritatis ut a > b significet a majorem quam b (The sign of majority a > b indicates that a is greater than b)" and "Signum minoritatis ut a \< b significet a minorem quam b (The sign of minority a \< b indicates that a is less than b)."

According to historian Art Johnson, while Harriot was surveying North America, he saw a [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) with a symbol that resembled the greater-than sign,[^o4] in both backwards and forwards forms.[^o5] Johnson says it is likely Harriot developed the two symbols from this symbol.[^o5]

## Usage in text markup {#Usage_in_text_markup}

### Angle brackets {#Angle_brackets}

The greater-than sign is sometimes used for an approximation of the [closing angle bracket](wikipedia://en/59338#Angle_brackets), ⟩. The proper Unicode character is 232A. ASCII does not have angular brackets.

### HTML {#HTML}

In [HTML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML) (and [SGML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGML) and [XML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XML)), the greater-than sign is used at the end of tags. The greater-than sign may be included with >, while ≥ produces the greater-than or equal to sign.

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Greater-than quoting"}

### Quoting {#Quoting}

In some [e-mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mail) systems, the greater-than sign is used to denote [quotations](wikipedia://en/25199).[^o11][^o12] The sign is also used to denote quotations in [Markdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markdown).[^o13]

The [diple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diple_%28textual_symbol%29) was a mark resembling a greater-than that was sometimes used to mark quotations.

:::

## Usage in programming {#Usage_in_programming}

The 'greater-than sign' > is encoded in [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) as character hex 3E, decimal 62. The [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) [code point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) is 003E, inherited from ASCII.

For use with [HTML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML), the mnemonics > or > may also be used.

### Programming language {#Programming_language}

[BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC) and [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29)-family languages (including [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29)[^o16] and [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++)) use the [comparison operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28computer_programming%29) > to mean "greater than". In [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29)-family languages, > is a function used to mean "greater than". In [Coldfusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldfusion) and [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran), operator .GT. means "greater than".

### Double greater-than sign {#Double_greater-than_sign}

\>> is used for an approximation of the *[much-greater-than sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Much-greater-than_sign)* ≫. ASCII does not have the much greater-than sign.

The double greater-than sign is also used for an approximation of the closing [guillemet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillemet), ».

In [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29), [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29), and [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++), the operator >> is the [right-shift operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-shift_operator). In C++ it is also used to get input from a [stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_%28computing%29), similar to the C functions getchar and fgets.

In [Haskell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskell_%28programming_language%29), the >> function is a monadic operator. It is used for sequentially composing two actions, discarding any value produced by the first. In that regard, it is like the statement sequencing operator in imperative languages, such as the semicolon in C.

In [XPath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XPath) the >> operator returns true if the left operand follows the right operand in document order; otherwise it returns false.[^o18]

### Triple greater-than sign {#Triple_greater-than_sign}

\>>> is the unsigned-right-shift operator in [JavaScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JavaScript). Three greater-than signs form the distinctive prompt of the firmware console in [MicroVAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroVAX), [VAXstation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAXstation), and [DEC Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEC_Alpha) computers (known as the [SRM console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Reference_Manual) in the latter). This is also the default prompt of the [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) interactive shell, often seen for code examples that can be executed interactively in the interpreter:

```pycon
$ python
Python 3.9.2 (default, Feb 20 2021, 18:40:11) 
[GCC 10.2.0] on linux
Type "help", "copyright", "credits" or "license" for more information.
>>> print("Hello World")
Hello World
>>> 
```

### Greater-than sign with equals sign {#Greater-than_sign_with_equals_sign}

\>= is sometimes used for an approximation of the [greater than or equal to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_than_or_equal_to) sign, ≥ which was not included in the ASCII repertoire. The sign is, however, provided in [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode), as 2265.

In [BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC), [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29)-family languages, [Lua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lua_%28programming_language%29) and [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29)-family languages (including [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29) and [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++)) the operator >= means "greater than or equal to". In [Sinclair BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinclair_BASIC) it is encoded as a single-byte code point token.

In [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran), the operator .GE. means "greater than or equal to".

In [Bourne shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourne_shell) and [Windows PowerShell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_PowerShell), the operator -ge means "greater than or equal to".

### Hyphen-minus with greater-than sign {#Hyphen-minus_with_greater-than_sign}

\-> is used in some programming languages (for example [F#](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F_Sharp_%28programming_language%29)) to create an arrow. Arrows like these could also be used in text where other [arrow symbols](wikipedia://en/4912832) are unavailable. In the [R programming language](wikipedia://en/376707), this can be used as the right assignment operator. In the [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29), [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++), and [PHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHP), this is used as a member access operator. In [Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_%28programming_language%29) and [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29), it is used to indicate the return value type when defining a [function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_%28Programming%29) (i.e., func foo() -> MyClass {...}).

### Shell scripts {#Shell_scripts}

In [Bourne shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourne_shell) (and many other shells), greater-than sign is used to [redirect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redirection_%28Unix%29) output to a file. Greater-than plus ampersand (>&) is used to redirect to a [file descriptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File_descriptor).

### Spaceship operator {#Spaceship_operator}

Greater-than sign is used in the '[spaceship operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceship_operator)', \<=>.

### ECMAScript and C# {#ECMAScript_and_C#}

In [ECMAScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECMAScript) and [C#](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_Sharp_%28programming_language%29), the greater-than sign is used in lambda function expressions.

In ECMAScript:

```javascript
const square = x => x * x;
console.log(square(5)); // 25
```

In C#:

```c#
Func<int, int> square = x => x * x;
Console.WriteLine(square(5)); // 25
```

### PHP {#PHP}

In [PHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHP), the greater-than sign is used in conjunction with the less-than sign as a not equal to operator. It is the same as the `!=` operator.

```php
$x = 5;
$y = 3;
$z = 5;

echo $x <> $y; // true
echo $x <> $z; // false
```
