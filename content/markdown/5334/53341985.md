# Bickley–Naylor functions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bickley–Naylor functions"}

*Image: Fonction Bickley-Naylor.png.*

In physics, engineering, and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), the **Bickley–Naylor functions** are a sequence of special functions arising in formulas for [thermal radiation](wikipedia://en/185239) intensities in hot enclosures. The solutions are often quite complicated unless the problem is essentially one-dimensional[^o2] (such as the radiation field in a thin layer of gas between two parallel rectangular plates). These functions have practical applications in several engineering problems related to transport of thermal[^o3][^o4] or neutron,[^o5][^o6] radiation in systems with special symmetries (e.g. spherical or axial symmetry). W. G. Bickley was a British mathematician born in 1893.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The *n*th Bickley−Naylor function $\operatorname{Ki}_n(x)$ is defined by

$$\operatorname{Ki}_n (x) = \int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-x/\cos \theta}\cos^{n-1}\theta \, d\theta.$$

and it is classified as one of the generalized exponential integral functions.

All of the functions $\operatorname{Ki}_n(x)$ for positive integer *n* are monotonously decreasing functions, because $e^{-x}$ is a decreasing function and $\sin x$ is a positive increasing function for $x\in (0,\pi /2)$.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The integral defining the function $\operatorname{Ki}_n(x)$ generally cannot be evaluated analytically, but can be approximated to a desired accuracy with [Riemann sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_sums) or other methods, taking the limit as *a* → 0 in the interval of integration, \[*a*, $\pi$/2\].

Alternative ways to define the function $\operatorname{Ki}_n(x)$ include the integral,[^o12] [integral forms the Bickley-Naylor function](https://lib.dr.iastate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=10261&context=rtd):

$$\operatorname{Ki}_n (x) = \int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-x/\cos \theta}\cos^{n-1}\theta \, d\theta.$$

$$\operatorname{Ki}_n (x) = \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x\cosh t}}{\cosh^n t} \, dt.$$

$$\operatorname{Ki}_n (x)=\int_1^\infty \frac{e^{-xt}dt}{t^n \sqrt{t^2-1}}$$

$$\operatorname{Ki}_n (x) =\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_x^\infty (t-x)^{n-1}K_0(t)dt.$$

$$\frac{\operatorname{Ki}_n (x)}{x^n}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_1^\infty (t-1)^{n-1}K_0(xt)dt.$$

where $\operatorname{K}_0(x)$ is the modified [Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700) of the zeroth order. Also by definition we have $\operatorname{Ki}_0(x)=\operatorname{K}_0(x)$.

:::

## Series expansions {#Series_expansions}

The series expansions of the first and second order Bickley functions are given by:

$$\operatorname{Ki}_1 (x)=\frac{\pi}{2}+x\left(\gamma+\ln\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right) \sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(x^2/4)^k}{(k!)^2(2k+1)}-x \sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(x^2/4)^k}{(k!)^2(2k+1)^2} -x\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{(x^2/4)^kH_k}{(k!)^2(2k+1)}$$

$$\operatorname{Ki}_2 (x)=1-\frac{\pi}{2}x-\frac{x^2}{2}\left(\gamma+\ln\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right)\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(x^2/4)^k}{k!(k+1)!(2k+1)}+\frac{x^2}{4}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(4k+3)(x^2/4)^k}{k!(k+1)!(2k+1)^2}+\frac{x^2}{2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{(x^2/4)^kH_k}{k!(k+1)!(2k+1)}$$

where $\gamma$ is the Euler constant and

$$H_k=1+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\ldots+\frac{1}{k}$$

is the $k$th [harmonic number](wikipedia://en/214729).

## Recurrence relation {#Recurrence_relation}

The Bickley functions also satisfy the following recurrence relation:[^o15]

$$n \operatorname{Ki}_{n+1}(x)=(n-1)\operatorname{Ki}_{n-1}(x)-x \operatorname{Ki}_n(x)+x \operatorname{Ki}_{n-2}(x),~~~~~n\ge 2$$

where $\operatorname{Ki}_0(x)=\operatorname{K}_0(x)$.

## Asymptotic expansions {#Asymptotic_expansions}

The asymptotic expansions of Bickley functions are given as[^o17]

$$\operatorname{Ki}_n(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}e^{-x}\left(1-\frac{(1+4n)}{8x}+\frac{3(3+24n+16n^2)}{12 8x^2}+\ldots\right)$$

for $x \gg 1$.

## Successive differentiation {#Successive_differentiation}

Differentiating $\operatorname{Ki}_{n+1}(x)$ with respect to x gives

$$\frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{Ki}_{n+1}(x)=-\operatorname{Ki}_n(x)$$

Successive differentiation yields

$$\frac{d^n}{dx^n}\operatorname{Ki}_n(x)=(-1)^n\operatorname{K}_0(x)$$
