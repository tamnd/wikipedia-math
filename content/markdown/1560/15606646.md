# Exceptional inverse image functor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exceptional inverse image functor"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory), a branch of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **exceptional inverse image functor** is the fourth and most sophisticated in a series of [image functors for sheaves](wikipedia://en/15769518). It is needed to express [Verdier duality](wikipedia://en/7190735) in its most general form.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *f*: *X* → *Y* be a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) of [topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_spaces) or a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754). Then the exceptional inverse image is a functor

R*f*<sup>!</sup>: D(*Y*) → D(*X*)

where D(–) denotes the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) of abelian groups or modules over a fixed ring.

It is defined to be the [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) of the [total derived functor](wikipedia://en/433875) R*f*<sub>!</sub> of the [direct image with compact support](wikipedia://en/13910217). Its existence follows from certain properties of R*f*<sub>!</sub> and general theorems about existence of adjoint functors, as does the unicity.

The notation R*f*<sup>!</sup> is an abuse of notation insofar as there is in general no functor *f*<sup>!</sup> whose derived functor would be R*f*<sup>!</sup>.

:::

## Examples and properties {#Examples_and_properties}

- If *f*: *X* → *Y* is an [immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953) of a [locally closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_closed) subspace, then it is possible to define

*f*<sup>!</sup>(*F*) := *f*<sup>∗</sup> *G*,

where *G* is the subsheaf of *F* of which the sections on some open subset *U* of *Y* are the sections *s* ∈ *F*(*U*) whose [support](wikipedia://en/381013) is contained in *X*. The functor *f*<sup>!</sup> is [left exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_exact_functor), and the above R*f*<sup>!</sup>, whose existence is guaranteed by [abstract nonsense](wikipedia://en/630022), is indeed the derived functor of this *f*<sup>!</sup>. Moreover *f*<sup>!</sup> is right adjoint to [*f*<sub>!</sub>](wikipedia://en/13910217), too.

- Slightly more generally, a similar statement holds for any [quasi-finite morphism](wikipedia://en/2143056) such as an [étale morphism](wikipedia://en/3022771).
- If *f* is an [open immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_immersion), the exceptional inverse image equals the usual [inverse image](wikipedia://en/7086661).

## Duality of the exceptional inverse image functor {#Duality_of_the_exceptional_inverse_image_functor}

Let $X$ be a smooth manifold of dimension $d$ and let $f: X \rightarrow *$ be the unique map which maps everything to one point. For a ring $\Lambda$, one finds that $f^{!} \Lambda=\omega_{X, \Lambda}[d]$ is the shifted $\Lambda$-[orientation sheaf](wikipedia://en/49112440).

On the other hand, let $X$ be a smooth $k$-variety of dimension $d$. If $f: X \rightarrow \operatorname{Spec}(k)$ denotes the structure morphism then $f^{!} k \cong \omega_{X}[d]$ is the shifted [canonical sheaf](wikipedia://en/734893) on $X$.

Moreover, let $X$ be a smooth $k$-variety of dimension $d$ and $\ell$ a prime invertible in $k$. Then $f^{!} \mathbb{Q}_{\ell} \cong \mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(d)[2 d]$ where $(d)$ denotes the [Tate twist](wikipedia://en/3094946).

Recalling the definition of the compactly supported cohomology as [lower-shriek pushforward](wikipedia://en/13910217) and noting that below the last $\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}$ means the constant sheaf on $X$ and the rest mean that on $*$, $f:X\to *$, and

$$\mathrm{H}_{c}^{n}(X)^{*} \cong \operatorname{Hom}\left(f_! f^{*} \mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[n], \mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\right) \cong \operatorname{Hom}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}, f_{*} f^{!} \mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[-n]\right),$$

the above computation furnishes the $\ell$-adic [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732)

$$\mathrm{H}_{c}^{n}\left(X ; \mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\right)^{*} \cong \mathrm{H}^{2 d-n}(X ; \mathbb{Q}(d))$$

from the repeated application of the adjunction condition.
