# Siobhán Vernon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Siobhán Vernon"}

**Siobhán Vernon** (née O'Shea) was the first Irish-born woman to obtain a PhD in pure mathematics in Ireland, in 1964.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Siobhán O'Shea was born in [Macroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroom), County Cork in 1932 and was the daughter of Joseph J. O'Shea and his wife M. O'Shea.

Her post-primary education was at the Convent of Mercy in Macroom, but she also attended the De La Salle, a secondary school for boys, for the higher level mathematics classes required for the Irish [Leaving Certificate](wikipedia://en/179302) examination. She entered [University College, Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Cork) in 1949 and was awarded a college scholarship in 1950, based on the results of her first year examinations. She graduated in 1952 with a first class honours B.Sc. in Mathematics and Mathematical Physics. She went on to complete her M.Sc. in Mathematics and Mathematics Statistics, awarded in 1954.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Siobhán Vernon worked as a demonstrator in the Department of Mathematics at University College, Cork while she completed her M.Sc. and was then appointed Senior Demonstrator.[^o5] Encouraged by Dr Patrick Brendan Kennedy, Siobhán began to publish research in 1956 and was appointed to the full-time post of Assistant in 1957. Continuing her research career, she spent a year as a visiting lecturer in [Royal Holloway College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway,_University_of_London), University of London, in 1962-63.[^o6]

Returning to University College, Cork, she submitted her published papers for the award of PhD, which was awarded in 1964 by the [National University of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Ireland). She was appointed lecturer in 1965.[^o6]

Following her marriage to geologist Peter Vernon, Siobhán reduced her teaching to half-time, as they raised their four children. She later returned to full time teaching, retiring in 1988.[^o5]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1995 she was honoured with a Catherine McAuley award as a distinguished past pupil by the Convent of Mercy in Macroom.[^o6]

## Later life and death {#Later_life_and_death}

Her final publication came after her retirement, when she contributed a chapter on Paddy Kennedy in Creators of Mathematics: The Irish Connection.[^o10] She died on 18 September 2002.[^o11]
