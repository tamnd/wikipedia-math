# Mary Wheeler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mary Wheeler"}

**Mary Fanett Wheeler** (born December 28, 1938)[^o3] is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). She is known for her work on [numerical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_methods) for [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), including [domain decomposition methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_decomposition_method).

:::

In 1998, Wheeler was elected a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) for "the computer simulation of subsurface flow and the underlying mathematical algorithms".[^o4]

In 2009 she was awarded the [Theodore von Kármán Prize](wikipedia://en/24419775) by the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM).[^o5]

## Personal background {#Personal_background}

Mary Fanett Wheeler was born on December 28, 1938, in [Cuero, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuero,_Texas).[^o3] She earned a double major in social sciences and mathematics from the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) in 1960, and a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1963.[^o3] She did her masters thesis on the [Peaceman-Rachford method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_direction_implicit_method), and later went on to do her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) under Rachford at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) in 1971.[^o7]

## Professional background {#Professional_background}

Wheeler studies [finite element analysis](wikipedia://en/18233581) and porous media problems with applications in engineering, oil-field exploitation, and the cleaning up of environmental pollution.[^o7] Her early work consisted of fundamental contributions to [finite element methods](wikipedia://en/18233581) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o7] She then moved into porous media problems, using her numerical expertise to study problems in the oil industry such as managing oil-field extraction.[^o7] She also studies environmental problems such as cleaning up underground reservoirs, spills of toxic waste, and [carbon dioxide sequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration).[^o7] In addition, Wheeler has worked with the [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) on environmental impact in the [Chesapeake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_Bay), [Delaware Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Bay), and [Florida Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Bay).[^o7]

On the matter of pure versus applied math, Wheeler has been noted to say "To me it is important to see your work used. I do abstract things as well, and I don't know if I will live to see them applied."[^o7]

Wheeler worked at the [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) from 1971 to 1995, with a two-year hiatus at [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston) from 1988 to 1990.[^o7] In 1995 she moved to the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) (UT) where she served as the director of the [Center for Subsurface Modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Subsurface_Modeling)[^o9] at the [Oden Institute for Computational Engineering and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oden_Institute_for_Computational_Engineering_and_Sciences).[^o10] She retired from UT in 2024.[^o11]

Wheeler is a Professional [Engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) registered with the State of Texas since 1999.[^o12] In 1989, she gave the prestigious [Noether Lecture](wikipedia://en/11566873) for the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in [Phoenix, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona).[^o13] Her talk was titled "Large Scale Modeling of Problems Arising in Flow in Porous Media".[^o13]
