# Pompeiu derivative

*Image: Pompeiu derivative and integral.svg.* A graph of a function (top) whose derivative is a Pompeiu derivative (bottom).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pompeiu derivative"}

In [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), a **Pompeiu derivative** is a [real-valued function](wikipedia://en/22284647) of one real variable that is the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of an everywhere [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) function and that vanishes in a [dense set](wikipedia://en/23523594). In particular, a Pompeiu derivative is discontinuous at every point where it is not 0. Whether non-identically zero such functions may exist was a problem that arose in the context of early-1900s research on functional differentiability and [integrability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_function). The question was affirmatively answered by [Dimitrie Pompeiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Pompeiu) by constructing an explicit example; these functions are therefore named after him.

:::

## Pompeiu's construction {#Pompeiu's_construction}

Pompeiu's construction is described here. Let $\sqrt[3]{x}$ denote the real [cube root](wikipedia://en/292506) of the [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) $x$. Let $\{q_j\}_{j \in \mathbb{N}}$ be an [enumeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enumeration_algorithm) of the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) in the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) $[0, 1]$. Let $\{a_j\}_{j \in \mathbb{N}}$ be [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571) with $\sum_j a_j < \infty.$ Define $g\colon [0, 1] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ by

$$g(x): = a_0+\sum_{j=1}^\infty \,a_j \sqrt[3]{x-q_j}.$$

For each $x$ in $[0, 1]$, each term of the series is less than or equal to $a_{j}$ in [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991), so the series [uniformly converges](wikipedia://en/50652) to a continuous, [strictly increasing](wikipedia://en/48260) function $g(x)$, by the [Weierstrass $M$-test](wikipedia://en/444456). Moreover, it turns out that the function $g$ is differentiable, with

$$g'(x) := \frac{1}{3} \sum_{j=1}^\infty \frac{a_j}{\sqrt[3]{(x-q_j)^2}}>0,$$

at every point where the sum is finite; also, at all other points, in particular, at each of the $q_{j}$, one has $g'(x) := +\infty$. Since the [image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_%28function%29) of $g$ is a [closed bounded interval](wikipedia://en/49172#Definitions) with left endpoint

$$g(0) = a_0-\sum_{j=1}^\infty \,a_j \sqrt[3]{q_j},$$

up to the choice of $a_0$, we can assume $g(0)=0$ and up to the choice of a multiplicative factor we can assume that $g$ maps the interval $[0, 1]$ [onto](wikipedia://en/27873) itself. Since $g$ is strictly increasing it is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective), and hence a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660); and by the theorem of differentiation of the [inverse function](wikipedia://en/14907), its inverse $f := g^{-1}$ has a finite derivative at every point, which vanishes at least at the points $\{g(q_j)\}_{j \in \mathbb{N}}.$ These form a dense subset of $[0, 1]$ (actually, it vanishes in many other points; see below).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- It is known that the zero-set of a derivative of any everywhere differentiable function (and more generally, of any [Baire class one](wikipedia://en/3575729#Baire_class_1) function) is a [$G_{\delta}$ subset](wikipedia://en/252076) of the real line. By definition, for any Pompeiu function, this set is a *dense* $G_{\delta}$ set; therefore it is a [residual set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residual_set). In particular, it possesses [uncountably](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountably) many points.
- A [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) $af(x) + bg(x)$ of Pompeiu functions is a derivative, and vanishes on the set $\{ f = 0\} \cap \{g = 0\}$, which is a dense $G_{\delta}$ set by the [Baire category theorem](wikipedia://en/55227). Thus, Pompeiu functions form a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of functions.
- A limit function of a [uniformly convergent](wikipedia://en/50652) [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of Pompeiu derivatives is a Pompeiu derivative. Indeed, it is a derivative, due to the theorem of limit under the sign of derivative. Moreover, it vanishes in the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of the zero sets of the functions of the sequence: since these are dense $G_{\delta}$ sets, the zero set of the limit function is also dense.
- As a consequence, the [class](wikipedia://en/20696) $E$ of all [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) Pompeiu derivatives on an interval $[a, b]$ is a *closed* [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) of all bounded functions under the uniform distance (hence, it is a Banach space).
- Pompeiu's above construction of a *positive* function is a rather peculiar example of a Pompeiu's function: a theorem of Weil states that *generically* a Pompeiu derivative assumes both positive and negative values in dense sets, in the precise meaning that such functions constitute a residual set of the Banach space $E$.

:::
