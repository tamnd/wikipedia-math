# Barnes integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Barnes integral"}

In mathematics, a **Barnes integral** or **[Mellin](wikipedia://en/1948218)–Barnes integral** is a [contour integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_integral) involving a product of [gamma functions](wikipedia://en/12316). They were introduced by (txt). They are closely related to [generalized hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_hypergeometric_series).

:::

The integral is usually taken along a contour which is a deformation of the imaginary axis passing to the right of all poles of factors of the form Γ(*a* + *s*) and to the left of all poles of factors of the form Γ(*a* − *s*).

## Hypergeometric series {#Hypergeometric_series}

The [hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/2055223) is given as a Barnes integral (Barnes 1908) by

$${}_2F_1(a,b;c;z) =\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)} \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty} \frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)\Gamma(-s)}{\Gamma(c+s)}(-z)^s\,ds,$$

see also (Andrews Askey Roy 1999, Theorem 2.4.1). This equality can be obtained by moving the contour to the right while picking up the [residues](wikipedia://en/73102) at *s* = 0, 1, 2, ... . for $z\ll 1$, and by analytic continuation elsewhere. Given proper convergence conditions, one can relate more general Barnes' integrals and [generalized hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/361184) <sub>*p*</sub>*F*<sub>*q*</sub> in a similar way (Slater 1966).

## Barnes lemmas {#Barnes_lemmas}

The first Barnes lemma (Barnes 1908) states

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty} \Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)\Gamma(c-s)\Gamma(d-s)ds =\frac{\Gamma(a+c)\Gamma(a+d)\Gamma(b+c)\Gamma(b+d)}{\Gamma(a+b+c+d)}.$$

This is an analogue of [Gauss's <sub>2</sub>*F*<sub>1</sub> summation formula](wikipedia://en/2055223#Special_values_at_z_=_1), and also an extension of [Euler's beta integral](wikipedia://en/245658). The integral in it is sometimes called **Barnes's beta integral**.

The second Barnes lemma (Barnes 1910) states

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty} \frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)\Gamma(c+s)\Gamma(1-d-s)\Gamma(-s)}{\Gamma(e+s)}ds$$

$$=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c)\Gamma(1-d+a)\Gamma(1-d+b)\Gamma(1-d+c)}{\Gamma(e-a)\Gamma(e-b)\Gamma(e-c)}$$

where *e* = *a* + *b* + *c* − *d* + 1. This is an analogue of [Saalschütz's summation formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saalschütz's_summation_formula).

:::

## q-Barnes integrals {#q-Barnes_integrals}

There are analogues of Barnes integrals for [basic hypergeometric series](wikipedia://en/2233526), and many of the other results can also be extended to this case (Gasper Rahman 2004, chapter 4).
