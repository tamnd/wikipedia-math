# Jackson's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jackson's inequality"}

In [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), **Jackson's inequality** is an inequality bounding the value of function's best approximation by [algebraic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomials) or [trigonometric polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_polynomials) in terms of the [modulus of continuity](wikipedia://en/964161) or [modulus of smoothness](wikipedia://en/44605337) of the function or of its derivatives.[^o2] Informally speaking, the smoother the function is, the better it can be approximated by polynomials.

:::

## Statement: trigonometric polynomials {#Statement:_trigonometric_polynomials}

For trigonometric polynomials, the following was proved by [Dunham Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunham_Jackson):

**Theorem 1**: If $f:[0,2\pi]\to \mathbb{C}$ is an $r$ times differentiable [periodic function](wikipedia://en/76848) such that

$$\left |f^{(r)}(x) \right | \leq 1, \qquad x\in[0,2\pi],$$

then, for every positive integer $n$, there exists a [trigonometric polynomial](wikipedia://en/591568) $T_{n-1}$ of degree at most $n-1$ such that

$$\left |f(x) - T_{n-1}(x) \right | \leq \frac{C(r)}{n^r}, \qquad x\in[0,2\pi],$$

where $C(r)$ depends only on $r$.

The **[Akhiezer](wikipedia://en/9119648)–[Krein](wikipedia://en/1190470)–[Favard](wikipedia://en/9995378) theorem** gives the sharp value of $C(r)$ (called the [Akhiezer–Krein–Favard constant](wikipedia://en/8531319)):

$$C(r) = \frac{4}{\pi} \sum_{k=0}^\infty \left[\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}\right]^{r+1}~.$$

Jackson also proved the following generalisation of Theorem 1:

**Theorem 2**: One can find a [trigonometric polynomial](wikipedia://en/591568) $T_n$ of degree $\le n$ such that

$$|f(x) - T_n(x)| \leq \frac{C(r) \omega \left (\frac{1}{n}, f^{(r)} \right )}{n^r}, \qquad x\in[0,2\pi],$$

where $\omega(\delta, g)$ denotes the [modulus of continuity](wikipedia://en/964161) of function $g$ with the step $\delta.$

An even more general result of four authors can be formulated as the following Jackson theorem.

**Theorem 3**: For every natural number $n$, if $f$ is $2\pi$-periodic continuous function, there exists a [trigonometric polynomial](wikipedia://en/591568) $T_n$ of degree $\le n$ such that

$$|f(x)-T_n(x)|\leq c(k)\omega_k\left(\tfrac{1}{n},f\right),\qquad x\in[0,2\pi],$$

where constant $c(k)$ depends on $k\in\mathbb{N},$ and $\omega_k$ is the $k$-th order [modulus of smoothness](wikipedia://en/44605337).

For $k=1$ this result was proved by Dunham Jackson. [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477) proved the inequality in the case when $k=2, \omega_2(t,f)\le ct, t>0$ in 1945. [Naum Akhiezer](wikipedia://en/9119648) proved the theorem in the case $k=2$ in 1956. For $k>2$ this result was established by [Sergey Stechkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Stechkin) in 1967.

## Further remarks {#Further_remarks}

Generalisations and extensions are called Jackson-type theorems. A converse to Jackson's inequality is given by [Bernstein's theorem](wikipedia://en/32140327). See also [constructive function theory](wikipedia://en/32135416).
