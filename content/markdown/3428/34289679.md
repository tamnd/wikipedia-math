# Smooth completion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smooth completion"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **smooth completion** (or **smooth compactification**) of a [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) [affine algebraic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_curve) *X* is a [complete](wikipedia://en/1047627) smooth [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) which contains *X* as an open subset.[^o2] Smooth completions exist and are unique over a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088).

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

An affine form of a [hyperelliptic curve](wikipedia://en/499361) may be presented as $y^2=P(x)$ where $(x, y)\in\mathbb{C}^2$ and $P$($x$) [has distinct roots](wikipedia://en/362400) and has degree at least 5. The Zariski closure of the affine curve in $\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2$ is singular at the unique [infinite](wikipedia://en/403139#Projective_plane) point added. Nonetheless, the affine curve can be embedded in a unique compact [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) called its smooth completion. The projection of the Riemann surface to $\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^1$ is 2-to-1 over the singular point at infinity if $P(x)$ has even degree, and 1-to-1 (but ramified) otherwise.

This smooth completion can also be obtained as follows. Project the affine curve to the affine line using the *x*-coordinate. Embed the affine line into the projective line, then take the normalization of the projective line in the function field of the affine curve.

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

A smooth connected curve over an algebraically closed field is called **hyperbolic** if $2g-2+r>0$ where *g* is the genus of the smooth completion and *r* is the number of added points.

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of *X* is free with $2g+r-1$ generators if *r*>0.

(Analogue of [Dirichlet's unit theorem](wikipedia://en/460227)) Let *X* be a smooth connected curve over a finite field. Then the units of the ring of regular functions *O(X)* on *X* is a finitely generated abelian group of rank *r* -1.

:::

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Suppose the base field is perfect. Any affine curve *X* is isomorphic to an open subset of an integral projective (hence complete) curve. Taking the normalization (or [blowing up](wikipedia://en/1817790) the singularities) of the projective curve then gives a smooth completion of *X*. Their points correspond to the [discrete valuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_valuation) of the [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) that are trivial on the base field.

By construction, the smooth completion is a [projective](wikipedia://en/320469) curve which contains the given curve as an everywhere dense open subset, and the added new points are smooth. Such a (projective) completion always exists and is unique.

If the base field is not perfect, a smooth completion of a smooth affine curve doesn't always exist. But the above process always produces a [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#Properties_of_schemes) completion if we start with a regular affine curve (smooth varieties are regular, and the converse is true over perfect fields). A regular completion is unique and, by the [valuative criterion of properness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuative_criterion_of_properness#Valuative_criterion_of_properness), any morphism from the affine curve to a complete algebraic variety extends uniquely to the regular completion.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

If *X* is a [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#Separated_and_proper_morphisms) algebraic variety, a [theorem of Nagata](wikipedia://en/37613671)[^o11] says that *X* can be embedded as an open subset of a complete algebraic variety. If *X* is moreover smooth and the base field has characteristic 0, then by [Hironaka's theorem](wikipedia://en/9914115#Resolution_of_singularities_in_higher_dimensions) *X* can even be embedded as an open subset of a complete smooth algebraic variety, with boundary a normal crossing divisor. If *X* is quasi-projective, the smooth completion can be chosen to be projective.

However, contrary to the one-dimensional case, there is no uniqueness of the smooth completion, nor is it canonical.

:::
