# Michelangelo Ricci

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michelangelo Ricci"}

**Michelangelo Ricci** (30 January 1619 – 12 May 1682) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a [Cardinal of the Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_of_the_Roman_Catholic_Church).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Michelangelo Ricci was born on 30 January 1619 in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), then capital of the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States), to a family of low social standing that originated in Bergamo.

He studied [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) and [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law) in Rome, where he was a contemporary of [René-François de Sluse](wikipedia://en/2680203). He also studied mathematics under [Benedetto Castelli](wikipedia://en/855653) who himself had been a student of [Galileo Galilei](wikipedia://en/29688374). He was a friend of [Evangelista Torricelli](wikipedia://en/210413), kept close links with contemporary scientific culture, and played an important role in the development of the [Galilean](wikipedia://en/29688374) school.

Like de Sluze, he spent his entire career in the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) and served the [pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope) in various roles on several occasions. A trained theologian, he acted as consultant to various Congregations of the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia). Having suffered from [epilepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epilepsy) since his birth, he was (according to [canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law) of the time) disqualified from [ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordination). Nonetheless, he was created a [Cardinal-Deacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal-Deacon) in the [Consistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_consistory) of 1 September 1681 by Pope [Innocent XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innocent_XI), with the title 'Cardinal-Deacon of [Santa Maria in Aquiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Aquiro)'. His position in the church was very useful for protecting his friends and fellow scientists in their controversies with the opposing [scholastic school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism).

He played a significant part in the theoretical debates and experiments that led up to Torricelli's discovery of [atmospheric pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_pressure) and invention of the mercury [barometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barometer). In particular he followed the experiments in this field by [Gasparo Berti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasparo_Berti), in Rome.

There is an unpublished manuscript by Ricci, devoted to [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), in the library of the Mathematical Institute of [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa). It shows that by 1640 he was familiar with the '[New Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Algebra)' of [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238). In this book he provides a critique of the solutions given by the geometer [Marino Ghetaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marino_Ghetaldi) of [Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa) in his *De Resolutione et Compositione Matematica* to the problems posed by [Apollonius of Perga](wikipedia://en/257242).[^o4]

His published mathematical work is summarised in a treatise of nineteen pages, *Exercitatio geometrica, de maximis et minimis* (1666) in which he studies the maxima of functions of the form $x^m(a - x)^n$ and [tangents](wikipedia://en/31482) to curves with equation $y^m = kx^n$, using methods that are an early form of [induction](wikipedia://en/18881). This treatise was much admired by his contemporaries and was republished as an appendix to [Mercator](wikipedia://en/1486120)'s 'Logarithmo-Technia' (1688). He also studied [spirals](wikipedia://en/61563) (1644) and [cycloids](wikipedia://en/41638) (1674) and recognised that the study of tangents and the calculation of areas are reciprocal operations.

Ricci is also known for his correspondence with Torricelli, [Vincenzo Viviani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Viviani), René de Sluze and Cardinal [Leopoldo de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_de'_Medici), founder of the [Accademia del Cimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_del_Cimento). These letters give his thoughts on [paraboloids](wikipedia://en/145845) and [hyperboloids](wikipedia://en/145844) when cut by parallel planes, on the surface of a ring, and on the properties of the vacuum. It was Ricci who welcomed [Marin Mersenne](wikipedia://en/72947), when he came to Italy to present the work of [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525). In [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics) he studied the magnifying effect of [lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_%28optics%29). With [Giovanni Battista Baliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Baliani) he discussed the Galilean revolution. Ricci was also a close associate of [Antonio Nardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Nardi), another mathematician in Rome. He collaborated with Nardi on his [*Scene*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Nardi#The_Scene), an unpublished manuscript that circulated among Torricelli and others, in which Ricci included some of the material that would later appear in his 1666 *Geometrica Exercitatio.*

Following the death of Torricelli and the disappearance of [Bonaventura Cavalieri](wikipedia://en/412561), he was requested to collate and publish his correspondence with these two men. However he declined the invitation, leading to Torricelli's work being forgotten for some time.

He endeavoured to defend [Francesco Redi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Redi) against Cardinal [Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_de'_Medici) when Redi published his *Esperienze Intorno alla Generazione degl'Insetti*, arguing against the [spontaneous generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_generation) of [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insects).

Ricci was also the backer of Abbot [Francesco Nazzari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Nazzari) in the publication of the first review of Italian literature, the *[Giornale de' Letterati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giornale_de'_Letterati)* (1668-1683).

He died in Rome, age 63, on 12 May 1682.
