# Orthocentric tetrahedron

*Image: Tetraortho.gif.* The four altitudes of an orthogonal tetrahedron meet at its *orthocenter*. Edges AB, BC, CA are perpendicular to, respectively, edges CD, AD, BD.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthocentric tetrahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **orthocentric tetrahedron** is a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) where all three pairs of opposite [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944). It can also be known as the **perpendicular tetrahedron**. It is also known as an **orthogonal tetrahedron** since [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) means perpendicular. It was first studied by [Simon Lhuilier](wikipedia://en/27357356) in 1782, and got the name orthocentric tetrahedron by [G. de Longchamps](wikipedia://en/36941284) in 1890.[^o2]

:::

In an orthocentric tetrahedron the four [altitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_%28geometry%29) are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157). This common point is called the **tetrahedron orthocenter** (a generalization of the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of a triangle). It has the property that it is the symmetric point of the center of the [circumscribed sphere](wikipedia://en/991784) with respect to the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926).[^o2] Hence the orthocenter coincides with the [Monge point](wikipedia://en/30606#Properties_analogous_to_those_of_a_triangle) of the tetrahedron.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterizations"}

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

All tetrahedra can be inscribed in a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975). A tetrahedron is orthocentric [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its circumscribed parallelepiped is a [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406). Indeed, in any tetrahedron, a pair of opposite edges is perpendicular if and only if the corresponding faces of the circumscribed parallelepiped are rhombi. If four faces of a parallelepiped are rhombi, then all edges have equal lengths and all six faces are rhombi; it follows that if two pairs of opposite edges in a tetrahedron are perpendicular, then so is the third pair, and the tetrahedron is orthocentric.[^o2]

A tetrahedron $ABCD$ is orthocentric if and only if the sum of the squares of opposite edges is the same for the three pairs of opposite edges:[^o5][^o6]

$$\overline{AB}^2 + \overline{CD}^2 = \overline{AC}^2 + \overline{BD}^2 = \overline{AD}^2 + \overline{BC}^2.$$

Another [necessary and sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_and_sufficient_condition) for a tetrahedron to be orthocentric is that its three [bimedians](wikipedia://en/30606#Properties_analogous_to_those_of_a_triangle) have equal length.[^o6]

:::

## Volume {#Volume}

The characterization regarding the edges implies that if only four of the six edges of an orthocentric tetrahedron are known, the remaining two can be calculated as long as they are not opposite to each other. Therefore the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of an orthocentric tetrahedron can be expressed in terms of four edges *a*, *b*, *c*, *d*. The formula is[^o8]

$$V=\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{4(c^2+d^2)s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)-a^2b^2c^2}$$

where *c* and *d* are opposite edges, and $s=\tfrac{1}{2}(a+b+c)$.
