# Affine manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine manifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), an **affine manifold** is a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) equipped with a [flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_connection), [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/4342484) [connection](wikipedia://en/607690).

:::

Equivalently, it is a manifold that is (if connected) [covered](wikipedia://en/250323) by an open subset of ${\mathbb R}^n$, with [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) acting by [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449). This equivalence is an easy corollary of [Cartan–Ambrose–Hicks theorem](wikipedia://en/60439463).

Equivalently, it is a manifold equipped with an atlas—called **the affine structure**—such that all transition functions between [charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chart_%28topology%29) are [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449) (that is, have constant Jacobian matrix);[^n1] two atlases are equivalent if the manifold admits an atlas subjugated to both, with transitions from both atlases to a smaller atlas being affine. A manifold having a distinguished affine structure is called an **affine manifold** and the charts which are affinely related to those of the affine structure are called **affine charts**. In each affine coordinate domain the coordinate [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) form a [parallelisation](wikipedia://en/33569276) of that domain, so there is an associated connection on each domain. These locally defined connections are the same on overlapping parts, so there is a unique connection associated with an affine structure. Note there is a link between [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear) [connection](wikipedia://en/308815) (also called [affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690)) and a [web](wikipedia://en/34816085).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

An **affine manifold** $M\,$ is a real [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with charts $\psi_i\colon U_i\to{\mathbb R}^n$ such that $\psi_i\circ\psi_j^{-1}\in \operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb R}^n)$ for all $i, j\, ,$ where $\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb R}^n)$ denotes the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449). In fancier words it is a [(G,X)-manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28G,X%29-manifold) where $X=\mathbb R^n$ and $G$ is the group of affine transformations.

An affine manifold is called **complete** if its [universal covering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_covering) is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to ${\mathbb R}^n$.

In the case of a compact affine manifold $M$, let $G$ be the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of $M$ and $\widetilde M$ be its [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover). One can show that each $n$-dimensional affine manifold comes with a developing map $D\colon {\widetilde M}\to{\mathbb R}^n$, and a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) $\varphi\colon G\to \operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb R}^n)$, such that $D$ is an [immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953) and equivariant with respect to $\varphi$.

A [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a compact complete flat affine manifold is called **an affine [crystallographic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic_group)**. Classification of affine crystallographic groups is a difficult problem, far from being solved. The [Riemannian crystallographic groups](wikipedia://en/463721) (also known as [Bieberbach groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_group)) were classified by [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186), answering a question posed by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). In his work on [Hilbert's 18-th problem](wikipedia://en/2336224), [Bieberbach proved](wikipedia://en/463721#Bieberbach's_theorems) that any Riemannian crystallographic group contains an abelian subgroup of finite index.

### Complex affine manifolds {#Complex_affine_manifolds}

An *affine complex manifold* is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) that has an atlas whose [transition maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_map) belong to the group of complex [affine transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_transformations), that is, have the form $z \mapsto A \cdot z + c$ where $n$ is the (complex) dimension of the manifold, $c\in \mathbb C^n,$ and $A$ is an invertible $n \times n$ matrix with complex entries.[^o6] In other words, it is a $(G, X)$-manifold where $X=\mathbb{C}^n$ and $G$ is the group of complex affine transformations of $\mathbb{C}^n.$

## Important longstanding conjectures {#Important_longstanding_conjectures}

Geometry of affine manifolds is essentially a network of longstanding conjectures; most of them proven in low dimension and some other special cases.

The most important of them are:

- Markus conjecture (1962) stating that a compact affine manifold is complete [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it has parallel volume. Known in dimension 2.
- Auslander conjecture (1964)[^o9][^o10] stating that any affine crystallographic group contains a [polycyclic subgroup](wikipedia://en/4000598) of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581). Known in dimensions up to 6,[^o11] and when the [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) of the flat connection preserves a [Lorentz metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_metric).[^o12] Since every virtually polycyclic crystallographic group preserves a [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811), Auslander conjecture implies the "only if" part of the Markus conjecture.[^o13]
- [Chern conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_conjecture_%28affine_geometry%29) (1955) The [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) of an affine manifold vanishes.[^o14]
