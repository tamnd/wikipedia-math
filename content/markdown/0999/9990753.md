# Edward Collingwood

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Collingwood"}

**Sir Edward Foyle Collingwood** [LLD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legum_Doctor) (17 January 1900 – 25 October 1970) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist). He was a member of the [Eglingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglingham) branch of a prominent [Northumbrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Northumbria) family, the son of Col. Cuthbert Collingwood of the [Lancashire Fusiliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire_Fusiliers), whose family seat was at [Lilburn Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilburn_Tower), near [Wooler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooler), [Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland). His great grandfather was a brother of [Admiral Lord Collingwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuthbert_Collingwood,_1st_Baron_Collingwood).[^o6]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Collingwood was born at his family home, Lilburn Tower, near [Wooler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooler) in [Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland), the son of Col. Cuthbert George Collingwood and his wife, Dorothy Fawcett.[^o8][^o9]

Collingwood was educated at the [Royal Naval College, Osborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_College,_Osborne), on the [Isle of Wight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Wight) and at [Dartmouth Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_Royal_Naval_College) and was commissioned into the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy). By arrangement his first service was aboard the [dreadnought battleship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreadnought_battleship) [HMS *Collingwood*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Collingwood_%281908%29) but his naval career was cut short during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) when in 1916 he was invalided out of the Navy following an accidental injury.

In 1918 he enrolled to study mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). His early academic results were not special and in 1922 he moved to [Aberystwyth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University) where he became interested in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and published a paper relating to [Nevanlinna's theory](wikipedia://en/391950). He was awarded the [Rayleigh Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) in 1923 and following the award of the [Rouse Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouse_Ball) travelling scholarship in 1925 he spent a year at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris).

Collingwood returned to Cambridge and was in 1929 awarded a doctorate for a thesis entitled *Contributions to the theory of integral functions*.[^o4] Collingwood left Cambridge in 1937 when he was appointed [High Sheriff of Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Sheriff_of_Northumberland) for that year. He was later appointed [Deputy Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Lieutenant) of his home county.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he served in the [RNVR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Volunteer_Reserve) with the rank of Captain and was employed as a naval scientist. In 1945 he was appointed Chief Scientist in the Mine Design department of the Admiralty. For his service he was awarded the [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBE).

Collingwood returned to mathematics after the war and continued his interest in [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) and in 1949 published his research on the theory of cluster sets.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Collingwood was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1954 and of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) of London in 1965. He held several professional and civic appointments including President of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) 1969–70, Chairman of the Council of [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University) from 1953, Chairman of the Newcastle Hospital Board 1953–1968, Vice President of the International Hospital Federation 1959-1967 and Treasurer of the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28UK%29).

He was knighted in 1962.[^o5]

## Family {#Family}

Collingwood never married.[^o12]
