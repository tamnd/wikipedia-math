# Poloidal–toroidal decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poloidal–toroidal decomposition"}

In [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640), a topic in pure and applied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **poloidal–toroidal decomposition** is a restricted form of the [Helmholtz decomposition](wikipedia://en/820253). It is often used in the [spherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinates) analysis of [solenoidal vector fields](wikipedia://en/419444), for example, [magnetic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_fields) and [incompressible fluids](wikipedia://en/461477).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a three-dimensional [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) **F** with zero [divergence](wikipedia://en/8328)

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 0,$$

this $\mathbf{F}$ can be expressed as the sum of a toroidal field $\mathbf{T}$ and poloidal vector field $\mathbf{P}$

$$\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{T} + \mathbf{P}$$

where $\mathbf{r}$ is a radial vector in [spherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinates) $(r,\theta,\phi)$. The toroidal field is obtained from a [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879),$\Psi(r,\theta,\phi)$,[^n1] as the following [curl](wikipedia://en/6123),

$$\mathbf{T} = \nabla \times (\mathbf{r} \Psi(\mathbf{r}))$$

and the poloidal field is derived from another scalar field $\Phi(r,\theta, \phi)$,[^n2] as a twice-iterated curl,

$$\mathbf{P} = \nabla \times (\nabla \times (\mathbf{r} \Phi (\mathbf{r})))\,.$$

This [decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_decomposition) is symmetric in that the curl of a toroidal field is poloidal, and the curl of a poloidal field is toroidal, known as [Chandrasekhar–Kendall function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrasekhar–Kendall_function).[^n3]

:::

## Geometry {#Geometry}

A toroidal vector field is tangential to spheres around the origin,[^n3]

$$\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{T} = 0$$

while the curl of a poloidal field is tangential to those spheres

$\mathbf{r} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{P}) = 0.$[^n4]

The poloidal–toroidal decomposition is unique if it is required that the average of the scalar fields Ψ and Φ vanishes on every sphere of radius *r*.[^n2]

## Cartesian decomposition {#Cartesian_decomposition}

A poloidal–toroidal decomposition also exists in [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates), but a mean-field flow has to be included in this case. For example, every solenoidal vector field can be written as

$$\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \nabla \times g(x,y,z) \hat{\mathbf{z}} + \nabla \times (\nabla \times h(x,y,z) \hat{\mathbf{z}}) + b_x(z) \hat{\mathbf{x}} + b_y(z)\hat{\mathbf{y}},$$

where $\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}, \hat{\mathbf{z}}$ denote the unit vectors in the coordinate directions.[^n5]
