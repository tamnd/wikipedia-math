# Trace identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trace identity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **trace identity** is any [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) involving the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Trace identities are invariant under simultaneous [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_theory%29).

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

They are frequently used in the [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) of $n \times n$ matrices to find the [generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generating_set) and [relations](wikipedia://en/22517558) of the [ring of invariants](wikipedia://en/363762), and therefore are useful in answering questions similar to that posed by [Hilbert's fourteenth problem](wikipedia://en/1695231).

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [Cayley–Hamilton theorem](wikipedia://en/173547) says that every square matrix satisfies its own [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268). This also implies that all square matrices satisfy $$\operatorname{tr}\left(A^n\right) - c_{n-1} \operatorname{tr}\left(A^{n - 1}\right) + \cdots + (-1)^n n \det(A) = 0\,$$ where the coefficients $c_i$ are given by the [elementary symmetric polynomials](wikipedia://en/1440695) of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of $A$.
- All [square matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_matrices) satisfy $$\operatorname{tr}(A) = \operatorname{tr}\left(A^\mathsf{T}\right).\,$$

:::
