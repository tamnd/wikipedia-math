# Line of sight

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Line of sight"}

The **line of sight**, also known as **visual axis** or **sightline** (also **sight line**), is an [imaginary line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_line) between a viewer/[observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation)/spectator's [eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye)(s) and a subject of interest, or their [relative direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_direction_%28geometry%29).[^o2] The subject may be any definable object taken note of or to be taken note of by the observer, at any distance more than least distance of distinct vision. In [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics), refraction of a ray due to use of [lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenses) can cause [distortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distortion_%28optics%29).[^o3] Shadows, patterns and movement can also influence line of sight interpretation[^o4][^o5] (as in [optical illusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_illusions)).

:::

The term "line" typically presumes that the [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light) by which the observed object is seen travels as a straight [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28optics%29), which is sometimes not the case as light can take a curved/angulated path when [reflected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28physics%29) from a [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror),[^o6] [refracted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refract) by a [lens](wikipedia://en/18320) or [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) changes in the [traversed media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_medium), or [deflected by a gravitational field](wikipedia://en/48824). Fields of study feature specific targets, such as vessels in navigation, marker flags or natural features in surveying, celestial objects in astronomy, and so on. To have optimal observational outcome, it is preferable to have a completely unobstructed sightline.

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

- [Sightline (architecture)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sightline_%28architecture%29)
- [Line-of-sight fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-of-sight_fire), shooting directly at a visible target on a relatively flat trajectory. The target is in the *line of sight* of the [sighting device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sighting_device) and the [rifleman's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifleman's_rule) applies.
- [Line-of-sight range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-of-sight_range)
- [Line-of-sight (missile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-of-sight_%28missile%29), the straight line between the missile and the target
- [Radar horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_horizon)
- [Line-of-sight propagation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-of-sight_propagation), electro-magnetic waves travelling in a straight line
  - [Non-line-of-sight propagation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-line-of-sight_propagation)
- [Line-of-sight velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-of-sight_velocity), an object's speed straight towards or away from an observer
- Line-of-sight [double star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_star), one in which two stars are only coincidentally close together as seen from Earth
- [Beyond visual line of sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_visual_line_of_sight)
