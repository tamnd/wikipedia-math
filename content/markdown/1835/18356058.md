# Cryptography Research

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cryptography Research"}

**Cryptography Research, Inc.** was a [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco) based cryptography company specializing in applied cryptographic engineering, including technologies for building tamper-resistant [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor). It was purchased on June 6, 2011, by [Rambus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambus) for \$342.5M and remained active as a [subsidiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary) until 2015.[^o3][^o4] The company licensed patents for protecting cryptographic devices against [power analysis](wikipedia://en/754362) attacks.[^o5] The company's CryptoFirewall-brand [ASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASIC) cores were used in pay [TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV) [conditional access](wikipedia://en/2183934) systems and anti-counterfeiting applications.[^o6] CRI also developed [BD+](wikipedia://en/11070229), a security component in the Blu-ray disc format, and played a role in the [format war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_optical_disc_format_war) between [HD DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_DVD) and [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray).[^o7][^o8] The company's services group assisted with security testing, disaster recovery, and training.

:::

Cryptography Research protected its core operations from outside attack by maintaining a secured local network that is not connected to the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) at all. Employees who needed to work with sensitive data had two computers on their desks — one to access the secure network, and a separate computer to access the Internet.[^o9]
