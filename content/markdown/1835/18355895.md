# Davenport–Schmidt theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Davenport–Schmidt theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically the area of [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), the **Davenport–Schmidt theorem** tells us how well a certain kind of [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) can be approximated by another kind. Specifically it tells us that we can get a good approximation to irrational numbers that are not quadratic by using either [quadratic irrationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_irrational) or simply [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). It is named after [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111) and [Wolfgang M. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_M._Schmidt).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Given a number α which is either rational or a quadratic irrational, we can find unique integers *x*, *y*, and *z* such that *x*, *y*, and *z* are not all zero, the first non-zero one among them is positive, they are relatively prime, and we have

$$x\alpha^2 +y\alpha +z=0.$$

If α is a quadratic irrational we can take *x*, *y*, and *z* to be the coefficients of its [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106). If α is rational we will have *x* = 0. With these integers uniquely determined for each such α we can define the *height* of α to be

$$H(\alpha)=\max\{|x|,|y|,|z|\}.$$

The theorem then says that for any real number ξ which is neither rational nor a quadratic irrational, we can find infinitely many real numbers α which *are* rational or quadratic irrationals and which satisfy

$$|\xi-\alpha|<CH(\alpha)^{-3}\max(1,\xi^2),$$

where *C* is any real number satisfying *C* > 160/9.[^o4]

While the theorem is related to [Roth's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thue–Siegel–Roth_theorem), its real use lies in the fact that it is [effective](wikipedia://en/250237), in the sense that the constant *C* can be worked out for any given ξ.

:::
