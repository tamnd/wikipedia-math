# Donald Bentley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donald Bentley"}

**Donald Lyon Bentley** is an American statistician and mathematician.[^o6] A doctoral student of biostatistician [Rupert Griel Miller](wikipedia://en/73622334) at the [Stanford University School of Humanities and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University_School_of_Humanities_and_Sciences),[^o3] Bentley graduated with a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Mathematics) and [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in 1962.[^o3][^o4] He then taught at the Mathematics and Statistics Department of [Pomona College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona_College) in [Claremont, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claremont,_California) from 1964 to 2001,[^o9] becoming Lingurn H. Burkhead Professor of Mathematics,[^o5] an [endowed chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endowed_chair), before retiring to become a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus).[^o6] He was also president of the Southern California Chapter of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) from 1987 to 1988,[^o7] and was named a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1990.[^o8]

:::

Bentley is known locally for his role in creating [Pomona College's tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditions_of_Pomona_College) of [revering the number 47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona_College#Forty-seven_reverence).[^o5][^o9] It began in the summer of 1964,[^o10] when two students, Laurie Mets and Bruce Elgin, conducted a research project seeking to find out whether the number occurs more often in nature than would be expected by chance. They documented various 47 sightings, and Bentley produced a [false mathematical proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_proof) that 47 was equal to all other integers. The number became a [meme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meme) among the class, which spread once the academic year began and snowballed over time.[^o11] Many Pomona alumni have since deliberately inserted 47 references into their work.[^o12] In the early 2010s, the college's clock tower would chime on the 47th minute of the hour.[^o13]
