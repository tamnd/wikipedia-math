# Pentagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pentagonal tiling"}

*Image: P5-type15-chiral coloring.png.* The 15th monohedral convex pentagonal type, discovered in 2015

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **pentagonal tiling** is a [tiling of the plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_of_the_plane) where each individual piece is in the shape of a [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014).

:::

A [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tiling) [pentagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagonal) tiling on the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) is impossible because the [internal angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) of a [regular pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014#Regular_pentagons), 108°, is not a divisor of 360°, the angle measure of a whole [turn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28geometry%29). However, regular pentagons can tile the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane) with [four pentagons around each vertex](wikipedia://en/9421111) ([or more](wikipedia://en/38358615)) and [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) with [three pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_dodecahedron); the latter produces a tiling that is topologically equivalent to the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361).[^o2]

## Monohedral convex pentagonal tilings {#Monohedral_convex_pentagonal_tilings}

*Image: Monohedral\_pentagonal\_tiling\_labels.png.* An example pentagonal tile with angle labels $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$, and edge length labels $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $e$

Fifteen types of convex pentagons are known to tile the plane [monohedrally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monohedral_tiling) (i.e., with one type of tile).[^n1] The most recent one was discovered in 2015. This list has been shown to be complete by (Rao 2017), result subject to [peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review).[^n2] (Bagina 2011) showed that there are only eight [edge-to-edge](wikipedia://en/321671#Introduction_to_tessellations) convex types, a result obtained independently by (Sugimoto 2012).[^o9]

[Michaël Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michaël_Rao) of the *[École normale supérieure de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_Lyon)* claimed in May 2017 to have found the proof that there are, in fact, no convex pentagons that tile beyond these 15 types.[^n2] As of 11 July 2017, the first half of Rao's proof had been independently verified (computer code available[^o11]) by Thomas Hales, a professor of mathematics at the University of Pittsburgh.[^n3] As of December 2017, the proof was not yet fully peer reviewed.

Each enumerated tiling family contains pentagons that belong to no other type; however, some individual pentagons may belong to multiple types. In addition, some of the pentagons in the known tiling types also permit alternative tiling patterns beyond the standard tiling exhibited by all members of its type.

The sides of length $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $e$ are directly clockwise from the angles at vertices $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$ respectively. (Thus, $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$ are opposite to $d$, $e$, $a$, $b$, $c$ respectively.)

Many of these monohedral tile types have degrees of freedom. These freedoms include variations of [internal angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) and edge lengths. In the limit, edges may have lengths that approach zero or angles that approach 180°. Types 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 13 allow parametric possibilities with nonconvex prototiles.

Periodic tilings are characterised by their [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214) symmetry, for example [p2 (2222)](wikipedia://en/386214#Group_p2_%282222%29) is defined by four 2-fold gyration points. This nomenclature is used in the diagrams below, where the tiles are also colored by their [k-isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) positions within the symmetry.

A '[primitive unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_unit)' is a section of the tiling that generates the whole tiling using only translations, and is as small as possible.

### Reinhardt (1918) {#Reinhardt_(1918)}

(Reinhardt 1918) found the first five types of pentagonal tile. All five can create [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) tilings, meaning that the symmetries of the tiling can take any tile to any other tile (more formally, the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) [acts transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on the tiles).

B. Grünbaum and G. C. Shephard have shown that there are exactly twenty-four distinct "types" of isohedral tilings of the plane by pentagons according to their classification scheme.[^n4] All use Reinhardt's tiles, usually with additional conditions necessary for the tiling. There are two tilings by all type 2 tiles, and one by all of each of the other four types. Fifteen of the other eighteen tilings are by special cases of type 1 tiles. Nine of the twenty-four tilings are edge-to-edge.[^n5]

There are also 2-isohedral tilings by special cases of type 1, type 2, and type 4 tiles, and 3-isohedral tilings, all edge-to-edge, by special cases of type 1 tiles. There is no upper bound on k for k-isohedral tilings by certain tiles that are both type 1 and type 2, and hence neither on the number of tiles in a primitive unit.

The wallpaper group symmetry for each tiling is given, with [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678) in parentheses. A second lower symmetry group is given if tile [chirality](wikipedia://en/1170160) exists, where mirror images are considered distinct. These are shown as yellow and green tiles in those cases.

### Stein (1985) Type 14 {#Stein_(1985)_Type_14}

A 14th convex pentagon type was found by Rolf Stein in 1985.[^n6]

The tiling is 3-isohedral and non-edge-to-edge. It has completely determined tiles, with no degrees of freedom. The exact proportions are specified by

$$\frac{b}{a} = \sqrt{\frac{11 \sqrt{57}-25}{8}}$$

and angle $B$ [obtuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_angle) with

$$\sin B = \frac{\sqrt{57}-3}{8}.$$

Other relations can easily be deduced.

The primitive units contain six tiles respectively. It has p2 (2222) symmetry.

### Mann/McLoud/Von Derau (2015) Type 15 {#Mann/McLoud/Von_Derau_(2015)_Type_15}

### No more periodic pentagonal tiling types {#No_more_periodic_pentagonal_tiling_types}

In July 2017 Michaël Rao completed a computer-assisted proof showing that there are no other types of convex pentagons that can tile the plane. The complete list of convex polygons that can tile the plane includes the above 15 pentagons, [three types of hexagons](wikipedia://en/2863719#Monohedral_convex_hexagonal_tilings), and all quadrilaterals and triangles.[^n3] A consequence of this proof is that no convex polygon exists that tiles the plane only aperiodically, since all of the above types allow for a periodic tiling.

## Dual uniform tilings {#Dual_uniform_tilings}

There are three [isohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral) pentagonal tilings generated as [duals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28polyhedron%29) of the [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635), those with 5-valence vertices. They represent special higher symmetry cases of the 15 monohedral tilings above. Uniform tilings and their duals are all edge-to-edge. These dual tilings are also called [Laves tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laves_tiling). The symmetry of the uniform dual tilings is the same as the uniform tilings. Because the uniform tilings are [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560), the duals are [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120).

## Pentagonal/hexagonal tessellation {#Pentagonal/hexagonal_tessellation}

*Image: Pentagonal Tessellation of Hexagons.png.* Pentagonal subdivisions of a hexagon

Pentagons have a peculiar relationship with hexagons. As demonstrated graphically below, some types of hexagons can be subdivided into pentagons. For example, a regular hexagon bisects into two type 1 pentagons. Subdivision of convex hexagons is also possible with three (type 3), four (type 4) and nine (type 3) pentagons.

## Non-convex pentagons {#Non-convex_pentagons}

*Image: Sphinx tiling pg a.svg.* Periodic tiling by the sphinx

With pentagons that are not required to be [convex](wikipedia://en/660019), additional types of tiling are possible. An example is the [sphinx tiling](wikipedia://en/35188912), a non-periodic formed by a pentagonal [rep-tile](wikipedia://en/33478101).[^n13] The sphinx may also tile the plane periodically, by fitting two sphinx tiles together to form a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) and then tiling the plane by translation of this parallelogram,[^n13] a pattern that can be extended to any non-convex pentagon that has two consecutive angles adding to 2$\pi$.

It is possible to divide an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) into three congruent non-convex pentagons, meeting at the center of the triangle, and to tile the plane with the resulting three-pentagon unit.[^n14] A similar method can be used to subdivide [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) into four congruent non-convex pentagons, or [regular hexagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon) into six congruent non-convex pentagons, and then tile the plane with the resulting unit.

## In non-Euclidean geometry {#In_non-Euclidean_geometry}

### Spherical tiling {#Spherical_tiling}

A [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) can be considered a regular tiling of 12 pentagons on the surface of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {5,3}, having three pentagons around each vertex.

One may also consider a [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) tiling by two [hemispheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemisphere_%28geometry%29), with the [great circle](wikipedia://en/48082) between them subdivided into five equal arcs, as a pentagonal tiling with Schläfli symbol {5,2}.

### Regular hyperbolic tilings {#Regular_hyperbolic_tilings}

In the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane), one can construct regular pentagons that have any [interior angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_angle) $2\pi/n$ for $n\ge 4$. The resulting pentagons tile the plane regularly, with $n$ pentagons around each vertex. For instance, the [order-4 pentagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/9421111), {5,4}, has four right-angled pentagons around each vertex. A limiting case is the [infinite-order pentagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/42309332) {5,∞} produced by ideal regular pentagons. These pentagons have [ideal points](wikipedia://en/4995922) as their vertices, with angle equal to zero.
