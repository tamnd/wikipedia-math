# Ludvig Faddeev

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ludvig Faddeev"}

**Ludvig Dmitrievich Faddeev** (also *Ludwig Dmitriyevich*; Лю́двиг Дми́триевич Фадде́ев; 23 March 1934 – 26 February 2017) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [mathematical physicist](wikipedia://en/173416). He is known for the discovery of the [Faddeev equations](wikipedia://en/2678192) in the quantum-mechanical [three-body problem](wikipedia://en/1208420) and for the development of [path-integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation) methods in the quantization of non-abelian [gauge field theories](wikipedia://en/23912155), including the introduction of the [Faddeev–Popov ghosts](wikipedia://en/671768) (with [Victor Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Popov)). He led the Leningrad School, in which he along with many of his students developed the [quantum inverse scattering method](wikipedia://en/25672752) for studying [quantum integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213#Quantum_integrable_systems) in one space and one time dimension. This work led to the invention of [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) by [Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747) and [Jimbo](wikipedia://en/22363355).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Faddeev was born in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) to a family of mathematicians. His father, [Dmitry Faddeev](wikipedia://en/48446570), was a well-known algebraist, professor of [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_University) and member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences). His mother, [Vera Faddeeva](wikipedia://en/48501513), was known for her work in numerical linear algebra. Faddeev attended [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_University), receiving his undergraduate degree in 1956. He enrolled in physics, rather than mathematics, "to be independent of \[his\] father".[^o4] Nevertheless, he received a solid education in mathematics as well "due to the influence of [V. A. Fock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Fock) and [V. I. Smirnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Ivanovich_Smirnov_%28mathematician%29)".[^o4] His doctoral work on [scattering](wikipedia://en/164483) theory was completed in 1959 under the direction of [Olga Ladyzhenskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Aleksandrovna_Ladyzhenskaya).

From 1976 to 2000, Faddeev was head of the [St. Petersburg Department of Steklov Institute of Mathematics of Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Department_of_Steklov_Institute_of_Mathematics_of_Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) (PDMI RAS).[^o6] He was an invited visitor to the [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN) Theory Division for the first time in 1973 and made several further visits there.[^o7]

In 1988 he founded the [Euler International Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_International_Mathematical_Institute), now a department of PDMI RAS.[^o6][^o8]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Faddeev was a member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) since 1976, and was a member of a number of foreign academies, including the [U. S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Brazilian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o10] the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o11] He received numerous honors including [USSR State Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize) (1971), [Dannie Heineman Prize](wikipedia://en/4854281) (1975),[^o12] [Dirac Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_%28ICTP%29) (1990), an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from the Faculty of Mathematics and Science at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden),[^o13] [Max Planck Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Medal) (1996), [Demidov Prize](wikipedia://en/6905037) (2002 – "For outstanding contribution to the development of mathematics, quantum mechanics, string theory and solitons") and the [State Prize of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Prize_of_the_Russian_Federation) (1995, 2004). He was president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) (1986–1990). He was awarded the [Henri Poincaré Prize](wikipedia://en/4890712)[^o14] in 2006 and the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in mathematical sciences in 2008.[^o15] Also the Karpinsky International Prize and the Max Planck Medal (German Physical Society). He also received the [Lomonosov Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Gold_Medal) in 2013.

Faddeev also received state awards:

- [Order of Merit for the Fatherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_for_the_Fatherland);
  - 3rd class (25 October 2004) – for outstanding contribution to the development of fundamental and applied domestic science and many years of fruitful activity
  - 4th class (4 June 1999) – for outstanding contribution to the development of national science and training of highly qualified personnel in connection with the 275th anniversary of the Russian Academy of Sciences
- [Order of Friendship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Friendship) (6 June 1994) – for his great personal contribution to the development of mathematical physics and training of highly qualified scientific personnel
- [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin)
- [Order of the Red Banner of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Red_Banner_of_Labour)
- [State Prize of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Prize_of_the_Russian_Federation) in Science and Technology 2004 (6 June 2005), for outstanding achievement in the development of mathematical physics and in 1995 for science and technology (20 June 1995), for the monograph "Introduction to quantum gauge field theory"
- [USSR State Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize) (1971)
- Honorary citizen of St. Petersburg (2010)
- [Academician (Finland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician#Finland) (1991)
