# Moral graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moral graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **moral graph** is used to find the equivalent undirected form of a [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002). It is a key step of the [junction tree algorithm](wikipedia://en/4855682), used in [belief propagation](wikipedia://en/800010) on [graphical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_models).

:::

The moralized counterpart of a directed acyclic graph is formed by adding edges between all pairs of non-adjacent nodes that have a common child, and then making all edges in the graph undirected. Equivalently, a moral graph of a directed acyclic graph $G$ is an undirected graph in which each node of the original $G$ is now connected to its [Markov blanket](wikipedia://en/1169984). The name stems from the fact that, in a moral graph, two nodes that have a common child are required to be *married* by sharing an edge.[^o2]

Moralization may also be applied to [mixed graphs](wikipedia://en/38713429), called in this context "chain graphs". In a chain graph, a connected component of the undirected subgraph is called a chain. Moralization adds an undirected edge between any two vertices that both have outgoing edges to the same chain, and then forgets the orientation of the directed edges of the graph.

## Weakly recursively simplicial {#Weakly_recursively_simplicial}

A graph is **weakly recursively simplicial** if it has a [simplicial vertex](wikipedia://en/79562353) and the subgraph after removing a simplicial vertex and some edges (possibly none) between its neighbours is weakly recursively simplicial. A graph is moral if and only if it is weakly recursively simplicial.

A [chordal](wikipedia://en/744165) graph (a.k.a., recursive simplicial) is a special case of weakly recursively simplicial when no edge is removed during the elimination process. Therefore, a chordal graph is also moral. But a moral graph is not necessarily chordal.[^o4]

## Recognising moral graphs {#Recognising_moral_graphs}

Unlike chordal graphs that can be recognised in polynomial time, (Verma Pearl 1993) proved that deciding whether or not a graph is moral is NP-complete.[^o7]
