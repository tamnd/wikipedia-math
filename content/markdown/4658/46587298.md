# Almgren–Pitts min-max theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Almgren–Pitts min-max theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Almgren–Pitts min-max theory** (named after [Frederick J. Almgren, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_J._Almgren,_Jr.) and his student [Jon T. Pitts](wikipedia://en/46721841)) is an analogue of [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560) for [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101). It is a collection of [global](wikipedia://en/42027498) [variational methods](wikipedia://en/171882) for constructing [critical points](wikipedia://en/298420#Maxima_or_minima_of_a_functional) of [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948).

:::

The theory started with the efforts for generalizing [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906)'s method ([Birkhoff's minimax principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkhoff's_minimax_principle)) for the construction of simple closed [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on the sphere, to allow the construction of [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) in arbitrary [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).[^o2]

It has played roles in the solutions to a number of [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) found by Almgren and Pitts themselves and also by other mathematicians, such as [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Leonidovich_Gromov), [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen), [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137), [Fernando Codá Marques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Codá_Marques), [André Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Neves), [Ian Agol](wikipedia://en/32084718), among others.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11]

## Description and basic concepts {#Description_and_basic_concepts}

The theory allows the construction of [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) minimal hypersurfaces through variational methods.[^o13]

In his PhD thesis, Almgren [proved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almgren_Isomorphism_Theorem) that the m-th [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) of the space of flat k-dimensional cycles on a closed [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) is isomorphic to the (m+k)-th dimensional [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) group of M. This result is a generalization of the [Dold–Thom theorem](wikipedia://en/31336432), which can be thought of as the k=0 case of Almgren's theorem. Existence of non-trivial homotopy classes in the space of cycles suggests the possibility of constructing minimal submanifolds as saddle points of the volume function, as in [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560). In his subsequent work Almgren used these ideas to prove that for every k=1,...,n-1 a closed n-dimensional Riemannian manifold contains a stationary integral k-dimensional [varifold](wikipedia://en/6706053), a generalization of minimal submanifold that may have singularities. Allard showed that such generalized minimal submanifolds are regular on an open and dense subset.

In the 1980s Almgren's student [Jon Pitts](wikipedia://en/46721841) greatly improved the regularity theory of minimal submanifolds obtained by Almgren in the case of codimension 1. He showed that when the dimension n of the manifold is between 3 and 6 the minimal hypersurface obtained using Almgren's min-max method is smooth. A key new idea in the proof was the notion of 1/j-almost minimizing varifolds. [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen) and [Leon Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Simon) extended this result to higher dimensions. More specifically, they showed that every n-dimensional Riemannian manifold contains a closed minimal hypersurface constructed via min-max method that is smooth away from a closed set of dimension n-8.

By considering higher parameter families of codimension 1 cycles one can find distinct minimal hypersurfaces. Such construction was used by [Fernando Codá Marques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Codá_Marques) and [André Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Neves) in their proof of the [Willmore conjecture](wikipedia://en/13722767).[^o14][^o15]
