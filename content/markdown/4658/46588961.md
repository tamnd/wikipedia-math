# Galois ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Galois ring"}

In mathematics, **Galois rings** are a type of [finite](wikipedia://en/23706953) [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) which generalize both the [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) and the [rings of integers modulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_integers_modulo_n) a [prime power](wikipedia://en/1630483). A Galois ring is constructed from the ring $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ similar to how a finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$ is constructed from $\mathbb{F}_p$. It is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$, when the concept of a Galois extension is generalized beyond the context of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603).

:::

Galois rings were studied by [Krull](wikipedia://en/964328) (1924),[^o2] and independently by Janusz (1966)[^o3] and by Raghavendran (1969),[^o4] who both introduced the name *Galois ring*. They are named after [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815), similar to *Galois fields*, which is another name for finite fields. Galois rings have found applications in [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869), where certain codes are best understood as [linear codes](wikipedia://en/1866743) over $\mathbb{Z} / 4\mathbb{Z}$ using Galois rings GR(4, *r*).[^o5][^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A Galois ring is a commutative ring of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*<sup>*n*</sup> which has *p*<sup>*nr*</sup> elements, where *p* is prime and *n* and *r* are positive integers. It is usually denoted GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*). It can be defined as a [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635)

$$\operatorname{GR}(p^n, r) \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(p^n, f(x))$$

where $f(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) of degree *r* which is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) modulo *p*.[^o9][^o10] Up to isomorphism, the ring depends only on *p*, *n*, and *r* and not on the choice of *f* used in the construction.[^o11]

:::

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The simplest examples of Galois rings are important special cases:

- The Galois ring GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, 1) is the [ring of integers modulo *p*<sup>*n*</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_integers_modulo_n).
- The Galois ring GR(*p*, *r*) is the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) of order *p*<sup>*r*</sup>.

A less trivial example is the Galois ring GR(4, 3). It is of characteristic 4 and has 4<sup>3</sup> = 64 elements. One way to construct it is $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(4, x^3 + 2x^2 + x - 1)$, or equivalently, $(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})[\xi]$ where $\xi$ is a root of the polynomial $f(x) = x^3 + 2x^2 + x - 1$. Although any monic polynomial of degree 3 which is irreducible modulo 2 could have been used, this choice of *f* turns out to be convenient because

$$x^7 - 1 = (x^3 + 2x^2 + x - 1)(x^3 - x^2 + 2x - 1)(x - 1)$$

in $(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})[x]$, which makes $\xi$ a 7th [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) in GR(4, 3). The elements of GR(4, 3) can all be written in the form $a_2 \xi^2 + a_1 \xi + a_0$ where each of *a*<sub>0</sub>, *a*<sub>1</sub>, and *a*<sub>2</sub> is in $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. For example, $\xi^3 = 2\xi^2 - \xi + 1$ and $\xi^4 = 2\xi^3 - \xi^2 + \xi = -\xi^2 - \xi + 2$.[^o5]

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

### (*p*<sup>*r*</sup> – 1)-th roots of unity {#(pr_–_1)-th_roots_of_unity}

Every Galois ring GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*) has a [primitive (*p*<sup>*r*</sup> – 1)-th root of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_root_of_unity). It is the equivalence class of *x* in the quotient $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(p^n, f(x))$ when *f* is chosen to be a *primitive polynomial*. This means that, in $(\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z})[x]$, the polynomial $f(x)$ divides $x^{p^r - 1} - 1$ and does not divide $x^m - 1$ for all *m* \< *p*<sup>*r*</sup> – 1. Such an *f* can be computed by starting with a [primitive polynomial](wikipedia://en/1029177) of degree *r* over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$ and using [Hensel lifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hensel_lifting).[^o16]

A primitive (*p*<sup>*r*</sup> – 1)-th root of unity $\xi$ can be used to express elements of the Galois ring in a useful form called the *p-adic representation*. Every element of the Galois ring can be written uniquely as

$$\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 p + \cdots + \alpha_{n-1} p^{n-1}$$

where each $\alpha_i$ is in the set $\{0, 1, \xi, \xi^2, ..., \xi^{p^r - 2}\}$.[^o10][^o16]

### Ideals, quotients, and subrings {#Ideals,_quotients,_and_subrings}

Every Galois ring is a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391). The unique [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) is the [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) $(p) = p \operatorname{GR}(p^n, r)$, consisting of all elements which are multiples of *p*. The [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) $\operatorname{GR}(p^n, r)/(p)$ is isomorphic to the finite field of order *p*<sup>*r*</sup>. Furthermore, $(0), (p^{n-1}), ..., (p), (1)$ are all the ideals.[^o9]

The Galois ring GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*) contains a unique [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) isomorphic to GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *s*) for every *s* which divides *r*. These are the only subrings of GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*).[^o18]

### Group of units {#Group_of_units}

The [units](wikipedia://en/289666) of a Galois ring *R* are all the elements which are not multiples of *p*. The group of units, *R*<sup>×</sup>, can be decomposed as a [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) *G*<sub>1</sub>×*G*<sub>2</sub>, as follows. The subgroup *G*<sub>1</sub> is the group of (*p*<sup>*r*</sup> − 1)-th roots of unity. It is a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *p*<sup>*r*</sup> − 1. The subgroup *G*<sub>2</sub> is 1+*pR*, consisting of all elements congruent to 1 modulo *p*. It is a group of order *p*<sup>*r*(*n*−1)</sup>, with the following structure:

- if *p* is odd or if *p* = 2 and *n* ≤ 2, then $G_2 \cong (C_{p^{n-1}})^r$, the direct product of *r* copies of the cyclic group of order *p*<sup>*n*−1</sup>
- if *p* = 2 and *n* ≥ 3, then $G_2 \cong C_2 \times C_{2^{n-2}} \times (C_{2^{n-1}})^{r-1}$

This description generalizes the structure of the [multiplicative group of integers modulo *p*<sup>*n*</sup>](wikipedia://en/1195234), which is the case *r* = 1.[^o20]

### Automorphisms {#Automorphisms}

Analogous to the automorphisms of the finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$, the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the Galois ring GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*) is a cyclic group of order *r*.[^o22] The automorphisms can be described explicitly using the *p*-adic representation. Specifically, the map

$$\phi(\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 p + \cdots + \alpha_{n-1} p^{n-1}) = \alpha_0^p + \alpha_1^p p + \cdots + \alpha_{n-1}^p p^{n-1}$$

(where each $\alpha_i$ is in the set $\{0, 1, \xi, \xi^2, ..., \xi^{p^r - 2}\}$) is an automorphism, which is called the generalized [Frobenius automorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_automorphism). The [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738) of the generalized Frobenius automorphism are the elements of the subring $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$. Iterating the generalized Frobenius automorphism gives all the automorphisms of the Galois ring.[^o23]

The automorphism group can be thought of as the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*) over $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$, and the ring GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*) is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$. More generally, whenever *r* is a multiple of *s*, GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *r*) is a Galois extension of GR(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>, *s*), with Galois group isomorphic to $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^r} / \mathbb{F}_{p^s})$.[^o24][^o23]
