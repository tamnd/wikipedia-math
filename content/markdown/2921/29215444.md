# Clelia Grillo Borromeo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clelia Grillo Borromeo"}

**Clelia Grillo Borromeo Arese** (1684 – 23 August 1777[^o3]) was an Italian ([Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa)) [natural philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy),[^o3] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist).[^n1]

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Clelia Borromeo was a member of one of the most important patrician families of [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy). She was born in [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa), the daughter of Marcantonio Grillo, duke of [Mondragone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondragone), and Maria Antonia [Imperiali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperiali_family).[^o6]

Borromeo was educated in several languages, mathematics, [natural science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science) and mechanics.[^o3] She spoke eight languages and was interested in geometry, natural science and mathematics.[^o6] She was educated first by her mother and then in a convent, but it is unknown where she received education in the subjects she became known for.[^o6]

In 1707, she married count Giovanni Benedetto Borromeo Arese (1707–1744), and became the mother of eight children.[^o6]

Borromeo died in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan) on 23 August 1777.[^n1] She was buried in the [collegiate church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) of [Santa Maria Podone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Podone).[^n1]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Borromeo was famous for her ability to solve every mathematical problem presented to her. Borromeo was described as an independent person, which was regarded as eccentric because it was not considered natural for her gender. She opened her [salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_%28gathering%29) to the best scientific minds of the time during those years. The most frequent visitor to the salon of the Countess was [Antonio Vallisneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Vallisneri), [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician), [naturalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history) and Professor of medicine at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua).

In 1719 she founded the Clelian Academy (Academia Clœlia Vigilantium), whose members gathered in her palace in Milan.[^n1] The Academy was modeled on the [Accademia del Cimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_del_Cimento), the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) and the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences). Prominent members included the Abbot [Luigi Guido Grandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Guido_Grandi), [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) and distinguished mathematicians [Tommaso Ceva](wikipedia://en/2005652) and [Giovanni Girolamo Saccheri](wikipedia://en/30875270), the [physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Giovanni Francesco Crivelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Francesco_Crivelli) ([fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society), proponent of the equal importance of [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) with [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics)) and [Alessandro Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Volta) and the scholars [Francesco Saverio Quadrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Saverio_Quadrio) and [Giuseppe Antonio Sassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Antonio_Sassi).[^n1] Vallisneri himself drew up the statutes of the Academy.[^n1]

The Clelian Academy played a major role in the scientific cultural debate at the time. It played a significant role in the dissemination of [Newtonian physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_physics) and [Leibnizian](wikipedia://en/12281) philosophy in Italy.[^n1]

During the war in 1746, Borromeo took the side of [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) against [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) and was therefore exiled. When she was allowed to return to Milan, she was celebrated as a heroine.[^o6]

## Clelia curves {#Clelia_curves}

In 1728, [Luigi Guido Grandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Guido_Grandi) described the so-called [Clelia curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clelia_curve) in his book *Flores geometrici ex rhodonearum et cloeliarum*, which he dedicated to Borromeo. These are curves on the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) for whose points there is a linear relationship between [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) and [colatitude](wikipedia://en/1828681). That is, their [spherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinates) satisfy $\varphi = m\theta$ for some constant $m$.[^o3]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

The city of Genoa honored her with a medal with the inscription *Genuensium Gloria* (The Honor of Genoa).[^o3]
