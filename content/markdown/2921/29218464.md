# Frobenius manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frobenius manifold"}

In the mathematical field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Frobenius manifold**, introduced by [Dubrovin](wikipedia://en/58454441),[^o2] is a flat [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with a certain compatible multiplicative structure on the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807). The concept generalizes the notion of [Frobenius algebra](wikipedia://en/1814652) to tangent bundles.

:::

Frobenius manifolds occur naturally in the subject of [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology), more specifically [quantum cohomology](wikipedia://en/7781359). The broadest definition is in the category of Riemannian [supermanifolds](wikipedia://en/891255). We will limit the discussion here to smooth (real) manifolds. A restriction to complex manifolds is also possible.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *M* be a smooth manifold. An *affine flat* structure on *M* is a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) *T*<sup>*f*</sup> of vector spaces that pointwisely span *TM* the tangent bundle and the tangent bracket of pairs of its sections vanishes.

As a local example consider the coordinate vectorfields over a chart of *M*. A manifold admits an affine flat structure if one can glue together such vectorfields for a covering family of charts.

Let further be given a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) *g* on *M*. It is compatible to the flat structure if *g*(*X*, *Y*) is locally constant for all flat vector fields *X* and *Y*.

A Riemannian manifold admits a compatible affine flat structure if and only if its [curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) vanishes everywhere.

A family of *commutative products \** on *TM* is equivalent to a section *A* of *S*<sup>2</sup>(T<sup>\*</sup>*M*) ⊗ *TM* via

$$X*Y = A(X,Y). \,$$

We require in addition the property

$$g(X*Y,Z)=g(X,Y*Z). \,$$

Therefore, the composition *g*<sup>\#</sup>∘*A* is a symmetric 3-tensor.

Given (*g*, *T*<sup>*f*</sup>, *A*), a *local potential Φ* is a local smooth function such that

$$g(A(X,Y),Z)=X[Y[Z[\Phi]]] \,$$

for all flat vector fields *X*, *Y*, and *Z*.

A *Frobenius manifold* (*M*, *g*, \*) is now a flat Riemannian manifold (*M*, *g*) with symmetric 3-tensor *A* that admits everywhere a local potential and is associative. It follows, in particular, that a linear Frobenius manifold (*M*, *g*, \*) with constant product is a Frobenius algebra *M*.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Elementary properties"}

## Elementary properties {#Elementary_properties}

The associativity of the product \* is equivalent to the following quadratic [PDE](wikipedia://en/52564) in the local potential *Φ*

$$\Phi_{,abe}g^{ef}\Phi_{,cdf} = \Phi_{,ade}g^{ef}\Phi_{,bcf} \,$$

where Einstein's sum convention is implied, Φ<sub>,a</sub> denotes the partial derivative of the function Φ by the coordinate vectorfield ∂/∂*x*<sup>*a*</sup> which are all assumed to be flat. *g*<sup>*ef*</sup> are the coefficients of the inverse of the metric.

The equation is therefore called associativity equation or Witten–Dijkgraaf–Verlinde–Verlinde (WDVV) equation.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Beside Frobenius algebras, examples arise from quantum cohomology. Namely, given a semipositive [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) (*M*, *ω*) then there exists an open neighborhood *U* of 0 in its even [quantum cohomology](wikipedia://en/7781359) QH<sup>even</sup>(*M*, *ω*) with [Novikov ring](wikipedia://en/7781429) over **C** such that the big quantum product \*<sub>*a*</sub> for *a* in *U* is analytic. Now *U* together with the [intersection form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form_%284-manifold%29) *g* = \<·,·> is a (complex) Frobenius manifold.

The second large class of examples of Frobenius manifolds come from the singularity theory. Namely, the space of miniversal deformations of an isolated singularity has a Frobenius manifold structure. This Frobenius manifold structure also relates to [Kyoji Saito](wikipedia://en/56830460)'s primitive forms.
