# Lemniscate of Bernoulli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lemniscate of Bernoulli"}

*Image: Lemniskate bernoulli2.svg.* A lemniscate of Bernoulli and its two foci $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$

*Image: Lemniscate of Bernoulli.gif.* The lemniscate of Bernoulli is the [pedal curve](wikipedia://en/845864) of a rectangular [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052)

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **lemniscate of Bernoulli** is a [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) whose shape resembles the [numeral 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_%28number%29) or the [∞ symbol](wikipedia://en/1605389). It can be defined from two given [points](wikipedia://en/593693) $F_1$ and $F_2$, called the *foci*, as the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of points $P$ satisfying the relation $|PF_1|\cdot|PF_2| = c^2,$ where the notation $|AB|$ means the distance between two points $A$ and $B$, and $c = \tfrac12|F_1F_2|$ is half the distance between foci. The name *lemniscate* derives from the Latin word la, meaning "decorated with hanging ribbons". The lemniscate of Bernoulli is a special case of the [Cassini oval](wikipedia://en/2040906) and is a rational [algebraic](wikipedia://en/253260) [curve of degree four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve).

:::

The curve was first studied in 1694 by [Jakob Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Bernoulli), who introduced it as a modification of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277). An ellipse is defined as the locus of points for which the sum of the distances to two fixed focal points is constant, whereas a Cassini oval is defined as the locus of points for which the product of these distances is constant. The lemniscate of Bernoulli is the special case of a Cassini oval which passes through the midpoint between its foci.

The lemniscate of Bernoulli results from applying a [circle inversion transformation](wikipedia://en/295844) to a [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052), where the center of inversion is the midpoint of the hyperbola's foci. It can also be drawn mechanically using a [mechanical linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_linkage) known as [Watt's linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt's_linkage), provided that the lengths of the three bars and the distance between the fixed endpoints are chosen to form an [crossed parallelogram](wikipedia://en/5817627).[^o2]

## Equations {#Equations}

The lemniscate of Bernoulli may be described using either the focal parameter $c$ or the half-width $a$. These parameters are related by $a = c\sqrt{2}$.

- In [Cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) (up to translation and rotation): $$\begin{aligned} \left(x^2 + y^2\right)^2 &= a^2\left(x^2 - y^2\right) \\ &= 2c^2\left(x^2 - y^2\right) \end{aligned}$$
- Solved for $\textstyle y^2$ as a function of $x$: $$y^2 = \left(\sqrt{8x^2 + a^2} - a\right)\frac{a}{2} - x^2$$
- As a [parametric equation](wikipedia://en/576108): $$x = \frac{a\cos t}{1 + \sin^2 t}, \qquad y = \frac{a\sin t\cos t}{1 + \sin^2 t}$$
- A rational parametrization:[^o4] $$x = a\frac{t + t^3}{1 + t^4}, \qquad y = a\frac{t - t^3}{1 + t^4}$$
- In [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates): $$r^2 = a^2\cos 2\theta$$
- In the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628): $$|z - c|\cdot|z + c| = c^2$$
- In [two-center bipolar coordinates](wikipedia://en/8367611): $$rr' = c^2$$

## Arc length and elliptic functions {#Arc_length_and_elliptic_functions}

*Image: The lemniscate sine and cosine related to the arclength of the lemniscate of Bernoulli.png.* The [lemniscate sine and cosine](wikipedia://en/3754835) relate the arc length of an arc of the lemniscate to the distance of one endpoint from the origin.

The determination of the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of arcs of the lemniscate leads to [elliptic integrals](wikipedia://en/9960), as was discovered in the eighteenth century. Around 1800, the [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) inverting those integrals were studied by [C. F. Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._F._Gauss) (largely unpublished at the time, but allusions in the notes to his *[Disquisitiones Arithmeticae](wikipedia://en/736713)*). The [period lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_lattice) are of a very special form, being proportional to the [Gaussian integers](wikipedia://en/48628). For this reason the case of elliptic functions with [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) by [$\sqrt{-1}$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_root_of_minus_one) is called the *[lemniscatic case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemniscatic_case)* in some sources.

Using the elliptic integral

$$\operatorname{arcsl}x \stackrel{\text{def}}{{}={}} \int_0^x\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^4}}$$

the formula of the arc length $L$ can be given as

$$\begin{aligned} L &= 4a \int_{0}^1\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^4}} = 4a\,\operatorname{arcsl}1 = 2\varpi a \\[6pt] &= \frac{\Gamma (1/4)^2}{\sqrt\pi}\,c =\frac{2\pi}{\operatorname{M}(1,1/\sqrt{2})}c\approx 7{.}416 \cdot c \end{aligned}$$

where $c$ and $a = \sqrt{2}c$ are defined as above, $\varpi = 2 \operatorname{arcsl}{1}$ is the [lemniscate constant](wikipedia://en/2079065), $\Gamma$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and $\operatorname{M}$ is the [arithmetic–geometric mean](wikipedia://en/3090).

## Angles {#Angles}

Given two distinct points $\rm A$ and $\rm B$, let $\rm M$ be the midpoint of $\rm AB$. Then the lemniscate of [diameter](wikipedia://en/68283333) $\rm AB$ can also be defined as the set of points $\rm A$, $\rm B$, $\rm M$, together with the locus of the points $\rm P$ such that $|\widehat{\rm APM}-\widehat{\rm BPM}|$ is a right angle (cf. [Thales' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales'_theorem) and its converse).[^o7]

*Image: Lemniskate vechtmann.svg.* relation between angles at Bernoulli's lemniscate

The following theorem about angles occurring in the lemniscate is due to German mathematician [Gerhard Christoph Hermann Vechtmann](wikipedia://en/51356755), who described it 1843 in his dissertation on lemniscates.[^o8]

$F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$ are the foci of the lemniscate, $O$ is the midpoint of the line segment $F_{1}F_{2}$ and $P$ is any point on the lemniscate outside the line connecting $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$. The normal $n$ of the lemniscate in $P$ intersects the line connecting $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$ in $R$. Now the interior angle of the triangle $OPR$ at $O$ is one third of the triangle's exterior angle at $R$ (see also [angle trisection](wikipedia://en/91111)). In addition the interior angle at $P$ is twice the interior angle at $O$.

## Further properties {#Further_properties}

*Image: Lemniskate hyperbel.svg.* The inversion of hyperbola yields a lemniscate

- The lemniscate is symmetric to the line connecting its foci $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$ and as well to the perpendicular bisector of the line segment $F_{1}F_{2}$.
- The lemniscate is symmetric to the midpoint of the line segment $F_{1}F_{2}$.
- The area enclosed by the lemniscate is $a^{2} = 2c^{2}$.
- The lemniscate is the [circle inversion](wikipedia://en/295844#Circle_inversion) of a [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) and vice versa.
- The two tangents at the midpoint $O$ are perpendicular, and each of them forms an angle of $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ with the line connecting $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$.
- The planar cross-section of a standard [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) tangent to its inner equator is a lemniscate.
- The [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) at $(x,y)$ is ${3\over a^2}\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. The maximum curvature, which occurs at $(\pm a,0)$, is therefore $3/a$.
