# Existence theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Existence theorem"}

*Image: Sqrt2 is irrational.svg.* Geometrical proof that an irrational number exists: If the isosceles right triangle ABC had integer side lengths, so had the strictly smaller triangle A'B'C. Repeating this construction would obtain an infinitely descending sequence of integer side lengths.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **existence theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) which asserts the existence of a certain object.[^o2] It might be a statement which begins with the phrase "[there exist(s)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_exists)", or it might be a universal statement whose last [quantifier](wikipedia://en/43507260) is [existential](wikipedia://en/91420) (e.g., "for all $x$, $y$, ... there exist(s) ..."). In the formal terms of [symbolic logic](wikipedia://en/10983), an existence theorem is a theorem with a [prenex normal form](wikipedia://en/421068) involving the [existential quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_quantifier), even though in practice, such theorems are usually stated in standard mathematical language. For example, the statement that the [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) function is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) everywhere, or any theorem written in [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578), can be considered as theorems which are existential by nature—since the quantification can be found in the definitions of the concepts used.

:::

A controversy that goes back to the early twentieth century concerns the issue of purely theoretic existence theorems, that is, theorems which depend on non-constructive foundational material such as the [axiom of infinity](wikipedia://en/246449), the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) or the [law of excluded middle](wikipedia://en/17637). Such theorems provide no indication as to how to construct (or exhibit) the object whose existence is being claimed. From a [constructivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28mathematics%29) viewpoint, such approaches are not viable as it leads to mathematics losing its concrete applicability,[^o3] while the opposing viewpoint is that abstract methods are far-reaching, in a way that [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) cannot be.

## "Pure" existence results {#"Pure"_existence_results}

In mathematics, an existence theorem is purely theoretical if the proof given for it does not indicate a construction of the object whose existence is asserted. Such a proof is non-constructive,[^o5] since the whole approach may not lend itself to construction.[^o6] In terms of [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775), purely theoretical existence theorems bypass all algorithms for finding what is asserted to exist. These are to be contrasted with the so-called "constructive" existence theorems,[^o7] which many constructivist mathematicians working in extended logics (such as [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262)) believe to be intrinsically stronger than their non-constructive counterparts.

Despite that, the purely theoretical existence results are nevertheless ubiquitous in contemporary mathematics. For example, [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash,_Jr.)'s original proof of the existence of a [Nash equilibrium](wikipedia://en/45337) in 1951 was such an existence theorem. An approach which is constructive was also later found in 1962.[^o8]

## Constructivist ideas {#Constructivist_ideas}

From the other direction, there has been considerable clarification of what [constructive mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_mathematics) is—without the emergence of a "master theory". For example, according to [Errett Bishop](wikipedia://en/321995)'s definitions, the continuity of a function such as $\sin(x)$ should be proved as a constructive bound on the [modulus of continuity](wikipedia://en/964161), meaning that the existential content of the assertion of continuity is a promise that can always be kept. Accordingly, Bishop rejects the standard idea of pointwise continuity, and proposed that continuity should be defined in terms of "local uniform continuity".[^o10] One could get another explanation of existence theorem from [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282), in which a proof of an existential statement can come only from a *term* (which one can see as the computational content).
