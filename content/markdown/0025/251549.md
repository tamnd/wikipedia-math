# Deming regression

*Image: Total least squares.svg.* Deming regression. The red lines show the error in both *x* and *y*. This is different from the traditional least squares method, which measures error parallel to the *y* axis. The case shown, with deviations measured perpendicularly, arises when errors in *x* and *y* have equal variances.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deming regression"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Deming regression**, named after [W. Edwards Deming](wikipedia://en/70534), is an [errors-in-variables model](wikipedia://en/23531798) that tries to find the [line of best fit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_best_fit) for a two-dimensional [data set](wikipedia://en/8495). It differs from the [simple linear regression](wikipedia://en/2593771) in that it accounts for [errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) in observations on both the *x*- and the *y*- axis. It is a special case of [total least squares](wikipedia://en/971437), which allows for any number of predictors and a more complicated error structure.

:::

Deming regression is equivalent to the [maximum likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood) estimation of an [errors-in-variables model](wikipedia://en/23531798) in which the errors for the two variables are assumed to be independent and [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462), and the ratio of their variances, denoted *δ*, is known.[^n1] In practice, this ratio might be estimated from related data-sources; however the regression procedure takes no account for possible errors in estimating this ratio.

The Deming regression is only slightly more difficult to compute than the [simple linear regression](wikipedia://en/2593771). Most statistical software packages used in clinical chemistry offer Deming regression.

The model was originally introduced by (Adcock 1878) who considered the case *δ* = 1, and then more generally by (Kummell 1879) with arbitrary *δ*. However their ideas remained largely unnoticed for more than 50 years, until they were revived by (Koopmans 1936) and later propagated even more by (Deming 1943). The latter book became so popular in [clinical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_chemistry) and related fields that the method was even dubbed *Deming regression* in those fields.[^n2]

## Specification {#Specification}

Assume that the available data (*y<sub>i</sub>*, *x<sub>i</sub>*) are measured observations of the "true" values (*y<sub>i</sub>\**, *x<sub>i</sub>\**), which lie on the regression line:

$$\begin{aligned} y_i &= y^*_i + \varepsilon_i, \\ x_i &= x^*_i + \eta_i, \end{aligned}$$

where errors *ε* and *η* are independent and the ratio of their variances is assumed to be known:

$$\delta = \frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{\sigma_\eta^2}.$$

In practice, the variances of the $x$ and $y$ parameters are often unknown, which complicates the estimate of $\delta$. Note that when the measurement method for $x$ and $y$ is the same, these variances are likely to be equal, so $\delta = 1$ for this case.

We seek to find the line of "best fit"

$$y^* = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x^*,$$

such that the weighted sum of squared residuals of the model is minimized:[^n3]

$$SSR = \sum_{i=1}^n\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_i^2}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2} + \frac{\eta_i^2}{\sigma_\eta^2}\bigg) = \frac{1}{\sigma_\epsilon^2} \sum_{i=1}^n\Big((y_i-\beta_0-\beta_1x^*_i)^2 + \delta(x_i-x^*_i)^2\Big) \ \to\ \min_{\beta_0,\beta_1,x_1^*,\ldots,x_n^*} SSR$$

See (Jensen 2007) for a full derivation.

## Solution {#Solution}

The solution can be expressed in terms of the second-degree sample moments. That is, we first calculate the following quantities (all sums go from *i* = 1 to *n*):

$$\begin{aligned} \overline{x} &= \tfrac{1}{n}\sum x_i & \overline{y} &= \tfrac{1}{n}\sum y_i,\\ s_{xx} &= \tfrac{1}{n}\sum (x_i-\overline{x})^2 &&= \overline{x^2} - \overline{x}^2, \\ s_{xy} &= \tfrac{1}{n}\sum (x_i-\overline{x})(y_i-\overline{y}) &&= \overline{x y} - \overline{x} \, \overline{y}, \\ s_{yy} &= \tfrac{1}{n}\sum (y_i-\overline{y})^2 &&= \overline{y^2} - \overline{y}^2. \end{aligned}\,$$

Finally, the least-squares estimates of model's parameters will be[^n4]

$$\begin{aligned} & \hat\beta_1 = \frac{s_{yy}-\delta s_{xx} + \sqrt{(s_{yy}-\delta s_{xx})^2 + 4\delta s_{xy}^2}}{2s_{xy}}, \\ & \hat\beta_0 = \overline{y} - \hat\beta_1\overline{x}, \\ & \hat{x}_i^* = x_i + \frac{\hat\beta_1}{\hat\beta_1^2+\delta}(y_i-\hat\beta_0-\hat\beta_1x_i). \end{aligned}$$

## Orthogonal regression {#Orthogonal_regression}

For the case of equal error variances, i.e., when $\delta=1$, Deming regression becomes **orthogonal regression**: it minimizes the sum of squared [perpendicular distances from the data points to the regression line](wikipedia://en/23972889). In this case, denote each observation as a point $z_j = x_j +i y_j$ in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) (i.e., the point $(x_j, y_j)$ where $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358)). Denote as $S=\sum{(z_j - \overline z)^2}$ the sum of the squared differences of the data points from the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) $\overline z = \tfrac{1}{n} \sum z_j$ (also denoted in complex coordinates), which is the point whose horizontal and vertical locations are the averages of those of the data points. Then:[^n5]

- If $S=0$, then every line through the centroid is a line of best orthogonal fit.
- If $S \neq 0$, the orthogonal regression line goes through the centroid and is parallel to the vector from the origin to $\sqrt{S}$.

A [trigonometric](wikipedia://en/18717261) representation of the orthogonal regression line was given by Coolidge in 1913.[^n6] The [distance](wikipedia://en/23972889#Another_formula) can also be calculated using the more typical equation of a line, given as $y=mx+k$.

::: {#o17 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

### Application {#Application}

In the case of three [non-collinear](wikipedia://en/946975) points in the plane, the [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) with these points as its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) has a unique [Steiner inellipse](wikipedia://en/17185864) that is tangent to the triangle's sides at their midpoints. The [major axis of this ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277#Elements_of_an_ellipse) falls on the orthogonal regression line for the three vertices.[^n7] The quantification of a biological cell's intrinsic [cellular noise](wikipedia://en/32104707) can be quantified upon applying Deming regression to the observed behavior of a two reporter [synthetic biological circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_biological_circuit).[^n8]

When humans are asked to draw a linear regression on a scatterplot by guessing, their answers are closer to orthogonal regression than to [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) regression.[^o20]

:::

## Scale-invariant version {#Scale-invariant_version}

The [geometric mean functional relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_mean_functional_relationship) is a case of the Deming regression where *δ* = *σ²<sub>x</sub>*/*σ²<sub>y</sub>*. It assumes that both variables have the same [reliability](wikipedia://en/239138#Classical_test_theory), i.e. *σ<sub>y</sub>*/*σ<sub>ε</sub>* = *σ<sub>x</sub>*/*σ<sub>η</sub>*. The resulting slope is the geometric mean of the ordinary least squares slope and the reverse least squares slope, which makes it easy to calculate using existing tools. It has been recommended for use in biology.[^o22] This method is invariant under scaling, translation, and interchange of variables, the only method that depend on only first and second moments to have this property.[^o23]

## York regression {#York_regression}

The York regression extends Deming regression by allowing correlated errors in x and y.[^o25]
