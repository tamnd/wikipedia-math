# Bony–Brezis theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bony–Brezis theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Bony–Brezis theorem**, due to the French mathematicians [Jean-Michel Bony](wikipedia://en/50769397) and [Haïm Brezis](wikipedia://en/18329570), gives [necessary and sufficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_and_sufficient) conditions for a closed subset of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) to be invariant under the [flow](wikipedia://en/2619735) defined by a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641), namely at each point of the [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) the vector field must have non-positive inner product with any **exterior normal vector** to the set. A vector is an *exterior normal* at a point of the closed set if there is a real-valued continuously [differentiable function](wikipedia://en/330206) maximized locally at the point with that vector as its derivative at the point. If the closed subset is a smooth [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) with boundary, the condition states that the vector field should not point outside the subset at boundary points. The generalization to non-smooth subsets is important in the theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564).

:::

The theorem had in fact been previously discovered by [Mitio Nagumo](wikipedia://en/50491692) in 1942 and is also known as the **Nagumo theorem**.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *F* be closed subset of a C<sup>2</sup> manifold *M* and let *X* be a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) on *M* which is [Lipschitz continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_continuous). The following conditions are equivalent:

- Every [integral curve](wikipedia://en/1322061) of *X* starting in *F* remains in *F*.
- (*X*(*m*),*v*) ≤ 0 for every exterior normal vector *v* at a point *m* in *F*.

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Following (Hörmander 1983), to prove that the first condition implies the second, let *c*(*t*) be an integral curve with *c*(0) = *x* in *F* and *dc/dt*= *X*(*c*). Let *g* have a local maximum on *F* at *x*. Then *g*(*c*(*t*)) ≤ *g* (*c*(0)) for *t* small and positive. Differentiating, this implies that *g* '(*x*)⋅*X*(*x*) ≤ 0.

To prove the reverse implication, since the result is local, it is enough to check it in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. In that case *X* locally satisfies a Lipschitz condition

$$\displaystyle{|X(a)-X(b)|\le C|a-b|.}$$

If *F* is closed, the distance function *D*(*x*) = *d*(*x*,*F*)<sup>2</sup> has the following differentiability property:

$$\displaystyle{D(x+h)=D(x) + \min_{z} 2h\cdot(x-z) + o(|h|),}$$

where the minimum is taken over the closest points *z* to *x* in *F*.

To check this, let

$$\displaystyle{f_\varepsilon(h)=\min_{z} 2h\cdot(x- z),}$$

where the minimum is taken over *z* in *F* such that *d*(*x*,*z*) ≤ *d*(*x*,*F*) + ε.

Since *f*<sub>ε</sub> is homogeneous in *h* and increases uniformly to *f*<sub>0</sub> on any sphere,

$$\displaystyle{f_0(h) \ge f_\varepsilon(h) \ge f_0(h) - C(\varepsilon) |h|,}$$

with a constant *C*(ε) tending to 0 as ε tends to 0.

This differentiability property follows from this because

$$\displaystyle{D(x+h) \le |x+h-z|^2 \le |z-x|^2 +2h\cdot (x-z) + |h|^2 =D(x) +f_0(h) +|h|^2}$$

and similarly if |*h*| ≤ ε

$$\displaystyle{D(x+h) \ge D(x) + f_\varepsilon(h) + |h|^2.}$$

The differentiability property implies that

$$\displaystyle{\lim{\delta\downarrow 0} {D(c(t+\delta)) - D(c(t))\over \delta} =2\min_z X(c(t))\cdot (c(t)-z),}$$

minimized over closest points *z* to *c*(*t*). For any such *z*

$$\displaystyle{2X(c(t))\cdot (c(t)-z)= 2 X(z)\cdot (c(t)-z) - 2(X(z)-X(c(t)))\cdot (c(t)-z).}$$

Since −|*y* − *c*(*t*)|<sup>2</sup> has a local maximum on *F* at *y* = *z*, *c*(*t*) − *z* is an exterior normal vector at *z*. So the first term on the right hand side is non-negative. The Lipschitz condition for *X* implies the second term is bounded above by 2*C*⋅*D*(*c*(*t*)). Thus the [derivative from the right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-sided_derivatives) of

$$\displaystyle{e^{-2Ct}D(c(t))}$$

is non-positive, so it is a non-increasing function of *t*. Thus if *c*(0) lies in *F*, *D*(*c*(0))=0 and hence *D*(*c*(*t*)) = 0 for *t* > 0, i.e. *c*(*t*) lies in *F* for *t* > 0.

:::
