# Marion Lee Johnson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marion Lee Johnson"}

**Marion Lee Johnson** is an American mathematician whose work was crucial to the landing of the [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) mission. She was a mathematician on the [Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing)/[NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) team, where she worked in preparing data for the vehicle impact trajectories.[^o2] Her perfect score over 20 successful missions earned her a place on the Apollo/Saturn V Roll of Honor.[^o3][^o2]

:::

After completion of the project, she worked for [Pfizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizer) for 26 years. She currently lives in New Jersey.

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

She was born in a working-class family in [Savannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah,_Georgia), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_%28U.S._state%29), with three sisters and a brother.[^o5] She attended school at Moses Jackson, in a segregated neighborhood. Very early, she fell in love with mathematics, and credits this love to her 7th grade maths teacher, Walter B. Simmons.[^o6][^o7] She graduated class valedictorian from high school at [Thompkins High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tompkins_High_School) in 1963.[^o8][^o3] In 1967, she was granted a scholarship at [Talladega College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talladega_College) in Talladega, [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama), to study mathematics,[^o9] and assumed that her degree would lead her to become a teacher.[^o6] After losing her scholarship because her grades had slipped, Johnson took out a loan to continue her degree, and prioritised her studies in order to requalify for the scholarship.[^o5]

After graduation, at the age of 21, she went to work as an associate engineer at Boeing Company in [Huntsville, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntsville,_Alabama).[^o10][^o3][^o11] She was assigned to the Launch Systems branch of the Boeing/NASA team at the Marshall Space Flight Centre to prepare the landing of the Apollo 11 mission.[^o2][^o3] At the time, very few women worked at the Space Flight Centre.[^o7] Johnson worked under the supervision of [Arthur Rudolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rudolph) and rocket designer [Wernher von Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wernher_von_Braun), on the calculations to simulate vehicle piece impact trajectories (where the booster rockets would fall).[^o2][^o3][^o11] According to Lee, Boeing was a diverse place. In an interview for 1010 WINS' with Larry Mullins in 2018, she said: "You had a lot of people there - a lot of people from all different cultures - and we all worked together."[^o10] According to Johnson, it was only after seeing the 2016 movie [Hidden Figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Figures) that she realised how important her own contributions were to NASA.[^o12]

After two years working for Boeing/NASA, Johnson then went to work at Pfizer, Inc., where she became Project Leader for the Corporate Information Technology Division.[^o3] She retired from Pfizer after 26 years.[^o3]

A resident of [Union County, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_County,_New_Jersey), she recently retired from [Branford Hall Career Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branford_Hall_Career_Institute) as a Computer Networking and Security Instructor.[^o8] The city of Plainfield awarded Johnson the key to the city, and designated two days to honor her legacy.[^o10][^o13] She is married to J. Frank Johnson, the owner of an accounting, tax, audit, and advisory services company, with whom she has three children.[^o3]
