# Stacky curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stacky curve"}

In mathematics, a **stacky curve** is an object in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) that is roughly an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) with potentially "fractional points" called **stacky points**. A stacky curve is a type of [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) used in studying [Gromov–Witten theory](wikipedia://en/2301291), [enumerative geometry](wikipedia://en/3069092), and [rings of modular forms](wikipedia://en/62004450).

:::

Stacky curves are closely related to 1-dimensional [orbifolds](wikipedia://en/293504) and therefore sometimes called **orbifold curves** or **orbicurves**.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A stacky curve $\mathfrak{X}$ over a field $k$ is a [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) [proper](wikipedia://en/603782) [geometrically connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [Deligne–Mumford stack](wikipedia://en/40639179) of [dimension](wikipedia://en/253251) 1 over $k$ that contains a dense open subscheme.[^o4][^o8][^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A stacky curve is uniquely determined (up to isomorphism) by its coarse space $X$ (a smooth [quasi-projective](wikipedia://en/1321785) curve over $k$), a finite set of points $x_{i}$ (its stacky points) and integers $n_{i}$ (its ramification orders) greater than 1.[^o5] The [canonical divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_divisor) of $\mathfrak{X}$ is [linearly equivalent](wikipedia://en/1186886) to the sum of the canonical divisor of $X$ and a ramification divisor $R$:[^o4]

$$K_\mathfrak{X} \sim K_X + R.$$

Letting $g$ be the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) of the coarse space $X$, the degree of the [canonical divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_divisor) of $\mathfrak{X}$ is therefore:[^o4]

$$d = \deg K_\mathfrak{X} = 2g - 2 + \sum_{i=1}^r \frac{n_i - 1}{n_i}.$$

A stacky curve is called **hyperbolic** if $d$ is positive, **Euclidean** if $d$ is zero, and **spherical** if $d$ is negative.[^o5]

Although the corresponding statement of [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261) does not hold for stacky curves,[^o4] there is a generalization of [Riemann's existence theorem](wikipedia://en/630505#Riemann's_existence_theorem) that gives an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051) between the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of stacky curves over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and the category of complex orbifold curves.[^o4][^o8][^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The generalization of GAGA for stacky curves is used in the derivation of [algebraic structure theory of rings of modular forms](wikipedia://en/62004450#General_Fuchsian_groups).[^o8]

The study of stacky curves is used extensively in equivariant Gromov–Witten theory and enumerative geometry.[^o4][^o12]

:::
