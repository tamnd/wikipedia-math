# Ring of modular forms

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ring of modular forms"}

In mathematics, the **ring of modular forms** associated to a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $\Gamma$ of the [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z})$ is the [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094) generated by the [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) of $\Gamma$. The study of rings of modular forms describes the algebraic structure of the space of modular forms.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\Gamma$ be a subgroup of $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z})$ that is of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) and let $M_{k}(\Gamma)$ be the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of modular forms of weight $k$. The ring of modular forms of $\Gamma$ is the graded ring $M(\Gamma) = \bigoplus_{k \geq 0} M_k(\Gamma)$.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The ring of modular forms of the full [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019) $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z})$ is [freely generated](wikipedia://en/59735) by the [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458) $E_{4}$ and $E_{6}$. In other words, $M_{k}(\Gamma)$ is isomorphic as a [$\mathbb{C}$-algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) to $\mathbb{C}[E_4, E_6]$, which is the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) of two variables over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).[^o8]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The ring of modular forms is a graded [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) since the Lie bracket $[f,g] = kfg' - \ell f'g$ of modular forms $f$ and $g$ of respective weights $k$ and $\ell$ is a modular form of weight $k + \ell + 2$.[^o8] A bracket can be defined for the $n$-th derivative of modular forms and such a bracket is called a [Rankin–Cohen bracket](wikipedia://en/42535054).[^o8]

### Congruence subgroups of SL(2, Z) {#Congruence_subgroups_of_SL(2,_Z)}

In 1973, [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) and [Michael Rapoport](wikipedia://en/26629548) showed that the ring of modular forms $M(\Gamma)$ is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/11985661) when $\Gamma$ is a [congruence subgroup](wikipedia://en/382694) of $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z})$.[^o10]

In 2003, Lev Borisov and Paul Gunnells showed that the ring of modular forms $M(\Gamma)$ is [generated](wikipedia://en/24873145#Subring_generated_by_a_set) in weight at most 3 when $\Gamma$ is the congruence subgroup $\Gamma_1(N)$ of prime level $N$ in $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z})$ using the theory of [toric modular forms](wikipedia://en/737087).[^o11] In 2014, Nadim Rustom extended the result of Borisov and Gunnells for $\Gamma_1(N)$ to all levels $N$ and also demonstrated that the ring of modular forms for the congruence subgroup $\Gamma_0(N)$ is generated in weight at most 6 for some levels $N$.[^o12]

In 2015, John Voight and David Zureick-Brown generalized these results: they proved that the graded ring of modular forms of even weight for any congruence subgroup $\Gamma$ of $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z})$ is generated in weight at most 6 with [relations](wikipedia://en/99494) generated in weight at most 12.[^o13] Building on this work, in 2016, Aaron Landesman, Peter Ruhm, and Robin Zhang showed that the same bounds hold for the full ring (all weights), with the improved bounds of 5 and 10 when $\Gamma$ has some nonzero odd weight modular form.[^o14]

### General Fuchsian groups {#General_Fuchsian_groups}

A [Fuchsian group](wikipedia://en/471365) $\Gamma$ corresponds to the [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) obtained from the quotient $\Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}$ of the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826) $\mathbb{H}$. By a stacky generalization of [Riemann's existence theorem](wikipedia://en/630505#Riemann's_existence_theorem), there is a correspondence between the ring of modular forms of $\Gamma$ and a particular [section ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_ring) closely related to the [canonical ring](wikipedia://en/1866872) of a [stacky curve](wikipedia://en/62004667).[^o13]

There is a general formula for the weights of generators and relations of rings of modular forms due to the work of Voight and Zureick-Brown and the work of Landesman, Ruhm, and Zhang. Let $e_i$ be the stabilizer orders of the stacky points of the stacky curve (equivalently, the cusps of the orbifold $\Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}$) associated to $\Gamma$. If $\Gamma$ has no nonzero odd weight modular forms, then the ring of modular forms is generated in weight at most $6 \max(1, e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_r)$ and has relations generated in weight at most $12 \max(1, e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_r)$.[^o13] If $\Gamma$ has a nonzero odd weight modular form, then the ring of modular forms is generated in weight at most $\max(5, e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_r)$ and has relations generated in weight at most $2\max(5, e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_r)$.[^o14]

:::

::: {#o17 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and [supersymmetric gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetric_gauge_theory), the algebraic structure of the ring of modular forms can be used to study the structure of the [Higgs vacua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_vacuum) of four-dimensional [gauge theories](wikipedia://en/23912155) with N = 1 [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636).[^o18] The stabilizers of [superpotentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpotential) in [N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/36625367) are rings of modular forms of the congruence subgroup $\Gamma(2)$ of $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z})$.[^o18][^o19]

:::
