# Prasad Raghavendra

::: {#o7 .subject cue=lead name="Prasad Raghavendra"}

**Prasad Raghavendra** is an Indian-American [theoretical computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_computer_scientist) and mathematician, working in [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization), [complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [approximation algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_algorithms), [hardness of approximation](wikipedia://en/20677277) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). He is a professor of computer science at the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley).[^o8]

:::

## Education {#Education}

After completing a BTech at [IIT Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIT_Madras) in 2005, he obtained an MSc (2007) and PhD (2009) at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) under the supervision of [Venkatesan Guruswami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venkatesan_Guruswami). After a postdoctoral position at [Microsoft Research New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research_New_England), he became faculty at the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley).

## Career {#Career}

Raghavendra showed that assuming the [unique games conjecture](wikipedia://en/3000842), [semidefinite programming](wikipedia://en/4993539) is the optimal algorithm for solving [constraint satisfaction problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_satisfaction_problems).

Together with [David Steurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Steurer), he developed the [small set expansion hypothesis](wikipedia://en/73292008), for which they won the Michael and Shiela Held Prize in 2018.

He developed [sum of squares](wikipedia://en/30474456) as a versatile algorithmic technique. Together with David Steurer, he gave an invited talk on the topic at the 2018 [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842).
