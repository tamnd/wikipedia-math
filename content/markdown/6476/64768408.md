# ICD-10-CM

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="ICD-10-CM"}

The **ICD-10 Clinical Modification** (**ICD-10-CM**) is a set of [diagnosis codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnosis_code) used in the [United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America).[^o3] It was developed by a component of the U.S. Department of Health and Human services,[^o4] as an adaptation of the [ICD-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICD-10) with authorization from the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization). In 2015, ICD-10-CM replaced [ICD-9-CM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICD-9-CM) as the federally mandated classification. Annual updates are provided.

:::

## Development {#Development}

Since 1979, the US had required ICD-9-CM codes[^o6] for [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_%28United_States%29) and [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid) claims, and most of the rest of the medical industry in the US followed suit. On January 1, 1999, the ICD-10 (without clinical extensions) was adopted for reporting mortality, however, ICD-9-CM continued to be used for [morbidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbidity).

During that time, the U.S. [National Center for Health Statistics](wikipedia://en/1042734) (NCHS) received permission from the WHO to create a clinical modification of the ICD-10.

ICD-10-CM adapted ICD-10 in the following ways:

- Allow the capture of information for ambulatory and managed care encounters
- Expand available injury codes
- Combine codes for diagnosis/symptoms to reduce the number of codes needed to describe a problem fully
- Provide additional sixth and seventh digit classifications
- Add classifications specific to laterality
- Refine classification for increased data granularity

## Adoption {#Adoption}

Adoption of ICD-10-CM was slow. On August 21, 2008, the [US Department of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services) (HHS) proposed new code sets to be used for reporting diagnoses and procedures on health care transactions. Under the proposal, the ICD-9-CM code sets would be replaced with the ICD-10-CM code sets, effective October 1, 2013. On April 17, 2012, the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) published a proposed rule that would delay the compliance date for the ICD-10-CM and PCS by 12 months-from October 1, 2013, to October 1, 2014.[^o8] Congress further delayed the implementation date to October 1, 2015, after it was inserted into the "Doc Fix" Bill without debate over the objections of many.

## Release {#Release}

On October 1, 2015, ICD-10-CM replaced volumes 1 and 2 of ICD-9-CM, and [ICD-10-PCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICD-10-PCS) replaced volume 3.

### Annual review {#Annual_review}

The ICD-10-CM code set is reviewed every year.[^o11] The code set for the 2023 [fiscal year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year) applies to patient discharges and encounters between October 1, 2022, and September 30, 2023 (inclusive)[^o12] – with the exception of four codes that were in effect from April 1, 2022.[^o12]
