# Lamé's special quartic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lamé's special quartic"}

*Image: Superellipse chamfered square.svg.* Lamé's special quartic with "radius" 1.

**Lamé's special quartic**, named after [Gabriel Lamé](wikipedia://en/52145), is the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of the [equation](wikipedia://en/9284)

:::

$$x^4 + y^4 = r^4$$

where $r > 0$.[^o2] It looks like a rounded [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) with "sides" of length $2r$ and centered on the origin. This curve is a [squircle](wikipedia://en/5565333) centered on the origin, and it is a special case of a [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211).[^o3]

Because of [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966)'s only surviving [proof](wikipedia://en/82285), that of [the *n* = 4 case](wikipedia://en/23070485#n=4) of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953), if *r* is [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) there is no non-trivial rational point (*x*, *y*) on this curve (that is, no point for which both *x* and *y* are non-zero rational numbers).
