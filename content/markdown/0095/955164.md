# Geometric group theory

*Image: F2 Cayley Graph.svg.* The [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) with two generators. This is a [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817) whose [Gromov boundary](wikipedia://en/41160090) is a [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172). Hyperbolic groups and their boundaries are important topics in geometric group theory, as are Cayley graphs.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric group theory"}

**Geometric group theory** is an area in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) devoted to the study of [finitely generated groups](wikipedia://en/963042) by exploring the connections between [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) properties of such [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) and [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) and [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) properties of spaces on which these groups can [act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) non-trivially (that is, when the groups in question are realized as geometric symmetries or [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) transformations of some spaces).

:::

An important idea in geometric group theory is to consider finitely generated groups themselves as geometric objects. This is usually done by studying the [Cayley graphs](wikipedia://en/358277) of groups, which, in addition to the [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) structure, are endowed with the structure of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), given by the so-called [word metric](wikipedia://en/1289716).

Geometric group theory, as a distinct area, is relatively new, and became a clearly identifiable branch of mathematics in the late 1980s and early 1990s. Geometric group theory closely interacts with [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774), [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [computational group theory](wikipedia://en/618584) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). There are also substantial connections with [complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), the study of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and their discrete subgroups, [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748), and other areas of mathematics.

## History {#History}

Geometric group theory grew out of [combinatorial group theory](wikipedia://en/979834), which largely studied properties of [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536) by analyzing [group presentations](wikipedia://en/99494) (ways of describing groups as [quotients](wikipedia://en/11526) of [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735)); this field was first systematically studied in the early 1880s by [Walther von Dyck](wikipedia://en/752612), a student of [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887).[^n1] A precursor is found in the 1856 [icosian calculus](wikipedia://en/316081) of [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817), who studied the [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844) group via the edge graph of the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407). Currently, combinatorial group theory as a discipline is largely subsumed by geometric group theory. Moreover, the term "geometric group theory" came to often include studying discrete groups using probabilistic, [measure-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), arithmetic and analytic approaches that lie outside of the traditional combinatorial group theory arsenal.

In the first half of the 20th century, pioneering work of [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602), [Jakob Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516), [Kurt Reidemeister](wikipedia://en/520188), [Otto Schreier](wikipedia://en/4355201), [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179), and [Egbert van Kampen](wikipedia://en/8156533), among others, introduced topological and geometric ideas into the study of discrete groups.[^n2] Other precursors of geometric group theory include [small cancellation theory](wikipedia://en/19882883) and [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708). Small cancellation theory was introduced by [Martin Greendlinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Greendlinger) in the 1960s[^n3][^n4] and further developed by [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon) and [Paul Schupp](wikipedia://en/54677806).[^n5] It studies [van Kampen diagrams](wikipedia://en/18871460), corresponding to finite group presentations, via combinatorial curvature conditions and derives algebraic and algorithmic properties of groups from such analysis. Bass–Serre theory, introduced by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) in the 1970s,[^n6] derives structural algebraic information about groups by studying group actions on [simplicial trees](wikipedia://en/48560). External precursors of geometric group theory include the study of lattices in Lie groups, especially [Mostow's rigidity theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostow's_rigidity_theorem), the study of [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652), and the progress achieved in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774) and hyperbolic geometry in the 1970s and early 1980s, spurred, in particular, by [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s [geometrization program](wikipedia://en/220642).

The emergence of geometric group theory as a distinct area of mathematics is usually traced to the late 1980s and early 1990s. It was spurred by the [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29)'s 1987 monograph "Hyperbolic groups"[^n7] that introduced the notion of a [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817) (also known as *word-hyperbolic* or *Gromov-hyperbolic* or *negatively curved* group), which captures the idea of a finitely generated group having large-scale negative curvature, and by his subsequent monograph *Asymptotic Invariants of Infinite Groups*[^n8] that outlined his program of understanding discrete groups up to [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/490861#Q). Gromov's work had a transformative effect on the study of discrete groups[^n9][^n10][^n11] and the phrase "geometric group theory" started appearing soon afterwards. (see e.g.[^o15]).

## Modern themes and developments {#Modern_themes_and_developments}

section

Notable themes and developments in geometric group theory in 1990s and 2000s include:

- Gromov's program to study quasi-isometric properties of groups.

A particularly influential broad theme in the area is [Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29)'s program[^o17] of classifying [finitely generated groups](wikipedia://en/99945#Finitely_generated_group) according to their large scale geometry. Formally, this means classifying finitely generated groups with their [word metric](wikipedia://en/1289716) up to [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/490861#Q). This program involves:

  1. The study of properties that are invariant under [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259). Examples of such properties of finitely generated groups include: the [growth rate](wikipedia://en/498727) of a finitely generated group; the [isoperimetric function](wikipedia://en/20017026#Isoperimetric_function) or [Dehn function](wikipedia://en/18871460) of a [finitely presented group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group); the number of [ends of a group](wikipedia://en/6862105#Ends_of_graphs_and_groups); [hyperbolicity of a group](wikipedia://en/3172817); the [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) type of the [Gromov boundary](wikipedia://en/41160090) of a hyperbolic group;[^o18] [asymptotic cones](wikipedia://en/18609360) of finitely generated groups (see e.g.[^o19][^o20]); [amenability](wikipedia://en/657430) of a finitely generated group; being virtually [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) (that is, having an abelian subgroup of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581)); being virtually [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569); being virtually [free](wikipedia://en/59735); being [finitely presentable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group); being a finitely presentable group with solvable [Word Problem](wikipedia://en/33563); and others.
  1. Theorems which use quasi-isometry invariants to prove algebraic results about groups, for example: [Gromov's polynomial growth theorem](wikipedia://en/635483); [Stallings' ends theorem](wikipedia://en/19196631); [Mostow rigidity theorem](wikipedia://en/2782164).
  1. Quasi-isometric rigidity theorems, in which one classifies algebraically all groups that are quasi-isometric to some given group or metric space. This direction was initiated by the work of [Schwartz](wikipedia://en/1213887) on quasi-isometric rigidity of rank-one lattices[^o21] and the work of [Benson Farb](wikipedia://en/32518776) and Lee Mosher on quasi-isometric rigidity of [Baumslag–Solitar groups](wikipedia://en/11485892).[^o22]
- The theory of [word-hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/3172817) and [relatively hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/21993809) groups. A particularly important development here is the work of [Zlil Sela](wikipedia://en/19309289) in 1990s resulting in the solution of the [isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/18054026) for word-hyperbolic groups.[^o23] The notion of a relatively hyperbolic groups was originally introduced by Gromov in 1987[^n7] and refined by Farb[^o25] and [Brian Bowditch](wikipedia://en/13677348),[^o26] in the 1990s. The study of relatively hyperbolic groups gained prominence in the 2000s.
- Interactions with mathematical logic and the study of the first-order theory of free groups. Particularly important progress occurred on the famous [Tarski conjectures](wikipedia://en/59735#Tarski.27s_problems), due to the work of Sela[^o27] as well as of [Olga Kharlampovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Kharlampovich) and Alexei Myasnikov.[^o28] The study of [limit groups](wikipedia://en/78938535) and introduction of the language and machinery of [non-commutative algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/25493347) gained prominence.
- Interactions with computer science, complexity theory and the theory of formal languages. This theme is exemplified by the development of the theory of [automatic groups](wikipedia://en/4224977),[^o29] a notion that imposes certain geometric and language theoretic conditions on the multiplication operation in a finitely generated group.
- The study of isoperimetric inequalities, Dehn functions and their generalizations for finitely presented group. This includes, in particular, the work of Jean-Camille Birget, Aleksandr Olʹshanskiĭ, [Eliyahu Rips](wikipedia://en/673693) and [Mark Sapir](wikipedia://en/58965999)[^o30][^o31] essentially characterizing the possible Dehn functions of finitely presented groups, as well as results providing explicit constructions of groups with fractional Dehn functions.[^o32]
- The theory of toral or [JSJ-decompositions](wikipedia://en/228599) for [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) was originally brought into a group theoretic setting by Peter Kropholler.[^o33] This notion has been developed by many authors for both finitely presented and finitely generated groups.[^o34][^o35][^o36][^o37][^o38]
- Connections with [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474), the study of [C\*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebras) associated with discrete groups and of the theory of free probability. This theme is represented, in particular, by considerable progress on the [Novikov conjecture](wikipedia://en/3035194) and the [Baum–Connes conjecture](wikipedia://en/11774498) and the development and study of related group-theoretic notions such as topological amenability, asymptotic dimension, uniform embeddability into [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932), rapid decay property, and so on (see e.g.[^o39][^o40][^o41]).
- Interactions with the theory of quasiconformal analysis on metric spaces, particularly in relation to [Cannon's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon's_conjecture) about characterization of hyperbolic groups with [Gromov boundary](wikipedia://en/41160090) homeomorphic to the 2-sphere.[^o42][^o43][^o44]
- [Finite subdivision rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_subdivision_rules), also in relation to [Cannon's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon's_conjecture).[^o45]
- Interactions with [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713) in the contexts of studying actions of discrete groups on various compact spaces and group compactifications, particularly [convergence group](wikipedia://en/52297304) methods[^o46][^o47]
- Development of the theory of group actions on [$\mathbb R$-trees](wikipedia://en/371914) (particularly the [Rips machine](wikipedia://en/31275945)), and its applications.[^o48]
- The study of group actions on [CAT(0) spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) and CAT(0) cubical complexes,[^n12] motivated by ideas from Alexandrov geometry.
- Interactions with low-dimensional topology and hyperbolic geometry, particularly the study of 3-manifold groups,[^n13] [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295) of surfaces, [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138) and [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652).
- Introduction of probabilistic methods to study algebraic properties of "random" group theoretic objects (groups, group elements, subgroups, etc.). A particularly important development here is the work of Gromov who used probabilistic methods to prove[^o51] the existence of a finitely generated group that is not uniformly embeddable into a Hilbert space. Other notable developments include introduction and study of the notion of [generic-case complexity](wikipedia://en/24731030)[^o52] for group-theoretic and other mathematical algorithms and algebraic rigidity results for generic groups.[^o53]
- The study of [automata groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automata_group) and [iterated monodromy groups](wikipedia://en/4649761) as [groups of automorphisms](wikipedia://en/56661772) of infinite rooted trees. In particular, [Grigorchuk's groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigorchuk's_group) of intermediate growth, and their generalizations, appear in this context.[^o54][^o55]
- The study of measure-theoretic properties of group actions on [measure spaces](wikipedia://en/45270), particularly introduction and development of the notions of [measure equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_equivalence) and [orbit equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_equivalence), as well as measure-theoretic generalizations of Mostow rigidity.[^o56][^o57]
- The study of unitary representations of discrete groups and [Kazhdan's property (T)](wikipedia://en/1362724)[^o58]
- The study of *Out*(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) (the [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) of rank *n*) and of individual automorphisms of free groups. Introduction and the study of Culler-Vogtmann's [outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_space_%28group_theory%29)[^o59] and of the theory of [train tracks](wikipedia://en/1277396)[^o60] for free group automorphisms played a particularly prominent role here.
- Development of [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708), particularly various accessibility results[^o61][^o62][^o63] and the theory of tree lattices.[^o64] Generalizations of Bass–Serre theory such as the theory of complexes of groups.[^n12]
- The study of [random walks](wikipedia://en/235451) on groups and related boundary theory, particularly the notion of [Poisson boundary](wikipedia://en/51465170) (see e.g.[^o66]). The study of [amenability](wikipedia://en/657430) and of groups whose amenability status is still unknown.
- Interactions with finite group theory, particularly progress in the study of [subgroup growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroup_growth).[^o67]
- Studying subgroups and lattices in [linear groups](wikipedia://en/3983656), such as $SL(n, \mathbb R)$, and of other Lie groups, via geometric methods (e.g. [buildings](wikipedia://en/275015)), [algebro-geometric](wikipedia://en/1997) tools (e.g. [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) and representation varieties), analytic methods (e.g. unitary representations on Hilbert spaces) and arithmetic methods.
- [Group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635), using algebraic and topological methods, particularly involving interaction with [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and the use of [morse-theoretic](wikipedia://en/348560) ideas in the combinatorial context; large-scale, or coarse (see e.g.[^o68]) homological and cohomological methods.
- Progress on traditional combinatorial group theory topics, such as the [Burnside problem](wikipedia://en/151452),[^o69][^o70] the study of [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) and [Artin groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_group), and so on (the methods used to study these questions currently are often geometric and topological).
