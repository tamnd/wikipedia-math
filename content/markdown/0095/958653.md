# Michel Hénon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Hénon"}

**Michel Hénon** (; 23 July 1931, [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) – 7 April 2013, [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice)) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).[^o3] He worked for a long time at the [Nice Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice_Observatory).

:::

In astronomy, Hénon is well known for his contributions to [stellar dynamics](wikipedia://en/456270). In the late 1960s and early 1970s he made important contributions on the dynamical evolution of star clusters, in particular [globular clusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globular_cluster). He developed a numerical technique using [Monte Carlo methods](wikipedia://en/56098) to follow the dynamical evolution of a spherical star cluster much faster than the so-called [*n*-body methods](wikipedia://en/23859945).

In mathematics, he is well known for the [Hénon map](wikipedia://en/485467), a simple discrete [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) that exhibits [chaotic behavior](wikipedia://en/6295). He published a two-volume work on the [restricted three-body problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_three-body_problem).

In 1978 he was awarded the *[Prix Jean Ricard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Jean_Ricard)*.

The [European Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Space_Agency)'s *[HENON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HENON)* spacecraft is named after him and will use a type of orbit he developed.[^o4]
