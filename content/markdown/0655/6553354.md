# Tightness of measures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tightness of measures"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **tightness** is a concept in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory). The intuitive idea is that a given collection of measures does not "escape to [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824)".

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $(X, T)$ be a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637), and let $\Sigma$ be a [σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_algebra) on $X$ that contains the topology $T$. (Thus, every [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358) of $X$ is a [measurable set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set) and $\Sigma$ is at least as fine as the [Borel σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_sigma_algebra) on $X$.) Let $M$ be a collection of (possibly [signed](wikipedia://en/2985811) or [complex](wikipedia://en/3057532)) measures defined on $\Sigma$. The collection $M$ is called **tight** (or sometimes **uniformly tight**) if, for any $\varepsilon > 0$, there is a [compact subset](wikipedia://en/6042) $K_{\varepsilon}$ of $X$ such that, for all measures $\mu \in M$,

$$|\mu| (X \setminus K_{\varepsilon}) < \varepsilon.$$

where $|\mu|$ is the [total variation measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_variation_measure) of $\mu$. Very often, the measures in question are [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437), so the last part can be written as

$$\mu (K_{\varepsilon}) > 1 - \varepsilon. \,$$

If a tight collection $M$ consists of a single measure $\mu$, then (depending upon the author) $\mu$ may either be said to be a **tight measure** or to be an **[inner regular measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_regular_measure)**.

If $Y$ is an $X$-valued [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) whose [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on $X$ is a tight measure then $Y$ is said to be a **separable random variable** or a **Radon random variable**.

Another equivalent criterion of the tightness of a collection $M$ is sequential weak compactness. We say the family $M$ of probability measures is sequentially weakly compact if for every sequence $\left\{\mu_n\right\}$ from the family, there is a subsequence of measures that converges weakly to some probability measure $\mu$. It can be shown that a family of measures is tight if and only if it is sequentially weakly compact.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### Compact spaces {#Compact_spaces}

If $X$ is a [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable) [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042), then every collection of (possibly complex) measures on $X$ is tight. This is not necessarily so for non-metrisable compact spaces. If we take $[0,\omega_1]$ with its [order topology](wikipedia://en/213650), then there exists a measure $\mu$ on it that is not inner regular. Therefore, the singleton $\{\mu\}$ is not tight.

### Polish spaces {#Polish_spaces}

If $X$ is a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622), then every finite measure on $X$ is tight; this is Ulam's theorem. Furthermore, by [Prokhorov's theorem](wikipedia://en/6553549), a collection of probability measures on $X$ is tight if and only if it is [precompact](wikipedia://en/596600) in the topology of [weak convergence](wikipedia://en/6811795).

### A collection of point masses {#A_collection_of_point_masses}

Consider the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) $\mathbb{R}$ with its usual Borel topology. Let $\delta_{x}$ denote the [Dirac measure](wikipedia://en/1019002), a unit mass at the point $x$ in $\mathbb{R}$. The collection

$$M_{1} := \{ \delta_{n} \mid n \in \mathbb{N} \}$$

is not tight, since the compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are precisely the [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) and [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) subsets, and any such set, since it is bounded, has $\delta_{n}$-measure zero for large enough $n$. On the other hand, the collection

$$M_{2} := \{ \delta_{1 / n} \mid n \in \mathbb{N} \}$$

is tight: the compact interval $[0, 1]$ will work as $K_{\varepsilon}$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$. In general, a collection of Dirac delta measures on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is tight if, and only if, the collection of their [supports](wikipedia://en/6591796) is bounded.

### A collection of Gaussian measures {#A_collection_of_Gaussian_measures}

Consider $n$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with its usual Borel topology and σ-algebra. Consider a collection of [Gaussian measures](wikipedia://en/6572909)

$$\Gamma = \{ \gamma_{i} \mid i \in I \},$$

where the measure $\gamma_{i}$ has [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) ([mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean)) $m_{i} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ and [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) $C_{i} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. Then the collection $\Gamma$ is tight if, and only if, the collections $\{ m_{i} \mid i \in I \} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $\{ C_{i} \mid i \in I \} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ are both bounded.

:::

## Tightness and convergence {#Tightness_and_convergence}

Tightness is often a necessary criterion for proving the [weak convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures) of a sequence of probability measures, especially when the measure space has [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398). See

- [Finite-dimensional distribution](wikipedia://en/6594303)
- [Prokhorov's theorem](wikipedia://en/6553549)
- [Lévy–Prokhorov metric](wikipedia://en/6789891)
- [Weak convergence of measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures)
- [Tightness in classical Wiener space](wikipedia://en/6810363#Tightness_in_classical_Wiener_space)
- [Tightness in Skorokhod space](wikipedia://en/30862839#Tightness_in_Skorokhod_space)

## Tightness and stochastic ordering {#Tightness_and_stochastic_ordering}

A family of real-valued random variables $\{X_i\}_{i \in I}$ is tight if and only if there exists an almost surely finite random variable $X$ such that $|X_i| \le_\mathrm{st} X$ for all $i \in I$, where $\le_\mathrm{st}$ denotes the [stochastic order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_order) defined by $A \le_\mathrm{st} B$ if $\mathbb{E}[ \phi(A)] \le \mathbb{E}[ \phi(B)]$ for all nondecreasing functions $\phi$. [^o12]

## Exponential tightness {#Exponential_tightness}

A strengthening of tightness is the concept of exponential tightness, which has applications in [large deviations theory](wikipedia://en/3772909). A family of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) $(\mu_{\delta})_{\delta > 0}$ on a [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) topological space $X$ is said to be **exponentially tight** if, for any $\varepsilon > 0$, there is a compact subset $K_{\varepsilon}$ of $X$ such that

$$\limsup_{\delta \downarrow 0} \delta \log \mu_{\delta} (X \setminus K_{\varepsilon}) < - \varepsilon.$$
