# Prokhorov's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prokhorov's theorem"}

In [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) **Prokhorov's theorem** relates [tightness of measures](wikipedia://en/6553354) to relative [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness) (and hence [weak convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures)) in the space of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437). It is credited to the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) mathematician [Yuri Vasilyevich Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Vasilevich_Prokhorov), who considered probability measures on complete separable metric spaces. The term "Prokhorov’s theorem" is also applied to later generalizations to either the direct or the inverse statements.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $(S, \rho)$ be a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). Let $\mathcal{P}(S)$ denote the collection of all probability measures defined on $S$ (with its [Borel σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_sigma_algebra)).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

1. If a collection $K\subset \mathcal{P}(S)$ of probability measures is [tight](wikipedia://en/6553354), then the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of $K$ is [sequentially compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequentially_compact) in the space $\mathcal{P}(S)$ equipped with the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [weak convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures).
1. The space $\mathcal{P}(S)$ with the topology of weak convergence is [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable).
1. Suppose that in addition, $(S,\rho)$ is a [complete metric space](wikipedia://en/7832) (so that $(S,\rho)$ is a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622)). Then the converse of (1) also holds; hence $K\subset \mathcal{P}(S)$ is tight if and only if its closure is sequentially compact. Moreover, there exists a complete metric $d_0$ on $\mathcal{P}(S)$ equivalent to the topology of weak convergence, and for this metric $K\subset \mathcal{P}(S)$ is tight if and only if its closure in $(\mathcal{P}(S),d_0)$ is compact.

:::

:::

## Corollaries {#Corollaries}

For Euclidean spaces we have that:

- If $(\mu_n)$ is a tight [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) in $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^m)$ (the collection of probability measures on $m$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697)), then there exist a [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098) $(\mu_{n_k})$ and a probability measure $\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^m)$ such that $\mu_{n_k}$ converges weakly to $\mu$.
- If $(\mu_n)$ is a tight sequence in $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^m)$ such that every weakly convergent subsequence $(\mu_{n_k})$ has the same limit $\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^m)$, then the sequence $(\mu_n)$ converges weakly to $\mu$.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extension"}

## Extension {#Extension}

Prokhorov's theorem can be extended to consider [complex measures](wikipedia://en/3057532) or finite [signed measures](wikipedia://en/2985811).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Suppose that $(S,\rho)$ is a complete separable metric space and $\Pi$ is a family of Borel complex measures on $S$. The following statements are equivalent:

- $\Pi$ is sequentially precompact; that is, every sequence $\{\mu_n\}\subset\Pi$ has a weakly convergent subsequence.
- $\Pi$ is tight and uniformly bounded in [total variation norm](wikipedia://en/683561#Total_variation_in_measure_theory).

:::

:::

## Comments {#Comments}

Since Prokhorov's theorem expresses tightness in terms of compactness, the [Arzelà–Ascoli theorem](wikipedia://en/596622) is often used to substitute for compactness: in function spaces, this leads to a characterization of tightness in terms of the [modulus of continuity](wikipedia://en/964161) or an appropriate analogue—see [tightness in classical Wiener space](wikipedia://en/6810363#Tightness_in_classical_Wiener_space) and [tightness in Skorokhod space](wikipedia://en/30862839#Tightness_in_Skorokhod_space).

There are several deep and non-trivial extensions to Prokhorov's theorem. However, those results do not overshadow the importance and the relevance to applications of the original result.
