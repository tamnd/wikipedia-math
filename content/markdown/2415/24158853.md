# Overring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Overring"}

In mathematics, an **overring** of an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) contains the integral domain, and the integral domain's [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) contains the overring. Overrings provide an improved understanding of different types of rings and [domains](wikipedia://en/487627).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In this article, all [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) are [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), and ring and overring share the same [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962).

Let $Q(A)$ represent the field of fractions of an integral domain $A$. Ring $B$ is an overring of integral domain $A$ if $A$ is a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of $B$ and $B$ is a subring of the field of fractions $Q(A)$;[^n1] the relationship is $A \subseteq B \subseteq Q(A)$.[^n2]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Ring of fractions {#Ring_of_fractions}

The rings $R_{A},S_{A},T_{A}$ are the [rings of fractions](wikipedia://en/4276418) of rings $R,S,T$ by [multiplicative set](wikipedia://en/252216) $A$.[^n3] Assume $T$ is an overring of $R$ and $A$ is a multiplicative set in $R$. The ring $T_{A}$ is an overring of $R_{A}$. The ring $T_{A}$ is the [total ring of fractions](wikipedia://en/4276418) of $R_{A}$ if every [nonunit](wikipedia://en/289666) element of $T_{A}$ is a zero-divisor.[^n4] Every overring of $R_{A}$ contained in $T_{A}$ is a ring $S_{A}$, and $S$ is an overring of $R$.[^n4] Ring $R_{A}$ is [integrally closed](wikipedia://en/9478630) in $T_{A}$ if $R$ is integrally closed in $T$.[^n4]

### Noetherian domain {#Noetherian_domain}

::: {#o15 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

#### Definitions {#Definitions}

A [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) satisfies the 3 equivalent [finitenss](wikipedia://en/11742) conditions i) every [ascending chain](wikipedia://en/3189) of [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) is finite, ii) every non-empty family of ideals has a [maximal element](wikipedia://en/303398) and iii) every ideal has a [finite basis](wikipedia://en/13733).[^n3]

An [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) is a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) if every ideal of the domain is a finite product of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928).[^n3]

A ring's restricted [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) is the maximum [rank](wikipedia://en/56079) among the ranks of all prime ideals that contain a regular element.[^n4]

A ring $R$ is [locally](wikipedia://en/153391) [nilpotentfree](wikipedia://en/252235) if every ring $R_{M}$ with [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) $M$ is free of nilpotent elements or a ring with every nonunit a [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441).[^n4]

An [affine ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_ring) is the [homomorphic](wikipedia://en/13564) [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) (a [finitely generated algebra](wikipedia://en/11985661)) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603).[^n4]

:::

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

Every overring of a Dedekind ring is a Dedekind ring.[^n5][^n6]

Every overrring of a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of rings whose non-unit elements are all zero-divisors is a Noetherian ring.[^n4]

Every overring of a [Krull](wikipedia://en/56079) 1-dimensional Noetherian domain is a Noetherian ring.[^n4]

These statements are equivalent for Noetherian ring $R$ with integral closure $\bar{R}$.[^n4]

- Every overring of $R$ is a Noetherian ring.
- For each maximal ideal $M$ of $R$, every overring of $R_{M}$ is a Noetherian ring.
- Ring $R$ is locally nilpotentfree with restricted dimension 1 or less.
- Ring $\bar{R}$ is Noetherian, and ring $R$ has restricted dimension 1 or less.
- Every overring of $\bar{R}$ is integrally closed.

These statements are equivalent for affine ring $R$ with integral closure $\bar{R}$.[^n4]

- Ring $R$ is locally nilpotentfree.
- Ring $\bar{R}$ is a finite $R$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410).
- Ring $\bar{R}$ is Noetherian.

An integrally closed local ring $R$ is an integral domain or a ring whose non-unit elements are all zero-divisors.[^n4]

A Noetherian integral domain is a Dedekind ring if every overring of the Noetherian ring is integrally closed.[^n7]

Every overring of a Noetherian integral domain is a ring of fractions if the Noetherian integral domain is a Dedekind ring with a torsion class group.[^n7]

:::

### Coherent rings {#Coherent_rings}

::: {#o34 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

#### Definitions {#Definitions}

A [coherent ring](wikipedia://en/14777654) is a commutative ring with each [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/251424) ideal [finitely presented](wikipedia://en/251424).[^n2] Noetherian domains and [Prüfer domains](wikipedia://en/12367835) are coherent.[^n8]

A pair $(R,T)$ indicates an integral domain [extension](wikipedia://en/251424) of $T$ over $R$.[^n9]

Ring $S$ is an intermediate domain for pair $(R,T)$ if $R$ is a subdomain of $S$ and $S$ is a subdomain of $T$.[^n9]

:::

::: {#o40 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

A Noetherian ring's Krull dimension is 1 or less if every overring is coherent.[^n2]

For integral domain pair $(R,T)$, $T$ is an overring of $R$ if each intermediate integral domain is integrally closed in $T$.[^n9][^n10]

The integral closure of $R$ is a Prüfer domain if each [proper](wikipedia://en/27631) overring of $R$ is coherent.[^n8]

The overrings of Prüfer domains and Krull 1-dimensional Noetherian domains are coherent.[^n8]

:::

### Prüfer domains {#Prüfer_domains}

::: {#o48 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

A ring has QR property if every overring is a localization with a multiplicative set.[^n11] The QR domains are Prüfer domains.[^n11] A Prüfer domain with a torsion [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) is a QR domain.[^n11] A Prüfer domain is a QR domain if the [radical](wikipedia://en/372242) of every finitely generated ideal equals the radical generated by a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126).[^n12]

The statement $R$ is a [Prüfer domain](wikipedia://en/12367835) is equivalent to:[^n13]

- Each overring of $R$ is the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of localizations of $R$, and $R$ is integrally closed.
- Each overring of $R$ is the intersection of rings of fractions of $R$, and $R$ is integrally closed.
- Each overring of $R$ has prime ideals that are extensions of the prime ideals of $R$, and $R$ is integrally closed.
- Each overring of $R$ has at most 1 prime ideal lying over any prime ideal of $R$, and $R$ is integrally closed
- Each overring of $R$ is integrally closed.
- Each overring of $R$ is coherent.

The statement $R$ is a [Prüfer domain](wikipedia://en/12367835) is equivalent to:[^n1]

- Each overring $S$ of $R$ is [flat](wikipedia://en/276410) as a $S$-module.
- Each [valuation overring](wikipedia://en/966856) of $R$ is a ring of fractions.

:::

### Minimal overring {#Minimal_overring}

::: {#o57 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

#### Definitions {#Definitions}

A minimal ring homomorphism $f$ is an [injective](wikipedia://en/45196) non-[surjective](wikipedia://en/27873) homomorophism, and if the homomorphism $f$ is a composition of homomorphisms $g$ and $h$ then $g$ or $h$ is an isomorphism.[^n14]

A proper minimal ring extension $T$ of subring $R$ occurs if the ring [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29) of $R$ in to $T$ is a minimal ring homomorphism. This implies the ring pair $(R,T)$ has no proper intermediate ring.[^n15]

A minimal overring $T$ of ring $R$ occurs if $T$ contains $R$ as a subring, and the ring pair $(R,T)$ has no proper intermediate ring.[^n16]

The Kaplansky ideal transform (Hayes transform, S-transform) of ideal $I$ with respect to integral domain $R$ is a subset of the fraction field $Q(R)$. This subset contains elements $x$ such that for each element $y$ of the ideal $I$ there is a positive integer $n$ with the product $x \cdot y^{n}$ contained in integral domain $R$.[^n17][^n16]

:::

::: {#o64 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

Any domain generated from a minimal ring extension of domain $R$ is an overring of $R$ if $R$ is not a field.[^n17][^n15]

The field of fractions of $R$ contains minimal overring $T$ of $R$ when $R$ is not a field.[^n16]

Assume an integrally closed integral domain $R$ is not a field, If a minimal overring of integral domain $R$ exists, this minimal overring occurs as the Kaplansky transform of a maximal ideal of $R$.[^n16]

:::

::: {#o70 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [Bézout integral domain](wikipedia://en/3131407) is a type of Prüfer domain; the Bézout domain's defining property is every finitely generated ideal is a principal ideal. The Bézout domain will share all the overring properties of a Prüfer domain.[^n1]

The integer ring is a Prüfer ring, and all overrings are rings of quotients.[^n7] The [dyadic rational](wikipedia://en/56263) is a fraction with an integer numerator and power of 2 denominators. The dyadic rational ring is the localization of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) by powers of two and an overring of the integer ring.

:::
