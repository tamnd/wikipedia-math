# Beverly Anderson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beverly Anderson"}

**Beverly Jacques Anderson** (born September 10, 1943) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) professor at the [University of the District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_District_of_Columbia). In the 1990s she worked at the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) as Director of Minority Programs for the Mathematical Sciences Education Board, and led the *Making Mathematics Work for Minorities* program.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Anderson grew up in the [7th Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Ward_of_New_Orleans) of [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans) in a time of [segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segregated_school).[^o4] She was born to Alvin Joseph and Dorothy Ann, who never finished high school.[^o5][^o6] Anderson attended [Valena C. Jones School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valena_C._Jones_School), an elementary school.[^o7] She attended a high school for African-Americans, where she excelled in mathematics.[^o4] She earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics at [Dillard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dillard_University). She was the first member of her family to leave [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans) when she moved to [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University) for graduate school.[^o6] She completed a PhD at the [Catholic University of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_America) in 1978.[^o8]

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

Anderson began teaching mathematics at the [University of the District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_District_of_Columbia) in 1969.[^o10] She was awarded one of the faculty awards from the White House Initiative for Historically Black Colleges and Universities.[^o11] She took a leave of absence to join the National Research Council of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1988, where she launched the *Making Mathematics Work for Minorities* program*.*[^o12][^o13] The program had three aims: to attract national attention to the need for minority students in mathematics, to identify best practice in mathematics education for minority students and develop a national strategy and alliance to improve mathematics education.[^o12] She ran a series of workshops around the country, sharing ideas about how to help minorities excel in mathematics.[^o14] Anderson argued that teaching mathematics without acknowledging the cultural bias that favoured students aligned with European tradition could significantly disadvantage people of colour.[^o15]

Anderson identified that minority students enrolled in two-year college programs were 20% less likely to earn a bachelor's degree than those who started in four-year programs.[^o16] She emphasized that school mathematics programs needed to be maintained long-term, for magnet schools to support the teaching profession and for comprehensive four-year college programs.[^o17]

In her 40-year career at the University of the District of Columbia, Anderson was a Professor of Mathematics, Mathematics Department Chair, and Dean of the College of Arts and Sciences. She was made Provost and Vice President of Academic Affairs in 1997;[^o18][^o19] however her appointment as provost was rescinded in 1999 after it was deemed to have been made without a proper search.[^o20] She remained as a consultant on the National Academy of Sciences mathematics education program, serving as a consultant for the Everybody Counts report in 2000.[^o21] In 2013 Anderson was appointed to the [Prince George's County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_George's_County,_Maryland) School Board.[^o18]
