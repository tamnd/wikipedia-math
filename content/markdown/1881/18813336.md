# Szpiro's conjecture

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Modified Szpiro conjecture"}

**Modified Szpiro conjecture.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Szpiro's conjecture"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Szpiro's conjecture** relates the [conductor](wikipedia://en/43604164) of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) to its discriminant. In a slightly modified form, it is equivalent to the well-known [*abc* conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108). It is named for [Lucien Szpiro](wikipedia://en/26992384), who formulated it in the 1980s.

:::

Szpiro's conjecture and its equivalent forms have been described as "the most important unsolved problem in [Diophantine analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_analysis)",[^o3] in part due to its large number of consequences in number theory including [Roth's theorem](wikipedia://en/787853), [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199),[^o4] the [Fermat–Catalan conjecture](wikipedia://en/22736152), and [Brocard's problem](wikipedia://en/11301521).[^o5][^o6][^o7]

## Original statement {#Original_statement}

The conjecture states that given $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a constant $C(\varepsilon)$ such that for any elliptic curve $E$ defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ with minimal discriminant $\Delta$ and conductor $f$,

$$\vert\Delta\vert \leq C(\varepsilon ) \cdot f^{6+\varepsilon}.$$

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Modified Szpiro conjecture"}

## Modified Szpiro conjecture {#Modified_Szpiro_conjecture}

The **modified Szpiro conjecture** states that: given $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a constant $C(\varepsilon)$ such that for any elliptic curve $E$ defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ with invariants $c_4$, $c_6$ and conductor $f$ (using [notation from Tate's algorithm](wikipedia://en/14795246#Notation)),

$$\max\{|c_4|^3, |c_6|^2\} \leq C(\varepsilon) \cdot f^{6+\varepsilon}.$$

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="abc conjecture"}

### *abc* conjecture {#abc_conjecture}

The [*abc* conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108) originated as the outcome of attempts by [Joseph Oesterlé](wikipedia://en/1604631) and [David Masser](wikipedia://en/2172777) to understand Szpiro's conjecture,[^o13] and was then shown to be equivalent to the modified Szpiro's conjecture.[^o14]

:::

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

Szpiro's conjecture and its modified form are known to imply several important mathematical results and conjectures, including [Roth's theorem](wikipedia://en/787853),[^o16] [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199),[^o17] the [Fermat–Catalan conjecture](wikipedia://en/22736152),[^o18] and a negative solution to the [Erdős–Ulam problem](wikipedia://en/52483865).[^o19]

## Claimed proofs {#Claimed_proofs}

In August 2012, [Shinichi Mochizuki](wikipedia://en/36940578) claimed a proof of Szpiro's conjecture by developing a new theory called [inter-universal Teichmüller theory](wikipedia://en/36968172) (IUTT).[^o21] However, the papers have not been accepted by the mathematical community as providing a proof of the conjecture,[^o22][^o23][^o24] with [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306) and [Jakob Stix](wikipedia://en/58556057) concluding in March 2018 that they had issues "so severe that … small modifications will not rescue the proof strategy".[^o25][^o26][^o27]
