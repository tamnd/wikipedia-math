# Andrew Munro (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrew Munro"}

**Andrew Munro**, M.A., (6 July 1869 – 1 July 1935) was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_culture) lecturer in mathematics, Vice President, Bursar, Steward and Senior Fellow of [Queens' College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens'_College,_Cambridge) for 45 years from 1893 to 1935. The Munro scholarships and studentships at Queens' College, Cambridge are named in his honour.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Andrew Munro was born 6 July 1869, in Rosskeen, [Ross and Cromarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_and_Cromarty), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), the son of Andrew Munro and Margaret Small of [Invergordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invergordon) in Ross and Cromarty.[^o3] His father was a banker, mill owner and farmer,[^o4] who also served as [Chief Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Magistrate) for [Invergordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invergordon) and [Justice of the Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_of_the_Peace) for Ross and Cromarty.[^o5] The Munros were members of the [Clan Munro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan_Munro). His mother was the daughter of John Small (1797–1847), under Librarian of the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), and the sister of [John Small (1828–1886)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Small_%28librarian%29), who succeeded his father as under Librarian and later was appointed Librarian. Munro's mother and family were members of the Smalls of [Dirnanean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirnanean_House), Perthshire, Scotland.

## Academics {#Academics}

Munro initially attended [Aberdeen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_University), Scotland, later transferring to Cambridge.[^o3] He won a foundation scholarship to [Queens' College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens'_College,_Cambridge) in 1890, and two years later was designated as the fourth [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29).[^o3]

In 1892, Munro received his Bachelor of Arts degree, followed by a [Masters of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_of_Arts) degree in 1896.[^o7] In 1893, he was elected a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) at Queens' College, Cambridge.[^o3] For the next 20 years he served as a lecturer, director of studies, and supervisor in mathematics at the college.[^o3]

In 1913, Munro became [Bursar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursar) of Queens' College, Cambridge.[^o3] In this role, Munro advised the college to dispose of most of its farmland after [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) and invest in government stocks, which significantly increased the college's [endowments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_endowment).[^o3][^o10]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Andrew Munro died on 1 July 1935 at Dormy House Hotel in [Sheringham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheringham), Norfolk, England.[^o9] He was buried in St. Giles Cemetery in Cambridge, now referred to as [Parish of the Ascension Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground,_Cambridge), on 4 July 1935.[^o10]

A portion of Munro's estate, upwards of £26,000, was left to Queens' College, Cambridge to fund scholarships in mathematics and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939).[^o11] These scholarships and studentships are named in Munro's honour.

The Munro Room at Queens' College, Cambridge which faces both [Old Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens'_College,_Cambridge#Old_Court) and [Walnut Tree Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens'_College,_Cambridge#Walnut_Tree_Court), is named for Munro. A portrait of Munro, painted by [Arthur Trevor Haddon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Trevor_Haddon), appropriately hangs in the room.
