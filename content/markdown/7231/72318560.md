# Hayato Chiba

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hayato Chiba"}

**Hayato Chiba** (千葉 逸人, born 18 January 1982) is a Japanese mathematician and professor at the Advanced Institute for Materials Research of [Tōhoku University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tōhoku_University).[^o2] He mainly concerns himself with the theory of dynamical systems, especially [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042), and proved the [Kuramoto conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuramoto_Model) about infinite-dimensional differential equations with [chaotic](wikipedia://en/6295) behavior.[^o3] He obtained his doctorate degree in information sciences at the [University of Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kyoto).

:::

## Education and Work {#Education_and_Work}

Chiba studied at [Kyōto University's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyōto_University) engineering department, where he graduated in 2005. He continued on to pursue a doctorate degree, studying mainly analytic mechanics and non-linear differential equations until 2009, also at the University of Kyōto.

In the third year of his undergraduate studies, Chiba published a textbook on mathematical methods in engineering with Pleiades Publishing.[^o5] A textbook about analytic geometry soon followed.

Starting from 2013, he worked at [Kyūshū University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyūshū_University)'s research institute Mathematics for Industry as an untenured professor.[^o6] During this time, he finalised and submitted his proof for the Kuramoto conjecture, which was then published in [Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_Theory_and_Dynamical_Systems) in 2015.

2017, he appeared in a pop science TV show moderated by [Naoki Matayoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoki_Matayoshi).[^o7]

Following this, in 2019, he followed a call to [Tōhoku University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tōhoku_University), where he is currently working as a professor of mathematics [^o8]
