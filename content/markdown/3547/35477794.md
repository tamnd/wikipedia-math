# Bring's curve

*Image: Bring sextic with dual graphs.svg.* The [fundamental polygon](wikipedia://en/1668732) for Bring's curve is a regular hyperbolic [icosagon](wikipedia://en/445032) (20-gon), shown here with [dodecadodecahedral](wikipedia://en/4157379) graph in green and [its dual](wikipedia://en/20823052) in violet. It is a [quotient](wikipedia://en/237213) of the [order-4 pentagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/9421111) and its dual [square tiling](wikipedia://en/9421082).20-gon edges marked with the same letter are equal.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bring's curve"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Bring's curve** (also called **Bring's surface** and, by analogy with the [Klein quartic](wikipedia://en/390976), **the Bring sextic**) is a highly-symmetric [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181). It can be defined as an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) using five [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) as [projective coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_coordinates), as the [space curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve) in the four-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbb{P}_4(\mathbb{C})$ cut out by the homogeneous equations

$$v+w+x+y+z=v^2+w^2+x^2+y^2+z^2=v^3+w^3+x^3+y^3+z^3=0.$$

It was named by (Klein 2003, p.157) after [Erland Samuel Bring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erland_Samuel_Bring) who studied a similar construction in 1786 in a Promotionschrift submitted to the [University of Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lund). Note that the roots *x*<sub>i</sub> of the [Bring quintic](wikipedia://en/3191883) $x^5+ax+b = 0$ satisfies Bring's curve since $\sum_{i=1}^5 x_i^k = 0$ for $k = 1,2,3.$

:::

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the curve is the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>5</sub> of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) 120, given by [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of the 5 coordinates. This is the largest possible automorphism group of a genus 4 complex curve.

The curve can be realized as a [triple cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branched_cover) of the sphere branched in 12 points, and is the [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) associated to the [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843). It has genus 4. The full group of symmetries (including reflections) is the direct product $S_{5}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}$, which has order 240.

The curve may also be modelled by the singular plane curve[^o4] in $\mathbb{P}^2$

$$X(Y^5 + Z^5) + (XYZ)^2 - X^4YZ - 2(YZ)^3=0.$$

This form was used by Hulek[^o5] and Craig[^o6] to study modular properties of the curve.

## Fundamental domain and systole {#Fundamental_domain_and_systole}

Bring's curve can be obtained as a Riemann surface by associating sides of a hyperbolic [icosagon](wikipedia://en/445032) (see [fundamental polygon](wikipedia://en/1668732)). The identification pattern is given in the adjoining diagram. The icosagon (of area $12\pi$, by the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Bonnet_theorem)) can be tessellated by 240 (2,4,5) triangles. The actions that transport one of these triangles to another give the full group of automorphisms of the surface (including reflections). Discounting reflections, we get the 120 automorphisms mentioned in the introduction. Note that 120 is less than 252, the maximum number of orientation preserving automorphisms allowed for a genus 4 surface, by [Hurwitz's automorphism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz's_automorphism_theorem). Therefore, Bring's surface is not a [Hurwitz surface](wikipedia://en/12807841). This also tells us that there does not exist a Hurwitz surface of genus 4.

The full group of symmetries has the following presentation:

$$\langle r,\,s,\,t\,|\,r^5=s^2=t^2=rtrt=stst=(rs)^{4}=(sr^{3}sr^{2})^{2}=e\rangle,$$

where $e$ is the identity action, $r$ is a rotation of order 5 about the centre of the fundamental polygon, $s$ is a rotation of order 2 at the vertex where 4 (2,4,5) triangles meet in the tessellation, and $t$ is reflection in the real line. From this presentation, information about the linear [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of the symmetry group of Bring's surface can be computed using [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28computer_algebra_system%29). In particular, the group has four 1 dimensional, four 4 dimensional, four 5 dimensional, and two 6 dimensional irreducible representations, and we have

$$4(1^2)+4(4^2)+4(5^2)+2(6^2)=4+64+100+72=240$$

as expected.

The [systole](wikipedia://en/2545455) of the surface has length

$$12\sinh^{-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{5}-1)}\right)\approx4.60318$$

and multiplicity 20, a geodesic loop of that length consisting of the concatenated altitudes of twelve of the 240 (2,4,5) triangles. Similarly to the [Klein quartic](wikipedia://en/390976), Bring's surface does not maximize the systole length among compact Riemann surfaces in its topological category (that is, surfaces having the same genus) despite maximizing the size of the automorphism group. The systole is presumably maximized by the surface referred to as M4 in (Schmutz 1993). The systole length of M4 is

$$2\cosh^{-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(5+3\sqrt{3})\right)\approx4.6245,$$

and has multiplicity 36.

## Spectral theory {#Spectral_theory}

Little is known about the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of Bring's surface, however, it could potentially be of interest in this field. The [Bolza surface](wikipedia://en/12576332) and Klein quartic have the largest symmetry groups among compact Riemann surfaces of constant negative curvature in genera 2 and 3 respectively, and thus it has been conjectured that they maximize the first positive eigenvalue in the Laplace spectrum. There is strong numerical evidence to support this hypothesis, particularly in the case of the Bolza surface, although providing a rigorous proof is still an open problem. Following this pattern, one may reasonably conjecture that Bring's surface maximizes the first positive eigenvalue of the Laplacian (among surfaces in its topological class).
