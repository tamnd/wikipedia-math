# Kalman's conjecture

*Image: Block scheme of control system.jpg.* Fig. 1. Block scheme of control system. *G*(*s*) – linear [transfer function](wikipedia://en/31146), *f*(*e*) – single-valued, continuous, differentiable function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kalman's conjecture"}

**Kalman's conjecture** or **Kalman problem** is a disproved [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) on absolute stability of [nonlinear control](wikipedia://en/3359147) system with one scalar nonlinearity, which belongs to the sector of [linear stability](wikipedia://en/3652079). Kalman's conjecture is a strengthening of [Aizerman's conjecture](wikipedia://en/18207705) and is a special case of [Markus–Yamabe conjecture](wikipedia://en/7726067). This conjecture was proven false but led to the (valid) [sufficient criteria on absolute stability](wikipedia://en/3359147#Absolute_stability_problem).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Mathematical statement of Kalman's conjecture (Kalman problem)"}

## Mathematical statement of Kalman's conjecture (Kalman problem) {#Mathematical_statement_of_Kalman's_conjecture_(Kalman_problem)}

In 1957 [R. E. Kalman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._E._Kalman) in his paper [^o4] stated the following:

> If *f*(*e*) in Fig. 1 is replaced by constants *K* corresponding to all possible values of *f*'(*e*), and it is found that the closed-loop system is stable for all such *K*, then it intuitively clear that the system must be monostable; i.e., all transient solutions will converge to a unique, stable critical point.

Kalman's statement can be reformulated in the following conjecture:[^o5]

> Consider a system with one scalar nonlinearity
>
> $\frac{dx}{dt}=Px+qf(e),\quad e=r^*x \quad x\in R^n,$ where *P* is a constant *n*×*n* matrix, *q*, *r* are constant *n*-dimensional vectors, ∗ is an operation of transposition, *f*(*e*) is scalar function, and *f*(0) = 0. Suppose, *f*(*e*) is a differentiable function and the following condition
>
> $k_1 < f'(e)< k_2. \,$ is valid. Then Kalman's conjecture is that the system is stable in the large (i.e. a unique stationary point is a global [attractor](wikipedia://en/191094)) if all linear systems with *f*(*e*) = *ke*, *k* ∈ (*k*<sub>1</sub>, *k*<sub>2</sub>) are asymptotically stable.

In [Aizerman's conjecture](wikipedia://en/18207705) in place of the condition on the derivative of nonlinearity it is required that the nonlinearity itself belongs to the linear sector.

Kalman's conjecture is true for *n* ≤ 3 and for *n* > 3 there are effective methods for construction of counterexamples:[^o6][^o7] the nonlinearity derivative belongs to the sector of linear stability, and a unique stable equilibrium coexists with a stable periodic solution ([hidden oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_oscillation)). In discrete-time, the Kalman conjecture is only true for n=1, counterexamples for *n* ≥ 2 can be constructed.[^o8][^o9]

The development of Kalman's ideas on global stability based on the stability of [linear approximation](wikipedia://en/1107596) for a cylindrical [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) gave rise to the [Viterbi problem on the coincidence of phase-locked loop ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Viterbi#Viterbi%27s_problem_on_the_coincidence_of_PLL_ranges).[^o10]
