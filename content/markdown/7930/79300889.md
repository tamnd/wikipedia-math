# Longuerre's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Longuerre's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), **Longuerre's theorem** is a result concerning the [collinearity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear) of points constructed from a [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131). It is a generalization of the [Simson line](wikipedia://en/3307757), which states that the three projections of a point on the circumcircle of a triangle to its sides are collinear.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

> **Longuerre's theorem.** Let $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ be a [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131), and let $P$ be an arbitrary point. For each triple of vertices, construct the [Simson line](wikipedia://en/3307757) of $P$ with respect to that triangle. Let $D_i$ be the [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) of $P$ onto the Simson line corresponding to the triangle formed by omitting vertex $A_i$. Then the four points $D_1, D_2, D_3, D_4$ are [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear).[^o5]

Longuerre's theorem can be generalized to [cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon) $n$-gons.[^o5]
