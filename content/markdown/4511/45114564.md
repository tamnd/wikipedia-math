# Charles Earl Rickart

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Earl Rickart"}

**Charles Earl Rickart** (1913 – 17 April 2002) was an American mathematician,[^o2] known for [Rickart spaces](wikipedia://en/30727109).

:::

Rickart was born in [Osage City, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osage_City,_Kansas), and earned his B.A. and M.A. from the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas). In 1941, he received his PhD from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) under [Theophil Henry Hildebrandt](wikipedia://en/36561562) with the thesis *Integration in a Convex Linear Topological Space*. Rickart was for two years the Benjamin Pierce Instructor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). He joined the [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) mathematics faculty in 1943, served as chair of the department from 1959 to 1965, became the first Percey F. Smith Professor of Mathematics in 1963, and retired in 1983.[^o2]

Rickart did research on [Banach algebras](wikipedia://en/4665) and was the author of three books. In the late 1950s and early 1960s, he was one of the pioneers in introducing the "[new math](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_math)" into American schools.[^o2] His doctoral students include [Samuel Merrill III](wikipedia://en/1680661).[^o3]

He died in [North Branford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Branford,_Connecticut) where he lived, survived by his wife, Annabel Erickson Rickart, three sons, and four grandchildren.[^o2]
