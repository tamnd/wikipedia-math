# Antoon Kolen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Antoon Kolen"}

**Anthonius Wilhelmus Johannes (Antoon) Kolen** (22 May 1953 – 3 October 2004) was a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Professor at the [Maastricht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_University), in the Department of Quantitative Economics. He is known for his work on [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297), such as [interval scheduling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_scheduling) and [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Tilburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilburg), Kolen obtained his engineering degree from the [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology) in 1978. In 1982, he obtained his PhD at the [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063), [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) under [Gijsbert de Leve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gijsbert_de_Leve) and [Jan Karel Lenstra](wikipedia://en/33685896) with the thesis, entitled "Location Problems on Trees and in the Rectilinear Plane."

After his graduation, Kolen started his academic career at the [Econometric Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Institute) of [Erasmus University Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_University_Rotterdam). Late 1980s he moved to the [Maastricht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_University), where he was appointed Professor at the Department of Quantitative Economics and head of its operations research group.[^o6]

His PhD students at the Erasmus University Rotterdam were Leo Kroon (graduated in 1990), [Albert Wagelmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Wagelmans) (1990), [C. Stan van Hoesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Stan_van_Hoesel) (1991), Wim Pijls (1991), Peter Verbeek (1991), and A. Woerlee (1991); W. Hennen at the Wageningen University and Research Centre (graduated in 1995), and at the Maastricht University Alwin Oerlemans (graduated in 1992), Ron van der Wal (1995), Maarten Oosten (1996), Jons van de Klundert (1996), Robert van de Leensel (1999), Arie Koster (1999), and Alexander Grigoriev (2003).[^o5]
