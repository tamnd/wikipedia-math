# Alexander Durie Russell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Durie Russell"}

**Alexander Durie Russell** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) [FRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society) (1872–1955) was a 20th-century Scottish mathematician, schoolmaster and amateur astronomer. He was President of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) 1915/16.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 22 July 1872, the son of Janet Durie and her husband, Thomas Russell, a grocer and spirit dealer.[^o3] The family lived at 19 Graham Street and had a shop at 42 West Richmond Street in the city's South Side.[^o4] Russell was educated at [George Heriot's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot's_School) then studied mathematics and natural philosophy (physics) at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) graduating with a BSc in 1896. While at University he was awarded a Neil Arnott Scholarship in Experimental Physics, and studied chemistry under [Alexander Crum Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Crum_Brown), mathematics with [George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803) and physics with [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait).[^o3]

On graduating he became a Demonstrator in physics at the University. He then taught, first at Morelands School in Edinburgh then in the summer of 1897 went to [Stranraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranraer) High School.[^o5] In 1899 he joined [Falkirk High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkirk_High_School) where he stayed for the rest of his career.[^o3]

In 1905 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [William Peddie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Peddie), [John Brown Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_Clark), [Robert Traill Omond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Traill_Omond), and [Cargill Gilston Knott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill_Gilston_Knott).[^o6]

He retired in 1937 and died on 20 January 1955.[^o5]
