# Bhargav Bhatt (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bhargav Bhatt"}

**Bhargav Bhatt** (born 1983[^o3]) is an Indian-American mathematician[^o4] who is the Fernholz Joint Professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and works in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990).[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Bhatt graduated with a B.S. in Applied Mathematics, *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude)* from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) under the supervision of [Shou-Wu Zhang](wikipedia://en/3582724).[^o7] He received his Ph.D. from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2010 under the supervision of [Aise Johan de Jong](wikipedia://en/29023548).[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Bhatt was a Postdoctoral Assistant Professor in mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) from 2010 to 2014 (on leave from 2012 to 2014).[^o7] Bhatt was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 2012 to 2014.[^o7][^o10] He then returned to the University of Michigan, serving as an associate professor from 2014 to 2015, a Gehring Associate Professor from 2015 to 2018, a Professor from 2018 to 2020, and a Frederick W and Lois B Gehring Professor since 2020.[^o7] In July 2022, he was appointed as the Fernholz Joint Professor in the School of Mathematics at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), with a joint appointment at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o5]

Bhatt is currently (July 2025) on the editorial board of the journal [Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785).

## Research {#Research}

Bhatt's research focuses on [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), especially on [*p*-adic cohomology](wikipedia://en/46927724).[^o10][^o16] Bhatt and [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306) have developed a theory of [prismatic cohomology](wikipedia://en/83781807), which has been described as progress towards [motivic cohomology](wikipedia://en/809314) by unifying [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology), [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530), [ℓ-adic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic_cohomology), and [crystalline cohomology](wikipedia://en/10220067).[^o12][^o13]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2015, Bhatt was awarded a 5-year [Packard Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packard_Fellowship).[^o7][^o15] Bhatt received the 2021 [New Horizons in Mathematics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Horizons_in_Mathematics_Prize).[^o7][^o16] He was elected to become a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society) in 2021.[^o7][^o17] Also in 2021 he received the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167).[^o18] In 2022 he was awarded the [Nemmers Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1252473).[^o19] Bhatt won the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize) 2023 in [Mathematical Sciences](https://www.infosysprize.org/about-the-prize/categories/mathematical-sciences.html) for his outstanding contributions to arithmetic geometry and commutative algebra. Bhatt's fundamental work on prismatic cohomology (joint with Peter Scholze), his work around the direct summand conjecture in commutative algebra, introduces new ideas and powerful methods in an area at the heart of pure mathematics.
