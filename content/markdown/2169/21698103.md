# Crepant resolution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crepant resolution"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **crepant resolution** of a [singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) is a [resolution](wikipedia://en/9914115) that does not affect the [canonical class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_class) of the [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). The term "crepant" was coined by (txt) by removing the prefix "dis" from the word "discrepant", to indicate that the resolutions have no [discrepancy](wikipedia://en/45159977) in the canonical class.

:::

The **crepant resolution conjecture** of (Ruan 2006) states that the orbifold cohomology of a [Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/1569478) [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) is isomorphic to a semiclassical limit of the quantum cohomology of a crepant resolution.

In 2 dimensions, crepant resolutions of complex Gorenstein quotient singularities ([du Val singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Val_singularities)) always exist and are unique, in 3 dimensions they exist[^o4] but need not be unique as they can be related by [flops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flop_%28algebraic_geometry%29), and in dimensions greater than 3 they need not exist.

A substitute for crepant resolutions which always exists is a **terminal model**. Namely, for every variety *X* over a field of characteristic zero such that *X* has [canonical](wikipedia://en/22910728) singularities (for example, [rational](wikipedia://en/2629851) Gorenstein singularities), there is a variety *Y* with **Q**-factorial [terminal](wikipedia://en/22910728) singularities and a [birational](wikipedia://en/382733) projective morphism *f*: *Y* → *X* which is crepant in the sense that *K*<sub>*Y*</sub> = *f*\**K*<sub>*X*</sub>.[^o5]
