# Quantum Markov chain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantum Markov chain"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **quantum Markov chain** is a reformulation of the ideas of a classical [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876), replacing the classical definitions of probability with [quantum probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_probability).

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

Very roughly, the theory of a quantum Markov chain resembles that of a [measure-many automaton](wikipedia://en/7926008#Measure-many_automata), with some important substitutions: the initial state is to be replaced by a [density matrix](wikipedia://en/62844), and the projection operators are to be replaced by [positive operator valued measures](wikipedia://en/3793084).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

More precisely, a quantum Markov chain is a pair $(E,\rho)$ with $\rho$ a [density matrix](wikipedia://en/62844) and $E$ a [quantum channel](wikipedia://en/1013550) such that

$$E:\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}$$

is a [completely positive trace-preserving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_positive_trace-preserving) map, and $\mathcal{B}$ a [C<sup>\*</sup>-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-star_algebra) of bounded operators. The pair must obey the quantum Markov condition, that

$$\operatorname{Tr} \rho (b_1\otimes b_2) = \operatorname{Tr} \rho E(b_1, b_2)$$

for all $b_1,b_2\in \mathcal{B}$.

:::
