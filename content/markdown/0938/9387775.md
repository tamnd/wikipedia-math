# Kármán–Howarth equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kármán–Howarth equation"}

In [isotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropic) [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) the **Kármán–Howarth** equation (after [Theodore von Kármán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_von_Kármán) and [Leslie Howarth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Howarth) 1938), which is derived from the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395), is used to describe the evolution of non-dimensional longitudinal [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724).[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Mathematical description {#Mathematical_description}

Consider a two-point velocity correlation tensor for homogeneous turbulence

$$R_{ij}(\mathbf{r},t) = \overline{u_i(\mathbf{x},t) u_j(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{r},t)}.$$

For isotropic turbulence, this correlation tensor can be expressed in terms of two scalar functions, using the [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) of full rotation group, first derived by [Howard P. Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_P._Robertson) in 1940,[^o8]

$$R_{ij}(\mathbf{r},t) = u'^2 \left\{ [f(r,t)-g(r,t)]\frac{r_ir_j}{r^2} + g(r,t) \delta_{ij}\right\}, \quad f(r,t) = \frac{R_{11}}{u'^2}, \quad g(r,t) = \frac{R_{22}}{u'^2}$$

where $u'$ is the [root mean square](wikipedia://en/66819) turbulent velocity and $u_1,\ u_2, \ u_3$ are turbulent velocity in all three directions. Here, $f(r)$ is the longitudinal correlation and $g(r)$ is the lateral correlation of velocity at two different points. From continuity equation, we have

$$\frac{\partial R_{ij}}{\partial r_j}=0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad g(r,t) = f(r,t) + \frac{r}{2} \frac{\partial}{\partial r}f(r,t)$$

Thus $f(r,t)$ uniquely determines the two-point correlation function. [Theodore von Kármán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_von_Kármán) and [Leslie Howarth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Howarth) derived the evolution equation for $f(r,t)$ from [Navier–Stokes equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier–Stokes_equation) as

$$\frac \partial {\partial t} (u'^2 f) - \frac{u'^3}{r^4} \frac \partial {\partial r} (r^4 h) = \frac{2\nu u'^2}{r^4} \frac\partial {\partial r} \left(r^4 \frac{\partial f}{\partial r}\right)$$

where $h(r,t)$ uniquely determines the triple correlation tensor

$$S_{ij} = {} \frac{\partial }{\partial r_k} \left( \overline{u_i(\mathbf{x},t) u_k(\mathbf{x},t)u_j(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{r},t)}-\overline{u_i(\mathbf{x},t) u_k(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{r},t)u_j(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{r},t)}\right).$$

## Loitsianskii's invariant {#Loitsianskii's_invariant}

L.G. Loitsianskii derived an integral invariant for the decay of the turbulence by taking the fourth moment of the Kármán–Howarth equation in 1939,[^o10][^o11] i.e.,

$$\frac \partial {\partial t} \left(u'^2 \int_0^\infty r^4 f\ dr\right) = \left[2\nu u'^2 r^4 \frac{\partial f}{\partial r} + u'^3 r^4 h\right]_0^\infty.$$

If $f(r)$ decays faster than $r^{-3}$ as $r\rightarrow\infty$ and also in this limit, if we assume that $r^4 h$ vanishes, we have the quantity,

$$\Lambda = u'^2 \int_0^\infty r^4 f\ dr = \mathrm{constant}$$

which is invariant. [Lev Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Landau) and [Evgeny Lifshitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Lifshitz) showed that this invariant is equivalent to [conservation of angular momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_angular_momentum).[^o12] However, Ian Proudman and W.H. Reid showed that this invariant does not hold always since $\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty} (r^4 h)$ is not in general zero, at least, in the initial period of the decay.[^o13][^o14] In 1967, [Philip Saffman](wikipedia://en/18983968) showed that this integral depends on the initial conditions and the integral can diverge under certain conditions.[^o15]

## Decay of turbulence {#Decay_of_turbulence}

For the viscosity dominated flows, during the decay of turbulence, the Kármán–Howarth equation reduces to a [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) once the triple correlation tensor is neglected, i.e.,

$$\frac \partial {\partial t} (u'^2 f) = \frac{2\nu u'^2}{r^4} \frac\partial {\partial r} \left(r^4 \frac{\partial f}{\partial r}\right).$$

With suitable boundary conditions, the solution to above equation is given by[^o17]

$$f(r,t) = e^{-r^2/8\nu t}, \quad u'^2 = \mathrm{const.}\times (\nu t)^{-5/2}$$

so that,

$$R_{ij}(r,t) \sim (\nu t)^{-5/2} e^{-r^2/8\nu t}.$$
