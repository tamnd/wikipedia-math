# Nagel point

*Image: Extouch Triangle and Nagel Point.svg.* solid #333333 solid orange solid red solid #1e90ff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nagel point"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Nagel point** (named for [Christian Heinrich von Nagel](wikipedia://en/21413563)) is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488), one of the points associated with a given [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) whose definition does not depend on the placement or scale of the triangle. It is the point of [concurrency](wikipedia://en/2206157) of all three of the triangle's [splitters](wikipedia://en/14961391).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Given a triangle $\triangle ABC$, let $T_{A}, T_{B}, T_{C}$ be the [extouch points](wikipedia://en/11125142) in which the $A$-[excircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) meets line $BC$, the $B$-excircle meets line $CA$, and the $C$-excircle meets line $AB$, respectively. The lines $AT_{A}, BT_{B}, CT_{C}$ [concur](wikipedia://en/2206157) in the Nagel point $N$ of triangle $\triangle ABC$.

Another construction of the point $T_{A}$ is to start at $A$ and trace around triangle $\triangle ABC$ [half its perimeter](wikipedia://en/886617), and similarly for $T_{B}$ and $T_{C}$. Because of this construction, the Nagel point is sometimes also called the **bisected perimeter point**, and the segments $\overline{AT}_{A}, \overline{BT}_{B}, \overline{CT}_{C}$ are called the triangle's [splitters](wikipedia://en/14961391).

There exists an easy construction of the Nagel point. Starting from each vertex of a triangle, it suffices to carry twice the length of the opposite edge. We obtain three lines which concur at the Nagel point.[^o4]

## Relation to other triangle centers {#Relation_to_other_triangle_centers}

The Nagel point is the [isotomic conjugate](wikipedia://en/9492657) of the [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point). The Nagel point, the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926), and the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) are [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear) on a line called the *Nagel line*. The incenter is the Nagel point of the [medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743);[^o6][^o7] equivalently, the Nagel point is the incenter of the [anticomplementary triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticomplementary_triangle). The [isogonal conjugate of the Nagel point](wikipedia://en/69072799) is the point of concurrency of the lines joining the mixtilinear touchpoint and the opposite vertex.

## Barycentric coordinates {#Barycentric_coordinates}

The un-normalized [barycentric coordinates](wikipedia://en/762954) of the Nagel point are $(s-a:s-b:s-c)$ where $s = \tfrac{a+b+c}{2}$ is the semi-perimeter of the reference triangle $\triangle ABC$.

## Trilinear coordinates {#Trilinear_coordinates}

The [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of the Nagel point are[^o10] as

$$\csc^2\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)\,:\,\csc^2\left(\frac{B}{2}\right)\,:\,\csc^2\left(\frac{C}{2}\right)$$

or, equivalently, in terms of the side lengths $a=\left|\overline{BC}\right|, b=\left|\overline{CA}\right|, c=\left|\overline{AB}\right|,$

$$\frac{b + c - a}{a}\,:\,\frac{c + a - b}{b}\,:\,\frac{a + b - c}{c}.$$

## History {#History}

The Nagel point is named after [Christian Heinrich von Nagel](wikipedia://en/21413563), a nineteenth-century German mathematician, who wrote about it in 1836. Early contributions to the study of this point were also made by [August Leopold Crelle](wikipedia://en/973559) and [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^o12]
