# Cover (algebra)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cover"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **cover** is one instance of some [mathematical structure](wikipedia://en/1809181) mapping [onto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onto) another instance, such as a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) (trivially) covering a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011). This should not be confused with the concept of a [cover in topology](wikipedia://en/317552).

:::

When some object *X* is said to cover another object *Y*, the cover is given by some [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) and [structure-preserving](wikipedia://en/13564) map *f* : *X* → *Y*. The precise meaning of "structure-preserving" depends on the kind of mathematical structure of which *X* and *Y* are instances. In order to be interesting, the cover is usually endowed with additional properties, which are highly dependent on the context.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A classic result in [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) theory due to [D. B. McAlister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._B._McAlister) states that every [inverse semigroup](wikipedia://en/1757814) has an [E-unitary](wikipedia://en/1757814#E-unitary_inverse_semigroups) cover; besides being surjective, the homomorphism in this case is also *[idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent) separating*, meaning that in its [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) an idempotent and non-idempotent never belong to the same equivalence class.; something slightly stronger has actually be shown for inverse semigroups: every inverse semigroup admits an [F-inverse](wikipedia://en/1757814#F-inverse_semigroups) cover.[^o4] McAlister's covering theorem generalizes to [orthodox semigroups](wikipedia://en/22653593): every orthodox semigroup has a unitary cover.[^o5]

Examples from other areas of algebra include the [Frattini cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frattini_cover) of a [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266)[^o6] and the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945).

:::

## Modules {#Modules}

If *F* is some family of modules over some ring *R*, then an *F*-cover of a module *M* is a homomorphism *X*→*M* with the following properties:

- *X* is in the family *F*
- *X*→*M* is surjective
- Any surjective map from a module in the family *F* to *M* factors through *X*
- Any endomorphism of *X* commuting with the map to *M* is an automorphism.

In general an *F*-cover of *M* need not exist, but if it does exist then it is unique up to (non-unique) isomorphism.

Examples include:

- [Projective covers](wikipedia://en/10273917) (always exist over [perfect rings](wikipedia://en/22658615))
- [flat covers](wikipedia://en/34846642) (always exist)
- [torsion-free covers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_cover) (always exist over integral domains)
- [injective covers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_cover)
