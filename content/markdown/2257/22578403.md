# Bender–Knuth involution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bender–Knuth involution"}

In [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), a **Bender–Knuth involution** is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) on the set of [semistandard tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistandard_tableau), introduced by (Bender Knuth 1972, 46–47) in their study of [plane partitions](wikipedia://en/2933308).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Bender–Knuth involutions $\sigma_k$ are defined for integers *$k$*, and act on the set of semistandard skew Young tableaux of some fixed shape $\mu/\nu$, where $\mu$ and $\nu$ are partitions. It acts by changing some of the elements $k$ of the tableau to $k+1$, and some of the entries $k+1$ to *$k$*, in such a way that the numbers of elements with values $k$ or $k+1$ are exchanged. Call an entry of the tableau **free** if it is *$k$* or $k+1$ and there is no other element with value $k$ or $k+1$ in the same column. For any $i$, the free entries of row $i$ are all in consecutive columns, and consist of $a_i$ copies of $k$ followed by $b_i$ copies of $k+1$, for some $a_i$ and $b_i$. The Bender–Knuth involution $\sigma_k$ replaces them with $b_i$ copies of $k$ followed by $a_i$ copies of $k+1$.

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Bender–Knuth involutions can be used to show that the number of semistandard skew tableaux of given shape and weight is unchanged under permutations of the weight. In turn this implies that the [Schur function](wikipedia://en/3147062) of a partition is a symmetric function.

Bender–Knuth involutions were used by (Stembridge 2002) to give a short proof of the [Littlewood–Richardson rule](wikipedia://en/16004359).

:::
