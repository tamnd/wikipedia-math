# Gamas's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gamas's theorem"}

**Gamas's theorem** is a result in [multilinear algebra](wikipedia://en/243946) which states the necessary and sufficient conditions for a [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) symmetrized by an [irreducible representation of the symmetric group](wikipedia://en/874641) $S_n$ to be zero. It was proven in 1988 by Carlos Gamas.[^o2] Additional proofs have been given by Pate[^o3] and Berget.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) and $\lambda$ be a [partition](wikipedia://en/104790) of $n$. From the representation theory of the symmetric group $S_n$ it is known that the partition $\lambda$ corresponds to an irreducible representation of $S_n$. Let $\chi^{\lambda}$ be the [character](wikipedia://en/293418) of this representation. The tensor $v_1 \otimes v_2 \otimes \dots \otimes v_n \in V^{\otimes n}$ symmetrized by $\chi^{\lambda}$ is defined to be

$$\frac{\chi^{\lambda}(e)}{n!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \chi^{\lambda}(\sigma) v_{\sigma(1)} \otimes v_{\sigma(2)} \otimes \dots \otimes v_{\sigma(n)},$$

where $e$ is the identity element of $S_n$. Gamas's theorem states that the above symmetrized tensor is non-zero if and only if it is possible to partition the set of vectors $\{v_i\}$ into [linearly independent](wikipedia://en/101863) sets whose sizes are in [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) with the lengths of the columns of the partition $\lambda$.

:::
