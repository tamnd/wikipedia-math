# Xiuxiong Chen

Chen Xiuxiong

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Xiuxiong Chen"}

**Xiuxiong Chen** () is a Chinese-American mathematician whose research concerns [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309).[^o2] Professor at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) since 2010, he was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2015 and awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) in 2019. In 2019, he was awarded the [Simons Investigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Investigator) award.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Chen was born in [Qingtian County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingtian_County), [Zhejiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang), China.[^o5] He entered the Department of Mathematics of the [University of Science and Technology of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Science_and_Technology_of_China) in 1982,[^o6] and graduated in 1987.[^o5] He subsequently studied under Peng Jiagui (彭家贵) at the [Graduate School of the Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_School_of_the_Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences), where he earned his master's degree. [^o5]

In 1989, he moved to the United States to study at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).[^o5] The last doctoral student of [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000),[^o5][^o8] he obtained his Ph.D. in mathematics in 1994, with his dissertation on "Extremal [Hermitian Matrices](wikipedia://en/189682) with Curvature Distortion in a [Riemann Surface](wikipedia://en/173181)".[^o8]

Chen was an instructor at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University) in Canada from 1994 to 1996. For the next two years he was a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) postdoctoral fellow at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). He was an assistant professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1998 to 2002, before becoming an associate professor at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). He was promoted to full professor in 2005. Since October 2010 he has been a professor at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).[^o7] In 2006, he founded the Pacific Rim Conference on Complex Geometry at the University of Science and Technology of China.[^o5]

As of 2019, Chen has advised 17 Ph.D. students, including [Song Sun](wikipedia://en/60406143) and Bing Wang (王兵).[^o5][^o8] He was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2015 "for contributions to differential geometry, particularly the theory of extremal [Kahler metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahler_metrics)".[^o9] He was an invited speaker at the 2002 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), in Beijing.[^o10]

## Conjecture on Fano manifolds and Veblen Prize {#Conjecture_on_Fano_manifolds_and_Veblen_Prize}

In 2019, Chen was awarded the prestigious [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075), together with English mathematician [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305) and Chen's former student [Song Sun](wikipedia://en/60406143), for proving a long-standing conjecture on [Fano manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_manifolds), which states "that a Fano manifold admits a [Kähler–Einstein metric](wikipedia://en/7335270) if and only if it is K-stable". It had been one of the most actively investigated topics in geometry since a loose version of it was first proposed in the 1980s by eventual [Fields Medalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medalist) [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) after his proof of the [Calabi conjecture](wikipedia://en/5263012). More precise versions were subsequently proposed by Chinese mathematician [Gang Tian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_Tian) and Donaldson. The solution by Chen, Donaldson and Sun was published in the *[Journal of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)* in 2015 as a three-article series, "Kähler–Einstein metrics on Fano manifolds, I, II and III".[^o13][^o14]

:::
