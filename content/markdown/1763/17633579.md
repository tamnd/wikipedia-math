# Topological combinatorics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological combinatorics"}

The [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) discipline of **topological combinatorics** is the application of [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) and [algebro-topological](wikipedia://en/38801) methods to solve problems in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

## History {#History}

The discipline of [combinatorial topology](wikipedia://en/363400) used combinatorial concepts in topology and in the early 20th century this turned into the field of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

In 1978 the situation was reversed—methods from algebraic topology were used to solve a problem in combinatorics—when [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938) proved the [Kneser conjecture](wikipedia://en/2724154), thus beginning the new field of **topological combinatorics**. Lovász's proof used the [Borsuk–Ulam theorem](wikipedia://en/4064) and this theorem retains a prominent role in this new field. This theorem has many equivalent versions and analogs and has been used in the study of [fair division](wikipedia://en/669899) problems.

In another application of [homological](wikipedia://en/142432) methods to [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), Lovász proved both the undirected and directed versions of a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) of [András Frank](wikipedia://en/22912777): Given a [*k*-connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-connected_graph) *G*, *k* points $v_1,\ldots,v_k \in V(G)$, and *k* positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $n_1,n_2,\ldots,n_k$ that sum up to $|V(G)|$, there exists a partition $\{V_1,\ldots,V_k\}$ of $V(G)$ such that $v_i \in V_i$, $|V_i|=n_i$, and $V_i$ spans a connected subgraph.

In 1987 the [necklace splitting problem](wikipedia://en/20417521) was solved by [Noga Alon](wikipedia://en/4939651) using the Borsuk–Ulam theorem. It has also been used to study [complexity problems](wikipedia://en/7543) in [linear decision tree algorithms](wikipedia://en/232602) and the [Aanderaa–Karp–Rosenberg conjecture](wikipedia://en/21681084). Other areas include [topology of partially ordered sets](wikipedia://en/5909536) and [Bruhat orders](wikipedia://en/10163003).

Additionally, methods from [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) now have a combinatorial analog in [discrete Morse theory](wikipedia://en/17637383).
