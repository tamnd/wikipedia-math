# Rolando Chuaqui

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rolando Chuaqui"}

**Rolando Basim Chuaqui Kettlun** (December 30, 1935–April 23, 1994[^o3]) was a [Chilean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on the foundations of probabilities and foundations of mathematics. Throughout his lifetime, he published two books and over 50 journal articles in mathematics and logic.[^o3] He also spearheaded the creation and expansion of mathematics departments across multiple Chilean universities.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Chuaqui was born into a [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrians) immigrant family from [Homs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homs) in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria).[^o5] He entered the [University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chile) in 1953 to study medicine. He obtained a Ph.D. in Logic and the Methodology of Science, an interdisciplinary program between the Department of Mathematics and Department of Philosophy, from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1965. His doctoral advisor was [David Blackwell](wikipedia://en/2202900). Chuaqui returned to Chile after graduating, serving as a professor at the University of Chile and then the [Pontifical Catholic University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Chile). During his time at the Pontifical Catholic, he advised three doctoral students.[^o6]

Chuaqui held several visiting positions, including at UCLA (1967), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) (1970), [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo) (1971 and 1982), [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) (1973–74), [University of Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Campinas) (1976, 1977 and 1978), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (1984), and [San José State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University) (1986-89).

He was a long-term collaborator of [Patrick Suppes](wikipedia://en/4824664), with whom he worked on non-standard analysis and measurement in sciences.[^o7] In 1986, he proposed a mathematical formulation for [pragmatic truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatic_theory_of_truth).[^o8]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

He was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in Mathematics in 1983.[^o10]

Since 1999, a series of annual research conferences in Chile, known as the *Jornadas Rolando Chuaqui Kettlun*, is held in his memory.[^o11][^o12] The [Pontifical Catholic University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Chile) also has a building named after him, which houses its Department of Mathematics.[^o13]
