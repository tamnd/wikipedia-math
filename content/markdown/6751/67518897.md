# Sklyanin algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sklyanin algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically the field of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), **Sklyanin algebras** are a class of [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/294390) [algebra](wikipedia://en/106364) named after [Evgeny Sklyanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Sklyanin). This class of algebras was first studied in the classification of Artin-Schelter regular[^o12] algebras of [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739) 3 in the 1980s.[^o2] Sklyanin algebras can be grouped into two different types, the **non-degenerate** Sklyanin algebras and the **degenerate** Sklyanin algebras, which have very different properties. A need to understand the non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras better has led to the development of the study of point modules in [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $k$ be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) with a [primitive cube root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950#General_definition). Let $\mathfrak{D}$ be the following subset of the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) $\textbf{P}_k^2$:

$$\mathfrak{D} = \{ [1:0:0], [0:1:0], [0:0:1] \} \sqcup \{ [a:b:c ] \big| a^3=b^3=c^3\}.$$

Each point $[a:b:c] \in \textbf{P}_k^2$ gives rise to a (quadratic 3-dimensional) Sklyanin algebra,

$$S_{a,b,c} = k \langle x,y,z \rangle / (f_1, f_2, f_3),$$

where,

$$f_1 = ayz + bzy + cx^2, \quad f_2 = azx + bxz + cy^2, \quad f_3 = axy + b yx + cz^2.$$

Whenever $[a:b:c ] \in \mathfrak{D}$ we call $S_{a,b,c}$ a **degenerate Sklyanin algebra** and whenever $[a:b:c] \in \textbf{P}^2 \setminus \mathfrak{D}$ we say the algebra is **non-degenerate.**[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The non-degenerate case shares many properties with the [commutative](wikipedia://en/294390) [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $k[x,y,z]$, whereas the degenerate case enjoys almost none of these properties. Generally the non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras are more challenging to understand than their degenerate counterparts.

### Properties of degenerate Sklyanin algebras {#Properties_of_degenerate_Sklyanin_algebras}

Let $S_{\text{deg}}$ be a degenerate Sklyanin algebra.

- $S_{\text{deg}}$ contains non-zero [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441).[^o9]
- The [Hilbert series](wikipedia://en/6612596) of $S_{\text{deg}}$ is $H_{S_{\text{deg}}} = \frac{1+t}{1-2t}$.[^o9]
- Degenerate Sklyanin algebras have infinite [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension](wikipedia://en/35987396).[^o9]
- $S_{\text{deg}}$ is neither left nor right [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129).[^o9]
- $S_{\text{deg}}$ is a [Koszul algebra](wikipedia://en/7987653).[^o9]
- Degenerate Sklyanin algebras have infinite [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739).[^o9]

### Properties of non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras {#Properties_of_non-degenerate_Sklyanin_algebras}

Let $S$ be a non-degenerate Sklyanin algebra.

- $S$ contains no non-zero [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441).[^o11]
- The [hilbert series](wikipedia://en/6612596) of $S$ is $H_{S} = \frac{1}{(1-t)^3}$.[^o11]
- Non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras are [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129).[^o11]
- $S$ is [Koszul](wikipedia://en/7987653).[^o11]
- Non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras are Artin-Schelter [^o12] regular.[^o11] Therefore, they have [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739) 3 and [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension](wikipedia://en/35987396) 3.[^o12]
- There exists a [normal](wikipedia://en/21454688) [central](wikipedia://en/342453) element in every non-degenerate Sklyanin algebra.[^o13]

## Examples {#Examples}

### Degenerate Sklyanin algebras {#Degenerate_Sklyanin_algebras}

The subset $\mathfrak{D}$ consists of 12 points on the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350), which give rise to 12 expressions of degenerate Sklyanin algebras. However, some of these are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) and there exists a classification of degenerate Sklyanin algebras into two different cases. Let $S_{\text{deg}} = S_{a,b,c}$ be a degenerate Sklyanin algebra.

- If $a=b$ then $S_{\text{deg}}$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to $k \langle x,y,z \rangle /(x^2,y^2,z^2)$, which is the Sklyanin algebra corresponding to the point $[0:0:1] \in \mathfrak{D}$.
- If $a \neq b$ then $S_{\text{deg}}$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to $k \langle x,y,z \rangle /(xy,yx,zx)$, which is the Sklyanin algebra corresponding to the point $[1:0:0] \in \mathfrak{D}$.[^o5]

These two cases are Zhang twists of each other[^o5] and therefore have many properties in common.[^o17]

### Non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras {#Non-degenerate_Sklyanin_algebras}

The [commutative](wikipedia://en/294390) [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $k[x,y,z]$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to the non-degenerate Sklyanin algebra $S_{1,-1,0} = k \langle x,y,z \rangle /( xy-yx, yz-zy, zx- xz)$ and is therefore an example of a non-degenerate Sklyanin algebra.

:::

## Point modules {#Point_modules}

The study of point modules is a useful tool which can be used much more widely than just for Sklyanin algebras. Point modules are a way of finding [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) in the underlying structure of [noncommutative graded rings](wikipedia://en/24062516). Originally, the study of point modules was applied to show some of the properties of non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras. For example to find their [Hilbert series](wikipedia://en/6612596) and determine that non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras do not contain [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441).[^o2]

### Non-degenerate Sklyanin algebras {#Non-degenerate_Sklyanin_algebras}

Whenever $abc \neq 0$ and $\left( \frac{a^3+b^3+c^3}{3abc} \right) ^3 \neq 1$ in the definition of a non-degenerate Sklyanin algebra $S=S_{a,b,c}$, the point modules of $S$ are parametrised by an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225).[^o2] If the parameters $a,b,c$ do not satisfy those constraints, the point modules of any non-degenerate Sklyanin algebra are still parametrised by a closed [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) on the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350).[^o21] If $S$ is a Sklyanin algebra whose point modules are parametrised by an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225), then there exists an element $g \in S$ which annihilates all point modules i.e. $Mg = 0$ for all point modules $M$ of $S$.

### Degenerate Sklyanin algebras {#Degenerate_Sklyanin_algebras}

The point modules of degenerate Sklyanin algebras are not parametrised by a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469).[^o9]
