# Kontorovich–Lebedev transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kontorovich–Lebedev transform"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kontorovich–Lebedev transform** is an [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912) which uses a Macdonald function (modified [Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700) of the second kind) with [imaginary](wikipedia://en/14554) index as its **kernel**. Unlike other Bessel function transforms, such as the [Hankel transform](wikipedia://en/1452960), this transform involves integrating over the **index** of the function rather than its argument.

:::

The transform of a function $f(x)$ and its inverse (provided they exist) are given below:

$$g(y) = \int_0^\infty f(x) K_{iy}(x) \, dx$$

$$f(x) = \frac{2}{\pi^2 x} \int_0^\infty g(y) K_{iy}(x) \sinh (\pi y) y \, dy .$$

Laguerre previously studied a similar transform regarding [Laguerre function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguerre_function) as:

$$g(y) = \int_0^\infty f(x)e^{-x} L_{y}(x) \, dx$$

$$f(x) = \int_0^\infty \frac{g(y)}{\Gamma (y)} L_y(x) \, dy.$$

[Erdélyi](wikipedia://en/4388684) *et al.*, for instance, contains a short list of Kontorovich–Lebedev transforms as well references to the original work of Kontorovich and Lebedev in the late 1930s. This transform is mostly used in solving the [Laplace equation](wikipedia://en/36941) in [cylindrical coordinates](wikipedia://en/176733) for wedge shaped domains by the method of [separation of variables](wikipedia://en/387750).
