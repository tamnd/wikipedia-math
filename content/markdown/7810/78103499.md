# Tom Leinster

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tom Leinster"}

*Image: Tom Leinster November 2003 (cropped).jpg.* Leinster in 2003

**Thomas "Tom" Stephen Hampden Leinster** (born 1971) is a British mathematician, known for his work on [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Leinster graduated in 2000 with a PhD from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). His PhD thesis *Operads in Higher-Dimensional Category Theory* was supervised by [Martin Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Hyland). After teaching at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), Leinster became, and is now, a professor at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). He published textbooks on [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869)[^o4] and [higher categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_categories) and [operads](wikipedia://en/3126358).[^o5] In the 2010s, he was mainly concerned with a generalisation of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) in category theory, the *magnitude*. He also considered such generalisations in [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) with application in biology (measurement of biodiversity).

## Award and honour {#Award_and_honour}

[Leinster groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster_group) (*i.e.*, [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) whose [order](wikipedia://en/493527) is equal to the sum of the orders of their [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918)) are named in his honour.[^o7] He received the 2019 [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for *Rethinking Set Theory*[^o8] (based upon an [axiomatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatisation) published in 1964 by [F. William Lawvere](wikipedia://en/872760)).[^o9] He is a frequent author and moderator for the academic group blog [*n*-Category Café](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Baez#Forums), where topics from mathematics, science and philosophy are discussed, often from the perspective of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869).[^o10] International media attention resulted from a 2014 article by Leinster in the *[New Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Scientist)*.[^o11] Leinster's article called, on the basis of ethics, for mathematicians to refuse to work for [intelligence agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_agency).[^o12][^o13][^o14] In German-speaking countries, this was reported by, among others, *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)*[^o15] and *[Zeit Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeit_Online)*.[^o16]
