# Representation ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Representation ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the area of [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), the **representation ring** (or **Green ring** after [J. A. Green](wikipedia://en/10670093)) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) formed from all the ([isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of the) finite-dimensional [linear representations](wikipedia://en/12695) of the group. Elements of the representation ring are sometimes called virtual representations.[^o2] For a given group, the ring will depend on the base [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of the representations. The case of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) coefficients is the most developed, but the case of [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p* where the [Sylow *p*-subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_subgroup) are [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) is also theoretically approachable.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Given a group *G* and a field *F*, the elements of its **representation ring** *R*<sub>*F*</sub>(*G*) are the formal differences of isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional *F*-representations of *G*. For the ring structure, addition is given by the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687#Direct_sum_of_group_representations) of representations, and multiplication by their [tensor product](wikipedia://en/43079145) over *F*. When *F* is omitted from the notation, as in *R*(*G*), then *F* is implicitly taken to be the field of complex numbers.

The representation ring of *G* is the [Grothendieck ring](wikipedia://en/1634790) of the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of finite-dimensional representations of *G*.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- For the complex representations of the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) *n*, the representation ring *R*<sub>***C***</sub>(*C*<sub>*n*</sub>) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to **Z**\[*X*\]/(*X*<sup>*n*</sup> − 1), where *X* corresponds to the complex representation sending a generator of the group to a [primitive](wikipedia://en/171950#General_definition) *n*th [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950).
- More generally, the complex representation ring of a [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) may be identified with the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) of the [character group](wikipedia://en/1384568).
- For the [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) representations of the cyclic group of order 3, the representation ring *R*<sub>**Q**</sub>(C<sub>3</sub>) is isomorphic to **Z**\[*X*\]/(*X*<sup>2</sup> − *X* − 2), where *X* corresponds to the [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) rational representation of dimension 2.
- For the [modular representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_representation) of the cyclic group of order 3 over a field *F* of characteristic 3, the representation ring *R*<sub>*F*</sub>(*C*<sub>3</sub>) is isomorphic to **Z**\[*X*,*Y*\]/(*X*<sup>2</sup> − *Y* − 1, *XY* − 2*Y*,*Y*<sup>2</sup> − 3*Y*).
- The continuous representation ring *R*(S<sup>1</sup>) for the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177) is isomorphic to **Z**\[*X*, *X*<sup>−1</sup>\]. The ring of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) representations is the [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of *R*(*G*) of elements fixed by the [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) on *R*(*G*) given by *X* ↦ *X*<sup>−1</sup>.
- The ring *R*<sub>**C**</sub>(*S*<sub>3</sub>) for the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of degree three is isomorphic to **Z**\[*X*,*Y*\]/(*XY* − *Y*,*X*<sup>2</sup> − 1,*Y*<sup>2</sup> − *X* − *Y* − 1), where *X* is the 1-dimensional alternating representation and *Y* the 2-dimensional irreducible representation of *S*<sub>3</sub>.

:::

## Characters {#Characters}

Any finite-dimensional complex representation ρ of a group *G* defines a function χ:*G* → $\mathbb{C}$ by the formula χ(*g*) = tr(ρ(*g*)). Such a function is a so-called [class function](wikipedia://en/1001329), meaning that it is constant on each [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) of *G*. Denote the ring of complex-valued class functions by *C*(*G*). The map sending isomorphism classes of representations to their characters gives a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) *R*(*G*) → *C*(*G*), and when *G* is finite this is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective), so that *R*(*G*) can be identified with a subring of *C*(*G*).

In the case of finite groups this ring homomorphism *R*(*G*) → *C*(*G*) extends to an algebra isomorphism $\mathbb{C} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}$ *R*(*G*) → *C*(*G*). Since isomorphism classes of [irreducible representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_representations) of a finite group form a basis of $\mathbb{C} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}$*R*(*G*), while [characteristic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_functions) of conjugacy classes form a basis of *C*(*G*), this shows that a finite group has as many isomorphism classes of irreducible representations as it has conjugacy classes.[^o8]

For a [compact](wikipedia://en/750326) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), *R*(*G*) is isomorphic to the subring of *R*(*T*) (where *T* is a maximal torus) consisting of those class functions that are invariant under the action of the Weyl group (Atiyah and Hirzebruch, 1961). For the general compact [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), see Segal (1968).

## λ-ring and Adams operations {#λ-ring_and_Adams_operations}

Given a representation of *G* and a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n*, we can form the *n*-th [exterior power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power) of the representation, which is again a representation of *G*. This induces an operation λ<sup>*n*</sup> : *R*(*G*) → *R*(*G*). With these operations, *R*(*G*) becomes a [λ-ring](wikipedia://en/32055176).

The *[Adams operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_operations)* on the representation ring *R*(*G*) are maps Ψ<sup>*k*</sup> characterised by their effect on characters χ:

$$\Psi^k \chi (g) = \chi(g^k) \ .$$

The operations Ψ<sup>*k*</sup> are ring homomorphisms of *R*(*G*) to itself, and on representations *ρ* of dimension *d*

$$\Psi^k (\rho) = N_k(\Lambda^1\rho,\Lambda^2\rho,\ldots,\Lambda^d\rho) \$$

where the Λ<sup>*i*</sup>*ρ* are the [exterior powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power) of *ρ* and *N*<sub>*k*</sub> is the *k*-th power sum expressed as a function of the *d* elementary symmetric functions of *d* variables.
