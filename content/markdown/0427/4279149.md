# Quantum mysticism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantum mysticism"}

**Quantum mysticism**, sometimes referred to pejoratively as **quantum quackery** or **quantum woo**,[^o2] is a set of [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) beliefs and associated practices that seek to relate spirituality or [mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystical) worldviews to the ideas of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) and its [interpretations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretations_of_quantum_mechanics).[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8] Quantum mysticism is considered [pseudoscience](wikipedia://en/23047) and [quackery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quackery) by quantum mechanics experts.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o13][^o14]

:::

Before the 1970s the term was usually used in reference to the postulate that "[consciousness causes collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness_causes_collapse)" but was later more closely associated with the purportedly pseudoscientific views espoused by [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age) thinkers such as [Fritjof Capra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritjof_Capra) and other members of the [Fundamental Fysiks Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Fysiks_Group), who were influential in popularizing the modern form of quantum mysticism.[^o11] Quantum mysticism commonly connects the [consciousness causes collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness_causes_collapse) interpretation of quantum physics with [Eastern mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_mysticism), most notably the [Advaita Vedanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta) school of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), which posits a cosmic consciousness ([Brahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahman)) which contains the entire universe.

## History {#History}

Many early quantum physicists held some interest in traditionally [Eastern metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_philosophy). Physicists [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg) and [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger), two of the main pioneers of quantum mechanics in the 1920s, were interested in [Eastern mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_mysticism), but are not known to have directly associated one with the other.

The historian of religion [Olav Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olav_Hammer) said that "[Schrödinger's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger) studies of Hindu mysticism never compelled him to pursue the same course as [quantum metaphysicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_metaphysics) such as [David Bohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bohm) or [Fritjof Capra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritjof_Capra)." Schrödinger biographer Walter J. Moore said that Schrödinger's two interests of quantum physics and Hindu mysticism were "strangely dissociated".[^o13]

In his 1961 paper "Remarks on the mind–body question", [Eugene Wigner](wikipedia://en/10101) suggested that a conscious observer played a fundamental role in quantum mechanics,[^o14][^o15] a concept which is part of the [consciousness causes collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness_causes_collapse) interpretation. While his paper served as inspiration for later mystical works by others,[^o14] Wigner's ideas were primarily philosophical and were not considered overtly pseudoscientific like the mysticism that followed.[^o16] By the late 1970s, Wigner had shifted his position and rejected the role of consciousness in quantum mechanics.[^o17] Harvard historian Juan Miguel Marin suggests that "consciousness \[was\] introduced hypothetically at the birth of quantum physics, \[and\] the term 'mystical' was also used by its founders, to argue in favor of and against such an introduction."[^o18]

Mysticism was argued against by [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736). Einstein's theories have often been falsely believed to support mystical interpretations of quantum theory. Einstein said, with regard to quantum mysticism, "No physicist believes that. Otherwise he wouldn't be a physicist."[^o18] He debates several arguments about the approval of mysticism, even suggesting [Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr) and [Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli) to be in support of and to hold a positive belief in mysticism which he believes to be false.

Niels Bohr denied quantum mysticism and had rejected the hypothesis that quantum theory requires a conscious observer as early as 1927,[^o18] despite having been "sympathetic towards the hypothesis that understanding consciousness might require an extension of quantum theory to accommodate laws other than those of physics".[^o18]

## In New Age thought {#In_New_Age_thought}

In the early 1970s, [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age) culture began to incorporate ideas from [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics), beginning with books by [Arthur Koestler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Koestler), [Lawrence LeShan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_LeShan) and others which suggested that purported [parapsychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapsychology) phenomena could be explained by quantum mechanics.[^o15]

In this decade, the [Fundamental Fysiks Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Fysiks_Group) emerged. This group of physicists embraced quantum mysticism, parapsychology, [Transcendental Meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_Meditation), and various New Age and [Eastern mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_mysticism) practices.[^o20]

Inspired in part by Wigner's exploration that [consciousness causes collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness_causes_collapse),[^o14] [Fritjof Capra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritjof_Capra), a member of the Fundamental Fysiks Group,[^o20] wrote *[The Tao of Physics: An Exploration of the Parallels Between Modern Physics and Eastern Mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tao_of_Physics)* (1975),[^o21] which espoused New Age quantum physics; the book was popular among the non-scientific public.[^o15] In 1979, [Gary Zukav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Zukav),[^o22] a non-scientist and "the most successful of Capra's followers", published *[The Dancing Wu Li Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dancing_Wu_Li_Masters).*[^o15] The Fundamental Fysiks Group and Capra's book are said to be major influences for the rise of quantum mysticism as a pseudoscientific interpretation of quantum mechanics.[^o20]

## Modern usage and examples {#Modern_usage_and_examples}

In contrast to the mysticism of the early 20th century, today quantum mysticism typically refers to [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age) beliefs that combine ancient mysticism with the language of quantum mechanics.[^o34] Called a [pseudoscience](wikipedia://en/23047) and a "hijacking" of quantum physics, it draws upon "coincidental similarities of language rather than genuine connections" to quantum mechanics.[^o10] Physicist [Murray Gell-Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Gell-Mann) coined the phrase "quantum flapdoodle" to refer to the misuse and misapplication of quantum physics to other topics.[^o25]

An example of such use is New Age guru [Deepak Chopra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepak_Chopra)'s "quantum theory" that aging is caused by the mind, expounded in his books *Quantum Healing* (1989) and *Ageless Body, Timeless Mind* (1993).[^o25] In 1998, Chopra was awarded the parody [Ig Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ig_Nobel_Prize) in the physics category for "his unique interpretation of quantum physics as it applies to life, liberty, and the pursuit of economic happiness".[^o26] In 2012, [Stuart Hameroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Hameroff) and Chopra proposed that the "quantum soul" could exist "apart from the body" and "in space-time geometry, outside the brain, distributed nonlocally".[^o27]

The 2004 film *[What the Bleep Do We Know!?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_the_Bleep_Do_We_Know!?)* dealt with a range of New Age ideas in relation to physics. It was produced by the [Ramtha School of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramtha_School_of_Enlightenment), founded by [J.Z. Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.Z._Knight), a [channeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channeler) who said that her teachings were based on a discourse with a 35,000-year-old disembodied entity named [Ramtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramtha).[^o28] Featuring Fundamental Fysiks Group member [Fred Alan Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Alan_Wolf),[^o20] the film misused some aspects of quantum mechanics—including the [Heisenberg uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_uncertainty_principle) and the [observer effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_effect_%28physics%29)—as well as biology and medicine.[^o29] Numerous critics dismissed the film for its use of [pseudoscience](wikipedia://en/23047).[^o30][^o31]
