# Kathleen Ollerenshaw

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kathleen Ollerenshaw"}

**Dame Kathleen Mary Ollerenshaw**, (*née* **Timpson**; 1 October 1912 – 10 August 2014) was a British mathematician and politician who was [Lord Mayor of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lord_mayors_of_Manchester) from 1975 to 1976 and an advisor on educational matters to [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher)'s government in the 1980s.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

She was born Kathleen Mary Timpson in [Withington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withington), Manchester, where she attended [Lady Barn House School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Barn_House_School) (1918–26).[^o4] She was a grandchild of the founder of the [Timpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timpson_%28retailer%29) shoe repair business, who had moved to Manchester from [Kettering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettering) and established the business there by 1870.[^o5] She became fascinated with mathematics, inspired by the Lady Barn headmistress, Miss Jenkin Jones. While at Lady Barn, she met her future husband, Robert Ollerenshaw.

Ollerenshaw became completely [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf) at age eight and was taught to [lip read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lip_reading). She gravitated toward the study of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) as it is not dependent on hearing. She was further inspired by a headmistress at Lady Barn House School who studied mathematics at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).[^o6]

As a young woman, she attended [St Leonards School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Leonards_School) and Sixth Form College in St Andrews, Scotland where today the house of young male boarders is named after her. At the age of 19 she gained admittance to [Somerville College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_College,_Oxford), to study mathematics. She completed her doctorate at Somerville in 1945 on "Critical Lattices" under the supervision of [Theo Chaundy](wikipedia://en/32792232). She wrote five original research papers which were sufficient for her to earn her [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree without the need of a formal written thesis.[^o7]

While an undergraduate, she became engaged to Robert Ollerenshaw, who became a distinguished military [surgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgeon) ([Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_%28United_Kingdom%29) R.G.W. Ollerenshaw, ERD, TD, BM, DMRD)[^o8][^o9][^o10] and a pioneer of [medical illustration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_illustration). They married in September 1939 and had two children, Charles (1941–99) and Florence (1946–72). In 1942 she suffered a [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage) and "cried nonstop for three days" as a result of stress when her husband was posted abroad for front-line [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) service.[^o11]

## Career {#Career}

After the Second World War, the Ollerenshaws moved to Manchester, where Kathleen worked as a part-time lecturer in the [mathematics department at Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester) while raising her children and continued her work on lattices. In 1949, at the age of 37, she received her first effective hearing aid.

*Image: Dame Kathleen Ollerenshaw MAS 2007.jpg.* Kathleen Ollerenshaw, aged 95, at Manchester Astronomical Society in 2007

Outside of academia, Ollerenshaw served as a [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_%28UK%29) councillor for [Rusholme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusholme) for twenty-five years (1954–1979), a member of the city council's finance committee (1968–1971), a chairman of the education committee of the Association of Municipal Corporations (1967–1971), Lord Mayor of Manchester (1975–1976), [High Sheriff of Greater Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Sheriff_of_Greater_Manchester) from 1978 to 1979, and the prime motivator in the creation of the [Royal Northern College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Northern_College_of_Music). She was made a [Freeman of the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_City) of Manchester and was an advisor on educational matters to [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher)'s government in the 1980s.

She was President of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) from 1978 to 1979.[^o13] She published at least 26 mathematical papers, her best-known contribution being to [most-perfect pandiagonal magic squares](wikipedia://en/52202).[^o14] Upon her death, she left a legacy in trust to support distinguished research visitors and public engagement activities at the [School of Mathematics, University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester). An annual public lecture at the university is named in her honour.[^o15]

An amateur astronomer, Ollerenshaw donated her telescope to [Lancaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_University), and an [observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory) there bears her name. She was an honorary member of the Manchester Astronomical Society and held the post of vice-president for a number of years.

Ollerenshaw attended [St Leonards School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Leonards_School) in [St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrews), Fife, and served as the school's president from 1981 to 2003. She was succeeded by [Baroness Byford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazel_Byford,_Baroness_Byford), [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_%28UK%29) spokeswoman in the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords). She [turned 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenarian) in October 2012.[^o16]

She died in [Didsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didsbury) on 10 August 2014, at the age of 101.[^o17] Her husband and both their children had predeceased her.

## Honours and legacy {#Honours_and_legacy}

- In 1970, Ollerenshaw was appointed [Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) for services to education.
- Composer Sir [Peter Maxwell Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Maxwell_Davies) dedicated his [Naxos Quartet No. 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxos_Quartets) to her.[^o19]
