# Killing spinor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Killing spinor"}

**Killing [spinor](wikipedia://en/29276)** is a term used in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

By the more narrow definition, commonly used in mathematics, the term Killing spinor indicates those [twistor](wikipedia://en/260836) spinors which are also [eigenspinors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspinor) of the [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448).[^o4][^o5][^o6] The term is named after [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824).

Another equivalent definition is that Killing spinors are the solutions to the [Killing equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_equation) for a so-called Killing number.

More formally:[^o7]

A **Killing spinor** on a [Riemannian](wikipedia://en/144652) [spin](wikipedia://en/2844303) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* is a [spinor field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinor_field) $\psi$ which satisfies

$$\nabla_X\psi=\lambda X\cdot\psi$$

for all [tangent vectors](wikipedia://en/30807) *X*, where $\nabla$ is the spinor [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848), $\cdot$ is [Clifford multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_multiplication) and $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ is a constant, called the **Killing number** of $\psi$. If $\lambda=0$ then the spinor is called a parallel spinor.

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In physics, Killing spinors are used in [supergravity](wikipedia://en/432632) and [superstring theory](wikipedia://en/19595676), in particular for finding solutions which preserve some [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636). They are a special kind of spinor field related to [Killing vector fields](wikipedia://en/1025455) and [Killing tensors](wikipedia://en/7913943).

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If $\mathcal{M}$ is a manifold with a Killing spinor, then $\mathcal{M}$ is an [Einstein manifold](wikipedia://en/644814) with [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) $Ric=4(n-1)\alpha^2$, where $\alpha$ is the Killing constant.[^o12]

### Types of Killing spinor fields {#Types_of_Killing_spinor_fields}

If $\alpha$ is purely imaginary, then $\mathcal{M}$ is a [noncompact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncompact); if $\alpha$ is 0, then the spinor field is parallel; finally, if $\alpha$ is real, then $\mathcal{M}$ is compact, and the spinor field is called a "real spinor field".

:::
