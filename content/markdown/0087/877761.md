# Stiefel–Whitney class

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stiefel–Whitney class"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Stiefel–Whitney classes** are a set of [topological invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_invariant) of a [real vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524#Definition_and_first_consequences) that describe the [obstructions](wikipedia://en/2179639) to constructing everywhere independent sets of [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of the vector bundle. Stiefel–Whitney classes are indexed from 0 to *n*, where *n* is the rank of the vector bundle. If the Stiefel–Whitney class of index *i* is nonzero, then there cannot exist $(n-i+1)$ everywhere linearly independent sections of the vector bundle. A nonzero *n*th Stiefel–Whitney class indicates that every section of the bundle must vanish at some point. A nonzero first Stiefel–Whitney class indicates that the vector bundle is not [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable). For example, the first Stiefel–Whitney class of the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817), as a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) over the circle, is not zero, whereas the first Stiefel–Whitney class of the [trivial line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle) over the circle, $S^1 \times\mathbb{R}$, is zero.

:::

The Stiefel–Whitney class was named for [Eduard Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876) and [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) and is an example of a [$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$](wikipedia://en/11137263)-[characteristic class](wikipedia://en/363530) associated to real vector bundles.

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) one can also define analogous Stiefel–Whitney classes for vector bundles with a non-degenerate [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478), taking values in [etale cohomology groups](wikipedia://en/396231) or in [Milnor K-theory](wikipedia://en/2914128). As a special case one can define Stiefel–Whitney classes for quadratic forms over fields, the first two cases being the discriminant and the [Hasse–Witt invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse–Witt_invariant) (Milnor 1970).

## Introduction {#Introduction}

### General presentation {#General_presentation}

For a real vector bundle $E$, the **Stiefel–Whitney class of $E$** is denoted by $w(E)$. It is an element of the [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723)

$$H^\ast(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) = \bigoplus_{i\geq0} H^i(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$$

where $X$ is the [base space](wikipedia://en/276524#Definition_and_first_consequences) of the bundle $E$, and [$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$](wikipedia://en/52327#Definition) (often alternatively denoted by $\mathbb{Z}_2$) is the [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) whose only elements are 0 and 1. The [component](wikipedia://en/58899#Construction_for_an_arbitrary_family_of_modules) of $w(E)$ in $H^i(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$ is denoted by $w_i(E)$ and called the **$i$-th Stiefel–Whitney class of $E$**. Thus,

$$w(E) = w_0(E) + w_1(E) + w_2(E) + \cdots,$$

where each $w_i(E)$ is an element of $H^i(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$.

The Stiefel–Whitney class $w(E)$ is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) of the real vector bundle $E$; i.e., when $F$ is another real vector bundle which has the same base space $X$ as $E$, and if $F$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/276524#Vector_bundle_morphisms) to $E$, then the Stiefel–Whitney classes $w(E)$ and $w(F)$ are equal. (Here *isomorphic* means that there exists a [vector bundle isomorphism](wikipedia://en/276524#Vector_bundle_morphisms) $E \to F$ which [covers](wikipedia://en/276524#Vector_bundle_morphisms) the identity $\mathrm{id}_X\colon X\to X$.) While it is in general difficult to decide whether two real vector bundles $E$ and $F$ are isomorphic, the Stiefel–Whitney classes $w(E)$ and $w(F)$ can often be computed easily. If they are different, one knows that $E$ and $F$ are not isomorphic.

As an example, [over](wikipedia://en/276524) the [circle](wikipedia://en/39782) $S^1$, there is a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) (i.e., a real vector bundle of [rank](wikipedia://en/276524#Definition_and_first_consequences) 1) that is not isomorphic to a [trivial](wikipedia://en/276526#Trivial_bundle) bundle. This line bundle $L$ is the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/276526#Möbius_strip) (which is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) whose fibers can be equipped with [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) structures in such a way that it becomes a vector bundle). The cohomology group $H^1(S^1; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$ has just one element other than 0. This element is the first Stiefel–Whitney class $w_1(L)$ of $L$.[^o5] Since the trivial line bundle over $S^1$ has first Stiefel–Whitney class 0, it is not isomorphic to $L$.

Two real vector bundles $E$ and $F$ which have the same Stiefel–Whitney class are not necessarily isomorphic. This happens for instance when $E$ and $F$ are trivial real vector bundles of different ranks over the same base space $X$. It can also happen when $E$ and $F$ have the same rank: the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of the [2-sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) $S^2$ and the trivial real vector bundle of rank 2 over $S^2$ have the same Stiefel–Whitney class, but they are not isomorphic. But if two real *line* bundles over $X$ have the same Stiefel–Whitney class, then they are isomorphic.

### Origins {#Origins}

The Stiefel–Whitney classes $w_i(E)$ get their name because [Eduard Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876) and [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) discovered them as [mod-2](wikipedia://en/20087) reductions of the [obstruction classes](wikipedia://en/2179639) to constructing $n-i+1$ everywhere [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of the [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) $E$ restricted to the *i*-skeleton of *X*. Here *n* denotes the dimension of the fibre of the vector bundle $F\to E\to X$.

To be precise, provided *X* is a [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex), Whitney defined classes $W_i(E)$ in the *i*-th cellular [cohomology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) of *X* with twisted coefficients. The coefficient system being the $(i-1)$-st [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) of the [Stiefel manifold](wikipedia://en/1392495) $V_{n-i+1}(F)$ of $n-i+1$ linearly independent vectors in the fibres of *E*. Whitney proved that $W_i(E)=0$ if and only if *E*, when restricted to the *i*-skeleton of *X*, has $n-i+1$ linearly-independent sections.

Since $\pi_{i-1}V_{n-i+1}(F)$ is either infinite-[cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) or [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, there is a [canonical](wikipedia://en/515096) reduction of the $W_i(E)$ classes to classes $w_i(E) \in H^i(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$ which are the Stiefel–Whitney classes. Moreover, whenever $\pi_{i-1}V_{n-i+1}(F) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, the two classes are identical. Thus, $w_1(E) = 0$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the bundle $E\to X$ is [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable).

The $w_0(E)$ class contains no information, because it is equal to 1 by definition. Its creation by Whitney was an act of creative notation, allowing the [Whitney sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_sum) Formula $w(E_1 \oplus E_2) = w(E_1)w(E_2)$ to be true.[^o7][^o8]

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Throughout, $H^i(X; G)$ denotes [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology) of a space $X$ with coefficients in the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$. The word *map* means always a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122#Continuous_functions_between_topological_spaces) between [topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_spaces).

### Axiomatic definition {#Axiomatic_definition}

The Stiefel-Whitney characteristic class $w(E)\in H^*(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$ of a finite rank real vector bundle *E* on a [paracompact base space](wikipedia://en/48631) *X* is defined as the unique class such that the following axioms are fulfilled:

1. **Normalization:** The Whitney class of the [tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tautological_line_bundle) over the [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694) $\mathbf{P}^1(\mathbb{R})$ is nontrivial, i.e., $w(\gamma^1_1)= 1 + a \in H^*(\mathbf{P}^1(\mathbb{R}); \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})= (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})[a]/(a^2)$.[^o5][^o12]
1. **Rank:** $w_0(E) = 1 \in H^0(X),$ and for *i* above the rank of *E*, $w_i(E) = 0 \in H^i(X)$, that is, $w(E) \in H^{\leqslant \mathrm{rank} (E)}(X).$[^o13]
1. **Whitney product formula:** $w(E\oplus F)= w(E) \smile w(F)$,[^o7][^o8][^o12] that is, the Whitney class of a direct sum is the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) of the summands' classes.
1. **Naturality:** $w(f^*E) = f^*w(E)$ for any real vector bundle $E \to X$ and map $f\colon X' \to X$, where $f^*E$ denotes the [pullback vector bundle](wikipedia://en/1665392).[^o14][^o12]

The uniqueness of these classes is proved for example, in section 17.2 – 17.6 in Husemoller or section 8 in Milnor and Stasheff. There are several proofs of the existence, coming from various constructions, with several different flavours, their coherence is ensured by the unicity statement.

### Definition *via* infinite Grassmannians {#Definition_via_infinite_Grassmannians}

#### The infinite Grassmannians and vector bundles {#The_infinite_Grassmannians_and_vector_bundles}

This section describes a construction using the notion of [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556).

For any vector space *V*, let $Gr_n(V)$ denote the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810), the space of *n*-dimensional linear subspaces of *V*, and denote the infinite Grassmannian

$$Gr_n = Gr_n(\mathbb{R}^\infty).$$

Recall that it is equipped with the [tautological bundle](wikipedia://en/2913502) $\gamma^n \to Gr_n,$ a rank *n* vector bundle that can be defined as the subbundle of the trivial bundle of fiber *V* whose fiber at a point $W \in Gr_n (V)$ is the subspace represented by *W*.

Let $f\colon X \to Gr_n$, be a continuous map to the infinite Grassmannian. Then, up to isomorphism, the bundle induced by the map *f* on *X*

$$f^*\gamma^n \in \mathrm{Vect}_n(X)$$

depends only on the homotopy class of the map \[*f*\]. The pullback operation thus gives a morphism from the set

$$[X; Gr_n]$$

of maps $X \to Gr_n$ *modulo* homotopy equivalence, to the set

$$\mathrm{Vect}_n(X)$$

of isomorphism classes of vector bundles of rank *n* over *X*.

(The important fact in this construction is that if *X* is a [paracompact space](wikipedia://en/48631), this map is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942). This is the reason why we call infinite Grassmannians the classifying spaces of vector bundles.)

Now, by the naturality axiom (4) above, $w_j (f^*\gamma^n)= f^* w_j (\gamma^n)$. So it suffices in principle to know the values of $w_j (\gamma^n)$ for all *j*. However, the cohomology ring $H^*(Gr_n, \mathbb{Z}_2)$ is free on specific generators $x_j\in H^j(Gr_n, \mathbb{Z}_2)$ arising from a standard cell decomposition, and it then turns out that these generators are in fact just given by $x_j=w_j (\gamma^n)$. Thus, for any rank-n bundle, $w_j= f^*x_j$, where *f* is the appropriate classifying map. This in particular provides one proof of the existence of the Stiefel–Whitney classes.

#### The case of line bundles {#The_case_of_line_bundles}

We now restrict the above construction to line bundles, *ie* we consider the space, $\mathrm{Vect}_1(X)$ of line bundles over *X*. The Grassmannian of lines $Gr_1$ is just the infinite [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135)

$$\mathbf{P}^\infty(\mathbf{R}) = \mathbf{R}^\infty/\mathbf{R}^*,$$

which is doubly covered by the infinite sphere $S^{\infty}$ with [antipodal points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipodal_points) as fibres. This sphere $S^{\infty}$ is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible), so we have

$$\begin{aligned} \pi_1(\mathbf{P}^\infty(\mathbf{R})) &= \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} \\ \pi_i(\mathbf{P}^\infty(\mathbf{R})) &= \pi_i(S^\infty) = 0 && i > 1 \end{aligned}$$

Hence **P**<sup>∞</sup>(**R**) is the [Eilenberg-Maclane space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg-Maclane_space) $K(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, 1)$.

It is a property of Eilenberg-Maclane spaces, that

$$[X; \mathbf{P}^\infty(\mathbf{R}) ] = H^1(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$$

for any *X*, with the isomorphism given by *f* → *f\**η, where η is the generator of

$$H^1(\mathbf{P}^\infty(\mathbf{R}); \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

Applying the former remark that α : \[*X*, *Gr*<sub>1</sub>\] → Vect<sub>1</sub>(*X*) is also a bijection, we obtain a bijection

$$w_1\colon \text{Vect}_1(X) \to H^1(X; \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})$$

this defines the Stiefel–Whitney class *w*<sub>1</sub> for line bundles.

#### The group of line bundles {#The_group_of_line_bundles}

If Vect<sub>1</sub>(*X*) is considered as a group under the operation of tensor product, then the Stiefel–Whitney class, *w*<sub>1</sub> : Vect<sub>1</sub>(*X*) → *H*<sup>1</sup>(*X*; **Z**/2**Z**), is an isomorphism. That is, *w*<sub>1</sub>(λ ⊗ μ) = *w*<sub>1</sub>(λ) + *w*<sub>1</sub>(μ) for all line bundles λ, μ → *X*.

For example, since *H*<sup>1</sup>(*S*<sup>1</sup>; **Z**/2**Z**) = **Z**/2**Z**, there are only two line bundles over the circle up to bundle isomorphism: the trivial one, and the open Möbius strip (i.e., the Möbius strip with its boundary deleted).

The same construction for [complex vector bundles](wikipedia://en/1538109) shows that the [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) defines a bijection between complex line bundles over *X* and *H*<sup>2</sup>(*X*; **Z**), because the corresponding classifying space is **P**<sup>∞</sup>(**C**), a K(**Z**, 2). This isomorphism is true for topological line bundles, the obstruction to injectivity of the Chern class for algebraic vector bundles is the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

### Topological interpretation of vanishing {#Topological_interpretation_of_vanishing}

1. *w<sub>i</sub>*(*E*) = 0 whenever *i* > rank(*E*).
1. If *E<sup>k</sup>* has $s_1,\ldots,s_{\ell}$ [sections](wikipedia://en/276526#Sections) which are everywhere [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) then the $\ell$ top degree Whitney classes vanish: $w_{k-\ell+1}=\cdots=w_k=0$.
1. The first Stiefel–Whitney class is zero if and only if the bundle is [orientable](wikipedia://en/44844703). In particular, a manifold *M* is orientable if and only if *w*<sub>1</sub>(*TM*) = 0.
1. The bundle admits a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) if and only if both the first and second Stiefel–Whitney classes are zero.
1. For an orientable bundle, the second Stiefel–Whitney class is in the image of the natural map *H*<sup>2</sup>(*M*, **Z**) → *H*<sup>2</sup>(*M*, **Z**/2**Z**) (equivalently, the so-called third **integral** Stiefel–Whitney class is zero) if and only if the bundle admits a spin<sup>c</sup> structure.
1. All the Stiefel–Whitney *numbers* (see below) of a smooth compact manifold *X* vanish if and only if the manifold is the boundary of some smooth compact (unoriented) manifold (Note that some Stiefel-Whitney *class* could still be non-zero, even if all the Stiefel- Whitney *numbers* vanish!)

### Uniqueness of the Stiefel–Whitney classes {#Uniqueness_of_the_Stiefel–Whitney_classes}

The bijection above for line bundles implies that any functor θ satisfying the four axioms above is equal to *w*,[^o23] by the following argument. The second axiom yields θ(γ<sup>1</sup>) = 1 + θ<sub>1</sub>(γ<sup>1</sup>). For the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) *i* : **P**<sup>1</sup>(**R**) → **P**<sup>∞</sup>(**R**), the pullback bundle $i^*\gamma^1$ is equal to $\gamma_1^1$. Thus the first and third axiom imply

$$i^* \theta_1 (\gamma^1 ) = \theta_1 (i^* \gamma^1 ) = \theta_1 (\gamma_1^1 ) = w_1 (\gamma_1^1 ) = w_1 (i^* \gamma^1 ) = i^* w_1 (\gamma^1 ).$$

Since the map

$$i^*: H^1 (\mathbf{P}^\infty(\mathbf{R} ); \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}) \to H^1 (\mathbf{P}^1(\mathbf{R}); \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} )$$

is an isomorphism, $\theta_1(\gamma^1) = w_1(\gamma^1)$ and θ(γ<sup>1</sup>) = *w*(γ<sup>1</sup>) follow. Let *E* be a real vector bundle of rank *n* over a space *X*. Then *E* admits a [splitting map](wikipedia://en/15958803), i.e. a map *f* : *X′* → *X* for some space *X′* such that $f^*:H^*(X; \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})) \to H^*(X'; \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})$ is injective and $f^* E = \lambda_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \lambda_n$ for some line bundles $\lambda_i \to X'$. Any line bundle over *X* is of the form $g^*\gamma^1$ for some map *g*, and

$$\theta (g^*\gamma^1 ) = g^*\theta ( \gamma^1 ) = g^* w ( \gamma^1 ) = w ( g^*\gamma^1 ),$$

by naturality. Thus θ = *w* on $\text{Vect}_1(X)$. It follows from the fourth axiom above that

$$f^*\theta(E) = \theta(f^*E) = \theta(\lambda_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \lambda_n) = \theta(\lambda_1) \cdots \theta(\lambda_n) = w(\lambda_1) \cdots w(\lambda_n) = w(f^*E) = f^* w(E).$$

Since $f^*$ is injective, θ = *w*. Thus the Stiefel–Whitney class is the unique functor satisfying the four axioms above.

### Non-isomorphic bundles with the same Stiefel–Whitney classes {#Non-isomorphic_bundles_with_the_same_Stiefel–Whitney_classes}

Although the map $w_1 \colon \mathrm{Vect}_1(X) \to H^1(X; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$ is a bijection, the corresponding map is not necessarily injective in higher dimensions. For example, consider the tangent bundle $TS^n$. With the canonical embedding of $S^n$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, the [normal bundle](wikipedia://en/2370200) $\nu$ to $S^n$ is a line bundle. Since $S^n$ is orientable, $\nu$ is trivial. The sum $TS^n \oplus \nu$ is just the restriction of $T\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ to $S^n$, which is trivial since $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ is contractible. Hence *w*(*TS<sup>n</sup>*) = *w*(*TS<sup>n</sup>*)*w*(ν) = *w*(*TS<sup>n</sup>* ⊕ ν) = 1. But, provided $n$ is even, *TS<sup>n</sup>* → *S<sup>n</sup>* is not trivial; $e(TS^n)[S^n] = \chi(TS^n) = 2 \ne0$ (where \[*S<sup>n</sup>*\] denotes a [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of *S<sup>n</sup>* and $\chi$ the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420)), hence its [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) $e$ must be nonzero.

:::

## Related invariants {#Related_invariants}

::: {#o27 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Stiefel-Whitney number"}

### Stiefel–Whitney numbers {#Stiefel–Whitney_numbers}

If we work on a manifold of dimension *n*, then any product of Stiefel–Whitney classes of total degree *n* can be paired with the **Z**/2**Z**-[fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of the manifold to give an element of **Z**/2**Z**, a **Stiefel–Whitney number** of the vector bundle. For example, if the manifold has dimension 3, there are three linearly independent Stiefel–Whitney numbers, given by $w_1^3, w_1 w_2, w_3$. In general, if the manifold has dimension *n*, the number of possible independent Stiefel–Whitney numbers is the number of [partitions](wikipedia://en/104790) of *n*.

The Stiefel–Whitney numbers of the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold are called the Stiefel–Whitney numbers of the manifold. They are known to be [cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) invariants. It was proven by [Lev Pontryagin](wikipedia://en/582075) that if *B* is a smooth compact (*n*+1)–dimensional manifold with boundary equal to *M*, then the Stiefel-Whitney numbers of *M* are all zero.[^o28] Moreover, it was proved by [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633) that if all the Stiefel-Whitney numbers of *M* are zero then *M* can be realised as the boundary of some smooth compact manifold.[^o29]

One Stiefel–Whitney number of importance in [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115) is the *[de Rham invariant](wikipedia://en/25901631)* of a (4*k*+1)-dimensional manifold, $w_2w_{4k-1}.$

:::

::: {#o31 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Wu class"}

### Wu classes {#Wu_classes}

The Stiefel–Whitney classes $w_k$ are the [Steenrod squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steenrod_square) of the **Wu classes** $v_k$, defined by [Wu Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Wenjun) in 1947.[^o32] Most simply, the total Stiefel–Whitney class is the total Steenrod square of the total Wu class: $\operatorname{Sq}(v) = w$. Wu classes are most often defined implicitly in terms of Steenrod squares, as the cohomology class representing the Steenrod squares. Let the manifold *X* be *n* dimensional. Then, for any cohomology class *x* of degree $n-k$,

$$v_k \cup x = \operatorname{Sq}^k(x).$$

Or more narrowly, we can demand $\langle v_k \cup x, \mu\rangle = \langle \operatorname{Sq}^k(x), \mu \rangle$, again for cohomology classes *x* of degree $n-k$.[^o33]

:::

## Integral Stiefel–Whitney classes {#Integral_Stiefel–Whitney_classes}

The element $\beta w_i \in H^{i+1}(X;\mathbf{Z})$ is called the *i* + 1 *integral* Stiefel–Whitney class, where β is the [Bockstein homomorphism](wikipedia://en/3116297), corresponding to reduction modulo 2, **Z** → **Z**/2**Z**:

$$\beta\colon H^i(X;\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}) \to H^{i+1}(X;\mathbf{Z}).$$

For instance, the third integral Stiefel–Whitney class is the obstruction to a [Spin<sup>c</sup> structure](wikipedia://en/2844303#Spinc_structures).

### Relations over the Steenrod algebra {#Relations_over_the_Steenrod_algebra}

Over the [Steenrod algebra](wikipedia://en/2146220), the Stiefel–Whitney classes of a smooth manifold (defined as the Stiefel–Whitney classes of the tangent bundle) are generated by those of the form $w_{2^i}$. In particular, the Stiefel–Whitney classes satisfy the **Wu formula**, named for [Wu Wenjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Wenjun):[^o36]

$$Sq^i(w_j)=\sum_{t=0}^i {j+t-i-1 \choose t} w_{i-t}w_{j+t}.$$
