# Mathesis universalis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathesis universalis"}

*Image: Wallis - Opera mathematica, 1657 - 4611280.tif.* [Frontispiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_frontispiece) of *Operum Mathematicorum Pars Prima* (1657) by [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290), the first volume of *Opera Mathematica* including a chapter entitled *Mathesis Universalis*.

**Mathesis universalis** (from μάθησις, el "science or learning", and universalis "universal") is a hypothetical [universal science](wikipedia://en/1232743) modelled on [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) envisaged by [Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) and [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281), among a number of other 16th- and 17th-century philosophers and mathematicians. For Leibniz, it would be supported by a *[calculus ratiocinator](wikipedia://en/876255)*. [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290) invokes the name as title in his *Opera Mathematica*, a textbook on [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and [Cartesian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_geometry).

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Roomen - Universae mathesis idea, 1602 - 151336.jpg.* Frontispiece of *Universae mathesis idea* (1602) by [Adriaan van Roomen](wikipedia://en/628991)

Descartes' most explicit description of *mathesis universalis* occurs in *Rule Four* of the *[Rules for the Direction of the Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_for_the_Direction_of_the_Mind)*, written before 1628.[^n1] Leibniz attempted to work out the possible connections between [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [infinitesimal calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_calculus), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [universal characteristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristica_universalis) in an incomplete treatise titled "*Mathesis Universalis*" in 1695.

[Predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic) could be seen as a modern system with some of these *universal* qualities, at least as far as mathematics and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) are concerned. More generally, *mathesis universalis*, along with perhaps [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238)'s [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), represents one of the earliest attempts to construct a [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102).

One of the perhaps most prominent critics of the idea of *mathesis universalis* was [Ludwig Wittgenstein and his philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/28359471).[^n2] As Anthropologist Emily Martin notes:[^n3] Tackling mathematics, the realm of symbolic life perhaps most difficult to regard as contingent on social norms, Wittgenstein commented that people found the idea that numbers rested on conventional social understandings "unbearable".

## René Descartes {#René_Descartes}

In Descartes' corpus the term *mathesis universalis* appears only in the *[Rules for the Direction of the Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_for_the_Direction_of_the_Mind)*.[^n1] In the discussion of *Rule Four*, Descartes' provides his clearest description of *mathesis universalis*:

Rule Four

We need a method if we are to investigate the truth of things. \[...\] I began my investigation by inquiring what exactly is generally meant by the term 'mathematics' and why it is that, in addition to arithmetic and geometry, sciences such as astronomy, music, optics, mechanics, among others, are called branches of mathematics. \[...\] This made me realize that there must be a general science which explains all the points that can be raised concerning order and measure irrespective of the subject-matter, and that this science should be termed ***mathesis universalis*** — a venerable term with a well-established meaning — for it covers everything that entitles these other sciences to be called branches of mathematics. \[...\]

## Gottfried Leibniz {#Gottfried_Leibniz}

In his account of *mathesis universalis*, Leibniz proposed a dual method of universal synthesis and analysis for the ascertaining [truth](wikipedia://en/29920), described in *De Synthesi et Analysi universale seu Arte inveniendi et judicandi* (1890).[^n4][^n5]

::: {#o13 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Ars inveniendi"}

### *Ars inveniendi* {#Ars_inveniendi}

*Ars inveniendi* ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for "art of invention") is the constituent part of *mathesis universalis* corresponding to the method of synthesis.[^n4][^n5]

:::

### *Ars combinatoria* {#Ars_combinatoria}

Leibniz also identified synthesis with the *[ars combinatoria](wikipedia://en/2265373)*, viewing it in terms of the recombination of symbols or human thoughts.[^n4]

### *Ars judicandi* {#Ars_judicandi}

*Ars judicandi* ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for "art of judgement") is the constituent part of *mathesis universalis* corresponding to the method of analysis.[^n4]
