# Pietro De Martino

*Image: De\_Martino\_-\_Nuove\_instituzioni\_di\_aritmetica\_pratica,\_1762\_-\_776706.tif.* *Nuove instituzioni di aritmetica pratica*, Section One, 1762 ed.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pietro De Martino"}

**Pietro De Martino** or **Di Martino** (31 May 1707 – 28 January 1746) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) from the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Faicchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faicchio), he was the brother of Angelo, professor first of [medical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_physics) then of mathematics at the [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples); and of [Nicola Antonio De Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Antonio_De_Martino), professor of mathematics and director of the Real Corpo degli Ingegneri (Royal Engineers Corp) and Marine Guard. Pietro De Martino was a pupil of [Agostino Ariani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agostino_Ariani) and of Giacinto De Cristoforo (1650-1730). In 1735, he was assigned to the astronomical and nautical chair at the University of Naples.[^o5]

He disputed with [Roger Joseph Boscovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Joseph_Boscovich) on the question if it is possible to gain a right result starting from a wrong [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis).[^o3]

He authored various works; his *Nuove istituzioni di aritmetica pratica*, published originally in 1739 in Naples, had many reprints (the better known of the 1758; one also in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) in 1762).[^o3] He died in Naples in 1746.[^o5]
