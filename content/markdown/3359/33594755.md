# Blakers–Massey theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Blakers–Massey theorem"}

In mathematics, the first **Blakers–Massey theorem**, named after [Albert Blakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Blakers) and [William S. Massey](wikipedia://en/4967089),[^o2][^o3][^o4] gave vanishing conditions for certain [triad](wikipedia://en/42523481) [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) of [spaces](wikipedia://en/30450).

:::

## Description of the result {#Description_of_the_result}

This connectivity result may be expressed more precisely, as follows. Suppose *X* is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) which is the [pushout](wikipedia://en/582024) of the diagram

$$A\xleftarrow{\ f\ } C \xrightarrow{\ g\ } B,$$

where *f* is an [*m*-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-connected_space#n-connected_map) map and *g* is *n*-connected. Then the map of pairs

$$(A,C)\rightarrow (X,B)$$

induces an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) in relative [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) in degrees $k\le (m+n-1)$ and a surjection in the next degree.

However the third paper of Blakers and Massey in this area[^o6] determines the critical, i.e., first non-zero, triad homotopy group as a [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836), under a number of assumptions, including some simple connectivity. This condition and some dimension conditions were relaxed in work of [Ronald Brown](wikipedia://en/27079705) and [Jean-Louis Loday](wikipedia://en/36183327).[^o7] The algebraic result implies the connectivity result, since a tensor product is zero if one of the factors is zero. In the non [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) case, one has to use the nonabelian tensor product of Brown and Loday.[^o7]

The triad connectivity result can be expressed in a number of other ways, for example, it says that the pushout square above behaves like a [homotopy pullback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_pullback) up to dimension $m+n$.

## Generalization to higher toposes {#Generalization_to_higher_toposes}

The generalization of the connectivity part of the theorem from traditional homotopy theory to any other [infinity-topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity-topos) with an [infinity-site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity-site) of definition was given by [Charles Rezk](wikipedia://en/62553423) in 2010.[^o9]

## Fully formal proof {#Fully_formal_proof}

In 2013 a fairly short, fully formal proof using [homotopy type theory](wikipedia://en/33974223) as a [mathematical foundation](wikipedia://en/169358) and an [Agda variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agda_%28programming_language%29) as a [proof assistant](wikipedia://en/1258607) was announced by [Peter LeFanu Lumsdaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_LeFanu_Lumsdaine);[^o11] this became Theorem 8.10.2 of *Homotopy Type Theory – Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*.[^o12] This induces an internal proof for any [infinity-topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity-topos) (i.e. without reference to a site of definition); in particular, it gives a new proof of the original result.
