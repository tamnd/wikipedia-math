# A Mathematician's Lament

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="A Mathematician's Lament"}

***A Mathematician's Lament***, often referred to informally as **Lockhart's Lament**, is a short book on [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) by Paul Lockhart, originally a research mathematician at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) and U.C. Santa Cruz, and subsequently a math teacher at [Saint Ann's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Ann's_School_%28Brooklyn%29) in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) for many years. This strongly worded [opinion piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_piece) is organized into two parts. The first part, "Lamentation", criticizes the way mathematics is typically taught in American schools and argues for an aesthetic, [intuitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuition_%28psychology%29), and problem-oriented approach to teaching. The second part, "Exultation", gives specific examples of how to teach [mathematics as an art](wikipedia://en/22468661#Mathematics_as_an_art).

:::

## Background {#Background}

This book was developed from a 25-page essay that was written in 2002, originally circulated in typewritten manuscript copies, and subsequently published by [Keith Devlin](wikipedia://en/574895) on his online column for the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)'s [webzine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webzine) *MAA Online*.[^o4]

Lockhart has since written *Measurement* (2012), *Arithmetic* (2017),[^o5][^o6] and *The Mending of Broken Bones* (2025).

## Summary {#Summary}

In the first part, “Lamentation”, Lockhart emphasizes [mathematics as an art](wikipedia://en/22468661#Mathematics_as_an_art). He criticizes [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) for prioritizing rote memorization and notation, which he argues is of little interest to the average student. Lockhart pushes instead for a focus on [mathematical proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proofs) and showcasing the [beauty in mathematics](wikipedia://en/18974136), absent of any application. He also criticizes the use of [two-column proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-column_proof) in the teaching of geometry for obscuring this beauty and misrepresenting how mathematicians create proofs.

In the second part, “Exultation”, Lockhart gives specific examples from [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) to argue that math primarily arises from [play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_%28activity%29). He argues that this play should be the primary focus of mathematics education.

## Reception {#Reception}

Daniel Farlow and William Schmidt, writing in *[Math Horizons](wikipedia://en/21340642)* and *[Notices of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4977512)*, respectively, praised Lockhart's criticisms of mathematics education.[^o9][^o10] Schmidt, however, also criticized Lockhart's proposed approach to math education for exacerbating preexisting inequalities within the education system. Timo Tossavainen, writing in *[The Mathematical Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Intelligencer)*, criticized Lockhart for "overlook\[ing\] what is realistically possible in mathematics teachers’ education", but he ultimately praised the book as a "necessary reminder of how all is lost if the joy of doing mathematics and the students’ right to experience it are not at the heart of mathematics education."[^o11]
