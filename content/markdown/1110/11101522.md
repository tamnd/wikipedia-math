# Isomorphism-closed subcategory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isomorphism-closed subcategory"}

In category theory, a branch of mathematics, a [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) $\mathcal{A}$ of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\mathcal{B}$ is said to be **isomorphism closed** or **replete** if every $\mathcal{B}$-[isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) $h:A\to B$ with $A\in\mathcal{A}$ belongs to $\mathcal{A}.$[^o2] This implies that both $B$ and $h^{-1}:B\to A$ belong to $\mathcal{A}$ as well.

:::

A subcategory that is isomorphism closed and [full](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) is called **strictly full**. In the case of full subcategories it is sufficient to check that every $\mathcal{B}$-object that is isomorphic to an $\mathcal{A}$-object is also an $\mathcal{A}$-object.

This condition is very natural. For example, in the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) one usually studies properties that are invariant under [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660)—so-called [topological properties](wikipedia://en/1337282). Every topological property corresponds to a strictly full subcategory of $\mathbf{Top}.$
