# Franklin P. Peterson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Franklin P. Peterson"}

**Franklin Paul Peterson** (1930–2000) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). He was a professor of mathematics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Peterson was born in [Aurora, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora,_Illinois), on August 27, 1930, the older of two brothers. His father died when he was young, and he was raised by his mother and uncle.[^o3] He attended [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University), graduating in 1952, and earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1955 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under the supervision of [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod).[^o2][^o3] After postdoctoral studies at Princeton, he joined the MIT faculty in 1958.[^o2][^o3]

Peterson edited the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)* from 1966 to 1970.[^o2] He also served for many years as treasurer of the AMS; in that role he played a key role in resolving tensions between the dual directors of the society as it was then structured, and worked to build up a large reserve fund for the society.[^o2][^o3]

Peterson married Marilyn Rutz in 1959.[^o3] He died of a stroke on September 1, 2000, near [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC).[^o2][^o3]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Peterson's early research used [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) to study [homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/212250). Later, he did important work on the properties of [loop spaces](wikipedia://en/2174332).[^o3]

The [Peterson–Stein formula](wikipedia://en/35124443) is named after him, after he wrote about it with Norman Stein in 1960. He also introduced the [Brown–Peterson cohomology](wikipedia://en/13698492) with [Edgar H. Brown](wikipedia://en/57293598) in 1966.[^o7]

He advised over 20 doctoral students (different sources give different numbers, in part because Robert E. Mosher, whom Peterson considered his first student, had a different official advisor) and has over 100 academic descendants.[^o2][^o3][^o5]
