# Pierre Joseph Étienne Finck

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre Joseph Étienne Finck"}

**Pierre Joseph Étienne Finck** (1797–1870) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Finck, who became orphan at twelve years, was educated by a merchant of [Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau) ([Pfalz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfalz_%28region%29)). In 1815, he entered in the *[École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique)*, where he graduated in 1817. After some time studying in the Artillery School, he left [París](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/París) for [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) before 1821.[^o4]

From 1825 he was professor in the Artillery School of Strasburg and, simultaneously, professor of special mathematics in the *Collége de Strasbourg*. In 1842 he was appointed adjunct professor of applied mathematics in the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) where he was appointed full professor in 1847.[^o5]

From 1862 he began to suffer from ill health. He was intellectually diminished,[^o6] and he was forced to take sick leave in 1866. He retired in 1868.

Finck wrote seven text books on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) and [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), and more than twenty articles published in the *[Journal de Mathematiques Pures et Appliquees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_de_Mathematiques_Pures_et_Appliquees)*, in the *Annales de Gergone* and in the *Comptes rendus* of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o7]
