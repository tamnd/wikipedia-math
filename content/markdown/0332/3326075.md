# Concept class

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Concept class"}

In [computational learning theory](wikipedia://en/387537) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **concept** over a domain *X* is a total [Boolean function](wikipedia://en/753349) over *X*. A **concept class** is a class of concepts. Concept classes are a subject of [computational learning theory](wikipedia://en/387537).

:::

Concept class terminology frequently appears in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) associated with [probably approximately correct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probably_approximately_correct) (PAC) learning.[^o2] In this setting, if one takes a set *Y* as a set of (classifier output) labels, and *X* is a set of examples, the map $c: X\to Y$, i.e. from examples to classifier labels (where $Y = \{0, 1\}$ and where *c* is a subset of *X*), *c* is then said to be a *concept*. A *concept class* $C$ is then a collection of such concepts.

Given a class of concepts *C*, a subclass *D* is **reachable** if there exists a sample *s* such that *D* contains exactly those concepts in *C* that are extensions to *s*.[^o3] Not every subclass is reachable.[^o3]

## Background {#Background}

A *sample* $s$ is a [partial function](wikipedia://en/23577) from $X$ to $\{0, 1\}$.[^o3] Identifying a concept with its characteristic function mapping $X$ to $\{0, 1\}$, it is a special case of a sample.[^o3]

Two samples are *consistent* if they agree on the intersection of their domains.[^o3] A sample $s'$ *extends* another sample $s$ if the two are consistent and the domain of $s$ is contained in the domain of $s'$.[^o3]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Suppose that $C = S^+(X)$. Then:

- the subclass $\{\{x\}\}$ is reachable with the sample $s = \{(x, 1)\}$;[^o3]
- the subclass $S^+(Y)$ for $Y\subseteq X$ are reachable with a sample that maps the elements of $X - Y$ to zero;[^o3]
- the subclass $S(X)$, which consists of the singleton sets, is *not* reachable.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Let $C$ be some concept class. For any concept $c\in C$, we call this concept $1/d$*\-good* for a positive integer $d$ if, for all $x\in X$, at least $1/d$ of the concepts in $C$ agree with $c$ on the classification of $x$.[^o3] The *fingerprint dimension* $FD(C)$ of the entire concept class $C$ is the least positive integer $d$ such that every reachable subclass $C'\subseteq C$ contains a concept that is $1/d$-good for it.[^o3] This quantity can be used to bound the minimum number of equivalence queries needed to learn a class of concepts according to the following [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489):$FD(C) - 1\leq \#EQ(C)\leq \lceil FD(C)\ln(|C|)\rceil$.[^o3]

:::
