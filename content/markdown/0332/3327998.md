# Invariant basis number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Invariant basis number"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* has the **invariant basis number** (**IBN**) property if all finitely generated [free modules](wikipedia://en/326454) over *R* have a well-defined rank. In the case of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603), the IBN property is the fact that finite-dimensional [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) have a unique [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* has **invariant basis number** (IBN) if for all positive integers *m* and *n*, *R*<sup>*m*</sup> [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to *R*<sup>*n*</sup> (as left *R*-modules) implies that *m* = *n*.

Equivalently, this means there do not exist distinct positive integers *m* and *n* such that *R*<sup>*m*</sup> is isomorphic to *R*<sup>*n*</sup>.

Rephrasing the definition of invariant basis number in terms of matrices, it says that, whenever *A* is an *m*-by-*n* matrix over *R* and *B* is an *n*-by-*m* matrix over *R* such that *AB* = *I* and *BA* = *I*, then *m* = *n*. This form reveals that the definition is left–right symmetric, so it makes no difference whether we define IBN in terms of left or right modules; the two definitions are equivalent.[^o4]

Note that the isomorphisms in the definitions are *not* ring isomorphisms, they are module isomorphisms, even when one of *n* or *m* is 1.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The main purpose of the invariant [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) number condition is that free modules over an IBN ring satisfy an analogue of the [dimension theorem for vector spaces](wikipedia://en/346992): any two bases for a free module over an IBN ring have the same cardinality. Assuming the [ultrafilter lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_lemma) (a strictly weaker form of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840)), this result is actually equivalent to the definition given here, and can be taken as an alternative definition.

The **rank** of a free module *R*<sup>*n*</sup> over an IBN ring *R* is defined to be the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the exponent *m* of any (and therefore every) *R*-module *R*<sup>*m*</sup> isomorphic to *R*<sup>*n*</sup>. Thus the IBN property asserts that every isomorphism class of free *R*-modules has a unique rank. The rank is not defined for rings not satisfying IBN. For vector spaces, the rank is also called the [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamel_dimension). Thus the result above is in short: the rank is uniquely defined for all free *R*-modules [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) it is uniquely defined for [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) free *R*-modules.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Any field satisfies IBN, and this amounts to the fact that finite-dimensional vector spaces have a well defined dimension. Moreover, any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) (except the [zero ring](wikipedia://en/1872874)) satisfies IBN,[^o9] as does any [left-Noetherian ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-Noetherian_ring) and any [semilocal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semilocal_ring).

::: {#o10 .proof cue=collapse name="Proof"}

Let *A* be a commutative ring and assume there exists an *A*-module isomorphism $f \colon A^n \to A^p$. Let $(e_1,\dots,e_n)$ the canonical basis of *A*<sup>*n*</sup>, which means $e_i\in A^n$ is all zeros except a one in the *i*-th position. By [Krull's theorem](wikipedia://en/4128358), let *I* a [maximal](wikipedia://en/48164) proper [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of *A* and $(i_1,\dots,i_n)\in I^n$. An *A*-module morphism means

$$f(i_1,\dots,i_n) = \sum_{k=1}^n i_k f(e_k) \in I^p$$

because *I* is an ideal. So *f* induces an *A*/*I*-module morphism $f' \colon \left(\frac{A}{I}\right)^n\to \left(\frac{A}{I}\right)^p$, that can easily be proven to be an isomorphism. Since *A*/*I* is a field, *f**is an isomorphism between finite dimensional vector spaces, so *n* = *p*.***

:::

An example of a nonzero ring that does not satisfy IBN is the ring of [column finite matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859#Infinite_matrices) $\mathbb{CFM}_\mathbb{N}(R)$, the matrices with coefficients in a ring *R*, with entries indexed by $\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}$ and with each column having only finitely many non-zero entries. That last requirement allows us to define the product of infinite matrices *MN*, giving the ring structure. A left module isomorphism $\mathbb{CFM}_\mathbb{N}(R)\cong\mathbb{CFM}_\mathbb{N}(R)^2$ is given by:

$$\begin{array}{rcl} \psi : \mathbb{CFM}_\mathbb{N}(R) &\to & \mathbb{CFM}_\mathbb{N}(R)^2 \\ M &\mapsto & (\text{odd columns of } M, \text{ even columns of } M) \end{array}$$

This infinite matrix ring turns out to be isomorphic to the [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of a right [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) over *R* of [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) rank.[^o11]

From this isomorphism, it is possible to show (abbreviating $\mathbb{CFM}_\mathbb{N}(R)=S$) that *S* ≅ *S*<sup>*n*</sup> for any positive integer *n*, and hence *S*<sup>*n*</sup> ≅ *S*<sup>*m*</sup> for any two positive integers *m* and *n*. There are other examples of non-IBN rings without this property, among them [Leavitt algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavitt_algebras).[^o12]

:::

## Other results {#Other_results}

IBN is a necessary (but not sufficient) condition for a ring with no zero divisors to be embeddable in a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) (compare [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) in the commutative case). See also the [Ore condition](wikipedia://en/3100560).

Every nontrivial [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) or [stably finite ring](wikipedia://en/26021350) has invariant basis number.

Every ring satisfying the rank condition (i.e. having [unbounded generating number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbounded_generating_number)) must have invariant basis number.[^o14]
