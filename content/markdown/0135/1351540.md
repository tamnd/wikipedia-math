# Eugene Dynkin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eugene Dynkin"}

**Eugene Borisovich Dynkin** (Евгений Борисович Дынкин; 11 May 1924 – 14 November 2014) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) and American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o9] He made contributions to the fields of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), especially [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), and [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process). The [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109), the [Dynkin system](wikipedia://en/2549191), and [Dynkin's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynkin's_lemma) are named after him.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Dynkin was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) family, living in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) until 1935, when his family was exiled to [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan).[^o5] Two years later, when Dynkin was 13, his father disappeared in the [Gulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag).[^o9][^o5]

### Moscow University {#Moscow_University}

At the age of 16, in 1940, Dynkin was admitted to [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University). He avoided military service in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) because of his poor eyesight, and received his [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in 1945 and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1948. He became an assistant professor at Moscow, but was not awarded a "chair" until 1954 because of his political undesirability. His academic progress was made difficult due to his father's fate, as well as Dynkin's Jewish origin; the special efforts of [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), his PhD supervisor, made it possible for Dynkin to progress through graduate school into a teaching position.[^o5]

### USSR Academy of Sciences {#USSR_Academy_of_Sciences}

In 1968, Dynkin was forced to transfer from the Moscow University to the [Central Economic Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Economic_Mathematical_Institute) of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o9] He worked there on the theory of [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) and [economic equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_equilibrium).

### Cornell {#Cornell}

He remained at the Institute until 1976, when he emigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).[^o9] In 1977, he became a professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o9][^o10]

### Death {#Death}

Dynkin died at the [Cayuga Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayuga_Medical_Center) in [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca,_New_York), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29), aged 90.[^o12][^o13] Dynkin was an atheist.[^o14]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

*Image: Eugene Dynkin.jpg.* Eugene Dynkin, 1976

Dynkin is considered to be a rare example of a mathematician who made fundamental contributions to two very distinct areas of mathematics: [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o16] The algebraic period of Dynkin's mathematical work was between 1944 and 1954, though even during this time a probabilistic theme was noticeable.[^o17] Indeed, Dynkin's first publication was in 1945, jointly with N. A. Dmitriev, solved a problem on the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of [stochastic matrices](wikipedia://en/217313). This problem was raised at Kolmogorov's seminar on [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876), while both Dynkin and Dmitriev were undergraduates.[^o17]

### Lie Theory {#Lie_Theory}

While Dynkin was a student at Moscow University, he attended [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643)'s seminar on [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945). In 1944, Gelfand asked him to prepare a survey on the structure and classification of [semisimple Lie groups](wikipedia://en/1337587), based on the papers by [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and [Bartel Leendert van der Waerden](wikipedia://en/91390). Dynkin found the papers difficult to read, and in an attempt to better understand the results, he invented the notion of a ["simple root"](wikipedia://en/277087#Positive_roots_and_simple_roots) in a [root system](wikipedia://en/277087). He represented the pairwise angles between these simple roots in the form of a [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109). In this way he obtained a cleaner exposition of the classification of complex semisimple Lie algebras.[^o19] Of Dynkin's 1947 paper "Structure of semisimple Lie algebras", [Bertram Kostant](wikipedia://en/3520590) wrote: In this paper, using only elementary mathematics, and starting with almost nothing, Dynkin, brilliantly and elegantly developed the structure and machinery of semisimple Lie algebras. What he accomplished in this paper was to take a hitherto esoteric subject, and to make it into beautiful and powerful mathematics.

Dynkin's 1952 influential paper "Semisimple subalgebras of semisimple Lie algebras", contained large tables and lists, and studied the subalgebras of the [exceptional Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/292831).

### Probability theory {#Probability_theory}

Dynkin is considered one of the founders of the modern theory of [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process). The results obtained by Dynkin and other participants of his seminar at Moscow University were summarized in two books. The first of these, "Theory of Markov Processes", was published in 1959, and laid the foundations of the theory.

Dynkin's one-hour talk at the 1962 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), was delivered by Kolmogorov, since prior to his emigration, Dynkin was never permitted to travel to [the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world).[^o21] This talk was titled "Markov processes and problems in analysis".

- Institute of Mathematical Statistics, Fellow, 1962
- American Academy of Arts and Sciences, Fellow, 1978
- National Academy of Sciences of the USA, Member, 1985[^o23]
- American Mathematical Society, [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Total Mathematical Work, 1993
- Moscow Mathematical Society, Honorary Member, 1995
