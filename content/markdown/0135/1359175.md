# Ian Grojnowski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ian Grojnowski"}

**Ian Henry Grojnowski** (born April 1966)[^o6] is a mathematician working at the [Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Pure_Mathematics_and_Mathematical_Statistics) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o7]

:::

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Grojnowski was the first recipient of the [Fröhlich Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fröhlich_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2004 for his work in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). The citation reads[^o9]Grojnowski's insights into geometric contexts for representation theory go back to his thesis with [George Lusztig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lusztig) on character sheaves over homogeneous spaces.[^o10] He has exploited these ideas to make breakthroughs in several completely unexpected areas, including representations of the [affine Hecke algebras](wikipedia://en/10361630) at roots of 1 (generalising results of [Kazhdan](wikipedia://en/3052833) and Lusztig), the [representation theory of the symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/874641) S<sub>*n*</sub> in [characteristic *p*](wikipedia://en/1066621), the introduction (simultaneously with Nakajima) of vertex operators on the cohomology of the Hilbert schemes of finite subschemes of a complex [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311), and (in joint work with Fishel and Teleman) the proof of the strong Macdonald conjecture of Hanlon and Feigin for [reductive](wikipedia://en/863720) [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).
