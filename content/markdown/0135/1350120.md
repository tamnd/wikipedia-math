# Knot complement

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Knot complement"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **knot complement** of a [tame knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tame_knot) *K* is the space where the knot is not. If a knot is embedded in the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792), then the complement is the 3-sphere minus the space near the knot. To make this precise, suppose that *K* is a knot in a three-manifold *M* (most often, *M* is the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792)). Let *N* be a [tubular neighborhood](wikipedia://en/3192042) of *K*; so *N* is a [solid torus](wikipedia://en/1251473). The knot complement is then the [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) of *N*,

:::

$$X_K = M - \mbox{interior}(N).$$

The knot complement *X<sub>K</sub>* is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257); the boundary of *X<sub>K</sub>* and the boundary of the neighborhood *N* are homeomorphic to a two-[torus](wikipedia://en/74800). Sometimes the ambient manifold *M* is understood to be the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792). Context is needed to determine the usage. There are analogous definitions for the **[link](wikipedia://en/1161784) complement**.

Many [knot invariants](wikipedia://en/166898), such as the [knot group](wikipedia://en/1164126), are really invariants of the complement of the knot. When the ambient space is the three-sphere no information is lost: the [Gordon&ndash;Luecke theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon&ndash;Luecke_theorem) states that a knot is determined by its complement. That is, if *K* and *K*′ are two knots with [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) complements then there is a homeomorphism of the three-sphere taking one knot to the other.

Knot complements are [Haken manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haken_manifolds).[^o2] More generally complements of [links](wikipedia://en/1161784) are Haken manifolds.
