# Fibered knot

*Image: Blue Figure-Eight Knot.png.* [Figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565) *is* fibered.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fibered knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) or [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) $K$ in the [3-dimensional sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) $S^3$ is called **fibered** or **fibred** (sometimes **Neuwirth knot** in older texts, after [Lee Neuwirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Neuwirth)) if there is a 1-parameter family $F_t$ of [Seifert surfaces](wikipedia://en/1343951) for $K$, where the parameter $t$ runs through the points of the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) $S^1$, such that if $s$ is not equal to $t$ then the intersection of $F_s$ and $F_t$ is exactly $K$.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Knots that are fibered {#Knots_that_are_fibered}

For example:

- The [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075), [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773), and [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565) are fibered knots.
- The [Hopf link](wikipedia://en/1515511) is a fibered link.

### Knots that are not fibered {#Knots_that_are_not_fibered}

*Image: Blue Stevedore Knot.png.* The [stevedore knot](wikipedia://en/26652059) is *not* fibered

The [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) of a fibered knot is [monic](wikipedia://en/314730), i.e. the coefficients of the highest and lowest powers of *t* are plus or minus 1. Examples of knots with nonmonic Alexander polynomials abound, for example the [twist knots](wikipedia://en/26701261) have Alexander polynomials $qt-(2q+1)+qt^{-1}$, where *q* is the number of half-twists.[^o6] In particular the [stevedore knot](wikipedia://en/26652059) is not fibered.

:::

## Related constructions {#Related_constructions}

Fibered knots and links arise naturally, but not exclusively, in [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry). For instance, each [singular point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) of a [complex plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_plane_curve) can be described topologically as the [cone](wikipedia://en/782162) on a fibered knot or link called the **link of the singularity**. The [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773) is the link of the [cusp singularity](wikipedia://en/6851758) $z^2+w^3$; the Hopf link (oriented correctly) is the link of the [node singularity](wikipedia://en/4230456) $z^2+w^2$. In these cases, the family of [Seifert surfaces](wikipedia://en/1343951) is an aspect of the [Milnor fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_fibration) of the singularity.

A knot is fibered if and only if it is the binding of some [open book decomposition](wikipedia://en/5608629) of $S^3$.
