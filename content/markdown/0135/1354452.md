# Arnold Schönhage

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Arnold Schönhage"}

**Arnold Schönhage** (born 1 December 1934 in [Lockhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockhausen), now [Bad Salzuflen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Salzuflen)) is a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist).

:::

Schönhage was professor at the [Rheinische Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität, Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn),[^o3] and also in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen) and [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanz).[^o4]

Together with [Volker Strassen](wikipedia://en/771960), he developed the [Schönhage–Strassen algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schönhage–Strassen_algorithm) for the multiplication of large numbers[^o3][^o5] that has a [runtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runtime_%28program_lifecycle_phase%29) of *[O](wikipedia://en/44578)*(*N* log *N* log log *N*). For many years, this was the fastest known way to multiply large integers, although Schönhage and Strassen predicted that an algorithm with a run-time of N(logN) should exist. In 2019, [Joris van der Hoeven](wikipedia://en/62070387) and David Harvey finally developed an algorithm with this runtime, proving that Schönhage's and Strassen's prediction had been correct.[^o6]

Schönhage designed and implemented together with [Andreas F. W. Grotefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_F._W._Grotefeld) and [Ekkehart Vetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekkehart_Vetter) a multitape [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403), called [TP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_processor), in software. The machine is programmed in [TPAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPAL), an [assembler language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembler_language). They implemented numerous numerical algorithms, including the Schönhage–Strassen algorithm, on this machine.

The [Odlyzko–Schönhage algorithm](wikipedia://en/14099326)[^o7] from 1988 is regularly used in research on the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).
