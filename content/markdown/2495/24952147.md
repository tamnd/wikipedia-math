# Lieb–Liniger model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lieb–Liniger model"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **Lieb–Liniger model** describes a gas of particles moving in one dimension and satisfying [Bose–Einstein statistics](wikipedia://en/188935). More specifically, it describes a one dimensional [Bose gas](wikipedia://en/1064839) with [Dirac delta](wikipedia://en/37021) interactions. It is named after [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb) and Werner Liniger who introduced the model in 1963.[^o2] The model was developed to compare and test [Nikolay Bogolyubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Bogolyubov)'s theory of a [weakly interacting Bose gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_interacting_Bose_gas).[^o7] It can be seen as one model in the theory of [generalized hydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/82737261).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given $N$ [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) moving in one-dimension on the $x$-axis defined from $[0,L]$ with [periodic boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/9667552), a state of the *N*-body system must be described by a many-body [wave function](wikipedia://en/145343) $\psi(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_j, \dots,x_N)$. The [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381), of this model is introduced as

$$H = -\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x_i^2} + 2c \sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{j>i}^N \delta(x_i-x_j)\ ,$$

where $\delta$ is the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021). The constant $c$ denotes the strength of the interaction, $c>0$ represents a repulsive interaction and $c<0$ an attractive interaction.[^o5] The hard core limit $c\to\infty$ is known as the [Tonks–Girardeau gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonks–Girardeau_gas).[^o5]

For a collection of bosons, the wave function is unchanged under permutation of any two particles (permutation symmetry), i.e., $\psi(\dots, x_i,\dots, x_j, \dots) = \psi(\dots, x_j,\dots, x_i, \dots)$ for all $i \neq j$ and $\psi$ satisfies $\psi( \dots, x_j=0, \dots ) =\psi(\dots, x_j=L,\dots )$ for all $j$.

The delta function in the Hamiltonian gives rise to a boundary condition when two coordinates, say $x_1$ and $x_2$ are equal. The condition is that as $x_2$ approaches $x_1$ from above ($x_2 \searrow x_1$), the derivative satisfies

$$\left.\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_2} - \frac{\partial}{\partial x_1} \right) \psi (x_1, x_2)\right|_{x_2=x_1+}= c \psi (x_1=x_2).$$

:::

## Solution {#Solution}

*Image: lieb-liniger-1.gif.* Fig. 1: The ground state energy (per particle) $e$ as a function of the interaction strength per density $\gamma=Lc/N$, from.[^o2]

The time-independent [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) $H\psi = E\psi$, is solved by explicit construction of $\psi$. Since $\psi$ is symmetric it is completely determined by its values in the simplex $\mathcal{R}$, defined by the condition that $0 \leq x_1 \leq x_2 \leq \dots, \leq x_N \leq L$.

The solution can be written in the form of a [Bethe ansatz](wikipedia://en/4327884) as[^o7]

$$\psi(x_1, \dots, x_N) = \sum_P a(P)\exp \left( i \sum_{j=1}^N k_{P j} x_j\right),$$

with wave vectors $0 \leq k_1 \leq k_2 \leq \dots, \leq k_N$, where the sum is over all $N !$ permutations, $P$, of the integers $1,2, \dots, N$, and $P$ maps $1,2,\dots,N$ to $P_1,P_2,\dots,P_N$. The coefficients $a(P)$, as well as the $k$'s are determined by the condition $H\psi =E\psi$, and this leads to a total energy

$$E= \sum_{j=1}^N\, k_j^2,$$

with the amplitudes given by

$a(P) = \prod_{1\leq i<j \leq N} \left(1+\frac{ic}{k_{Pi} -k_{Pj}}\right) \, .$[^o8]

These equations determine $\psi$ in terms of the $k$'s. These lead to $N$ equations:[^o7]

$$L\, k_j= 2\pi I_j\ -2 \sum_{i=1}^N \arctan \left(\frac{k_j-k_i}{c} \right) \qquad \qquad \text{for } j=1, \, \dots,\, N \ ,$$

where $I_1 < I_2 < \cdots < I_N$ are integers when $N$ is odd and, when $N$ is even, they take values $\pm \frac12, \pm \frac32, \dots$ . For the ground state the $I$'s satisfy

$$I_{j+1} - I_j = 1, \quad {\rm for} \ 1\leq j <N \qquad \text{and } I_1=-I_N.$$
