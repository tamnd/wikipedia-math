# Sphere packing in a cube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphere packing in a cube"}

*Image: Close packing box (cropped).svg.* Spheres packed in a cube

In geometry, **sphere packing in a cube** is a three-dimensional [sphere packing](wikipedia://en/368621) problem with the objective of packing [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) inside a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285). It is the three-dimensional equivalent of the [circle packing in a square](wikipedia://en/27471721) problem in two dimensions. The problem consists of determining the optimal packing of a given number of spheres inside the cube.

:::

Gensane[^o2] traces the origin of the problem to work of J. Schaer in the mid-1960s.[^o3] Reviewing Schaer's work, [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) writes that he "proves that the arrangements for $k=2,3,4,8,9$ are what anyone would have guessed".[^o4] The cases $k=7$ and $k=10$ were resolved in later work of Schaer,[^o5] and a packing for $k=14$ was proven optimal by Joós.[^o6] For larger numbers of spheres, all results so far are conjectural.[^o2] In a 1971 paper, Goldberg found many non-optimal packings for other values of $k$ and three that may still be optimal.[^o7] Gensane improved the rest of Goldberg's packings and found good packings for up to 32 spheres.[^o2]

Goldberg also conjectured that for numbers of spheres of the form $k=\lceil p^3/2\rceil$, the optimal packing of spheres in a cube is a form of [cubic close-packing](wikipedia://en/901260). However, omitting as few as two spheres from this number allows a different and tighter packing.[^o8]
