# Weyl–Lewis–Papapetrou coordinates

solutions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weyl–Lewis–Papapetrou coordinates"}

In [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), the **Weyl–Lewis–Papapetrou coordinates** are used in solutions to the [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) region surrounding an [axisymmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axisymmetric) distribution of [mass–energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass–energy). They are named for [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), Thomas Lewis, and [Achilles Papapetrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_Papapetrou).[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

## Details {#Details}

The square of the [line element](wikipedia://en/855138) is of the form:[^o6]

$$ds^2 = -e^{2\nu}dt^2 + \rho^2 B^2 e^{-2\nu}(d\phi - \omega dt)^2 + e^{2(\lambda - \nu)}(d\rho^2 + dz^2)$$

where $(t, \rho, \phi, z)$ are the cylindrical Weyl–Lewis–Papapetrou coordinates in $3+1$-dimensional [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758), and $\lambda$, $\nu$, $\omega$, and $B$, are unknown functions of the spatial non-angular coordinates $\rho$ and $z$ only. Different authors define the functions of the coordinates differently.
