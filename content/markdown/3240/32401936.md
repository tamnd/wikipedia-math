# Hugh Hamilton (bishop)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hugh Hamilton"}

**Hugh Hamilton** (26 March 1729 – 1 December 1805) was a mathematician, [natural philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy) (scientist) and professor at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin), and later a [Church of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Ireland) bishop, [Bishop of Clonfert and Kilmacduagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Clonfert_and_Kilmacduagh) and then [Bishop of Ossory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Ossory).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born at Knock, near [Balrothery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balrothery) in [County Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Dublin) (now [Fingal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingal)), on 26 March 1729, the eldest son of Alexander (died 1768)[^o4][^o5] and Isabella Hamilton.[^o10] His father was a solicitor and politician who represented the [Killyleagh constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killyleagh_%28Parliament_of_Ireland_constituency%29) in the [Irish House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_House_of_Commons) from 1739 to 1759.[^o6] Alexander's great-grandfather Hugh Hamilton migrated from Scotland to [County Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Down) in the early 17th century. The Scottish architect [James Hamilton of Finnart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hamilton_of_Finnart) was an ancestor.[^o10] Isabella Hamilton was born Isabella Maxwell, the daughter of Robert Maxwell of Finnebrogue, [Downpatrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downpatrick).[^o6] Hugh's siblings included [George Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hamilton_%28Irish_politician%29), Baron of the [Court of Exchequer (Ireland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Exchequer_%28Ireland%29) and Charles, father of the wealthy Canadian lumber merchant and politician [George Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hamilton_%28lumber_baron%29).

Hamilton entered [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) on 17 November 1742 at the age of 13 with Thomas McDonnell as his tutor. He graduated [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) (BA) in 1747 and [Trinity Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxbridge_and_Dublin%29) (MA Dubl) in 1750.[^o10] He took the competitive examination for a vacant fellowship of the college in 1750, but the position was secured instead by his friend [Richard Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Murray_%28mathematician%29), who was a few years older. Two fellowships became vacant the following year and Hamilton was elected to one of them at the age of 22.[^o7] He was appointed [Erasmus Smith's Professor of Natural and Experimental Philosophy at Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Smith's_Professor_of_Natural_and_Experimental_Philosophy_at_Trinity_College_Dublin) in 1759[^o8] and that same year graduated [Bachelor of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Divinity) (BD).[^o10] He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) on 19 February 1761[^o9] and graduated [Doctor of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity) (DD) in 1762.[^o10]

Trinity College presented him to the [rectory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28ecclesiastical%29) of [Kilmacrenan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilmacrenan) in the diocese of [Raphoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphoe), [County Donegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Donegal), in 1764. This was a small [living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefice) in the gift of the college, for which he resigned his fellowship. He retained the Erasmus Smith's chair, however,[^o7] being succeeded in that by Thomas Wilson in 1769.[^o11] He resigned from Kilmacrenan in 1767 and become [vicar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicar) of [St. Anne's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Ann's_Church,_Dawson_Street) in Dublin.

He then became [Dean of Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_Armagh), the chief resident cleric of [St Patrick's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_Cathedral,_Armagh_%28Church_of_Ireland%29) in [Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armagh), from April 1768 to 1796.[^o10] Finding the existing dean's house inconvenient and poorly situated, he had a new one built in a better location[^o7] just off Portadown Road, now known as Dean's Hill. The house, of three stories and a semi-basement,[^o13] was built in 1772–74.[^o12] The house was later sold by the church and the present owners provide bed and breakfast accommodation in it.[^o13] While dean he also acted as treasurer for the infirmary or county hospital, he established Sunday schools in the districts of the parish, and he founded a charitable loan for poor tradesmen. He was also instrumental in planning a piped water supply for the town, which was later put into effect.[^o7] Hamilton was one of the 38 original members of the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy) when it was founded in 1785.[^o14] [Gilbert Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Stuart) painted his portrait in about 1790.[^o15]

He was promoted to [Bishop of Clonfert and Kilmacduagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Clonfert_and_Kilmacduagh) on 20 January 1796, without seeking it.[^o20] On 24 January 1799 he was [translated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_%28ecclesiastical%29) to Ossory,[^o16] where he was bishop until 1805.[^o17] He died of a fever at [Kilkenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilkenny) on 1 December 1805.[^o18] He was buried in the graveyard of [St Canice's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Canice's_Cathedral) at Kilkenny, and there is a memorial to him inside the cathedral.

## Works {#Works}

Hamilton wrote a mathematical treatise on conic sections called *De Sectionibus Conicis: Tractatus Geometricus*, published in 1758.[^o10] In this book he "was the first to deduce the properties of the conic section from the properties of the cone, by demonstrations which were general, unencumbered by lemmas, and proceeding in a more natural and perspicuous order", according to writer [James Wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wills_%28poet%29) in 1847.[^o20] The work was acclaimed for its lucidity and [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) described it as a perfect book.[^o21] It was "soon adopted in all the British universities"[^o20] and was translated from Latin into English as *A Geometrical Treatise of the Conic Sections* in 1773.

He also wrote *Philosophical Essays on Vapours* (1767), *Four Introductory Lectures on Natural Philosophy* (1774), and *An Essay on the Existence and Attributes of the Supreme Being* (1784). His principal works were collected and republished, with a memoir, as *The Works of the Right Rev. Hugh Hamilton* by his eldest son, Alexander Hamilton, in two volumes in 1809.[^o10]

## Family {#Family}

Hamilton married Isabella, daughter of Hans Widman Wood of Rosmead, [County Westmeath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Westmeath), in 1772. Isabella's mother Frances was the twin sister of [Edward King, 1st Earl of Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_King,_1st_Earl_of_Kingston). Hugh and Isabella had five sons and two daughters. They were Alexander, who was a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister); Frances; Hans, who was rector of [Knocktopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocktopher) and associated with the [Carrickshock incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrickshock_incident) of 1831; Isabella; Henry; [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hamilton_%28biblical_scholar%29), who was a biblical scholar;[^o23][^o24] and Hugh,[^o25] who married Elizabeth Staples, a daughter of [John Staples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Staples), a Member of Parliament. The younger Hugh was the great-grandfather of Clive Staples Lewis, better known as [C. S. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._S._Lewis).[^o26] Bishop Hugh Hamilton was a great-great-great-grandfather of the mathematicians [John Lighton Synge](wikipedia://en/1252509) and his brother [Edward Hutchinson Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Hutchinson_Synge).[^o27] [Dodgson Hamilton Madden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodgson_Hamilton_Madden), the High Court judge and noted scholar, was the Bishop's great-grandson.
