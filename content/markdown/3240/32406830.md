# James J. Andrews (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James J. Andrews"}

**James J. Andrews** (March 18, 1930 – July 28, 1998) was an American mathematician, a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University) who specialized in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).[^o2]

:::

Andrews was born March 18, 1930, in [Seneca Falls, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca_Falls,_New_York).[^o2] He did his undergraduate studies at [Hofstra College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstra_College),[^o2] and earned his doctorate in 1957 from the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) under the supervision of [M. K. Fort, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._K._Fort,_Jr.)[^o3] He worked at [Oak Ridge National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Ridge_National_Laboratory), the University of Georgia, and the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) before joining the FSU faculty in 1961. Andrews was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1963–64.[^o4] From 1965 to 1967, he looked into [cryptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptology) research at the Institute for Defense Analysis, [Naval Postgraduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Postgraduate_School), [Monterey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey), California.[pg5](https://web.archive.org/web/20120819094237/http://www.fsu.edu/~fstime/FS-Times/Volume4/oct98web/5oct98.html) He retired in 1994,[^o2][^o5] and died July 28, 1998, in [Tallahassee, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallahassee,_Florida).[^o2][^o6]

Andrews is known with [Morton L. Curtis](wikipedia://en/32407013) for the [Andrews–Curtis conjecture](wikipedia://en/7754592) concerning [Nielsen transformations](wikipedia://en/18400182) of balanced [group presentations](wikipedia://en/99494).[^o2] Andrews and Curtis formulated the conjecture in a 1965 paper;[^o7] it remains open.
