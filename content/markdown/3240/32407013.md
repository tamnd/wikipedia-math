# Morton L. Curtis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morton L. Curtis"}

**Morton Landers Curtis** (November 11, 1921 – February 4, 1989)[^o2] was an American mathematician, an expert on [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and the [W. L. Moody, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lewis_Moody,_Jr.) Professor of Mathematics at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University).[^o3]

:::

Born in [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas),[^o4] Curtis earned a bachelor's degree in 1948 from [Texas A&I University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University–Kingsville),[^o3] and received his Ph.D. in 1951 from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) under the supervision of [Raymond Louis Wilder](wikipedia://en/4297377). Subsequently, he taught mathematics at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University) before moving to Rice. At Rice, he was the Ph.D. advisor of well-known mathematician [John Morgan](wikipedia://en/8487966).

Curtis is, with [James J. Andrews](wikipedia://en/32406830), the namesake of the [Andrews–Curtis conjecture](wikipedia://en/7754592) concerning [Nielsen transformations](wikipedia://en/18400182) of balanced [group presentations](wikipedia://en/99494). Andrews and Curtis formulated the conjecture in a 1965 paper;[^o6] it remains open. Together with [Gustav A. Hedlund](wikipedia://en/18794778) and [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon), he proved the [Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon theorem](wikipedia://en/18903091) characterizing [cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata) as being defined by [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) functions on a [shift space](wikipedia://en/15470598).[^o7]

Curtis was the author of two books, *Matrix Groups* (Springer-Verlag, 1979), and *Abstract Linear Algebra* (Springer-Verlag, 1990).
