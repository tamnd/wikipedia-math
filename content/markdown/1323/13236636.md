# Victor Guillemin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Victor Guillemin"}

**Victor William Guillemin** (born 1937 in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston)) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He works at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in the field of [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), and he has also made contributions to the fields of [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841), [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713), and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). [^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Guillemin obtained a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1959, as well as an [M. A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1960.[^o4] He received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics from Harvard University in 1962; his dissertation, entitled *Theory of Finite [G-Structures](wikipedia://en/1277699),* was written under the direction of [Shlomo Sternberg](wikipedia://en/5375996).[^o6]

He worked at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) from 1963 to 1966 and then moved to the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) as assistant professor. He became associate professor in 1969 and full professor in 1973.[^o3][^o4]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Guillemin was awarded in 1969 a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship),[^o8] in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Guggenheim_Fellowships_awarded_in_1988) a [Guggenheim fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship)[^o9] and in 1996 a [Humboldt fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_fellowship).[^o10] In 1970 he was [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065#1970,_Nice) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Nice.[^o11]

He was elected a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1984[^o12] and of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1985.[^o13] In 2003, he was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Lifetime Achievement by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14][^o4] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o15]

## Research {#Research}

Guillemin worked in several areas in analysis and geometry, including [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841), [symplectic](wikipedia://en/294298) [group actions](wikipedia://en/39904936), and [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) on [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).[^o4]

He is the author or co-author of numerous books and monographs, including a widely used textbook on [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), written jointly with Alan Pollack in 1974, and a monograph on symplectic geometry in physics, written jointly with Shlomo Sternberg in 1986.[^o17][^o18]

## Family {#Family}

Victor Guillemins's uncle [Ernst Guillemin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Guillemin) was a Professor of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at MIT, his younger brother [Robert Charles Guillemin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalk_Sam) was a sidewalk artist, his brother-in-law [Ray Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Raphael) is an historian, and his daughter [Karen Guillemin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Guillemin) is a Professor of Biology at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon).
