# Furstenberg's proof of the infinitude of primes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Furstenberg's proof of the infinitude of primes"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [Hillel Furstenberg](wikipedia://en/6450152)'s proof of the infinitude of primes is a [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) that the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) contain [infinitely](wikipedia://en/142679) many [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). When examined closely, the proof is less a statement about topology than a statement about certain properties of [arithmetic sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_sequence).[^o7][^o2] Unlike [Euclid's classical proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_Theorem#Euclid's_proof), Furstenberg's proof is a [proof by contradiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_by_contradiction). The proof was published in 1955 in the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* while he was still an [undergraduate student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_student) at [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University).

:::

## Furstenberg's proof {#Furstenberg's_proof}

Define a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) on the integers $\mathbb{Z}$, called the [evenly spaced integer topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenly_spaced_integer_topology), by declaring a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) *U* ⊆ $\mathbb{Z}$ to be an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is a [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of arithmetic sequences *S*(*a*, *b*) for *a* ≠ 0, or is [empty](wikipedia://en/9566) (which can be seen as a [nullary union](wikipedia://en/47949#Arbitrary_unions) (empty union) of arithmetic sequences), where

$$S(a, b) = \{ a n + b \mid n \in \mathbb{Z} \} = a \mathbb{Z} + b.$$

Equivalently, *U* is open if and only if for every *x* in *U* there is some non-zero integer *a* such that *S*(*a*, *x*) ⊆ *U*. The [axioms for a topology](wikipedia://en/30450) are easily verified:

- ∅ is open by definition, and $\mathbb{Z}$ is just the sequence *S*(1, 0), and so is open as well.
- Any union of open sets is open: for any collection of open sets *U*<sub>*i*</sub> and *x* in their union *U*, any of the numbers *a*<sub>*i*</sub> for which *S*(*a*<sub>*i*</sub>, *x*) ⊆ *U*<sub>*i*</sub> also shows that *S*(*a*<sub>*i*</sub>, *x*) ⊆ *U*.
- The intersection of two (and hence finitely many) open sets is open: let *U*<sub>1</sub> and *U*<sub>2</sub> be open sets and let *x* ∈ *U*<sub>1</sub> ∩ *U*<sub>2</sub> (with numbers *a*<sub>1</sub> and *a*<sub>2</sub> establishing membership). Set *a* to be the [least common multiple](wikipedia://en/17961) of *a*<sub>1</sub> and *a*<sub>2</sub>. Then *S*(*a*, *x*) ⊆ *S*(*a*<sub>*i*</sub>, *x*) ⊆ *U*<sub>i</sub>.

This topology has two notable properties:

1. Since any non-empty open set contains an infinite sequence, a finite non-empty set cannot be open; put another way, the [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) of a finite non-empty set cannot be a [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279).
1. The basis sets *S*(*a*, *b*) are [both open and closed](wikipedia://en/233398): they are open by definition, and we can write *S*(*a*, *b*) as the complement of an open set as follows:

$$S(a, b) = \mathbb{Z} \setminus \bigcup_{j = 1}^{a - 1} S(a, b + j).$$

The only integers that are not integer multiples of prime numbers are −1 and +1, i.e.

$$\mathbb{Z} \setminus \{ -1, + 1 \} = \bigcup_{p \mathrm{\, prime}} S(p, 0).$$

Now, by the first topological property, the set on the left-hand side cannot be closed. On the other hand, by the second topological property, the sets *S*(*p*, 0) are closed. So, if there were only finitely many prime numbers, then the set on the right-hand side would be a finite union of closed sets, and hence closed. This would be a [contradiction](wikipedia://en/75807), so there must be infinitely many prime numbers.

## Topological properties {#Topological_properties}

The [evenly spaced integer topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenly_spaced_integer_topology) on $\mathbb{Z}$ is the topology induced by the inclusion $\mathbb{Z}\subset \hat\mathbb{Z}$, where $\hat\mathbb{Z}$ is the [profinite integer](wikipedia://en/46531203) ring with its profinite topology.

It is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) $\mathbb{Q}$ with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) inherited from the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line),[^o5] which makes it clear that any finite subset of it, such as $\{-1, +1\}$, cannot be open.
