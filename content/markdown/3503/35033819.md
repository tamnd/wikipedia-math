# Smooth scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smooth scheme"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **smooth scheme** over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) which is well approximated by [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) near any point. Smoothness is one way of making precise the notion of a scheme with no [singular](wikipedia://en/981915) points. A special case is the notion of a smooth [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) over a field. Smooth schemes play the role in algebraic geometry of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

First, let *X* be an affine scheme of [finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#finite) over a field *k*. Equivalently, *X* has a [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986) into affine space *A<sup>n</sup>* over *k* for some natural number *n*. Then *X* is the closed subscheme defined by some equations *g*<sub>1</sub> = 0, ..., *g*<sub>*r*</sub> = 0, where each *g<sub>i</sub>* is in the polynomial ring *k*\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>\]. The affine scheme *X* is **smooth** of dimension *m* over *k* if *X* has [dimension](wikipedia://en/253251) at least *m* in a neighborhood of each point, and the matrix of derivatives (∂*g*<sub>*i*</sub>/∂*x*<sub>*j*</sub>) has rank at least *n*−*m* everywhere on *X*.[^o4] (It follows that *X* has dimension equal to *m* in a neighborhood of each point.) Smoothness is independent of the choice of immersion of *X* into affine space.

The condition on the matrix of derivatives is understood to mean that the closed subset of *X* where all (*n*−*m*) × (*n* − *m*) [minors](wikipedia://en/262107) of the matrix of derivatives are zero is the empty set. Equivalently, the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) generated by all *g*<sub>*i*</sub> and all those minors is the whole polynomial ring.

In geometric terms, the matrix of derivatives (∂*g*<sub>*i*</sub>/∂*x*<sub>*j*</sub>) at a point *p* in *X* gives a linear map *F*<sup>*n*</sup> → *F*<sup>*r*</sup>, where *F* is the residue field of *p*. The kernel of this map is called the [Zariski tangent space](wikipedia://en/510207) of *X* at *p*. Smoothness of *X* means that the dimension of the Zariski tangent space is equal to the dimension of *X* near each point; at a [singular point](wikipedia://en/981915), the Zariski tangent space would be bigger.

More generally, a scheme *X* over a field *k* is **smooth** over *k* if each point of *X* has an open neighborhood which is a smooth affine scheme of some dimension over *k*. In particular, a smooth scheme over *k* is [locally of finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#locally_of_finite_type).

There is a more general notion of a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041) of schemes, which is roughly a morphism with smooth fibers. In particular, a scheme *X* is smooth over a field *k* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the morphism *X* → Spec *k* is smooth.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

A smooth scheme over a field is [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#regular) and hence [normal](wikipedia://en/2629423). In particular, a smooth scheme over a field is [reduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#reduced).

Define a **variety** over a field *k* to be an [integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#integral) [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#separated) scheme of finite type over *k*. Then any smooth separated scheme of finite type over *k* is a finite disjoint union of smooth varieties over *k*.

For a smooth variety *X* over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), the space *X*(**C**) of complex points of *X* is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742), using the classical (Euclidean) topology. Likewise, for a smooth variety *X* over the real numbers, the space *X*(**R**) of real points is a real [manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219), possibly empty.

For any scheme *X* that is locally of finite type over a field *k*, there is a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) Ω<sup>1</sup> of [differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahler_differentials) on *X*. The scheme *X* is smooth over *k* if and only if Ω<sup>1</sup> is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) of rank equal to the dimension of *X* near each point.[^o7] In that case, Ω<sup>1</sup> is called the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) of *X*. The [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of a smooth scheme over *k* can be defined as the dual bundle, *TX* = (Ω<sup>1</sup>)<sup>\*</sup>.

Smoothness is a [geometric property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_property), meaning that for any [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *E* of *k*, a scheme *X* is smooth over *k* if and only if the scheme *X<sub>E</sub>* := *X* ×<sub>Spec *k*</sub> Spec *E* is smooth over *E*. For a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) *k*, a scheme *X* is smooth over *k* if and only if *X* is locally of finite type over *k* and *X* is [regular](wikipedia://en/43016199).

:::

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Generic smoothness"}

## Generic smoothness {#Generic_smoothness}

A scheme *X* is said to be **generically smooth** of dimension *n* over *k* if *X* contains an open dense subset that is smooth of dimension *n* over *k*. Every variety over a perfect field (in particular an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018)) is generically smooth.[^o10]

:::

::: {#o12 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Affine space and [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) are smooth schemes over a field *k*.
- An example of a smooth [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) in projective space **P**<sup>*n*</sup> over *k* is the [Fermat hypersurface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_hypersurface) *x*<sub>0</sub><sup>*d*</sup> + ... + *x*<sub>*n*</sub><sup>*d*</sup> = 0, for any positive integer *d* that is invertible in *k*.
- An example of a singular (non-smooth) scheme over a field *k* is the closed subscheme *x*<sup>2</sup> = 0 in the affine line *A*<sup>1</sup> over *k*.
- An example of a singular (non-smooth) variety over *k* is the [cuspidal cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuspidal_cubic) curve *x*<sup>2</sup> = *y*<sup>3</sup> in the affine plane *A*<sup>2</sup>, which is smooth outside the origin (*x*,*y*) = (0,0).
- A 0-dimensional variety *X* over a field *k* is of the form *X* = Spec *E*, where *E* is a finite extension field of *k*. The variety *X* is smooth over *k* if and only if *E* is a [separable](wikipedia://en/363340) extension of *k*. Thus, if *E* is not separable over *k*, then *X* is a regular scheme but is not smooth over *k*. For example, let *k* be the field of rational functions **F**<sub>*p*</sub>(*t*) for a prime number *p*, and let *E* = **F**<sub>*p*</sub>(*t*<sup>1/*p*</sup>); then Spec *E* is a variety of dimension 0 over *k* which is a regular scheme, but not smooth over *k*.
- [Schubert varieties](wikipedia://en/10469987) are in general not smooth.

:::
