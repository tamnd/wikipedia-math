# Gordon F. Newell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gordon F. Newell"}

**Gordon Frank Newell** (January 25, 1925 – February 16, 2001)[^o2] was an American scientist, known for his contributions to applied mathematics, in particular [traffic flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_flow) analysis and [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578). Newell authored over one hundred articles and wrote several books. The [Gordon–Newell theorem](wikipedia://en/21256722) is named after him and his colleague [William J. Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_J._Gordon). Their algorithms helped form the basis of most modern automatically controlled and networked traffic-light control systems.

:::

He obtained a [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.) from [Union College, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College,_New_York) (1945) and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) from [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) (1950). He continued his focus on [solid-state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics) and the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) of [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) with research teams under [Elliott Montroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Montroll) at [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) (1950–53). His next job was at the [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) faculty at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) (1953), where he began studies of automobile traffic analysis and road signalling theory. His final period was with the [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) faculty at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) (1965–91), where he remained until retirement. He then held a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) of Transportation Engineering position. The annual *Gordon Newell fellowship* has been awarded since 2002.

He was born in [Dayton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio) and raised in [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York). Newell died in an automobile accident in [Carmel-by-the-Sea, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmel-by-the-Sea,_California), after attending a party with friends.[^o3]

## Books and publications {#Books_and_publications}

- *Mathematical Models of Freely Flowing Traffic Flow*. [Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research:_A_Journal_of_the_Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences), 3 (1955)
- *Statistical Analysis of the Flow of Highway Traffic through a Signalized Intersection*, in Q. Appl. Math. 13, 1956
- *Maintaining a bus schedule*, Proceedings of 2nd Australian Road Research Board, Part 1, pp. 388–393, 1964. With [R. J. Potts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._J._Potts)
- *Cyclic Queuing Systems with Restricted Length Queues*, Op.res., Vol. 15, No. 2, March–April 1967, pp. 266–277. With [William J. Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_J._Gordon)
- *Applications of queueing theory* ([Chapman & Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_&_Hall), 1971).
- *Scheduling, Location, Transportation and Continuum Mechanics; Some Simple Approximations to Optimization Problems*, in SIAM J. Appl. Math. 25, 1973
- *Control of pairing of vehicles on a public transportation route, two vehicles, one control point*, Transportation Science, Vol. 8, No. 3, pp. 248–264, 1974
- *Traffic on Transportation Networks* ([MIT Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Press), 1980)
- *Asymptotic Distribution of Eigenvalues for the Multidimensional Schroedinger Equation*, in J. Math. Physics 21, 1980
- *Theory of Highway Traffic Signals* ([Institute of Transportation Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Transportation_Studies), 1988)
- *Theory of Highway Traffic Flow, 1945–1965* (Inst. Trans. Studies, 1995)
