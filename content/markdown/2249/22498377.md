# Zeldovich pancake

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zeldovich pancake"}

A **Zeldovich pancake** is a theoretical condensation of gas out of a primordial density fluctuation following the [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116). In 1970, [Yakov B. Zeldovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakov_Borisovich_Zel'dovich) showed that for an [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381) of gas on a supergalactic scale, an approximation can be used that will model the collapse as occurring most rapidly along the shortest axis, resulting in a pancake form.[^o2] This approximation assumes that the [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381) of gas is sufficiently large that the effect of pressure is negligible and only [gravitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) attraction needs to be considered. That is, the gas will collapse without being significantly perturbed by outward pressure. This assumption is especially valid if the collapse occurs before the [recombination](wikipedia://en/26244469) era that resulted in the formation of [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) atoms.[^o3]

:::

In 1989, Zeldovich and S. F. Shandarin showed that initial overlapping density fluctuations of random Gaussian fields would result in "dense pancakes, filaments, and compact clumps of matter".[^o4] This model became known as a top-down model of galactic formation, with the supergalactic condensation fragmenting into [protogalaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protogalaxies).[^o5] The formation of plane concentrations would compress the gas through [shock waves](wikipedia://en/146253) generated during the collapse, increasing the temperature.[^o6]

At a higher level, the collapse of larger structures according to the [Zeldovich approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeldovich_approximation) is known as second-generation pancakes or superpancakes. At still higher levels there is a transition to a hierarchical clustering model in which a hierarchy of collapsing structures exist. The Truncated Zeldovich Approximation allowed the method to be applied to these hierarchical, or bottom-up models of cosmological structure. This approach truncates the power law [fluctuation spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluctuation_spectrum) at large values of *k* before applying the Zeldovich Approximation.[^o7] Generalizations of the Zel'dovich Approximation have also been shown to apply in the case of a non-zero [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992).[^o8]

The first example of a Zeldovich pancake may have been identified in 1991 using the [Very Large Array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_Large_Array) in [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico).[^o9][^o10]
