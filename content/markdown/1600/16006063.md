# Frederic M. Lord

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frederic M. Lord"}

**Frederic Mather Lord** (November 12, 1912 – February 5, 2000) was a [psychometrician](wikipedia://en/24982) for [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service). The [SAT](wikipedia://en/144716), [GRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Record_Examinations), [GMAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMAT), [LSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSAT) and [TOEFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOEFL) are all based on Lord's research.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Lord was born on November 12, 1912, in Hanover, New Hampshire. His great-great-grandfather, [Nathan Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Lord), served as the sixth president of [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), from which Lord graduated with a bachelor's degree in Sociology in 1936.[^o4] He later earned a master's degree in Educational Psychology from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), followed by a PhD in Psychology from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1951.[^o4][^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Lord first worked for the [Carnegie Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Foundation_for_the_Advancement_of_Teaching) in 1944.[^o4][^o5] By 1950, he began working for the [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service) (ETS).[^o5] He was the source of much of the seminal research on [item response theory](wikipedia://en/420159),[^o7] including two important books: *Statistical Theories of Mental Test Scores* (1968, with [Melvin R. Novick](wikipedia://en/58886524), and two chapters by [Allan Birnbaum](wikipedia://en/1590647)), and *Applications of Item Response Theory to Practical Testing Problems* (1980).

Lord's research shaped the [SAT](wikipedia://en/144716), [GRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Record_Examinations), [GMAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMAT), [LSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSAT) and the [TOEFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOEFL).[^o4][^o5] Lord was called the "Father of Modern Testing" by the [National Council on Measurement in Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_on_Measurement_in_Education).[^o8]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Lord was married twice. He had three sons with his first wife. His second wife was Muriel Kendall.[^o4]

Lord died on February 5, 2000, in [Naples, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples,_Florida), at 87.[^o4][^o5]
