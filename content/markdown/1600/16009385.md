# Taub–NUT space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Taub–NUT space"}

The **Taub–NUT metric** (,[^o2] ) is an [exact solution](wikipedia://en/1686520) to [Einstein's equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein's_equations). It may be considered a first attempt in finding the metric of a spinning black hole. It is sometimes also used in [homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneity_%28physics%29) but [anisotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropic) [cosmological models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_model) formulated in the framework of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024).

:::

The underlying Taub space was found by (txt), and extended to a larger manifold by (txt, 915–923), whose initials form the "NUT" of "Taub–NUT".

## Description {#Description}

Taub's solution is an empty space solution of Einstein's equations with topology **R**×**S**<sup>3</sup> and metric (or equivalently [line element](wikipedia://en/855138))

$$g =-dt^2/U(t) + 4l^2U(t)(d\psi+ \cos\theta d\phi)^2+(t^2+l^2)(d\theta^2+(\sin\theta)^2d\phi^2)$$

where

$$U(t)=\frac{2mt+l^2-t^2}{t^2+l^2}$$

and *m* and *l* are positive constants.

Taub's metric has coordinate singularities at $U=0, t=m+(m^2+l^2)^{1/2}$, and Newman, Tamburino and Unti showed how to extend the metric across these surfaces.

## Related work {#Related_work}

### Kerr metric {#Kerr_metric}

When [Roy Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Kerr) developed the [Kerr metric](wikipedia://en/456715) for spinning black holes in 1963, he ended up with a four-parameter solution, one of which was the mass and another the angular momentum of the central body. One of the two other parameters was the NUT-parameter, which he threw out of his solution because he found it to be nonphysical since it caused the metric to be not asymptotically flat,[^o8][^o9] while other sources interpret it either as a gravomagnetic monopole parameter of the central mass,[^o10] or a twisting property of the surrounding spacetime.[^o11]

### Misner spacetime {#Misner_spacetime}

A simplified 1+1-dimensional version of the Taub–NUT spacetime is the [Misner spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misner_space).
