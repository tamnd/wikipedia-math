# Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem** relates [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) on spaces of continuous functions on a [locally compact space](wikipedia://en/48632) to [measures](wikipedia://en/19873) in measure theory. The theorem is named for (txt) who introduced it for [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324), (txt) who extended the result to some non-compact spaces, and (txt) who extended the result to [compact Hausdorff spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Hausdorff_space).

:::

There are many closely related variations of the theorem, as the linear functionals can be complex, real, or [positive](wikipedia://en/454930), the space they are defined on may be the unit interval or a compact space or a [locally compact space](wikipedia://en/48632), the continuous functions may be [vanishing at infinity](wikipedia://en/461986) or have [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support), and the measures can be [Baire measures](wikipedia://en/13520509) or [regular Borel measures](wikipedia://en/542326) or [Radon measures](wikipedia://en/521933) or [signed measures](wikipedia://en/2985811) or [complex measures](wikipedia://en/3057532).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The representation theorem for positive linear functionals on Cc(X)"}

## The representation theorem for positive linear functionals on *C<sub>c</sub>*(*X*) {#The_representation_theorem_for_positive_linear_functionals_on_Cc(X)}

The statement of the theorem for positive [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functionals) on $C_{c}(X)$, the space of [compactly](wikipedia://en/6042) [supported](wikipedia://en/381013) complex-valued [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122), is as follows:

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem** Let $X$ be a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) and $\psi$ a [positive linear functional](wikipedia://en/454930) on $C_{c}(X)$. Then there exists a unique positive [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) $\mu$ on $X$ such that[^n1]

$$\psi(f) = \int_X f(x) \, d\mu(x), \quad \forall f \in C_c(X),$$

which has the following additional properties for some $\Sigma$ containing the [Borel σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/43327) on $X$:

- $\mu(K)<\infty$ for every compact $K\subset X$,
- **[Outer regularity](wikipedia://en/3390453)**: $\mu(E) = \inf \{\mu(U): E \subseteq U, U \mbox{ open}\}$ holds for every [Borel set](wikipedia://en/43327) $E\in\Sigma$;
- **[Inner regularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_regular_measure)**: $\mu(E) = \sup \{\mu(K): K \subseteq E, K \mbox{ compact}\}$ holds whenever $E$ is open or when $E$ is Borel and $\mu(E)<\infty$;
- $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ is a [complete measure space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_measure_space)

One approach to [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) is to start with a [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933),[^n2] defined as a positive linear functional on $C_{c}(X)$. This is the way adopted by [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394); it does of course assume that $X$ starts life as a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), rather than simply as a set. For locally compact spaces an integration theory is then recovered.

Without the condition of [regularity](wikipedia://en/3390453) the Borel measure need not be unique. For example, let $X$ be the set of ordinals at most equal to the [first uncountable ordinal](wikipedia://en/20860540) $\Omega$, with the topology generated by "[open intervals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval)". The linear functional taking a continuous function to its value at $\Omega$ corresponds to the regular Borel measure with a point mass at $\Omega$. However it also corresponds to the (non-regular) Borel measure that assigns measure $1$ to any Borel set $B\subseteq [0,\Omega]$ if there is [closed and unbounded set](wikipedia://en/1251559) $C\subseteq [0,\Omega]$ with $C\subseteq B$, and assigns measure $0$ to other Borel sets. (In particular the [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) $\{\Omega\}$ gets measure $0$, contrary to the point mass measure.)

:::

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The representation theorem for the continuous dual of C0(X)"}

## The representation theorem for the continuous dual of *C*<sub>0</sub>(*X*) {#The_representation_theorem_for_the_continuous_dual_of_C0(X)}

The following representation, also referred to as the *Riesz–Markov theorem*, gives a concrete realisation of the [topological dual space](wikipedia://en/7988#Continuous_dual_space) of $C_{0}(X)$, the set of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on $X$ which [vanish at infinity](wikipedia://en/461986).

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem** Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space. For any continuous [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) $\psi$ on $C_{0}(X)$, there is a unique complex-valued [regular Borel measure](wikipedia://en/542326) $\mu$ on $X$ such that

$$\psi(f) = \int_X f(x) \, d \mu(x), \quad \forall f \in C_0(X).$$

A complex-valued Borel measure $\mu$ is called regular if the positive measure $| \mu |$ satisfies the [regularity](wikipedia://en/3390453) conditions defined above. The norm of $\psi$ as a linear functional is the [total variation](wikipedia://en/683561) of $\mu$, that is

$$\|\psi\| = |\mu|(X).$$

Finally, $\psi$ is [positive](wikipedia://en/454930) if and only if the measure $\mu$ is positive.

One can deduce this statement about linear functionals from the statement about positive linear functionals by first showing that a bounded linear functional can be written as a finite linear combination of positive ones.

:::

:::

## Historical remark {#Historical_remark}

In its original form by (txt) the theorem states that every continuous linear functional $A$ over the space $C([0, 1])$ of continuous functions $f$ in the interval $[0, 1]$ can be represented as

$$A[f(x)] = \int_0^1 f(x)\,d\alpha(x),$$

where $\alpha(x)$ is a function of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) on the interval $[0, 1]$, and the integral is a [Riemann–Stieltjes integral](wikipedia://en/330491). Since there is a one-to-one correspondence between Borel regular measures in the interval and functions of bounded variation (that assigns to each function of bounded variation the corresponding Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure, and the integral with respect to the Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure agrees with the Riemann–Stieltjes integral for continuous functions), the above stated theorem generalizes the original statement of F. Riesz.[^n3]
