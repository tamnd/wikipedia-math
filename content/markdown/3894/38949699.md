# William Herrick Macaulay

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Herrick Macaulay"}

*Image: William Herrick Macaulay.jpg.* William Herrick Macaulay, Vice-Provost, Kings College Cambridge

**William Herrick Macaulay** (16 November 1853 – 28 November 1936) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_the_United_Kingdom) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), Fellow and Vice-Provost of [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), and close friend of [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695).[^o2] He also corresponded with [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes).[^o3]

:::

## Family {#Family}

W. H. Macaulay was born in [Hodnet, Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodnet,_Shropshire), in 1853, son of the Rev. Samuel Herrick Macaulay, rector of Hodnet and grandson of the Rev. [Aulay Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulay_Macaulay_%28writer%29). His brothers included [George Campbell Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Campbell_Macaulay), the father of Dame [Rose Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Macaulay), and [Reginald Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Macaulay). He never married, and died in [Clent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clent), near [Stourbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stourbridge), [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire), in 1936.[^o5]

## Education, career and later life {#Education,_career_and_later_life}

Unlike his elder brother (who went to [Eton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College)), Macaulay was a pupil at [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College). Interested in the applied sciences rather than classics, upon leaving school he matriculated at [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University), where he was awarded both a B.A. degree and a prize fellowship in 1874. In October that year, he began a further degree in mathematics at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), where he was made an Abbott Scholar and graduated as sixth [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28Cambridge%29) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1878.[^o7]

Macaulay is best known for his work in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering), most notably for his proposed [mechanical technique](wikipedia://en/12186373) in [structural analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_analysis) for the purposes of beam or shaft deflection. He was made a Fellow of King's in 1879, which was followed by further appointments as University Lecturer in mathematics in 1883 and as College Lecturer in applied mechanics a year later. He subsequently helped to establish the Department of Engineering at Cambridge.[^o7] He also worked for a time in a granite quarry.[^o8]

Macaulay was appointed [Bursar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursar) of King's in 1887, and following a decade-long role as Tutor in the college he became its Vice-Provost in 1918, remaining in post for six years until his retirement.[^o7] T. E. B. Howarth, in his book on inter-war Cambridge, remarked that Macaulay was "the last fellow \[of King's\] to follow the old practice of keeping a horse and riding regularly to hounds."[^o9]
