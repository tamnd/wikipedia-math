# Standard L-function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Standard L-function"}

In mathematics, the term **standard L-function** refers to a particular type of [automorphic L-function](wikipedia://en/32328300) described by [Robert P. Langlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_P._Langlands).[^o2][^o3] Here, *standard* refers to the finite-dimensional representation r being the standard representation of the [L-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual) as a matrix group.

:::

## Relations to other L-functions {#Relations_to_other_L-functions}

Standard L-functions are thought to be the most general type of [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283). Conjecturally, they include all examples of L-functions, and in particular are expected to coincide with the [Selberg class](wikipedia://en/2200454). Furthermore, all L-functions over arbitrary [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) are widely thought to be instances of standard L-functions for the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(n) over the rational numbers Q. This makes them a useful testing ground for statements about L-functions, since it sometimes affords structure from the theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146).

## Analytic properties {#Analytic_properties}

These L-functions were proven to always be entire by [Roger Godement](wikipedia://en/971810) and [Hervé Jacquet](wikipedia://en/25600925),[^o6] with the sole exception of [Riemann ζ-function](wikipedia://en/25809), which arises for *n* = 1. Another proof was later given by [Freydoon Shahidi](wikipedia://en/29138216) using the [Langlands–Shahidi method](wikipedia://en/31789393). For a broader discussion, see (Gelbart Shahidi 1988).[^o8]
