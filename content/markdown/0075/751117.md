# Charles-Jean de La Vallée Poussin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles-Jean de La Vallée Poussin"}

**Charles-Jean Étienne Gustave Nicolas, baron de La Vallée Poussin** (; 14 August 1866 – 2 March 1962) was a Belgian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is best known for proving the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).

:::

The King of Belgium ennobled him with the title of [baron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron).

## Biography {#Biography}

De La Vallée Poussin was born in [Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven), Belgium. He studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Catholic University of Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Leuven_%281834–1968%29) under his uncle Louis-Philippe Gilbert, after he had earned his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering). De La Vallée Poussin was encouraged to study for a doctorate in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and mathematics, and in 1891, at the age of just 25, he became an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) in mathematical analysis.

De La Vallée Poussin became a professor at the same university (as was his father, [Charles Louis de la Vallée Poussin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Louis-Joseph-Xavier_de_la_Vallée-Poussin), who taught [mineralogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineralogy) and [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology)) in 1892. De La Vallée Poussin was awarded with Gilbert's chair when Gilbert died. While he was a professor there, de La Vallée Poussin carried out research in mathematical analysis and the theory of numbers, and in 1905 was awarded the Decennial Prize for Pure Mathematics 1894–1903. He was awarded this prize a second time in 1924 for his work during 1914–23.

In 1898, de La Vallée Poussin was appointed as the correspondent to the [Royal Belgian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Belgian_Academy_of_Sciences), and he became a Member of the academy in 1908. In 1923, he became the President of the Division of Sciences.

In August 1914, de La Vallée Poussin escaped from Leuven at the time of its destruction by the invading [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_%28German_Empire%29) of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), and he was invited to teach at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in the United States. He accepted this invitation. In 1918, de La Vallée Poussin returned to Europe to accept professorships in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) and at [the Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_%28building%29).

After the war was over, de La Vallée Poussin returned to Belgium, The International Union of Mathematicians was created, and he was invited to become its president. Between 1918 and 1925, de La Vallée Poussin traveled extensively, lecturing in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), and [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid). and then in the United States where he gave lectures at the Universities of Chicago, California, Pennsylvania, and Brown University, Yale University, Princeton University, Columbia University, and the Rice Institute of Houston.

He was awarded the [*Prix Poncelet*](wikipedia://en/20842828) for 1916.[^o4] De La Vallée Poussin was given the titles of Doctor Honoris Causa of the Universities of Paris, Toronto, Strasbourg, and Oslo, an Associate of the Institute of France, and a Member of the [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o5] Nazionale dei Lincei, Madrid, Naples, Boston. He was awarded the title of Baron by [King Albert I of the Belgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_I_of_Belgium) in 1928.

In 1961, de La Vallée Poussin fractured his shoulder, and this accident and its complications led to his death in [Watermael-Boitsfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermael-Boitsfort), near [Brussels, Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels,_Belgium), a few months later.[^o6]

A student of his, [Georges Lemaître](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Lemaître), was the first to propose the [Big Bang theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang_theory) of the formation of the [Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe).

## Work {#Work}

Although his first mathematical interests were in analysis, he became suddenly famous as he proved the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) independently of his contemporary, [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675), in 1896.

Afterwards, he found interest in [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713). He defined, for any [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) *f* on the standard [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) $[-1, 1]$, the sums

$$V_n=\frac{S_n+S_{n+1}+\cdots+S_{2n-1}}{n},$$

where

$$S_n=\frac{1}{2}c_0(f)+\sum_{i=1}^n c_i(f) T_i$$

and

$$c_i(f) \,$$

are the vectors of the [dual basis](wikipedia://en/1182982) with respect to the [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_basis) of [Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/184539) (defined as

$$(T_0/2,T_1,\ldots,T_n).$$

Note that the formula is also valid with $S_n$ being the [Fourier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Baptiste_Joseph_Fourier) sum of a $2\pi$-[periodic function](wikipedia://en/76848) $F$ such that

$$F(\theta)=f(\cos\theta). \,$$

Finally, the de La Vallée Poussin sums can be evaluated in terms of the so-called [Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357) sums (say $F_n$)

$$V_n=2F_{2n-1}-F_{n-1}. \,$$

The kernel is bounded ($V_n \le 3$) and obeys the property

$f*V_n = f \,$, if $f(x)= \sum_{j=-n}^n a_j e^{i j x}. \,$

Later, he worked on [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).

*Image: Poussin graph planar.svg.* The [Poussin graph](wikipedia://en/47091427)

He also published a counterexample to [Alfred Kempe](wikipedia://en/9092767)'s false proof of the [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949). The [Poussin graph](wikipedia://en/47091427), the graph he used for this counterexample, is named after him.

## *Cours d’analyse* {#Cours_d’analyse}

The textbooks of his mathematical analysis course have been a reference for a long time and had some international influence.[^o9]

The second edition (1909–1912) is remarkable for its introduction of the Lebesgue integral. It was in 1912, "the only textbook on analysis containing both Lebesgue integral and its application to Fourier series, and a general theory of approximation of functions by polynomials".[^o9]

The third edition (1914) introduced the now classical definition of [differentiability](wikipedia://en/330206) due to [Otto Stolz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Stolz). The second volume of this third edition was burnt in the [fire of Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Louvain) during the [German invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Belgium_%281914%29).

The further editions were much more conservative, returning essentially to the first edition. Starting from the eighth edition, Fernand Simonart took over the revision and the publication of the *Cours d’analyse.*
