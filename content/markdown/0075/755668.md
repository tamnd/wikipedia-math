# Leopold Löwenheim

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leopold Löwenheim"}

**Leopold Löwenheim** \[ˈle:o:pɔl̩d ˈlø:vɛnhaɪm\] (26 June 1878 in [Krefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krefeld) – 5 May 1957 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) doing work in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). The [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) regime forced him to retire because under the [Nuremberg Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws) he was considered only three quarters [Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan). In 1943 much of his work was destroyed during a [bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing) raid on Berlin. Nevertheless, he survived the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), after which he resumed teaching [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

:::

Löwenheim (1915) gave the first proof of what is now known as the [Löwenheim–Skolem theorem](wikipedia://en/341482), often considered the starting point for [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).

Leopold was the son of Ludwig Löwenheim, a mathematics teacher at the [polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_technology) in [Krefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krefeld) and Elizabeth Röhn, a writer. In 1881 the three of them left Krefeld first for [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples) and then [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) where Ludwig was a private scholar working on a comprehensive account of the influence of [Democritus](wikipedia://en/8211) on modern science. Although he hoped this would gain him a teaching job at [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University) Ludwig died in 1894.[^o4]
