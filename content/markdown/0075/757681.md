# 2,147,483,647

*Image: Leonhard Euler.jpg.* By 1772, [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) had proven that 2,147,483,647 is a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666).

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="2,147,483,647"}

The [number](wikipedia://en/21690) **2147483647** is the eighth [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908), equal to 2<sup>31</sup> − 1. It is one of only four known [double Mersenne primes](wikipedia://en/627828).[^o3]

:::

The [primality](wikipedia://en/183751) of this number was proven by [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), who reported the proof in a letter to [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784) written in 1772.[^o4] Euler used [trial division](wikipedia://en/557660), improving on [Pietro Cataldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Cataldi)'s method, so that at most 372 divisions were needed.[^o5] It thus improved upon the previous record-holding prime, 6,700,417 – also discovered by Euler – forty years earlier. The number 2,147,483,647 remained the [largest known prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_known_prime) until 1867.[^o6]

In computing, this number is the largest value that a [signed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_number) [32-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32-bit) integer field can hold.

## Barlow's prediction {#Barlow's_prediction}

At the time of its discovery, 2,147,483,647 was the [largest known prime number](wikipedia://en/1669332). In 1811, [Peter Barlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Barlow_%28mathematician%29) wrote (in *An Elementary Investigation of the Theory of Numbers*):

> Euler ascertained that 2<sup>31</sup> − 1 = 2147483647 is a prime number; and this is the greatest at present known to be such, and, consequently, the last of the above [perfect numbers](wikipedia://en/23670) \[i.e., 2<sup>30</sup>(2<sup>31</sup> − 1)\], which depends upon this, is the greatest perfect number known at present, and probably the greatest that ever will be discovered; for as they are merely curious, without being useful, it is not likely that any person will attempt to find one beyond it.[^o8]

He repeated this prediction in his work from 1814, *A New Mathematical and Philosophical Dictionary*.[^o9][^o10] In 1867, the number 3,203,431,780,337 was proven to be prime.[^o6]

## In computing {#In_computing}

The number 2,147,483,647 (or [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263) 7FFFFFFF<sub>16</sub>) is the maximum positive value for a [32-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32-bit) signed [binary integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_%28computer_science%29) in [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing). It is therefore the maximum value for variables declared as integers (e.g., as `int`) in many programming languages.

The data type [time\_t](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_t), used on operating systems such as [Unix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix), is a signed integer counting the number of seconds since the start of the [Unix epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix_epoch) ([midnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight) [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC) of 1 January 1970), and is often implemented as a 32-bit integer.[^o13] The latest time that can be represented in this form is 03:14:07 UTC on Tuesday, 19 January 2038 (corresponding to 2,147,483,647 seconds since the start of the epoch). This means that systems using a 32-bit `time_t` type are susceptible to the [Year 2038 problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_2038_problem).[^o14]

On 1 January 2022, a bug was reported for [Microsoft Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Exchange_Server) systems where email delivery would fail. An internal malware scanner (enabled by default since 2013) used the date and time as a signed 32-bit integer. The integer would change during the new year to 2,201,010,001 (with the first two digits representing the year), surpassing the maximum value for this data type.[^o15]

### In video games {#In_video_games}

The number 2,147,483,647 often becomes a hard limit for various statistics in video games, such as points or money, if they are represented by signed 32-bit integers (rather than [floating-point](wikipedia://en/11376), [double-precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-precision_floating-point_format) or [arbitrary-precision](wikipedia://en/600892)).[^o17][^o18] Going over this limit by legitimate means, or by modding or hacking the game, results in many different outcomes caused by [integer overflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_overflow).[^o19] The most common outcome is the number "wrapping" into the negatives. Another potential outcome is *game crashing*, thus meaning there was no failsafe implemented in the event the value exceeds the signed 32-bit limit—generally if the underlying engine has undefined behavior, instead of a wraparound behavior, for integer overflow. A well-known example in video games is that of *[Old School RuneScape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_RuneScape) and [Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_V),* where the number is used as the maximum amount of coins (or any other item) that a player can hold at once with normal methods, known as a "max cash stack".[^o20][^o21] In similar cases, where an [unsigned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsigned_int) instead of signed 32-bit integer is used, the limit might be extended to [4,294,967,295](wikipedia://en/18805747).[^o17]
