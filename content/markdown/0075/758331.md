# Marshall H. Stone

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marshall H. Stone"}

*Image: Marshall Stone ICM 1950 letter.jpg.* Marshall Stone's 1950 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) letter of resignation

**Marshall Harvey Stone** (April 8, 1903 – January 9, 1989) was an American mathematician who contributed to [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and the study of [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Stone was the son of [Harlan Fiske Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlan_Fiske_Stone), who was the [Chief Justice of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_United_States) in 1941–1946. Marshall Stone's family expected him to become a lawyer like his father, but he became enamored of mathematics while he was an undergraduate at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he was a classmate of future judge [Henry Friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Friendly). He completed a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) there in 1926, with a thesis on [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) that was supervised by [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906).[^o4] Between 1925 and 1937, he taught at Harvard, [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). Stone was promoted to a full professor at Harvard in 1937.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Stone did classified research as part of the "Office of Naval Operations" and the "Office of the Chief of Staff" of the [United States Department of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War). In 1946, he became the chairman of the Mathematics Department at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), a position that he held until 1952. While chairman, Stone hired several notable mathematicians including [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598), [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574), [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477), and [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905). He remained on the faculty at this university until 1968, after which he taught at the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst) until 1980.

In 1989, Stone died in [Madras, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras,_India) (now referred to as Chennai), due to a stroke. Following his death, many mathematicians praised Stone for his contributions to various mathematical fields. For instance, University of Massachusetts Amherst mathematician Larry Mann claimed that "Professor Stone was one of the greatest American mathematicians of this century," while Mac Lane described how Stone made the University of Chicago mathematics department the "best department in mathematics in the country in that period."[^o5]

## Accomplishments {#Accomplishments}

Stone made several advances in the 1930s:

- In 1930, he proved the [Stone–von Neumann](wikipedia://en/744569) uniqueness theorem.
- In 1932, he published a 662 page long monograph titled *Linear transformations in [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) and their applications to analysis*, which was a presentation about [self-adjoint operators](wikipedia://en/187408). Much of its content is now deemed to be part of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).
- In 1932, he proved conjectures by [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) on [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713), arising from the application of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) to [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202).
- In 1934, he published two papers setting out what is now called [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) theory. This theory grew out of his attempts to understand more deeply his results on spectral theory.
- In 1936, he published a long paper that included [Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/604111), an important result in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869). The theorem has been the starting point for what is now called [Stone duality](wikipedia://en/379619).
- In 1937, he published the [Stone–Weierstrass theorem](wikipedia://en/28858) which generalized [Weierstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass)'s theorem on the uniform approximation of continuous functions by polynomials.

Stone was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1933 and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) (United States) in 1938.[^o7][^o8] He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1943.[^o9] He presided over the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), 1943–44, and the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868), 1952–54. In 1982, he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science).[^o10]
