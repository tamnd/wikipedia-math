# Orthogonal basis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal basis"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), an **orthogonal basis** for an [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856) $V$ is a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) for $V$ whose vectors are mutually [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal). If the vectors of an orthogonal basis are [normalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalize_%28linear_algebra%29), the resulting basis is an *[orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063)*.

:::

## As coordinates {#As_coordinates}

Any orthogonal basis can be used to define a system of [orthogonal coordinates](wikipedia://en/5006088) $V.$ Orthogonal (not necessarily orthonormal) bases are important due to their appearance from [curvilinear](wikipedia://en/755300) orthogonal coordinates in [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697), as well as in [Riemannian](wikipedia://en/144652) and [pseudo-Riemannian](wikipedia://en/287555) manifolds.

## In functional analysis {#In_functional_analysis}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), an orthogonal basis is any basis obtained from an orthonormal basis (or Hilbert basis) using multiplication by nonzero [scalars](wikipedia://en/3588331).

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

## Extensions {#Extensions}

### Symmetric bilinear form {#Symmetric_bilinear_form}

The concept of an orthogonal basis is applicable to a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $V$ (over any [field](wikipedia://en/10603)) equipped with a [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$, where *[orthogonality](wikipedia://en/102221)* of two vectors $v$ and $w$ means $\langle v, w \rangle = 0$. For an orthogonal basis $\left\{e_k\right\}$:

$$\langle e_j, e_k\rangle = \begin{cases} q(e_k) & j = k \\ 0 & j \neq k, \end{cases}$$

where $q$ is a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) associated with $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle:$ $q(v) = \langle v, v \rangle$ (in an inner product space, $q(v) = \Vert v \Vert^2$).

Hence for an orthogonal basis $\left\{e_k\right\}$,

$$\langle v, w \rangle = \sum_k q(e_k) v_k w_k,$$

where $v_k$ and $w_k$ are components of $v$ and $w$ in the basis.

### Quadratic form {#Quadratic_form}

The concept of orthogonality may be extended to a vector space over any field of characteristic not 2 equipped with a quadratic form $q(v)$. Starting from the observation that, when the characteristic of the underlying field is not 2, the associated symmetric bilinear form $\langle v, w \rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}(q(v+w) - q(v) - q(w))$ allows vectors $v$ and $w$ to be defined as being orthogonal with respect to $q$ when $q(v+w) - q(v) - q(w) = 0$.
