# Octahedral number

*Image: Octahedral number.jpg.* 146 [magnetic balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neodymium_magnet_toys), packed in the form of an octahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octahedral number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **octahedral number** is a [figurate number](wikipedia://en/325260) that represents the number of spheres in an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) formed from [close-packed spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-packed_spheres). The $n$th octahedral number $O_n$ can be obtained by the formula:[^o2]

:::

$$O_n={n(2n^2 + 1) \over 3}.$$

The first few octahedral numbers are:

[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29), [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29), [19](wikipedia://en/208292), [44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/44_%28number%29), [85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85_%28number%29), 146, 231, 344, 489, 670, 891 .

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties and applications"}

## Properties and applications {#Properties_and_applications}

The octahedral numbers have a [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993)

$$\frac{z(z+1)^2}{(z-1)^4} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} O_n z^n = z +6z^2 + 19z^3 + \cdots .$$

[Sir Frederick Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Pollock,_1st_Baronet) conjectured in 1850 that every positive integer is the sum of at most 7 octahedral numbers.[^o5] This statement, the [Pollock octahedral numbers conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollock_octahedral_numbers_conjecture), has been proven true for all but finitely many numbers.[^o6]

In [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), octahedral numbers may be used to describe the numbers of atoms in octahedral clusters; in this context they are called [magic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_number_%28chemistry%29).[^o7][^o8]

:::

## Relation to other figurate numbers {#Relation_to_other_figurate_numbers}

### Square pyramids {#Square_pyramids}

An octahedral packing of spheres may be partitioned into two [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455), one upside-down underneath the other, by splitting it along a square cross-section. Therefore, the $n$th octahedral number $O_n$ can be obtained by adding two consecutive [square pyramidal numbers](wikipedia://en/542343) together:[^o2]

$$O_n = P_{n-1} + P_n.$$

### Tetrahedra {#Tetrahedra}

If $O_n$ is the $n$th octahedral number and $T_n$ is the $n$th [tetrahedral number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_number) then

$$O_n+4T_{n-1}=T_{2n-1}.$$

This represents the geometric fact that gluing a tetrahedron onto each of four non-adjacent faces of an octahedron produces a tetrahedron of twice the size.

Another relation between octahedral numbers and tetrahedral numbers is also possible, based on the fact that an octahedron may be divided into four tetrahedra each having two adjacent original faces (or alternatively, based on the fact that each square pyramidal number is the sum of two tetrahedral numbers):

$$O_n = T_n + 2T_{n-1} + T_{n-2}.$$

### Cubes {#Cubes}

If two tetrahedra are attached to opposite faces of an octahedron, the result is a [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406).[^o13] The number of close-packed spheres in the rhombohedron is a [cube](wikipedia://en/451445), justifying the equation

$$O_n+2T_{n-1}=n^3.$$

### Centered squares {#Centered_squares}

*Image: Pyramides quadratae secundae.svg.* Square pyramids in which each layer has a [centered square number](wikipedia://en/723297) of cubes. The total number of cubes in each pyramid is an octahedral number.

The difference between two consecutive octahedral numbers is a [centered square number](wikipedia://en/723297):[^o2]

$$O_n - O_{n-1} = C_{4,n} = n^2 + (n-1)^2.$$

Therefore, an octahedral number also represents the number of points in a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) formed by stacking centered squares; for this reason, in his book *Arithmeticorum libri duo* (1575), [Francesco Maurolico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maurolico) called these numbers "pyramides quadratae secundae".[^o15]

The number of cubes in an octahedron formed by stacking centered squares is a [centered octahedral number](wikipedia://en/39529359), the sum of two consecutive octahedral numbers. These numbers are

1, 7, 25, 63, 129, 231, 377, 575, 833, 1159, 1561, 2047, 2625, ...

given by the formula

$O_n+O_{n-1}=\frac{(2n-1)(2n^2-2n+3)}{3}$ for *n* = 1, 2, 3, ...

## History {#History}

The first study of octahedral numbers appears to have been by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525), around 1630, in his *De solidorum elementis*. Prior to Descartes, figurate numbers had been studied by the ancient Greeks and by [Johann Faulhaber](wikipedia://en/9768822), but only for [polygonal numbers](wikipedia://en/213996), [pyramidal numbers](wikipedia://en/509081), and [cubes](wikipedia://en/451445). Descartes introduced the study of figurate numbers based on the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) and some of the [semiregular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/1548894); his work included the octahedral numbers. However, *De solidorum elementis* was lost, and not rediscovered until 1860. In the meantime, octahedral numbers had been studied again by other mathematicians, including [Friedrich Wilhelm Marpurg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_Marpurg) in 1774, [Georg Simon Klügel](wikipedia://en/19043679) in 1808, and [Sir Frederick Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Pollock,_1st_Baronet) in 1850.[^o17]
