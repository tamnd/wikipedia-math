# Edge-transitive graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edge-transitive graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **edge-transitive graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G$ such that, given any two edges $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}$ of $G$, there is an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/15094186) of $G$ that [maps](wikipedia://en/516931) $e_{1}$ to $e_{2}$.[^o2]

:::

In other words, a graph is edge-transitive if its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) acts [transitively](wikipedia://en/12781#Remarkable_properties_of_actions) on its edges.

## Examples and properties {#Examples_and_properties}

*Image: Gray graph 2COL.svg.* The [Gray graph](wikipedia://en/2554738) is edge-transitive and [regular](wikipedia://en/85821), but not [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175).

The number of connected simple edge-transitive graphs on n vertices is 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 5, 8, 9, 13, 7, 19, 10, 16, 25, 26, 12, 28 ...

Edge-transitive graphs include all [symmetric graphs](wikipedia://en/1577896), such as the vertices and edges of the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285).[^o2] Symmetric graphs are also [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175) (if they are [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492)), but in general edge-transitive graphs need not be vertex-transitive. Every [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) edge-transitive graph that is not vertex-transitive must be [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431),[^o2] (and hence can be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) with only two colors), and either semi-symmetric or [biregular](wikipedia://en/36895088).[^o4]

Examples of edge but not vertex transitive graphs include the [complete bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{m,n}$ where m ≠ n, which includes the star graphs $K_{1,n}$. For graphs on n vertices, there are (n-1)/2 such graphs for odd n and (n-2) for even n. Additional edge transitive graphs which are not symmetric can be formed as subgraphs of these complete bi-partite graphs in certain cases. Subgraphs of complete bipartite graphs K<sub>m,n</sub> exist when m and n share a factor greater than 2. When the greatest common factor is 2, subgraphs exist when 2n/m is even or if m=4 and n is an odd multiple of 6.[^o5] So edge transitive subgraphs exist for K<sub>3,6</sub>, K<sub>4,6</sub> and K<sub>5,10</sub> but not K<sub>4,10</sub>. An alternative construction for some edge transitive graphs is to add vertices to the midpoints of edges of a symmetric graph with v vertices and e edges, creating a bipartite graph with e vertices of order 2, and v of order 2e/v.

An edge-transitive graph that is also [regular](wikipedia://en/85821), but still not vertex-transitive, is called [semi-symmetric](wikipedia://en/2554240). The [Gray graph](wikipedia://en/2554738), a cubic graph on 54 vertices, is an example of a regular graph which is edge-transitive but not vertex-transitive. The [Folkman graph](wikipedia://en/23761072), a quartic graph on 20 vertices is the smallest such graph.

The [vertex connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) of an edge-transitive graph always equals its [minimum degree](wikipedia://en/1374448).[^o6]
