# George Darwin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="George Darwin"}

**Sir George Howard Darwin** (9 July 1845 – 7 December 1912)[^o3] was an English barrister and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), the second son and fifth child of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin) and [Emma Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Darwin). He is known for the [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) of the [theory of tides](wikipedia://en/10610469) as well as the discredited fission theory of the [origin of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_the_Moon). The Darwin symbols for tides are named after him.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

George H. Darwin was born 9 July 1845 at [Down House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_House), Kent, the fifth child of biologist [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin) and [Emma Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Darwin).

From the age of 11 he studied under [Charles Pritchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Pritchard) at [Clapham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clapham) Grammar School, and entered [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), in 1863, though he soon moved to [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where his tutor was [Edward John Routh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_John_Routh). He graduated BA in mathematics in 1868 (as [second wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_wrangler)), when he was also placed second for the [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) and was appointed to a college fellowship. He became M.A. in 1871.[^o5] He was admitted to the [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_%28law%29) in 1872, but returned to science.[^o5] George Darwin conducted studies into the prevalence and health outcomes of contemporary first-cousin marriages (such as his parents') in Great Britain. His father Charles had become concerned after the death of three of his children, including his favourite daughter, Annie, from [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis) in 1851, that his and Emma's union may have been a mistake from a biological perspective. He was reassured by George's results.[^o6]

## Tides and geophysics {#Tides_and_geophysics}

Although George Darwin was the son of the famous [geologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologist) turned [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist),[^o8] [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin), rather than moving predominantly into the field of [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), George kept his focus on [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology). Subsequently, his efforts within geology caused him to stumble onto many seemingly radical ideas, some of which were related to the notion that preserved within the physical structure of the planet was the [mechanical energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_energy) (or the collective [inertial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial) motion), which may have allowed an ancient rapidly spinning Earth to somehow expel a piece of its mass, and it was this expelled mass which later congealed to create the natural satellite that was now in orbit around the Earth. So, before the Apollo mission and the rise to prominence of the relativistic notion that the [origin of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_the_Moon) was due in part to collisions within a very active [protoplanetary disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protoplanetary_disk), there was a radically different depiction of [lunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) and [planetary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) evolution, which was proposed by George Darwin, in 1879, called the fission theory.[^o9][^o10]

In 1883 Darwin became [Plumian Professor of Astronomy and Experimental Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plumian_Professor_of_Astronomy_and_Experimental_Philosophy) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). He studied [tidal forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_forces) involving the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun), [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon), and [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228), and formulated the [fission theory of the Moon formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_the_Moon#Fission).[^o9] In 1885, he collaborated with [Andrew Wilson Baird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wilson_Baird) to produce a definitive "Report on the Harmonic Analysis of Tidal Observations" for the British Association, a work that utilised Baird's extensive field data from the [Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_of_India) to refine the mathematical reduction of tidal components.[^o11]

He was an invited speaker in the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) 1908, Rome on the topic of "*Mechanics, Physical Mathematics, Astronomy*."[^o12] As President of the [Cambridge Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Philosophical_Society), he also gave the Introductory Address to the Congress in 1912 on the character of pure and applied mathematics.[^o13]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 1879, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) and won their [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal) in 1884[^o15] and their [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal) in 1911.[^o16] He delivered their [Bakerian Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakerian_Lecture) in 1891 on the subject of "tidal prediction".

Darwin was a Fellow of the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society) (RAS) and won the [Gold Medal of the RAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Medal_of_the_Royal_Astronomical_Society) in 1892. He was elected to both the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1898.[^o17][^o18] From 1899–1901 he served as [President of the RAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Royal_Astronomical_Society). He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1904.[^o19] The RAS founded a prize lectureship in 1984 and named it the [George Darwin Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Darwin_Lectureship) in Darwin's honour.

He received the degree of *Doctor mathematicae ([honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa))* from the [Royal Frederick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo) on 6 September 1902, when they celebrated the centennial of the birth of [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573).[^o20][^o21]

[Darwin crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_%28Martian_crater%29) on Mars is named after him.[^o22]

## Family {#Family}

Darwin married [Martha (Maud) du Puy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maud_Darwin), the daughter of Charles du Puy of [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), in 1884; his wife was a member of the [Ladies Dining Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies_Dining_Society) in Cambridge, with 11 other members.

She died on 6 February 1947. They had three sons and two daughters:

- [Gwen Raverat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwen_Raverat) (1885–1957), artist.
- Sir [Charles Galton Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Galton_Darwin) (1887–1962), physicist and applied mathematician.
- Margaret Elizabeth Darwin (1890–1974), married Sir [Geoffrey Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Keynes).
- William Robert Darwin (1894–1970)
- Leonard Darwin (1899–1899)

George and Maud Darwin bought [Newnham Grange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_Grange), Cambridge in 1885. The Darwins extensively remodelled the house. Since 1962 the Grange has been part of [Darwin College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_College,_Cambridge).

He is buried in Trumpington Extension Cemetery in Cambridge with his son Leonard and his daughter Gwen (Raverat), whilst his wife, [Maud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maud_Darwin), was cremated at Cambridge Crematorium; his brothers Sir [Francis Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Darwin) and Sir [Horace Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Darwin) and their respective wives are interred in the [Parish of the Ascension Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground,_Cambridge).
