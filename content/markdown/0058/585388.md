# Homology sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Homology sphere"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a **homology sphere** is an *n*-[manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *X* having the [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) of an *n*-[sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), for some integer $n\ge 1$. That is,

:::

$$H_0(X,\mathbb{Z}) = H_n(X,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$$

and

$H_i(X,\mathbb{Z}) = \{0\}$ for all other *i*.

Therefore *X* is a [connected space](wikipedia://en/6233), with one non-zero higher [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262), namely, $b_n=1$. It does not follow that *X* is [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected), only that its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) is [perfect](wikipedia://en/585271) (see [Hurewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/591587)).

A [rational homology sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_homology_sphere) is defined similarly but using homology with rational coefficients.

## Poincaré homology sphere {#Poincaré_homology_sphere}

The Poincaré homology sphere (also known as Poincaré dodecahedral space) is a particular example of a homology sphere, first constructed by [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740). Being a [spherical 3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1205989), it is the only homology 3-sphere (besides the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) itself) with a finite [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004). Its fundamental group is known as the [binary icosahedral group](wikipedia://en/3195145) and has order 120. Since the fundamental group of the 3-sphere is trivial, this shows that there exist 3-manifolds with the same homology groups as the 3-sphere that are not homeomorphic to it.

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

A simple construction of this space begins with a [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407). Each face of the dodecahedron is identified with its opposite face, using the minimal clockwise twist to line up the faces. [Gluing](wikipedia://en/237213) each pair of opposite faces together using this identification yields a closed 3-manifold. (See [Seifert–Weber space](wikipedia://en/1233773) for a similar construction, using more "twist", that results in a [hyperbolic 3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1237700).)

Alternatively, the Poincaré homology sphere can be constructed as the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) [SO(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%283%29)/I where I is the [icosahedral group](wikipedia://en/2877844) (i.e., the rotational [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of the regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) and dodecahedron, isomorphic to the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) A<sub>5</sub>). More intuitively, this means that the Poincaré homology sphere is the space of the distinct configurations of a regular icosahedron, centered at the origin, in Euclidean 3-space. One can also pass instead to the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of SO(3) which can be realized as the group of unit [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the 3-sphere. In this case, the Poincaré homology sphere is isomorphic to $S^3/\widetilde{I}$ where $\widetilde{I}$ is the [binary icosahedral group](wikipedia://en/3195145), the perfect [double cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_covering_group) of I [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in $S^3$.

Another approach is by [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699). The Poincaré homology sphere results from +1 surgery on the right-handed [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773).

:::

### Cosmology {#Cosmology}

In 2003, lack of structure on the largest scales (above 60 degrees) in the [cosmic microwave background](wikipedia://en/7376) as observed for one year by the [WMAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkinson_Microwave_Anisotropy_Probe) spacecraft led to the suggestion, by [Jean-Pierre Luminet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Luminet) of the [Observatoire de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatoire_de_Paris) and colleagues, that the [shape of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe) is a Poincaré sphere.[^o6][^o7] In 2008, astronomers found the best orientation on the sky for the model and confirmed some of the predictions of the model, using three years of observations by the WMAP spacecraft.[^o8] Data analysis from the [Planck spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_%28spacecraft%29) suggests that there is no observable non-trivial topology to the universe.[^o9]

## Constructions and examples {#Constructions_and_examples}

- [Surgery](wikipedia://en/5961115) on a knot in the 3-sphere *S*<sup>3</sup> with framing +1 or −1 gives a homology sphere.
- More generally, surgery on a link gives a homology sphere whenever the matrix given by intersection numbers (off the diagonal) and framings (on the diagonal) has determinant +1 or −1.
- If *p*, *q*, and *r* are pairwise relatively prime positive integers then the link of the singularity *x*<sup>*p*</sup> + *y*<sup>*q*</sup> + *z*<sup>*r*</sup> = 0 (in other words, the intersection of a small 3-sphere around 0 with this complex surface) is a [Brieskorn manifold](wikipedia://en/34748487) that is a homology 3-sphere, called a [Brieskorn](wikipedia://en/34748690) 3-sphere Σ(*p*, *q*, *r*). It is homeomorphic to the standard 3-sphere if one of *p*, *q*, and *r* is 1, and Σ(2, 3, 5) is the Poincaré sphere.
- The [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of two oriented homology 3-spheres is a homology 3-sphere. A homology 3-sphere that cannot be written as a connected sum of two homology 3-spheres is called **irreducible** or **prime**, and every homology 3-sphere can be written as a connected sum of prime homology 3-spheres in an essentially unique way. (See [Prime decomposition (3-manifold)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29).)
- Suppose that $a_1, \ldots, a_r$ are integers all at least 2 such that any two are coprime. Then the [Seifert fiber space](wikipedia://en/1631931)

$$\{b, (o_1,0);(a_1,b_1),\dots,(a_r,b_r)\}\,$$

over the sphere with exceptional fibers of degrees *a*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>*r*</sub> is a homology sphere, where the *b**s are chosen so that***

$$b+b_1/a_1+\cdots+b_r/a_r=1/(a_1\cdots a_r).$$

(There is always a way to choose the *b*′s, and the homology sphere does not depend (up to isomorphism) on the choice of *b*′s.) If *r* is at most 2 this is just the usual 3-sphere; otherwise they are distinct non-trivial homology spheres. If the *a*′s are 2, 3, and 5 this gives the Poincaré sphere. If there are at least 3 *a*′s, not 2, 3, 5, then this is an acyclic homology 3-sphere with infinite fundamental group that has a [Thurston geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurston_geometry) modeled on the universal cover of [SL<sub>2</sub>(**R**)](wikipedia://en/11105238).

## Invariants {#Invariants}

- The [Rokhlin invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokhlin_invariant) is a $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-valued invariant of homology 3-spheres.
- The [Casson invariant](wikipedia://en/7066452) is an integer valued invariant of homology 3-spheres, whose reduction mod 2 is the Rokhlin invariant.

## Applications {#Applications}

If *A* is a homology 3-sphere not homeomorphic to the standard 3-sphere, then the [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702) of *A* is an example of a 4-dimensional [homology manifold](wikipedia://en/7338342) that is not a [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193). The double suspension of *A* is homeomorphic to the standard 5-sphere, but its [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) (induced by some triangulation of *A*) is not a [PL manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL_manifold). In other words, this gives an example of a finite [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) that is a topological manifold but not a PL manifold. (It is not a PL manifold because the [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_link) of a point is not always a 4-sphere.)

Galewski and Stern showed that all compact topological manifolds (without boundary) of dimension at least 5 are homeomorphic to simplicial complexes [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) there is a homology 3 sphere Σ with [Rokhlin invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokhlin_invariant) 1 such that the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) Σ#Σ of Σ with itself bounds a smooth acyclic 4-manifold. [Ciprian Manolescu](wikipedia://en/3034280) showed[^o14] that there is no such homology sphere with the given property, and therefore, there are 5-manifolds not homeomorphic to simplicial complexes. In particular, the example originally given by Galewski and Stern[^o15] is not triangulable.
