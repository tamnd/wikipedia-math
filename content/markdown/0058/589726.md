# Strong topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strong topology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **strong topology** is a [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) which is stronger than some other "default" topology. This term is used to describe different topologies depending on context, and it may refer to:

:::

- the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736) on the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/1564380)
- the topology arising from a [norm](wikipedia://en/21538)
- the [strong operator topology](wikipedia://en/458699)
- the [strong topology (polar topology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_topology_%28polar_topology%29), which subsumes all topologies above.

A topology τ is stronger than a topology σ (is a [finer topology](wikipedia://en/393069)) if τ contains all the open sets of σ.[^o2]

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), it usually means the topology of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) as [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) or subspace of [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977), as opposed to the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) (which is rarely even a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637)).
