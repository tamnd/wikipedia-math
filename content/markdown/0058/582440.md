# Luhn algorithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luhn algorithm"}

Luhn The **Luhn algorithm** or **Luhn formula** (creator: [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) scientist [Hans Peter Luhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Peter_Luhn)), also known as the "[modulus](wikipedia://en/20087) 10" or "mod 10" [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775), is a simple [check digit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check_digit) formula used to validate a variety of identification numbers. The purpose is to design a numbering scheme in such a way that when a human is entering a number, a computer can quickly check it for errors.

:::

The algorithm is in the [public domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_domain) and is in wide use today. It is specified in [ISO/IEC 7812-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/IEC_7812-1).[^o2] It is not intended to be a [cryptographically secure hash function](wikipedia://en/439526); it was designed to protect against accidental errors, not malicious attacks. Most [credit card numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card_number) and many [government identification numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_identification_numbers) use the algorithm as a simple method of distinguishing valid numbers from mistyped or otherwise incorrect numbers.

## Description {#Description}

The check digit is computed as follows:

1. Drop the check digit from the number (if it's already present). This leaves the payload.
1. Start with the payload digits and double **every second digit** (a digit in an odd position in reversed order) when numbered from the left.
1. Process the payload from right-to-left. **If a doubled digit exceeds 9**, subtract 9 from the digit.
1. Sum all the resulting digits (including the ones that were not doubled).
1. The check digit is calculated by $(10 - (s \bmod{10})) \bmod{10}$, where s is the sum from step 4. This is the smallest number (possibly zero) that must be added to $s$ to make a multiple of 10.
1. Other valid formulas giving the same value are $9 - ((s + 9)\bmod{10})$, $(10 - s)\bmod{10}$, and $10\lceil s/10\rceil - s$. Note that the formula $(10 - s)\bmod{10}$ will not work in all environments due to differences in how negative numbers are handled by the [modulo](wikipedia://en/1352428) operation.

### Example for computing check digit {#Example_for_computing_check_digit}

Assume an example of an account number 1789372997 (just the "payload", check digit not yet included):

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| Digits reversed | 7 | 9 | 9 | 2 | 7 | 3 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multipliers | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
|  | \= | \= | \= | \= | \= | \= | \= | \= | \= | \= |
|  | **14** | 9 | **18** | 2 | **14** | 3 | **18** | 8 | **14** | 1 |
| Sum digits | **5** (1+4) | 9 | **9** (1+8) | 2 | **5** (1+4) | 3 | **9** (1+8) | 8 | **5** (1+4) | 1 |

:::

The sum of the resulting digits is 56.

The check digit is equal to $(10 - (56 \bmod{10}))\bmod{10} = 4$.

This makes the full account number read 17893729974.

### Example for validating check digit {#Example_for_validating_check_digit}

1. Drop the check digit (last digit) of the number to validate. (e.g. 17893729974 → 1789372997)
1. Calculate the check digit (see above)
1. Compare your result with the original check digit. If both numbers match, the result is valid. (e.g. (givenCheckDigit = calculatedCheckDigit) ⇔ (isValidCheckDigit)).

## Strengths and weaknesses {#Strengths_and_weaknesses}

The Luhn algorithm will detect all single-digit errors, as well as almost all transpositions of adjacent digits. It will not, however, detect transposition of the two-digit sequence *09* to *90* (or vice versa). It will detect most of the possible twin errors (it will not detect *22* ↔ *55*, *33* ↔ *66* or *44* ↔ *77*).

Other, more complex check-digit algorithms (such as the [Verhoeff algorithm](wikipedia://en/4766949) and the [Damm algorithm](wikipedia://en/37957721)) can detect more transcription errors. The [Luhn mod N algorithm](wikipedia://en/6870554) is an extension that supports non-numerical strings.

Because the algorithm operates on the digits in a right-to-left manner and zero digits affect the result only if they cause shift in position, zero-padding the beginning of a string of numbers does not affect the calculation. Therefore, systems that pad to a specific number of digits (by converting 1234 to 0001234 for instance) can perform Luhn validation before or after the padding and achieve the same result.

The algorithm appeared in a United States Patent[^n1] for a simple, hand-held, mechanical device for computing the checksum. The device took the mod 10 sum by mechanical means. The *substitution digits*, that is, the results of the double and reduce procedure, were not produced mechanically. Rather, the digits were marked in their permuted order on the body of the machine.

## Pseudocode implementation {#Pseudocode_implementation}

The following function takes a card number, including the check digit, as an array of integers and outputs **true** if the check digit is correct, **false** otherwise.

**function** isValid(cardNumber\[1..length\]) sum := 0 **for** i from 1 to length **do** digit := cardNumber\[i\] offsetFromEnd := length - i **if** (offsetFromEnd mod 2) == 1 **then** sum := sum + digit **elseif** digit \< 5 **then** sum := sum + 2 \* digit **else** sum := sum + 2 \* digit - 9 **end if** **end for** **return** (sum mod 10) == 0 **end function**

## Uses {#Uses}

The Luhn algorithm is used in a variety of systems, including:

- [Credit card numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_card_number)
- [IMEI numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mobile_Equipment_Identity)
- [CUSIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUSIP) numbers for North American financial instruments
- [National Provider Identifier numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Provider_Identifier) in the United States
- [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) [social insurance numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_insurance_number)
- [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_identity_card) ID numbers
- [South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa) ID numbers
- [South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa) Tax reference numbers
- [Swedish Personal identity numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_identity_number_%28Sweden%29)
- [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) Corporate Identity Numbers (OrgNr)
- [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) Social Security Numbers (ΑΜΚΑ)
- [ICCID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM_card#ICCID) of SIM cards
- [European patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Convention) application numbers
- Survey codes appearing on McDonald's, Taco Bell, and Tractor Supply Co. receipts
- [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) package tracking numbers use a modified Luhn algorithm[^o11]
- Italian VAT numbers ([Partita Iva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAT_identification_number#European_Union_VAT_identification_numbers))[^o12]

:::

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

In 1995, during a television spot \\ segment about buying movie tickets online[^o14], actress Sandra Bullock jokingly remarked that a verification system was needed to catch typos when entering credit card numbers. The video resurfaced online in 2026[^o15], amusing users who jokingly credited the actress with inventing "two-factor authentication" though the concept she suggested actually aligns more accurately with the Luhn algorithm, which had been devised back in 1954[^o16].
