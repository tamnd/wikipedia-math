# Frank Morley

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Morley"}

**Frank Morley** (September 9, 1860 – October 17, 1937) was a leading mathematician, known mostly for his teaching and research in the fields of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). Among his mathematical accomplishments was the discovery and proof of the celebrated [Morley's trisector theorem](wikipedia://en/1096323) in elementary [plane geometry](wikipedia://en/9417).

:::

He led 50 Ph.D. students, including [Clara Latimer Bacon](wikipedia://en/58789158), to their degrees, and was said to be

... one of the more striking figures of the relatively small group of men who initiated that development which, within his own lifetime, brought Mathematics in America from a minor position to its present place in the sun.[^o3]

## Life {#Life}

Morley was born in the town of [Woodbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodbridge,_Suffolk) in Suffolk, England. His parents were Elizabeth Muskett and Joseph Roberts Morley, [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Society_of_Friends) who ran a [china](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain) shop. After being educated at [Woodbridge School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodbridge_School), Morley went on to [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge) (B.A., 1884).

In 1887, Morley moved to [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania). He taught at [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College) until 1900, when he became chairman of the mathematics department at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). His publications include *Elementary Treatise on the Theory of Functions* (1893), with [James Harkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Harkness_%28mathematician%29); and *Introduction to the Theory of Analytic Functions* (1898). In 1897, he was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o6] He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1917.[^o7] He was President of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1919 to 1920[^o8] and was the editor of the *[American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics)* from 1900 to 1921. He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in 1912 at Cambridge (England), in 1924 at Toronto, and in 1936 at Oslo.

In 1933 he and his son [Frank Vigor Morley](wikipedia://en/65617697) published the "stimulating volume" *Inversive Geometry*.[^o9][^o10] The book develops [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) as a tool for geometry and [function theory](wikipedia://en/5759). Some non-standard terminology is used such as "[base-circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base-circle_%28mathematics%29)" for [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) and "[turn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28geometry%29)" for a point on it.

He was a strong [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) player and once beat world chess champion [Emanuel Lasker](wikipedia://en/244649) in a game.

He died in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), Maryland, at age 77.

He had three sons: novelist [Christopher Morley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Morley); [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize)-winning columnist, journalist, and college president [Felix Morley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Morley); and [Frank Vigor Morley](wikipedia://en/65617697), also a mathematician.
