# Lev Pontryagin

Pontryagin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lev Pontryagin"}

**Lev Semyonovich Pontryagin** (Лев Семёнович Понтрягин, also written **Pontriagin** or **Pontrjagin**, first name sometimes anglicized as **Leon**) (3 September 1908 – 3 May 1988) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Completely blind from the age of 14, he made major discoveries in a number of fields of mathematics, including [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) and [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565).

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Pontryagin was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) and lost his eyesight completely due to an unsuccessful [eye surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_surgery) after a [primus stove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primus_stove) explosion when he was 14. His mother Tatyana Andreyevna, who did not know mathematical symbols, read mathematical books and papers (notably those of [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082), [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179), and [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794)) to him, and later worked as his secretary. His mother used alternative names for math symbols, such as "tails up" for the set-union symbol $\cup$.[^o4]

In 1925 he entered [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), where he was strongly influenced by the lectures of [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245) who would become his doctoral thesis advisor. After graduating in 1929, he obtained a position at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University). In 1934 he joined the [Steklov Institute](wikipedia://en/984494) in Moscow. In 1970 he became vice president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868).

## Work {#Work}

Pontryagin worked on [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) theory for [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) while still a student. He went on to lay foundations for the abstract theory of the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247), now called [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136). Using these tools, he was able to solve the case of [Hilbert's fifth problem](wikipedia://en/152760) for abelian groups in 1934.

In 1935, he was able to compute the homology groups of the classical compact [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), which he would later call his greatest achievement.[^o6]

With [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633), he is regarded as one of the co-founders of [cobordism theory](wikipedia://en/391832), and co-discoverers of the central idea of this theory, that framed cobordism and [stable homotopy](wikipedia://en/2946655) are equivalent.[^o7] This led to the introduction around 1940 of a theory of certain [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530), now called [Pontryagin classes](wikipedia://en/385944), designed to vanish on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) that is a [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824).

In 1942 he introduced the cohomology operations now called [Pontryagin squares](wikipedia://en/31438027). Moreover, in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) there are specific instances of [Krein spaces](wikipedia://en/5571012) called [Pontryagin spaces](wikipedia://en/5571012).

Starting in 1952, he worked in [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565) theory. His [maximum principle](wikipedia://en/831689) is fundamental to the modern theory of optimization. He also introduced the idea of a [bang–bang principle](wikipedia://en/5971657), to describe situations where the applied control at each moment is either the maximum positive 'steer', or the maximum negative 'steer'.

Pontryagin authored several influential monographs as well as popular textbooks in mathematics.

Pontryagin's students include [Dmitri Anosov](wikipedia://en/2320194), [Vladimir Boltyansky](wikipedia://en/20464140), [Revaz Gamkrelidze](wikipedia://en/32712098), Yevgeny Mishchenko, [Mikhail Postnikov](wikipedia://en/19633564), [Vladimir Rokhlin](wikipedia://en/7703611), and [Mikhail Zelikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Zelikin).

## Controversy and antisemitism allegations {#Controversy_and_antisemitism_allegations}

Pontryagin participated in a few notorious political campaigns in the Soviet Union. In 1930, he and several other young members of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9098251) publicly denounced as counter-revolutionary the Society's head [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov), who openly supported the Russian Orthodox Church and had recently been arrested. They then proceeded to follow their plan of reorganizing the Society.[^o6]

Pontryagin was accused of anti-Semitism on several occasions.[^o6] For example, he attacked [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) for being a "mediocre scientist" representing the "[Zionism movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism)", while both men were vice-presidents of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868).[^o9][^o10] When a prominent Soviet Jewish mathematician, [Grigory Margulis](wikipedia://en/1104948), was selected by the [IMU](wikipedia://en/14868) to receive the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) at the upcoming 1978 [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842), Pontryagin, who was a member of the executive committee of the IMU at the time, vigorously objected.[^o11] Although the IMU stood by its decision to award Margulis the Fields Medal, Margulis was denied a Soviet exit visa by the Soviet authorities and was unable to attend the 1978 ICM in person.[^o11]

Pontryagin rejected charges of antisemitism in an article published in *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29)* in 1979.[^o12] In his memoirs Pontryagin claims that he struggled with [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism), which he considered a form of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism).[^o10]
