# Invertible sheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Invertible sheaf"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **invertible sheaf** is a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) on a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014) that has an inverse with respect to [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of [sheaves of modules](wikipedia://en/44887605). It is the equivalent in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) of the topological notion of a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366). Due to their interactions with [Cartier divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor), they play a central role in the study of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let (*X*, *O*<sub>*X*</sub>) be a ringed space. Isomorphism classes of sheaves of *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-modules form a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) under the operation of tensor product of *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-modules. The [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) for this operation is *O*<sub>*X*</sub> itself. Invertible sheaves are the invertible elements of this monoid. Specifically, if *L* is a sheaf of *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-modules, then *L* is called **invertible** if it satisfies any of the following equivalent conditions:[^o4][^o5]

- There exists a sheaf *M* such that $L \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} M \cong \mathcal{O}_X$.
- The natural homomorphism $L \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} L^\vee \to \mathcal{O}_X$ is an isomorphism, where $L^\vee$ denotes the dual sheaf $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(L, \mathcal{O}_X)$.
- The functor from *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-modules to *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-modules defined by $F \mapsto F \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} L$ is an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051).

Every [locally free sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaf) of rank one is invertible. If *X* is a locally ringed space, then *L* is invertible if and only if it is locally free of rank one. Because of this fact, invertible sheaves are closely related to [line bundles](wikipedia://en/361366), to the point where the two are sometimes conflated.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Let *X* be an affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec} R$. Then an invertible sheaf on *X* is the sheaf associated to a rank one [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) over *R*. For example, this includes [fractional ideals](wikipedia://en/460700) of [algebraic number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_fields), since these are rank one projective modules over the rings of integers of the number field.

:::

## The Picard group {#The_Picard_group}

Quite generally, the isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves on *X* themselves form an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) under tensor product. This group generalises the [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907). In general it is written

$$\mathrm{Pic}(X)\$$

with *Pic* the [Picard functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_functor). Since it also includes the theory of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260), the study of this functor is a major issue in algebraic geometry.

The direct construction of invertible sheaves by means of data on *X* leads to the concept of [Cartier divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor).
