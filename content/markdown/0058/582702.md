# Quasistatic process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasistatic process"}

In [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952), a **quasi-static process**, also known as a **quasi-equilibrium process** (from Latin *quasi*, meaning ‘as if’[^o2]), is a [thermodynamic process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_process) that happens slowly enough for the system to remain in internal physical (but not necessarily chemical) [thermodynamic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/265823). An example of this is quasi-static expansion of a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen gas, where the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of the system changes so slowly that the [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) remains uniform throughout the system at each instant of time during the process.[^o3] Such an idealized process is a succession of physical equilibrium states, characterized by infinite slowness.[^o4]

:::

Only in a quasi-static thermodynamic process can we exactly define [intensive quantities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_quantities) (such as pressure, [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050), [specific volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_volume), [specific entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_entropy)) of the system at any instant during the whole process; otherwise, since no internal equilibrium is established, different parts of the system would have different values of these quantities, so a single value per quantity may not be sufficient to represent the whole system. In other words, when an [equation for a change in a state function](wikipedia://en/5529757) contains *P* or *T*, it implies a quasi-static process.

## Relation to reversible process {#Relation_to_reversible_process}

While all [reversible processes](wikipedia://en/416681) are quasi-static, most authors do not require a general quasi-static process to maintain equilibrium between system and surroundings and avoid dissipation,[^o6] which are defining characteristics of a reversible process. For example, quasi-static compression of a system by a piston subject to [friction](wikipedia://en/11062) is irreversible; although the system is always in internal thermal equilibrium, the friction ensures the generation of dissipative entropy, which goes against the definition of reversibility. Any engineer would remember to include friction when calculating the dissipative entropy generation.

An example of a quasi-static process that is not idealizable as reversible is slow [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726) between two bodies on two finitely different temperatures, where the heat transfer rate is controlled by a poorly conductive partition between the two bodies. In this case, no matter how slowly the process takes place, the state of the composite system consisting of the two bodies is far from equilibrium, since thermal equilibrium for this composite system requires that the two bodies be at the same temperature. Nevertheless, the entropy change for each body can be calculated using the Clausius equality for reversible heat transfer.

## PV-work in various quasi-static processes {#PV-work_in_various_quasi-static_processes}

1. Constant pressure: [Isobaric processes](wikipedia://en/414765), $$W_{1-2} = \int P \, dV = P(V_2 - V_1)$$
1. Constant volume: [Isochoric processes](wikipedia://en/414736), $$W_{1-2} = \int P dV = 0$$
1. Constant temperature: [Isothermal processes](wikipedia://en/409951), $$W_{1-2} = \int P \, dV,$$ where $P$ (pressure) varies with $V$ (volume) via $PV = P_1 V_1 = C$, so $$W_{1-2} = P_1 V_1 \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}$$
1. [Polytropic processes](wikipedia://en/4151504), $$W_{1-2} = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{n-1}$$
