# Plus construction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plus construction"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **plus construction** is a method for simplifying the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a space without changing its [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) and [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) [groups](wikipedia://en/19447).

:::

Explicitly, if $X$ is a based connected [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) and $P$ is a [perfect](wikipedia://en/585271) [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of $\pi_1(X)$ then a map $f\colon X \to Y$ is called a +-construction relative to $P$ if $f$ induces an isomorphism on homology, and $P$ is the kernel of $\pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(Y)$.[^o2]

The plus construction was introduced by (txt), and was used by [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) to define [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500). Given a [perfect](wikipedia://en/585271) [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of the fundamental group of a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) $X$, attach two-cells along loops in $X$ whose images in the fundamental group generate the subgroup. This operation generally changes the homology of the space, but these changes can be reversed by the addition of three-cells.

The most common application of the plus construction is in algebraic K-theory. If $R$ is a [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), we denote by $\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ the group of [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) $n$-by-$n$ [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) with elements in $R$. $\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ embeds in $\operatorname{GL}_{n+1}(R)$ by attaching a $1$ along the diagonal and $0$s elsewhere. The [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of these groups via these maps is denoted $\operatorname{GL}(R)$ and its [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) is denoted $B\operatorname{GL}(R)$. The plus construction may then be applied to the perfect normal subgroup $E(R)$ of $\operatorname{GL}(R) = \pi_1(B\operatorname{GL}(R))$, generated by matrices which only differ from the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) in one off-diagonal entry. For $n>0$, the $n$-th [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) of the resulting space, $B\operatorname{GL}(R)^+$, is isomorphic to the $n$-th $K$-group of $R$, that is,

$$\pi_n\left( B\operatorname{GL}(R)^+\right) \cong K_n(R).$$
