# Isogonal figure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isogonal figure"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) (e.g. a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) or [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470)) or a [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) is **isogonal** or **vertex-transitive** if all its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) are equivalent under the [symmetries](wikipedia://en/53741) of the figure. This implies that each vertex is surrounded by the same kinds of [face](wikipedia://en/149926) in the same or reverse order, and with the same [angles](wikipedia://en/338046) between corresponding faces.

:::

Technically, one says that for any two vertices there exists a symmetry of the polytope [mapping](wikipedia://en/516931) the first [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) onto the second. Other ways of saying this are that the [group of automorphisms](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the polytope *[acts transitively](wikipedia://en/12781#Remarkable_properties_of_actions)* on its vertices, or that the vertices lie within a single *[symmetry orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_orbit)*.

All vertices of a finite $n$-dimensional isogonal figure exist on an [$(n-1)$-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782).[^o2]

The term **isogonal** has long been used for polyhedra. **Vertex-transitive** is a synonym borrowed from modern ideas such as [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).

The [pseudorhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/1128551) – which is *not* isogonal – demonstrates that simply asserting that "all vertices look the same" is not as restrictive as the definition used here, which involves the group of isometries preserving the polyhedron or tiling.

All [regular polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polygons), [apeirogons](wikipedia://en/5658261) and [regular star polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_star_polygon) are *isogonal*. The [dual](wikipedia://en/19865549) of an isogonal polygon is an [isotoxal polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal_polygon).

Some even-sided polygons and [apeirogons](wikipedia://en/5658261) which alternate two edge lengths, for example a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048), are *isogonal*.

All planar isogonal 2*n*-gons have [dihedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry) (D<sub>*n*</sub>, *n* = 2, 3, ...) with reflection lines across the mid-edge points.

An **isogonal polyhedron** and 2D tiling has a single kind of vertex. An **isogonal polyhedron** with all regular faces is also a **[uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223)** and can be represented by a [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) notation sequencing the faces around each vertex. Geometrically distorted variations of uniform polyhedra and tilings can also be given the vertex configuration.

Isogonal polyhedra and 2D tilings may be further classified:

- *[Regular](wikipedia://en/333299)* if it is also [isohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral) (face-transitive) and [isotoxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal) (edge-transitive); this implies that every face is the same kind of [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306).
- *[Quasi-regular](wikipedia://en/8810651)* if it is also [isotoxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal) (edge-transitive) but not [isohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral) (face-transitive).
- *[Semi-regular](wikipedia://en/1548894)* if every face is a regular polygon but it is not [isohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral) (face-transitive) or [isotoxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal) (edge-transitive). (Definition varies among authors; e.g. some exclude solids with dihedral symmetry, or nonconvex solids.)
- *[Uniform](wikipedia://en/2537223)* if every face is a regular polygon, i.e. it is regular, quasiregular or semi-regular.
- *Semi-uniform* if its elements are also isogonal but allow for multiple edges lengths
- *Scaliform* if all the edges are the same length.
- *[Noble](wikipedia://en/9352244)* if it is also [isohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral) (face-transitive).

## *N* dimensions: Isogonal polytopes and tessellations {#N_dimensions:_Isogonal_polytopes_and_tessellations}

These definitions can be extended to higher-dimensional [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471) and [tessellations](wikipedia://en/6008654). All [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) are *isogonal*, for example, the [uniform 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/1645899) and [convex uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961).

The [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope) of an isogonal polytope is an [isohedral figure](wikipedia://en/6286120), which is transitive on its [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647).

:::

## *k*-isogonal and *k*-uniform figures {#k-isogonal_and_k-uniform_figures}

A polytope or tiling may be called ***k*-isogonal** if its vertices form *k* transitivity classes. A more restrictive term, ***k*-uniform** is defined as a *k-isogonal figure* constructed only from [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306). They can be represented visually with colors by different [uniform colorings](wikipedia://en/18624814).
