# Charles George Broyden

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles George Broyden"}

**Charles George Broyden** (3 February 1933 – 20 May 2011) was a mathematician who specialized in [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization) problems and [numerical linear algebra](wikipedia://en/7330660).[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] While a physicist working at [English Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Electric_Company) from 1961 to 1965, he adapted the [Davidon–Fletcher–Powell formula](wikipedia://en/11231265) to solving some nonlinear systems of equations that he was working with, leading to his widely cited 1965 paper, "A class of methods for solving nonlinear simultaneous equations".[^o3] He was a lecturer at [UCW Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University) from 1965 to 1967.[^o4] He later became a senior lecturer at [University of Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Essex) from 1967 to 1970,[^o4] where he independently discovered the [Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno (BFGS) method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFGS_method).[^o2] The BFGS method has then become a key technique in solving nonlinear optimization problems. Moreover, he was among those who derived the symmetric rank-one updating formula, and his name was also attributed to Broyden's methods and Broyden family of quasi-Newton methods.[^o6] After leaving the University of Essex, he continued his research career in the Netherlands and Italy, being awarded the chair at [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna). In later years, he began focusing on numerical linear algebra, in particular conjugate gradient methods and their taxonomy.[^o2]

:::

Broyden died from complications of a severe stroke at the age of 78. He was survived by his wife, Joan, and their three children Chris, Jane and Nick.[^o2]

A Charles Broyden Prize was established in 2009 to "honor this remarkable researcher" by Optimization Methods and Software in the international optimization community.[^o6][^o7]
