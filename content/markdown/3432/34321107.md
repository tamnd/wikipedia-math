# Unevenly spaced time series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unevenly spaced time series"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390), an **unevenly** (or **unequally** or **irregularly**) **spaced time series** is a sequence of observation time and value pairs (t<sub>n</sub>, X<sub>n</sub>) in which the spacing of observation times is not constant.

:::

Unevenly spaced [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) naturally occur in many industrial and scientific domains: [natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disaster) such as earthquakes, floods, or volcanic eruptions typically occur at irregular time intervals. In [observational astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_astronomy), measurements such as spectra of celestial objects are taken at times determined by weather conditions, availability of observation time slots, and suitable planetary configurations. In [clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trials) (or more generally, [longitudinal studies](wikipedia://en/1557623)), a patient's state of health may be observed only at irregular time intervals, and different patients are usually observed at different points in time. Wireless sensors in the [Internet of things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_things) often transmit information only when a state changes to conserve battery life. There are many more examples in [climatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatology), [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology), [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology), and [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324).

## Analysis {#Analysis}

A common approach to analyzing unevenly spaced time series is to transform the data into equally spaced observations using some form of [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) - most often linear - and then to apply existing methods for equally spaced data. However, transforming data in such a way can introduce a number of significant and hard to quantify [biases](wikipedia://en/184099),[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o13] especially if the spacing of observations is highly irregular.

Ideally, unevenly spaced time series are analyzed in their unaltered form. However, most of the basic theory for [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis) was developed at a time when limitations in computing resources favored an analysis of equally spaced data, since in this case efficient [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) routines can be used and many problems have an [explicit solution](wikipedia://en/585143). As a result, fewer methods currently exist specifically for analyzing unevenly spaced time series data.[^o13][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o14][^o15][^o16]

The [least-squares spectral analysis](wikipedia://en/13609399) methods are commonly used for computing a [frequency spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_spectrum) from such time series without any data alterations.

## Software {#Software}

- [Traces](https://traces.readthedocs.io/) is a [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) library for analysis of unevenly spaced time series in their unaltered form.
- [CRAN Task View: Time Series Analysis](https://cran.r-project.org/view=TimeSeries) is a list describing many [R (programming language)](wikipedia://en/376707) packages dealing with both unevenly (or irregularly) and evenly spaced time series and many related aspects, including uncertainty.
- [MessyTimeSeries](https://github.com/fipelle/MessyTimeSeries.jl) and [MessyTimeSeriesOptim](https://github.com/fipelle/MessyTimeSeriesOptim.jl) are Julia packages dedicated to incomplete time series.
