# Belt problem

*Image: Belt Problem v2.svg.* The belt problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Belt problem"}

The **belt problem** is a [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) problem which requires finding the length of a crossed [belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_%28mechanical%29) that connects two circular [pulleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley) with [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) *r*<sub>1</sub> and *r*<sub>2</sub> whose centers are separated by a distance *P*. The solution of the belt problem requires [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) and the concepts of the [bitangent line](wikipedia://en/2605212), the [vertical angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_angle), and [congruent angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congruent_angles).

:::

## Solution {#Solution}

Clearly [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) ACO and ADO are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [right angled triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_angled_triangle), as are [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) BEO and BFO. In addition, [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) ACO and BEO are [similar](wikipedia://en/39377). Therefore [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) CAO, DAO, EBO and FBO are all equal. Denoting this [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) by $\varphi$ (denominated in [radians](wikipedia://en/26003)), the length of the belt is

$$CO + DO + EO + FO + \text {arc} CD + \text {arc} EF \,\!$$

$$=2r_1\tan(\varphi) + 2r_2\tan(\varphi) + (2\pi-2\varphi)r_1 + (2\pi-2\varphi)r_2 \,\!$$

$$=2(r_1+r_2)(\tan(\varphi) + \pi- \varphi) \,\!$$

This exploits the convenience of denominating angles in radians that the length of an [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) = the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) × the measure of the [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) facing the [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29).

To find $\varphi$ we see from the [similarity](wikipedia://en/39377) of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) ACO and BEO that

$$\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{AC}{BE} \,\!$$

$$\Rightarrow \frac{P-x}{x} = \frac{r_1}{r_2} \,\!$$

$$\Rightarrow \frac{P}{x} = \frac{r_1+r_2}{r_2} \,\!$$

$$\Rightarrow {x} = \frac{P r_2}{r_1+r_2} \,\!$$

$$\cos(\varphi) = \frac{r_2}{x} = \frac{r_2}{\left(\dfrac{P r_2}{r_1+r_2}\right)} = \frac{r_1+r_2}{P} \,\!$$

$$\Rightarrow \varphi=\arccos\left(\frac{r_1+r_2}{P}\right) \,\!$$

For fixed *P* the length of the belt depends only on the sum of the radius values *r*<sub>1</sub> + *r*<sub>2</sub>, and not on their individual values.

## Pulley problem {#Pulley_problem}

*Image: Pulley Problem.svg.* The pulley problem

There are other types of problems similar to the belt problem. The [pulley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley) problem, as shown, is similar to the belt problem; however, the [belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_%28mechanical%29) does not cross itself. In the pulley problem the length of the belt is

$$2 P \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)+r_1(2\pi-\theta)+r_2{\theta}\, ,$$

where *r*<sub>1</sub> represents the radius of the larger pulley, *r*<sub>2</sub> represents the radius of the smaller one, and:

$$\theta=2\arccos\left(\frac{r_1-r_2}{P}\right)\, .$$

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

The belt problem is used [^o7] in the design of [aeroplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroplane), [bicycle gearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_gearing), [cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car), and other items with [pulleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley) or [belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_%28mechanical%29) that cross each other. The pulley problem is also used in the design of [conveyor belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conveyor_belt) found in [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) [luggage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luggage) belts and [automated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated) [factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory) lines.[^o8]
