# Pentagonal gyrobicupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pentagonal gyrobicupola"}

The **pentagonal gyrobicupola** is a polyhedron that is constructed by attaching two [pentagonal cupolas](wikipedia://en/1131073) base-to-base, each of its cupolas is twisted at 36°. It is an example of a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) and a [composite polyhedron](wikipedia://en/77684519).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The pentagonal gyrobicupola is a [composite polyhedron](wikipedia://en/77684519): it is constructed by attaching two [pentagonal cupolas](wikipedia://en/1131073) base-to-base. This construction is similar to the [pentagonal orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1177964); the difference is that one of the cupolas in the pentagonal gyrobicupola is twisted at 36°, as suggested by the prefix *gyro-*. The resulting polyhedron has the same faces as the pentagonal orthobicupola does: those cupolas cover their decagonal bases, replacing them with ten [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), ten [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squares), and two [regular pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon). A [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) polyhedron in which all of its faces are [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) is the [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). The pentagonal gyrobicupola has these, enumerating it as the thirty-first Johnson solid $J_{31}$.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: J31 pentagonal gyrobicupola.stl.* 3D model of a pentagonal gyrobicupola

The surface area of a pentagonal gyrobicupola $A$ is the sum of its faces' area, and its volume $V$ is twice the volume of a pentagonal cupola:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac{20 + \sqrt{100 + 10 \sqrt{5} + 10\sqrt{75+30\sqrt{5}}}}{2}a^2 \approx 17.771a^2, \\ V &= \frac{5+4\sqrt{5}}{3}a^3 \approx 4.648a^3. \end{aligned}$$

The pentagonal gyrobicupola has a [three-dimensional symmetry group](wikipedia://en/2694525), the [antiprismatic symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiprismatic_symmetry) of $D_{5d}$. Its dihedral angles (i.e., the angle between two adjacent polygonal faces) are as follows:

- the angle between a pentagon and a square is 159.09°.
- the angle between a square and a triangle, within one cupola, is 148.28°;
- the dihedral angle at the plane joining the two cupolas is the sum of the dihedral angle between square-to-decagon and triangle-to-decagon, 69.09°.
