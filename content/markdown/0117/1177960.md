# Gyroelongated pentagonal cupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyroelongated pentagonal cupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyroelongated pentagonal cupola** is one of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) (*J*<sub>24</sub>). As the name suggests, it can be constructed by gyroelongating a [pentagonal cupola](wikipedia://en/1131073) (*J*<sub>5</sub>) by attaching a [decagonal antiprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decagonal_antiprism) to its base. It can also be seen as a [gyroelongated pentagonal bicupola](wikipedia://en/1178048) (*J*<sub>46</sub>) with one pentagonal cupola removed.

:::

*Image: J24 gyroelongated pentagonal cupola.stl.* 3D model of a gyroelongated pentagonal cupola

## Area and volume {#Area_and_volume}

With edge length a, the surface area is

$$A=\frac{1}{4}\left( 20+25\sqrt{3}+\left(10+\sqrt{5}\right)\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}}\right)a^2\approx25.240003791...a^2,$$

and the volume is

$$V=\left(\frac{5}{6}+\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{5} + \frac{5}{6}\sqrt{2\sqrt{650+290\sqrt{5}}-2\sqrt{5}-2}\right) a^3\approx 9.073333194...a^3.$$

## Dual polyhedron {#Dual_polyhedron}

The dual of the gyroelongated pentagonal cupola has 25 faces: 10 kites, 5 rhombi, and 10 pentagons.
