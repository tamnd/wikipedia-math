# Gyrobifastigium

*Image: J26 gyrobifastigium.stl.* 3D model of a gyrobifastigium

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyrobifastigium"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyrobifastigium** is a polyhedron that is constructed by attaching a triangular prism to the square face of another one. It is an example of a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). It is the only Johnson solid outside of the uniform polyhedra that can [tile three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/6008654).

:::

## Naming and construction {#Naming_and_construction}

The name of the gyrobifastigium comes from the Latin *fastigium*, meaning a "sloping roof".[^o4] In the standard naming convention of the Johnson solids, *bi-* means two solids connected at their bases, and *gyro-* means the two halves are twisted with respect to each other.

The gyrobifastigium is constructed by attaching two [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584) along corresponding square faces, giving a quarter-turn to one prism. These prisms cover the square faces so the resulting polyhedron has four [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and four [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), making eight faces in total, an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635). Because its faces are all [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) and it is [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron), the gyrobifastigium is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), named after American mathematician [Norman W. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_W._Johnson) who listed the 92 such polyhedra; it is indexed as twenty-sixth Johnson solid $J_{26}$. The gyrobifastigium is [composite](wikipedia://en/77684519), because it can be sliced by a plane into two triangular prisms; these prisms are elementaries because they cannot be sliced into many convex, regular-faced polyhedra.

[Cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) for the gyrobifastigium with regular faces and unit edge lengths may easily be derived from the formula of the height of unit edge length $h = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ as follows:

$$\left(\pm\frac{1}{2},\pm\frac{1}{2},0\right),\left(0,\pm\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right),\left(\pm\frac{1}{2},0,-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right).$$

## Properties {#Properties}

To calculate the formula for the [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) and [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of a gyrobifastigium with regular faces and with edge length $a$, one may adapt the corresponding formulae for the triangular prism. Its surface area $A$ can be obtained by summing the area of four equilateral triangles and four squares. The area of an equilateral triangle and a square of side $a$ are $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$ and $a^2$, respectively. Therefore,

$$A = 4\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2\right) + 4a^2 = \left(4+\sqrt{3}\right)a^2 \approx 5.73205a^2.$$

Its volume $V$ is twice the triangular prism's volume. The formula of a uniform triangular prism with edge length $a$ is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^3$. That is:

$$V = 2 \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^3\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a^3 \approx 0.86603a^3.$$

A gyrobifastigium has a [three-dimensional symmetry group](wikipedia://en/2694525), the [antiprismatic symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiprismatic_symmetry) $D_{2\mathrm{d}}$ of order eight. It has three kinds of edge, each with a different [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046):

- between two squares of either prism, the interior angle of an equilateral triangle $\frac{\pi}{3} = 60^\circ$;
- between a triangle and a square of the *same* prism, $\frac{\pi}{2} = 90^\circ$;
- between a triangle and a square of the *other* prism, across the plane joining the two prisms, $\frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{5 \pi}{6} = 150^\circ$.

## Related figures {#Related_figures}

The Schmitt–Conway–Danzer biprism, also called an SCD prototile, is a polyhedron combinatorially equivalent to the gyrobifastigium, but with [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) and irregular triangle faces instead of squares and equilateral triangles. Like the gyrobifastigium, it can fill space, but only [aperiodically](wikipedia://en/868145) or with a [screw symmetry](wikipedia://en/3656192), not with a full three-dimensional group of symmetries. Thus, it provides a partial solution to the three-dimensional [einstein problem](wikipedia://en/43542926).

The [gyrated triangular prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_triangular_prismatic_honeycomb) can be constructed by packing together large numbers of identical gyrobifastigiums. The gyrobifastigium is one of five convex polyhedra with regular faces capable of [space-filling](wikipedia://en/58607583) (the others being the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654), [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584), and [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668)) and it is the only Johnson solid capable of doing so.

The vertices of a gyrobifastigium's [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) can be constructed in the [Cartesian plane](wikipedia://en/7706) with the coordinates

$$\begin{aligned} \quad (0,0,0), \quad (0,0,1), \quad (1,0,0), \quad (1,0,1), \\ \left(\tfrac{1}{2}, \pm \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right), \quad \left(\tfrac{1}{2}, \pm \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 1\right). \end{aligned}$$

By these coordinates, the dual of a gyrobifastigium has four triangles and four quadrilaterals as its faces. It is combinatorially equivalent to a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) with two of its opposite faces subdivided into triangles by diagonals that are not parallel to each other.

A combinatorially equivalent form of the [elongated gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/53773496), a related space-filling polyhedron, can be obtained by instead subdividing two opposite faces of a cube into rectangles by midlines, again choosing the subdivision lines to be non-parallel.
