# Pasch's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pasch's theorem"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Pasch's theorem**, stated in 1882 by the German mathematician [Moritz Pasch](wikipedia://en/4449765),[^o2] is a result in [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry) which cannot be derived from [Euclid's postulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_postulates).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The statement is as follows:

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Pasch's theorem"}

**Pasch's theorem.**

Given points $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ on a line, if it is known that the points are ordered as ($a$, $b$, $c$) and ($b$, $c$, $d$), then it is also true that ($a$, $b$, $d$).[^o6]

:::

\[Here, for example, ($a$, $b$, $c$) means that point $b$ lies between points $a$ and $c$.\]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Hilbert's use of Pasch's theorem"}

## Hilbert's use of Pasch's theorem {#Hilbert's_use_of_Pasch's_theorem}

[David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) originally included Pasch's theorem as an axiom in his modern treatment of Euclidean geometry in *The Foundations of Geometry* (1899). However, it was found by [E. H. Moore](wikipedia://en/1532860) in 1902 that the axiom is redundant,[^o9] and revised editions now list it as a theorem. Thus Pasch's theorem is also known as [Hilbert's discarded axiom](wikipedia://en/838151#Hilbert's_discarded_axiom).

[Pasch's axiom](wikipedia://en/5751973), a separate statement, is also included and remains an axiom in Hilbert's treatment.

:::
