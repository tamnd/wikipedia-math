# Augmented hexagonal prism

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Augmented hexagonal prism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **augmented hexagonal prism** is one of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) ($J_{54}$). As the name suggests, it can be constructed by augmenting a hexagonal [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) by attaching a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) ($J_{1}$) to one of its equatorial faces. When two or three such pyramids are attached, the result may be a [parabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177980) ($J_{55}$), a [metabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177982) ($J_{56}$), or a [triaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177983) ($J_{57}$).

## Construction {#Construction}

The augmented hexagonal prism is constructed by attaching one [equilateral square pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilateral_square_pyramid) onto the square face of a [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668), a process known as [augmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_%28geometry%29). This construction involves the removal of the prism square face and replacing it with the square pyramid, so that there are eleven faces: four [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), five [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), and two regular [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733). A convex polyhedron in which all of the faces are regular is a Johnson solid, and the augmented hexagonal prism is among them, enumerated as $J_{54}$. Relatedly, two or three equilateral square pyramids attaching onto more square faces of the prism give more different Johnson solids; these are the [parabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177980) $J_{55}$, the [metabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177982) $J_{56}$, and the [triaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177983) $J_{57}$.

## Properties {#Properties}

An augmented hexagonal prism with edge length $a$ has surface area

$$\left(5 + 4\sqrt{3}\right)a^2 \approx 11.928a^2,$$

the sum of two hexagons, four equilateral triangles, and five squares area. Its volume

$$\frac{\sqrt{2} + 9\sqrt{3}}{2}a^3 \approx 8.501a^3,$$

can be obtained by slicing into one equilateral square pyramid and one hexagonal prism, and adding their volume up.

It has an [axis of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry) passing through the apex of a square pyramid and the centroid of a prism square face, rotated in a half and full-turn angle. Its [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) can be obtained by calculating the angle of a square pyramid and a hexagonal prism in the following:

- The dihedral angle of an augmented hexagonal prism between two adjacent triangles is the dihedral angle of an equilateral square pyramid, $\arccos \left(-1/3\right) \approx 109.5^\circ$
- The dihedral angle of an augmented hexagonal prism between two adjacent squares is the interior of a regular hexagon, $2\pi/3 = 120^\circ$
- The dihedral angle of an augmented hexagonal prism between square-to-hexagon is the dihedral angle of a hexagonal prism between its base and its lateral face, $\pi/2$
- The dihedral angle of a square pyramid between triangle (its lateral face) and square (its base) is $\arctan \left(\sqrt{2}\right) \approx 54.74^\circ$. Therefore, the dihedral angle of an augmented hexagonal prism between square-to-triangle and between triangle-to-hexagon, on the edge in which the square pyramid and hexagonal prism are attached, are $$\begin{aligned} \arctan \left(\sqrt{2}\right) + \frac{2\pi}{3} \approx 174.74^\circ, \\ \arctan \left(\sqrt{2}\right) + \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 144.74^\circ. \end{aligned}$$ .
