# Lindström's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lindström's theorem"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **Lindström's theorem** (named after Swedish logician [Per Lindström](wikipedia://en/28868368), who published it in 1969) states that [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) is the *[strongest logic](wikipedia://en/28870339)*[^o2] (satisfying certain conditions, e.g. [closure](wikipedia://en/293802) under [classical negation](wikipedia://en/161019)) having both the [(countable) compactness property](wikipedia://en/152207) and the [(downward) Löwenheim–Skolem property](wikipedia://en/341482).[^o3]

:::

Lindström's theorem is perhaps the best known result of what later became known as [abstract model theory](wikipedia://en/31474975),[^o4] the basic notion of which is an [abstract logic](wikipedia://en/28877379);[^o5] the more general notion of an [institution](wikipedia://en/2156522) was later introduced, which advances from a [set-theoretical](wikipedia://en/27553) notion of model to a [category](wikipedia://en/5869)-theoretical one.[^o6] Lindström had previously obtained a similar result in studying first-order logics extended with [Lindström quantifiers](wikipedia://en/8030626).[^o7]

Lindström's theorem has been extended to various other systems of logic, in particular [modal logics](wikipedia://en/333365) by [Johan van Benthem](wikipedia://en/1498176) and [Sebastian Enqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Enqvist).
