# Brauer–Siegel theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer–Siegel theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Brauer–Siegel theorem**, named after [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) and [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602), is an asymptotic result on the behaviour of [algebraic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822), obtained by [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) and [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602). It attempts to generalise the results known on the [class numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_%28number_theory%29) of [imaginary quadratic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field), to a more general sequence of number fields

:::

$$K_1, K_2, \ldots.\$$

In all cases other than the rational field **Q** and imaginary quadratic fields, the [regulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulator_of_a_number_field) *R*<sub>*i*</sub> of *K*<sub>*i*</sub> must be taken into account, because *K*<sub>i</sub> then has units of infinite order by [Dirichlet's unit theorem](wikipedia://en/460227). The quantitative hypothesis of the standard Brauer–Siegel theorem is that if *D*<sub>*i*</sub> is the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) of *K*<sub>*i*</sub>, then

$$\frac{[K_i : \mathbf Q]}{\log|D_i|} \to 0\text{ as }i \to\infty.$$

Assuming that, and the algebraic hypothesis that *K*<sub>*i*</sub> is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of **Q**, the conclusion is that

$$\frac{ \log(h_i R_i) }{ \log\sqrt{|D_i|} } \to 1\text{ as }i \to\infty$$

where *h*<sub>*i*</sub> is the class number of *K*<sub>*i*</sub>. If one assumes that all the degrees $[K_i : \mathbf Q]$ are bounded above by a uniform constant *N*, then one may drop the assumption of normality - this is what is actually proved in Brauer's paper.

This result is [ineffective](wikipedia://en/250237), as indeed was the result on quadratic fields on which it built. Effective results in the same direction were initiated in work of [Harold Stark](wikipedia://en/788807) from the early 1970s.
