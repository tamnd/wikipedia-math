# Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem** characterizes when a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable). The theorem states that a topological space $X$ is metrizable if and only if it is [regular](wikipedia://en/53058) and has a [countably locally finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_locally_finite) (that is, -locally finite) [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29).

:::

A topological space $X$ is called a regular space if every non-empty closed subset $C$ of $X$ and a point $p$ not contained in $C$ admit non-overlapping open neighborhoods. A collection in a space $X$ is countably locally finite (or -locally finite) if it is the union of a countable family of locally finite collections of subsets of $X.$

Unlike [Urysohn's metrization theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urysohn's_metrization_theorem), which provides only a sufficient condition for metrizability, this theorem provides both a necessary and sufficient condition for a topological space to be metrizable. The theorem is named after [Junichi Nagata](wikipedia://en/36481031) and [Yuriĭ Mikhaĭlovich Smirnov](wikipedia://en/68008035), whose (independent) proofs were published in 1950[^o2] and 1951,[^o3] respectively.
