# Abraham Wald

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Abraham Wald"}

**Abraham Wald** (; ; Wald Ábrahám, אברהם וואַלד; 1902-10-31 – 1950) was a Hungarian-American mathematician and statistician who contributed to [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216),[^o3] [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390), and founded the field of [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530).[^o4] One of his well-known statistical works was written during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) on how to minimize the damage to bomber aircraft and took into account the [survivorship bias](wikipedia://en/1745325) in his calculations.[^o5] He spent his research career at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). He was the grandson of Rabbi [Moshe Shmuel Glasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Shmuel_Glasner).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

*Image: Abraham Wald.jpg.* Photograph of Abraham Wald from the [Oberwolfach Research Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1368669)

Wald was born on 31 October 1902 in [Kolozsvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca), [Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania), in the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary). A religious [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew), he did not attend school on Saturdays, as was then required by the Hungarian school system, and so he was [homeschooled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooled) by his parents until college.[^o4] His parents were quite knowledgeable and competent as teachers.

In 1928, he graduated in mathematics from the [King Ferdinand I University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University).[^o8] In 1927, he had entered [graduate school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_education) at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), from which he graduated in 1931 with a Ph.D. in mathematics. His advisor there was [Karl Menger](wikipedia://en/149431).[^o4][^o3]

Despite Wald's brilliance, he could not obtain a university position because of Austrian discrimination against Jews. However, [Oskar Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Morgenstern) created a position for Wald in economics. When [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) [annexed Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss) in 1938, the discrimination against Jews intensified. In particular, Wald and his family were persecuted as Jews. Wald emigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) at the invitation of the [Cowles Commission for Research in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Commission_for_Research_in_Economics), to work on econometrics research.[^o4] By September 1938 Wald was a Fellow of the Carnegie Corporation at Columbia University learning about modern English language statistics from [Harold Hotelling](wikipedia://en/935621). He was appointed to the Columbia Faculty in 1941 and remained there until his death.

*Image: Survivorship-bias.svg.* The damaged portions of returning planes show locations where they can sustain damage and still return home; those hit in other places presumedly do not survive. (Image shows hypothetical data.)

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Wald was a member of the [Statistical Research Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Statistical_Research_Group_of_World_War_II) (SRG) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), where he applied his statistical skills to various wartime problems.[^o9] There was a difficulty: the work was secret and Wald was officially an enemy alien and, as such, barred from access to restricted matter. Hotelling recounts, “This impasse led to a facetious suggestion that each page he wrote should immediately be snatched away and never shown to him again, but was resolved when a federal court granted him a hearing long before his turn on the docket and promptly naturalized him.” [^o10]

The problems that the SRG worked on included methods of sequential analysis and sampling inspection.[^o9] Another was to examine the distribution of [damage to aircraft returning after flying missions](wikipedia://en/1745325#Military) to provide advice on how to minimize bomber losses to enemy fire. Wald derived a useful means of estimating the damage distribution for all aircraft that flew from the data on the damage distribution of all aircraft that returned.[^o5][^o11] His work is considered seminal in the discipline of [operational research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_research), which was then fledgling.

Wald and his wife died in 1950 when the [Air India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India) plane (VT-CFK, a DC-3 aircraft[^o12]) in which they were travelling crashed near the [Rangaswamy Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangaswamy_Peak_and_Pillar) in the northern part of the [Nilgiri Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiri_Mountains), in southern [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India), on an extensive lecture tour at the invitation of the Indian government.[^o4] He had visited the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418) at Calcutta and was to attend the Indian Science Congress at Bangalore in January. Their two children were back at home in the United States.[^o13]

After his death, Wald was criticized by Sir [Ronald A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_A._Fisher) [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society). Fisher attacked Wald for being a mathematician without scientific experience who had written an incompetent book on statistics. Fisher particularly criticized Wald's work on the design of experiments and alleged ignorance of the basic ideas of the subject, as set out by Fisher and [Frank Yates](wikipedia://en/165733).[^o14] Wald's work was defended by [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106) the next year. Neyman explained Wald's work, particularly with respect to the design of experiments.[^o15] [Lucien Le Cam](wikipedia://en/2629104) credits him in his own book, *Asymptotic Methods in Statistical Decision Theory*: "The ideas and techniques used reflect first and foremost the influence of Abraham Wald's writings."[^o16]

He is the father of the noted American physicist [Robert Wald](wikipedia://en/5635914).
