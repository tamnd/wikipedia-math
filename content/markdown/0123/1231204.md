# Constructive quantum field theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Constructive quantum field theory"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), **[constructive](wikipedia://en/360113) quantum field theory** is the field devoted to showing that [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) can be defined in terms of precise mathematical structures. This demonstration requires new [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in a sense analogous to classical [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), putting [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) on a [mathematically rigorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_rigor) foundation. [Weak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_force), [strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_force), and [electromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism) [forces of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_interaction) are believed to have their natural description in terms of [quantum fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29#Quantum_fields).

:::

Attempts to put [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) on a basis of completely defined concepts have involved most branches of mathematics, including [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). It is known that a *quantum field* is inherently hard to handle using conventional mathematical techniques like explicit estimates. This is because a quantum field has the general nature of an [operator-valued distribution](wikipedia://en/293622), a type of object from [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). The [existence theorems](wikipedia://en/251177) for quantum fields can be expected to be very difficult to find, if indeed they are possible at all.

One discovery of the theory that can be related in non-technical terms, is that the dimension *d* of the [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) involved is crucial. Notable work in the field by [James Glimm](wikipedia://en/6901985) and [Arthur Jaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Jaffe) showed that with *d* \< 4 many examples can be found. Along with work of their students, coworkers, and others, constructive field theory resulted in a mathematical foundation and exact interpretation to what previously was only a set of [recipes](wikipedia://en/775), also in the case *d* \< 4.

[Theoretical physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist) had given these rules the name "[renormalization](wikipedia://en/291453)," but most physicists had been skeptical about whether they could be turned into a [mathematical theory](wikipedia://en/30746#Mathematics). Today one of the most important open problems, both in theoretical physics and in mathematics, is to establish similar results for [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) in the realistic case *d* = 4.

The traditional basis of constructive quantum field theory is the set of [Wightman axioms](wikipedia://en/293622). [Konrad Osterwalder](wikipedia://en/19188374) and [Robert Schrader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Schrader) showed that there is an equivalent problem in mathematical probability theory. The examples with *d* \< 4 satisfy the Wightman axioms as well as the [Osterwalder–Schrader axioms](wikipedia://en/1577800). They also fall in the related framework introduced by [Rudolf Haag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Haag) and [Daniel Kastler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kastler), called [algebraic quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_quantum_field_theory). There is a firm belief in the physics community that the gauge theory of [C.N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning) and [Robert Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mills_%28physicist%29) (the [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202)) can lead to a tractable theory, but new ideas and new methods will be required to actually establish this, and this could take many years.
