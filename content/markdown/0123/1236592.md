# Projective determinacy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Projective determinacy"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **projective determinacy** is the special case of the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300) applying only to [projective sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_set).

:::

The **axiom of projective determinacy**, abbreviated **PD**, states that for any two-player infinite game of [perfect information](wikipedia://en/321438) of length [ω](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ω_%28ordinal_number%29) in which the players play [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474), if the victory set (for either player, since the projective sets are closed under complementation) is projective, then one player or the other has a [winning strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winning_strategy).

The axiom is not a theorem of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) (assuming ZFC is consistent), but unlike the full axiom of determinacy (AD), which contradicts the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), it is not known to be inconsistent with ZFC. PD follows from certain [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) axioms, such as the existence of infinitely many [Woodin cardinals](wikipedia://en/248105).

## Consequences {#Consequences}

PD implies that all projective sets are [Lebesgue measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_measurable) (in fact, [universally measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universally_measurable)) and have the [perfect set property](wikipedia://en/2221109) and the [property of Baire](wikipedia://en/2213768). It also implies that every projective [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) may be [uniformized](wikipedia://en/2148548) by a projective set.

PD implies that for all positive integers $n$, there is a largest countable $\Sigma^1_{2n}$ set.[^o3]
