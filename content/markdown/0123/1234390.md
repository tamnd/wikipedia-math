# Tobias Dantzig

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tobias Dantzig"}

**Tobias Dantzig** (; February 19, 1884 – August 9, 1956) was a Russian-American mathematician, the father of [George Dantzig](wikipedia://en/13111), and the author of *[Number: The Language of Science (A critical survey written for the cultured non-mathematician)](wikipedia://en/42245723)* (1930) and *Aspects of Science* (New York, Macmillan, 1937).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Shavli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Šiauliai)[^o3][^o4] (then [Imperial Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia), now [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania)) into the family of Shmuel Dantzig (?-1940) and Guta Dimant (1863–1917), he grew up in [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łódź) and studied mathematics with [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o5] His brother Jacob (1891-1942) was murdered by the Nazis during the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust); he also had a brother Naftali (who lived in Moscow) and sister Emma.

Tobias married a fellow [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University) student, Anja Ourisson, and the couple emigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1910. He worked for a time as a lumberjack, road worker, and house painter in [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon), until returning to academia at the encouragement of [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College) mathematician Frank Griffin.[^o5] Dantzig received his Ph.D. in mathematics from [Indiana University Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington) in 1917, while working as a professor there.[^o5][^o6] He later taught at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), and the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park).

Dantzig died in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles) in 1956. He was the father of [George Dantzig](wikipedia://en/13111), a key figure in the development of [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730).
