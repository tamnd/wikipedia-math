# Ana Caraiani

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ana Caraiani"}

**Ana Caraiani** (born 1984)[^o3] is a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) mathematician, who is a [Royal Society University Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_University_Research_Fellowship) and Professor at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London). Her research interests include [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).

:::

## Education {#Education}

She was born in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest)[^o5] and studied at [Mihai Viteazul High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihai_Viteazul_National_College_%28Bucharest%29).[^o7] In 2001, Caraiani became the first Romanian female competitor in 25 years at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), where she won a silver medal. In the following two years, she won two gold medals.[^o12][^o6][^o7]

After graduating high school in 2003, she pursued her studies in the United States.[^o8] As an undergraduate student at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), Caraiani was a two-time [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) (the only female competitor at the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) to win more than once) and [Elizabeth Lowell Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Lowell_Putnam) Award winner.[^o12][^o16][^o9] Caraiani graduated *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude)* from Princeton in 2007, with an undergraduate thesis on [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module) supervised by [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027).[^o12]

Caraiani did her graduate studies at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under the supervision of Wiles' student [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931), earning her Ph.D. in 2012 with a dissertation concerning local-global compatibility in the [Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/171905).[^o12][^o10]

## Career {#Career}

After spending a year as an L.E. Dickson Instructor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), she returned to Princeton and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) as a Veblen Instructor and NSF Postdoctoral Fellow.[^o12] In 2016, she moved to the [Hausdorff Center for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/23690221) as a Bonn Junior Fellow.[^o12] She moved to [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) in 2017 as a Royal Society University Research Fellow and Senior Lecturer.[^o12] In 2019, she became a Royal Society University Research Fellow and Reader at Imperial College London.[^o12] As of 2021, Caraiani is a full professor at Imperial College London.[^o13] She was also Hausdorff Chair at [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 2022-2023. [^o14]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2007, the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) awarded Caraiani their [Alice T. Schafer Prize](wikipedia://en/57181252).[^o12][^o16] In 2018, she was one of the winners of the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o17]

She was elected as a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society) in the 2020 Class, for "contributions to arithmetic geometry and number theory, in particular the $p$-adic Langlands program".[^o18] She is one of the 2020 winners of the [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize).[^o19] In September 2022 she was awarded the 2023 [New Horizons in Mathematics Prize](wikipedia://en/43125933).[^o20] She was elected to the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2024.[^o21] In 2025 she was awarded the [Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/37554140).[^o3]
