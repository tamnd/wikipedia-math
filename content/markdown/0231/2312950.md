# Obversion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Obversion"}

In [traditional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_logic), **obversion** is a "type of [immediate inference](wikipedia://en/25379366) in which from a given [proposition](wikipedia://en/81094) another proposition is inferred whose subject is the same as the original subject, whose predicate is the contradictory of the original predicate, and whose quality is affirmative if the original proposition's quality was negative and vice versa".[^o2] The quality of the inferred [categorical proposition](wikipedia://en/2418429) is changed but the [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) is the same to the original proposition. The immediately inferred proposition is termed the "obverse" of the original proposition, and is a valid form of inference for all types (A, E, I, O) of categorical propositions.

:::

In a [universal affirmative](wikipedia://en/321652) and a universal negative proposition the [subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subject_%28grammar%29) term and the [predicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28grammar%29) term are both replaced by their [negated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negated) counterparts:

The universal affirmative ("A" proposition) is obverted to a universal negative ("E" proposition).

*"All S are P"* and *"No S are non-P"*

*"All [cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat) are animals"* and *"No cats are non-animals"*

The universal negative ("E" proposition) is obverted to a universal affirmative ("A" proposition).

*"No S are P"* and *"All S are non-P"*

*"No cats are friendly"* and *"All cats are non-friendly"*

In the [particular affirmative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_logic) the quantity of the subject term remains unchanged, but the predicate term of the inferred proposition negates the complement of the predicate term of the original proposition. The particular affirmative ("I" proposition) is obverted to a particular negative ("O" proposition).

*"Some S are P"* and *"Some S are not non-P"*

*"Some animals are friendly creatures"* and *"Some animals are not unfriendly creatures."*

In the obversion of a [particular negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_logic) to a particular affirmative the quantity of the subject also remains unchanged, and the predicate term is changed from simple negation to a term of the complementary class. The particular negative ("O") proposition is obverted to a particular affirmative ("I" proposition).

*"Some S are not P"* and *"Some S are non-P"*

*"Some animals are not friendly creatures"* and *"Some animals are unfriendly creatures."*

Note that the truth-value of an original statement is preserved in its resulting obverse form. Because of this, obversion can be used to determine the immediate inferences of all categorical propositions, regardless of quality or quantity.

In addition, obversion allows us to navigate through the traditional [square of logical opposition](wikipedia://en/246976) by providing a means to proceed from "A" Propositions to "E" Propositions, as well as from "I" Propositions to "O" Propositions, and vice versa. However, although the resulting propositions from obversion are [logically equivalent](wikipedia://en/169324) to the original statements in terms of truth-value, they are not semantically equivalent to their original statements in their standard form.
