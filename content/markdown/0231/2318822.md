# Quantum fingerprinting

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantum fingerprinting"}

**Quantum fingerprinting** is a proposed technique that uses a [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer) to generate a string with a similar function to the [cryptographic hash function](wikipedia://en/439526). Alice and Bob hold $n$-bit strings $x$ and $y$. Their goal and a referee's is to obtain the correct value of $f(x,y) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x = y, \\ 0 & \text{if } x \neq y. \\ \end{cases}$. To do this, $2^{n}$ quantum states are produced from the O(logn)-qubit state fingerprints and sent to the referee who performs the [Swap test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swap_test) to detect if the fingerprints are similar or different with a high probability.[^o2]

:::

If unconditional guarantees of security are needed, and if it is impractical for the communicating parties to arrange to share a secret that can be used in a [Carter–Wegman MAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter–Wegman_MAC), this technique might one day be faster than classical techniques given a [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer) with 5 to 10 [qubits](wikipedia://en/25284). However, these circumstances are very unusual and it is unlikely the technique will ever have a practical application; it is largely of theoretical interest.
