# Hosohedron

*Image: BeachBall.jpg.* This [beach ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_ball) would be a hosohedron with 6 [spherical lune](wikipedia://en/23024648) faces, if the 2 white caps on the ends were removed and the lunes extended to meet at the poles.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hosohedron"}

In [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026), an [$n$-gonal](wikipedia://en/23621) **hosohedron** is a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of [lunes](wikipedia://en/23024648) on a [spherical surface](wikipedia://en/27859), such that each lune shares the same two [polar opposite](wikipedia://en/367577) vertices.

:::

A [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) $n$-gonal hosohedron has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{2,n\},$ with each [spherical lune](wikipedia://en/23024648) having [internal angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) $\tfrac{2\pi}{n}$[radians](wikipedia://en/26003) ($\tfrac{360}{n}$ degrees).[^o3][^o4]

## Hosohedra as regular polyhedra {#Hosohedra_as_regular_polyhedra}

For a regular polyhedron whose Schläfli symbol is {*m*, *n*}, the number of polygonal faces is :

$$N_2=\frac{4n}{2m+2n-mn}.$$

The [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) known to antiquity are the only integer solutions for *m* ≥ 3 and *n* ≥ 3. The restriction *m* ≥ 3 enforces that the polygonal faces must have at least three sides.

When considering polyhedra as a [spherical tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_tiling), this restriction may be relaxed, since [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728) (2-gons) can be represented as [spherical lunes](wikipedia://en/23024648), having non-zero [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_%28geometry%29).

Allowing *m* = 2 makes

$$N_2=\frac{4n}{2\times2+2n-2n}=n,$$

and admits a new infinite class of regular polyhedra, which are the hosohedra. On a spherical surface, the polyhedron {2, *n*} is represented as *n* abutting lunes, with interior angles of $\tfrac{2\pi}{n}$. All these spherical lunes share two common vertices.

## Kaleidoscopic symmetry {#Kaleidoscopic_symmetry}

The $2n$ digonal [spherical lune](wikipedia://en/23024648) faces of a $2n$-hosohedron, $\{2,2n\}$, represent the fundamental domains of [dihedral symmetry in three dimensions](wikipedia://en/2922089): the cyclic symmetry $C_{nv}$, $[n]$, $(*nn)$, order $2n$. The reflection domains can be shown by alternately colored lunes as mirror images.

Bisecting each lune into two spherical triangles creates an $n$-gonal [bipyramid](wikipedia://en/4153), which represents the [dihedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry) $D_{nh}$, order $4n$.

## Relationship with the Steinmetz solid {#Relationship_with_the_Steinmetz_solid}

The tetragonal hosohedron is topologically equivalent to the [bicylinder Steinmetz solid](wikipedia://en/7564265#Bicylinder), the intersection of two cylinders at right-angles.

## Derivative polyhedra {#Derivative_polyhedra}

The [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of the n-gonal hosohedron {2, *n*} is the *n*-gonal [dihedron](wikipedia://en/6109457), {*n*, 2}. The polyhedron {2,2} is self-dual, and is both a hosohedron and a dihedron.

A hosohedron may be modified in the same manner as the other polyhedra to produce a [truncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_polyhedron) variation. The truncated *n*-gonal hosohedron is the n-gonal [prism](wikipedia://en/283007).

## Hosotopes {#Hosotopes}

[Multidimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) analogues in general are called **hosotopes**. A regular hosotope with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {2,*p*,...,*q*} has two vertices, each with a [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) {*p*,...,*q*}.

The [two-dimensional hosotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regular_polytopes_and_compounds#Spherical), {2}, is a [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728).

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The term “hosohedron” appears to derive from the Greek ὅσος (*hosos*) “as many”, the idea being that a hosohedron can have “**as many** faces as desired”.[^o17] It was introduced by Vito Caravelli in the eighteenth century.[^o18]
