# Institutional model theory

*This page is about the concept in mathematical logic. For the concepts in [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology), see [Institutional theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_theory) and [Institutional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_logic)*.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Institutional model theory"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **institutional model theory** generalizes a large portion of [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) to an arbitrary [logical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_system), formalized as [institutions](wikipedia://en/2156522).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The notion of "logical system" here is formalized as an [institution](wikipedia://en/2156522). Institutions constitute a model-oriented meta-theory on logical systems similar to how the theory of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) and [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) constitute a meta-theory for classical [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). Another analogy can be made with [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) versus [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) etc. By abstracting away from the realities of the actual conventional logics, it can be noticed that institution theory comes in fact closer to the realities of non-conventional logics.

Institutional model theory analyzes and generalizes classical model-theoretic notions and results, such as

- elementary diagrams
- [elementary embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_embedding)
- [ultraproducts](wikipedia://en/273642), [Los' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los'_theorem)
- [saturated models](wikipedia://en/535836)
- [axiomatizability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatizable_class)
- [varieties](wikipedia://en/1021753), [Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916) axiomatizability
- [Craig interpolation](wikipedia://en/2056790)
- [Robinson consistency](wikipedia://en/12761741)
- [Beth definability](wikipedia://en/12923821)
- [Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel)'s [completeness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completeness_theorem)

For each concept and theorem, the infrastructure and properties required are analyzed and formulated as conditions on institutions, thus providing a detailed insight to which properties of first-order logic they rely on and how much they can be generalized to other logics.
