# Compact semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compact semigroup"}

In mathematics, a **compact semigroup** is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) in which the sets of solutions to equations can be described by finite sets of equations. The term "compact" here does not refer to any [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) on the semigroup.

:::

Let *S* be a semigroup and *X* a finite set of letters. A system of equations is a subset *E* of the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) *X*<sup>∗</sup> × *X*<sup>∗</sup> of the [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) (finite strings) over *X* with itself. The system *E* is satisfiable in *S* if there is a map *f* from *X* to *S*, which extends to a [semigroup morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_morphism) *f* from *X*<sup>\+</sup> to *S*, such that for all (*u*,*v*) in *E* we have *f*(*u*) = *f*(*v*) in *S*. Such an *f* is a *solution*, or *satisfying assignment*, for the system *E*.[^o2]

Two systems of equations are *equivalent* if they have the same set of satisfying assignments. A system of equations if *independent* if it is not equivalent to a proper subset of itself.[^o2] A semigroup is *compact* if every independent system of equations is finite.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A free monoid on a finite alphabet is compact.[^o6]
- A free monoid on a countable alphabet is compact.[^o7]
- A finitely generated [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) is compact.[^o8]
- A [trace monoid](wikipedia://en/10739141) on a finite set of generators is compact.[^o7]
- The [bicyclic monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicyclic_monoid) is not compact.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The class of compact semigroups is closed under taking subsemigroups and finite direct products.[^o12]
- The class of compact semigroups is not closed under taking morphic images or infinite direct products.[^o12]

:::

## Varieties {#Varieties}

The class of compact semigroups does not form an [equational variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equational_variety). However, a variety of monoids has the property that all its members are compact if and only if all finitely generated members satisfy the [maximal condition on congruences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_condition_on_congruences) (any family of congruences, ordered by inclusion, has a maximal element).[^o14]
