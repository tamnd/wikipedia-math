# Johann Heinrich Ernesti

*Image: Johann Heinrich Ernesti.jpg.* Johann Heinrich Ernesti

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Heinrich Ernesti"}

**Johann Heinrich Ernesti** (; ; 12 March 1652 – 16 October 1729) was a German philosopher, [Lutheran theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_theologian), Latin classicist and poet. He was rector of the [Thomasschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomasschule), and Professor of Poetry at [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University). He gained fame through his writings on [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Ernesti was born in [Königsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsfeld,_Saxony)[^o3] ([Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Saxony)), the son of the preacher Daniel Ernesti.[^o4] He received his first lessons from his father, who was a [Königsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsfeld,_Saxony) [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28ecclesiastical%29). Later, he attended high school in [Altenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altenburg) under his cousin Jacob Daniel Ernesti. From 1670, he studied theology and philosophy at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig), where he became Baccalaureus in 1672, and in 1674 Master of Philosophy.

In 1680 he was appointed as an assessor at the Faculty of Arts of the University.[^o4] Furthermore, he worked until 1682 as a Saturday preacher at the [Church of St. Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Nicholas_Church,_Leipzig). He was conrector (vice-principal) from 1680 and from 1684 to 1729 [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) (principal) of the Thomasschule, succeeding [Jacob Thomasius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Thomasius).[^o5] He introduced a new education policy, including allowing school prayer in German. He served for 45 years, the longest term of office of all known principals. His successor was the influential [Johann Matthias Gesner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Matthias_Gesner).

From 1691 Ernesti was professor of poetry at the University of Leipzig. He taught about Roman poets Horace, Ovid, Juvenal and Virgil and also more recent Latin writers such as [Helius Eobanus Hessus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helius_Eobanus_Hessus). Ernesti himself was active as a writer, his output included [panegyrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panegyric). His most important work was *14 Selectarum Orationum Liber* ([Marcus Tullius Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Tullius_Cicero)).[^o6]

*Image: Der Geist hilft unser Schwachheit auf.JPEG.* Autograph of Bach's motet *[Der Geist hilft unser Schwachheit auf, BWV 226](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Geist_hilft_unser_Schwachheit_auf,_BWV_226)*, composed in 1729 for the funeral of Ernesti

When composer [Johann Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach) applied for the post of cantor (choirmaster) in 1723, Ernesti was the rector of the school.[^o7]

Ernesti died on 16 October 1729 in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig).

For the memorial service for Ernesti's death held on 21 October 1729 in the [Paulinerkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulinerkirche,_Leipzig), the university church, Bach composed the motet [*Der Geist hilft unser Schwachheit auf*, BWV 226](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Geist_hilft_unser_Schwachheit_auf,_BWV_226).[^o5]
