# Heawood graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Heawood graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Heawood graph** is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with 14 vertices and 21 edges, named after [Percy John Heawood](wikipedia://en/3605843).

:::

## Combinatorial properties {#Combinatorial_properties}

The graph is [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508), and all cycles in the graph have six or more edges. Every smaller cubic graph has shorter cycles, so this graph is the 6-[cage](wikipedia://en/7735022), the smallest cubic graph of [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 6. It is a [distance-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/7768222) (see the [Foster census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_census)) and therefore [distance regular](wikipedia://en/7768943).[^o5]

There are 24 [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031) in the Heawood graph; for each matching, the set of edges not in the matching forms a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle). For instance, the figure shows the vertices of the graph placed on a cycle, with the internal diagonals of the cycle forming a matching. By subdividing the cycle edges into two matchings, we can partition the Heawood graph into three perfect matchings (that is, [3-color its edges](wikipedia://en/690647)) in eight different ways.[^o5] Every two perfect matchings, and every two Hamiltonian cycles, can be transformed into each other by a symmetry of the graph.[^o6]

There are 28 six-vertex cycles in the Heawood graph. Each 6-cycle is disjoint from exactly three other 6-cycles; among these three 6-cycles, each one is the symmetric difference of the other two. The graph with one node per 6-cycle, and one edge for each disjoint pair of 6-cycles, is the [Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/5358570).[^o7]

## Geometric and topological properties {#Geometric_and_topological_properties}

*Image: Heawood map on a hexagon.svg.* Heawood's map. Opposite edges of the large hexagon are connected to form a torus.

The Heawood graph is a [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057); that is, it can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/8149170) without crossings onto a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800). The result is the [regular map](wikipedia://en/21533914) $\{6,3\}_{2,1}$, with 7 [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) faces.[^o9] Each face of the map is adjacent to every other face, thus as a result [coloring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map_coloring_%28mathematics%29) the map requires 7 colors. The map and graph were discovered by [Percy John Heawood](wikipedia://en/3605843) in 1890, who proved that no map on the torus could require more than seven colors and thus this map is maximal.[^o10][^o11]

The map can be faithfully realized as the [Szilassi polyhedron](wikipedia://en/7652409),[^o12] the only known polyhedron apart from the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) such that every pair of faces is adjacent.

The Heawood graph is the [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404),[^o9] the graph representing incidences between points and lines in that geometry. With this interpretation, the 6-cycles in the Heawood graph correspond to [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) in the Fano plane. Also, the Heawood graph is the [Tits building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tits_building) of the group [SL<sub>3</sub>(F<sub>2</sub>)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GL%283,2%29).

The Heawood graph has [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298594) 3, and is the smallest cubic graph with that crossing number . Including the Heawood graph, there are 8 distinct graphs of order 14 with crossing number 3.

The Heawood graph is the smallest cubic graph with [Colin de Verdière graph invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_de_Verdière_graph_invariant) $\mu = 6$.[^o13]

The Heawood graph is a [unit distance graph](wikipedia://en/7158665): it can be embedded in the plane such that adjacent vertices are exactly at distance one apart, with no two vertices embedded to the same point and no vertex embedded into a point within an edge.[^o14]

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the Heawood graph is isomorphic to the [projective linear group](wikipedia://en/382667) PGL<sub>2</sub>(7), a group of order 336.[^o17] It acts transitively on the vertices, on the edges and on the arcs of the graph. Therefore, the Heawood graph is a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896). It has automorphisms that take any vertex to any other vertex and any edge to any other edge. More strongly, the Heawood graph is [4-arc-transitive](wikipedia://en/1577896).[^o18] According to the [Foster census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_census), the Heawood graph, referenced as F014A, is the only cubic symmetric graph on 14 vertices.[^o19][^o20]

It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o21]

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Heawood graph is $(x-3) (x+3) (x^2-2)^6$. It is the only graph with this characteristic polynomial, making it a graph determined by its spectrum.
