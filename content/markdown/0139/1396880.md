# Tutte–Coxeter graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tutte–Coxeter graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Tutte–Coxeter graph** or **Tutte eight-cage** or **Cremona–Richmond graph** is a 3-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) with 30 vertices and 45 edges. As the unique smallest [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) of [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 8, it is a [cage](wikipedia://en/7735022) and a [Moore graph](wikipedia://en/4112719). It is [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431), and can be constructed as the [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of the [generalized quadrangle](wikipedia://en/3947006) *W*<sub>2</sub> (known as the [Cremona–Richmond configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona–Richmond_configuration)). The graph is named after [William Thomas Tutte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thomas_Tutte) and [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328); it was discovered by Tutte (1947) but its connection to geometric configurations was investigated by both authors in a pair of jointly published papers (Tutte 1958; Coxeter 1958a).

:::

All the [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [distance-regular graphs](wikipedia://en/7768943) are known.[^o3] The Tutte–Coxeter is one of the 13 such graphs.

It has [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298594) 13,[^o4][^o5] [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o6]

## Constructions and automorphisms {#Constructions_and_automorphisms}

The Tutte–Coxeter graph is the [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431) [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) connecting the 15 [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031) of a 6-vertex [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) K<sub>6</sub> to its 15 edges, as described by Coxeter (1958b), based on work by Sylvester (1844). Each vertex corresponds to an edge or a perfect matching, and connected vertices represent the [incidence structure](wikipedia://en/723125) between edges and matchings.

Based on this construction, Coxeter showed that the Tutte–Coxeter graph is a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896); it has a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of 1440 [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/15094186), which may be identified with the automorphisms of the group of permutations on six elements (Coxeter 1958b). The [inner automorphisms](wikipedia://en/61839) of this group correspond to permuting the six vertices of the *K*<sub>6</sub> graph; these permutations act on the Tutte–Coxeter graph by permuting the vertices on each side of its bipartition while keeping each of the two sides fixed as a set. In addition, the [outer automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_automorphism) of the group of permutations swap one side of the bipartition for the other. As Coxeter showed, any path of up to five edges in the Tutte–Coxeter graph is equivalent to any other such path by one such automorphism.

## The Tutte–Coxeter graph as a building {#The_Tutte–Coxeter_graph_as_a_building}

This graph is the [spherical building](wikipedia://en/275015#Spherical_building) associated to the symplectic group $Sp_4(\mathbb{F}_{2})$ (there is an exceptional isomorphism between this group and the symmetric group $S_6$). More specifically, it is the incidence graph of a [generalized quadrangle](wikipedia://en/3947006).

Concretely, the Tutte-Coxeter graph can be defined from a 4-dimensional [symplectic vector space](wikipedia://en/292852) $V$ over $\mathbb{F}_{2}$ as follows:

- vertices are either nonzero vectors, or isotropic 2-dimensional subspaces,
- there is an edge between a nonzero vector *v* and an isotropic 2-dimensional subspace $W\subset V$ if and only if $v \in W$.
