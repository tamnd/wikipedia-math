# Stiefel manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stiefel manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Stiefel manifold** $V_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the set of all [orthonormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal) [*k*-frames](wikipedia://en/22256304) in $\mathbb{R}^n.$ That is, it is the set of ordered orthonormal *k*-tuples of [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28mathematics%29) in $\mathbb{R}^n.$ It is named after Swiss mathematician [Eduard Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876). Likewise one can define the [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) Stiefel manifold $V_k(\mathbb{C}^n)$ of orthonormal *k*-frames in $\mathbb{C}^n$ and the [quaternionic](wikipedia://en/51440) Stiefel manifold $V_k(\mathbb{H}^n)$ of orthonormal *k*-frames in $\mathbb{H}^n$. More generally, the construction applies to any real, complex, or quaternionic [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856).

:::

In some contexts, a non-[compact](wikipedia://en/6042) Stiefel manifold is defined as the set of all [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) *k*-frames in $\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{C}^n,$ or $\mathbb{H}^n;$ this is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to the more restrictive definition, as the compact Stiefel manifold is a [deformation retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_retract) of the non-compact one, by employing the [Gram–Schmidt process](wikipedia://en/82361). Statements about the non-compact form correspond to those for the compact form, replacing the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) (or [unitary](wikipedia://en/173993) or [symplectic](wikipedia://en/173977) group) with the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564).

## Topology {#Topology}

Let $\mathbb{F}$ stand for $\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},$ or $\mathbb{H}.$ The Stiefel manifold $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ can be thought of as a set of *n* × *k* [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) by writing a *k*-frame as a matrix of *k* [column vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_vector) in $\mathbb F^n.$ The orthonormality condition is expressed by *A*\**A* = $I_k$ where *A*\* denotes the [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548) of *A* and $I_k$ denotes the *k* × *k* [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718). We then have

$$V_k(\mathbb F^n) = \left\{A \in \mathbb F^{n\times k} : A^* A = I_k \right\}.$$

The [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28structure%29) on $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ is the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) inherited from $\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}.$ With this topology $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) whose dimension is given by

$$\begin{aligned} \dim V_k(\mathbb{R}^n) &= nk - \frac{1}{2}k(k+1) \\ \dim V_k(\mathbb{C}^n) &= 2nk - k^2 \\ \dim V_k(\mathbb{H}^n) &= 4nk - k(2k-1) \end{aligned}$$

## As a homogeneous space {#As_a_homogeneous_space}

Each of the Stiefel manifolds $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ can be viewed as a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) for the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of a [classical group](wikipedia://en/10172878) in a natural manner.

Every orthogonal transformation of a *k*-frame in $\mathbb{R}^n$ results in another *k*-frame, and any two *k*-frames are related by some orthogonal transformation. In other words, the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) O(*n*) acts [transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_action) on $V_k(\mathbb{R}^n).$ The [stabilizer subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) of a given frame is the subgroup isomorphic to O(*n*−*k*) which acts nontrivially on the [orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833) of the space spanned by that frame.

Likewise the [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) U(*n*) acts transitively on $V_k(\mathbb{C}^n)$ with stabilizer subgroup U(*n*−*k*) and the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) Sp(*n*) acts transitively on $V_k(\mathbb{H}^n)$ with stabilizer subgroup Sp(*n*−*k*).

In each case $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ can be viewed as a homogeneous space:

$$\begin{aligned} V_k(\mathbb{R}^n) &\cong \mbox{O}(n)/\mbox{O}(n-k)\\ V_k(\mathbb{C}^n) &\cong \mbox{U}(n)/\mbox{U}(n-k)\\ V_k(\mathbb{H}^n) &\cong \mbox{Sp}(n)/\mbox{Sp}(n-k) \end{aligned}$$

When *k* = *n*, the corresponding action is free so that the Stiefel manifold $V_n(\mathbb F^n)$ is a [principal homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363441) for the corresponding classical group.

When *k* is strictly less than *n* then the [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) SO(*n*) also acts transitively on $V_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with stabilizer subgroup isomorphic to SO(*n*−*k*) so that

$$V_k(\mathbb{R}^n) \cong \mbox{SO}(n)/\mbox{SO}(n-k)\qquad\mbox{for } k < n.$$

The same holds for the action of the [special unitary group](wikipedia://en/173997) on $V_k(\mathbb{C}^n)$

$$V_k(\mathbb{C}^n) \cong \mbox{SU}(n)/\mbox{SU}(n-k)\qquad\mbox{for } k < n.$$

Thus for *k* ≤ *n* − 1, the Stiefel manifold is a principal homogeneous space for the corresponding *special* classical group.

## Uniform measure {#Uniform_measure}

The Stiefel manifold can be equipped with a [uniform measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29), i.e. a [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) that is [invariant](wikipedia://en/7635201) under the action of the groups noted above. For example, $V_1(\mathbb{R}^2)$ which is isomorphic to the unit circle in the Euclidean plane, has as its uniform measure the natural uniform measure ([arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401)) on the circle. It is straightforward to sample this measure on $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ using Gaussian [random matrices](wikipedia://en/1648765): if $A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}$ is a random matrix with [independent entries identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_and_identically_distributed) according to the [standard normal distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_normal_distribution) on $\mathbb{F}$ and *A* = *QR* is the [QR factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QR_factorization) of *A*, then the matrices, $Q\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}, R\in\mathbb{F}^{k\times k}$ are [independent random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables) and *Q* is distributed according to the uniform measure on $V_k(\mathbb F^n).$ This result is a consequence of the [Bartlett decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/364996#Bartlett_decomposition).[^o5]

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

A 1-frame in $\mathbb{F}^n$ is nothing but a unit vector, so the Stiefel manifold $V_1(\mathbb F^n)$ is just the [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) in $\mathbb{F}^n.$ Therefore:

$$\begin{aligned} V_1(\mathbb{R}^n) &= S^{n-1}\\ V_1(\mathbb{C}^n) &= S^{2n-1}\\ V_1(\mathbb{H}^n) &= S^{4n-1} \end{aligned}$$

Given a 2-frame in $\mathbb{R}^n,$ let the first vector define a point in *S*<sup>*n*−1</sup> and the second a unit [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) to the sphere at that point. In this way, the Stiefel manifold $V_2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ may be identified with the [unit tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/7932644) to *S*<sup>*n*−1</sup>.

When *k* = *n* or *n*−1 we saw in the previous section that $V_k(\mathbb{F}^n)$ is a principal homogeneous space, and therefore [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to the corresponding classical group:

$$\begin{aligned} V_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^n) &\cong \mathrm{SO}(n)\\ V_{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^n) &\cong \mathrm{SU}(n) \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} V_{n}(\mathbb{R}^n) &\cong \mathrm O(n)\\ V_{n}(\mathbb{C}^n) &\cong \mathrm U(n)\\ V_{n}(\mathbb{H}^n) &\cong \mathrm{Sp}(n) \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Functoriality {#Functoriality}

Given an orthogonal inclusion between vector spaces $X \hookrightarrow Y,$ the image of a set of *k* orthonormal vectors is orthonormal, so there is an induced closed inclusion of Stiefel manifolds, $V_k(X) \hookrightarrow V_k(Y),$ and this is [functorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functorial). More subtly, given an *n*-dimensional vector space *X*, the [dual basis](wikipedia://en/1182982) construction gives a bijection between bases for *X* and bases for the dual space $X^*,$ which is continuous, and thus yields a homeomorphism of top Stiefel manifolds $V_n(X) \stackrel{\sim}{\to} V_n(X^*).$ This is also functorial for isomorphisms of vector spaces.

## As a principal bundle {#As_a_principal_bundle}

There is a natural projection

$$p : V_k(\mathbb F^n) \to G_k(\mathbb F^n)$$

from the Stiefel manifold $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ to the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of *k*-planes in $\mathbb{F}^n$ which sends a *k*-frame to the [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) spanned by that frame. The [fiber](wikipedia://en/6603892) over a given point *P* in $G_k(\mathbb F^n)$ is the set of all orthonormal *k*-frames contained in the space *P*.

This projection has the structure of a [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) where *G* is the associated classical group of degree *k*. Take the real case for concreteness. There is a natural right action of O(*k*) on $V_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ which rotates a *k*-frame in the space it spans. This action is free but not transitive. The [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) of this action are precisely the orthonormal *k*-frames spanning a given *k*-dimensional subspace; that is, they are the fibers of the map *p*. Similar arguments hold in the complex and quaternionic cases.

We then have a sequence of principal bundles:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm O(k) &\to V_k(\mathbb{R}^n) \to G_k(\mathbb{R}^n)\\ \mathrm U(k) &\to V_k(\mathbb{C}^n) \to G_k(\mathbb{C}^n)\\ \mathrm{Sp}(k) &\to V_k(\mathbb{H}^n) \to G_k(\mathbb{H}^n) \end{aligned}$$

The [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) [associated](wikipedia://en/398874) to these principal bundles via the natural action of *G* on $\mathbb{F}^k$ are just the [tautological bundles](wikipedia://en/2913502) over the Grassmannians. In other words, the Stiefel manifold $V_k(\mathbb F^n)$ is the orthogonal, unitary, or symplectic [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) associated to the tautological bundle on a Grassmannian.

When one passes to the $n\to \infty$ limit, these bundles become the [universal bundles](wikipedia://en/2179570) for the classical groups.

## Homotopy {#Homotopy}

The Stiefel manifolds fit into a family of [fibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_fibration):

$$V_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}) \to V_k(\mathbb{R}^n) \to S^{n-1},$$

thus the first non-trivial [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) of the space $V_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is in dimension *n* − *k*. Moreover,

$$\pi_{n-k} V_k(\mathbb{R}^n) \simeq \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & n-k>0 \text{ even or } k=1 \\ \mathbb{Z}_2 & n-k \text{ odd and } k>1 \text{ or }k=n\end{cases}$$

This result is used in the obstruction-theoretic definition of [Stiefel–Whitney classes](wikipedia://en/877761).
