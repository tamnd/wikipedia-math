# Econometric model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Econometric model"}

**Econometric models** are [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576) used in [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390). An econometric model specifies the [statistical](wikipedia://en/26685) relationship that is believed to hold between the various economic quantities pertaining to a particular economic phenomenon. An econometric model can be derived from a [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_system_%28philosophy%29) [economic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_model) by allowing for uncertainty, or from an economic model which itself is [stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222). However, it is also possible to use econometric models that are not tied to any specific economic theory.[^o2]

:::

A simple example of an econometric model is one that assumes that monthly [spending by consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_%28economics%29) is linearly [dependent](wikipedia://en/437701) on consumers' income in the previous month. Then the model will consist of the equation

$$C_t = a + bY_{t-1} + e_t,$$

where *C*<sub>*t*</sub> is [consumer spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_spending) in month *t*, *Y*<sub>*t*-1</sub> is income during the previous month, and *e<sub>t</sub>* is an [error term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) measuring the extent to which the model cannot fully explain consumption. Then one objective of the [econometrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrician) is to obtain estimates of the [parameters](wikipedia://en/25065) *a* and *b*; these estimated parameter values, when used in the model's equation, enable predictions for future values of consumption to be made contingent on the prior month's income.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

In [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390), as in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in general, it is presupposed that the quantities being analyzed can be treated as [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685). An econometric model then is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [joint probability distributions](wikipedia://en/879637) to which the true joint probability distribution of the variables under study is supposed to belong. In the case in which the elements of this set can be [indexed](wikipedia://en/393055) by a finite number of real-valued *parameters*, the model is called a [parametric model](wikipedia://en/2883137); otherwise it is a [nonparametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics) or [semiparametric model](wikipedia://en/4415208). A large part of econometrics is the study of methods for [selecting](wikipedia://en/3664073) models, [estimating](wikipedia://en/1565926) them, and carrying out [inference](wikipedia://en/27577) on them.

The most common econometric models are [structural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_equation_models), in that they convey causal and [counterfactual](wikipedia://en/491578) information,[^o5] and are used for policy evaluation. For example, an equation modeling consumption spending based on income could be used to see what consumption would be contingent on any of various hypothetical levels of income, only one of which (depending on the choice of a [fiscal policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_policy)) will end up actually occurring.

:::

## Basic models {#Basic_models}

Some of the common econometric models are:

- [Linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386)
- [Generalized linear models](wikipedia://en/747122)
- [Probit](wikipedia://en/1689101)
- [Logit](wikipedia://en/172068)
- [Tobit](wikipedia://en/3722064)
- [ARIMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIMA)
- [Vector Autoregression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_Autoregression)
- [Cointegration](wikipedia://en/1822603)
- Hazard

## Use in policy-making {#Use_in_policy-making}

Comprehensive models of [macroeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics) relationships are used by [central banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) and governments to evaluate and guide economic policy. One famous econometric model of this nature is the [Federal Reserve Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank) econometric model.
