# Closed monoidal category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Closed monoidal category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **closed monoidal category** (or a *monoidal closed category*) is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) that is both a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) and a [closed category](wikipedia://en/1397197) in such a way that the structures are compatible.

:::

A classic example is the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), **Set**, where the monoidal product of sets $A$ and $B$ is the usual [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $A \times B$, and the [internal Hom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Hom) $B^A$ is the set of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) from $A$ to $B$. A non-[cartesian](wikipedia://en/43676277) example is the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces), ***K*-Vect**, over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$. Here the monoidal product is the usual [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of [vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_spaces), and the internal Hom is the vector space of [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) from one vector space to another.

The [internal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_language) of closed symmetric monoidal categories is [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675) and the [type system](wikipedia://en/199701) is the [linear type system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_type_system). Many examples of closed monoidal categories are [symmetric](wikipedia://en/3865249). However, this need not always be the case, as non-symmetric monoidal categories can be encountered in category-theoretic formulations of [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics); roughly speaking, this is because word-order in natural language matters.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **closed monoidal category** is a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) $\mathcal{C}$ such that for every object $B$ the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) given by right tensoring with $B$

$$A\mapsto A\otimes B$$

has a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint), written

$$A\mapsto (B \Rightarrow A).$$

This means that there exists a bijection, called '[currying](wikipedia://en/6600)', between the [Hom-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hom-set)

$$\text{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(A\otimes B, C)\cong\text{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(A,B\Rightarrow C)$$

that is natural in both *A* and *C*. In a different, but common notation, one would say that the functor

$$-\otimes B:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$$

has a right adjoint

$$[B, -]:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$$

Equivalently, a closed monoidal category $\mathcal{C}$ is a category equipped, for every two objects *A* and *B*, with

- an object $A\Rightarrow B$,
- a morphism $\mathrm{eval}_{A,B} : (A\Rightarrow B) \otimes A \to B$,

satisfying the following universal property: for every morphism

$$f : X\otimes A\to B$$

there exists a unique morphism

$$h : X \to A\Rightarrow B$$

such that

$$f = \mathrm{eval}_{A,B}\circ(h \otimes \mathrm{id}_A).$$

It can be shown that this construction defines a functor $\Rightarrow : \mathcal{C}^{op} \times \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$. This functor is called the [internal Hom functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Hom_functor), and the object $A \Rightarrow B$ is called the **internal Hom** of $A$ and $B$. Many other notations are in common use for the internal Hom. When the tensor product on $\mathcal{C}$ is the cartesian product, the usual notation is $B^A$ and this object is called the [exponential object](wikipedia://en/2237858).

:::

## Biclosed and symmetric categories {#Biclosed_and_symmetric_categories}

Strictly speaking, we have defined a **right closed** monoidal category, since we required that *right* tensoring with any object $A$ has a right adjoint. In a **left closed** monoidal category, we instead demand that the functor of left tensoring with any object $A$

$$B\mapsto A\otimes B$$

have a right adjoint

$$B\mapsto(B\Leftarrow A)$$

A **biclosed** monoidal category is a monoidal category that is both left and right closed.

A [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/3865249) is left closed if and only if it is right closed. Thus we may safely speak of a 'symmetric monoidal closed category' without specifying whether it is left or right closed. In fact, the same is true more generally for [braided monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/1470466): since the braiding makes $A \otimes B$ naturally isomorphic to $B \otimes A$, the distinction between tensoring on the left and tensoring on the right becomes immaterial, so every right closed braided monoidal category becomes left closed in a canonical way, and vice versa.

We have described closed monoidal categories as monoidal categories with an extra property. One can equivalently define a closed monoidal category to be a [closed category](wikipedia://en/1397197) with an extra property. Namely, we can demand the existence of a [tensor product](wikipedia://en/330604) that is [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint) to the [internal Hom functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Hom_functor). In this approach, closed monoidal categories are also called **monoidal closed categories**.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [cartesian closed category](wikipedia://en/237037) is a symmetric, monoidal closed category, when the monoidal structure is the cartesian product structure. The internal Hom functor is given by the [exponential object](wikipedia://en/2237858) $B^A$.
  - In particular, the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), **Set**, is a symmetric, closed monoidal category. Here the internal Hom $A \Rightarrow B$ is just the set of functions from $A$ to $B$.
- The [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812), ***R*-Mod** over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* is a non-cartesian, symmetric, monoidal closed category. The monoidal product is given by the [tensor product of modules](wikipedia://en/2993836) and the internal Hom $M\Rightarrow N$ is given by the space of [*R*-linear maps](wikipedia://en/474118) $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M, N)$ with its natural *R*-module structure.
  - In particular, the category of vector spaces over a field $K$ is a symmetric, closed monoidal category.
  - [Abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) can be regarded as **Z**-modules, so the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748) is also a symmetric, closed monoidal category.
- A [symmetric compact closed category](wikipedia://en/5557623) is a symmetric monoidal closed category in which the internal Hom functor $A\Rightarrow B$ is given by $A^*\otimes B$. The canonical example is the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces, **FdVect**.

::: {#o8 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Counterexamples"}

### Counterexamples {#Counterexamples}

- The [category of rings](wikipedia://en/15300181) is a symmetric, monoidal category under the [tensor product of rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_rings), with $\mathbb{Z}$ serving as the unit object. This category is *not* closed. If it were, there would be exactly one homomorphism between any pair of rings: $\operatorname{Hom}(R,S)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}\otimes R,S)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},R\Rightarrow S)\cong\{\bullet\}$. The same holds for the category of *R*-[algebras](wikipedia://en/191788) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*.
