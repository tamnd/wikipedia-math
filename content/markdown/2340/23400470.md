# Weighted Voronoi diagram

*Image: Voronoi AAAAA14.png.* A circular Dirichlet tessellation with randomly assigned weights

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weighted Voronoi diagram"}

In mathematics, a **weighted Voronoi diagram** in *n* dimensions is a generalization of a [Voronoi diagram](wikipedia://en/177668). The Voronoi cells in a weighted Voronoi diagram are defined in terms of a distance function. The distance function may specify the usual [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932), or may be some other, special distance function. In weighted Voronoi diagrams, each site has a weight that influences the distance computation. The idea is that larger weights indicate more important sites, and such sites will get bigger Voronoi cells.

:::

In a **multiplicatively weighted Voronoi diagram**, the distance between a point and a site is divided by the (positive) weight of the site.[^o2] In the plane under the ordinary [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932), the multiplicatively weighted Voronoi diagram is also called **circular Dirichlet tessellation**[^o3][^o4] and its edges are circular arcs and straight line segments. A Voronoi cell may be non-convex, disconnected and may have holes. This diagram arises, e.g., as a model of [crystal growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_growth), where crystals from different points may grow with different speed. Since crystals may grow in empty space only and are continuous objects, a natural variation is the **crystal Voronoi diagram**, in which the cells are defined somewhat differently.

In an **additively weighted Voronoi diagram**, weights are subtracted from the distances. In the plane under the ordinary [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) this diagram is also known as the **hyperbolic Dirichlet tessellation** and its edges are arcs of hyperbolas and straight line segments.[^o2]

The [power diagram](wikipedia://en/35793286) is defined when weights are subtracted from the squared Euclidean distance. It can also be defined using the [power distance](wikipedia://en/2094044) defined from a set of circles.[^o5]
