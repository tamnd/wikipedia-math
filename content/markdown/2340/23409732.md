# Theorem of three moments

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theorem of three moments"}

In civil engineering and [structural analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_analysis) **Clapeyron's** **theorem of three moments** (by [Émile Clapeyron](wikipedia://en/203175)) is a relationship among the [bending moments](wikipedia://en/6018334) at three consecutive supports of a horizontal beam.

:::

Let *A,B,C-D be the three consecutive points of support, and denote by-***l***the length of*AB*and $l'$ the length of*BC*, by*w*and $w'$ the weight per unit of length in these segments. Then[^o2] the bending moments $M_A,\, M_B,\, M_C$ at the three points are related by:*

$$M_A l + 2 M_B (l+l') +M_C l' = \frac{1}{4} w l^3 + \frac{1}{4} w' (l')^3.$$

This equation can also be written as [^o3]

$$M_A l + 2 M_B (l+l') +M_C l' = \frac{6 a_1 x_1}{l} + \frac{6 a_2 x_2}{l'}$$

where *a*<sub>1</sub> is the area on the [bending moment diagram](wikipedia://en/15954084) due to vertical loads on AB, *a*<sub>2</sub> is the area due to loads on BC, *x*<sub>1</sub> is the distance from A to the centroid of the bending moment diagram of beam AB, *x*<sub>2</sub> is the distance from C to the centroid of the area of the bending moment diagram of beam BC.

The second equation is more general as it does not require that the weight of each segment be distributed uniformly.

## Derivation of three moments equations {#Derivation_of_three_moments_equations}

[Christian Otto Mohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Otto_Mohr)'s theorem[^o5] can be used to derive the three moment theorem[^o6] (TMT).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Mohr's first theorem"}

### Mohr's first theorem {#Mohr's_first_theorem}

The change in [slope](wikipedia://en/29368) of a [deflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflection_%28physics%29) curve between two points of a beam is equal to the area of the M/EI diagram between those two points.(Figure 02)

*Image: Mohr's First Theorem.png.* Figure 02-Mohr's First Theorem

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Mohr's second theorem"}

### Mohr's second theorem {#Mohr's_second_theorem}

Consider two points k1 and k2 on a [beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_%28structure%29). The [deflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflection_%28physics%29) of k1 and k2 relative to the point of intersection between tangent at k1 and k2 and vertical through k1 is equal to the moment of M/EI diagram between k1 and k2 about k1.(Figure 03)

*Image: Mohr's Second Theorem.png.* Figure03-Mohr's Second Theorem

The three moment equation expresses the relation between [bending moments](wikipedia://en/6018334) at three successive supports of a continuous beam, subject to a loading on a two adjacent span with or without [settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_%28structural%29) of the supports.

:::

### The sign convention {#The_sign_convention}

According to the Figure 04,

1. The moment M1, M2, and M3 be positive if they cause [compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_%28physical%29) in the upper part of the beam. (sagging positive)
1. The [deflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflection_%28physics%29) downward positive. (Downward settlement positive)
1. Let ABC is a continuous beam with support at A,B, and C. Then moment at A,B, and C are M1, M2, and M3, respectively.
1. Let A' B' and C' be the final positions of the beam ABC due to support [settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_%28structural%29).

*Image: Deflection Curve of a Continuous Beam.png.* Figure 04-Deflection Curve of a Continuous Beam Under Settlement

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Derivation of three moment theorem"}

### Derivation of three moment theorem {#Derivation_of_three_moment_theorem}

PB'Q is a tangent drawn at B' for final [Elastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29) Curve A'B'C' of the [beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_%28structure%29) ABC. RB'S is a horizontal line drawn through B'. Consider, Triangles RB'P and QB'S.

$$\dfrac{PR}{RB'} = \dfrac{SQ}{B'S},$$

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\dfrac{PR}{L1} = \dfrac{SQ}{L2} \tag{1}$$

:::

::: {#o15 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$PR = \Delta B - \Delta A + PA' \tag{2}$$

:::

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$SQ = \Delta C - \Delta B - QC' \tag{3}$$

:::

From (1), (2), and (3),

$$\dfrac{\Delta B - \Delta A + PA'}{L1} = \dfrac{\Delta C - \Delta B - QC'}{L2}$$

::: {#o17 .equation cue=numblk name="a"}

$$\dfrac{PA'}{L1} + \dfrac{QC'}{L2} = \dfrac{\Delta A -\Delta B}{L1} + \dfrac{\Delta C -\Delta B}{L2} \tag{a}$$

:::

Draw the M/EI diagram to find the PA' and QC'.

*Image: Moment Diagram.png.* Figure 05 - M / EI Diagram

**From Mohr's Second Theorem** PA' = First moment of area of M/EI diagram between A and B about A.

$$PA' = \left(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{M_1}{E_1 I_1} \times L_1\right)\times L_1\times \frac{1}{3} + \left(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{M_2}{E_2 I_2} \times L_1\right)\times L_1\times\frac{2}{3}+ \frac{A_1 X_1}{E_1 I_1}$$

QC' = First moment of area of M/EI diagram between B and C about C.

$$QC' = \left(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{M_3}{E_2 I_2} \times L_2\right)\times L_2\times\frac{1}{3} + \left(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{M_2}{E_2 I_2} \times L_2\right)\times L_2\times\frac{2}{3}+ \frac{A_2 X_2}{E_2 I_2}$$

Substitute in PA' and QC' on equation (a), the Three Moment Theorem (TMT) can be obtained.

:::

## Three moment equation {#Three_moment_equation}

$$\frac{M_1 L_1}{E_1 I_1}+ 2M_2\left(\frac{L_1}{E_1 I_1} + \frac{L_2}{E_2 I_2}\right)+\frac{M_3 L_2}{E_2 I_2} = 6 [\frac{\Delta A - \Delta B}{L_1} + \frac{\Delta C - \Delta B}{L_2}] - 6 [\frac{A_1 X_1}{E_1 I_1 L_1} + \frac{A_2 X_2}{E_2 I_2 L_2}]$$
