# Prime end

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime end"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **prime end** compactification is a method to [compactify](wikipedia://en/6617) a [topological disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_disc) (i.e. a simply connected open set in the plane) by adding the boundary circle in an appropriate way.

:::

## Historical notes {#Historical_notes}

The concept of prime ends was introduced by [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625) to describe the boundary behavior of [conformal maps](wikipedia://en/50627) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) in geometric terms.[^o3] The theory has been generalized to more general open sets.[^o4] The expository paper of (Epstein 1981) provides a good account of this theory with complete proofs: it also introduces a definition which make sense in any open set and dimension.[^o4] (Milnor 2006) gives an accessible introduction to prime ends in the context of complex dynamical systems.

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

The set of prime ends of the domain $B$ is the set of equivalence classes of chains of arcs converging to a point on the boundary of $B$.

In this way, a point in the boundary may correspond to many points in the prime ends of $B$, and conversely, many points in the boundary may correspond to a point in the prime ends of $B$.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Carathéodory's principal theorem on the correspondence between boundaries under conformal mappings can be expressed as follows:

If $\text{ƒ}$ maps the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) conformally and one-to-one onto the domain $B$, it induces a [one-to-one mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_mapping) between the points on the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) and the prime ends of $B$.

:::
