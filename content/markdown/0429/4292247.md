# Solvmanifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Solvmanifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **solvmanifold** is a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) of a [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_%28topology%29) [solvable Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvable_Lie_group). It may also be characterized as a quotient of a connected solvable [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) by a [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_%28topology%29) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011). (Some authors also require that the Lie group be simply-connected, or that the quotient be compact.) A special class of solvmanifolds, [nilmanifolds](wikipedia://en/3668693), was introduced by [Anatoly Maltsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Maltsev), who proved the first structural theorems. Properties of general solvmanifolds are similar, but somewhat more complicated.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A solvable Lie group is trivially a solvmanifold.
- Every [nilpotent group](wikipedia://en/144569) is solvable, therefore, every [nilmanifold](wikipedia://en/3668693) is a solvmanifold. This class of examples includes *n*-dimensional [tori](wikipedia://en/74800) and the quotient of the 3-dimensional real [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518) by its integral Heisenberg subgroup.
- The [Möbius band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_band) and the [Klein bottle](wikipedia://en/17412) are solvmanifolds that are not nilmanifolds.
- The [mapping torus](wikipedia://en/21034384) of an [Anosov diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/2320078) of the *n*-torus is a solvmanifold. For $n=2$, these manifolds belong to **Sol**, one of the eight [Thurston geometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurston_geometry).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A solvmanifold is diffeomorphic to the total space of a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over some compact solvmanifold. This statement was conjectured by [George Mostow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mostow) and proved by [Louis Auslander](wikipedia://en/22378651) and Richard Tolimieri.
- The [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of an arbitrary solvmanifold is [polycyclic](wikipedia://en/4000598).
- A compact solvmanifold is determined up to diffeomorphism by its fundamental group.
- Fundamental groups of compact solvmanifolds may be characterized as [group extensions](wikipedia://en/345807) of [free abelian groups](wikipedia://en/247296) of finite rank by finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent groups.
- Every solvmanifold is [aspherical](wikipedia://en/7147287). Among all compact homogeneous spaces, solvmanifolds may be characterized by the properties of being aspherical and having a solvable fundamental group.

:::

## Completeness {#Completeness}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a real [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944). It is called a **complete Lie algebra** if each map

$$\operatorname{ad}(X)\colon \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}, X \in \mathfrak{g}$$

in its [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_algebra) is hyperbolic, i.e., it has only real [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue). Let *G* be a solvable Lie group whose Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is complete. Then for any closed subgroup $\Gamma$ of *G*, the solvmanifold $G/\Gamma$ is a **complete solvmanifold**.
