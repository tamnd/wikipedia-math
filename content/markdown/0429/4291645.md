# Emanuel Parzen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Emanuel Parzen"}

**Emanuel Parzen** (April 21, 1929 – February 6, 2016) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). He worked and published on [signal detection theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_detection_theory) and [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) analysis, where he pioneered the use of [kernel density estimation](wikipedia://en/2090057) (also known as the [Parzen window](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parzen_window)[^o3] in his honor). Parzen was the recipient of the 1994 [Samuel S. Wilks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Stanley_Wilks) Memorial Medal of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Parzen attended [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science). He then matriculated to [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he earned his undergraduate degree in mathematics in 1949. From there, he went on to [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), earning his master and doctorate degrees in mathematics in 1951 and 1953, respectively. His dissertation, "On Uniform Convergence of Families of Sequences of Random Variables", was written under [Michel Loève](wikipedia://en/1044534).

Parzen went directly into academia after graduate school, first serving as a research scientist in the physics department and assistant professor of mathematical statistics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). He left there in 1956 for [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), where he stayed for the next 14 years. During this time, he wrote what has become one of the classical texts in probability theory.[^o6] In 1970, he accepted the chair of the statistics department at [SUNY Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo,_The_State_University_of_New_York) and in 1978 moved to his last post as a Distinguished Professor at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University).[^o4]

Parzen died in Boca Raton, Florida on February 6, 2016.[^o7] His son, Michael Parzen, is a Senior Lecturer of Statistics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).
