# Link (simplicial complex)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Link"}

*Image: Tetrahedron.svg.* The [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) is a 2-complex.

The **link** in a simplicial complex is a generalization of the [neighborhood](wikipedia://en/7309251) of a vertex in a graph. The link of a vertex encodes information about the local structure of the complex at the vertex.

:::

## Link of a vertex {#Link_of_a_vertex}

Given an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) $X$ and $v$ a vertex in $V(X)$, its **link** $\operatorname{Lk}(v,X)$ is a set containing every face $\tau \in X$ such that $v\not\in \tau$ and $\tau\cup \{v\}$ is a face of $X$.

- In the special case in which $X$ is a 1-dimensional complex (that is: a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806)), $\operatorname{Lk}(v,X)$ contains all vertices $u\neq v$ such that $\{u,v\}$ is an edge in the graph; that is, $\operatorname{Lk}(v, X)=\mathcal{N}(v)=$the [neighborhood system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_system) of $v$ in the graph.

Given a [geometric simplicial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_simplicial_complex) $X$ and $v\in V(X)$, its **link** $\operatorname{Lk}(v,X)$ is a set containing every face $\tau \in X$ such that $v\not\in \tau$ and there is a simplex in $X$ that has $v$ as a vertex and $\tau$ as a face.[^o3]p. 3 Equivalently, the [join](wikipedia://en/3127114) $v \star \tau$ is a face in $X$.[^o4]p. 20

- As an example, suppose v is the top vertex of the tetrahedron at the left. Then the link of *v* is the triangle at the base of the tetrahedron. This is because, for each edge of that triangle, the join of v with the edge is a triangle (one of the three triangles at the sides of the tetrahedron); and the join of *v* with the triangle itself is the entire tetrahedron. *Image: Graphe complet K3.png.* The link of a vertex of a tetrahedron is the triangle.

An alternative definition is: the **link** of a vertex $v\in V(X)$ is the [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $\operatorname{Lk}(v, X)$ constructed as follows. The vertices of $\operatorname{Lk}(v, X)$ are the edges of $X$ incident to $v$. Two such edges are [adjacent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjacent_%28graph_theory%29) in $\operatorname{Lk}(v, X)$ [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) they are [incident](wikipedia://en/387224) to a common 2-cell at $v$.

- The graph $\operatorname{Lk}(v, X)$ is often given the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of a [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) of small radius centred at $v$; it is an analog to a sphere centered at a point.[^o5]

## Link of a face {#Link_of_a_face}

The definition of a link can be extended from a single vertex to any face.

Given an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) $X$ and any face $\sigma$ of $X$, its **link** $\operatorname{Lk}(\sigma,X)$ is a set containing every face $\tau \in X$ such that $\sigma, \tau$ are disjoint and $\tau\cup \sigma$ is a face of $X$: $\operatorname{Lk}(\sigma,X) := \{\tau\in X: ~\tau\cap \sigma = \emptyset,~ \tau\cup \sigma \in X\}$.

Given a [geometric simplicial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_simplicial_complex) $X$ and any face $\sigma \in X$, its **link** $\operatorname{Lk}(\sigma,X)$ is a set containing every face $\tau \in X$ such that $\sigma, \tau$ are disjoint and there is a simplex in $X$ that has both $\sigma$ and $\tau$ as faces.[^o3]p. 3

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The link of a vertex of a tetrahedron is a triangle – the three vertices of the link corresponds to the three edges incident to the vertex, and the three edges of the link correspond to the faces incident to the vertex. In this example, the link can be visualized by cutting off the vertex with a plane; formally, intersecting the tetrahedron with a plane near the vertex – the resulting cross-section is the link.

## Properties {#Properties}

- For any simplicial complex $X$, every link $\operatorname{Lk}(\sigma,X)$ is downward-closed, and therefore it is a simplicial complex too; it is a sub-complex of $X$.
- Because $X$ is simplicial, there is a [set isomorphism](wikipedia://en/3942) between $\operatorname{Lk}(\sigma,X)$ and the set $X_{\sigma} := \{\rho \in X\text{ such that }\sigma \subseteq \rho\}$: every $\tau\in \operatorname{Lk}(\sigma,X)$ corresponds to $\tau \cup \sigma$, which is in $X_{\sigma}$.

:::

## Link and star {#Link_and_star}

A concept closely related to the link is the **star**.

Given an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) $X$ and any face $\sigma \in X$,$V(X)$, its **star** $\operatorname{St}(\sigma,X)$ is a set containing every face $\tau \in X$ such that $\tau\cup \sigma$ is a face of $X$. In the special case in which $X$ is a 1-dimensional complex (that is: a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806)), $\operatorname{St}(v,X)$ contains all edges $\{u,v\}$ for all vertices $u$ that are neighbors of $v$. That is, it is a [graph-theoretic star](wikipedia://en/19310129) centered at *$u$*.

Given a [geometric simplicial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_simplicial_complex) $X$ and any face $\sigma \in X$, its **star** $\operatorname{St}(\sigma,X)$ is a set containing every face $\tau \in X$ such that there is a simplex in $X$ having both $\sigma$ and $\tau$ as faces: $\operatorname{St}(\sigma,X) := \{\tau\in X: \exists \rho\in X: \tau, \sigma \text{ are faces of }\rho \}$. In other words, it is the closure of the set $\{\rho\in X: \sigma \text{ is a face of }\rho \}$—the set of simplices having $\sigma$ as a face.

So the link is a subset of the star. The star and link are related as follows:

- For any $\sigma\in X$, $\operatorname{Lk}(\sigma,X) = \{\tau\in \operatorname{St}(\sigma,X): \tau\cap \sigma=\emptyset \}$.[^o3]p. 3
- For any $v\in V(X)$, $\operatorname{St}(v,X) = v \star \operatorname{Lk}(v,X)$, that is, the star of $v$ is the [cone](wikipedia://en/782162) of its link at $v$.[^o4]p. 20
