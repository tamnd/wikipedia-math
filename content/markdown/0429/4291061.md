# Mario Szegedy

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mario Szegedy"}

**Mario Szegedy** (born October 23, 1960) is a Hungarian and American [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University). He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in computer science in 1989 from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) after completing his dissertation titled *Algebraic Methods in Lower Bounds for Computational Models*. He held a [Lady Davis Postdoctoral Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Davis_Fellowship) at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) (1989–90), a postdoc at the University of Chicago, 1991–92, and a postdoc at [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) (1992).

:::

Szegedy's research areas include [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220), [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), and [computational theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_theory).[^o4]

He was awarded the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) twice, in 2001 and 2005, for his work on [probabilistically checkable proofs](wikipedia://en/504509) and on the [space complexity](wikipedia://en/338946) of approximating the frequency moments in streamed data.[^o5] His work on streaming algorithms and the resulting data analysis was also recognized by the 2019 [Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Theory_and_Practice_Award).[^o6] With computer scientists [Uriel Feige](wikipedia://en/24734804), [Shafi Goldwasser](wikipedia://en/842178), [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938), and [Shmuel Safra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Safra), Szegedy won the Test of Time Award at the 2021 IEEE Foundations of Computer Science Conference for their work titled *Approximating Clique is Almost NP-Complete.*

He is married and has two daughters.
