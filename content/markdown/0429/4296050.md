::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Infinite dihedral group"}

*Image: Isogonal apeirogon linear.png.* In one dimension, the *infinite dihedral group* is seen in the symmetry of an [apeirogon](wikipedia://en/5658261) alternating two edge lengths, containing reflection points at the center of each edge.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **infinite dihedral group** Dih<sub>∞</sub> is an [infinite group](wikipedia://en/4587030) with properties analogous to those of the finite [dihedral groups](wikipedia://en/100267).

:::

In [two-dimensional geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional_geometry), the **infinite dihedral group** represents the [frieze group](wikipedia://en/375503) symmetry, *p*1*m*1, seen as an infinite set of parallel reflections along an axis.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Every dihedral group is generated by a rotation *r* and a reflection s; if the [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789) is a rational multiple of a full rotation, then there is some integer *n* such that *r<sup>n</sup>* is the identity, and we have a finite dihedral group of order 2*n*. If the rotation is *not* a rational multiple of a full rotation, then there is no such *n* and the resulting group has [infinitely](wikipedia://en/20110824) many elements and is called Dih<sub>∞</sub>. It has [presentations](wikipedia://en/99494)

$$\langle r, s \mid s^2 = 1, srs = r^{-1} \rangle \,\!$$

$\langle x, y \mid x^2 = y^2 = 1 \rangle \,\!$[^o6]

and is isomorphic to a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of **Z** and **Z**/2, and to the [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) **Z**/2 \* **Z**/2. It is the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the graph consisting of a path infinite to both sides. Correspondingly, it is the [isometry group](wikipedia://en/1101694) of **Z** (see also [symmetry groups in one dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_groups_in_one_dimension)), the group of permutations *α*: **Z** → **Z** satisfying |*i* − *j*| = |*α*(*i*) − *α*(*j*)|, for all *i*, *j* in **Z**.[^o7]

The infinite dihedral group can also be defined as the [holomorph](wikipedia://en/2841008) of the [infinite cyclic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic_group).

:::

## Aliasing {#Aliasing}

*Image: Aliasing-folding.svg.* When periodically sampling a sinusoidal function at rate $f_{s}$, the abscissa above represents its frequency, and the ordinate represents another sinusoid that could produce the same set of samples. An infinite number of abscissas have the same ordinate (an equivalence class with the [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) $[0, f_{s}/2]$), and they exhibit dihedral symmetry. The many-to-one phenomenon is known as [aliasing](wikipedia://en/151474).

An example of infinite dihedral symmetry is in [aliasing](wikipedia://en/151474) of real-valued signals.

When sampling a function at frequency $f_{s}$ (intervals $1/f_{s}$), the following functions yield identical sets of samples: $\{\sin(2\pi( f + Nf_{s}) t + \varphi), N = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \pm3, ...$}. Thus, the detected value of frequency $f$ is *periodic*, which gives the translation element $r = f_{s}$. The functions and their frequencies are said to be *aliases* of each other. Noting the trigonometric identity:

$$\sin(2\pi (f+Nf_s)t + \varphi) = \begin{cases} +\sin(2\pi (f+Nf_s)t + \varphi), & f+Nf_s \ge 0, \\[4pt] -\sin(2\pi |f+Nf_s|t - \varphi), & f+Nf_s < 0, \end{cases}$$

we can write all the alias frequencies as positive values: $|f+Nf_s|$. This gives the reflection ($f$) element, namely $f$ ↦ $-f$. For example, with $f = 0.6f_{s}$ and $N = -1$, $f + Nf_{s} = -0.4f_{s}$ *reflects* to $0.4f_{s}$, resulting in the two left-most black dots in the figure.[^o9] The other two dots correspond to $N = -2$ and $N = 1$. As the figure depicts, there are reflection symmetries, at 0.5$f_{s}$, $f_{s}$, 1.5$f_{s}$, etc. Formally, the quotient under aliasing is the *[orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504)* \[0, 0.5$f_{s}$\], with a **Z**/2 action at the endpoints (the orbifold points), corresponding to reflection.
