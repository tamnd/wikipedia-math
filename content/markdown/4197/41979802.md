# Daniel Edwin Rutherford

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Edwin Rutherford"}

**Daniel Edwin Rutherford** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (4 January 1906, in [Stirling](wikipedia://en/61311) – 9 November 1966, in [St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrews)) was a British mathematician, known for his work on the representation theory of [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Rutherford completed his secondary education at [Perth Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Academy) in 1924 and then, with the aid of a [bursary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursary), he went to the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), where he received his B.Sc. in 1927 and his M.A. in 1928 in mathematics. Upon the advice of [Herbert Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Turnbull), Rutherford did his graduate work at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), where he wrote a *doctorandus* thesis under [Roland Weitzenböck](wikipedia://en/35767598). Rutherford's [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) was published in 1932 as *Modular Invariants* in the Cambridge Tracts. He became an assistant lecturer at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) and then an assistant lecturer at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), where he in 1934 was promoted to "Lecturer in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics" and given the task of building up the department in applied mathematics. At St Andrews, he received in 1949 a D.Sc. and then became in 1952 a reader and in 1964 a professor in the Gregory Chair of Applied Mathematics. His research specialty was algebra and in particular the representation theory of symmetric groups. His most famous book, *Substitutional Analysis* (1948), presents his research on the [Young tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_tableaux) of [Alfred Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Young_%28mathematician%29). Rutherford published some research on numerical methods in fluid dynamics. He was the author of 3 books on pure mathematics and 3 books on applied mathematics and also the coauthor of a textbook on elementary abstract algebra.

In 1934 Rutherford was elected a member of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [Herbert Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Turnbull), Sir [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [Edward Copson](wikipedia://en/8918906) and [Geoffrey Timms](wikipedia://en/59410794).[^o4] He won the Society's [Keith Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Medal) in 1953. In 1966 he (posthumously) won the [Makdougall Brisbane Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makdougall_Brisbane_Prize). He was survived by his widow and two daughters.[^o5]
