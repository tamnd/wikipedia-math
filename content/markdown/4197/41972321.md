# Karen E. Smith

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karen E. Smith"}

**Karen Ellen Smith** (born 1965 in [Red Bank, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bank,_New_Jersey)) is an American mathematician, specializing in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). She completed her bachelor's degree in mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) before earning her PhD in mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1993. Currently she is the Keeler Professor of Mathematics at the University of Michigan. In addition to being a researcher in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra, Smith with others wrote the textbook *[An Invitation to Algebraic Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Invitation_to_Algebraic_Geometry)*.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Smith graduated in 1987 with a bachelor's degree in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where she was influenced in her freshman year by [Charles Fefferman](wikipedia://en/502831). She was a high school mathematics teacher in the academic year 1987/1988. In 1988 she became a graduate student at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), where in 1993 she earned her PhD with thesis *Tight closure of parameter ideals and f-rationality* under the supervision of [Melvin Hochster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Hochster). In the academic year 1993–1994 she was a postdoc at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) working with [Craig Huneke](wikipedia://en/41178067). In 1994 she became a [C.L.E. Moore Instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.L.E._Moore_Instructor) and then an associate professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). Since 1997 she has been a professor at the University of Michigan.

In 1991 she married the Finnish mathematician [Juha Heinonen](wikipedia://en/65791210). He died in 2007.

## Honors {#Honors}

In 2001 Smith won the [Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/37554140) for her development of [tight closure](wikipedia://en/6921893) methods, introduced by Hochster and Huneke, in commutative algebra and her application of these methods in algebraic geometry. The prize committee specifically cited her papers "Tight closure of parameter ideals" (*[Inventiones Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventiones_Mathematicae)* 1994), "F-rational rings have rational singularities" (*American J. Math.* 1997, and, with Gennady Lyubeznik, "Weak and strong F-regularity are equivalent in graded rings" (*American J. Math.*, 1999).

In addition to the Satter Prize, Smith was the recipient of a 1997 Sloan Research Fellowship,[^o5] a Fulbright award, and a University of Michigan Faculty Recognition Award for outstanding contributions as a teacher, scholar and member of the University community.[^o6]

In 1996, Smith was the first recipient of the [Etta Z. Falconer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecture), where she presented the lecture "Calclulus mod p."[^o7]

Smith was selected to give the 2015 Earle Raymond Hedrick Lectures at the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)'s MathFest.[^o8] Smith was chosen to give the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698)-[American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) 2016 [Noether Lecture](wikipedia://en/11566873) at the Joint Mathematics Meetings.[^o9]

In 2015 she was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to commutative algebra and algebraic geometry."[^o10] She was named [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) Clay Senior Scholar for 2012-2013.[^o11] In 2019, she was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o12] The Association for Women in Mathematics has included her in the 2020 class of AWM Fellows for "her tireless support of women in mathematics; throughout her career, she has officially and unofficially mentored numerous female mathematicians at every level from undergraduate to full professor; she continues to be an incredibly strong role model for women everywhere".[^o13] She was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2021.[^o14]
