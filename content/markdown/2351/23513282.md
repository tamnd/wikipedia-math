# Szegő kernel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Szegő kernel"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) study of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), the **Szegő kernel** is an [integral kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_kernel) that gives rise to a [reproducing kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproducing_kernel) on a natural [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110). It is named for its discoverer, the Hungarian mathematician [Gábor Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065).

:::

Let Ω be a bounded domain in **C**<sup>*n*</sup> with *C*<sup>2</sup> boundary, and let *A*(Ω) denote the space of all holomorphic functions in Ω that are continuous on $\overline{\Omega}$. Define the [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780) *H*<sup>2</sup>(∂Ω) to be the closure in *L*<sup>2</sup>(∂Ω) of the restrictions of elements of *A*(Ω) to the boundary. The [Poisson integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_integral) implies that each element *ƒ* of *H*<sup>2</sup>(∂Ω) extends to a holomorphic function *Pƒ* in Ω. Furthermore, for each *z* ∈ Ω, the map

$$f\mapsto Pf(z)$$

defines a [continuous linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_functional) on *H*<sup>2</sup>(∂Ω). By the [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/26452), this linear functional is represented by a kernel *k*<sub>*z*</sub>, which is to say

$$Pf(z) = \int_{\partial\Omega} f(\zeta)\overline{k_z(\zeta)}\,d\sigma(\zeta).$$

The Szegő kernel is defined by

$$S(z,\zeta) = \overline{k_z(\zeta)},\quad z\in\Omega,\zeta\in\partial\Omega.$$

Like its close cousin, the [Bergman kernel](wikipedia://en/3205923), the Szegő kernel is holomorphic in *z*. In fact, if *φ*<sub>*i*</sub> is an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) of *H*<sup>2</sup>(∂Ω) consisting entirely of the restrictions of functions in *A*(Ω), then a [Riesz–Fischer theorem](wikipedia://en/1836188) argument shows that

$$S(z,\zeta) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \phi_i(z)\overline{\phi_i(\zeta)}.$$
