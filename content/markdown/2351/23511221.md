# Christoph Rudolff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christoph Rudolff"}

*Image: First square root.jpg.* [^o2]

**Christoph Rudolff** (born 1499 in [Jawor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawor), [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia), died 1545 in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866)) was a German mathematician, the author of the first German textbook on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923).

:::

From 1517 to 1521, Rudolff was a student of [Henricus Grammateus](wikipedia://en/5787009) (Schreyber from Erfurt) at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) and was the author of a book computing, under the title: *Behend und hübsch Rechnung durch die kunstreichen regeln Algebre so gemeinicklich die Coss genent werden* (Nimble and beautiful calculation via the artful rules of algebra \[which\] are so commonly called "coss").[^o3]

He introduced the radical symbol (√) for the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208). It is believed that this was because it resembled a lowercase "r" (for "radix"),[^o4][^o5] though there is no direct evidence.[^o6] [Florian Cajori](wikipedia://en/542738) only says that a "dot is the embryo of our present symbol for the square root"[^o7] though it is 'possible, perhaps probable' that Rudolff's later symbols are not dots but 'r's.[^o8]

Furthermore, he used the meaningful definition that [*x*<sup>0</sup> = 1](wikipedia://en/99491#Zero_exponent).
