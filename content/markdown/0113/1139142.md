# Bruhat decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bruhat decomposition"}

In mathematics, the **Bruhat decomposition** (introduced by [François Bruhat](wikipedia://en/9169813) for [classical groups](wikipedia://en/10172878) and by [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040) in general) $G=BWB$ of certain [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) $G=BWB$ into cells can be regarded as a general expression of the principle of [Gauss–Jordan elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss–Jordan_elimination), which generically writes a matrix as a product of an upper triangular and lower triangular matrices—but with exceptional cases. It is related to the [Schubert cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_cell) decomposition of [flag varieties](wikipedia://en/385339): see [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) for this.

:::

More generally, any group with a [(*B*, *N*) pair](wikipedia://en/1772133) has a Bruhat decomposition.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

- $G$ is a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386) [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018).
- $B$ is a [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of $G$
- $W$ is a [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) of $G$ corresponding to a maximal torus of $B$.

The **Bruhat decomposition** of $G$ is the decomposition

$$G=BWB =\bigsqcup_{w\in W}BwB$$

of $G$ as a disjoint union of [double cosets](wikipedia://en/982771) of $B$ parameterized by the elements of the Weyl group $W$. (Note that although $W$ is not in general a subgroup of $G$, the coset $wB$ is still well defined because the maximal torus is contained in $B$.)

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Let $G$ be the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) **GL**<sub>*n*</sub> of invertible $n \times n$ matrices with entries in some algebraically closed field, which is a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386). Then the Weyl group $W$ is isomorphic to the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_n$ on $n$ letters, with [permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_matrices) as representatives. In this case, we can take $B$ to be the subgroup of upper triangular invertible matrices, so Bruhat decomposition says that one can write any invertible matrix $A$ as a product $U_1PU_2$ where $U_1$ and $U_2$ are upper triangular, and $P$ is a permutation matrix. Writing this as $P=U_{1}^{-1}AU_{2}^{-1}$, this says that any invertible matrix can be transformed into a permutation matrix via a series of row and column operations, where we are only allowed to add row $i$ (resp. column $i$) to row $j$ (resp. column $j$) if $i>j$ (resp. $i<j$). The row operations correspond to $U_{1}^{-1}$, and the column operations correspond to $U_{2}^{-1}$.

The [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) **SL**<sub>*n*</sub> of invertible $n \times n$ matrices with [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) $1$ is a [semisimple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group), and hence reductive. In this case, $W$ is still isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_n$. However, the determinant of a permutation matrix is the sign of the permutation, so to represent an odd permutation in **SL**<sub>*n*</sub>, we can take one of the nonzero elements to be $-1$ instead of $1$. Here $B$ is the subgroup of upper triangular matrices with determinant $1$, so the interpretation of Bruhat decomposition in this case is similar to the case of **GL**<sub>*n*</sub>.

:::

## Geometry {#Geometry}

The cells in the Bruhat decomposition correspond to the [Schubert cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_cell) decomposition of flag varieties. The dimension of the cells corresponds to the [length](wikipedia://en/3021393) of the word $w$ in the Weyl group. [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) constrains the topology of the cell decomposition, and thus the algebra of the Weyl group; for instance, the top dimensional cell is unique (it represents the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440)), and corresponds to the [longest element of a Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/5355571).

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computations"}

## Computations {#Computations}

The number of cells in a given dimension of the Bruhat decomposition are the coefficients of the $q$-polynomial[^o9] of the associated [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109).

:::

## Double Bruhat cells {#Double_Bruhat_cells}

With two opposite Borel subgroups, one may intersect the Bruhat cells for each of them, giving a further decomposition

$$G=\bigsqcup_{w_1 , w_2\in W} ( Bw_1 B \cap B_- w_2 B_- ).$$
