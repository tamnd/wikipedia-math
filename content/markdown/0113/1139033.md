# Pyotr Novikov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pyotr Novikov"}

Sergeyevich **Pyotr Sergeyevich Novikov** (Пётр Серге́евич Но́виков; 15 August 1901, [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) – 9 January 1975, Moscow) was a Soviet [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890). His son, [Sergei Novikov](wikipedia://en/628515), was also a mathematician.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Pyotr Sergeyevich Novikov was born on 15 August 1901 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) to Sergei Novikov, a merchant, and Alexandra Novikov.[^o11]

He served in the Red Army during the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) from 1920 to July 1922.[^o11] He studied at [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University) from 1919 to 1920 and again from 1922 until he graduated in 1925.[^o11] He studied under [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228) until he finished his graduate studies in 1929.[^o11]

## Career {#Career}

Novikov worked at the [Moscow D. Mendeleev Institute of Chemical Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_D._Mendeleev_Institute_of_Chemical_Technology) from 1929 until 1934, when he joined the Department of Real Function Theory at the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494). He was awarded his doctorate in 1935 and promoted to full professor in 1939.[^o11] Novikov became head of the Department of Analysis at the [Moscow State Teachers Training Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Teachers_Training_Institute) in 1944.[^o11] In 1957, he became the first head of the Department of Mathematical Logic at the Steklov Institute.[^o11] He jointly held both positions until he retired in 1972 and 1973 respectively.[^o11]

[Sergei Adian](wikipedia://en/5028401) and [Albert Muchnik](wikipedia://en/22647091) were among his students.

## Research {#Research}

Novikov is known for his work on [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) problems in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890): the [word problem for groups](wikipedia://en/33563), and his progress in the [Burnside problem](wikipedia://en/151452).[^o11] In 1955, he proved the [Novikov–Boone theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novikov–Boone_theorem): that there is a finite [presentation of a group](wikipedia://en/99494) $\langle S | R\rangle$ for which there is no algorithm which, given two words *u*, *v*, decides whether *u* and *v* describe the same element in the group.[^o7]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Novikov was elected a corresponding member and then a full member of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1953 and 1960, respectively.

He was awarded the [Lenin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Prize) in 1957 for [proving](wikipedia://en/82285) the [undecidability](wikipedia://en/15631055) of the word problem in groups.[^o9]

He received the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin) in 1961 and again in 1971. He was awarded the [Order of the Red Banner of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Red_Banner_of_Labour). The [State Prize of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Prize_of_the_Russian_Federation) was awarded to Novikov posthumously in 1999.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He was married to mathematician [Lyudmila Keldysh](wikipedia://en/48580898) (1904–1976). Their son [Sergei Novikov](wikipedia://en/628515) (1938–2024) became the first Soviet mathematician to receive the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859).[^o13]

He died on 9 January 1975 in Moscow.[^o11]
