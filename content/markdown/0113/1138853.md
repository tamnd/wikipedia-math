# Abelian integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abelian integral"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **abelian integral**, named after the Norwegian mathematician [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573), is an [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) of the form

:::

$$\int_{z_0}^z R(x,w) \, dx,$$

where $R(x,w)$ is an arbitrary [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210) of the two variables $x$ and $w$, which are related by the equation

$$F(x,w)=0,$$

where $F(x,w)$ is an [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) in $w$,

$$F(x,w)\equiv\varphi_n(x)w^n+\cdots+\varphi_1(x)w +\varphi_0\left(x\right),$$

whose coefficients $\varphi_j(x)$, $j=0,1,\ldots,n$ are [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) of $x$. The value of an abelian integral depends not only on the integration limits, but also on the path along which the integral is taken; it is thus a [multivalued function](wikipedia://en/222367) of $z$.

Abelian integrals are natural generalizations of [elliptic integrals](wikipedia://en/9960), which arise when

$$F(x,w)=w^2-P(x), \,$$

where $P\left(x\right)$ is a polynomial of degree 3 or 4. Another special case of an abelian integral is a [hyperelliptic integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperelliptic_integral), where $P(x)$, in the formula above, is a polynomial of degree greater than 4.

## History {#History}

The theory of abelian integrals originated with a paper by Abel[^o3] published in 1841. This paper was written during his stay in Paris in 1826 and presented to [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842) in October of the same year. This theory, later fully developed by others,[^o4] was one of the crowning achievements of nineteenth century mathematics and has had a major impact on the development of modern mathematics. In more abstract and geometric language, it is contained in the concept of [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152), or more precisely in the way an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) can be mapped into abelian varieties. Abelian integrals were later connected to the prominent mathematician [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s [16th problem](wikipedia://en/381750), and they continue to be considered one of the foremost challenges in contemporary mathematics.

## Modern view {#Modern_view}

In the theory of [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surfaces), an abelian integral is a function related to the [indefinite integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_integral) of a [differential of the first kind](wikipedia://en/1138912). Suppose we are given a Riemann surface $S$ and on it a [differential 1-form](wikipedia://en/221519) $\omega$ that is everywhere [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) on $S$, and fix a point $P_0$ on $S$, from which to integrate. We can regard

$$\int_{P_0}^P \omega$$

as a [multi-valued function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-valued_function) $f\left(P\right)$, or (better) an honest function of the chosen path $C$ drawn on $S$ from $P_0$ to $P$. Since $S$ will in general be [multiply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiply_connected), one should specify $C$, but the value will in fact only depend on the [homology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_class) of $C$.

In the case of $S$ a [compact Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface) of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 1, i.e. an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225), such functions are the [elliptic integrals](wikipedia://en/9960).

Such functions were first introduced to study [hyperelliptic integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperelliptic_integral), i.e., for the case where $S$ is a [hyperelliptic curve](wikipedia://en/499361). This is a natural step in the theory of integration to the case of integrals involving [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169) $\sqrt{A}$, where $A$ is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of degree $>4$. The first major insights of the theory were given by Abel; it was later formulated in terms of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) $J\left(S\right)$. Choice of $P_0$ gives rise to a standard [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110)

$$S\to J(S)$$

of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742). It has the defining property that the holomorphic 1-forms on $S\to J(S)$, of which there are *g* independent ones if *g* is the genus of *S*, [pull back](wikipedia://en/494995) to a basis for the differentials of the first kind on *S*.
