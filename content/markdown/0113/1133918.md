# Snub square antiprism

*Image: J85 snub square antiprism.stl.* 3D model of a snub square antiprism

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Snub square antiprism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **snub square antiprism** is the [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) that can be constructed by [snubbing](wikipedia://en/9428917) the [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045). It is one of the elementary Johnson solids that do not arise from "cut and paste" manipulations of the [Platonic](wikipedia://en/23905) and [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/1847) solids. However, it is a relative of the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) that has fourfold symmetry instead of threefold. It has 26 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926): 2 squares and 24 triangles; and two types of edges: triangle-square, triangle-triangle.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The [snub](wikipedia://en/9428917) is the process of constructing polyhedra by cutting loose the edge's faces, twisting them, and then attaching [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) to their edges. As the name suggests, the snub square antiprism is constructed by snubbing the [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045), resulting in twenty-four equilateral triangles and two squares as its faces. The [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) are the convex polyhedra whose faces are regular, and the snub square antiprism is one of them, enumerated as $J_{85}$, the 85th Johnson solid.

Let $k \approx 0.82354$ be the positive root of the [cubic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_polynomial)

$$9x^3+3\sqrt{3}\left(5-\sqrt{2}\right)x^2-3\left(5-2\sqrt{2}\right)x-17\sqrt{3}+7\sqrt{6}.$$

Furthermore, let $h \approx 1.35374$ be defined by

$$h = \frac{\sqrt{2}+8+2\sqrt{3}k-3\left(2+\sqrt{2}\right)k^2}{4\sqrt{3-3k^2}}.$$

Then, [Cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) of a snub square antiprism with edge length 2 are given by the union of the orbits of the points

$$(1,1,h),\,\left(1+\sqrt{3}k,0,h-\sqrt{3-3k^2}\right)$$

under the action of the [group](wikipedia://en/28186) generated by a rotation around the $z$-axis by 90° and by a rotation by 180° around a straight line perpendicular to the $z$-axis and making an angle of 22.5° with the $x$-axis.

## Properties {#Properties}

The snub square antiprism has the [three-dimensional symmetry](wikipedia://en/2922089) of [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) $D_{4d}$ of order 16. It cannot produce convex, regular-faced polyhedra whenever being sliced by a plane, an example of an [elementary polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_polyhedron).

The snub square antiprism has forty edges. Compartmentalized into two types of polygonal faces, there are thirty-two triangle-to-triangle edges and eight triangle-to-square edges. These types form a [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046), a measured angle between two faces. For triangle-to-triangle, there are three different angles: eight form 164.257°, sixteen form 144.144°, and eight form 114.645°; for triangle-to-square, only one angle forms 145.441°. All of these angles are in approximation.

The surface area and volume of a snub square antiprism with edge length $a$ can be calculated as:

$$\begin{aligned} A = \left(2+6\sqrt{3}\right)a^2 &\approx 12.392a^2, \\ V &\approx 3.602 a^3. \end{aligned}$$
