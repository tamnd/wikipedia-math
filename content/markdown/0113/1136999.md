# Noam Elkies

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Noam Elkies"}

**Noam David Elkies** (born August 25, 1966) is a professor of mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). At age 26, he became the youngest professor to receive [tenure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenure) at Harvard. He is also a pianist,[^o4] [chess national master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_title), and [chess composer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_composer).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Elkies was born to an engineer father and a piano teacher mother.[^o6] He attended [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) for three years[^o7] before graduating in 1982 at age 15.[^o8][^o9] A [child prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy), in 1981, at age 14, Elkies was awarded a gold medal at the 22nd [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), receiving a perfect score of 42, one of the [youngest to ever do so](wikipedia://en/15712699). He went on to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), where he won the [Putnam competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_competition) at age 16 and four months, making him one of the youngest [Putnam Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition#Putnam_Fellows) in history.[^o11] Elkies was a Putnam Fellow twice more during his undergraduate years.[^o12] He graduated valedictorian of his class in 1985.[^o13] He then earned his PhD in 1987 under the supervision of [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863) and [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).

From 1987 to 1990, Elkies was a junior fellow of the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows).

## Work in mathematics {#Work_in_mathematics}

In 1987, Elkies proved that an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) over the rational numbers is [supersingular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersingular_prime_%28for_an_elliptic_curve%29) at infinitely many primes. In 1988, he found a counterexample to [Euler's sum of powers conjecture](wikipedia://en/9660) for fourth powers.[^o16] His work on these and other problems won him recognition and a position as an associate professor at Harvard in 1990.[^o8] In 1993, Elkies was made a full, tenured professor at age 26. This made him the youngest full professor in Harvard's history.[^o17][^o18] He and [A. O. L. Atkin](wikipedia://en/3721430) extended [Schoof's algorithm](wikipedia://en/3596006) to create the [Schoof–Elkies–Atkin algorithm](wikipedia://en/3717018).

Elkies also studies the connections between [music and mathematics](wikipedia://en/5643937); he is on the advisory board of the *Journal of Mathematics and Music*.[^o19] He has discovered many new patterns in [Conway's Game of Life](wikipedia://en/37035)[^o20] and has studied the mathematics of [still life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_life_%28cellular_automaton%29) patterns in that cellular automaton rule.[^o21] Elkies is an associate of Harvard's [Lowell House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_House).[^o22]

Elkies is one of the principal investigators of the Simons Collaboration on Arithmetic Geometry, Number Theory, and Computation, a large multi-university collaboration involving [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University), [Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), [Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), Harvard, and [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT).[^o23]

Elkies is the discoverer (or joint-discoverer) of many current and past record-holding elliptic curves, including the curve with the highest-known lower bound (≥28) on its [rank](wikipedia://en/51244926), and the curve with the highest-known exact rank (=20).[^o24][^o25] In August 2024, he posted to a number theory listserv that he and Zev Klagsbrun had found an elliptic curve of rank at least 29 by methods similar to those used to find the rank 28 example.[^o26]

## Music {#Music}

Elkies is a [bass-baritone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass-baritone) and formerly played the piano for the [Harvard Glee Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Glee_Club). Jameson N. Marvin, former director of the Glee Club, compared him to "a [Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bach) or a [Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozart)", citing his "gifted musicality, superior musicianship and sight-reading ability".[^o28] He rings the bells of [Lowell House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_House).[^o29]

## Chess {#Chess}

Elkies is a [composer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_composer) and [solver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_problem) of [chess problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_problem) (winning the 1996 [World Chess Solving Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Solving_Championship)).[^o6] One of his problems appears in the chess trainer [Mark Dvoretsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Dvoretsky)'s book *Dvoretsky's Endgame Manual*.[^o31] Elkies holds the title of [National Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Master) from the [United States Chess Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Chess_Federation), but no longer plays competitively.[^o32]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1994, Elkies was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich).[^o34] In 2004, he received a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award)[^o35] and the [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize).[^o36] In 2017, Elkies was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o37]
