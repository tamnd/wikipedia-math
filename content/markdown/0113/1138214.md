# Poul Heegaard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poul Heegaard"}

**Poul Heegaard** (; November 2, 1871, [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) - February 7, 1948, [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo)) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) active in the field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). His 1898 thesis introduced a concept now called the [Heegaard splitting](wikipedia://en/864418) of a 3-[manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). Heegaard's ideas allowed him to make a careful critique of work of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740). Poincaré had overlooked the possibility of the appearance of [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330) in the [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) groups of a space.

:::

He later co-authored, with [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602), a foundational article on [combinatorial topology](wikipedia://en/363400), in the form of an encyclopedia entry.[^o3]

Heegaard studied mathematics at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) from 1889 to 1893. Following years of travelling, and teaching mathematics, he was appointed professor at University of Copenhagen in 1910. An English translation of his 1898 thesis, which laid a rigorous topological foundation for modern knot theory, may be found at https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/heegaardenglish.pdf. The section on "a visually transparent representation of the complex points of an algebraic surface" is especially important.

Following a dispute with the faculty over, among other things, the hiring of [Harald Bohr](wikipedia://en/638305) as [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the University (which Heegaard opposed);[^o4] Heegaard accepted a professorship at [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) in [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway), where he worked till his retirement in 1941.
