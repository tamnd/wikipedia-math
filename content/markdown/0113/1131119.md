# REDOC

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="REDOC"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **REDOC II** and **REDOC III** are [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594) designed by cryptographer [Michael Wood](wikipedia://en/35412737) for Cryptech Inc and are optimised for use in software. Both REDOC ciphers are patented.[^o2]

:::

REDOC II (Cusick and Wood, 1990) operates on 80-bit blocks with a 160-bit key. The cipher has 10 rounds, and uses key-dependent [S-boxes](wikipedia://en/240991) and *masks* used to select the tables for use in different rounds of the cipher. Cusick found an attack on one round, and Biham and Shamir (1991) used [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536) to attack one round with 2300 encryptions. Biham and Shamir also found a way of recovering three masks for up to four rounds faster than exhaustive search. A prize of US\$5,000 was offered for the best attack on one round of REDOC-II, and \$20,000 for the best practical [known-plaintext attack](wikipedia://en/517019).

REDOC III is a more efficient cipher. It operates on an [80-bit block](wikipedia://en/294065) and accepts a variable-length key of up to 20,480 bits. The algorithm consists only of [XORing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR) key bytes with message bytes, and uses no permutations or substitutions. Ken Shirriff describes a differential attack on REDOC-III requiring 2<sup>20</sup> [chosen plaintexts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosen_plaintext) and 2<sup>30</sup> memory.
