# Complex multiplication of abelian varieties

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex multiplication of abelian varieties"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) *A* defined over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is said to have **CM-type** if it has a large enough [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) in its [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) End(*A*). The terminology here is from [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) theory, which was developed for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) in the nineteenth century. One of the major achievements in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) of the twentieth century was to find the correct formulations of the corresponding theory for abelian varieties of [dimension](wikipedia://en/253251) *d* > 1. The problem is at a deeper level of abstraction, because it is much harder to manipulate [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variable).

:::

The formal definition is that

$$\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{Q}(A)$$

the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of End(*A*) with the [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) field **Q**, should contain a commutative subring of [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_space_dimension) 2*d* over **Q**. When *d* = 1 this can only be a [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781), and one recovers the cases where End(*A*) is an [order](wikipedia://en/1114171) in an [imaginary quadratic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field). For *d* > 1 there are comparable cases for [CM-fields](wikipedia://en/10240442), the complex [quadratic extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension) of [totally real fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field). There are other cases that reflect that *A* may not be a [simple abelian variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_abelian_variety) (it might be a [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of elliptic curves, for example). Another name for abelian varieties of CM-type is abelian varieties with **sufficiently many complex multiplications**.

It is known that if *K* is the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), then any such *A* has a [field of definition](wikipedia://en/9213667) which is in fact a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).[^o2] The possible types of endomorphism ring have been classified, as rings with [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) (the [Rosati involution](wikipedia://en/34645248)), leading to a classification of CM-type abelian varieties. To construct such varieties in the same style as for elliptic curves, starting with a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) Λ in **C**<sup>*d*</sup>, one must take into account the [Riemann relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_relations) of abelian variety theory.

The **CM-type** is a description of the action of a (maximal) commutative subring *L* of End<sub>**Q**</sub>(*A*) on the holomorphic [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) of *A* at the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962). [Spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of a simple kind applies, to show that *L* acts via a basis of [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector); in other words *L* has an action that is via [diagonal matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_matrices) on the holomorphic vector fields on *A*. In the simple case, where *L* is itself a number field rather than a product of some number of fields, the CM-type is then a list of [complex embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_embedding) of *L*. There are 2*d* of those, occurring in [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) pairs; the CM-type is a choice of one out of each pair. It is known that all such possible CM-types can be realised.

Basic results of [Goro Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724) and [Yutaka Taniyama](wikipedia://en/1077843) compute the [Hasse–Weil L-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse–Weil_L-function) of *A*, in terms of the CM-type and a Hecke L-function with [Hecke character](wikipedia://en/3149443), having [infinity-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity-type) derived from it. These generalise the results of [Max Deuring](wikipedia://en/5638258) for the elliptic curve case.
