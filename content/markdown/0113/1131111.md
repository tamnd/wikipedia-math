# Gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron** is one of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) ($J_{72}$). It is also a [canonical polyhedron](wikipedia://en/4709281#Canonical_polyhedron).

:::

*Image: J72 gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron can be constructed similarly as [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486): it is constructed from [parabidiminished rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1133303) by attaching two regular [pentagonal cupolas](wikipedia://en/1131073) onto its [decagonal](wikipedia://en/327393) faces. As a result, these pentagonal cupolas cover its dodecagonal faces, so the resulting polyhedron has 20 [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), 30 [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), and 10 [regular pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon) as its faces. The difference between those two polyhedrons is that one of two pentagonal cupolas from the gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron is rotated through 36°. A [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) polyhedron in which all faces are [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) is called the [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), and the gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron is among them, enumerated as the 72nd Johnson solid $J_{72}$.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Because the two aforementioned polyhedrons have similar construction, they have the same surface area and volume. A gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron with edge length has a surface area by adding all of the area of its faces:

$$\left(30+5\sqrt{3}+3\sqrt{25+10\sqrt{5}}\right)a^2 \approx 59.306a^2.$$

Its volume can be calculated by slicing it into two regular pentagonal cupolas and one parabigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron, and adding their volumes:

$$\frac{60+29\sqrt{5}}{3}a^3 \approx 41.615a^3.$$

The gyrate rhombicosidodecahedron is one of the five Johnson solids that are not known to have [Rupert property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_property), meaning no polyhedron of the same or larger size and the same shape as it is known that can pass through a hole in it. The other Johnson solids that are not known to have such property are [parabigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1177997), [metabigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1178006), [trigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1133367), and [paragyrate diminished rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1178000).

:::

## See also {#See_also}

Alternative Johnson solids, constructed by rotating different cupolae of a rhombicosidodecahedron, are:

- The [parabigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1177997) $J_{73}$ where two opposing cupolae are rotated;
- The [metabigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1178006) $J_{74}$ where two non-opposing cupolae are rotated;
- And the [trigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/1133367) $J_{75}$ where three cupolae are rotated.
