# Beta-model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beta-model"}

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a mathematical discipline, a **β-model** (from the French "bon ordre", well-ordering[^o2]) is a model that is correct about statements of the form "*X* is [well-ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-ordered)". The term was introduced by [Mostowski](wikipedia://en/1029314) (1959)[^o3][^o4] as a strengthening of the notion of ω-model. In contrast to the notation for set-theoretic properties named by [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932), such as [$\xi$-indescribability](wikipedia://en/248097), the letter β here is only denotational.

:::

## In analysis {#In_analysis}

β-models appear in the study of the [reverse mathematics](wikipedia://en/326365) of subsystems of [second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454). In this context, a β-model of a subsystem of second-order arithmetic is a model *M* where for any [Σ<sub>1</sub><sup>1</sup>](wikipedia://en/634233) formula $\phi$ with parameters from *M*, $(\omega,M,+,\times,0,1,<)\vDash\phi$ iff $(\omega,\mathcal P(\omega),+,\times,0,1,<)\vDash\phi$.[^o6]<sup>p. 243</sup> Every β-model of second-order arithmetic is also an ω-model, since working within the model we can prove that \< is a well-ordering, so \< really is a well-ordering of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) of the model.[^o3]

There is an incompleteness theorem for β-models: if T is a recursively axiomatizable theory in the language of second-order arithmetic, analogously to how there is a model of T+"there is no model of T" if there is a model of T, there is a β-model of T+"there are no countable coded β-models of T" if there is a β-model of T. A similar theorem holds for β<sub>n</sub>-models for any natural number $n\geq 1$.[^o7]

Axioms based on β-models provide a natural finer division of the strengths of subsystems of second-order arithmetic, and also provide a way to formulate reflection principles. For example, over $\mathsf{ATR}_0$, $\Pi^1_1\mathsf{-CA}_0$ is equivalent to the statement "for all $X$ \[of second-order sort\], there exists a countable β-model *M* such that $X\in M$.[^o6]<sup>p. 253</sup> (Countable ω-models are represented by their sets of integers, and their satisfaction is formalizable in the language of analysis by an inductive definition.) Also, the theory extending KP with a canonical [axiom schema](wikipedia://en/764613) for a recursively Mahlo universe (often called $KPM$)[^o8] is logically equivalent to the theory Δ${}^{1}_{2}$-CA+BI+(Every true Π${}^{1}_{3}$-formula is satisfied by a β-model of Δ${}^{1}_{2}$-CA).[^o9]

Additionally, $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ proves a connection between β-models and the hyperjump: for all sets $X$ of integers, $X$ has a hyperjump iff there exists a countable β-model $M$ such that $X\in M$.[^o6]<sup>p. 251</sup>

Every β-model of comprehension is elementarily equivalent to an ω-model which is not a β-model.[^o10]

## In set theory {#In_set_theory}

A notion of β-model can be defined for models of second-order set theories (such as Morse-Kelley [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553)) as a model $(M, \mathcal X)$ such that the membership relations of $(M, \mathcal X)$ is well-founded, and for any relation $R\in\mathcal X$, $(M, \mathcal X)\vDash$"$R$ is well-founded" iff $R$ is in fact well-founded. While there is no least transitive model of MK, there is a least β-model of MK.[^o12]<sup>pp. 17,154–156</sup>
