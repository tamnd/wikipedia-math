# Nicos Christofides

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicos Christofides"}

**Nicos Christofides** (born 1942 in Cyprus;[^o2] died 2019)[^o3] was a Cypriot mathematician and professor of financial mathematics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London).

:::

Christofides studied [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at Imperial College London, where he also received his PhD in 1966 (dissertation: *The origin of load losses in induction motors with cast aluminium rotors*). He was briefly with [Associated Electrical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Electrical_Industries) and then again at Imperial College.[^o5]

In 1976, he devised the [Christofides algorithm](wikipedia://en/3589536), an algorithm for finding [approximate solutions](wikipedia://en/563105) to the [travelling salesman problem](wikipedia://en/31248).[^o6] The Christofides algorithm is considered "groundbreaking" and has collected over 2200 citations.[^o7]

In 1982, he rejoined Imperial College London as a professor of [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476). In 1990, he was the co-founder and director of the *Centre for Quantitative Finance* (now the *Institute for Financial Engineering*). Christofides became Professor Emeritus of Quantitative Finance at Imperial College London in 2009. He died in 2019.
