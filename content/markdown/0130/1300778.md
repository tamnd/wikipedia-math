# DeWitt notation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="DeWitt notation"}

Physics often deals with classical models where the dynamical variables are a collection of functions {*φ*<sup>*α*</sup>}<sub>*α*</sub> over a d-dimensional space/spacetime [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* where *α* is the "[flavor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavor_%28particle_physics%29)" index. This involves [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948) over the *φ**s, [functional derivatives](wikipedia://en/301504), [functional integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_integral), etc. From a functional point of view this is equivalent to working with an infinite-dimensional [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) where its points are an assignment of a function for each*****α*, and the procedure is in analogy with [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) where the coordinates for a point*x*of the manifold*M*are*φ*<sup>*α*</sup>(*x*).***

:::

In the **DeWitt notation** (named after [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist) [Bryce DeWitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_DeWitt)), *φ*<sup>*α*</sup>(*x*) is written as *φ*<sup>*i*</sup> where *i* is now understood as an index covering both *α* and *x*.

So, given a smooth functional *A*, *A*<sub>,*i*</sub> stands for the [functional derivative](wikipedia://en/301504)

$$A_{,i}[\varphi] \ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \frac{\delta}{\delta \varphi^\alpha(x)}A[\varphi]$$

as a functional of *φ*. In other words, a "[1-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-form)" field over the infinite dimensional "functional manifold".

In integrals, the [Einstein summation convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_summation_convention) is used. Alternatively,

$$A^i B_i \ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \int_M \sum_\alpha A^\alpha(x) B_\alpha(x) d^dx$$
