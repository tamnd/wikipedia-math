# Canon Sinuum (Bürgi)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Canon Sinuum"}

The ***Canon Sinuum*** was a historic table of [sines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) thought to have given the sines to 8 sexagesimal places between 0 and 90 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294) in steps of 2 [arc seconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_seconds). Some authors believe that the table was only between 0 and 45 degrees. It was created by [Jost Bürgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jost_Bürgi) at the end of the [16th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_century). Such tables were essential for [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation) at sea. [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) called the *Canon Sinuum* the most precise known table of sines.

:::

This table is thought to be lost.

The *Canon Sinuum* was computed by Bürgi's [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) explained in his work [Fundamentum Astronomiae](wikipedia://en/49672578) presented to Emperor Rudolf II. in 1592.[^o2][^o3] These algorithms made use of differences and were one of the early uses of [difference calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_calculus).[^o4] The largest trigonometrical table actually contained in the Fundamentum Astronomiae is a table giving the sines for every minute of the quadrant and to 5 to 7 sexagesimal places.

The manuscript of Fundamentum Astronomiae is now in the collection of the Biblioteka Uniwersytecka in [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), Poland.
