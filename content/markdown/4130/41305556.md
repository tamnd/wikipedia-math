# Iris Runge

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Iris Runge"}

**Iris Anna Runge** (1 June 1888 – 27 January 1966) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Iris Runge was the eldest of six children of mathematician [Carl Runge](wikipedia://en/747636). She started studying [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography) at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1907, with the aim of becoming a teacher. At that time, she only attended the lectures, since women were not allowed to formally study at [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) universities until 1908–1909. She attended lectures given by her father and spent a semester at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich) working with [Arnold Sommerfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Sommerfeld), which led to her first publication, *Anwendungen der Vektorrechnung auf die Grundlagen der Geometrischen Optik* ("Applications of [vector](wikipedia://en/32533) calculations to the fundamentals of [geometric optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_optics)") in *[Annalen der Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annalen_der_Physik)* ("Annals of Physics"). After passing her state exams (higher teachers' exam) in 1912, she taught at several schools (Lyzeum Göttingen, Oberlyzeum Kippenberg near [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen)). She went back to the university in 1918 to study [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry). She took the supplementary examination for teachers in 1920. In 1920, she worked as a teacher at [Schule Schloss Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schule_Schloss_Salem). She received her doctorate in 1921 under the supervision of [Gustav Tammann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Heinrich_Johann_Apollon_Tammann), with a dissertation titled *Über Diffusion im festen Zustande* ("On diffusion in the solid state"). As a student, she was a personal assistant to [Leonard Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Nelson). During the political upheaval in Germany after the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) she was active in the election campaign of the *[Sozialdemokratische Partei Deutschlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sozialdemokratische_Partei_Deutschlands)* (Social Democratic Party of Germany, SPD), which at that time implemented [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) in Germany. She joined the party in 1929.

In 1923, she gave up teaching and worked at [Osram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osram) as an [industrial mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_mathematician). [Ellen Lax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Lax), who obtained her doctorate in 1919 under [Walther Nernst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Nernst), was Runge's colleague there. There, in accordance with the company's products (light bulbs and [radio tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_tube)), she worked on [heat conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_conduction) problems, electron emission in tubes, and statistics for quality control in mass production. On the last topic Runge co-authored a then-standard textbook. In 1929, she was promoted to a senior company official. From 1929 she was in the radio tubes department, and after the department was acquired by [Telefunken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telefunken) in 1939, she moved to work in the new company until the dissolution of the laboratory in 1945.

After 1945, she taught at the adult education center in [Spandau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spandau) and was a research assistant at [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin). In 1947, she [qualified as a professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). Her inaugural lecture was titled *Über das Rauschen von Elektronenröhren* ("On the noise in [electron tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_tube)"); her published works were accepted in lieu of a habilitation thesis. In 1947, she was offered a teaching position there, and she worked until 1949 as an assistant to [Friedrich Möglich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Möglich), the chair of the theoretical physics division at Humboldt University. In November 1949 she was appointed as a lecturer, and in July 1950 she became a professor with a teaching assignment. She was one of three women professors in the Faculty of Mathematics and Natural Sciences, the other two being [Elisabeth Schiemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Schiemann) and [Katharina Boll-Dornberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharina_Boll-Dornberger). From March 1949, she also worked part-time again for Telefunken. In 1952, she became an [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor) at Humboldt University, where she gave lectures on theoretical physics until the summer semester of 1952. She lived in [West Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Berlin) until 1965, and then moved to live with her brother in [Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm).

She translated the book *[What Is Mathematics?](wikipedia://en/8997784)* by [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238) (who was married to one of her sisters) and [Herbert Robbins](wikipedia://en/745193) into [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), and wrote a biography of her father, *Carl Runge und sein wissenschaftliches Werk* ("Carl Runge and his scientific works").
