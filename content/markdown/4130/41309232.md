# Isador Lubin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Isador Lubin"}

**Isador Lubin** (9 June 1896 – 6 July 1978) was the head of the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bureau_of_Labor_Statistics) from 1933 to 1946, and president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1946.

:::

## Career {#Career}

*Image: 1,500,000 drop in employment, Senate Committee told by government labor statistics chief. Washington, D.C., Jan. 4. Armed with many charts and his head full of figures, Isador Lubin, Chief LCCN2016872803.jpg.* Isador Lubin in 1937

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), at the [U.S. Food Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Food_Administration), Lubin analyzed labor and price policy related to food production for the Allied Nations. Later at the [War Industries Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Industries_Board)’s Price Section, he studied the effect of price shifts on the output of the petroleum and rubber industries.[^o14][^o4]

He was as an instructor at the Institute of Economics and earned a Ph.D. there in 1926. It became part of the [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution) in 1927. Lubin's book *Miners' Wages and the Cost of Coal* was accepted as a dissertation.[^o14]

"In 1932, as adviser to Senator [Robert M. La Follette Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette_Jr.), he pioneered the notion of government responsibility for the [national accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_accounts)."[^o14]

Lubin was appointed head of the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) by [Frances Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Perkins) in July 1933 and stayed in the position until January 1946.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11] For much of this time, Lubin had an office in the White House's [West Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Wing) "and served as special statistical adviser to President [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt)."[^o14] Lubin was sometimes described as a member of President Roosevelt's "[brain trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_Trust)."[^o12] In 1944 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o13]

In 1941 Lubin authorized BLS to start a research group at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) directed by [Wassily Leontief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassily_Leontief) which constructed the first official [table of U.S. industry inputs and outputs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input-output_model)." In 1945, Roosevelt appointed Lubin as Minister to the Allied Reparations Commission.[^o14]

"In his presidential address to the American Statistical Society in January \[1947\], Lubin emphasized \[the role\] of statistics in modern economic society and the value to the free world of pertinent data."[^o14]

Lubin was appointed the Industrial Commissioner of [New York state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_state) from 1955 to 1958 by Governor [W. Averell Harriman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Averell_Harriman).[^o15]

## Personal and legacy {#Personal_and_legacy}

In 1952 Lubin married the former Carol Riegelman (1909–2005), a longtime consultant to the UN/[ILO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILO).[^o17] He had two daughters by a previous marriage to Ann Shumaker Lubin, the editor of "Progressive Education" and co-author of the book, "The Child-Centered School" (1928):[^o18] Alice Lubin Everitt and [Ann Lubin Buttenwieser](https://bulletin.swarthmore.edu/bulletin-issue-archive/archive_p=277.html).[^o19]

Fellowships named for Dr. Lubin were established at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) and [The New School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_School).[^o20][^o21][^o22]
