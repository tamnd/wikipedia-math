# Poincaré–Birkhoff theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poincaré–Birkhoff theorem"}

In [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology) and [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), **Poincaré–Birkhoff theorem** (also known as **Poincaré–Birkhoff fixed point theorem** and **Poincaré's last geometric theorem**) states that every area-preserving, orientation-preserving [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) of an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158) that rotates the two boundaries in opposite directions has at least two [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738).

:::

## History {#History}

The Poincaré–Birkhoff theorem was discovered by [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740), who published it in a 1912 paper titled "Sur un théorème de géométrie", and proved it for some special cases. The general case was proved by [George D. Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_D._Birkhoff) in his 1913 paper titled "Proof of Poincaré's geometric theorem".[^o3][^o4]
