# Generalized space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized space"}

In mathematics, a **generalized space** is a generalization of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). Impetuses for such a generalization comes at least in two forms:

:::

1. A desire to apply concepts like [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) for objects that are not traditionally viewed as spaces. For example, a [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464) was originally introduced for this reason.
1. A practical need to remedy the deficiencies that some naturally occurring categories of spaces (e.g., ones in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180)) tend not to be [abelian](wikipedia://en/45063), a standard requirement to do homological algebra.

[Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042)'s dictum says a topos is a generalized space; precisely, he and his followers write in exposé 4 of SGA I:[^o2] On peut donc dire que la notion de topos, dérivé naturel du point de vue faisceautique en Topologie, constitue à son tour un élargissement substantiel de la notion d'espace topologique, un grand nombre de situations qui autrefois n'étaient pas considérées comme relevant de intuition topologique

However, [William Lawvere](wikipedia://en/872760) argues in his 1975 paper[^n1] that this dictum should be turned backward; namely, "a topos is the 'algebra of continuous (set-valued) functions' on a generalized space, not the generalized space itself."

A generalized space should not be confused with a geometric object that can substitute the role of spaces. For example, a [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) is typically *not* viewed as a space but as a geometric object with a richer structure.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A [locale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locale_%28mathematics%29) is a sort of a space but perhaps not with enough points.[^o6] The topos theory is sometimes said to be the theory of generalized locales.[^n2]
- [Jean Giraud](wikipedia://en/572753)'s [gros topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gros_topos), [Peter Johnstone](wikipedia://en/13469869)'s **topological topos**,[^o8] or more recent incarnations such as [condensed sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_set) or [pyknotic sets](wikipedia://en/77327179). These attempt to embed the category of (certain) topological spaces into a larger category of generalized spaces, in a way philosophically if not technically similar to the way one generalizes a function to a [generalized function](wikipedia://en/1580246). (Note these constructions are more precise than various [completions](wikipedia://en/77367858) of the category of topological spaces.)

:::
