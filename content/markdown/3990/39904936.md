# Lie group action

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lie group action"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Lie group action** is a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) adapted to the smooth setting: $G$ is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), $M$ is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold), and the action map is [differentiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_map).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\sigma: G \times M \to M, (g, x) \mapsto g \cdot x$ be a (left) group action of a Lie group $G$ on a smooth manifold $M$; it is called a **Lie group action** (or smooth action) if the map $\sigma$ is differentiable. Equivalently, a Lie group action of $G$ on $M$ consists of a [Lie group homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group_homomorphism) $G \to \mathrm{Diff}(M)$. A smooth manifold endowed with a Lie group action is also called a ***$G$*-manifold**.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

The fact that the action map $\sigma$ is smooth has a couple of immediate consequences:

- the [stabilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) $G_x \subseteq G$ of the group action are closed, thus are [Lie subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_subgroup) of *$G$*
- the [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) $G \cdot x \subseteq M$ of the group action are [immersed submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersed_submanifold).

Forgetting the smooth structure, a Lie group action is a particular case of a [continuous group action](wikipedia://en/7522685).

:::

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For every Lie group $G$, the following are Lie group actions:

- the trivial action of $G$ on any manifold;
- the action of $G$ on itself by left multiplication, right multiplication or [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_action%29);
- the action of any Lie subgroup $H \subseteq G$ on $G$ by left multiplication, right multiplication or conjugation;
- the [adjoint action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_action) of $G$ on its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$.

Other examples of Lie group actions include:

- the action of $\mathbb{R}$ on $M$ given by the flow of any [complete vector field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_vector_field);
- the actions of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$ and of its Lie subgroups $G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ by matrix multiplication;
- more generally, any [Lie group representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group_representation) on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370);
- any [Hamiltonian group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_group_action) on a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356);
- the transitive action underlying any [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325);
- more generally, the group action underlying any [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950).

:::

## Infinitesimal Lie algebra action {#Infinitesimal_Lie_algebra_action}

Following the spirit of the [Lie group-Lie algebra correspondence](wikipedia://en/43302095), Lie group actions can also be studied from the infinitesimal point of view. Indeed, any Lie group action $\sigma: G \times M \to M$ induces an **infinitesimal Lie algebra action** on $M$, i.e. a Lie algebra homomorphism $\mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{X}(M)$. Intuitively, this is obtained by differentiating at the identity the Lie group homomorphism $G \to \mathrm{Diff}(M)$, and interpreting the set of vector fields $\mathfrak{X}(M)$ as the Lie algebra of the (infinite-dimensional) Lie group $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$.

More precisely, fixing any $x \in M$, the orbit map $\sigma_x : G \to M, g \mapsto g \cdot x$ is differentiable and one can compute its differential at the identity $e \in G$. If $X \in \mathfrak{g}$, then its image under $\mathrm{d}_e\sigma_x\colon \mathfrak{g}\to T_xM$ is a [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) at $x$, and varying $x$ one obtains a vector field on $M$. The minus of this vector field, denoted by $X^\#$, is also called the [fundamental vector field](wikipedia://en/3946232) associated with $X$ (the minus sign ensures that $\mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{X}(M), X \mapsto X^\#$ is a Lie [algebra homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_homomorphism)).

Conversely, by [Lie–Palais theorem](wikipedia://en/37647580), any abstract infinitesimal action of a (finite-dimensional) Lie algebra on a compact manifold can be integrated to a Lie group action.[^o9]

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

An infinitesimal Lie algebra action $\mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{X}(M)$ is injective [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the corresponding global Lie group action is free. This follows from the fact that the kernel of $\mathrm{d}_e\sigma_x\colon \mathfrak{g}\to T_xM$ is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_x \subseteq \mathfrak{g}$ of the stabilizer $G_x \subseteq G$.

On the other hand, $\mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{X}(M)$ in general not surjective. For instance, let $\pi: P \to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle: the image of the infinitesimal action is actually equal to the [vertical subbundle](wikipedia://en/4067918) $T^\pi P \subset TP$.

:::

## Proper actions {#Proper_actions}

An important (and common) class of Lie group actions is that of [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_action) ones. Indeed, such a topological condition implies that

- the stabilizers $G_x \subseteq G$ are [compact](wikipedia://en/6042)
- the orbits $G \cdot x \subseteq M$ are [embedded submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_submanifold)
- the orbit space $M/G$ is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637)

In general, if a Lie group $G$ is compact, any smooth $G$-action is automatically proper. An example of proper action by a not necessarily compact Lie group is given by the action a Lie subgroup $H \subseteq G$ on $G$.

## Structure of the orbit space {#Structure_of_the_orbit_space}

Given a Lie group action of $G$ on $M$, the [orbit space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_space) $M/G$ does not admit in general a manifold structure. However, if the action is [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_action) and proper, then $M/G$ has a unique smooth structure such that the projection $M \to M/G$ is a [submersion](wikipedia://en/356413) (in fact, $M \to M/G$ is a principal *$G$*-bundle).[^o14]

The fact that $M/G$ is Hausdorff depends only on the properness of the action (as discussed above); the rest of the claim requires freeness and is a consequence of the [slice theorem](wikipedia://en/39906398). If the "free action" condition (i.e. "having zero stabilizers") is relaxed to "having finite stabilizers", $M/G$ becomes instead an [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) (or [quotient stack](wikipedia://en/40579087)).

### Equivariant cohomology {#Equivariant_cohomology}

An application of this principle is the [Borel construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_construction) from [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). Assuming that $G$ is compact, let $EG$ denote the [universal bundle](wikipedia://en/2179570), which we can assume to be a manifold since $G$ is compact, and let $G$ act on $EG \times M$ diagonally. The action is free since it is so on the first factor and is proper since $G$ is compact; thus, one can form the quotient manifold $M_G = (EG \times M)/G$ and define the [equivariant cohomology](wikipedia://en/2976342) of *M* as

$$H^*_G(M) = H^*_{\text{dr}}(M_G),$$

where the right-hand side denotes the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) of the manifold $M_G$.
