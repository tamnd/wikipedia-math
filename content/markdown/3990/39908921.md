# Differential graded Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential graded Lie algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **differential graded Lie algebra** (or **dg Lie algebra**, or **dgla**) is a [graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734) with added [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) and [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) structures that are compatible. Such objects have applications in [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory)[^o2] and [rational homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/13998981).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **differential graded Lie algebra** is a graded vector space $L = \bigoplus L_i$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero together with a bilinear map $[\cdot,\cdot]\colon L_i \otimes L_j \to L_{i+j}$ and a differential $d: L_i \to L_{i-1}$ satisfying

$$[x,y] = (-1)^{|x||y|+1}[y,x],$$

the graded [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370):

$$(-1)^{|x||z|}[x,[y,z]] +(-1)^{|y||x|}[y,[z,x]] +(-1)^{|z||y|}[z,[x,y]] = 0,$$

and the graded [Leibniz rule](wikipedia://en/248988):

$$d [x,y] = [d x,y] + (-1)^{|x|}[x, d y]$$

for any homogeneous elements *x*, *y* and *z* in *L*. Notice here that the differential lowers the degree and so this differential graded Lie algebra is considered to be [homologically](wikipedia://en/142432) graded. If instead the differential raised degree the differential graded Lie algebra is said to be [cohomologically](wikipedia://en/194060) graded (usually to reinforce this point the grading is written in superscript: $L^i$). The choice of cohomological grading usually depends upon personal preference or the situation as they are equivalent: a homologically graded space can be made into a cohomological one via setting $L^i=L_{-i}$.

Alternative equivalent definitions of a differential graded Lie algebra include:

1. a Lie algebra object internal to the [category of chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404#Category_of_chain_complexes);
1. a strict $L_\infty$-algebra.

A [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) of differential graded Lie algebras is a [graded linear map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_linear_map) $f:L\to L^\prime$ that commutes with the bracket and the differential, i.e., $f [x,y]_{L} = [f(x),f(y)]_{L^\prime}$ and $f (d_L x) = d_{L^\prime} f (x)$. Differential graded Lie algebras and their morphisms define a [category](wikipedia://en/53759).

:::

## Products and coproducts {#Products_and_coproducts}

The [product](wikipedia://en/237868) of two differential graded Lie algebras, $L\times L^\prime$, is defined as follows: take the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of the two graded vector spaces $L\oplus L^\prime$, and equip it with the bracket $[(x,x^\prime),(y,y^\prime)]=([x,y],[x^\prime,y^\prime])$ and differential $D(x,x^\prime ) = (dx,d^\prime x^\prime)$.

The [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) of two differential graded Lie algebras, $L*L^\prime$, is often called the free product. It is defined as the free graded Lie algebra on the two underlying [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) with the unique differential extending the two original ones modulo the relations present in either of the two original Lie algebras.

## Connection to deformation theory {#Connection_to_deformation_theory}

The main application is to the [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) over [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) of characteristic zero (in particular over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).) The idea goes back to [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519)'s work on [rational homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/13998981). One way to formulate this thesis (due to [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747), [Boris Feigin](wikipedia://en/53823921), [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509), and others) might be:[^o2]

Any reasonable formal deformation problem in characteristic zero can be described by Maurer–Cartan elements of an appropriate differential graded Lie algebra.

A Maurer-Cartan element is a degree −1 element, $x\in L_{-1}$, that is a solution to the [Maurer–Cartan equation](wikipedia://en/964177#Maurer–Cartan_form_on_a_homogeneous_space):

$$dx +\frac{1}{2}[x,x]=0.$$
