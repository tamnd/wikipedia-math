# Slice theorem (differential geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Slice theorem"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **slice theorem** states:[^o2] given a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) $M$ on which a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) *$G$* [acts](wikipedia://en/39904936) as [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564), for any *$x$* in *$M$*, the map $G/G_x \to M, \, [g] \mapsto g \cdot x$ extends to an invariant neighborhood of $G/G_x$ (viewed as a zero section) in $G \times_{G_x} T_x M / T_x(G \cdot x)$ so that it defines an [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) diffeomorphism from the neighborhood to its image, which contains the orbit of *$x$*.

:::

The important application of the theorem is a proof of the fact that the quotient $M/G$ admits a manifold structure when *$G$* is compact and the action is free.

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), there is an analog of the slice theorem; it is called [Luna's slice theorem](wikipedia://en/37462514).

## Idea of proof when *G* is compact {#Idea_of_proof_when_G_is_compact}

Since *$G$* is compact, there exists an invariant metric; i.e., *$G$* acts as [isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometries). One then adapts the usual proof of the existence of a tubular neighborhood using this metric.
