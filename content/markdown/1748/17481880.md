# Jean-Marie Duhamel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Marie Duhamel"}

**Jean-Marie Constant Duhamel** (;[^o3] ; 5 February 1797 – 29 April 1872) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist).

:::

His studies were affected by the troubles of the [Napoleonic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_era). He went on to form his own school *École Sainte-Barbe*. [Duhamel's principle](wikipedia://en/4474775), a method of obtaining solutions to inhomogeneous linear evolution equations, is named after him. He was primarily a mathematician but did studies on the mathematics of [heat](wikipedia://en/19593167), [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics), and [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics).[^o4] He also did work in calculus using infinitesimals. Duhamel's theorem for infinitesimals says that the sum of a series of infinitesimals is unchanged by replacing the infinitesimal with its principal part.[^o5]

In 1853 he published about an early recording device he called a [vibroscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibroscope). Like other similar devices, the vibroscope was a type of measuring device similar to an [oscilloscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscilloscope), and could not play back the etchings it recorded.[^o6]
