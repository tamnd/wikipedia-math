# David Donoho

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Donoho"}

**David Leigh Donoho** (born March 5, 1957) is an American statistician. He is a professor of statistics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), where he is also the Anne T. and [Robert M. Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bass_%28businessman%29) Professor in the Humanities and Sciences.[^o13] His work includes the development of effective methods for the construction of low-dimensional representations for high-dimensional data problems ([multiscale geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/22223374)), development of [wavelets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelets) for denoising and [compressed sensing](wikipedia://en/11403316). He was elected a Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 2019.

:::

## Academic biography {#Academic_biography}

Donoho did his undergraduate studies at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), graduating in 1978.[^o10] His undergraduate thesis advisor was [John W. Tukey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Tukey).[^o4] Donoho obtained his Ph.D. from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1983, under the supervision of [Peter J. Huber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_J._Huber).[^o5] He was on the faculty of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), from 1984 to 1990 before moving to Stanford.

He has been the Ph.D. advisor of at least 20 doctoral students, including [Jianqing Fan](wikipedia://en/28282149) and [Emmanuel Candès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Candès).[^o5]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1991, Donoho was named a [MacArthur Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program).[^o7] He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1992.[^o8] He was the winner of the [COPSS Presidents' Award](wikipedia://en/11690483) in 1994. In 2001, he won the John von Neumann Prize of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376).[^o9] In 2002, he was appointed to the Bass professorship.[^o10] He was elected a [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) Fellow[^o11] and a foreign associate of the French [Académie des sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_sciences)[^o12] in 2009, and in the same year received an honorary doctorate from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o13] In 2010 he won the [Norbert Wiener Prize in Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener_Prize_in_Applied_Mathematics), given jointly by SIAM and the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14] He is also a member of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o10][^o15] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16] In 2013 he was awarded the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) for Mathematics.[^o17] In 2016, he was awarded an honorary degree at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo).[^o18] In 2018, he was awarded the Gauss Prize from IMU.
