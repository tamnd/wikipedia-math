# Mladen Bestvina

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mladen Bestvina"}

**Mladen Bestvina** (born 1959)[^o3] is a [Croatian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the area of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164). He is a Distinguished Professor in the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Mladen Bestvina is a three-time medalist at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) (two silver medals in 1976 and 1978 and a bronze medal in 1977).[^o5] He received a B. Sc. in 1982 from the [University of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zagreb).[^o6] He obtained a PhD in Mathematics in 1984 at the [University of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee) under the direction of John Walsh.[^o7] He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1987-88 and again in 1990–91.[^o8] Bestvina had been a faculty member at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), and joined the faculty in the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah) in 1993.[^o9] He was appointed a Distinguished Professor at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah) in 2008.[^o9] Bestvina received the [Alfred P. Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) in 1988–89[^o10][^o11] and a [Presidential Young Investigator Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PECASE) in 1988–91.[^o12]

Bestvina gave an [invited address at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing) in 2002,[^o13], was topology section member in Madrid in 2006 [^o14] and gave a plenary lecture at virtual ICM 2022.[^o15] He also gave a Unni Namboodiri Lecture in Geometry and Topology at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o16]

Bestvina served as an Editorial Board member for the [Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)[^o17] and as an associate editor of the [Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785).[^o18] Currently he is an editorial board member for [Duke Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Mathematical_Journal),[^o19] [Geometric and Functional Analysis](wikipedia://en/18820047),[^o20] [Geometry and Topology](wikipedia://en/2862181),[^o21] the *Journal of Topology and Analysis*,[^o22] *Groups, Geometry and Dynamics*,[^o23] *Michigan Mathematical Journal*,[^o24] *Rocky Mountain Journal of Mathematics*,[^o25] and *Glasnik Matematicki*.[^o26]

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o27] Since 2012, he has been a correspondent member of the HAZU (Croatian Academy of Science and Art).[^o3]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

A 1988 monograph of Bestvina[^o29] gave an abstract topological characterization of universal Menger compacta in all dimensions; previously only the cases of dimension 0 and 1 were well understood. John Walsh wrote in a review of Bestvina's monograph: 'This work, which formed the author's Ph.D. thesis at the [University of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee), represents a monumental step forward, having moved the status of the topological structure of higher-dimensional Menger compacta from one of "close to total ignorance" to one of "complete understanding".'[^o30]

In a 1992 paper Bestvina and Feighn obtained a *Combination Theorem* for [word-hyperbolic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group).[^o31] The theorem provides a set of sufficient conditions for [amalgamated free products](wikipedia://en/360036) and [HNN extensions](wikipedia://en/2823319) of word-hyperbolic groups to again be word-hyperbolic. The Bestvina–Feighn Combination Theorem became a standard tool in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and has had many applications and generalizations (e.g.[^o32][^o33][^o34][^o35]).

Bestvina and Feighn also gave the first published treatment of [Rips'](wikipedia://en/673693) theory of stable group actions on [**R**-trees](wikipedia://en/371914) (the [Rips machine](wikipedia://en/31275945))[^o36] In particular their paper gives a proof of the [Morgan–Shalen conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan–Shalen_conjecture)[^o37] that a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) *G* admits a free isometric [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on an [**R**-tree](wikipedia://en/371914) if and only if *G* is a [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of surface groups, [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735) and [free abelian groups](wikipedia://en/247296).

A 1992 paper of Bestvina and [Handel](wikipedia://en/64910566) introduced the notion of a [train track map](wikipedia://en/20304533) for representing elements of [Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)](wikipedia://en/12747184).[^o38] In the same paper they introduced the notion of a *relative train track* and applied train track methods to solve[^o38] the *Scott conjecture*, which says that for every automorphism *α* of a finitely generated [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) *F<sub>n</sub>* the fixed subgroup of *α* is free of [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) at most *n*. Since then train tracks became a standard tool in the study of algebraic, geometric and dynamical properties of automorphisms of free groups and of subgroups of Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*). Examples of applications of train tracks include: a theorem of Brinkmann[^o39] proving that for an automorphism *α* of *F<sub>n</sub>* the mapping torus group of *α* is [word-hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group) if and only if *α* has no periodic conjugacy classes; a theorem of Bridson and Groves[^o40] that for every automorphism *α* of *F<sub>n</sub>* the mapping torus group of *α* satisfies a quadratic [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/20017026); a proof of algorithmic solvability of the [conjugacy problem](wikipedia://en/4942384) for free-by-cyclic groups;[^o41] and others.

Bestvina, Feighn and Handel later proved that the group Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*) satisfies the [Tits alternative](wikipedia://en/12704641),[^o42][^o43] settling a long-standing open problem.

In a 1997 paper[^o44] Bestvina and Brady developed a version of [discrete Morse theory](wikipedia://en/17637383) for cubical complexes and applied it to study homological finiteness properties of subgroups of right-angled [Artin groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_group). In particular, they constructed an example of a group which provides a counter-example to either the [Whitehead asphericity conjecture](wikipedia://en/8213034) or to the [Eilenberg−Ganea conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg−Ganea_conjecture), thus showing that at least one of these conjectures must be false. Brady subsequently used their Morse theory technique to construct the first example of a [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) subgroup of a [word-hyperbolic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group) that is not itself word-hyperbolic.[^o45]
