# Locally closed subset

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally closed subset"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of mathematics, a subset $E$ of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is said to be **locally closed** if any of the following equivalent conditions are satisfied:[^o2][^o3][^o4][^n1]

:::

- $E$ is the intersection of an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) and a closed set in $X.$
- For each point $x\in E,$ there is a neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that $E \cap U$ is closed in $U.$
- $E$ is open in its closure $\overline{E}.$
- The set $\overline{E}\setminus E$ is closed in $X.$
- $E$ is the difference of two closed sets in $X.$
- $E$ is the difference of two open sets in $X.$

The second condition justifies the terminology *locally closed* and is Bourbaki's definition of locally closed.[^o2] To see the second condition implies the third, use the facts that for subsets $A \subseteq B,$ $A$ is closed in $B$ if and only if $A = \overline{A} \cap B$ and that for a subset $E$ and an open subset $U,$ $\overline{E} \cap U = \overline{E \cap U} \cap U.$

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The interval $(0, 1] = (0, 2) \cap [0, 1]$ is a locally closed subset of $\mathbb{R}.$ For another example, consider the relative interior $D$ of a closed disk in $\mathbb{R}^3.$ It is locally closed since it is an intersection of the closed disk and an open ball.

On the other hand, $\{ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 \mid x\ne0 \} \cup \{(0,0)\}$ is *not* a locally closed subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Recall that, by definition, a submanifold $E$ of an $n$-manifold $M$ is a subset such that for each point $x$ in $E,$ there is a chart $\varphi : U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ around it such that $\varphi(E \cap U) = \mathbb{R}^k \cap \varphi(U).$ Hence, a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) is locally closed.[^o8]

Here is an example in algebraic geometry. Let *U* be an open affine chart on a projective variety *X* (in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744)). Then each closed subvariety *Y* of *U* is locally closed in *X*; namely, $Y = U \cap \overline{Y}$ where $\overline{Y}$ denotes the closure of *Y* in *X*. (See also [quasi-projective variety](wikipedia://en/1321785) and [quasi-affine variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-affine_variety).)

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Finite intersections and the pre-image under a continuous map of locally closed sets are locally closed.[^o2] On the other hand, a union and a complement of locally closed subsets need not be locally closed.[^o11] (This motivates the notion of a [constructible set](wikipedia://en/23234121).)

Especially in [stratification theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratification_theory), for a locally closed subset $E,$ the complement $\overline{E} \setminus E$ is called the **boundary** of $E$ (not to be confused with [topological boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_boundary)).[^o3] If $E$ is a closed submanifold-with-boundary of a manifold $M,$ then the [relative interior](wikipedia://en/8612907) (that is, interior as a manifold) of $E$ is locally closed in $M$ and the boundary of it as a manifold is the same as the boundary of it as a locally closed subset.[^o3]

A topological space is said to be **submaximal** if every subset is locally closed. See [Glossary of topology#S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_topology#S) for more of this notion.

:::
