# Unibranch local ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unibranch local ring"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) *A* is said to be **unibranch** if the [reduced ring](wikipedia://en/539749) *A*<sub>red</sub> (obtained by quotienting *A* by its [nilradical](wikipedia://en/289636)) is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), and the [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) *B* of *A*<sub>red</sub> is also a local ring. A unibranch local ring is said to be **geometrically unibranch** if the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) of *B* is a [purely inseparable extension](wikipedia://en/4770099) of the residue field of *A*<sub>red</sub>. A complex [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *X* is called **topologically unibranch** at a point *x* if for all complements *Y* of closed algebraic subsets of *X* there is a fundamental system of neighborhoods (in the classical topology) of *x* whose intersection with *Y* is connected.

:::

In particular, a [normal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_ring) is unibranch. One result on unibranch points in algebraic geometry is the following:

::: {#o2 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem** Let *X* and *Y* be two integral [locally noetherian schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_noetherian_scheme) and $f \colon X \to Y$ a [proper](wikipedia://en/603782) [dominant morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_morphism). Denote their [function fields](wikipedia://en/381301) by *K(X)* and *K(Y)*, respectively. Suppose that the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of *K(Y)* in *K(X)* has [separable degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_degree) *n* and that $y \in Y$ is unibranch. Then the fiber $f^{-1}(y)$ has at most *n* connected components. In particular, if *f* is [birational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birational), then the fibers of unibranch points are connected.

In [EGA](wikipedia://en/471387), the theorem is obtained as a corollary of [Zariski's main theorem](wikipedia://en/13969950).

:::
