# Scientific calculator

*Image: Casio fx-991DE X.JPG.* Casio fx-991DE X - a modern digital calculator from Casio with a [dot matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot_matrix) "Natural Textbook" [LCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCD)

*Image: FX-77.JPG.* Casio fx-77, a [solar-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar-powered_calculator) digital calculator from the 1980s using a single-line [LCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCD)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scientific calculator"}

A **scientific calculator** is an [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) [calculator](wikipedia://en/7593), either desktop or handheld, designed to perform calculations using basic ([addition](wikipedia://en/61338), [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845), [division](wikipedia://en/53696)) and advanced ([trigonometric](wikipedia://en/30367), [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/56567), etc.) mathematical [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245) and [functions](wikipedia://en/185427). They have completely replaced [slide rules](wikipedia://en/28743) as well as books of [mathematical tables](wikipedia://en/239112) and are used in both educational and professional settings.

:::

In some areas of study and professions scientific calculators have been replaced by [graphing calculators](wikipedia://en/335004) and [financial calculators](wikipedia://en/28598560) which have the capabilities of a scientific calculator along with the capability to graph input data and [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), as well as by [numerical computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_computing), [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805), statistical, and spreadsheet software packages running on [personal computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer). Both desktop and mobile [software calculators](wikipedia://en/17056775) can also emulate many functions of a physical scientific calculator. Standalone scientific calculators remain popular in [secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education) and [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) because computers and smartphones are often prohibited during exams to reduce the likelihood of cheating.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Scientific function"}

## Functions {#Functions}

When [electronic calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_calculators) were originally marketed they normally had only four or five capabilities ([addition](wikipedia://en/61338), [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845), [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_%28military%29) and [square root](wikipedia://en/29208)). Modern scientific calculators generally have many more capabilities than the original four- or five-function calculator, and the capabilities differ between manufacturers and models.

The capabilities of a modern scientific calculator include:

- [Scientific notation](wikipedia://en/59715)
- [Floating-point decimal arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11376)
- [Logarithmic](wikipedia://en/17860) functions, using both [base 10](wikipedia://en/174482) and [base $e$](wikipedia://en/21476)
- [Trigonometric](wikipedia://en/18717261) functions (some including [hyperbolic trigonometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function))
- [Exponential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent) functions and [roots](wikipedia://en/235029) beyond the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208)
- Quick access to [constants](wikipedia://en/48779551) such as [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) and [$e$](wikipedia://en/9633)

In addition, high-end scientific calculators generally include some or all of the following:

- [Cursor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursor_%28user_interface%29) controls to edit [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) and view previous calculations (some calculators such as the *LCD-8310*, [badge engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badge_engineered) under both *[Olympia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Business_Systems)* and *[United Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Office)* keep the number of the previous result on-screen for convenience while the new calculation is being entered.[^o5])
- [Hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263), [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system), and [octal](wikipedia://en/22330) calculations, including basic [Boolean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_logic) mathematics
- [Complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers)
- [Fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_%28mathematics%29) calculations
- [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) calculations
- Programmability — see [Programmable calculator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmable_calculator)
- [Equation solving](wikipedia://en/537048) and [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805)
- [Matrix calculations](wikipedia://en/20556859)
- [Calculus](wikipedia://en/5176)
- [Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_%28alphabet%29) that can be used for spelling words or including [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) into an equation
- [Conversion of units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_of_units)
- [Physical constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_constants)
- [Vector calculations](wikipedia://en/23218956)
- [Random number generation](wikipedia://en/2736939)

While most scientific calculators have traditionally used a single-line display similar to traditional [pocket calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_calculators), many of them have more digits (10 to 12), sometimes with extra digits for the floating-point exponent. A few have multi-line displays, with some models from [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard), [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments) (both US manufacturers), [Casio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casio), [Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation), and [Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_%28company%29) (all three Japanese makers) using [dot matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot_matrix) displays similar to those found on [graphing calculators](wikipedia://en/335004).

## Uses {#Uses}

Scientific calculators are used widely in situations that require quick access to certain mathematical functions, especially those that were once looked up in [mathematical tables](wikipedia://en/239112), such as [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367), [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860), or [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543). They are also used for calculations of very large or very small numbers, as in some aspects of [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry).

They are very often required for math classes from the [junior high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_High_School) level through [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College),[^o8] and are generally either permitted or required on many [standardized tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardized_test) covering math and science subjects;[^o9] as a result, many are sold into educational markets to cover this demand, and some high-end models include features making it easier to translate a problem on a textbook page into calculator input, e.g. by providing a method to enter an entire problem in as it is written on the page using simple formatting tools.

## History {#History}

*Image: HP-35 Red Dot.jpg.* [HP-35](wikipedia://en/449629), the world's first scientific pocket calculator, was introduced in 1972 by Hewlett-Packard. It used [reverse Polish notation](wikipedia://en/26513) and an [LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LED) display.

*Image: Texas Instruments SR-50.jpg.* [TI SR-50](wikipedia://en/918930)

The first scientific calculator that included all of the basic ideas above was the programmable Hewlett-Packard [HP-9100A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard_9100A),[^o11] released in 1968, though the [Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Laboratories) LOCI-2 and the Mathatronics Mathatron[^o12] had some features later identified with scientific calculator designs. The HP-9100 series was built entirely from discrete [transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor) logic with no [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit), and was one of the first uses of the [CORDIC](wikipedia://en/859590) algorithm for trigonometric computation in a personal computing device, as well as the first calculator based on [reverse Polish notation](wikipedia://en/26513) (RPN) entry. HP became closely identified with RPN calculators from then on, and even today some of their high-end calculators (particularly the long-lived [HP-12C](wikipedia://en/952015) financial calculator and the [HP-48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP-48) series of [graphing calculators](wikipedia://en/335004)) still offer RPN as their default input mode due to having garnered a very large following.

The [HP-35](wikipedia://en/449629), introduced on February 1, 1972, was Hewlett-Packard's first [pocket calculator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_calculator) and the world's first handheld scientific calculator.[^o13] Like some of HP's desktop calculators it used RPN. Introduced at US\$395, the HP-35 was available from 1972 to 1975.

Texas Instruments (TI), after the production of several units with [scientific notation](wikipedia://en/59715), introduced a handheld scientific calculator on January 15, 1974, in the form of the [SR-50](wikipedia://en/918930).[^o14] TI's long-running [TI-30](wikipedia://en/811200) series being one of the most widely used scientific calculators in classrooms.

[Casio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casio), [Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Inc.), and [Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation), produced their graphing calculators, with Casio's FX series (beginning with the Casio FX-1 in 1972[^o15]). Casio was the first company to produce a Graphing calculator ([Casio fx-7000G](wikipedia://en/3419531)).
