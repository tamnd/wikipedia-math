# Daniel Quillen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Quillen"}

**Daniel Gray Quillen** (June 22, 1940 – April 30, 2011) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is known for being the "prime architect" of higher [algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/598500), for which he was awarded the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in 1975 and the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978.

:::

From 1984 to 2006, he was the [Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professorships#Waynflete_Professors_of_Pure_Mathematics) at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford).

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Quillen was born in [Orange, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_New_Jersey), and attended [Newark Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Academy). He entered [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he earned both his [AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree), in 1961, and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD), in 1964, degrees; the latter was completed under the supervision of [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457), with a thesis in [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). He was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1959.[^o4]

Quillen obtained a position at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) after completing his doctorate. He also spent a number of years at several other universities. He visited France twice: first as a [Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation) in Paris, during the academic year 1968–69, where he was greatly influenced by [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck), and then, during 1973–74, as a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow). In 1969–70, he was a visiting member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), where he came under the influence of [Sir Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698).

In 1978, Quillen received a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki).[^o5]

From 1984 to 2006, he was the [Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professor_of_Pure_Mathematics) at [Magdalen College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford).

Quillen retired at the end of 2006. He died from complications of [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease) on April 30, 2011, aged 70.[^o6][^o7]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Quillen's best known contribution (mentioned specifically in his Fields medal citation) was his formulation of higher algebraic *K*-theory in 1972. This new tool, formulated in terms of homotopy theory, proved to be successful in formulating and solving problems in algebra, particularly in ring theory and module theory. More generally, Quillen developed tools (especially his theory of [model categories](wikipedia://en/2895304) and in particular the [Kan–Quillen model structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan–Quillen_model_structure)) that allowed algebro-topological tools to be applied in other contexts.

Before his work in defining higher algebraic *K*-theory, Quillen worked on the [Adams conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_conjecture), formulated by [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959), in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831).[^o9] His proof of the conjecture used techniques from the [modular representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_representation) theory of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), which he later applied to work on [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of groups and algebraic *K*-theory. He also worked on [complex cobordism](wikipedia://en/1033666), showing that its [formal group law](wikipedia://en/480335) is essentially the universal one.

In related work, he also supplied a proof of [Serre's conjecture](wikipedia://en/539562) about the triviality of algebraic [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) on [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834), which led to the [Bass–Quillen conjecture](wikipedia://en/51717116). He was also an architect (along with [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608)) of [rational homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/13998981).[^o10]

He introduced the [Quillen determinant line bundle](wikipedia://en/35109122) and the [Mathai–Quillen formalism](wikipedia://en/35126429).
