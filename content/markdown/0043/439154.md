# Modular equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modular equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **modular equation** is an [algebraic equation](wikipedia://en/404001) satisfied by *moduli*, in the sense of [moduli problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_problem). That is, given a number of functions on a [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609), a modular equation is an equation holding between them, or in other words an [identity](wikipedia://en/298428) for moduli.

:::

The most frequent use of the term *modular equation* is in relation to the moduli problem for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). In that case the moduli space itself is of dimension one. That implies that any two [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) *F* and *G*, in the [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) of the modular curve, will satisfy a modular equation *P*(*F*,*G*) = 0 with *P* a non-zero [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of two variables over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). For suitable non-degenerate choice of *F* and *G*, the equation *P*(*X*,*Y*) = 0 will actually define the modular curve.

This can be qualified by saying that *P*, in the worst case, will be of high degree and the plane curve it defines will have [singular points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity); and the [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) of *P* may be very large numbers. Further, the 'cusps' of the moduli problem, which are the points of the modular curve not corresponding to honest elliptic curves but degenerate cases, may be difficult to read off from knowledge of *P*.

In that sense a modular equation becomes the **equation of a modular curve**. Such equations first arose in the theory of multiplication of [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) (geometrically, the *n<sup>2</sup>*-fold [covering map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_map) from a 2-[torus](wikipedia://en/74800) to itself given by the mapping *x* → *n*·*x* on the underlying group) expressed in terms of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).
