# Meissel–Mertens constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Meissel–Mertens constant"}

*Image: Meissel–Mertens constant definition.svg.* In the limit, the sum of the reciprocals of the primes \< *n* and the function ln(ln *n*) are separated by a constant, the Meissel–Mertens constant (labelled M above).

The **Meissel–Mertens constant** (named after [Ernst Meissel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Meissel) and [Franz Mertens](wikipedia://en/41999)), also referred to as the **Mertens constant**, **Kronecker's constant** (after [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450)), **Hadamard–de la Vallée-Poussin constant** (after [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) and [Charles Jean de la Vallée-Poussin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Jean_de_la_Vallée-Poussin)), or the **prime reciprocal constant**, is a [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), defined as the [limiting](wikipedia://en/26551602) difference between the [harmonic series](wikipedia://en/142488) summed only over the [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) and the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of the natural logarithm:

:::

$$M = \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty } \left( \sum_{\scriptstyle p\text{ prime}\atop \scriptstyle p\le n} \frac{1}{p} - \ln(\ln n) \right)=\gamma + \sum_{p} \left[ \ln\! \left( 1 - \frac{1}{p} \right) + \frac{1}{p} \right].$$

Here γ is the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant), which has an analogous definition involving a sum over all integers (not just the primes).

*Image: Primes harmonic.png.* The plot of the prime harmonic sum up to $n=2^{15}, 2^{16}, \ldots, 2^{46} \approx 7.04 \times 10^{13}$ and the Merten's approximation to it. If the n axis were plotted in the linear scale instead of logarithmic, then the figure would be $5.33(3) \times 10^9$ km long —approximately the distance to Neptune.

The value of *M* is approximately

*M* ≈ 0.2614972128476427837554268386086958590516... .

[Mertens' second theorem](wikipedia://en/982970) establishes that the limit exists.

The fact that there are two logarithms (log of a log) in the limit for the Meissel–Mertens constant may be thought of as a consequence of the combination of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) and the limit of the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

The Meissel-Mertens constant was used by [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) when bidding in the [Nortel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nortel) patent auction. Google posted three bids based on mathematical numbers: \$1,902,160,540 ([Brun's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brun's_constant)), \$2,614,972,128 (Meissel–Mertens constant), and \$3.14159 billion ([π](wikipedia://en/23601)).[^o3]
