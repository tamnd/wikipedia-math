# K. G. Ramanathan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K. G. Ramanathan"}

**Kollagunta Gopalaiyer Ramanathan** (13 November 1920 – 10 May 1992) was an Indian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). His contributions are also to the general development of mathematical research,[^o3] and teaching in India.

:::

## K. G. Ramanathan's early life and his family {#K._G._Ramanathan's_early_life_and_his_family}

K. G. Ramanathan was born in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad), South India, on 13 November 1920[^o5] to Sri Kollagunta Gopal Iyer and Smt. Anantalakshmi, and was one of three children in the family.[^o6]

Ramanathan received his early education from Wesleyan Mission High School in [Secunderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunderabad). He further pursued his academic interests in mathematics and obtained a BA degree from Nizam College, Hyderabad, in 1940. Later, in 1942, he completed his master's degree in mathematics from [Loyola College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_College,_Chennai) in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) (then known as Madras). Sources state that Ramanathan's academic journey was also influenced by mathematics teacher and [Jesuit priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits), Rev Fr. C Racine, who was a former student of the renowned French mathematician [E Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274).[^o6]

With a keen passion for teaching and research in mathematics, Ramanathan worked as an assistant lecturer at [Annamalai University in Chidambaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annamalai_University), [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu) from 1945 to 1946. He then joined [Osmania University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmania_University), Hyderabad as a lecturer for the following two years. In 1948, he became a research scholar at the [University of Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Madras), where he had the opportunity to work alongside esteemed mathematicians including Professors R Vaidhyanathaswamy and T Vijayraghavan. In the same year, Ramanathan received an invitation to attend the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). Whilst there, Ramanathan worked alongside [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and [Carl Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Siegel),[^o7] and is said to have sung Carnatic songs of [Tyagaraja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyagaraja) to the legendary physicist [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), who was Ramanathan's neighbour at the university. Until 1951, along with [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351), Siegel supervised Ramanathan in his PhD studies in mathematics.[^o6]

Following the successful completion of his PhD, Ramanathan returned to India to team up with [K. S. Chandrasekharan](wikipedia://en/22907213) at the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research) (TIFR) at [Colaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colaba) in 1951.[^o8]

Ramanathan was married to Jayalakshmi Ramanathan. He had two sons. His mother died at an early age.

## Career {#Career}

At TIFR, he built up the number theory group of young mathematicians from India. For several years, he took interest to study [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan)'s unpublished and published work. He was an editorial board member of [Acta Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_Arithmetica) for over 30 years. He retired from TIFR in 1985.

### Awards {#Awards}

Ramanathan was given numerous achievements during his more than 30 years service at TIFR.

- [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan), 1983[^o11]
- [Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanti_Swarup_Bhatnagar_Award), 1965
- Fellow of [Indian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Academy_of_Sciences)
- Fellow of [Indian National Science Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Science_Academy)
- Honorary fellow of TIFR.

### Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- On [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan)'s continued fraction, KG Ramanathan – Acta Arith, 1984
- Some applications of [Kronecker’s limit formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker’s_limit_formula), KG Ramanathan – J. Indian Math. Soc, 1987
