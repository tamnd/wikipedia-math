# Vysochanskij–Petunin inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vysochanskij–Petunin inequality"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Vysochanskij–[Petunin](wikipedia://en/38565969) inequality** gives a lower bound for the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with finite [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) lies within a certain number of [standard deviations](wikipedia://en/27590) of the variable's [mean](wikipedia://en/9653), or equivalently an upper bound for the probability that it lies further away. The sole restrictions on the [distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) are that it be [unimodal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimodal_function) and have finite [variance](wikipedia://en/32344); here *unimodal* implies that it is a continuous probability distribution except at the [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127), which may have a non-zero probability.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $X$ be a random variable with unimodal distribution and finite variance, and $\alpha\in \mathbb R$. If we define $\rho=\sqrt{\mathbb E[(X-\alpha)^2]}$ then for any $r>0$,

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Pr}(|X-\alpha|\ge r)\le \begin{cases} \frac{4\rho^2}{9r^2}&r\ge \sqrt{8/3}\rho \\ \frac{4\rho^2}{3r^2}-\frac{1}{3}&r\le \sqrt{8/3}\rho. \\ \end{cases} \end{aligned}$$

::: {#o5 .statement cue=named-heading name="Relation to Gauss's inequality"}

### Relation to Gauss's inequality {#Relation_to_Gauss's_inequality}

Taking $\alpha$ equal to a mode of $X$ yields the first case of [Gauss's inequality](wikipedia://en/22777799).

:::

### Tightness of Bound {#Tightness_of_Bound}

[Without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000), assume $\alpha=0$ and $\rho=1$.

- If $r<1$, the left-hand side can equal one, so the bound is useless.
- If $r\ge \sqrt{8/3}$, the bound is tight when $X=0$ with probability $1-\frac{4}{3r^2}$ and is otherwise distributed uniformly in the interval $\left[-\frac{3r}{2},\frac{3r}{2}\right]$.
- If $1\le r\le \sqrt{8/3}$, the bound is tight when $X=r$ with probability $\frac{4}{3r^2}-\frac{1}{3}$ and is otherwise distributed uniformly in the interval $\left[-\frac{r}{2},r\right]$.

### Specialization to mean and variance {#Specialization_to_mean_and_variance}

If $X$ has mean $\mu$ and finite, non-zero variance $\sigma^2$, then taking $\alpha=\mu$ and $r=\lambda \sigma$ gives that for any $\lambda > \sqrt{\frac{8}{3}} = 1.63299...,$

$$\operatorname{Pr}(\left|X-\mu\right|\geq \lambda\sigma)\leq\frac{4}{9\lambda^2}.$$

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof Sketch"}

### Proof Sketch {#Proof_Sketch}

For a relatively [elementary proof](wikipedia://en/9318685) see.[^o10] The rough idea behind the proof is that there are two cases: one where the mode of $X$ is close to $\alpha$ compared to $r$, in which case we can show $\operatorname{Pr}(|X-\alpha|\ge r)\le \frac{4\rho^2}{9r^2}$, and one where the mode of $X$ is far from $\alpha$ compared to $r$, in which case we can show $\operatorname{Pr}(|X-\alpha|\ge r)\le \frac{4\rho^2}{3r^2}-\frac{1}{3}$. Combining these two cases gives $\operatorname{Pr}(|X-\alpha|\ge r)\le \max\left(\frac{4\rho^2}{9r^2},\frac{4\rho^2}{3r^2}-\frac{1}{3}\right).$ When $\frac{r}{\rho}=\sqrt{\frac{8}{3}}$, the two cases give the same value.

:::

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The theorem refines [Chebyshev's inequality](wikipedia://en/156533) by including the factor of 4/9, made possible by the condition that the distribution be unimodal.

It is common, in the construction of [control charts](wikipedia://en/435754) and other statistical heuristics, to set $\lambda = 3$, corresponding to an upper probability bound of 4/81= 0.04938..., and to construct *3-sigma* limits to bound *nearly all* (i.e. 95%) of the values of a process output. Without unimodality Chebyshev's inequality would give a looser bound of $1/9 = 0.11111...$.

:::

## One-sided version {#One-sided_version}

A version of the Vysochanskij-Petunin inequality for one-sided tail bounds exists. For a unimodal random variable $X$ with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, and $r \geq 0$, a one-sided Vysochanskij-Petunin inequality derived by Mercadier and Strobel (2021)[^o14] holds as follows:

$$\mathbb{P}(X-\mu\geq r)\leq \begin{cases} \dfrac{4}{9}\dfrac{\sigma^2}{r^{2}+\sigma^2} & \mbox{for }r^{2}\geq\dfrac{5}{3}\sigma^2,\\ \dfrac{4}{3}\dfrac{\sigma^2}{r^{2}+\sigma^2}-\dfrac{1}{3} & \mbox{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

The above Mercadier-Strobel inequality, as well as the related [Cantelli inequality](wikipedia://en/36703918), can for instance be relevant in the financial area, in the sense of "how bad can losses get".

::: {#o16 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The proof is very similar to that of [Cantelli's inequality](wikipedia://en/36703918#Proof). For any $u\ge 0$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}(X-\mu\geq r)&=\mathbb{P}((X+u)-\mu\geq r+u)\\ &\le \mathbb{P}(|(X+u)-\mu)|\geq r+u).\\ \end{aligned}$$

Then we can apply the Vysochanskij-Petunin inequality. With $\rho^2=\mathbb E[((X+u)-\mu)^2]=u^2+\sigma^2$, we have:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}(|(X+u)-\mu)|\geq r+u) &\le \begin{cases} \frac{4}{9} \frac{\rho^2}{(r+u)^2} & r+u\ge \sqrt{8/3}\rho\\ \frac{4}{3} \frac{\rho^2}{(r+u)^2}-\frac{1}{3} & r+u\le \sqrt{8/3}\rho \end{cases}. \end{aligned}$$

As in the proof of Cantelli's inequality, it can be shown that the minimum of $\frac{\rho^2}{(r+u)^2}$ over all $u\ge 0$ is achieved at $u=\sigma^2/r$. Plugging in this value of $u$ and simplifying yields the desired inequality.

:::

## Generalisation {#Generalisation}

Dharmadhikari and Joag-Dev generalised the VP inequality to deviations from an arbitrary point and moments of order $k$ other than $2$

$$\begin{aligned} P(|X-\alpha| \geq r) \leq \max \left\{\frac{s \tau_k-r^k}{(s-1) r^k},\left[\frac{k}{k+1}\right]^k \frac{\tau_k}{r^k}\right\} \\ \end{aligned}$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} \tau_k=E\left(|X-\alpha|^k\right), s>(k+1), s(s-k-1)^k=k^k \end{aligned}$$

The standard form of the inequality can be recovered by setting $k = 2$ which leads to a unique value of $s = 4$.[^o18]
