# Category of topological spaces

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of topological spaces"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **category of topological spaces**, often denoted $\mathbf{Top}$, is the [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) whose [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) are [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) are [continuous maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map). This is a category because the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of two continuous maps is again continuous, and the identity function is continuous.

:::

Some authors use the name $\mathbf{Top}$ for the categories with [topological manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119193), with [compactly generated spaces](wikipedia://en/899382) as objects and continuous maps as morphisms or with the [category of compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/71508609).

## As a concrete category {#As_a_concrete_category}

Like many categories, the category $\mathbf{Top}$ is a [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001), meaning its objects are [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) with additional structure (i.e. topologies) and its morphisms are [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) preserving this structure. There is a natural [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783)

$$U:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}$$

to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) which assigns to each topological space the underlying set and to each continuous map the underlying [function](wikipedia://en/185427).

The forgetful functor $U$ has both a [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint)

$$D:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Top}$$

which equips a given set with the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology), and a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint)

$$I:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Top}$$

which equips a given set with the [indiscrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_topology). Both of these functors are, in fact, [right inverses](wikipedia://en/14907#Left_and_right_inverses) to $U$ (meaning that $UD$ and $UI$ are equal to the [identity functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_functor) on $\mathbf{Set}$). Moreover, since any function between discrete or between indiscrete spaces is continuous, both of these functors give [full embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_embedding) of $\mathbf{Set}$ into $\mathbf{Top}$.

$\mathbf{Top}$ is also *fiber-complete* meaning that the [category of all topologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_of_topologies) on a given set $X$ (called the *[fiber](wikipedia://en/6603892)* of $U$ above $X$) forms a [complete lattice](wikipedia://en/44777) when ordered by [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_inclusion). The [greatest element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_element) in this fiber is the discrete topology on $X$, while the [least element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_element) is the indiscrete topology.

$\mathbf{Top}$ is the model of what is called a [topological category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_concrete_category). These categories are characterized by the fact that every [structured source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_source) $(X \to UA_i)_I$ has a unique [initial lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_lift) $( A \to A_i)_I$. In $\mathbf{Top}$ the initial lift is obtained by placing the [initial topology](wikipedia://en/1197123) on the source. Topological categories have many properties in common with $\mathbf{Top}$ (such as fiber-completeness, discrete and indiscrete functors, and unique lifting of limits).

## Limits and colimits {#Limits_and_colimits}

The category $\mathbf{Top}$ is both [complete and cocomplete](wikipedia://en/62781), which means that all small [limits and colimits](wikipedia://en/39248) exist in $\mathbf{Top}$. In fact, the forgetful functor $U:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}$ uniquely lifts both limits and colimits and preserves them as well. Therefore, (co)limits in $\mathbf{Top}$ are given by placing topologies on the corresponding (co)limits in $\mathbf{Set}$.

Specifically, if $F$ is a [diagram](wikipedia://en/7651625) in $\mathbf{Top}$ and $(L,\varphi:L\to F)$ is a limit of $UF$ in $\mathbf{Set}$, the corresponding limit of $F$ in $\mathbf{Top}$ is obtained by placing the [initial topology](wikipedia://en/1197123) on $(L,\varphi:L\to F)$. Dually, colimits in $\mathbf{Top}$ are obtained by placing the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736) on the corresponding colimits in $\mathbf{Set}$.

Unlike many *algebraic* categories, the forgetful functor $U:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set}$ does not create or reflect limits since there will typically be non-universal [cones](wikipedia://en/7533645) in $\mathbf{Top}$ covering universal cones in $\mathbf{Set}$.

Examples of limits and colimits in $\mathbf{Top}$ include:

- The [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) (considered as a topological space) is the [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) of $\mathbf{Top}$; any [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) topological space is a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object). There are thus no [zero objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object) in $\mathbf{Top}$.
- The [product](wikipedia://en/237868) in $\mathbf{Top}$ is given by the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910) on the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095). The [coproduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coproduct_%28category_theory%29) is given by the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/1564380) of topological spaces.
- The [equalizer](wikipedia://en/310767#In_category_theory) of a pair of morphisms is given by placing the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) on the set-theoretic equalizer. Dually, the [coequalizer](wikipedia://en/431940) is given by placing the [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology) on the set-theoretic coequalizer.
- [Direct limits](wikipedia://en/211566) and [inverse limits](wikipedia://en/15109) are the set-theoretic limits with the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736) and [initial topology](wikipedia://en/1197123) respectively.
- [Adjunction spaces](wikipedia://en/1240842) are an example of [pushouts](wikipedia://en/582024) in $\mathbf{Top}$.

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

- The [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538) in $\mathbf{Top}$ are the [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) continuous maps, the [epimorphisms](wikipedia://en/59539) are the [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) continuous maps, and the [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) are the [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660).
- The [extremal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_monomorphism) monomorphisms are (up to isomorphism) the [subspace](wikipedia://en/910926) embeddings. In fact, in $\mathbf{Top}$ all extremal monomorphisms happen to satisfy the stronger property of being [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_monomorphism).
- The extremal epimorphisms are (essentially) the [quotient maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_map_%28topology%29). Every extremal epimorphism is regular.
- The split monomorphisms are (essentially) the inclusions of [retracts](wikipedia://en/2120001) into their ambient space.
- The split epimorphisms are (up to isomorphism) the continuous surjective maps of a space onto one of its retracts.
- There are no [zero morphisms](wikipedia://en/320638) in $\mathbf{Top}$, and in particular the category is not [preadditive](wikipedia://en/59521).
- $\mathbf{Top}$ is not [cartesian closed](wikipedia://en/237037) (and therefore also not a [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464)) since it does not have [exponential objects](wikipedia://en/2237858) for all spaces. When this feature is desired, one often restricts to the full subcategory of [compactly generated Hausdorff spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_generated_Hausdorff_space) $\mathbf{CGHaus}$ or the [category of compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/71508609). However, $\mathbf{Top}$ is contained in the exponential category of [pseudotopologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudotopologies), which is itself a subcategory of the (also exponential) category of [convergence spaces](wikipedia://en/66374616).[^o5]

## Relationships to other categories {#Relationships_to_other_categories}

- The category of [pointed topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointed_topological_space) $\mathbf{Top}$<sub>•</sub> is a [coslice category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coslice_category) over $\mathbf{Top}$.
- The [homotopy category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_category_of_topological_spaces) $\mathbf{hTop}$ has topological spaces for objects and [homotopy equivalence classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) of continuous maps for morphisms. This is a [quotient category](wikipedia://en/4004632) of $\mathbf{Top}$. One can likewise form the pointed homotopy category $\mathbf{hTop}$<sub>•</sub>.
- $\mathbf{Top}$ contains the important category $\mathbf{Haus}$ of [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) as a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory). The added structure of this subcategory allows for more epimorphisms: in fact, the epimorphisms in this subcategory are precisely those morphisms with [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) [images](wikipedia://en/579311) in their [codomains](wikipedia://en/50264), so that epimorphisms need not be [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective).
- $\mathbf{Top}$ contains the full subcategory $\mathbf{CGHaus}$ of [compactly generated Hausdorff spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_generated_Hausdorff_space), which has the important property of being a [Cartesian closed category](wikipedia://en/237037) while still containing all of the typical spaces of interest. This makes $\mathbf{CGHaus}$ a particularly *convenient category of topological spaces* that is often used in place of $\mathbf{Top}$.
- The forgetful functor to $\mathbf{Set}$ has both a left and a right adjoint, as described above in the concrete category section.
- There is a functor to the category of [locales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locale_%28mathematics%29) $\mathbf{Loc}$ sending a topological space to its locale of open sets. This functor has a right adjoint that sends each locale to its topological space of points. This adjunction restricts to an equivalence between the category of [sober spaces](wikipedia://en/602490) and spatial locales.
- The [homotopy hypothesis](wikipedia://en/40965653) relates $\mathbf{Top}$ with $\mathbf{\infty Grpd}$, the category of [∞-groupoids](wikipedia://en/40965675). The conjecture states that ∞-groupoids are equivalent to topological spaces modulo [weak homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/34615176).
