# Coequalizer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coequalizer"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **coequalizer** (or **coequaliser**) is a generalization of the [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_set) of a set by an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) to objects in an arbitrary [category](wikipedia://en/53759). It is the categorical construction [dual](wikipedia://en/310885) to the [equalizer](wikipedia://en/310767).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **coequalizer** is the [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) of a diagram consisting of two objects *X* and *Y* and two parallel [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) *f*, *g* : *X* → *Y*.

More explicitly, a coequalizer of the [parallel morphisms](wikipedia://en/2390225#parallel_morphism) *f* and *g* can be defined as an object *Q* together with a morphism *q* : *Y* → *Q* such that *q* ∘ *f* = *q* ∘ *g*. Moreover, the pair (*Q*, *q*) must be [universal](wikipedia://en/32245) in the sense that given any other such pair (*Q*′, *q*′) there exists a unique morphism *u* : *Q* → *Q*′ such that *u* ∘ *q* = *q*′. This information can be captured by the following [commutative diagram](wikipedia://en/138484):

*Image: Coequalizer-01.svg.*

As with all [universal constructions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_construction), a coequalizer, if it exists, is unique [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) a unique [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) (this is why, by abuse of language, one sometimes speaks of "the" coequalizer of two parallel arrows).

It can be shown that a coequalizing arrow *q* is an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) in any category.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- In the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), the coequalizer of two [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) *f*, *g* : *X* → *Y* is the [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_set) of *Y* by the smallest [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) ~ such that for every *x* ∈ *X*, we have *f*(*x*) ~ *g*(*x*).[^o6] In particular, if *R* is an equivalence relation on a set *Y*, and *r*<sub>1</sub>, *r*<sub>2</sub> are the natural projections (*R* ⊂ *Y* × *Y*) → *Y* then the coequalizer of *r*<sub>1</sub> and *r*<sub>2</sub> is the quotient set *Y* / *R*. (See also: [quotient by an equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/42185042).)
- The coequalizer in the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092) is very similar. Here if *f*, *g* : *X* → *Y* are [group homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396), their coequalizer is the [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of *Y* by the [normal closure](wikipedia://en/153099) of the set

$$S=\{f(x)g(x)^{-1}\mid x\in X\}$$

- For [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) the coequalizer is particularly simple. It is just the [factor group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_group) *Y* / im(*f* – *g*). (This is the [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) of the morphism *f* – *g*; see the next section).
- In the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051), the circle object *S*<sup>1</sup> can be viewed as the coequalizer of the two inclusion maps from the standard 0-simplex to the standard 1-simplex.
- Coequalizers can be large: There are exactly two [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) from the category **1** having one object and one identity arrow, to the category **2** with two objects and one non-identity arrow going between them. The coequalizer of these two functors is the [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) under addition, considered as a one-object category. In particular, this shows that while every coequalizing arrow is [epic](wikipedia://en/59539), it is not necessarily [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every coequalizer is an epimorphism.
- In a [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464), every [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) is the coequalizer of its kernel pair.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

In categories with [zero morphisms](wikipedia://en/320638), one can define a *[cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715)* of a morphism *f* as the coequalizer of *f* and the parallel zero morphism.

In [preadditive categories](wikipedia://en/59521) it makes sense to add and subtract morphisms (the [hom-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hom-set) actually form [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974)). In such categories, one can define the coequalizer of two morphisms *f* and *g* as the cokernel of their difference:

coeq(*f*, *g*) = coker(*g* – *f*).

A stronger notion is that of an **absolute coequalizer**, this is a coequalizer that is preserved under all functors. Formally, an absolute coequalizer of a pair of parallel arrows *f*, *g* : *X* → *Y* in a category *C* is a coequalizer as defined above, but with the added property that given any functor *F* : *C* → *D*, *F*(*Q*) together with *F*(*q*) is the coequalizer of *F*(*f*) and *F*(*g*) in the category *D*. [Split coequalizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_coequalizer) are examples of absolute coequalizers.
