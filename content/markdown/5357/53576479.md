# Nick Woodhouse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nick Woodhouse"}

The Honourable **Nicholas Michael John Woodhouse** (born 27 February 1949) is a British mathematician. He is Emeritus Fellow of [Wadham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford), [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and former President of the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655).

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

Woodhouse is the younger son and second child of [Christopher Montague Woodhouse, 5th Baron Terrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montague_Woodhouse,_5th_Baron_Terrington), and Lady Davidema (Davina) Bulwer-Lytton. He was educated at [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College), then the University of Oxford and [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London). He completed his undergraduate degree in Mathematics at [Christ Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford), Oxford in 1970 and PhD at the University of London, King's College in 1973, where he subsequently held an SRC Fellowship. In 1973, he also held a Procter Visiting Fellowship at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

He was Research Associate in the Department of Physics, Princeton University (1975-1976) and a Junior Lecturer, University of Oxford (1976-1977). In 1977, he was made a Fellow of Wadham College, University of Oxford, where he has been an Emeritus Fellow since 1988. He was Head of the [Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/7905850), University of Oxford (2001-2009). He served as Treasurer of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) (2002-2009) and President of the Clay Mathematics Institute (2012-2018).[^o5]

His research is in applications of geometry in mathematical physics.

## Honours {#Honours}

Woodhouse was appointed [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (CBE) in the [2020 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_New_Year_Honours) for services to mathematics.
