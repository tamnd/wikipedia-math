# Modular invariant theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modular invariant theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **modular invariant** of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is an invariant of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of positive characteristic (usually dividing the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of the group). The study of modular invariants was originated in about 1914 by (Dickson 2004).

:::

## Dickson invariant {#Dickson_invariant}

When *G* is the finite [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL<sub>*n*</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) **F**<sub>*q*</sub> of order a [prime power](wikipedia://en/1630483) *q* acting on the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) **F**<sub>*q*</sub>\[*X*<sub>1</sub>, ...,*X*<sub>*n*</sub>\] in the natural way, (Dickson 1911) found a complete set of invariants as follows. Write \[*e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*n*</sub>\] for the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) whose entries are *X*${}^{q^{e_{j}}}_{i}$, where *e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*n*</sub> are non-negative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563). For example, the [Moore determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_determinant_over_a_finite_field) \[0,1,2\] of order 3 is

$$\begin{vmatrix} x_1 & x_1^q & x_1^{q^2}\\x_2 & x_2^q & x_2^{q^2}\\x_3 & x_3^q & x_3^{q^2} \end{vmatrix}$$

Then under the action of an element *g* of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) these determinants are all multiplied by det(*g*), so they are all invariants of SL<sub>*n*</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) and the ratios \[*e*<sub>1</sub>, ...,*e*<sub>*n*</sub>\] / \[0, 1, ..., *n* − 1\] are invariants of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>), called **Dickson invariants**. Dickson proved that the full ring of invariants **F**<sub>*q*</sub>\[*X*<sub>1</sub>, ...,*X*<sub>*n*</sub>\]<sup>GL<sub>*n*</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>)</sup> is a polynomial algebra over the *n* Dickson invariants \[0, 1, ..., *i* − 1, *i* + 1, ..., *n*\] / \[0, 1, ..., *n* − 1\] for *i* = 0, 1, ..., *n* − 1. (Steinberg 1987) gave a shorter proof of Dickson's theorem.

The matrices \[*e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*n*</sub>\] are divisible by all non-zero linear forms in the variables *X*<sub>*i*</sub> with coefficients in the finite field **F**<sub>*q*</sub>. In particular the [Moore determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_determinant_over_a_finite_field) \[0, 1, ..., *n* − 1\] is a product of such linear forms, taken over 1 + *q* + *q*<sup>2</sup> + ... + *q*<sup>*n* – 1</sup> representatives of (*n* – 1)-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) over the field. This factorization is similar to the factorization of the [Vandermonde determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandermonde_determinant) into linear factors.
