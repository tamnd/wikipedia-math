# James Waddell Alexander II

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Waddell Alexander II"}

**James Waddell Alexander II** (September 19, 1888 – September 23, 1971) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of the pre-World War II era. He was part of an influential school of topology at Princeton University, along with [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen), [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384), and others. He was a professor at Princeton (1920–1951) and one of the first members of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (1933–1951).

:::

## Early life, family, and personal life {#Early_life,_family,_and_personal_life}

James was born on September 19, 1888, in [Sea Bright, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Bright,_New_Jersey).[^o4] Alexander came from an old, distinguished Princeton family. He was the only child of the American portrait painter [John White Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_White_Alexander) and Elizabeth Alexander. His maternal grandfather, James Waddell Alexander, was the president of the [Equitable Life Assurance Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AXA_Equitable_Life_Insurance_Company). Alexander's affluence and upbringing allowed him to interact with high society in America and elsewhere.

He married Natalia Levitzkaja on January 11, 1918, a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) woman. Together, they had two children.[^o5]

They would frequently spend time, until 1937, in the [Chamonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamonix) area of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), where he would also climb mountains and hills. Alexander was also a noted [mountaineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaineer), having succeeded in many major ascents, e.g. in the [Swiss Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Alps) and Colorado [Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains). When in Princeton, he liked to climb the university buildings, and always left his office window on the top floor of [Fine Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Department_of_Mathematics) open so that he could enter by climbing the building.[^o6]

## Education {#Education}

He graduated from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with a Bachelor of Science degree in 1910. He received his Masters of Arts degree in 1911 and his doctoral degree in 1915.[^o5]

## Military career {#Military_career}

During World War I, Alexander served with tech staff in the Ordnance Department of the United States Army overseas. He retired as a Captain.[^o5]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

He was a pioneer in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), setting the foundations for [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740)'s ideas on [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) and furthering it by founding [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory), which developed gradually in the decade after he gave a definition of [cochain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochain). For this, in 1928 he was awarded the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872). He also contributed to the beginnings of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) by inventing the [Alexander invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_invariant) of a knot, which in modern terms is a [graded module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_module) obtained from the homology of a "cyclic covering" of the [knot complement](wikipedia://en/1350120). From this invariant, he defined the first of the [polynomial knot invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_knot_invariant).

With [Garland Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garland_Briggs_%28mathematician%29), he also gave a combinatorial description of knot invariance based on certain moves, now (against the history) called the [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374); and also a means of computing homological invariants from the [knot diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram).

Alexander was an elected member of both the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o10][^o11]

Towards the end of his life, Alexander became a recluse. He was known as a socialist and his prominence brought him to the attention of [McCarthyists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism). The atmosphere of the McCarthy era pushed him into a greater seclusion. He was not seen in public after 1954, when he appeared to sign a letter supporting [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer).

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

He died on September 23, 1971.[^o5]

[Alexander's Chimney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander's_Chimney) in [Rocky Mountain National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_National_Park) is named after him.
