# Wishart distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wishart distribution"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Wishart distribution** is a generalization of the [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079) to multiple dimensions. It is named in honor of [John Wishart](wikipedia://en/944879), who first formulated the distribution in 1928.[^o2] Other names include **Wishart ensemble** (in [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory), probability distributions over matrices are usually called "ensembles"), or **Wishart–Laguerre ensemble** (since its eigenvalue distribution involves [Laguerre polynomials](wikipedia://en/943917)), or LOE, LUE, LSE (in analogy with [GOE, GUE, GSE](wikipedia://en/1648765#Gaussian_ensembles)).[^o3]

:::

It is a family of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) defined over symmetric, [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix) [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices) (i.e. [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859)-valued [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685)). These distributions are of great importance in the [estimation of covariance matrices](wikipedia://en/974723) in [multivariate statistics](wikipedia://en/19384). In [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/49571), the Wishart distribution is the [conjugate prior](wikipedia://en/846412) of the [inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_inverse) [covariance-matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) of a [multivariate-normal random vector](wikipedia://en/50347).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose $G$ is a $p \times n$ matrix, each column of which is [independently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) drawn from a [$p$-variate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) with zero mean:

$$G = (g_1,\dots,g_n) \sim \mathcal{N}_p(0,V).$$

It means $g_i=(g_{i,1},\dots,g_{i,p})^T\ \overset{iid}\sim\ \mathcal{N}_p(0,V)\ \forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$

Then the Wishart distribution is the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the $p \times p$ random matrix [^o7]

$$S= G G^T = \sum_{i=1}^n g_{i}g_{i}^T$$

known as the [scatter matrix](wikipedia://en/11394693). One indicates that $S$ has that probability distribution by writing

$$S\sim W_p(V,n).$$

The positive integer $n$ is the number of *[degrees of freedom](wikipedia://en/2321606)*. Sometimes this is written $W(V, p, n)$. For $n \ge p$ the matrix $S$ is invertible with probability $1$ if $V$ is invertible.

If $p = V = 1$ then this distribution is a [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424) with $n$ degrees of freedom.

:::

## Occurrence {#Occurrence}

The Wishart distribution arises as the distribution of the sample covariance matrix for a sample from a [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347). It occurs frequently in [likelihood-ratio tests](wikipedia://en/45035) in multivariate statistical analysis. It also arises in the spectral theory of [random matrices](wikipedia://en/1648765) and in multidimensional Bayesian analysis.[^o9] It is also encountered in wireless communications, while analyzing the performance of [Rayleigh fading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_fading) [MIMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIMO) wireless channels.[^o10]

## Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

*Image: Spectral density of Wishart-Laguerre ensemble (8, 15).png.* Spectral density of Wishart-Laguerre ensemble with dimensions (8, 15). A reconstruction of Figure 1 of.[^o12]

The Wishart distribution can be [characterized](wikipedia://en/780886) by its [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) as follows:

Let $\mathbf{X}$ be a $p \times p$ symmetric matrix of random variables that is [positive semi-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix). Let $\mathbf{V}$ be a (fixed) symmetric positive definite matrix of size $p \times p$.

Then, if $n \ge p$, $\mathbf{X}$ has a Wishart distribution with $n$ degrees of freedom if it has the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487)

$$f_{\mathbf X} (\mathbf X) = \frac{1}{2^{np/2} \left|{\mathbf V}\right|^{n/2} \Gamma_p\left(\frac {n}{2}\right ) }{\left|\mathbf{X}\right|}^{(n-p-1)/2} e^{-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}({\mathbf V}^{-1}\mathbf{X})}$$

where $\left|{\mathbf X}\right|$ is the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of $\mathbf X$ and $\Gamma_{p}$ is the [multivariate gamma function](wikipedia://en/946510) defined as

$$\Gamma_p \left (\frac n 2 \right )= \pi^{p(p-1)/4}\prod_{j=1}^p \Gamma\left( \frac{n}{2} - \frac{j-1}{2} \right ).$$

The density above is not the joint density of all the $p^2$ elements of the random matrix $\mathbf{X}$ (such $p^2$-dimensional density does not exist because of the symmetry constrains $X_{ij}=X_{ji}$), it is rather the joint density of $p(p+1)/2$ elements $X_{ij}$ for $i\le j$ (,[^o2] page 38). Also, the density formula above applies only to positive definite matrices $\mathbf x;$ for other matrices the density is equal to zero.

In fact the above definition can be extended to any real $n > p - 1$. If $n \le p - 1$, then the Wishart no longer has a density—instead it represents a singular distribution that takes values in a lower-dimension subspace of the space of $p \times p$ matrices.[^o13]

### Spectral density {#Spectral_density}

The joint-eigenvalue density for the eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots , \lambda_p\ge 0$ of a random matrix $\mathbf{X}\sim W_p(\mathbf{I},n)$ is,[^o15][^o30]

$$c_{n,p}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_i\lambda_i}\prod \lambda_i^{(n-p-1)/2}\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|$$

where $c_{n,p}$ is a constant. The spectral density can be marginalized to yield the density of a single eigenvalue, by evaluating a [Selberg integral](wikipedia://en/18583020).

The spectral density can be integrated to give the probability that all eigenvalues of a Wishart random matrix lie within an interval.[^o16]

## Use in Bayesian statistics {#Use_in_Bayesian_statistics}

In [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412), in the context of the [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347), the Wishart distribution is the conjugate prior to the [precision](wikipedia://en/24467258) matrix $\mathbf{\Omega} = \mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}$, where $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ is the covariance matrix.[^o66][^o18] The use of Normal-Wishart conjugate priors (for mean and precision) is particularly common for [vector autoregression](wikipedia://en/2399016) models.[^o19]

### Choice of parameters {#Choice_of_parameters}

The least informative, proper Wishart prior is obtained by setting $n = p$.

A common choice for **V** leverages the fact that the mean of **X** ~*W<sub>p</sub>*(**V**, *n*) is *n***V**. Then **V** is chosen so that *n***V** equals an initial guess for **X**. For instance, when estimating a precision matrix **Σ**<sup>−1</sup> ~ *W<sub>p</sub>*(**V**, *n*) a reasonable choice for **V** would be *n*<sup>−1</sup>**Σ**<sub>0</sub><sup>−1</sup>, where **Σ**<sub>0</sub> is some prior estimate for the covariance matrix **Σ**.

## Properties {#Properties}

### Log-expectation {#Log-expectation}

The following formula plays a role in [variational Bayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_Bayes) derivations for [Bayes networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayes_network) involving the Wishart distribution. From equation (2.63),[^o24]

$$\operatorname{E}[\, \ln\left|\mathbf{X}\right|\, ] = \psi_p\left(\frac n 2\right) + p \, \ln(2) + \ln|\mathbf{V}|$$

where $\psi_p$ is the multivariate digamma function (the derivative of the log of the [multivariate gamma function](wikipedia://en/946510)).

### Log-variance {#Log-variance}

The following variance computation could be of help in Bayesian statistics:

$$\operatorname{Var}\left[\, \ln\left|\mathbf{X}\right| \,\right]=\sum_{i=1}^p \psi_1\left(\frac{n+1-i} 2\right)$$

where $\psi_1$ is the trigamma function. This comes up when computing the Fisher information of the Wishart random variable.

### Entropy {#Entropy}

The [information entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_entropy) of the distribution has the following formula:[^o66]

$$\operatorname{H}\left[\, \mathbf{X} \,\right] = -\ln \left( B(\mathbf{V},n) \right) -\frac{n-p-1}{2} \operatorname{E}\left[\, \ln\left|\mathbf{X}\right|\,\right] + \frac{np}{2}$$

where $B(\mathbf{V}, n)$ is the [normalizing constant](wikipedia://en/398189) of the distribution:

$$B(\mathbf{V},n) = \frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{V}\right|^{n/2} 2^{np/2}\Gamma_p\left(\frac n 2 \right)}.$$

This can be expanded as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{H}\left[\, \mathbf{X}\, \right] & = \frac{n}{2} \ln \left|\mathbf{V}\right| +\frac{n p}{2} \ln 2 + \ln \Gamma_p \left(\frac{n}{2} \right) - \frac{n-p-1}{2} \operatorname{E}\left[\, \ln\left|\mathbf{X}\right|\, \right] + \frac{n p}{2} \\[8pt] &= \frac{n}{2} \ln\left|\mathbf{V}\right| + \frac{n p}{2} \ln 2 + \ln\Gamma_p\left(\frac{n}{2} \right) - \frac{n-p-1} 2 \left( \psi_p \left(\frac{n}{2}\right) + p\ln 2 + \ln\left|\mathbf{V}\right|\right) + \frac{n p}{2} \\[8pt] &= \frac{n}{2} \ln\left|\mathbf{V}\right| + \frac{n p}{2} \ln 2 + \ln\Gamma_p\left(\frac n 2\right) - \frac{n-p-1}{2} \psi_p\left(\frac n 2 \right) - \frac{n-p-1} 2 \left(p\ln 2 +\ln\left|\mathbf{V}\right| \right) + \frac{n p}{2} \\[8pt] &= \frac{p+1}{2} \ln\left|\mathbf{V}\right| + \frac1 2 p(p+1) \ln 2 + \ln\Gamma_p\left(\frac n 2\right) - \frac{n-p-1}{2} \psi_p\left(\frac n 2 \right) + \frac{n p}{2} \end{aligned}$$

### Cross-entropy {#Cross-entropy}

The [cross-entropy](wikipedia://en/1735250) of two Wishart distributions $p_0$ with parameters $n_0, V_0$ and $p_1$ with parameters $n_1, V_1$ is

$$\begin{aligned} H(p_0, p_1) &= \operatorname{E}_{p_0}[\, -\log p_1\, ]\\[8pt] &= \operatorname{E}_{p_0} \left[\, -\log \frac{\left|\mathbf{X}\right|^{(n_1 - p_1 - 1)/2} e^{-\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{V}_1^{-1} \mathbf{X})/2}}{2^{n_1 p_1/2} \left|\mathbf{V}_1\right|^{n_1/2} \Gamma_{p_1}\left(\tfrac{n_1}{2}\right)} \right]\\[8pt] &= \tfrac{n_1 p_1} 2 \log 2 + \tfrac{n_1} 2 \log \left|\mathbf{V}_1\right| + \log \Gamma_{p_1}(\tfrac{n_1} 2) - \tfrac{n_1 - p_1 - 1} 2 \operatorname{E}_{p_0}\left[\, \log\left|\mathbf{X}\right|\, \right] + \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{E}_{p_0}\left[\, \operatorname{tr}\left(\,\mathbf{V}_1^{-1}\mathbf{X}\,\right) \, \right] \\[8pt] &= \tfrac{n_1 p_1}{2} \log 2 + \tfrac{n_1} 2 \log \left|\mathbf{V}_1\right| + \log \Gamma_{p_1}(\tfrac{n_1}{2}) - \tfrac{n_1 - p_1 - 1}{2} \left( \psi_{p_0}(\tfrac{n_0} 2) + p_0 \log 2 + \log \left|\mathbf{V}_0\right|\right)+ \tfrac{1}{2} \operatorname{tr}\left(\, \mathbf{V}_1^{-1} n_0 \mathbf{V}_0\, \right) \\[8pt] &=-\tfrac{n_1}{2} \log \left|\, \mathbf{V}_1^{-1} \mathbf{V}_0\, \right| + \tfrac{p_1+1} 2 \log \left|\mathbf{V}_0\right| + \tfrac{n_0} 2 \operatorname{tr}\left(\, \mathbf{V}_1^{-1} \mathbf{V}_0\right)+ \log \Gamma_{p_1}\left(\tfrac{n_1}{2}\right) - \tfrac{n_1 - p_1 - 1}{2} \psi_{p_0}(\tfrac{n_0}{2}) + \tfrac{n_1(p_1 - p_0)+p_0(p_1+1)}{2} \log 2 \end{aligned}$$

Note that when $p_0=p_1$ and $n_0=n_1$ we recover the entropy.

### KL-divergence {#KL-divergence}

The [Kullback–Leibler divergence](wikipedia://en/1115052) of $p_1$ from $p_0$ is

$$\begin{aligned} D_{KL}(p_0 \| p_1) & = H(p_0, p_1) - H(p_0) \\[6pt] & =-\frac{n_1} 2 \log |\mathbf{V}_1^{-1} \mathbf{V}_0| + \frac{n_0}{2}(\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{V}_1^{-1} \mathbf{V}_0) - p)+ \log \frac{\Gamma_p\left(\frac{n_1} 2 \right)}{\Gamma_p\left(\frac{n_0} 2 \right)} + \tfrac{n_0 - n_1 } 2 \psi_p\left(\frac{n_0} 2\right) \end{aligned}$$

### Characteristic function {#Characteristic_function}

The [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) of the Wishart distribution is

$$\Theta \mapsto \operatorname{E}\left[ \, \exp\left( \,i \operatorname{tr}\left(\,\mathbf{X}{\mathbf\Theta}\,\right)\,\right)\, \right] = \left|\, 1 - 2i\, {\mathbf\Theta}\,{\mathbf V}\, \right|^{-n/2}$$

where $E[\cdot]$ denotes expectation. (Here $\Theta$ is any matrix with the same dimensions as $\mathbf{V}$, $1$ indicates the identity matrix, and $i$ is a square root of $-1$).[^o30] Properly interpreting this formula requires a little care, because noninteger complex powers are [multivalued](wikipedia://en/173181); when $n$ is noninteger, the correct branch must be determined via [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842).[^o31]

:::

## Theorem {#Theorem}

If a $p \times p$ random matrix $\mathbf{X}$ has a Wishart distribution with $m$ degrees of freedom and variance matrix $\mathbf{V}$ — write $\mathbf{X}\sim\mathcal{W}_p({\mathbf V},m)$ — and $\mathbf{C}$ is a $q \times p$ matrix of [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_%28matrix_theory%29) $q$, then [^o34]

$$\mathbf{C}\mathbf{X}{\mathbf C}^T \sim \mathcal{W}_q\left({\mathbf C}{\mathbf V}{\mathbf C}^T,m\right).$$

::: {#o36 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="Corollary 1"}

### Corollary 1 {#Corollary_1}

If $\mathbf{z}$ is a nonzero $p \times 1$ constant vector, then:[^o34]

$$\sigma_z^{-2} \, {\mathbf z}^T\mathbf{X}{\mathbf z} \sim \chi_m^2.$$

In this case, $\chi_m^2$ is the [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424) and $\sigma_z^2={\mathbf z}^T{\mathbf V}{\mathbf z}$ (note that $\sigma_z^2$ is a constant; it is positive because $\mathbf{V}$ is positive definite).

:::

::: {#o38 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="Corollary 2"}

### Corollary 2 {#Corollary_2}

Consider the case where $\mathbf{z}^{T} = (0, ..., 0, 1, 0, ..., 0)$ (that is, the $j$-th element is one and all others zero). Then corollary 1 above shows that

$$\sigma_{jj}^{-1} \, w_{jj}\sim \chi^2_m$$

gives the marginal distribution of each of the elements on the matrix's diagonal.

[George Seber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Seber) points out that the Wishart distribution is not called the “multivariate chi-squared distribution” because the marginal distribution of the [off-diagonal elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-diagonal_element) is not chi-squared. Seber prefers to reserve the term [multivariate](wikipedia://en/19384) for the case when all univariate marginals belong to the same family.[^o39]

:::

:::

## Estimator of the multivariate normal distribution {#Estimator_of_the_multivariate_normal_distribution}

The Wishart distribution is the [sampling distribution](wikipedia://en/520670) of the [maximum-likelihood estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood) (MLE) of the [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) of a [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347).[^o41] A [derivation of the MLE](wikipedia://en/974723) uses the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495).

## Bartlett decomposition {#Bartlett_decomposition}

The **Bartlett decomposition** of a matrix $\mathbf{X}$ from a $p$-variate Wishart distribution with scale matrix $\mathbf{V}$ and $n$ degrees of freedom is the factorization:

$$\mathbf{X} = {\textbf L}{\textbf A}{\textbf A}^T{\textbf L}^T,$$

where $\mathbf{L}$ is the [Cholesky factor](wikipedia://en/134433) of $\mathbf{V}$, and:

$$\mathbf A = \begin{pmatrix} c_1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0\\ n_{21} & c_2 &0 & \cdots& 0 \\ n_{31} & n_{32} & c_3 & \cdots & 0\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots &\ddots & \vdots \\ n_{p1} & n_{p2} & n_{p3} &\cdots & c_p \end{pmatrix}$$

where $c_i^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-i+1}$ and $n_{ij} \sim N(0, 1)$ independently.[^o43] This provides a useful method for obtaining random samples from a Wishart distribution.[^o44]

## Marginal distribution of matrix elements {#Marginal_distribution_of_matrix_elements}

Let $\mathbf{V}$ be a $2 \times 2$ variance matrix characterized by [correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_product-moment_correlation_coefficient) $-1 < \rho < 1$ and $\mathbf{L}$ its lower Cholesky factor:

$$\mathbf{V} = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_1^2 & \rho \sigma_1 \sigma_2 \\ \rho \sigma_1 \sigma_2 & \sigma_2^2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{L} = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_1 & 0 \\ \rho \sigma_2 & \sqrt{1-\rho^2} \sigma_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

Multiplying through the Bartlett decomposition above, we find that a random sample from the $2 \times 2$ Wishart distribution is

$$\mathbf{X} = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_1^2 c_1^2 & \sigma_1 \sigma_2 \left (\rho c_1^2 + \sqrt{1-\rho^2} c_1 n_{21} \right ) \\ \sigma_1 \sigma_2 \left (\rho c_1^2 + \sqrt{1-\rho^2} c_1 n_{21} \right ) & \sigma_2^2 \left(\left (1-\rho^2 \right ) c_2^2 + \left (\sqrt{1-\rho^2} n_{21} + \rho c_1 \right )^2 \right) \end{pmatrix}$$

The diagonal elements, most evidently in the first element, follow the $\chi^{2}$ distribution with $n$ degrees of freedom (scaled by $\sigma^{2}$) as expected. The off-diagonal element is less familiar but can be identified as a [normal variance-mean mixture](wikipedia://en/6526281) where the mixing density is a $\chi^{2}$ distribution. The corresponding marginal probability density for the off-diagonal element is therefore the [variance-gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/6534096)

$$f(x_{12}) = \frac{\left | x_{12} \right |^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right) \sqrt{2^{n-1} \pi \left (1-\rho^2 \right ) \left (\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \right )^{n+1}}} \cdot K_{\frac{n-1}{2}} \left(\frac{\left |x_{12} \right |}{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \left (1-\rho^2 \right )}\right) \exp{\left(\frac{\rho x_{12}}{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 (1-\rho^2)}\right)}$$

where $K_{\nu}(z)$ is the [modified Bessel function of the second kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_Bessel_function_of_the_second_kind).[^o46] Similar results may be found for higher dimensions. In general, if $X$ follows a Wishart distribution with parameters, $\Sigma, n$, then for $i \neq j$, the off-diagonal elements

$X_{ij} \sim \text{VG}(n, \Sigma_{ij}, (\Sigma_{ii} \Sigma_{jj} - \Sigma_{ij}^2)^{1/2}, 0)$.[^o47]

It is also possible to write down the [moment-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function) even in the *noncentral* case (essentially the *n*th power of Craig (1936)[^o48] equation 10) although the probability density becomes an infinite sum of Bessel functions.

## The range of the shape parameter {#The_range_of_the_shape_parameter}

It can be shown [^o50] that the Wishart distribution can be defined if and only if the shape parameter $\mathbf{n}$ belongs to the set

$$\Lambda_p:=\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\cup \left(p-1,\infty\right).$$

This set is named after [Simon Gindikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Gindikin), who introduced it[^o51] in the 1970s in the context of gamma distributions on homogeneous cones. However, for the new parameters in the discrete spectrum of the Gindikin ensemble, namely,

$$\Lambda_p^*:=\{0, \ldots, p-1\},$$

the corresponding Wishart distribution has no Lebesgue density.

## Wishart–Laguerre ensembles and β-extensions {#Wishart–Laguerre_ensembles_and_β-extensions}

In random matrix theory, the Wishart family is often studied through its **Laguerre ensembles**. For the real case (orthogonal symmetry, $\beta$=1), the joint density of the eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_p \ge 0$ of $\mathbf{X}\sim W_p(\mathbf{I},n)$ is

$$c_{n,p}\,\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^p \lambda_i\Big)\; \prod_{i=1}^p \lambda_i^{\tfrac{n-p-1}{2}}\; \prod_{1\le i<j\le p}\!|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|,$$

which is the **Laguerre orthogonal ensemble** (LOE). The complex and quaternion analogues are the **Laguerre unitary** (LUE, $\beta=2$) and **Laguerre symplectic** (LSE, $\beta=4$) ensembles, respectively.[^o53]

### β-Laguerre ensemble (general $\beta>0$) {#β-Laguerre_ensemble_(general_\beta>0)}

A further generalization, the **β-Laguerre ensemble**, allows the Dyson index $\beta>0$ to vary continuously. Its joint eigenvalue density has the [Coulomb gas](wikipedia://en/74470076) form

$$p(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p)\;\propto\; \Big(\prod_{i=1}^p \lambda_i^{\alpha}\,e^{-\tfrac{\beta}{2}\lambda_i}\Big)\; \Big(\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|^{\beta}\Big), \qquad \alpha>-1,$$

which reduces to LOE/LUE/LSE for $\beta=1,2,4$. For the classical Gaussian Wishart case (scale $\mathbf{I}$), one has the identification

$$\alpha \;=\; \tfrac{\beta}{2}\,(n-p+1)\;-\;1.$$

A concrete probabilistic construction for any $\beta>0$ is provided by the **Dumitriu–Edelman bidiagonal model**. One samples a random bidiagonal matrix $\mathbf{B}$ with independent chi variables and sets $\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{T}$; the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{L}$ follow the β-Laguerre law with parameter $\alpha$.[^o55][^o56]

**Sampling**

- **Classical $\beta=1,2,4$.** Bartlett's decomposition gives $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{L}^{T}$ with $\mathbf{A}$ lower-triangular having chi/normal entries, yielding LOE/LUE/LSE exactly.[^o57]
- **General $\beta>0$.** In the Dumitriu–Edelman model, for matrix size $p$ and parameter $\alpha>-1$, one samples independently

$$B_{ii}\sim \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\;\chi_{\beta(\alpha+p-i+1)},\qquad B_{i,i-1}\sim \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\;\chi_{\beta(p-i+1)} \quad (i=2,\dots,p),$$

and sets $\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{T}$. Then the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{L}$ have the β-Laguerre joint density above.[^o55]

**Rectangular data matrices**

If one wishes to realize these spectra as the singular values of a rectangular $M\times N$ matrix (with $M\ge N=p$), draw independent Haar-distributed $\mathbf{U}\in O(M)$ (or $U(M)$) and $\mathbf{V}\in O(N)$, and set

$$X \;=\; U\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1},\dots,\sqrt{\lambda_p})\\ 0\end{bmatrix} V^{*},$$

so that $X^{*}X$ has eigenvalues $\{\lambda_i\}$. For i.i.d. Gaussian entries (true Wishart), $\beta$ is fixed by the field (real, complex, quaternion) and $\alpha$ reduces to $\tfrac{\beta}{2}(n-p+1)-1$.[^o56][^o58]

### Hard-edge behavior and universality {#Hard-edge_behavior_and_universality}

At the “hard edge” (near $\lambda=0$), β-Laguerre ensembles exhibit **Bessel-kernel** correlations and level repulsion of order $\beta$. In particular, with $p\to\infty$ and fixed $\alpha$, the distribution of the smallest eigenvalue converges to a universal hard-edge (Bessel) law that depends only on $\alpha$ and $\beta$.[^o60][^o56]

::: {#o62 .statement cue=named-heading name="Macroscopic limit (Marchenko–Pastur law)"}

### Macroscopic limit (Marchenko–Pastur law) {#Macroscopic_limit_(Marchenko–Pastur_law)}

Under proportional growth $p/n\to\gamma\in(0,\infty)$, the empirical spectral distribution of $n^{-1}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}^{T}$ (with i.i.d. entries of variance $\sigma^2$) converges almost surely to the **Marchenko–Pastur law**, supported on $\sigma^2(1\pm\sqrt{\gamma})^2$. When $\gamma=1$, the density diverges as $x^{-1/2}$ at the origin (a hard-edge singularity).[^o63]

:::

## Relationships to other distributions {#Relationships_to_other_distributions}

- The Wishart distribution is related to the [inverse-Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/8143463), denoted by $W_p^{-1}$, as follows: If $\mathbf{X} \sim W_{p}(\mathbf{V}, n)$ and if we do the change of variables $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{X}^{-1}$, then $\mathbf{C}\sim W_p^{-1}(\mathbf{V}^{-1},n)$. This relationship may be derived by noting that the absolute value of the [Jacobian determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_determinant) of this change of variables is $|\mathbf{C}|^{p+1}$, see for example equation (15.15) in.[^o65]
- In [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412), the Wishart distribution is a [conjugate prior](wikipedia://en/846412) for the [precision parameter](wikipedia://en/24467258) of the [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347), when the mean parameter is known.[^o66]
- A generalization is the [multivariate gamma distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_gamma_distribution).
- A different type of generalization is the [normal-Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/35134479), essentially the product of a [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) with a Wishart distribution.
