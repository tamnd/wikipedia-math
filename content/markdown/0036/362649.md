# Stanisław Saks

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stanisław Saks"}

**Stanisław Saks** (30 December 1897 – 23 November 1942) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and university tutor, a member of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328), known primarily for his membership in the [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651) circle, an extensive monograph on the theory of [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532), his works on [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and the [Vitali–Hahn–Saks theorem](wikipedia://en/1075379).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Stanisław Saks was born on 30 December 1897 in [Kalisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalisz), [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland), to an assimilated [Polish-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Polish_Jews) family. In 1915 he graduated from a local gymnasium and joined the newly recreated [Warsaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University). In 1922 he received a doctorate of his *[alma mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_mater)* with a prestigious distinction *[maxima cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_cum_laude)*. Soon afterwards he also passed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) and received the [Rockefeller fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_fellowship), which allowed him to travel to the United States. Around that time he started publishing articles in various mathematical journals, mostly the *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)*, but also in the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)*. He participated in the [Silesian Uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Uprisings) and was awarded the [Cross of the Valorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzyż_Walecznych) and the [Medal of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_Niepodległości) for his bravery. Following the end of the uprising he returned to Warsaw and resumed his academic career.

For most of it he studied the theories of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) and [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948) in particular. In 1930 he published his most notable book, the *Zarys teorii całki* (Sketch on the Theory of the Integral), which later got expanded and translated into several languages, including English (*Theory of the Integral*), French (*Théorie de l'Intégrale*)[^o4] and Russian (*Teoriya Integrala*). Despite his successes, Saks was never awarded the title of [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) and remained an ordinary tutor, initially at his alma mater and the [Warsaw University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University_of_Technology), and later at the [Lwów University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_University) and [Wilno University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilno_University). He was also an active socialist and a journalist at the *[Robotnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotnik_%281894–1939%29)* weekly (1919–1926) and later a collaborator of the Association of Socialist Youth.

Saks wrote a mathematics book with [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477), *Analytic Functions*, in 1933. It was translated into English in 1952 by E. J. Scott.[^o5] In the preface to the English edition, Zygmund writes:[^n1]

> Stanislaw Saks was a man of moral as well as physical courage, of rare intelligence and wit. To his colleagues and pupils he was an inspiration not only as a mathematician but as a human being. In the period between the two world wars he exerted great influence upon a whole generation of Polish mathematicians in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) and [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów). In November 1942, at the age of 45, Saks died in a [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) prison, victim of a policy of extermination.

After the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) and the occupation of Poland by Germany, Saks joined the [Polish underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Secret_State). Arrested in November 1942, he was executed on 23 November 1942 by the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw).[^o7]
