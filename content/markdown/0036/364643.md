# Israel Gelfand

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Israel Gelfand"}

Moiseevich

**Israel Moiseevich Gelfand**, also written **Israïl Moyseyovich Gel'fand**, or **Izrail M. Gelfand** (ישראל געלפֿאַנד, Изра́иль Моисе́евич Гельфа́нд, Ізраїль Мойсейович Гельфанд; 2 September 1913 – 5 October 2009) was a prominent Soviet and American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), one of the greatest mathematicians of the 20th century, biologist, teacher and organizer of mathematical education. He made significant contributions to many branches of mathematics, including [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). The recipient of many awards, including the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin) and the first [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize), he was a Foreign [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) and professor at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) and, after immigrating to the United States shortly before his 76th birthday, at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University). Gelfand is also a [1994 MacArthur Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program).

:::

His legacy continues through his students, who include [Endre Szemerédi](wikipedia://en/663489), [Alexandre Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515), [Edward Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel),[^o3] [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383), [David Kazhdan](wikipedia://en/3052833), as well as his own son, Sergei Gelfand.

## Early years {#Early_years}

Gelfand was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family in [Okny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okny), in the [Kherson Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson_Governorate) of the Russian Empire (now in [Odesa Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Oblast), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)). According to his own account, Gelfand was expelled from high school under the [Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) because his father had been a mill owner. Bypassing both high school and college, he proceeded to postgraduate study at the age of 19 at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), where his advisor was the preeminent mathematician [Andrei Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Kolmogorov).[^o5] He received his PhD in 1935.[^o6]

Gelfand immigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1989.[^o7]

## Work {#Work}

Gelfand is known for many developments including:

- the book *Calculus of Variations* (1963), which he co-authored with [Sergei Fomin](wikipedia://en/3017297);
- [Gelfand's formula](wikipedia://en/386169#Gelfand's_formula), which expresses the spectral radius as a limit of matrix norms.
- the [Gelfand representation](wikipedia://en/364372) in [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665) theory;
- the [Gelfand–Mazur theorem](wikipedia://en/9129699) in Banach algebra theory;
- the [Gelfand–Naimark theorem](wikipedia://en/315414);
- the [Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction](wikipedia://en/297446);
- [Gelfand–Shilov spaces](wikipedia://en/29180166);
- the [Gelfand–Pettis integral](wikipedia://en/20983125);
- the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of the [complex classical Lie groups](wikipedia://en/10172878);
- contributions to the theory of [Verma modules](wikipedia://en/2975234) in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587) (with I. N. Bernstein and S. I. Gelfand);
- contributions to [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) theory and measures on infinite-dimensional spaces;[^o9]
- the first observation of the connection of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) with representations (with [Sergei Fomin](wikipedia://en/3017297));
- conjectures about the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752);
- [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) (Gelfand–[Levitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Levitan) theory);
- work on [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) theory (Gelfand–Dikii equations);
- contributions to the *[philosophy of cusp forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_cusp_forms)*;
- Gelfand–[Fuchs](wikipedia://en/56558134) [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944);
- [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension](wikipedia://en/35987396);
- [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336);
- combinatorial definition of the [Pontryagin class](wikipedia://en/385944);
- [Coxeter functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_functor);
- [general hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/30785488);
- Gelfand–[Tsetlin](wikipedia://en/37832091) patterns;
- Gelfand–Lokutsievski method;
- the [BGG correspondence](wikipedia://en/76601964) (with [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383) and [Sergei Gelfand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Gelfand));
- and many other results, particularly in the representation theory of [classical groups](wikipedia://en/10172878).

Gelfand ran a seminar at Moscow State University from 1943<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=61dlxxRqpD4> until May 1989 (when it continued at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University)). Covereing a wide range of topics, the seminar was an important school for many young mathematicians.[^o10][^o11][^o12]

## Influence outside mathematics {#Influence_outside_mathematics}

The [Gelfand–Tsetlin (also spelled Zetlin) basis](wikipedia://en/880754#Gelfand–Tsetlin_basis) is a widely used tool in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) and the result of Gelfand's work on the representation theory of the [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) and [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) in general.

Gelfand also published works on biology and medicine.[^o14] For a long time he took an interest in [cell biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology) and organized a research seminar on the subject.[^o15][^o16]

He worked extensively in mathematics education, particularly with correspondence education. In 1994, he was awarded a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship) for this work.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Gelfand was married to [Zorya Shapiro](wikipedia://en/58900715), and their two sons, Sergei and Vladimir, both live in the United States. A third son, Aleksandr, died of [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia). His first marriage ended in divorce; Gelfand later remarried. With his second wife, Tatiana, they had a daughter, also named Tatiana. The family also includes four grandchildren and three great-grandchildren.[^o18][^o19] Memories about I. Gelfand are collected at a dedicated website handled by his family.[^o20]

Gelfand was an advocate of [animal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights).[^o21] He became a [vegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarianism) in 1994 and [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism) in 2000.[^o21][^o22]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Gelfand held several honorary degrees and was awarded the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin) three times for his research. In 1977 he was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society). He won the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) in 1978, [Kyoto Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prize) in 1989 and MacArthur Foundation Fellowship in 1994. He held the presidency of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9098251) between 1968 and 1970, and was elected a foreign member of the [U.S. National Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy), the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).

In an October 2003 article in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, written on the occasion of his 90th birthday, Gelfand is described as a scholar who is considered "among the greatest mathematicians of the 20th century",[^o24] having exerted a tremendous influence on the field both through his own works and those of his students.

## Death {#Death}

Gelfand died at the [Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wood_Johnson_University_Hospital) near his home in [Highland Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_New_Jersey). He was less than five weeks past his 96th birthday. His death was first reported on the blog of his former collaborator [Andrei Zelevinsky](wikipedia://en/32415867)[^o26] and confirmed a few hours later by an obituary in the Russian online newspaper *Polit.ru*.[^o27]
