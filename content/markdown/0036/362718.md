# Władysław Orlicz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Władysław Orlicz"}

**Władysław Roman Orlicz** (Polish: w; May 24, 1903 – August 9, 1990) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) linked to the circle of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328). He was a professor at the [University of Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poznań) and a member of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences). From 1977 to 1979, he served as president of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).

:::

He is known for his research in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [vector-valued functions](wikipedia://en/3675281), [orthogonal series](wikipedia://en/71675950) and [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory). Concepts named after him include [Orlicz spaces](wikipedia://en/17686010) and the [Orlicz–Pettis theorem](wikipedia://en/27706836).[^o3]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Orlicz was born on 24 May 1903 in [Okocim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okocim), then part of the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria) within [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), as the third out of five children of Franciszek Orlicz and Maria (Rossknecht).[^o5] His father died when he was four years old. Orlicz attended schools in [Tarnów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnów), [Znojmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Znojmo) (now in [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic)), and [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) (now in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)), where his family settled in 1919. As a student, Orlicz excelled especially in the upper grades. He graduated in 1920 from the State Second Real School in Lwów ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Państwowa Druga Szkoła Realna we Lwowie*) and voluntarily enlisted in the [Polish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Armed_Forces), although he did not take part in any fighting during the [Polish-Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Soviet_War) (1919–2021).[^o6]

Orlicz initially enrolled at the [Lwów Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_Polytechnic) but after a year he changed the university and began studying in the Department of Mathematical and Natural Sciences of the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University) (now the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv)). His professors included [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735) and [Antoni Łomnicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Łomnicki).[^o7] In 1923, still as a student, he started working at the university, first as a demonstrator, and later as a research assistant. In 1925, Orlicz finished his studies at the university but he continued his education attending [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075)'s lectures at the [Lwów Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University_of_Technology).[^o7]

In 1926, at the age of 23, he published his first paper on the theory of summability, which was published in the *[Tohoku Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohoku_Mathematical_Journal)*.[^o6] In 1928, Orlicz earned his doctorate under [Eustachy Żyliński](wikipedia://en/73170532), based on his dissertation entitled *Z teorii szeregów ortogonalnych* (On the Theory of Orthogonal Series).[^o8] Orlicz influenced the young mathematician [Józef Marcinkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Marcinkiewicz) through their collaboration on this subject in the mid-1930s.[^o9] He was known to visit the [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651) and engage in mathematical discussions with many prominent mathematicians of the time. He was the author or co-author of 14 problems included in the *[Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115)*.[^o7]

In 1927–1929 and 1930–1931, Orlicz was employed as a teacher in a military cadet school and a private high school for girls.[^o6] From 1929 to 1930, Orlicz pursued further studies in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen)[^o7] after having received a scholarship from the Ministry of Religious Affairs and Public Education. His teachers included [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born), [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230), and [Harald Bohr](wikipedia://en/638305).[^o6] During his stay in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), Orlicz began working with [Zygmunt Birnbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygmunt_Birnbaum) on the subject of the extension of spaces. This led to Orlicz's subsequent independent research on [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630) that generalize [*L*<sup>p</sup> spaces](wikipedia://en/45194), now known as [Orlicz spaces](wikipedia://en/17686010).[^o7][^o6]

Upon his return to Lwów in 1930, Orlicz received the position of senior assistant in the Faculty of Mathematics of the Lwów Polytechnic. In 1934, he obtained his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) on the basis of his work in the field of [orthogonal series](wikipedia://en/71675950) and the following year, he was promoted to the position of [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at the Lwów Polytechnic.[^o10] In 1937, he became an extraordinary professor at the [University of Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poznań) following a nomination from President [Ignacy Mościcki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Mościcki).[^o6]

*Image: 27 Prospekt Shevchenka, Lviv.jpg.* Building that used to house the original [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651), now in [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv), Ukraine

The outbreak of the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) surprised him while he was on vacation in Lwów. Since he could not return to Poznań, he was appointed professor and continued to lecture at the Faculty of Mathematics of the University of Lwów until June 1941. During the [Nazi occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_%281939–1945%29) of Lwów (1941–1944), he worked at Professor [Rudolf Weigl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Weigl)'s Typhus Research Institute as a [lice feeder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louse-feeder), alongside [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646). This allowed him to avoid German repression and to support himself financially.[^o6]

When the war ended, Orlicz could not remain in Lwów as the [forced resettlement of Poles from the Eastern Borderlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_population_transfers_in_1944–1946) took place. He returned to Poznań, taking over the Chair of Mathematics at the University of Poznań (now [Adam Mickiewicz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz_University)). In 1948, Orlicz was awarded the title of ordinary professor.

In 1956, Orlicz became a corresponding member of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences), advancing to full membership in 1961.[^o11] Orlicz worked at the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/67042302) until 1974 when he retired.[^o7]

Orlicz was as an editor of *Commentationes Mathematicae* (1955–1990), and *[Studia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studia_Mathematica)* (1962–1990). He served as President of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791) from 1977 to 1979.[^o7] He was also a member of the [Warsaw Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Scientific_Society) and the [Polish Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Philosophical_Society).[^o6]

On 23 August 1980, he was among the signatories of the Appeal of the Sixty-Four, joining prominent writers, public intellectuals, and scholars in their effort to persuade the [communist authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism_in_Poland) to engage in dialogue with the [protesting workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Solidarity#Early_strikes_%281980%29).[^o6] He continued to host a seminar "Selected Problems of Functional Analysis" at the University of Poznań until 1989. His doctoral students included [Andrzej Alexiewicz](wikipedia://en/28952583),[^o12] [Feliks Barański](wikipedia://en/6111672),[^o13] [Zbigniew Ciesielski](wikipedia://en/57933971),[^o13] and [Stanisław Knapowski](wikipedia://en/59573298).[^o13] He died on 9 August 1990 in Poznań.[^o7]

## Work {#Work}

Orlicz made significant contributions to such areas of mathematics as [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630), [orthogonal series](wikipedia://en/71675950), unconditional convergence in [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), summability, [vector-valued functions](wikipedia://en/3675281), metric locally convex spaces, [real functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_function), [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and integration, polynomial operators and modular spaces.

One of Orlicz's most well-known contributions to mathematics is the introduction of [Orlicz spaces](wikipedia://en/17686010), a type of [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630) which generalizes [*L*<sup>p</sup> spaces](wikipedia://en/45194).[^o15] They were first formulated in his 1932 paper entitled *Über eine gewisse Klasse von Räumen vom Typus B* and published in *Bulletin International de l’Académie Polonaise des Sciences*.[^o16] [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) himself had already acknowledged in his monograph published the same year that Orlicz was the first one who introduced those spaces.[^o17] Orlicz spaces have found wide application in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234), [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), [constructive function theory](wikipedia://en/32135416), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), and [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711).[^o7]

The [Orlicz-Pettis theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlicz-Pettis_theorem) is a result in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) relating to [convergent series](wikipedia://en/1743431) or, equivalently, [countable additivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable_additivity) of [measures](wikipedia://en/19873) with values in abstract spaces.[^o18][^o19] Together with Julian Musielak, Orlicz introduced the concept in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) known as [weighted BV functions](wikipedia://en/330673#Weighted_BV_functions).

Other eponymous concepts appearing in the mathematical literature include the Orlicz property, Orlicz category theorem, [Mazur-Orlicz theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hahn-Banach_theorem#For_nonlinear_functions), Orlicz interpolation theorem, Musielak-Orlicz space, [Orlicz norm](wikipedia://en/17686010#Orlicz_norm_of_a_random_variable), Hardy-Orlicz spaces, Marcinkiewicz-Orlicz spaces and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.[^o7]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

*Image: Władysław Orlicz tablica Poznań.jpg.* Władysław Orlicz memorial plaque in [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań)

During his scientific career Orlicz received a number of awards and state decorations including [Stefan Banach Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Banach_Prize) of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791) (1948),[^o21] the [Golden Cross of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Merit_%28Poland%29) (1954),[^o22] [Medal of the 10th Anniversary of People's Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_the_10th_Anniversary_of_People's_Poland) (1955),[^o23] the Commander's Cross of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) (1958), Honorary Membership of the Polish Mathematical Society (1973), [Jurzykowski Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurzykowski_Prize) (1973),[^o25] Copernicus Medal of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) (1973), [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) Medal of the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) (1979), Medal of the Commission for National Education (1983) and the Individual State Prizes (2nd degree in 1952,[^o24] 1st degree in 1966).[^o25]

Orlicz participated at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) on four occasions: [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo) (1936), [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) (1958), [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) (1962) and [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) (1983), where he served as honorary president. He authored or co-authored 171 scientific papers and oversaw 39 doctoral dissertations. His influence on the mathematical community in Poznań was immense and he is sometimes described as the founder of the Poznań School of Mathematics.[^o6]

He was the recipient of [honorary doctorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) from [York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_University) in Canada (1974),[^o7] [Poznań University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań_University_of_Technology) (1978)[^o26] and [Adam Mickiewicz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz_University) (1983).

The Orlicz Memorial Conference was held in March, 1991 by the [University of Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mississippi) in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford,_Mississippi), United States. Other conferences in memory of Orlicz were organized in 1998 by the [Adam Mickiewicz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz_University) in [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań), and in 2000 by the Adam Mickiewicz University and the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/67042302) in [Bedlewo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedlewo).[^o7]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Orlicz had four brothers: Kazimierz, Tadeusz, Zbigniew and Michał. Zbigniew died in the [Polish-Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Soviet_War) (1919–1921). Kazimierz, the eldest brother, perished in the [Stutthof concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stutthof_concentration_camp). The remaining brothers became professors: Tadeusz specialized in timber technology while Michał in [climatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatology) and [metereology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metereology).[^o6]

In 1928, Orlicz married Zofia (Krzysik). She was an assistant in the Faculty of Physics at the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University) in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), she served in the [Home Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Army) and was awarded the Order of [Virtuti Militari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtuti_Militari) for her involvement in saving Jewish children from the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust). After the war, she was persecuted by the communist authorities and spent five years in prison (1948–1953) for distributing banned press.[^o6]
