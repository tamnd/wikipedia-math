# Samuel Eilenberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Eilenberg"}

**Samuel Eilenberg** (September 30, 1913 – January 30, 1998) was a [Polish-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who co-founded [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) (with [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574)) and [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

He was born in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), Kingdom of Poland to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) family. He spent much of his career as a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).

He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in 1936, with thesis *On the Topological Applications of Maps onto a Circle*; his thesis advisors were [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) and [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk). He died in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) in January 1998.

## Career {#Career}

Eilenberg's main body of work was in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). He worked on the axiomatic treatment of [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) with [Norman Earl Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Earl_Steenrod) (and the [Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms](wikipedia://en/3122600) are named for the pair), and on [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) with [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574). As a result of this work, Eilenberg and Mac Lane developed the field of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), for which they are now best known.

Eilenberg was a member of [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) and, with [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536), wrote the 1956 book *Homological Algebra*.[^o7]

Later in life he worked mainly in pure category theory, being one of the founders of the field. The [Eilenberg swindle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg_swindle) (or *telescope*) is a construction applying the [telescoping cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescoping_cancellation) idea to [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488).

Eilenberg contributed to [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356) and algebraic automata theory. In particular, he introduced a model of computation called [X-machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-machine) and a new prime decomposition algorithm for finite state machines in the vein of [Krohn–Rhodes theory](wikipedia://en/513506). He also identified a natural correspondence between certain [classes](wikipedia://en/20696) of [regular languages](wikipedia://en/25723) called varieties and [pseudovarieties](wikipedia://en/1021753) of finite monoids, a result which is now known as Eilenberg's theorem.

## Art collection {#Art_collection}

Eilenberg was also a prominent collector of [Asian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_art). His collection mainly consisted of small sculptures and other artifacts from [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia), [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia), [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka) and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia). In 1991–1992, the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art) in New York staged an exhibition from more than 400 items that Eilenberg had donated to the museum, entitled *The Lotus Transcendent: Indian and Southeast Asian Art From the Samuel Eilenberg Collection*.[^o9][^o10] In reciprocity, the Metropolitan Museum of Art donated substantially to the endowment of the Samuel Eilenberg Visiting Professorship in Mathematics at Columbia University.[^o11][^o12]
