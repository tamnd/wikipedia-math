# Projective module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Projective module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of **projective modules** enlarges the class of [free modules](wikipedia://en/326454) (that is, [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) with [basis vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_vector)) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), keeping some of the main properties of free modules. Various equivalent characterizations of these modules appear below.

:::

Every free module is a projective module, but the [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) fails to hold over some rings, such as [Dedekind rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_ring) that are not [principal ideal domains](wikipedia://en/25039). However, every projective module is a free module if the ring is a principal ideal domain such as the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), or a (multivariate) [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) (this is the [Quillen–Suslin theorem](wikipedia://en/539562)).

Projective modules were first introduced in 1956 in the influential book *Homological Algebra* by [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) and [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Lifting property {#Lifting_property}

The usual [category theoretical](wikipedia://en/5869) definition is in terms of the property of [*lifting*](wikipedia://en/53659730) that carries over from free to projective modules: a module *P* is projective [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) for every [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) [module homomorphism](wikipedia://en/474118) *f* : *N* ↠ *M* and every module homomorphism *g* : *P* → *M*, there exists a module homomorphism *h* : *P* → *N* such that *fh* = *g*. (We don't require the lifting homomorphism *h* to be unique; this is not a [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245).)

*Image: Projective-module-P.svg.*

The advantage of this definition of "projective" is that it can be carried out in [categories](wikipedia://en/53759) more general than [module categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_categories): we don't need a notion of "free object". It can also be [dualized](wikipedia://en/310885), leading to [injective modules](wikipedia://en/473775). The lifting property may also be rephrased as *every morphism from $P$ to $M$ factors through every epimorphism to $M$*. Thus, by definition, projective modules are precisely the [projective objects](wikipedia://en/3017382) in the [category of *R*-modules](wikipedia://en/11231812).

### Split-exact sequences {#Split-exact_sequences}

A module *P* is projective if and only if every [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of modules of the form

$$0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0$$

is a [split exact sequence](wikipedia://en/1531404). That is, for every surjective module homomorphism *f* : *B* ↠ *P* there exists a **section map**, that is, a module homomorphism *h* : *P* → *B* such that *fh* = id<sub>*P*</sub>. In that case, *h*(*P*) is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of *B*, *h* is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) from *P* to *h*(*P*), and *hf* is a [projection](wikipedia://en/519182) on the summand *h*(*P*). Equivalently,

$$B = \operatorname{Im}(h) \oplus \operatorname{Ker}(f) \ \ \text{ where } \operatorname{Ker}(f) \cong A\ \text{ and } \operatorname{Im}(h) \cong P.$$

### Direct summands of free modules {#Direct_summands_of_free_modules}

A module *P* is projective if and only if there is another module *Q* such that the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) of *P* and *Q* is a free module.

### Exactness {#Exactness}

An *R*-module *P* is projective if and only if the covariant [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) Hom(*P*, -): *R*-**Mod** → **Ab** is an [exact functor](wikipedia://en/453945), where *R*-**Mod** is the category of left *R*-modules and **Ab** is the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748). When the ring *R* is [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346), **Ab** is advantageously replaced by *R*-**Mod** in the preceding characterization. This functor is always [left exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_exact_functor), but, when *P* is projective, it is also right exact. This means that *P* is projective if and only if this functor preserves [epimorphisms](wikipedia://en/59539) (surjective homomorphisms), or if it preserves finite [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit).

### Dual basis {#Dual_basis}

A module *P* is projective if and only if there exists a set $\{a_i \in P \mid i \in I\}$ and a set $\{f_i\in \mathrm{Hom}(P,R) \mid i\in I\}$ such that for every *x* in *P*, *f*<sub>*i*</sub>(*x*) is only nonzero for finitely many *i*, and $x=\sum f_i(x)a_i$.

:::

## Elementary examples and properties {#Elementary_examples_and_properties}

The following properties of projective modules are quickly deduced from any of the above (equivalent) definitions of projective modules:

- Direct sums and direct summands of projective modules are projective.
- If $e = e^{2}$ is an [idempotent](wikipedia://en/361940) in the ring $R$, then $Re$ is a projective left module over *R*.

Let $R = R_1 \times R_2$ be the [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) of two rings $R_1$ and $R_2,$ which is a ring with operations defined componentwise. Let $e_1=(1,0)$ and $e_2=(0,1).$ Then $e_1$ and $e_2$ are idempotents, and belong to the [centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_of_a_ring) of $R.$ The [two-sided ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-sided_ideal) $Re_1$ and $Re_2$ are projective modules, since their direct sum (as $R$-modules) equals the free $R$-module $R$. However, if $R_1$ and $R_2$ are nontrivial, then they are not free as modules over $R$. For instance $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is projective but not free over $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.

## Relation to other module-theoretic properties {#Relation_to_other_module-theoretic_properties}

The relation of projective modules to free and [flat](wikipedia://en/492616) modules is subsumed in the following diagram of module properties:

*Image: Module properties in commutative algebra.svg.* Module properties in commutative algebra

The left-to-right implications are true over any ring, although some authors define [torsion-free modules](wikipedia://en/38400572) only over a [domain](wikipedia://en/487627). The right-to-left implications are true over the rings labeling them. There may be other rings over which they are true. For example, the implication labeled "[local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) or PID" is also true for (multivariate) polynomial rings over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603): this is the [Quillen–Suslin theorem](wikipedia://en/539562).

### Projective vs. free modules {#Projective_vs._free_modules}

Any free module is projective. The converse is true in the following cases:

- if *R* is a field or [skew field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew_field): *any* module is free in this case.
- if the ring *R* is a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039). For example, this applies to *R* = **Z** (the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563)), so an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) is projective if and only if it is a [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296). The reason is that any [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of a free module over a principal ideal domain is free.
- if the ring *R* is a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391). This fact is the basis of the intuition of "locally free = projective". This fact is easy to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) for [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) projective modules. In general, it is due to (Kaplansky 1958); see [Kaplansky's theorem on projective modules](wikipedia://en/62610482).

In general though, projective modules need not be free:

- Over a [direct product of rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_product_of_rings) *R* × *S* where *R* and *S* are [nonzero](wikipedia://en/1872874) rings, both *R* × 0 and 0 × *S* are non-free projective modules.
- Over a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) a non-[principal](wikipedia://en/145126) [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) is always a projective module that is not a free module.
- Over a [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) M<sub>*n*</sub>(*R*), the natural module *R*<sup>*n*</sup> is projective but is not free when *n* > 1.
- Over a [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring), *every* module is projective, but a nonzero proper left (or right) ideal is not a free module. Thus the only semisimple rings for which all projectives are free are [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067).

The difference between free and projective modules is, in a sense, measured by the [algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *K*<sub>0</sub>(*R*); see below.

### Projective vs. flat modules {#Projective_vs._flat_modules}

Every projective module is [flat](wikipedia://en/492616).[^o14] The converse is in general not true: the abelian group **Q** is a **Z**-module that is flat, but not projective.[^o15]

Conversely, a [finitely related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_related_module) flat module is projective.[^o16]

(Govorov 1965) and (Lazard 1969) proved that a module *M* is flat if and only if it is a [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of [finitely-generated](wikipedia://en/326430) [free modules](wikipedia://en/326454).

In general, the precise relation between flatness and projectivity was established by (Raynaud Gruson 1971) (see also (Drinfeld 2006) and (Braunling Groechenig Wolfson 2016)) who showed that a module *M* is projective if and only if it satisfies the following conditions:

- *M* is flat,
- *M* is a direct sum of [countably](wikipedia://en/6026) generated modules,
- *M* satisfies a certain [Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/1438792)-type condition.

This characterization can be used to show that if $R \to S$ is a [faithfully flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithfully_flat_morphism) map of commutative rings and $M$ is an $R$-module, then $M$ is projective if and only if $M \otimes_R S$ is projective.[^o22] In other words, the property of being projective satisfies [faithfully flat descent](wikipedia://en/46606018).

## The category of projective modules {#The_category_of_projective_modules}

Submodules of projective modules need not be projective; a ring *R* for which every submodule of a projective left module is projective is called [left hereditary](wikipedia://en/5123176).

[Quotients](wikipedia://en/364820) of projective modules also need not be projective, for example **Z**/*n* is a quotient of **Z**, but not [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/38400572), hence not flat, and therefore not projective.

The category of finitely generated projective modules over a ring is an [exact category](wikipedia://en/10987985). (See also [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500)).

## Projective resolutions {#Projective_resolutions}

Given a module, *M*, a **projective [resolution](wikipedia://en/19745659)** of *M* is an infinite [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) of modules

⋅⋅⋅ → *P*<sub>*n*</sub> → ⋅⋅⋅ → *P*<sub>2</sub> → *P*<sub>1</sub> → *P*<sub>0</sub> → *M* → 0,

with all the *P*<sub>*i*</sub> s projective. Every module possesses a projective resolution. In fact a **free resolution** (resolution by free modules) exists. The exact sequence of projective modules may sometimes be abbreviated to *P*(*M*) → *M* → 0 or *P*<sub>•</sub> → *M* → 0. A classic example of a projective resolution is given by the [Koszul complex](wikipedia://en/519992) of a [regular sequence](wikipedia://en/505119), which is a free resolution of the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the sequence.

The *length* of a finite resolution is the index *n* such that *P*<sub>*n*</sub> is [nonzero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_module) and *P*<sub>*i*</sub> = 0 for *i* greater than *n*. If *M* admits a finite projective resolution, the minimal length among all finite projective resolutions of *M* is called its **projective dimension** and denoted pd(*M*). If *M* does not admit a finite projective resolution, then by convention the projective dimension is said to be infinite. As an example, consider a module *M* such that pd(*M*) = 0. In this situation, the exactness of the sequence 0 → *P*<sub>0</sub> → *M* → 0 indicates that the arrow in the center is an isomorphism, and hence *M* itself is projective.

:::

## Projective modules over commutative rings {#Projective_modules_over_commutative_rings}

Projective modules over [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) have nice properties.

The [localization](wikipedia://en/252216) of a projective module is a projective module over the localized ring. A projective module over a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) is free. Thus a projective module is *locally free* (in the sense that its localization at every [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) is free over the corresponding localization of the ring). The converse is true for [finitely generated modules](wikipedia://en/326430) over [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129): a finitely generated module over a commutative Noetherian ring is locally free if and only if it is projective.

However, there are examples of finitely generated modules over a non-Noetherian ring that are locally free and not projective. For instance, a [Boolean ring](wikipedia://en/54356) has all of its localizations isomorphic to **F**<sub>2</sub>, the field of two elements, so any module over a Boolean ring is locally free, but there are some non-projective modules over Boolean rings. One example is *R*/*I* where *R* is a direct product of countably many copies of **F**<sub>2</sub> and *I* is the direct sum of countably many copies of **F**<sub>2</sub> inside of *R*. The *R*-module *R*/*I* is locally free since *R* is Boolean (and it is finitely generated as an *R*-module too, with a spanning set of size 1), but *R*/*I* is not projective because *I* is not a principal ideal. (If a quotient module *R*/*I*, for any commutative ring *R* and ideal *I*, is a projective *R*-module then *I* is principal.)

However, it is true that for [finitely presented modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_module) *M* over a commutative ring *R* (in particular if *M* is a finitely generated *R*-module and *R* is Noetherian), the following are equivalent.[^o27]

1. $M$ is flat.
1. $M$ is projective.
1. $M_\mathfrak{m}$ is free as $R_\mathfrak{m}$-module for every [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) $\mathfrak{m}$ of *R*.
1. $M_\mathfrak{p}$ is free as $R_\mathfrak{p}$-module for every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of *R*.
1. There exist $f_1,\ldots,f_n \in R$ generating the [unit ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ideal) such that $M[f_i^{-1}]$ is free as $R[f_i^{-1}]$-module for each *i*.
1. $\widetilde{M}$ is a [locally free sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaf) on the [affine scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) $\operatorname{Spec}R$ (where $\widetilde{M}$ is the [sheaf associated to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_associated_to_a_module) *M*.)

Moreover, if *R* is a Noetherian [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), then, by [Nakayama's lemma](wikipedia://en/1835001), these conditions are equivalent to

- The [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) of the $k(\mathfrak{p})$-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $M \otimes_R k(\mathfrak{p})$ is the same for all prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}$ of *R,* where $k(\mathfrak{p})$ is the residue field at $\mathfrak{p}$.[^o28] That is to say, *M* has constant rank (as defined below).

Let *A* be a commutative ring. If *B* is a (possibly non-commutative) *A*-[algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring) that is a finitely generated projective *A*-module containing *A* as a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145), then *A* is a direct factor of *B*.[^o29]

### Rank {#Rank}

Let *P* be a finitely generated projective module over a commutative ring *R* and *X* be the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of *R*. The *rank* of *P* at a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ in *X* is the rank of the free $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module $P_{\mathfrak{p}}$. It is a locally constant function on *X*. In particular, if *X* is connected (that is if *R* has no other idempotents than 0 and 1), then *P* has constant rank.

## Vector bundles and locally free modules {#Vector_bundles_and_locally_free_modules}

A basic motivation of the theory is that projective modules (at least over certain commutative rings) are analogues of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524). This can be made precise for the ring of [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued functions on a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637), as well as for the ring of [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) on a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) (see [Serre–Swan theorem](wikipedia://en/363540) that says a finitely generated projective module over the space of smooth functions on a compact manifold is the space of smooth sections of a [smooth vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_vector_bundle)).

Vector bundles are *locally free*. If there is some notion of "localization" that can be carried over to modules, such as the usual [localization of a ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring), one can define locally free modules, and the projective modules then typically coincide with the locally free modules.

## Projective modules over a polynomial ring {#Projective_modules_over_a_polynomial_ring}

The [Quillen–Suslin theorem](wikipedia://en/539562), which solves Serre's problem, is another [deep result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_result): if *K* is a field, or more generally a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039), and *R* = *K*\[*X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X*<sub>*n*</sub>\] is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) over *K*, then every projective module over *R* is free. This problem was first raised by Serre with *K* a field (and the modules being finitely generated). [Bass](wikipedia://en/7085910) settled it for non-finitely generated modules,[^o33] and [Quillen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Quillen) and [Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820) independently and simultaneously treated the case of finitely generated modules.

Since every projective module over a principal ideal domain is free, one might ask this question: if *R* is a commutative ring such that every (finitely generated) projective *R*-module is free, then is every (finitely generated) projective *R*\[*X*\]-module free? The answer is *no*. A [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) occurs with *R* equal to the local ring of the curve *y*<sup>2</sup> = *x*<sup>3</sup> at the origin. Thus the Quillen–Suslin theorem could never be proved by a simple [induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on the number of variables.
