# 23 (number)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="23"}

**23** (**twenty-three**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [22](wikipedia://en/362193) and preceding [24](wikipedia://en/362201). It is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666).

:::

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

### Prime number properties {#Prime_number_properties}

**Twenty-three** is the ninth [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) and the smallest odd prime that is not a [twin prime](wikipedia://en/41997). It forms a [cousin prime](wikipedia://en/343085) pair with [19](wikipedia://en/208292) and a [sexy prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexy_prime) pair with both [17](wikipedia://en/208288) and [29](wikipedia://en/362212). It is the largest member of the first [prime sextuplet](wikipedia://en/812561#Prime_sextuplets) (7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23). Twenty-three is also the next to last member of the first [Cunningham chain](wikipedia://en/321790) of the first kind ([2](wikipedia://en/64516), [5](wikipedia://en/6412297), 11, 23, [47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47_%28number%29)).[^o7] It is the sum of the [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) of the second set of consecutive [discrete semiprimes](wikipedia://en/339542#Examples_and_variations), ([21](wikipedia://en/209248), [22](wikipedia://en/362193)). 23 is the smallest odd prime to be a [highly cototient number](wikipedia://en/1372255), as the solution to $x-\phi(x)$ for the integers [95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95_%28number%29), [119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/119_%28number%29), [143](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/143_%28number%29), and [529](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/529_%28number%29).

23 is the second [Smarandache–Wellin prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smarandache–Wellin_prime) in [base](wikipedia://en/701207) ten, as it is the concatenation of the decimal representations of the first two primes (2 and 3) and is itself also prime, It is the fifth [factorial prime](wikipedia://en/409293), and since 14! + 1 is a multiple of 23, but 23 is not one more than a multiple of 14, 23 is the first [Pillai prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillai_prime). 23 is the second [Woodall prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodall_prime), and an [Eisenstein prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_prime) with no [imaginary part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_part) and [real part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part) of the form $3n-1.$ It is the fifth [Sophie Germain prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Germain_prime) and the fourth [safe prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_prime).

23 is the first prime *p* for which unique factorization of [cyclotomic integers](wikipedia://en/30872462) based on the *p*th root of unity breaks down.[^o15] 23 is the smallest [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ such that the largest consecutive pair of $p$-[smooth numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_numbers) (11859210, 11859211) is the same as the largest consecutive pair of $(p-1)$-smooth numbers.

The sum of the first nine primes up to 23 is a [square](wikipedia://en/213919): $2 + 3 + \dots + 23 = 100 = 10^{2}$ and the sum of the first 23 primes is [874](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/874_%28number%29), which is divisible by 23, a property shared by few other numbers.[^o18]

The first twenty-three odd prime numbers (between [3](wikipedia://en/208157) and [89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89_%28number%29) inclusive), are all [cluster primes](wikipedia://en/69356423) $p$ such that every even positive integer $k \le p - 3$ can be written as the sum of two prime numbers that do not exceed $p$.

The twenty-third [permutable prime](wikipedia://en/421593) in decimal $R_{19}$ is also the second to be a [prime repunit](wikipedia://en/215056#Repunit_primes) (after $R_{2}$), followed by $R_{23}$ and $R_{1031}$.[^o20]

In the list of [fortunate numbers](wikipedia://en/13040642), 23 occurs twice, since adding 23 to either the fifth or eighth [primorial](wikipedia://en/422786) gives a prime number (namely 2333 and 9699713).

### Other mathematical properties {#Other_mathematical_properties}

The twenty-third [highly composite number](wikipedia://en/208732) 20,160 is one less than the last number (the 339th [super-prime](wikipedia://en/13398615) 20,161) that cannot be expressed as the sum of two [abundant numbers](wikipedia://en/321831). Otherwise, $46 = 23 \times 2$ is the largest [even](wikipedia://en/143135) number that is not the sum of two abundant numbers.

23 has the distinction of being one of two integers that cannot be expressed as the sum of fewer than 9 cubes of positive integers (the other is [239](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/239_%28number%29)). See [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569).

There are 23 [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) on 8 unlabeled nodes.[^o28] It is also a [Wedderburn–Etherington number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedderburn–Etherington_number), which are numbers that can be used to count certain [binary trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_trees).

The [natural logarithms](wikipedia://en/21476) of all [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) lower than 23 are known to have binary [BBP-type formulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBP-type_formula).[^o30]

23 is a [happy number](wikipedia://en/542994).

23 is the smallest positive solution to [Sunzi](wikipedia://en/30291690)'s original formulation of the [Chinese remainder theorem](wikipedia://en/7713).

According to the [birthday paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthday_paradox), in a group of 23 or more randomly chosen people, the probability is more than 50% that some pair of them will have the same birthday.[^o32]

A related coincidence is that [365](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/365_%28number%29) times the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of 2, approximately 252.999, is very close to the number of pairs of 23 items and 22nd [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915), [253](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/253_%28number%29).

23 is the smallest [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) of imaginary quadratic fields with class number 3 (negated), and it is the smallest discriminant of [complex cubic fields](wikipedia://en/22779719#Cubic_fields) (also negated).

[Hilbert's problems](wikipedia://en/154584) are twenty-three problems in mathematics published by German mathematician David Hilbert in 1900.

### Mersenne numbers {#Mersenne_numbers}

The first [Mersenne number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_Prime#Factorization_of_composite_Mersenne_numbers) of the form $2^{n} - 1$ that does not yield a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) when inputting a prime [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent) is $2047 = 23 \times 89,$ with $n=11.$

On the other hand, the second [composite](wikipedia://en/82289) Mersenne number contains an exponent $n$ of twenty-three:

$$M_{23} = 2^{23} - 1 = 8\;388\;607 = 47 \times 178\;481$$

The twenty-third prime number ([83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/83_%28number%29)) is an exponent to the fourteenth composite Mersenne number, which factorizes into two prime numbers, the largest of which is twenty-three digits long when written in [base ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_ten):

$$M_{83} = 967...407 = 167 \times 57\;912\;614\;113\;275\;649\;087\;721$$

Further down in this sequence, the seventeenth and eighteenth composite Mersenne numbers have two prime factors each as well, where the largest of these are respectively twenty-two and twenty-four digits long,

$$\begin{aligned} M_{103} & = 101 \ldots 007 = 2\;550\;183\;799 \times 3\;976\;656\;429\;941\;438\;590\;393 \\ M_{109} & = 649 \ldots 511 = 745\;988\;807 \times 870\;035\;986\;098\;720\;987\;332\;873 \\ \end{aligned}$$

Where prime exponents for $M_{23}$ and $M_{83}$ add to [106](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/106_%28number%29), which lies in between prime exponents of $M_{103}$ and $M_{109}$, the index of the latter two ([17](wikipedia://en/208288) and [18](wikipedia://en/208294)) in the sequence of Mersenne numbers sum to [35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_%28number%29), which is the twenty-third composite number.

$23!$ is twenty-three [digits](wikipedia://en/280582) long in decimal, and there are only three other numbers $n$ whose [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606) generate numbers that are $n$ digits long in base ten: [1](wikipedia://en/77591639), [22](wikipedia://en/362193), and [24](wikipedia://en/362201).

### In geometry {#In_geometry}

The **[Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919)** **Λ<sub>24</sub>** is a 24-dimensional [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) through which 23 other [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_bilinear_form) even [unimodular](wikipedia://en/1648605) [Niemeier lattices](wikipedia://en/1652072) of [rank](wikipedia://en/244541) 24 are built, and vice-versa. **Λ<sub>24</sub>** represents the solution to the [kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555) in 24 dimensions as the precise lattice structure for the maximum number of [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) that can fill 24-dimensional space without overlapping, equal to 196,560 spheres. These 23 Niemeier lattices are located at *deep holes* of [radii](wikipedia://en/1780815) $\sqrt{2}$ in lattice points around its automorphism group, [Conway group](wikipedia://en/345511) $\mathbb C_{0}$. The Leech lattice can be constructed in various ways, which include:

- By means of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) of the form $\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix} Ia&H/2\\H/2&Ib\end{pmatrix}$ where $I$ is the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) and $H$ is a 24 by 24 [Hadamard matrix](wikipedia://en/507553) (**Z**/23**Z** ∪ ∞) with *a* = 2 and *b* = 3, and [entries](wikipedia://en/20556859#Definition) X(∞) = 1 and X(0) = -1 with X(*n*) the [quadratic residue](wikipedia://en/200091) symbol [mod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulo_operation) 23 for nonzero *n*.
- Through the [extended binary Golay code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_binary_Golay_code) $\mathbb B_{24}$ and [Witt design $\mathbb W_{24}$](wikipedia://en/28016#The_Steiner_system_S%285,_8,_24%29), which produce a construction of the 196,560 minimal [vectors](wikipedia://en/149968) in the Leech lattice. The extended binary Golay code is an extension of the [perfect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_code) [binary Golay code](wikipedia://en/344971) $\mathbb B_{23}$, which has codewords of [size](wikipedia://en/1134659#The_alphabet_Σ) 23. $\mathbb B_{23}$ has [Mathieu group $\mathbb M_{23}$](wikipedia://en/15200701) as its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772), which is the second largest member of the [first generation](wikipedia://en/324806#Happy_Family) in the [happy family](wikipedia://en/324806#Happy_Family) of [sporadic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_groups). $\mathbb M_{23}$ has a minimum [faithful complex representation](wikipedia://en/2617872) in 22 [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398) and [group-3 actions](wikipedia://en/12781) on [253](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/253_%28number%29) [objects](wikipedia://en/53507), with 253 equal to the number of pairs of objects in a set of 23 objects. In turn, $\mathbb M_{23}$ is the automorphism group of [Mathieu group $\mathbb M_{24}$](wikipedia://en/15200706), which works through $\mathbb W_{24}$ to generate **8**-element *octads* whose individual elements occur 253 times through its entire [block design](wikipedia://en/1188184).
- Using Niemer lattice **D**<sub>24</sub> of [group order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_order) 2<sup>23</sup>·24! and [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number) 46 = 2·23, it can be made into a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$, whereby multiplying **D**<sub>24</sub> by a [non-principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126#Examples_of_non-principle_ideals) of the ring of integers yields the Leech lattice.

[Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Conway) and [Sloane](wikipedia://en/400062) provided constructions of the Leech lattice from all other 23 Niemeier lattices.[^o41]

Twenty-three four-dimensional [crystal families](wikipedia://en/456410#Crystal_family) exist within the classification of [space groups](wikipedia://en/463721#In_other_dimensions). These are accompanied by six [enantiomorphic](wikipedia://en/1170160) forms, maximizing the total count to [twenty-nine](wikipedia://en/362212) crystal families. Five [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285) can be arranged to form twenty-three [free pentacubes](wikipedia://en/710426#Enumerating_polycubes), or twenty-nine distinct *[one-sided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycubes#Enumerating_polycubes)* pentacubes (with reflections).

There are 23 three-dimensional [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223) that are [cell facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) inside [uniform 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/1645899) that are not part of infinite families of [antiprismatic prisms](wikipedia://en/8813490) and [duoprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duoprisms): the five [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), the thirteen [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847), and five semiregular [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) (the [triangular](wikipedia://en/2859584), [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/2859659), [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/2859668), [octagonal](wikipedia://en/3026569), and [decagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decagonal_prism) prisms).

23 [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) of [paracompact hyperbolic honeycombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact_honeycomb) in the [third dimension](wikipedia://en/3054853) generate [151](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/151_%28number%29) unique [Wythoffian](wikipedia://en/2950777) constructions of paracompact honeycombs. 23 four-dimensional Euclidean honeycombs are generated from the [${\tilde{B}}_4$ cubic group](wikipedia://en/14415338#Regular_and_uniform_honeycombs), and 23 five-dimensional [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/14415338#Enumerating_the_convex_uniform_5-polytopes) are generated from the [$\mathrm D_{5}$ demihypercubic group](wikipedia://en/31337817).

In [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) geometry, the regular 23-sided [icositrigon](wikipedia://en/54460134) is the first regular polygon that is not constructible with a [compass and straight edge](wikipedia://en/523430#Compass_and_straightedge_constructions) or with the aide of an [angle trisector](wikipedia://en/91111) (since it is neither a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime) nor a [Pierpont prime](wikipedia://en/2595060)), nor by [neusis](wikipedia://en/3752339) or a double-notched straight edge.[^o45] It is also not constructible with [origami](wikipedia://en/232840#Constructions), however it is through other traditional methods for all regular polygons.[^o46]

## In religion {#In_religion}

- In [Biblical numerology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_numerology), it is associated with [Psalm 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_23), also known as the Shepherd Psalm. It is possibly the most quoted and best known Psalm.[^o48][^o49]
- *[Principia Discordia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principia_Discordia)*, the sacred text of [Discordianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discordianism), holds that 23 (along with the discordian prime [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_%28number%29)) is one of the sacred numbers of [Eris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eris_%28mythology%29), goddess of discord.

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

### Film and television {#Film_and_television}

- In the TV series *[Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_%282004_TV_series%29)*, 23 is one of the [6 reoccurring numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythology_of_Lost#The_Numbers) (4, 8, 15, 16, 23, 42) that appear frequently throughout the show.

### Other fields {#Other_fields}

- [23 skidoo (phrase)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23_skidoo_%28phrase%29) (sometimes 23 skiddoo) is an American slang phrase popularized during the early 20th century. 23 skidoo has been described as "perhaps the first truly national fad expression and one of the most popular fad expressions to appear in the U.S".
- The [23 enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23_enigma), proposed by [William S. Burroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs), plays a prominent role in the plot of *[the Illuminatus! Trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Illuminatus!_Trilogy)* by [Robert Shea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Shea) and [Robert Anton Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Anton_Wilson).
  - *[The Number 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Number_23)* is a 2007 film starring [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey) about a man who becomes obsessed with the [23 enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23_enigma).[^o53]
  - The number 23 is used a lot throughout the visuals and music by the band [Gorillaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorillaz), who have even devoted a whole page of their autobiography *[Rise Of The Ogre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rise_Of_The_Ogre)* to the [23 enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23_enigma) theory.
- Human cells have 23 pairs of chromosomes (22 pairs of autosomes and one pair of sex chromosomes). [^o54]
