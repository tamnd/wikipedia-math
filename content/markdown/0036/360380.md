# Sarah Flannery

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sarah Flannery"}

**Sarah Flannery** (born 1982, [County Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cork), Ireland) is an Irish mathematician who was, at sixteen years old, the winner of the 1999 [Esat Young Scientist Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Scientist_and_Technology_Exhibition) for her development of the [Cayley–Purser algorithm](wikipedia://en/1016345), based on work she had done with researchers at [Baltimore Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Technologies) during a brief internship there.[^o2] The project, entitled "Cryptography – A new algorithm versus the [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29)", also won her the [EU Young Scientist of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Contest_for_Young_Scientists) in 1999.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Flannery's education included a primary all-girls school and secondary education at Scoil Mhuire Gan Smál in Blarney.[^o8]

Following the competition win, in 2001 Flannery co-authored *In Code* with her father,[^o5] mathematician David Flannery (1952-2023).[^o6] It tells the story of the making and breaking of the Cayley-Purser algorithm, as well as the enjoyment she got from solving mathematical puzzles while growing up. She dedicates many of her accomplishments in the fields of mathematics and cryptography to her father's support during her childhood.

She studied [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at [Peterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse), a college of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), graduating in 2003, and, as of 2006, worked for [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts) as a software engineer. She worked at [TirNua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TirNua) as a "Chief Scientist".[^o7] She developed the virtual economy in a game and the back-end web services that powered the game features. She has also worked at RockYou, and several other institutions involved in software development and computer science.[^o8]

Before working at TirNua, Flannery was software engineer working directly with then Electronic Arts Worldwide Chief Technology Officer, Scott Cronce, and, later, with many fellow TirNua founders on her first virtual world.[^o9]

At EA, she successfully set up the EA Open Source program using the Essential Project. Flannery created data visualizations on software architecture and game content creation which were used to directly impact the quality of both. She also successfully ran and turned around the virtual economy within EA-Land (formerly [The Sims Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims_Online)).

Previously, she worked on the technical and scientific computing software product [Mathematica](wikipedia://en/49024) for [Wolfram Research](wikipedia://en/594109).[^o10]

The lights on St. Patrick's Street, one of the main thoroughfares of Flannery's home city of Cork, are named after her.

Flannery is the sister of the singer and songwriter [Mick Flannery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Flannery).[^o11]
