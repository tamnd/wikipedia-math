# Frame bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frame bundle"}

*Image: Mobius frame bundle.png.* The orthonormal frame bundle $\mathcal{F_O}(E)$ of the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) $E$ is a non-trivial principal $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-bundle over the circle.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **frame bundle** is a [principal fiber bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_fiber_bundle) $F(E)$ associated with any [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) *$E$*. The fiber of $F(E)$ over a point *$x$* is the set of all [ordered bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_basis), or *frames*, for *$E_x$*. The [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) acts naturally on $F(E)$ via a [change of basis](wikipedia://en/1179451), giving the frame bundle the structure of a principal *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$*-bundle (where *k* is the rank of *$E$*).

:::

The frame bundle of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) is the one associated with its [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794). For this reason it is sometimes called the **tangent frame bundle**.

## Definition and construction {#Definition_and_construction}

Let *$E \to X$* be a real [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) of rank *$k$* over a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *$X$*. A **frame** at a point *$x \in X$* is an [ordered basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_basis) for the vector space *$E_x$*. Equivalently, a frame can be viewed as a [linear isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_isomorphism)

$$p : \mathbf{R}^k \to E_x.$$

The set of all frames at *$x$*, denoted *$F_x$*, has a natural [right action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) by the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* of invertible *$k \times k$* matrices: a group element *$g \in \mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* acts on the frame *$p$* via [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) to give a new frame

$$p\circ g:\mathbf{R}^k\to E_x.$$

This action of *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* on *$F_x$* is both [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_action) and [transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_action) (this follows from the standard linear algebra result that there is a unique invertible linear transformation sending one basis onto another). As a topological space, *$F_x$* is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* although it lacks a group structure, since there is no "preferred frame". The space *$F_x$* is said to be a *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$*-[torsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor).

The **frame bundle** of *$E$*, denoted by $F(E)$ or $F_{\mathrm{GL}}(E)$, is the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of all the *$F_x$*:

$$\mathrm F(E) = \coprod_{x\in X}F_x.$$

Each point in $F(E)$ is a pair *$(x,p)$*, where *$x$* is a point in *$X$* and *$p$* is a frame at *$x$*. There is a natural projection $\pi: F(E)\to X$ which sends ***$(x,p)$*** to *$x$*. The group *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* acts on $F(E)$ on the right as above. This action is clearly free and the [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) are just the fibers of ***$\pi$***.

### Principal bundle structure {#Principal_bundle_structure}

The frame bundle $F(E)$ can be given a natural topology and bundle structure determined by that of *$E$*. Let ***$(U_i,\phi_i)$*** be a [local trivialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_trivialization) of *$E$*. Then for each *$x \in U_i$* one has a linear isomorphism ***$\phi_{i,x}: E_x \to \mathbb{R}^k$***. This data determines a bijection

$$\psi_i : \pi^{-1}(U_i)\to U_i\times \mathrm{GL}(k, \mathbb{R})$$

given by

$$\psi_i(x,p) = (x,\phi_{i,x}\circ p).$$

With these bijections, each ***$\pi^{-1}(U_i)$*** can be given the topology of *$U_i \times \mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$*. The topology on $F(E)$ is the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736) coinduced by the inclusion maps ***$\pi^{-1}(U_i) \to F(E)$***.

With all of the above data the frame bundle $F(E)$ becomes a [principal fiber bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_fiber_bundle) over *$X$* with [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group) *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* and local trivializations ***$(\{U_i\},\{\psi_i\})$***. One can check that the [transition functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_map) of $F(E)$ are the same as those of *$E$*.

The above all works in the smooth category as well: if *$E$* is a smooth vector bundle over a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *$M$* then the frame bundle of *$E$* can be given the structure of a smooth principal bundle over *$M$*.

## Associated vector bundles {#Associated_vector_bundles}

A vector bundle *$E$* and its frame bundle $F(E)$ are [associated bundles](wikipedia://en/398874). Each one determines the other. The frame bundle $F(E)$ can be constructed from *$E$* as above, or more abstractly using the [fiber bundle construction theorem](wikipedia://en/7762521). With the latter method, $F(E)$ is the fiber bundle with same base, structure group, trivializing neighborhoods, and transition functions as *$E$* but with abstract fiber *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$*, where the action of structure group *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* on the fiber *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* is that of left multiplication.

Given any [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) *$\rho: \mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R}) \to \mathrm{GL}(V,\mathbb{F})$* there is a vector bundle

$$\mathrm F(E)\times_{\rho}V$$

associated with $F(E)$ which is given by product $F(E) \times V$ modulo the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) ***$(pg,v) \sim (p, \rho(g)v)$*** for all *$g$* in *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$*. Denote the equivalence classes by ***$[p,v]$***.

The vector bundle *$E$* is [naturally isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturally_isomorphic) to the bundle $F(E) \times_\rho \mathbb{R}^k$ where ***$\rho$*** is the defining representation of *$\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})$* on ***$\mathbb{R}^k$***. The isomorphism is given by

$$[p,v]\mapsto p(v)$$

where ***$v$*** is a vector in ***$\mathbb{R}^k$*** and ***$p: \mathbb{R}^k \to E_x$*** is a frame at *$x$*. One can easily check that this map is [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined).

Any vector bundle associated with *$E$* can be given by the above construction. For example, the [dual bundle](wikipedia://en/7824691) of *$E$* is given by $F(E) \times_{\rho^*} (\mathbb{R}^k)^*$ where $\rho^*$ is the [dual](wikipedia://en/1442471) of the fundamental representation. [Tensor bundles](wikipedia://en/4060446) of *$E$* can be constructed in a similar manner.

## Tangent frame bundle {#Tangent_frame_bundle}

The **tangent frame bundle** (or simply the **frame bundle**) of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *$M$* is the frame bundle associated with the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of *$M$*. The frame bundle of *$M$* is often denoted *$FM$* or *$\mathrm{GL}(M)$* rather than *$F(TM)$*. In physics, it is sometimes denoted *$LM$*. If *$M$* is *$n$*-dimensional then the tangent bundle has rank *$n$*, so the frame bundle of *$M$* is a principal *$\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$* bundle over *$M$*.

### Smooth frames {#Smooth_frames}

[Local sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of the frame bundle of *$M$* are called [smooth frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_frame) on *$M$*. The cross-section theorem for principal bundles states that the frame bundle is trivial over any open set in *$U$* in *$M$* which admits a smooth frame. Given a smooth frame *$s: U \to FU$*, the trivialization *$\psi: FU \to U \times \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$* is given by

$$\psi(p) = (x, s(x)^{-1}\circ p)$$

where *$p$* is a frame at *$x$*. It follows that a manifold is [parallelizable](wikipedia://en/3108161) if and only if the frame bundle of *$M$* admits a global section.

Since the tangent bundle of *$M$* is trivializable over coordinate neighborhoods of *$M$* so is the frame bundle. In fact, given any coordinate neighborhood *$U$* with coordinates *$(x^1,\ldots,x^n)$* the coordinate vector fields

$$\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^1},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^n}\right)$$

define a smooth frame on *$U$*. One of the advantages of working with frame bundles is that they allow one to work with frames other than coordinates frames; one can choose a frame adapted to the problem at hand. This is sometimes called the [method of moving frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_moving_frames).

### Solder form {#Solder_form}

The frame bundle of a manifold *$M$* is a special type of principal bundle in the sense that its geometry is fundamentally tied to the geometry of *$M$*. This relationship can be expressed by means of a [vector-valued 1-form](wikipedia://en/3772116) on *$FM$* called the **[solder form](wikipedia://en/9792297)** (also known as the **fundamental** or [**tautological** 1-form](wikipedia://en/1855974)). Let *$x$* be a point of the manifold *$M$* and *$p$* a frame at *$x$*, so that

$$p : \mathbf{R}^n\to T_xM$$

is a linear isomorphism of ***$\mathbb{R}^n$*** with the tangent space of *$M$* at *$x$*. The solder form of *$FM$* is the ***$\mathbb{R}^n$***-valued 1-form *$\theta$* defined by

$$\theta_p(\xi) = p^{-1}\mathrm d\pi(\xi)$$

where ξ is a tangent vector to *$FM$* at the point *$(x,p)$*, and *$p^{-1}: T_x M \to \mathbb{R}^n$* is the inverse of the frame map, and *$d\pi$* is the [differential](wikipedia://en/1558510) of the projection map *$\pi: FM \to M$*. The solder form is horizontal in the sense that it vanishes on vectors tangent to the fibers of *$\pi$* and [right equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) in the sense that

$$R_g^*\theta = g^{-1}\theta$$

where *$R_g$* is right translation by *$g \in \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$*. A form with these properties is called a basic or [tensorial form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensorial_form) on *$FM$*. Such forms are in 1-1 correspondence with *$TM$*-valued 1-forms on *$M$* which are, in turn, in 1-1 correspondence with smooth [bundle maps](wikipedia://en/7220589) *$TM \to TM$* over *$M$*. Viewed in this light *$\theta$* is just the [identity map](wikipedia://en/15069) on *$TM$*.

As a naming convention, the term "tautological one-form" is usually reserved for the case where the form has a canonical definition, as it does here, while "solder form" is more appropriate for those cases where the form is not canonically defined. This convention is not being observed here.

## Orthonormal frame bundle {#Orthonormal_frame_bundle}

If a vector bundle *$E$* is equipped with a [Riemannian bundle metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_bundle_metric) then each fiber *$E_x$* is not only a vector space but an [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856). It is then possible to talk about the set of all [orthonormal frames](wikipedia://en/350995) for *$E_x$*. An orthonormal frame for *$E_x$* is an ordered [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) for *$E_x$*, or, equivalently, a [linear isometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_isometry)

$$p:\mathbb{R}^k \to E_x$$

where *$\mathbb{R}^k$* is equipped with the standard [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric). The [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) *$\mathrm{O}(k)$* acts freely and transitively on the set of all orthonormal frames via right composition. In other words, the set of all orthonormal frames is a right *$\mathrm{O}(k)$*-[torsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor).

The **orthonormal frame bundle** of *$E$*, denoted *$F_{\mathrm{O}}(E)$*, is the set of all orthonormal frames at each point *$x$* in the base space *$X$*. It can be constructed by a method entirely analogous to that of the ordinary frame bundle. The orthonormal frame bundle of a rank *$k$* Riemannian vector bundle *$E \to X$* is a principal *$\mathrm{O}(k)$*-bundle over *$X$*. Again, the construction works just as well in the smooth category.

If the vector bundle *$E$* is [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) then one can define the **oriented orthonormal frame bundle** of *$E$*, denoted *$F_{\mathrm{SO}}(E)$*, as the principal *$\mathrm{SO}(k)$*-bundle of all positively oriented orthonormal frames.

If *$M$* is an *$n$*-dimensional [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), then the orthonormal frame bundle of *$M$*, denoted *$F_{\mathrm{O}}(M)$* or *$\mathrm{O} (M)$*, is the orthonormal frame bundle associated with the tangent bundle of *$M$* (which is equipped with a Riemannian metric by definition). If *$M$* is orientable, then one also has the oriented orthonormal frame bundle *$F_{\mathrm{SO}}(M)$*.

Given a Riemannian vector bundle *$E$*, the orthonormal frame bundle is a principal *$\mathrm{O}(k)$*-[subbundle](wikipedia://en/2517151) of the general linear frame bundle. In other words, the inclusion map

$$i:{\mathrm F}_{\mathrm O}(E) \to {\mathrm F}_{\mathrm{GL}}(E)$$

is principal [bundle map](wikipedia://en/7220589). One says that *$F_{\mathrm{O}}(E)$* is a [reduction of the structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_of_the_structure_group) of *$F_{\mathrm{GL}}(E)$* from *$\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$* to *$\mathrm{O}(k)$*.

:::

## *G*-structures {#G-structures}

If a smooth manifold *$M$* comes with additional structure it is often natural to consider a subbundle of the full frame bundle of *$M$* which is adapted to the given structure. For example, if *$M$* is a Riemannian manifold we saw above that it is natural to consider the orthonormal frame bundle of *$M$*. The orthonormal frame bundle is just a reduction of the structure group of *$F_{\mathrm{GL}}(M)$* to the orthogonal group *$\mathrm{O}(n)$*.

In general, if *$M$* is a smooth *$n$*-manifold and *$G$* is a [Lie subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_subgroup) of *$\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$* we define a **[*G*-structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure)** on *$M$* to be a [reduction of the structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_of_the_structure_group) of *$F_{\mathrm{GL}}(M)$* to *$G$*. Explicitly, this is a principal *$G$*-bundle *$F_{G}(M)$* over *$M$* together with a *$G$*-equivariant [bundle map](wikipedia://en/7220589)

$${\mathrm F}_{G}(M) \to {\mathrm F}_{\mathrm{GL}}(M)$$

over *$M$*.

In this language, a Riemannian metric on *$M$* gives rise to an *$\mathrm{O}(n)$*-structure on *$M$*. The following are some other examples.

- Every [oriented manifold](wikipedia://en/187446) has an oriented frame bundle which is just a *$\mathrm{GL}^+(n,\mathbb{R})$*-structure on *$M$*.
- A [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811) on *$M$* determines a *$\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})$*-structure on *$M$*.
- A *$2n$*-dimensional [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) has a natural *$\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})$*-structure.
- A *$2n$*-dimensional [complex](wikipedia://en/509742) or [almost complex manifold](wikipedia://en/514258) has a natural *$\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})$*-structure.

In many of these instances, a *$G$*-structure on *$M$* uniquely determines the corresponding structure on *$M$*. For example, a *$\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})$*-structure on *$M$* determines a volume form on *$M$*. However, in some cases, such as for symplectic and complex manifolds, an added [integrability condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrability_condition) is needed. A *$\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})$*-structure on *$M$* uniquely determines a [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_form) [2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-form) on *$M$*, but for *$M$* to be symplectic, this 2-form must also be [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_differential_form).
