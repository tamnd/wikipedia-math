# Serre–Swan theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Serre–Swan theorem"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) fields of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Serre–Swan theorem**, also called **Swan's theorem**, relates the geometric notion of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) to the algebraic concept of [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488) and gives rise to a common intuition throughout [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831): "projective modules are like vector bundles".

:::

The three precise formulations of the theorem differ somewhat. The original theorem, as proved by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) in 1955, is more algebraic in nature, and concerns vector bundles on an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) (of any [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621)). [Richard Swan](wikipedia://en/18384954) in 1962 proved an analytic variant, concerning smooth vector bundles on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) ([real](wikipedia://en/20646438), [complex](wikipedia://en/5826), or [quaternionic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions)). His topological variant is about continuous (real or complex) vector bundles on a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637).

## Differential geometry {#Differential_geometry}

Suppose *M* is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) (not necessarily compact), and *E* is a [smooth vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_vector_bundle) over *M* of finite rank. Then *Γ(E)*, the space of [smooth sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of *E*, is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*) (the commutative algebra of smooth real-valued functions on *M*). Swan's theorem states that this module is [finitely generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_module) and [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) over C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*). In other words, every vector bundle is a direct summand of some trivial bundle: $M \times \mathbb{R}^k$ for some *k*. The theorem can be proved by constructing a bundle epimorphism from a trivial bundle $M \times \mathbb{R}^k \to E.$ This can be done by, for instance, exhibiting sections *s*<sub>1</sub>...*s*<sub>*k*</sub> with the property that for each point *p*, {*s*<sub>*i*</sub>(*p*)} span the fiber over *p*.

When *M* is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), the converse is also true: every [finitely generated projective module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_projective_module) over C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*) arises in this way from some smooth vector bundle on *M*. Such a module can be viewed as a smooth function *f* on *M* with values in the *n* × *n* idempotent matrices for some *n*. The fiber of the corresponding vector bundle over *x* is then the range of *f*(*x*). If *M* is not connected, the converse does not hold unless one allows for vector bundles of non-constant rank (which means admitting manifolds of non-constant dimension). For example, if *M* is a zero-dimensional 2-point manifold, the module $\mathbb{R}\oplus 0$ is finitely-generated and projective over $C^\infty(M)\cong\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$ but is not [free](wikipedia://en/326454), and so cannot correspond to the sections of any (constant-rank) vector bundle over *M* (all of which are trivial).

Another way of stating the above theorem is that for any connected smooth manifold *M*, the section [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) *Γ* from the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) of smooth vector bundles over *M* to the category of finitely generated, projective C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*)-modules is [full](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_functor), [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor), and [essentially surjective](wikipedia://en/4557668). Therefore the category of smooth vector bundles on *M* is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of finitely generated, projective C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*)-modules. Details may be found in (Nestruev 2003).

## Topology {#Topology}

Suppose *X* is a compact [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637), and C(*X*) is the ring of [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) real-valued functions on *X*. Analogous to the result above, the category of real finite-rank vector bundles on *X* is equivalent to the category of finitely generated projective modules over C(*X*). The same result holds if one replaces "real-valued" by "complex-valued" and "real vector bundle" by "complex vector bundle", but it does not hold if one replace the field by a [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected) field like the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024).

In detail, let Vec(*X*) be the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) of [complex vector bundles](wikipedia://en/1538109) over *X*, and let ProjMod(C(*X*)) be the category of [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) projective modules over the [C\*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cstar_algebra) C(*X*). There is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) Γ : Vec(*X*) → ProjMod(C(*X*)) which sends each complex vector bundle *E* over *X* to the C(*X*)-module Γ(*X*, *E*) of [sections](wikipedia://en/367621). If $\tau : (E_1, \pi_1) \to (E_2, \pi_2)$ is a [morphism of vector bundles over *X*](wikipedia://en/276524#Vector_bundle_morphisms) then $\pi_2 \circ \tau = \pi_1$ and it follows that

$$\forall s \in \Gamma(X, E_1) \quad \pi_2 \circ \tau \circ s = \pi_1 \circ s = \text{id}_X,$$

giving the map

$$\begin{cases} \Gamma \tau : \Gamma(X, E_1) \to \Gamma(X, E_2) \\ s \mapsto \tau \circ s \end{cases}$$

which respects the module structure (Várilly, 97). Swan's theorem asserts that the functor Γ is an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051).

## Algebraic geometry {#Algebraic_geometry}

The analogous result in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), due to (Serre 1955, §50) applies to vector bundles in the category of [affine varieties](wikipedia://en/320468) over an algebraically closed field. Let *X* be an affine variety with [structure sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_sheaf) $\mathcal{O}_X,$ and $\mathcal{F}$ a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) of $\mathcal{O}_X$ -modules on *X*. Then $\mathcal{F}$ is the sheaf of germs of a finite-dimensional vector bundle if and only if $\Gamma(\mathcal{F}, X),$ the space of sections of $\mathcal{F},$ is a projective module over the commutative ring $A = \Gamma(\mathcal{O}_X, X).$
