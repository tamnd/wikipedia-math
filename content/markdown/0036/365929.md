# Laplace–Stieltjes transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laplace–Stieltjes transform"}

The **Laplace–Stieltjes transform**, named for [Pierre-Simon Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783) and [Thomas Joannes Stieltjes](wikipedia://en/40589), is an [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912) similar to the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610). For [real-valued functions](wikipedia://en/22284647), it is the Laplace transform of a [Stieltjes measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stieltjes_measure), however it is often defined for functions with values in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). It is useful in a number of areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), including [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and certain areas of [theoretical](wikipedia://en/23542) and [applied probability](wikipedia://en/366719).

:::

## Real-valued functions {#Real-valued_functions}

The Laplace–Stieltjes transform of a real-valued function *g* is given by a [Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue-Stieltjes_integration) of the form

$$\int e^{-sx}\,dg(x)$$

for *s* a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826). As with the usual Laplace transform, one gets a slightly different transform depending on the domain of integration, and for the integral to be defined, one also needs to require that *g* be of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) on the region of integration. The most common are:

- The bilateral (or two-sided) Laplace–Stieltjes transform is given by $$\{\mathcal{L}^*g\}(s) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-sx}\,dg(x).$$
- The unilateral (one-sided) Laplace–Stieltjes transform is given by $$\{\mathcal{L}^*g\}(s) = \lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+} \int_{-\varepsilon}^\infty e^{-sx}\,dg(x).$$ The limit is necessary to ensure the transform captures a possible jump in $g(x)$ at $x = 0$, as is needed to make sense of the Laplace transform of the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021).
- More general transforms can be considered by integrating over a contour in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628); see (Zhavrid 2001).

The Laplace–Stieltjes transform in the case of a scalar-valued function is thus seen to be a special case of the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of a [Stieltjes measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stieltjes_measure). To wit,

$$\mathcal{L}^*g = \mathcal{L}(dg).$$

In particular, it shares many properties with the usual Laplace transform. For instance, the [convolution theorem](wikipedia://en/53268) holds:

$$\{\mathcal{L}^*(g * h)\}(s) = \{\mathcal{L}^*g\}(s)\{\mathcal{L}^*h\}(s).$$

Often only real values of the variable *s* are considered, although if the integral exists as a proper [Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288) for a given real value $s = \sigma$, then it also exists for all complex $s$ with $\operatorname{re}(s) \ge \sigma$.

The Laplace–Stieltjes transform appears naturally in the following context. If *X* is a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) *F*, then the Laplace–Stieltjes transform is given by the [expectation](wikipedia://en/9653):

$$\{\mathcal{L}^*F\}(s) = \mathrm{E}\left[e^{-sX}\right].$$

The Laplace-Stieltjes transform of a real random variable's cumulative distribution function is therefore equal to the random variable's [moment-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function), but with the sign of the argument reversed.

## Vector measures {#Vector_measures}

Whereas the Laplace–Stieltjes transform of a real-valued function is a special case of the Laplace transform of a measure applied to the associated Stieltjes measure, the conventional Laplace transform cannot handle [vector measures](wikipedia://en/9399072): measures with values in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). These are, however, important in connection with the study of [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799) that arise in [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). The most important semigroups are, respectively, the [heat semigroup](wikipedia://en/166415), [Riemann-Liouville semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann-Liouville_integral), and [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) and other [infinitely divisible processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitely_divisible_process).

Let *g* be a function from \[0,∞) to a Banach space *X* of **strongly bounded variation** over every finite interval. This means that, for every fixed subinterval \[0,*T*\] one has

$$\sup \sum_i \left \|g(t_i)-g(t_{i+1}) \right \|_X < \infty$$

where the [supremum](wikipedia://en/39382) is taken over all partitions of \[0,*T*\]

$$0=t_0 < t_1<\cdots< t_n=T.$$

The Stieltjes integral with respect to the vector measure *dg*

$$\int_0^T e^{-st}dg(t)$$

is defined as a [Riemann–Stieltjes integral](wikipedia://en/330491). Indeed, if π is the tagged partition of the interval \[0,*T*\] with subdivision 0 = *t*<sub>0</sub> ≤ *t*<sub>1</sub> ≤ ... ≤ *t*<sub>*n*</sub> = *T*, distinguished points $\tau_i \in [t_i, t_{i+1}]$ and mesh size $|\pi| = \max \left |t_i - t_{i+1} \right |,$ the Riemann–Stieltjes integral is defined as the value of the limit

$$\lim_{|\pi|\to 0} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e^{-s\tau_i} \left [g(t_{i+1})-g(t_i) \right ]$$

taken in the topology on *X*. The hypothesis of strong bounded variation guarantees convergence.

If in the topology of *X* the limit

$$\lim_{T\to\infty} \int_0^T e^{-st}dg(t)$$

exists, then the value of this limit is the Laplace–Stieltjes transform of *g*.

## Related transforms {#Related_transforms}

The Laplace–Stieltjes transform is closely related to other [integral transforms](wikipedia://en/430912), including the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) and the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610). In particular, note the following:

- If *g* has derivative *g'* then the Laplace–Stieltjes transform of *g* is the Laplace transform of *g′*. $$\{\mathcal{L}^*g\}(s) = \{\mathcal{L}g'\}(s),$$
- We can obtain the **[Fourier–Stieltjes transform](wikipedia://en/52247#Fourier–Stieltjes_transform_on_measurable_spaces)** of *g* (and, by the above note, the Fourier transform of *g′*) by $$\{\mathcal{F}^*g\}(s) = \{\mathcal{L}^*g\}(is), \qquad s \in \mathbb{R}.$$

## Probability distributions {#Probability_distributions}

If *X* is a continuous [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) *F*(*t*) then [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of *X* can be computed using[^o7]

$$\operatorname{E}[X^n] = (-1)^n \left.\frac{d^n \{\mathcal{L}^*F\}(s)}{ds^n} \right|_{s=0}.$$

### Exponential distribution {#Exponential_distribution}

For an exponentially distributed random variable *Y* with rate parameter *λ* the LST is,

$$\widetilde Y(s) = \{\mathcal{L}^*F_Y\}(s) = \int_0^\infty e^{-st} \lambda e^{-\lambda t} dt = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+s}$$

from which the first three moments can be computed as 1/*λ*, 2/*λ*<sup>2</sup> and 6/*λ*<sup>3</sup>.

### Erlang distribution {#Erlang_distribution}

For *Z* with [Erlang distribution](wikipedia://en/199028) (which is the sum of *n* exponential distributions) we use the fact that the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the sum of independent random variables is equal to the [convolution of their probability distributions](wikipedia://en/32251002). So if

$$Z = Y_1 + \cdots + Y_n$$

with the *Y<sub>i</sub>* independent then

$$\widetilde Z(s) = \widetilde Y_1(s) \cdots \widetilde Y_n(s)$$

therefore in the case where *Z* has an Erlang distribution,

$$\widetilde Z(s) = \left( \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+s} \right)^n.$$

### Uniform distribution {#Uniform_distribution}

For *U* with [uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29) on the interval (*a*,*b*), the transform is given by

$$\widetilde U(s) = \int_a^b e^{-st} \frac{1}{b-a} dt = \frac{e^{-sa}-e^{-sb}}{s(b-a)}.$$
