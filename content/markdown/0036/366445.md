# Mathematical puzzle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematical puzzle"}

*Image: Sequentials.jpg.* An example of a mathematical puzzle that challenges players to deduce the start of a [linear sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_sequence) that has changed into a continuous circle. The terms of the sequence have been expressed [Algebraically](wikipedia://en/18716923).

**Mathematical puzzles** make up an integral part of [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446). They have specific rules, but they do not usually involve competition between two or more players. Instead, to solve such a [puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle), the solver must find a solution that satisfies the given conditions. Mathematical puzzles require mathematics to solve them. [Logic puzzles](wikipedia://en/101595) are a common type of mathematical puzzle.

:::

[Conway's Game of Life](wikipedia://en/37035) and [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals), as two examples, may also be considered mathematical puzzles even though the solver interacts with them only at the beginning by providing a set of initial conditions. After these conditions are set, the rules of the puzzle determine all subsequent changes and moves. Many of the puzzles are well known because they were discussed by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) in his "Mathematical Games" column in Scientific American. Mathematical puzzles are sometimes used to motivate students in teaching elementary school [math problem](wikipedia://en/256700) solving techniques.[^o2] [Creative thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_thinking) – or "[thinking outside the box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking_outside_the_box)" – often helps to find the solution.
