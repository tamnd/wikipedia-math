# Henri Cartan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henri Cartan"}

**Henri Paul Cartan** (; 8 July 1904 – 13 August 2008) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made substantial contributions to [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

He was the son of the mathematician [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274), nephew of mathematician [Anna Cartan](wikipedia://en/62646204), oldest brother of composer Jean Cartan, [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) Louis Cartan and mathematician Hélène Cartan, and the son-in-law of physicist [Pierre Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Weiss).

## Life {#Life}

According to his own words, Henri Cartan was interested in mathematics at a very young age, without being influenced by his family.[^o7] He moved to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) with his family after his father's appointment at [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University) in 1909 and he attended secondary school at [Lycée Hoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Hoche) in [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles,_Yvelines).[^o9]

In 1923 he started studying mathematics at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), receiving an [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in 1926 and a doctorate in 1928.[^o8][^o9] His PhD thesis, entitled *Sur les systèmes de fonctions holomorphes à variétés linéaires lacunaires et leurs applications*, was supervised by [Paul Montel](wikipedia://en/1796347).

Cartan taught at [Lycée Malherbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Malherbe) in [Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen) from 1928 to 1929, at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille) from 1929 to 1931 and at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) from 1931 to 1939. After the [German invasion of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_France_%281940%29) the university staff was moved to [Clermont Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont_Ferrand). He recommended the mathematicians [Marie-Hélène Schwartz](wikipedia://en/46727387) and [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485) move to work at the University of Strasbourg in Clermont Ferrand for both their research and for their safety.[^o11][^o12] In 1940 he returned to Paris to work at [Université de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) and École Normale Supérieure.[^o9] From 1969 until his retirement in 1975 he was professor at [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University).[^o8]

Cartan died on 13 August 2008 at the age of 104. His funeral took place the following Wednesday on 20 August in [Die, Drome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die,_Drome).[^o13][^o14][^o15]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

In 1932 Cartan was invited to give a [Cours Peccot](wikipedia://en/60897406) at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France).[^o17] In 1950 he was elected president of the [Société mathématique de France](wikipedia://en/1691205)[^o18] and from 1967 to 1970 he was president of the [International Mathematics Union](wikipedia://en/14868).[^o19] He was awarded the [Émile Picard Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Picard_Medal) in 1959,[^o20] the [CNRS Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Gold_Medal) in 1976,[^o21] and the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) in 1980.[^o22]

He was an invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1932 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) and a Plenary Speaker at the ICM in 1950 in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts)[^o23] and in 1958 in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh).[^o24]

From 1974 until his death he had been a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o25] He was elected a foreign member of many academies and societies,[^o26][^o27] including the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (1950),[^o28] [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) (1959),[^o29] [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters) (1962), Accademia nazionale di scienze, lettere e arti (Palermo) (1967),[^o30] [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) (1971),[^o31] [Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) (1971),[^o32] [Spanish Royal Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Royal_Academy_of_Sciences) (1971),[^o33] [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) (1972),[^o34] [Bavarian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) (1974),[^o35] [Royal Academy of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy_of_Belgium) (1978), [Japan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy) (1979),[^o36] [Finnish Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) (1979), [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) (1981), [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) (1985) and [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) (1999).[^o37]

He was awarded [Honorary Doctorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Doctorate) from [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Münster_University) (1952),[^o38] [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) (1955), [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) (1961), [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_University) (1969), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) (1969),[^o27] [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) (1978), [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) (1980),[^o39] [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zaragoza) (1985)[^o40] and [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_Kapodistrian_University_of_Athens) (1992).[^o8]

The French government named him [Commandeur des Palmes Académiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandeur_des_Palmes_Académiques) in 1964, [Officier de la Légion d'honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officier_de_la_Légion_d'honneur) in 1965 and [Commandeur de l'Ordre du Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_national_du_Mérite) in 1971.[^o41]

## Political and social activities {#Political_and_social_activities}

During the 70's and the 80's Cartan used his influence to help obtain the release of several dissident mathematicians, including [Leonid Plyushch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Plyushch), imprisoned by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), [Jose Luis Massera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Luis_Massera), imprisoned between 1975 and 1984 by the [Uruguayan dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic-military_dictatorship_of_Uruguay), and [Sion Assidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sion_Assidon), imprisoned during the [Moroccan Years of Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_%28Morocco%29). For his humanitarian efforts, he received in 1989 the [Heinz R. Pagels Human Rights of Scientists Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_R._Pagels_Human_Rights_of_Scientists_Award) from the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o7][^o43][^o44][^o45]

Since the 30's Cartan had tight collaborations with many German mathematicians, including [Heinrich Behnke](wikipedia://en/22922678) and [Peter Thullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thullen). Right after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he put many efforts to improve the cooperation between French and German mathematicians and restore the flow of exchanges of ideas and students.[^o45][^o46]

Cartan supported the idea of [European Federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Federalism) and from 1974 to 1985 was president of the French section of the [Union of European Federalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_European_Federalists).[^o47] At the [1984 European elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_European_elections) he was the leader of the *Liste pour les États-Unis d'Europe*,[^o7] which obtained 0.4% of votes and did not elect any candidate.[^o48]

In 1992 he gave a speech at the first [European Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13505846) in Paris, remarking the common heritage and future of European countries and praising the first reunion between mathematicians from the two [previously separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain) parts of Europe.[^o45]

## Research {#Research}

Cartan worked in several fields across [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), focusing primarily on [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).[^o43][^o44]

He was a founding member of the [Bourbaki group](wikipedia://en/167394) in 1934 and one of its most active participants. After 1945 he started his own [seminar](wikipedia://en/3862616) in Paris, which deeply influenced [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672), [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) and [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959), amongst others of the leading lights of the younger generation. The number of his official students was small, but includes [Joséphine Guidy Wandja](wikipedia://en/60246310) (the first African woman to gain a PhD in mathematics),[^o50][^o51] [Adrien Douady](wikipedia://en/1604604), [Roger Godement](wikipedia://en/971810), [Max Karoubi](wikipedia://en/30942178), [Jean-Louis Koszul](wikipedia://en/5147910), Jean-Pierre Serre and [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633).[^o10][^o4]

Cartan's first research interests, until the 40's, were in the theory of [functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_of_several_complex_variables), which later gave rise to the theory of [complex varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variety) and [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/630505). Motivated by the solution to the [Cousin problems](wikipedia://en/622966), he worked on [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) and [coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaves) and [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) two powerful results, [Cartan's theorems A and B](wikipedia://en/621677).

Since the 50's he became more interested in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). Among his major contributions, he worked on [cohomology operations](wikipedia://en/1226719) and [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of the [Eilenberg–MacLane spaces](wikipedia://en/1073230),[^o52] he introduced the notion of [Steenrod algebra](wikipedia://en/2146220),[^o53] and, together with Jean-Pierre Serre, developed the method of "killing [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506)".[^o54][^o55] His 1956 book with [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501) on homological algebra[^o56] was an important text, treating the subject with a moderate level of abstraction with the help of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869).[^o57] They introduced fundamental concepts, including those of [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488), [weak dimension](wikipedia://en/38263686), and what is now called the [Cartan–Eilenberg resolution](wikipedia://en/21065169).

Among his other contributions, in [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) he introduced the notions of [filter](wikipedia://en/19719) and [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911)[^o58][^o59] and in [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) he developed the [fine topology](wikipedia://en/5170051) and proved [Cartan's lemma](wikipedia://en/27429912). The [Cartan model](wikipedia://en/4110341) for [equivariant cohomology](wikipedia://en/2976342)[^o60] is also named after him.
