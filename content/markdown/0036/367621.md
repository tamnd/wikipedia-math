# Section (fiber bundle)

*Image: Bundle section.pi.sigma.svg.* A section $\sigma$ of a bundle $\pi \colon E\to B$. A section $\sigma$ allows the base space $B$ to be identified with a subspace $\sigma(B)$ of $E$.

*Image: Vector field.svg.* A vector field on $\mathbb{R}^2$. A section of a [tangent vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_vector_bundle) is a vector field.

*Image: Vector bundle with section.png.* A vector bundle $E$ over a base $M$ with section $s$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Section"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **section** (or **cross section**)[^o2] of a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) $E$ is a continuous [right inverse](wikipedia://en/14907#Left_and_right_inverses) of the [projection function](wikipedia://en/3909097) $\pi$. In other words, if $E$ is a fiber bundle over a [base space](wikipedia://en/30450), $B$:

:::

$$\pi \colon E \to B$$

then a section of that fiber bundle is a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map),

$$\sigma \colon B \to E$$

such that

$\pi(\sigma(x)) = x$ for all $x \in B$.

A section is an abstract characterization of what it means to be a [graph](wikipedia://en/87352). The graph of a function $g\colon B \to Y$ can be identified with a function taking its values in the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $E = B \times Y$, of $B$ and $Y$:

$$\sigma\colon B\to E, \quad \sigma(x) = (x,g(x)) \in E.$$

Let $\pi\colon E \to B$ be the projection onto the first factor: $\pi(x,y) = x$. Then a graph is any function $\sigma$ for which $\pi(\sigma(x)) = x$.

The language of fibre bundles allows this notion of a section to be generalized to the case when $E$ is not necessarily a Cartesian product. If $\pi\colon E \to B$ is a fibre bundle, then a section is a choice of point $\sigma(x)$ in each of the fibres. The condition $\pi(\sigma(x)) = x$ simply means that the section at a point $x$ must lie over $x$. (See image.)

For example, when $E$ is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) a section of $E$ is an element of the vector space $E_x$ lying over each point $x \in B$. In particular, a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) $M$ is a choice of [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) at each point of $M$: this is a *section* of the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of $M$. Likewise, a [1-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-form) on $M$ is a section of the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600).

Sections, particularly of [principal bundles](wikipedia://en/310950) and vector bundles, are also very important tools in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). In this setting, the base space $B$ is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) $M$, and $E$ is assumed to be a smooth fiber bundle over $M$ (i.e., $E$ is a smooth manifold and $\pi\colon E\to M$ is a [smooth map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_map)). In this case, one considers the space of **smooth sections** of $E$ over an open set $U$, denoted $C^{\infty}(U,E)$. It is also useful in [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474) to consider spaces of sections with intermediate regularity (e.g., $C^k$ sections, or sections with regularity in the sense of [Hölder conditions](wikipedia://en/4005892) or [Sobolev spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobolev_spaces)).

## Local and global sections {#Local_and_global_sections}

Fiber bundles do not in general have such *global* sections (consider, for example, the fiber bundle over $S^1$ with fiber $F = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ obtained by taking the [Möbius bundle](wikipedia://en/37817) and removing the zero section), so it is also useful to define sections only locally. A **local section** of a fiber bundle is a continuous map $s \colon U \to E$ where $U$ is an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in $B$ and $\pi(s(x))=x$ for all $x$ in $U$. If $(U, \varphi)$ is a [local trivialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_trivialization) of $E$, where $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism from $\pi^{-1}(U)$ to $U\times F$ (where $F$ is the [fiber](wikipedia://en/276526)), then local sections always exist over $U$ in bijective correspondence with continuous maps from $U$ to $F$. The (local) sections form a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) over $B$ called the **sheaf of sections** of $E$.

The space of continuous sections of a fiber bundle $E$ over $U$ is sometimes denoted $C(U,E)$, while the space of global sections of $E$ is often denoted $\Gamma(E)$ or $\Gamma(B,E)$.

### Extending to global sections {#Extending_to_global_sections}

Sections are studied in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), where one of the main goals is to account for the existence or non-existence of **global sections**. An [obstruction](wikipedia://en/2179639) denies the existence of global sections since the space is too "twisted". More precisely, obstructions "obstruct" the possibility of extending a local section to a global section due to the space's "twistedness". Obstructions are indicated by particular [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530), which are cohomological classes. For example, a [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) has a global section if and only if it is [trivial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle). On the other hand, a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) always has a global section, namely the [zero section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_section). However, the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) obstructs the existence of a nowhere vanishing section.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

#### Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Obstructions to extending local sections may be generalized in the following manner: take a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) and form a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) whose objects are open subsets, and morphisms are inclusions. Thus we use a category to generalize a topological space. We generalize the notion of a "local section" using sheaves of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), which assigns to each object an abelian group (analogous to local sections).

There is an important distinction here: intuitively, local sections are like "vector fields" on an open subset of a topological space. So at each point, an element of a *fixed* vector space is assigned. However, sheaves can "continuously change" the vector space (or more generally abelian group).

This entire process is really the [global section functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_section_functor), which assigns to each sheaf its global section. Then [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) enables us to consider a similar extension problem while "continuously varying" the abelian group. The theory of [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530) generalizes the idea of obstructions to our extensions.

:::
