# Lebesgue–Stieltjes integration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lebesgue–Stieltjes integration"}

In [measure-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and related branches of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Lebesgue–Stieltjes integration** generalizes both [Riemann–Stieltjes](wikipedia://en/330491) and [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration), preserving the many advantages of the former in a more general measure-theoretic framework. The Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral is the ordinary Lebesgue integral with respect to a measure known as the Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure, which may be associated to any function of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) on the real line. The Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure is a [regular Borel measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Borel_measure), and conversely every regular Borel measure on the real line is of this kind.

:::

Lebesgue–Stieltjes [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532), named for [Henri Leon Lebesgue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Leon_Lebesgue) and [Thomas Joannes Stieltjes](wikipedia://en/40589), are also known as **Lebesgue–Radon integrals** or just **Radon integrals**, after [Johann Radon](wikipedia://en/339331), to whom much of the theory is due. They find common application in [probability](wikipedia://en/23542) and [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), and in certain branches of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) including [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dg(x)$$

is defined when $f : \left[a, b\right] \rightarrow \mathbb R$ is [Borel](wikipedia://en/4322)-[measurable](wikipedia://en/44055) and [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) and $g : \left[a, b\right] \rightarrow \mathbb R$ is of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) in $[a, b]$ and right-continuous, or when $\,f\,$ is non-negative and $g$ is [monotone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_function) and [right-continuous](wikipedia://en/6122#Directional_and_semi-continuity). To start, assume that $\,f\,$ is non-negative and $g$ is monotone non-decreasing and right-continuous. Define $w((s, t]) = g(t) - g(s)$ and $w(\{a\}) = 0$. (Alternatively, the construction works for $g$ left-continuous, $w([s,t)) = g(t) - g(s)$ and $w(\{b\}) = 0$.)

By [Carathéodory's extension theorem](wikipedia://en/4026968), there is a unique Borel measure $\mu_{g}$ on $[a, b]$ which agrees with $w$ on every interval $I$. The measure $\mu_{g}$ arises from an [outer measure](wikipedia://en/488605) (in fact, a [metric outer measure](wikipedia://en/11279549)) given by

$$\mu_g(E) = \inf\left\{\sum_i \mu_g(I_i) \ : \ E\subseteq \bigcup_i I_i \right\}$$

the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) taken over all coverings of $E$ by countably many semiopen intervals. This measure is sometimes called[^o4] the **Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure** associated with $g$.

The Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dg(x)$$

is defined as the [Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288) of $\,f\,$ with respect to the measure $\mu_{g}$ in the usual way. If $g$ is non-increasing, then define

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dg(x) := -\int_a^b f(x) \,d (-g)(x),$$

the latter integral being defined by the preceding construction.

If $g$ is of bounded variation, then it is possible to write

$$g(x)=g_1(x)-g_2(x)$$

where $g_{1}(x) = V^{x}_{a}g$ is the [total variation](wikipedia://en/683561) of $g$ in the interval $[a, x]$, and $g_{2}(x) = g_{1}(x) - g(x)$. Both $g_{1}$ and $g_{2}$ are monotone non-decreasing.

Now, if $f$ is bounded, the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral of $f$ with respect to $g$ is defined by

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dg(x) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dg_1(x)-\int_a^b f(x)\,dg_2(x),$$

where the latter two integrals are well-defined by the preceding construction.

### Daniell integral {#Daniell_integral}

An alternative approach (Hewitt Stromberg 1965) is to define the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral as the [Daniell integral](wikipedia://en/2904201) that extends the usual Riemann–Stieltjes integral. Let $g$ be a non-decreasing right-continuous function on $[a, b]$, and define $I(\,f\,)$ to be the Riemann–Stieltjes integral

$$I(f) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dg(x)$$

for all continuous functions $\,f\,$. The [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) $I$ defines a [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933) on $[a, b]$. This functional can then be extended to the class of all non-negative functions by setting

$$\begin{aligned} \overline{I}(h) &= \sup \left \{I(f) \ : \ f\in C[a,b], 0\le f\le h \right \} \\ \overline{\overline{I}}(h) &= \inf \left \{I(f) \ : \ f \in C[a,b], h\le f \right \}. \end{aligned}$$

For Borel measurable functions, one has

$$\overline{I}(h) = \overline{\overline{I}}(h),$$

and either side of the identity then defines the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral of $h$. The outer measure $\mu_{g}$ is defined via

$$\mu_g(A) := \overline{I}(\chi_A)= \overline{\overline{I}}(\chi_A)$$

where $\chi_{A}$ is the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of $A$.

Integrators of bounded variation are handled as above by decomposing into positive and negative variations.

## Example {#Example}

Suppose that $\,\gamma : [a, b] \to \mathbf{R}^{2}$ is a [rectifiable curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifiable_curve) in the plane and $\,\rho : \mathbf{R}^{2} \to [0, \infty)$ is Borel measurable. Then we may define the length of $\gamma$ with respect to the Euclidean metric weighted by ρ to be

$$\int_a^b \rho(\gamma(t))\,d\ell(t),$$

where $\ell(t)$ is the length of the restriction of $\gamma$ to $[a, t]$. This is sometimes called the $\rho$-length of $\gamma$. This notion is quite useful for various applications: for example, in muddy terrain the speed in which a person can move may depend on how deep the mud is. If $\,\rho(z)$ denotes the inverse of the walking speed at or near $z$, then the $\rho$-length of $\gamma$ is the time it would take to traverse $\gamma$. The concept of [extremal length](wikipedia://en/16819101) uses this notion of the $\rho$-length of curves and is useful in the study of [conformal mappings](wikipedia://en/50627).

:::

## Integration by parts {#Integration_by_parts}

A function $\,f\,$ is said to be "regular" at a point $a$ if the right and left hand limits $f\,(a+)$ and $f\,(a-)$ exist, and the function takes at $a$ the average value

$$f(a)=\frac{f(a-)+f(a+)}{2}.$$

Given two functions $U$ and $V$ of finite variation, if at each point either at least one of $U$ or $V$ is continuous or $U$ and $V$ are both regular, then an [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) formula for the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral holds:[^o10]

$$\int_a^b U\,dV+\int_a^b V\,dU = U(b+)V(b+)-U(a-)V(a-), \qquad -\infty < a < b < \infty.$$

Here the relevant Lebesgue–Stieltjes measures are associated with the right-continuous versions of the functions $U$ and $V$; that is, to $\tilde U(x) = \lim_{t\to x^+} U(t)$ and similarly $\tilde V(x).$ The bounded interval *a*, *b* may be replaced with an unbounded interval -∞, *b*, *a*, ∞ or -∞, ∞ provided that $U$ and $V$ are of finite variation on this unbounded interval. Complex-valued functions may be used as well.

An alternative result, of significant importance in the theory of [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001) is the following. Given two functions $U$ and $V$ of finite variation, which are both right-continuous and have left-limits (they are [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839) functions) then

$$U(t)V(t) = U(0)V(0) + \int_{(0,t]} U(s-)\,dV(s)+\int_{(0,t]} V(s-)\,dU(s)+\sum_{u\in (0,t]} \Delta U_u \Delta V_u,$$

where $\Delta U_{t} = U(t) - U(t-)$. This result can be seen as a precursor to [Itô's lemma](wikipedia://en/203962), and is of use in the general theory of stochastic integration. The final term is $\Delta U(t)\Delta V(t) = d[U, V],$which arises from the quadratic covariation of $U$ and $V$. (The earlier result can then be seen as a result pertaining to the [Stratonovich integral](wikipedia://en/2314574).)

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

### Lebesgue integration {#Lebesgue_integration}

When $g(x) = x$ for all real $x$, then $\mu_{g}$ is the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198), and the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral of $\,f\,$ with respect to $g$ is equivalent to the [Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288) of $\,f\,$.

### Riemann–Stieltjes integration and probability theory {#Riemann–Stieltjes_integration_and_probability_theory}

Where $\,f\,$ is a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) real-valued function of a real variable and $v$ is a non-decreasing real function, the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral is equivalent to the [Riemann–Stieltjes integral](wikipedia://en/330491), in which case we often write

$$\int_a^b f(x) \, dv(x)$$

for the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral, letting the measure $\mu_{v}$ remain implicit. This is particularly common in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) when $v$ is the [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) of a real-valued random variable $X$, in which case

$$\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x) \, dv(x) = \mathrm{E}[f(X)].$$

(See the article on [Riemann–Stieltjes integration](wikipedia://en/330491) for more detail on dealing with such cases.)
