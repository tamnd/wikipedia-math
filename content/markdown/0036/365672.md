# Armand Borel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Armand Borel"}

**Armand Borel** (21 May 1923 – 11 August 2003) was a Swiss [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), born in [La Chaux-de-Fonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Chaux-de-Fonds), and was a permanent professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), United States from 1957 to 1993. He worked in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), in the theory of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), and was one of the creators of the contemporary theory of [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He studied at the [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich), where he came under the influence of the topologist [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082) and Lie-group theorist [Eduard Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876). He was in Paris from 1949: he applied the Leray [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) to the topology of Lie groups and their [classifying spaces](wikipedia://en/923556), under the influence of [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430) and [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536). With [Hirzebruch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirzebruch), he significantly developed the theory of [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530) in the early 1950s.

He collaborated with [Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570) in fundamental work on [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924), and with [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190) on their [arithmetic subgroups](wikipedia://en/630741). In an algebraic group *G* a *[Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356)* *H* is one minimal with respect to the property that the [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) *G/H* is a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469). For example, if *G* is GL<sub>*n*</sub> then we can take *H* to be the subgroup of upper triangular matrices. In this case it turns out that *H* is a maximal [solvable subgroup](wikipedia://en/105012), and that the [parabolic subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356) *P* between *H* and *G* have a combinatorial structure (in this case the homogeneous spaces *G/P* are the various [flag manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_manifold)). Both those aspects generalize, and play a central role in the theory.

The [Borel−Moore homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel−Moore_homology) theory applies to general [locally compact spaces](wikipedia://en/48632), and is closely related to [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) theory.

He published a number of books, including a work on the history of Lie groups. In 1978 he received the [Brouwer Medal](wikipedia://en/42578624)[^o4] and in 1992 he was awarded the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize) "For his fundamental contributions to the theory of Lie groups, algebraic groups and arithmetic groups, and for his indefatigable action in favour of high quality in mathematical research and the propagation of new ideas" (motivation of the Balzan General Prize Committee). He was a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o5][^o6][^o7]

He died in Princeton. He used to answer the question of whether he was related to [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161) alternately by saying he was a nephew, and no relation.
