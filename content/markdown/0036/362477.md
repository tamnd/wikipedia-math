# Antoni Zygmund

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Antoni Zygmund"}

**Antoni Szczepan Zygmund** (; 26 December 1900 – 30 May 1992) was a Polish-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He worked mostly in the area of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), including [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), and he is considered one of the greatest analysts of the 20th century.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8] Zygmund was responsible for creating the [Chicago school of mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/13328208) together with his doctoral student [Alberto Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderón), for which he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science), the highest American science award, in 1986.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Zygmund was born on 26 December 1900 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), then part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), in a Polish family of Wincenty Zygmund and Antonina (Perowska).[^o10] He was the oldest of four siblings, with three younger sisters. In 1914, his secondary education in Warsaw was interrupted by the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) forcing his family to relocate to [Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava) (presnet-day Ukraine). In 1918, he returned to Poland, which had just become an independent state, and spent a year studying at Kazimierz Kulwiee’s [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in Warsaw.[^o10]

The following year, he enrolled at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw)[^o14] and studied under prominent mathematicians representing the [Warsaw School of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_School_of_Mathematics) including [Zygmunt Janiszewski](wikipedia://en/362471), [Stefan Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203) and [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122).[^o11] In 1920, Zygmund’s education was interrupted again when he was drafted into the army. He did not take part in active combat in the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Soviet_War) and was therefore able to resume his academic work. During his time at the University of Warsaw, Zygmund was particularly influenced by [Aleksander Rajchman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksander_Rajchman), attending his lectures and a seminar on the theory of [trigonometric series](wikipedia://en/4099125). In 1923, Zygmund obtained his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from the University of Warsaw based on his thesis concerning the Riemannian theory of trigonometric series written under the formal supervision of Mazurkiewicz.[^o10][^o11]

In 1922–26, Zygmund worked as an assistant at the [Warsaw University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University_of_Technology). In 1926, he earned his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) and assumed the position of a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) at the University of Warsaw. In 1929, he was granted a [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) scholarship[^o20] and spent a year in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford) and [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), which exerted a profound impact upon his scientific development. He collaborated with such mathematicians as [Raymond Paley](wikipedia://en/2788568),[^o12] [G.H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.H._Hardy) and [J. E. Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371), publishing five joint-papers with Paley between 1930 and 1931.[^o14][^o10]

In 1935, Zygmund published in Polish the original edition of what has become, in its English translation, the two-volume *[Trigonometric Series](wikipedia://en/35279846)*. It was described by Robert A. Fefferman as "one of the most influential books in the history of mathematical analysis" and "an extraordinarily comprehensive and masterful presentation of a ... vast field".[^o13] [Jean-Pierre Kahane](wikipedia://en/6949961) called the book "The Bible" of a [harmonic analyst](wikipedia://en/14147).[^o8] In 1988, a new edition of the work was published by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press).[^o25] The theory of trigonometric series had remained the largest component of Zygmund's mathematical investigations.[^o8]

Zygmund also worked with [Stanisław Saks](wikipedia://en/362649), with whom he co-authored the book *Analytic Functions* published in the *Monografie Matematyczne* (Mathematical Monographs) series in 1938.[^o14]

Zygmund was a professor at [Stefan Batory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University) in [Wilno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius) from 1930 to 1939. During this period, he began a highly productive scientific collaboration with his most gifted student, [Józef Marcinkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Marcinkiewicz), with whom he also developed a close personal friendship. Together they obtained significant results in [real](wikipedia://en/26478) and [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), and moment inequalities, contributions that later became foundational in several areas of modern mathematical analysis.[^o15][^o16] Zygmund also further developed many of Marcinkiewicz's ideas after his student's tragic death and played an important role in helping preserve his scientific legacy abroad.[^o14][^o15]

*Image: Harper\_Midway\_Chicago.jpg.* Zygmund spent most of his academic career at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago)

When [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) broke out, Poland was [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_%281939–1945%29) and, in 1940, Zygmund managed to emigrate to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). Thanks to the support from [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106), [Jacob Tamarkin](wikipedia://en/22870348) and [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185), he received an invitation to [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College) in [South Hadley, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hadley,_Massachusetts) where he became a professor.[^o14] In 1945–1947, he was a professor at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), and from 1947, until his retirement, at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). During his time in Chicago, Zygmund together with his student [Alberto Calderon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderon) (whom he had met in 1948 in Argentina),[^o11] founded the [Chicago school of mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_school_of_mathematical_analysis), a [school of thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_thought) in mathematics that highlights the use of Fourier analysis in the study of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). It became one of the most influential analytical schools of the 20th century, contributing many of the field’s major advances.[^o17][^o18][^o19]

He was a member of several scientific societies. From 1930 until 1952 he was a member of the [Warsaw Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Scientific_Society) (*TNW*), from 1946 of the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning) (*PAU*), from 1959 of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) (*PAN*), and from 1961 of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in the United States.[^o10]

In 1954, Zygmund participated as an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam).[^o20]

In 1979, Zygmund was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for "his cumulative influence on the theory of [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038), [real variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_variables), and related areas of analysis."[^o21]

In 1986, Zygmund received the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science), the highest science award in the United States,[^o22][^o23] from [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan) for his outstanding contribution to the development of Fourier analysis, as well as its application to partial differential equations and other branches of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).[^o24] He was also recognized for his "creation and leadership of the strongest school of analytical research in the contemporary mathematical world."[^o10][^o17][^o18][^o19]

In 1981, he was granted emeritus status.[^o25] Zygmund continued attending analysis seminars at Chicago even in his eighties, contributing profound and incisive insights.[^o28] He died on 30 May 1992 in Chicago, at the age of 91.[^o25]

Zygmund published six books and more than 180 scientific papers during his career.[^o25] He was the advisor of 40 doctoral students, including [Alberto Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderón), [Paul Cohen](wikipedia://en/22994) ([Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) winner), [Nathan Fine](wikipedia://en/6042215), [Józef Marcinkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Marcinkiewicz), [Victor L. Shapiro](wikipedia://en/58426630), [Guido Weiss](wikipedia://en/36535208), [Elias M. Stein](wikipedia://en/418073) and [Mischa Cotlar](wikipedia://en/35234149).[^o26]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

His work has had a pervasive influence in many fields of mathematics, mostly in mathematical analysis, and particularly in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). Among his chief interests in that area was expressing vibrating bodies in precise mathematical terms, a key issue in [spacecraft engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering), [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), and [laser holography](wikipedia://en/66338).[^o25]

Some of his most important contributions concerned the theory of [singular integral operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integrals), and were achieved in collaboration with Calderón.[^o28][^o13] In particular, the [Calderón–Zygmund lemma](wikipedia://en/12288578) is regarded as a fundamental result in this area of mathematics. It was developed to prove the weak-type continuity of singular integrals of [integrable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_function) becoming a standard tool in analysis and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). [George G. Lorentz](wikipedia://en/58614550) called it Zygmund's crowning achievement, one that "stands somewhat apart from the rest of Zygmund's work".[^o8]

Zygmund, together with [Raphaël Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphaël_Salem), demonstrated that a [Cantor-like set](wikipedia://en/6172) with dissection ratio ξ is a [set of uniqueness](wikipedia://en/799649) if and only if 1/ξ is a [Pisot number](wikipedia://en/519303), that is an [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981) characterized by the fact that all its [conjugates](wikipedia://en/458467) (if any) are smaller than 1. Their result was the first to show that the uniqueness property depends on arithmetic features rather than merely on notions of size.[^o29]

The [Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality](wikipedia://en/22583255) is another significant result by Zygmund. It establishes relationships between the [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of [independent random variables](wikipedia://en/27593) and constitutes a generalization of the rule for the sum of [variances](wikipedia://en/32344) of independent random variables to moments of arbitrary order.

Other concepts named after Zygmund include the [Paley–Zygmund inequality](wikipedia://en/9414239),[^o30] [Calderón–Zygmund kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calderón–Zygmund_kernel), [Calderón–Zygmund decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calderón–Zygmund_decomposition) and [Calderón–Zygmund operators](wikipedia://en/12791220#Singular_integrals_of_non-convolution_type).

## Family {#Family}

Antoni Zygmund, who had three sisters, married Irena Parnowska, a mathematician, in 1925.[^o6] Upon his death he was survived by four grandsons.[^o32]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

Zygmund received a number of awards and distinctions, some of which include:[^o10]

- 1953: [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship)[^o20]
- 1954: Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842)[^o20]
- 1959: Member of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences)
- 1961: Member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences)
- 1964: Member of the [National Academy of Sciences of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Argentina)
- 1967: Member of the [Spanish Royal Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy_of_Exact,_Physical_and_Natural_Sciences), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097)
- 1972: [Jurzykowski Foundation Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurzykowski_Prize), member of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791)
- 1979: [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550)
- 1986: [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science)

He accepted [honorary doctorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) from [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College), [Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University), the [Nicolaus Copernicus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University) in [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń),[^o34] the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), and the [University of Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Uppsala).[^o10]
