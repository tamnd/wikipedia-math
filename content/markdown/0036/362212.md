# 29 (number)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="29"}

**29** (**twenty-nine**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [28](wikipedia://en/362213) and preceding [30](wikipedia://en/341668). It is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666).

:::

[29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_29th) is the number of days [February](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February) has on a [leap year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leap_year).

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

### Integer properties {#Integer_properties}

29 is the tenth [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). It forms a [twin prime](wikipedia://en/41997) pair with 31.

29 is the fifth [primorial prime](wikipedia://en/409348), the sixth [Sophie Germain prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Germain_prime),[^o5] a [Lucas prime](wikipedia://en/398404#Lucas_primes),[^o6] a [Pell prime](wikipedia://en/1355482#Primes_and_squares),[^o7] and an [Eisenstein prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_prime) with no imaginary part and real part of the form 3n − 1.

Additionally, 29 represents the sum of the first cluster of consecutive [semiprimes](wikipedia://en/339542) with distinct [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) ([14](wikipedia://en/19223527), [15](wikipedia://en/208252)). These two numbers are the only numbers whose [arithmetic mean of divisors](wikipedia://en/36551969) is the first [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670) and [unitary perfect number](wikipedia://en/553027), [6](wikipedia://en/208171) (that is also the smallest semiprime with distinct factors). The pair (14, 15) is also the first [floor and ceiling](wikipedia://en/54267) values of [imaginary parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_part) of non-trivial zeroes in the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), $\zeta.$

29 is the largest [prime factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) of the smallest number with an [abundancy index](wikipedia://en/321831) of 3,

1018976683725 = 3<sup>3</sup> × 5<sup>2</sup> × 7<sup>2</sup> × 11 × 13 × 17 × 19 × 23 × 29

It is also the largest prime factor of the smallest abundant number not divisible by the first even (of only one) and odd primes, 5391411025 = 5<sup>2</sup> × 7 × 11 × 13 × 17 × 19 × 23 × 29. Both of these numbers are divisible by consecutive prime numbers ending in 29.

29 is the smallest positive whole number that cannot be made from the numbers $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, using each digit exactly once and using only addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.[^o12] None of the first twenty-nine [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) have more than two different prime factors. This is the longest such consecutive sequence. The first [sphenic number](wikipedia://en/245203) or triprime, [30](wikipedia://en/341668) is the product of the first three primes [2](wikipedia://en/64516), [3](wikipedia://en/208157), and [5](wikipedia://en/6412297)).

29 is a [tetranacci number](wikipedia://en/8006956#Tetranacci_numbers)[^o14] and a [Perrin number](wikipedia://en/30863738), preceded in the sequence by 12, 17, 22.[^o15]

29 is also a [Markov number](wikipedia://en/2965726), appearing in the solutions to *x*${}^{2}$ + *y*${}^{2}$ + *z*${}^{2}$ = 3*xyz*: {2, 5, 29}, {2, 29, 169}, {5, 29, 433}, {29, 169, 14701}, etc.

29 is the sum of three consecutive [squares](wikipedia://en/213919), [2](wikipedia://en/64516)<sup>2</sup> + [3](wikipedia://en/208157)<sup>2</sup> + [4](wikipedia://en/208161)<sup>2</sup>.

There are 29 [pentacubes](wikipedia://en/710426) if reflections are considered distinct.

#### 15 and 290 theorems {#15_and_290_theorems}

The [15 and 290 theorems](wikipedia://en/5405018) describes integer-quadratic matrices that describe all [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer), by the set of the first fifteen integers, or equivalently, the first two-hundred and ninety integers. Alternatively, a more precise version states that an integer quadratic matrix represents all positive integers when it contains the set of *twenty-nine* integers between [1](wikipedia://en/77591639) and [290](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/290_%28number%29):[^o18]

$$\{1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 21, 22, 23, 26, 29, 30, 31, 34, 35, 37, 42, 58, 93, 110, 145, 203, 290\}$$

The largest member 290 is the product between 29 and its index in the [sequence of prime numbers](wikipedia://en/442370), [10](wikipedia://en/208151). The largest member in this sequence is also the twenty-fifth even, [square](wikipedia://en/213919)-free [sphenic number](wikipedia://en/245203) with three distinct prime numbers $p \times q \times r$ as factors, and the fifteenth such that $p + q + r + 1$ is prime (where in its case, 2 + 5 + 29 + 1 = [37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_%28number%29)).

:::

### Dimensional spaces {#Dimensional_spaces}

The 29th dimension is the highest dimension for [compact hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram#Hyperbolic_Coxeter_groups) Coxeter polytopes that are bounded by a fundamental [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), and the highest dimension that holds arithmetic discrete groups of reflections with *noncompact* unbounded fundamental polyhedra.[^o24]

29 is the tenth [supersingular prime](wikipedia://en/22965333).[^o25]
