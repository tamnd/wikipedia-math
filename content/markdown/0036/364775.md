# Conformal group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conformal group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **conformal group** of a space is the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of one-to-one transformations from the space to itself that preserve angles, that is, the [conformal transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_transformation) on that space. More formally, it is the [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of the [conformal structure](wikipedia://en/295907) of the space. Conformal groups include [isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometries) (the distance-preserving transformations), but also angle-preserving transformations such as [homotheties (dilations)](wikipedia://en/386138) and [special conformal transformations](wikipedia://en/24675202) which do not preserve distances.

:::

Important conformal groups include the following:

- The [**conformal orthogonal group**](wikipedia://en/173954#Conformal_orthogonal_group). If *V* is a vector space with a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) *Q*, then the conformal orthogonal group CO(*V*, *Q*) is the group of linear transformations *T* of *V* for which there exists a scalar *λ* such that for all *x* in *V*

$$Q(Tx) = \lambda^2 Q(x)$$

For a [nondegenerate quadratic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_quadratic_form), the conformal orthogonal group is equal to the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) times the group of [dilations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation) with positive scale factor.

- The conformal group of the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) is generated by the [inversions in circles](wikipedia://en/295844). This group is also known as the [Möbius group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_group).
- In [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **E**<sup>*n*</sup>, *n* > 2, the conformal group is generated by inversions in [hyperspheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersphere).
- In a [pseudo-Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/10962546) **E**<sup>*p*,*q*</sup>, the conformal group is Conf(*p*, *q*) ≃ O(*p* + 1, *q* + 1) / Z<sub>2</sub>.[^o2]

Conformal groups can be defined for a number of different types of space with notions of angle, including [inner product spaces](wikipedia://en/14856) and [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652); the precise definition of the conformal group is dependent on which geometric structure one is considering. For example, when considering $\mathbb{R}^n$, one gets a slightly different conformal group depending on where one considers it as an inner product space, a Riemannian manifold or as Euclidean space (see the definition of the conformal orthogonal group above and the section below). All conformal groups are [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).

## Angle analysis {#Angle_analysis}

In Euclidean geometry one can expect the standard circular [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) to be characteristic, but in [pseudo-Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/10962546) there is also the [hyperbolic angle](wikipedia://en/1139981). In the study of [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) the various frames of reference, for varying velocity with respect to a rest frame, are related by [rapidity](wikipedia://en/4520673), a hyperbolic angle. One way to describe a [Lorentz boost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_boost) is as a [hyperbolic rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_rotation) which preserves the differential angle between rapidities. Thus, they are [conformal transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_transformation#Alternative_angles) with respect to the hyperbolic angle.

A method to generate an appropriate conformal group is to mimic the steps of the [Möbius group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_group) as the conformal group of the ordinary [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). Pseudo-Euclidean geometry is supported by alternative complex planes where points are [split-complex numbers](wikipedia://en/893559) or [dual numbers](wikipedia://en/42169). Just as the Möbius group requires the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799), a [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042), for a complete description, so the alternative complex planes require compactification for complete description of conformal mapping. Nevertheless, the conformal group in each case is given by [linear fractional transformations](wikipedia://en/401769) on the appropriate plane.[^o4]

## Mathematical definition {#Mathematical_definition}

Given a ([Pseudo](wikipedia://en/287555)-)[Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $M$ with [conformal class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_class) $[g]$, the **conformal group** $\text{Conf}(M)$ is the group of [conformal maps](wikipedia://en/50627) from $M$ to itself.

More concretely, this is the group of angle-preserving smooth maps from $M$ to itself. However, when the signature of $[g]$ is not definite, the 'angle' is a *hyper-angle* which is potentially infinite.

For [Pseudo-Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/10962546), the definition is slightly different.[^o6] $\text{Conf}(p,q)$ is the conformal group of the manifold arising from [conformal compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_compactification) of the pseudo-Euclidean space $\mathbf{E}^{p, q}$ (sometimes identified with $\mathbb{R}^{p,q}$ after a choice of [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063)). This conformal compactification can be defined using $S^p\times S^q$, considered as a submanifold of null points in $\mathbb{R}^{p+1, q+1}$ by the inclusion $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{t})\mapsto X = (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{t})$ (where $X$ is considered as a single spacetime vector). The conformal compactification is then $S^p\times S^q$ with 'antipodal points' identified. This happens by [projectivising](wikipedia://en/242135) the space $\mathbb{R}^{p+1,q+1}$. If $N^{p,q}$ is the conformal compactification, then $\text{Conf}(p,q) := \text{Conf}(N^{p,q})$. In particular, this group includes [inversion](wikipedia://en/295844#circle_inversion) of $\mathbb{R}^{p,q}$, which is not a map from $\mathbb{R}^{p,q}$ to itself as it maps the origin to infinity, and maps infinity to the origin.

## Lie algebra of the conformal group {#Lie_algebra_of_the_conformal_group}

For Pseudo-Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^{p,q}$, the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of the conformal group is given by the basis $\{M_{\mu\nu}, P_\mu, K_\mu, D\}$ with the following commutation relations:[^o8]

$$\begin{aligned} &[D,K_\mu]= -iK_\mu \,, \\ &[D,P_\mu]= iP_\mu \,, \\ &[K_\mu,P_\nu]=2i (\eta_{\mu\nu}D-M_{\mu\nu}) \,, \\ &[K_\mu, M_{\nu\rho}] = i ( \eta_{\mu\nu} K_{\rho} - \eta_{\mu \rho} K_\nu ) \,, \\ &[P_\rho,M_{\mu\nu}] = i(\eta_{\rho\mu}P_\nu - \eta_{\rho\nu}P_\mu) \,, \\ &[M_{\mu\nu},M_{\rho\sigma}] = i (\eta_{\nu\rho}M_{\mu\sigma} + \eta_{\mu\sigma}M_{\nu\rho} - \eta_{\mu\rho}M_{\nu\sigma} - \eta_{\nu\sigma}M_{\mu\rho})\,, \end{aligned}$$

and with all other brackets vanishing. Here $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ is the [Minkowski metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_metric).

In fact, this Lie algebra is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of the Lorentz group with one more space and one more time dimension, that is, $\mathfrak{conf}(p,q) \cong \mathfrak{so}(p+1, q+1)$. It can be easily checked that the dimensions agree. To exhibit an explicit isomorphism, define

$$\begin{aligned} &J_{\mu\nu} = M_{\mu\nu} \,, \\ &J_{-1, \mu} = \frac{1}{2}(P_\mu - K_\mu) \,, \\ &J_{0, \mu} = \frac{1}{2}(P_\mu + K_\mu) \,, \\ &J_{-1, 0} = D. \end{aligned}$$

It can then be shown that the generators $J_{ab}$ with $a, b = -1, 0, \cdots, n = p+q$ obey the [Lorentz algebra](wikipedia://en/230489#Lie_algebra) relations with metric $\tilde \eta_{ab} = \operatorname{diag}(-1, +1, -1, \cdots, -1, +1, \cdots, +1)$.

## Conformal group in two spacetime dimensions {#Conformal_group_in_two_spacetime_dimensions}

For two-dimensional Euclidean space or one-plus-one dimensional spacetime, the space of conformal symmetries is much larger. In physics it is sometimes said the conformal group is infinite-dimensional, but this is not quite correct as while the Lie algebra of local symmetries is infinite dimensional, these do not necessarily extend to a Lie group of well-defined global symmetries.

For spacetime dimension $n > 2$, the local conformal symmetries all extend to global symmetries. For $n = 2$ Euclidean space, after changing to a complex coordinate $z = x + iy$ local conformal symmetries are described by the infinite dimensional space of vector fields of the form

$$l_n = -z^{n+1}\partial_z.$$

Hence the local conformal symmetries of 2d Euclidean space is the infinite-dimensional [Witt algebra](wikipedia://en/1510857).

## Conformal group of spacetime {#Conformal_group_of_spacetime}

In 1908, [Harry Bateman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Bateman) and [Ebenezer Cunningham](wikipedia://en/11767545), two young researchers at [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), broached the idea of a **conformal group of spacetime**[^o12][^o13][^o14] They argued that the [kinematics](wikipedia://en/65914) groups are perforce conformal as they preserve the quadratic form of spacetime and are akin to [orthogonal transformations](wikipedia://en/1382381), though with respect to an [isotropic quadratic form](wikipedia://en/10917170). The liberties of an [electromagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field) are not confined to kinematic motions, but rather are required only to be locally *proportional to* a transformation preserving the quadratic form. Harry Bateman's paper in 1910 studied the [Jacobian matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_matrix) of a transformation that preserves the [light cone](wikipedia://en/310474) and showed it had the conformal property (proportional to a form preserver).[^o15] Bateman and Cunningham showed that this conformal group is "the largest group of transformations leaving [Maxwell’s equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell’s_equations) structurally invariant."[^o16] The conformal group of spacetime has been denoted $C(1,3)$[^o17]

[Isaak Yaglom](wikipedia://en/11606627) has contributed to the mathematics of spacetime conformal transformations in [split-complex](wikipedia://en/893559) and [dual numbers](wikipedia://en/42169).[^o18] Since split-complex numbers and dual numbers form [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), not [fields](wikipedia://en/10603), the linear fractional transformations require a [projective line over a ring](wikipedia://en/1271901) to be bijective mappings.

It has been traditional since the work of [Ludwik Silberstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwik_Silberstein) in 1914 to use the ring of [biquaternions](wikipedia://en/1207070) to represent the [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489). For the spacetime conformal group, it is sufficient to consider [linear fractional transformations](wikipedia://en/401769) on the projective line over that ring. Elements of the spacetime conformal group were called [spherical wave transformations](wikipedia://en/42475403) by Bateman. The particulars of the spacetime quadratic form study have been absorbed into [Lie sphere geometry](wikipedia://en/16646944).

Commenting on the continued interest shown in physical science, [A. O. Barut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Barut) wrote in 1985, "One of the prime reasons for the interest in the conformal group is that it is perhaps the most important of the larger groups containing the [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487)."[^o19]
