# Subobject classifier

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subobject classifier"}

In the mathematical field of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **subobject classifier** is a special object $\Omega$ of a category such that, informally, the [subobjects](wikipedia://en/540476) of any object $X$ correspond to the morphisms from $X$ to $\Omega$. This provides an analogue of the set of [Booleans](wikipedia://en/3954077) $\{0, 1\}$ in categories other than the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829).

:::

The main use of subobject classifiers is in [topos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topos_theory), where an [elementary topos](wikipedia://en/815802) is defined as a category with a subobject classifier and certain additional requirements. In the [internal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_language) of an elementary topos, the subobject classifier is used to interpret truth values, hence the alternative name “object of truth values”.

## Introduction {#Introduction}

Let $X$ be a set. A subset $Y \subseteq X$ can be equivalently described by its [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790)

$$\begin{aligned} \chi_Y : X &\to \{0, 1\} \\ x &\mapsto \begin{cases}1 \text{ if } x \in Y \\ 0 \text{ if } x \notin Y\end{cases} \end{aligned}$$

Informally, subsets of $X$ can be identified with functions $X \to \{0, 1\}$. A subobject classifier $\Omega$ of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\mathcal{C}$ is an object which plays a similar role as $\{0, 1\}$ does in the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829): subobjects of an object $X$ can be identified with morphisms from $X$ to the subobject classifier. To recover the subset with indicator function $\chi$ in a “purely categorical way”, one can take a [pullback](wikipedia://en/841689)

$$\begin{array}{lcl} & Y & \rightarrow & \{1\} & \\ & \downarrow && \downarrow \\ & X & \underset{\chi}\rightarrow & \{0, 1\} & \\ \end{array}$$

where the function from $\{1\}$ to $\{0, 1\}$ is the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516). Indeed, the subset $Y := \{x \in X \mid \chi(x) = 1\}$, equipped with the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) $Y \to X$ (and the unique, constant map $Y \to \{1\}$) is such a pullback because it has the correct universal property since a map into $X$ which gives the constant function 1 when composed with $\chi$ is the same as a map into $Y$.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a [finitely complete category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_complete_category) (we denote the [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) by $1$). A **subobject classifier** in $\mathcal{C}$ is an object $\Omega$ together with a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) $\operatorname{true} : 1 \hookrightarrow \Omega$ such that every monomorphism is uniquely a pullback of $\operatorname{true}$.[^n1] This means that for every monomorphism $\iota : Y \hookrightarrow X$, there exists a unique morphism $\chi_\iota : X \to \Omega$, called the **characteristic function** or **classifying map** of $\iota$, such that the following diagram is a [pullback square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullback_square) (where $!$ denotes the unique morphism into the terminal object $1$):

Equivalently (assuming $\mathcal{C}$ is [locally small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_small_category)), a subobject classifier is an object $\Omega$ which [represents](wikipedia://en/844461) the [subobject functor](wikipedia://en/540476) $\operatorname{Sub} : \mathcal{C}^\operatorname{op} \to \operatorname{Set}$. That is, there exists a bijection, [natural](wikipedia://en/45022) in $X \in \mathcal{C}$, between subobjects of $X$ and morphisms $X \to \Omega$. When starting from this definition, one can recover the monomorphism $\operatorname{true} : 1 \hookrightarrow \Omega$ as the subobject of $\Omega$ corresponding to the morphism $\operatorname{id} : \Omega \to \Omega$.[^n2]

## Further examples {#Further_examples}

### Sheaves of sets {#Sheaves_of_sets}

The category of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) of sets on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* has a subobject classifier Ω which can be described as follows: For any [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) *U* of *X*, Ω(*U*) is the set of all open subsets of *U*. The terminal object is the sheaf 1 which assigns the [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) {\*} to every open set *U* of *X.* The morphism η:1 → Ω is given by the family of maps η<sub>*U*</sub> : 1(*U*) → Ω(*U*) defined by η<sub>*U*</sub>(\*)=*U* for every open set *U* of *X*. Given a sheaf *F* on *X* and a sub-sheaf *j*: *G* → *F*, the classifying morphism *χ<sub>j</sub>* : *F* → Ω is given by the family of maps *χ<sub>j,U</sub>* : *F*(*U*) → Ω(*U*), where *χ<sub>j,U</sub>*(*x*) is the union of all open sets *V* of *U* such that the restriction of *x* to *V* (in the sense of sheaves) is contained in *j<sub>V</sub>*(*G*(*V*)).

Roughly speaking an assertion inside this topos is variably true or false, and its truth value from the viewpoint of an open subset *U* is the open subset of *U* where the assertion is true.

### Presheaves {#Presheaves}

Given a small category $C$, the category of [presheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaves) $\mathrm{Set}^{C^{op}}$ (i.e. the [functor category](wikipedia://en/407039) consisting of all contravariant functors from $C$ to $\mathrm{Set}$) has a subobject classifer given by the functor sending any $c \in C$ to the set of [sieves](wikipedia://en/2163544) on $c$. The classifying morphisms are constructed quite similarly to the ones in the sheaves-of-sets example above.

### Elementary topoi {#Elementary_topoi}

Both examples above are subsumed by the following general fact: every [elementary topos](wikipedia://en/815802), defined as a category with finite [limits](wikipedia://en/39248) and [power objects](wikipedia://en/12665139), necessarily has a subobject classifier.[^o12] The two examples above are [Grothendieck topoi](wikipedia://en/21391464), and every Grothendieck topos is an elementary topos.
