# Simon problems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simon problems"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Simon problems** (or **Simon's problems**) are a series of fifteen questions posed in the year 2000 by [Barry Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon), an American [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist).[^o2][^o3] Inspired by other collections of mathematical problems and open [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138), such as [the famous list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_problems) by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), the Simon problems concern [quantum operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28physics%29#Operators_in_quantum_mechanics).[^o4] Eight of the problems pertain to the anomalous [spectral](wikipedia://en/206137) behavior of [Schrödinger operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrodinger_operator), and five concern operators that incorporate the [Coulomb potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb_potential).[^o2][^o5]

:::

In 2014, [Artur Avila](wikipedia://en/23579022) won a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for work including the solution of three Simon problems.[^o6][^o7] Among these was the problem of [proving](wikipedia://en/82285) the set of [energy levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_level) of one particular abstract quantum system was, in fact, the [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172), a challenge known as the "[Ten Martini Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Martini_Problem)" after the reward [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425) offered for solving it.[^o7][^o8]

The 2000 list was a refinement of a similar set of problems Simon had posed in 1984.[^o9][^o10]

## Context {#Context}

Background definitions for the "Coulomb energies" problems ($N$ non-relativistic particles ([electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron)) in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ with [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829) $1/2$ and an infinitely heavy [nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus) with charge $Z$ and Coulombic mutual interaction):

- $\mathcal{H}_f^{(N)}$ is the space of functions on $L^2(\mathbb{R}^{3N}; \mathbb{C}^{2N})$ which are asymmetrical under exchange of the $N$ spin and space coordinates.[^o2] Equivalently, the subspace of $(L^2(\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes \mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}$ which is asymmetrical under the exchange of the $N$ factors.
- The [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381) is $H(N, Z) := \sum_{i = 1}^N(-\Delta_i - \frac{Z}{|x_i|} ) + \sum_{i < j}\frac{1}{|x_i - x_j|}$. Here $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the coordinate of the $i$-th particle, $\Delta_i$ is the [Laplacian](wikipedia://en/174706) with respect to the coordinate $x_i$. Even if the Hamiltonian does not explicitly depend on the state of the spin sector, the presence of spin has an effect due to the asymmetry condition on the total wave-function.
- We define $E(N, Z) := \min_{\mathcal{H}_f} H(N, Z)$, that is, the [ground state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state) energy of the $(N,Z)$ system.
- We define $N_0(Z)$ to be the smallest value of $N$ such that $E(N + j, Z) = E(N, Z)$ for all positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $j$; it is known such a number always exists and is always between $Z$ and $2Z$, inclusive.[^o2]
