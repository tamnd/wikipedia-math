# Charles Parsons (philosopher)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Parsons"}

**Charles Dacre Parsons** (April 13, 1933 – April 19, 2024) was an American philosopher best known for his work in the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) and the study of the philosophy of [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant). He was professor emeritus at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). In a 2014 review of one of his books, [Stewart Shapiro](wikipedia://en/3997685) and Teresa Kouri said of Parsons: "It surely goes without saying that \[he\] is one of the most important philosophers of mathematics in our generation".[^o4]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Born on April 13, 1933, Charles Dacre Parsons was a son of Harvard sociologist [Talcott Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talcott_Parsons). He earned his Ph.D. in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1961, under the direction of [Burton Dreben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Dreben) and [Willard Van Orman Quine](wikipedia://en/33833).[^o6][^o7] He taught for many years at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) before moving to Harvard University in 1989.[^o7] He retired in 2005 as the [Edgar Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Pierce) professor of philosophy, a position formerly held by Quine.[^o7]

Parsons was an elected Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o8]

Among his doctoral students were [Michael Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Levin_%28philosopher%29), [James Higginbotham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Higginbotham),[^o9] [Peter Ludlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Ludlow), [Gila Sher](wikipedia://en/11544797), and [Øystein Linnebo](wikipedia://en/26959763).

In 2017 Parsons gave the [Gödel Lecture](wikipedia://en/69216431), titled "Gödel and the Universe of Sets".[^o10]

Parsons died in Cambridge, Massachusetts, on April 19, 2024, at the age of 91.[^o11][^o12]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

In addition to his work in logic and the philosophy of mathematics, Parsons was an editor, with [Solomon Feferman](wikipedia://en/355401) and others, of the posthumous works of [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736).[^o14] He has also written on historical figures, especially [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant),[^o15] [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416),[^o16] [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736),[^o17] and [Willard Van Orman Quine](wikipedia://en/33833).[^o18]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

### Books {#Books}

- \1983. *Mathematics in Philosophy: Selected Essays*. Ithaca, N.Y.: Cornell Univ. Press.
- \2008. *Mathematical Thought and its Objects*. Cambridge Univ. Press.
- \2012. *From Kant to Husserl: Selected Essays*. Cambridge, Massachusetts, and London: Harvard Univ. Press.
- 2014a. *Philosophy of Mathematics in the Twentieth Century: Selected Essays*. Cambridge, Massachusetts, and London: Harvard Univ. Press.

### Articles {#Articles}

- \1987. "Developing Arithmetic in Set Theory without infinity: Some Historical Remarks". *History and Philosophy of Logic*, vol. 8, pp. 201–213.
- 1990a. "The Uniqueness of the Natural Numbers". *[Iyyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyyun)*, vol. 39, pp. 13–44. ISSN 0021-3306.
- 1990b. "The Structuralist View of Mathematical Objects". *[Synthese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthese)*, vol. 84 (3), pp. 303–346.
- 2014b. "Analyticity for Realists". In *Interpreting Gödel: Critical Essays,* ed. J. Kennedy. Cambridge University Press, pp. 131–150.
