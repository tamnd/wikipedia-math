# KPP–Fisher equation

*Image: FKPPwiki.jpg.* Numerical simulation of the Fisher–KPP equation. In colors: the solution *u*(*t*,*x*); in dots : slope corresponding to the theoretical velocity of the traveling wave.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="KPP–Fisher equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Fisher-KPP equation** (named after [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824) , [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), [Ivan Petrovsky](wikipedia://en/4598657), and [Nikolai Piskunov](wikipedia://en/67394703)) also known as the **Fisher equation**, **Fisher–KPP equation**, or **KPP equation** is the [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564):

It is a kind of [reaction–diffusion system](wikipedia://en/8635379) that can be used to model population growth and wave propagation.

:::

## Details {#Details}

Fisher-KPP equation belongs to the class of [reaction–diffusion equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction–diffusion_equation): in fact, it is one of the simplest semilinear reaction-diffusion equations, the one which has the inhomogeneous term

$$f(u,x,t) = r u (1-u),\,$$

which can exhibit traveling wave solutions that switch between equilibrium states given by $f(u) = 0$. Such equations occur, e.g., in [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology), [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology), [combustion](wikipedia://en/5638), [crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallization), [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics), and in general [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423) problems.

Fisher proposed this equation in his 1937 paper *The wave of advance of advantageous genes* in the context of [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466) to describe the spatial spread of an advantageous [allele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele) and explored its travelling wave solutions.[^o4] For every wave speed $c \geq 2 \sqrt{r D}$ ($c \geq 2$ in dimensionless form) it admits travelling [wave](wikipedia://en/33516) solutions of the form

$$u(x,t)=v(x \pm ct)\equiv v(z),\,$$

where $\textstyle v$ is increasing and

$$\lim_{z\rightarrow-\infty}v\left( z\right) =0,\quad\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty }v\left( z\right) =1.$$

That is, the solution switches from the equilibrium state *u* = 0 to the equilibrium state *u* = 1. No such solution exists for *c* \< 2.[^o4][^o5][^o6] The wave shape for a given wave speed is unique. The travelling-wave solutions are stable against near-field perturbations, but not to far-field perturbations which can thicken the tail. One can prove using the comparison principle and super-solution theory that all solutions with compact initial data converge to waves with the minimum speed.[^o7]

For the special wave speed $c=\pm 5/\sqrt{6}$, all solutions can be found in a closed form,[^o8] with

$$v(z) = \left( 1 + C \mathrm{exp}\left(\mp{z}/{\sqrt6}\right) \right)^{-2}$$

where $C$ is arbitrary, and the above limit conditions are satisfied for $C>0$.

Proof of the existence of travelling wave solutions and analysis of their properties is often done by the [phase space method](wikipedia://en/10416232).

## KPP equation {#KPP_equation}

In the same year (1937) as Fisher, Kolmogorov, Petrovsky and Piskunov[^o5] introduced the more general reaction-diffusion equation

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}=F(u)$$

where $F$ is a sufficiently smooth function with the properties that $F(0)=F(1)=0, F'(0)=r>0$ and $F(v)>0, F'(v)<r$ for all $0<v<1$. This too has the travelling wave solutions discussed above. Fisher's equation is obtained upon setting $F(u)=ru(1-u)$ and rescaling the $x$ coordinate by a factor of $\sqrt{D}$. A more general example is given by $F(u)=r u(1-u^q)$ with $q>0$.[^o10][^o11][^o12] Kolmogorov, Petrovsky and Piskunov[^o5] discussed the example with $q=2$ in the context of [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268).

The minimum speed of a KPP-type traveling wave is given by

$$2\sqrt{\left.\frac{dF}{du}\right |_{u=0}}$$

which differs from other type of waves, see for example [ZFK-type waves](wikipedia://en/70931046).
