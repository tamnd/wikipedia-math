# Edge-graceful labeling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edge-graceful labeling"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **edge-graceful labeling** is a type of [graph labeling](wikipedia://en/1609861) for [simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph), [connected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph) in which no two distinct [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) connect the same two distinct [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and no edge connects a vertex to itself.

:::

Edge-graceful labelings were first introduced by Sheng-Ping Lo in his seminal paper.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a graph $G$, we denote the set of its edges by $E(G)$ and that of its vertices by $V(G)$. Let $q$ be the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of $E(G)$ and $p$ be that of $V(G)$. Once a labeling of the edges is given, a vertex of the graph is labeled by the sum of the labels of the edges incident to it, [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) $p$. Or, in symbols, the induced labeling on a vertex is given by

$$V(u)=\Sigma E(e) \mod |V(G)|$$

where $V(u)$ is the resulting value for the vertex $u$ and $E(e)$ is the existing value of an edge $e$ incident to $u$.

The problem is to find a labeling for the edges such that all the labels from $1$ to $q$ are used once and that the induced labels on the vertices run from $0$ to $p - 1$. In other words, the resulting set of labels for the edges should be $\{1, 2, \ldots, q\}$, each value being used once, and that for the vertices should be $\{0, 1, \ldots, p - 1\}$.

A graph $G$ is said to be edge-graceful if it admits an edge-graceful labeling.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Cycles {#Cycles}

*Image: Edge graceful c5.svg.* An edge-graceful labeling of $C_{5}$

Consider the [cycle](wikipedia://en/878962) with three vertices, $C_{3}$. This is simply a triangle. One can label the edges 1, 2, and 3, and check directly that, along with the induced labeling on the vertices, this gives an edge-graceful labeling. Similar to paths, $C_{m}$ is edge-graceful when $m$ is odd and not when $m$ is even.[^o8]

### Paths {#Paths}

Consider a [path](wikipedia://en/638889) with two vertices, $P_{2}$. Here the only possibility is to label the only edge in the graph 1. The induced labeling on the two vertices are both 1. So $P_{2}$ is not edge-graceful.

Appending an edge and a vertex to $P_{2}$ gives $P_{3}$, the path with three vertices. Denote the vertices by $v_{1}$, $v_{2}$, and $v_{3}$. Label the two edges in the following way: the edge $(v_{1}, v_{2})$ is labeled 1 and $(v_{2}, v_{3})$ labeled 2. The induced labelings on $v_{1}$, $v_{2}$, and $v_{3}$ are then 1, 0, and 2 respectively. This is an edge-graceful labeling and so $P_{3}$ is edge-graceful.

Similarly, one can check that $P_{4}$ is not edge-graceful.

In general, $P_{m}$ is edge-graceful when $m$ is odd and not edge-graceful when it is even. This follows from a necessary condition for edge-gracefulness.

:::

## A necessary condition {#A_necessary_condition}

Lo gave a necessary condition for a graph with $q$ edges and $p$ vertices to be edge-graceful:[^o2]$q(q + 1)$ must be [congruent](wikipedia://en/20087#Congruence) to $\tfrac{p(p - 1)}{2} \bmod p$. In symbols:

$$q(q+1) \equiv \frac{p(p-1)}{2} \mod p.$$

This is referred to as **Lo's condition** in the literature.[^o11] This follows from the fact that the sum of the labels of the vertices is twice the sum of the edges, modulo $p$. This is useful for disproving a graph is edge-graceful. For instance, one can apply this directly to the path and cycle examples given above.

## Further selected results {#Further_selected_results}

- The [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) is not edge-graceful.
- The [star graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_graph) $S_m$ (a central node and *m* legs of length 1) is edge-graceful when *m* is [even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_number) and not when *m* is [odd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_number).[^o13]
- The [friendship graph](wikipedia://en/24363442) $F_m$ is edge-graceful when *m* is odd and not when it is even.
- [Regular trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tree), $T_{m,n}$ (depth *n* with each non-leaf node emitting *m* new vertices) are edge-graceful when *m* is even for any value *n* but not edge-graceful whenever *m* is odd.[^o14]
- The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) on *n* vertices, $K_n$, is edge-graceful unless *n* is [singly even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singly_even), $n=2\mod 4$.
- The [ladder graph](wikipedia://en/19657952) is never edge-graceful.
