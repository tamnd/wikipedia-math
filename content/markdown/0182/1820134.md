# Harry Golombek

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Harry Golombek"}

**Harold Golombek**[^o3][^o4] [^o5][^o6] [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) (1 March 1911 – 7 January 1995) was a British [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) player, chess author, and wartime codebreaker.[^o7][^o8] He was three times [British chess champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Chess_Championship), in 1947, 1949, and 1955 and finished second in 1948.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Lambeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambeth) to [Polish-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Jewish)[^o10] parents. He was the chess correspondent of the newspaper *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* from 1945 to 1985, after [Stuart Milner-Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Milner-Barry). He was a [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE) official, and served as arbiter for several important events, including the [Candidates' Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates'_Tournament) of 1959 in [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia), and the 1963 World Chess Championship match between [Mikhail Botvinnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Botvinnik) and [Tigran Petrosian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigran_Petrosian). He also edited the game collections of [Capablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Raúl_Capablanca) and [Réti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Réti), and was a respected author. He was editor of *[British Chess Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Chess_Magazine)* from 1938 to 1940, and its overseas editor in the 1960s and 1970s. Golombek also translated several chess books from Russian into English.

On the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) in September 1939, Golombek was in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires), [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina), competing in the [Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad) for Britain alongside [C. H. O'D. Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._H._O'D._Alexander) and [Stuart Milner-Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Milner-Barry).[^o11] They immediately returned to the UK, and were soon recruited into [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), the wartime [codebreaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codebreaking) centre. Golombek worked in [Hut 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_8),[^o12] the section responsible for solving German Naval [Enigma](wikipedia://en/9256), moving to another section in October/November 1942.[^o13] After the war he lived at 35 Albion Crescent, [Chalfont St Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalfont_St_Giles). He was unusual among public figures in replying with care to letters from unknown people, such as young schoolboys, from this address.

Golombek represented England nine times in the Chess Olympiad.[^o14] He earned the title of [International Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Master) in 1950 and was awarded that of [Honorary Grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_%28chess%29#Honorary_grandmasters) in 1985.[^o15][^o16] He was the first British player to qualify for an [Interzonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interzonal) tournament.

Golombek studied [philology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology) at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London),[^o17] having been a pupil at [Wilson's Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson's_School), Camberwell.[^o16] He was appointed OBE in 1966, the first to be so honoured for services to chess.[^o17]

Golombek died 7 January 1995.[^o18]
