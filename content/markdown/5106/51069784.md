# Dual uniform polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dual uniform polyhedron"}

A **dual uniform polyhedron** is the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of a [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223). Where a uniform polyhedron is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), a dual uniform polyhedron is [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Enumeration"}

## Enumeration {#Enumeration}

The face-transitive polyhedra comprise a set of 9 regular polyhedra, two finite sets comprising 66 non-regular polyhedra, and two infinite sets:

- 5 regular convex [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905): [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521), [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458), [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), and [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). The regular octahedron is dual to the cube, and the regular icosahedron is dual to the regular dodecahedron. The regular tetrahedron is [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual_polyhedron), meaning its dual is the regular tetrahedron itself.[^n1]
- 4 regular star [Kepler&ndash;Poinsot solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler&ndash;Poinsot_solid): [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441), [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843), [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898), and [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878). The great dodecahedron is dual to the small stellated dodecahedron, and the great icosahedron is dual to the great stellated dodecahedron.
- 13 convex [Catalan solids](wikipedia://en/564393), which are dual to the uniform convex [Archimedean solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_solids).
- 53 star polyhedra, which are dual to the [uniform star polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_star_polyhedra).
- The infinite series of [bipyramids](wikipedia://en/4153), which are dual to the uniform [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), both convex and star.
- The infinite series of [trapezohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezohedra), which are dual to the uniform [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851), both convex and star.

The full set are described by Wenninger, together with instructions for constructing models, in his book *Dual Models*.

## Dorman Luke construction {#Dorman_Luke_construction}

*Image: DormanLuke.svg.* The illustration here shows the vertex figure (red) of the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) being used to derive the corresponding face (blue) of the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631).

For a [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223), each face of the dual polyhedron may be derived from the original polyhedron's corresponding [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) by using the **Dorman Luke construction**.[^o7] Dorman Luke's construction proceeds as follows:

1. Mark the points $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ of each edge connected to the vertex $V$ (in this case, the midpoints) such that $VA = VB = VC = VD$.
1. Draw the vertex figure $ABCD$.
1. Draw the circumcircle of $ABCD$.
1. Draw the line tangent to the circumcircle at each corner $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$.
1. Mark the points $E$, $F$, $G$, $H$, where each two adjacent tangent lines meet.

The line segments $EF$, $FG$, $GH$, $HE$ are already drawn, as parts of the tangent lines. The polygon *EFGH* is the face of the dual polyhedron that corresponds to the original vertex $V$.

In this example, the size of the vertex figure was chosen so that its circumcircle lies on the [intersphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersphere) of the cuboctahedron, which also becomes the intersphere of the dual rhombic dodecahedron. Dorman Luke's construction can only be used when a polyhedron has such an intersphere so that the vertex figure has a circumcircle. For instance, it can be applied to the [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223).
