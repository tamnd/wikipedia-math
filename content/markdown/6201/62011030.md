# William Kantor

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Kantor"}

**William M. Kantor** (born September 19, 1944) is an American mathematician who works in [finite group theory](wikipedia://en/323707) and [finite geometries](wikipedia://en/267061), particularly in computational aspects of these subjects.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Kantor graduated with a bachelor's degree from [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College) in 1964.[^o4] He went on to graduate studies at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin), receiving his PhD in 1968 under the supervision of [Peter Dembowski](wikipedia://en/62025208) and [R. H. Bruck](wikipedia://en/36687122). He then worked at the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago) from 1968 to 1971 before moving in 1971 to the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon), where he remained for the rest of his career.

Kantor's research mostly involves [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707), often in relation to [finite geometries](wikipedia://en/267061) and [computation](wikipedia://en/618584). Algorithms developed by him have found use, for example, in the [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28computer_algebra_system%29) computer algebra system.[^o6]

Kantor has written over 170 papers,[^o7] and has advised 7 PhD students.[^o5]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- In 2013, Kantor was named a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) as a member of the inaugural class of fellows.[^o17]
- In 2004, a conference "Finite geometries, groups, and computation" was held in honor of Kantor's 60th birthday.[^o18]
- In 1998, Kantor gave an invited talk at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o19][^o20]
