# Parabolic Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parabolic Lie algebra"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **parabolic Lie algebra** $\mathfrak p$ is a subalgebra of a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) $\mathfrak g$ satisfying one of the following two conditions:

:::

- $\mathfrak p$ contains a maximal [solvable](wikipedia://en/2966308) subalgebra (a [Borel subalgebra](wikipedia://en/62074925)) of $\mathfrak g$;
- the [orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833) with respect to the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386) of $\mathfrak p$ in $\mathfrak g$ is isomorphic to the [nilradical](wikipedia://en/22468313) of $\mathfrak p$.

These conditions are equivalent over an [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed) [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero), such as the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers). If the field $\mathbb F$ is not algebraically closed, then the first condition is replaced by the assumption that

- $\mathfrak p\otimes_{\mathbb F}\overline{\mathbb F}$ contains a Borel subalgebra of $\mathfrak g\otimes_{\mathbb F}\overline{\mathbb F}$

where $\overline{\mathbb F}$ is the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of $\mathbb F$.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For the general linear Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb F)$, a parabolic subalgebra is the stabilizer of a partial [flag](wikipedia://en/390646) of $\mathbb F^n$, i.e. a sequence of nested linear subspaces. For a complete flag, the stabilizer gives a Borel subalgebra. For a single linear subspace $\mathbb F^k\subset \mathbb F^n$, one gets a maximal parabolic subalgebra $\mathfrak p$, and the space of possible choices is the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) $\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)$.

In general, for a complex simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, parabolic subalgebras are in [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) with subsets of simple [roots](wikipedia://en/277087), i.e. subsets of the nodes of the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109).

:::
