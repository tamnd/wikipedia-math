# Mark Pinsker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mark Pinsker"}

*Image: Pinsker mark.jpg.* 1976 in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad)

**Mark Semenovich Pinsker** (Марк Семено́вич Пи́нскер; April 24, 1925 – December 23, 2003) or **Mark Shlemovich Pinsker** (Марк Шлемо́вич Пи́нскер) was a noted [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician in the fields of [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), and [communication networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_network).

:::

Pinsker studied stochastic processes under [A. N. Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._N._Kolmogorov) in the 1950s, and later worked at the [Institute for Information Transmission Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Information_Transmission_Problems) (IITP), [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences), [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow).

His accomplishments included a classic paper on the [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) theory of dynamical systems which introduced the maximal partition with zero entropy, later known as [Pinsker's partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinsker's_partition). His work in mathematical statistics was devoted mostly to the applications of information theory, including asymptotically sufficient statistics for parameter estimation and nonparametric estimation; [Pinsker's inequality](wikipedia://en/20064401) is named after him. He also produced notable results in the theory of switching networks and complexity problems in coding theory.

Pinsker received the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) [Claude E. Shannon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award) in 1978, and the [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal) in 1996.[^o2]
