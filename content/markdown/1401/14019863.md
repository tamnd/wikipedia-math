# Multiple of the median

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiple of the median"}

A **multiple of the median** (**MoM**) is a measure of how far an individual test result [deviates](wikipedia://en/9252911) from the [median](wikipedia://en/18837). MoM is commonly used to report the results of [medical screening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screening_%28medicine%29) tests, particularly where the results of the individual tests are highly [variable](wikipedia://en/23773247).[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

MoM was originally used as a method to normalize data from participating laboratories of [Alpha-fetoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-fetoprotein) (AFP) so that individual test results could be compared. 35 years later, it is the established standard for reporting maternal serum screening results.[^o5]

An MoM for a test result for a patient can be determined by the following:

$$MoM(Patient) = \frac{Result(Patient)}{Median(PatientPopulation)}$$

As an example, [Alpha-fetoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-fetoprotein) (AFP) testing is used to screen for a [neural tube defect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_tube_defect) (NTD) during the second trimester of pregnancy. If the median AFP result at 16 weeks of [gestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation) is 30 ng/mL and a pregnant woman's AFP result at that same [gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_age) is 60 ng/mL, then her MoM is equal to 60/30 = 2.0. In other words, her AFP result is 2 times higher than median.
