# David Kelly (mathematics educator)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Kelly"}

**David C. Kelly** (1940–2025) was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Hampshire College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_College) in [Amherst, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst,_Massachusetts). Kelly founded and directed the [Hampshire College Summer Studies in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/242549) program (HCSSiM) for talented high-school students.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Kelly was originally from [New Rochelle, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York), but moved frequently as the child of a military father. He held an AB degree from [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), an SM from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) (after stopping out from a doctoral program there) and an AM from [Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College).[^o5]

## Career and later life {#Career_and_later_life}

Kelly taught at [Talladega College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talladega_College), [New College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College_of_Florida) and [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College) before joining Hampshire College in 1970 as one of its founding faculty members. After ten years of teaching summer math programs at [St. Paul's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_School_%28New_Hampshire%29) and [University of New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Hampshire), in 1971 he founded the Hampshire College Summer Studies in Mathematics, an annual six-week program for mathematically talented high-school students. He continued to direct the program until his death.[^o5]

He died on June 10, 2025, shortly before his 85th birthday. He was survived by his husband of 17 years and partner of 51 years, Al McNeely.[^o5]

## Yellow pigs and 17 {#Yellow_pigs_and_17}

*Image: YellowPigsDayCake2004.JPG.* Yellow Pigs Day cake (2004)

After meeting as Princeton students in the early 1960s, later in the decade Kelly and fellow mathematician [Michael Spivak](wikipedia://en/333393) created "[Yellow Pigs Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Pigs_Day)," an annual celebration of mathematics and the number [17](wikipedia://en/208288). The HCSSiM program organizes the holiday each year on July 17, commemorating the day with a reunion, mathematics, food and songs. Kelly was expertly familiar with the properties of the number 17, and gave a special lecture on the subject each summer.[^o7][^o8][^o9]

When Kelly retired from the faculty in 2015, Hampshire College officially changed the campus speed limit to 17 mph in his honor, reflecting his lifelong fascination with the number.[^o10]

## Legacy and recognition {#Legacy_and_recognition}

Kelly was well known for his inquiry-based teaching style. He often used the phrases "Interesting, if true" and "Let's find out" to encourage students to explore mathematical ideas.[^o12] Through his leadership at [HCSSiM](wikipedia://en/242549), he fostered a unique mathematical community that influenced generations of students.[^o13] Kelly was elected to the 2026 class of Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), but died before he could celebrate this honor.[^o14]

### *Hunting Yellow Pigs* documentary {#Hunting_Yellow_Pigs_documentary}

In 2026, the feature-length documentary *[Hunting Yellow Pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_Yellow_Pigs)* was officially released. Directed by HCSSiM alumnus **Jon Roberts**, who executive-produced with **Ming-I Huang**, the film explores the unique community and "questions-first" philosophy that Kelly established at HCSSiM. It includes interviews with dozens of prominent mathematicians and alumni, including [Eric Lander](wikipedia://en/610184), [Lisa Randall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Randall), [Steven Strogatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Strogatz) and [Lisa Su](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Su),[^o13][^o16] and chronicles the program's quirky traditions and its influence on American mathematics education.[^o17] The film was selected as "Best Documentary" by Tokyo's Golden Harvest Film Festival.[^o18]
