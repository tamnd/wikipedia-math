# Johann Andreas Segner

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Andreas Segner"}

**Johann Andreas von Segner** (Segner János András, Johann Andreas von Segner, Ján Andrej Segner, Iohannes Andreas de Segner; October 9, 1704 – October 5, 1777) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) scientist of German descent. He was born in the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), in the former Hungarian capital city of Pozsony, or Pressburg (today [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava)).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Johann Segner was born to Johann Michael von Segner and his wife Christine von Segner (née Fischer) on probably the 10 October 1704.[^o4] Other sources mention also the 4 or the 9 October.[^o4] His father was a tax clerk. His paternal ancestors came from [Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styria) to Pressburg[^o5] in the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hungary_1700-1919); by the 18th century. In 1596 the family was granted nobility for their military accomplishments by [Rudolph II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor).[^o4] He studied at Pressburg and [Debrecen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debrecen). In April 1725, Segner began studying at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena).[^o4] Under the guidance of [Georg Erhard Hamberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Erhard_Hamberger), Segner developed an evidence for the [cartesian rule of signs](wikipedia://en/2641493).[^o4] In his third year at Jena, he began to teach other students in mathematics.[^o6] During his stay in Jena, he was supported by [Hermann Friedrich Teichmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Friedrich_Teichmeyer), who lectured at the faculty of medicine.[^o7] In October 1729, he graduated under Simon Paul Hirscher from the faculty of medicine.[^o7] Following he returned to Pressburg where he worked as a medic until December 1731.[^o7] Following he worked temporarily as the city [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) in Debrecen until early 1732.[^o4] In 1732 he returned to Jena where he requested a degree from the faculty of philosophy and also the possibility to give lectures in the faculty of philosophy.[^o7] He graduated in May 1732 he began to lecture in 1733. In September the same year he was nominated a professor in the faculty of philosophy where he soon lectured the metaphysics of [Christian Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wolff_%28philosopher%29).[^o7]

## University of Göttingen {#University_of_Göttingen}

The university in Göttingen was established after a visit by [George II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain) to [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover) in 1732.[^o9] After Hamberger was denied his resignation from the University of Jena, he suggested von Segner instead.[^o9] 1735 Segner became the professor of mathematics and physics at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^o9] As in January 1736 the professor for medicine died, he also lectured in the faculty of medicine.[^o9]

## University of Halle {#University_of_Halle}

After [Christian Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wolff_%28philosopher%29) died, the Prussian King [Friedrich II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_the_Great) requested from the mathematician [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) advice on who could be succeed Wolff at the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_of_Halle-Wittenberg).[^o11] Euler suggested [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784) who did not accept.[^o11] Then Euler suggested von Segner, who requested the recognition of his Hungarian title and an extraordinary treatment at the university in return of his acceptance.[^o11] In 1755 he became a professor at Halle, where he divided the lectures in mathematics and physics with [Johann Joachim Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Joachim_Lange).[^o12] In Halle he also established an observatory. Von Segner died on the 5 October 1777.[^o13]

## Connections {#Connections}

One of the best-known scientists of his age, Segner was a member of the academies of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), and [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences). According to [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), [as of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_of) February 2013, he has over 66 thousand [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy), out of the total 170 thousand mathematicians in the database.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He was the first scientist to use the reactive force of water and constructed the first water-jet, the [Segner wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segner_wheel), which resembles one type of modern [lawn sprinkler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation_sprinkler). Segner, also produced the first proof of [Descartes' rule of signs](wikipedia://en/2641493). Historians of science remember him as the father of the [water turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_turbine). The lunar crater [Segner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segner_%28crater%29) is named after him, as is asteroid [28878 Segner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28878_Segner).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1732, he married Carolina Sophia, the daughter of Hermann Friedrich Teichmeier.[^o7] They had three children[^o7] of which two reached adulthood.[^o7]
