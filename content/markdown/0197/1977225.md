# Codd's cellular automaton

*Image: Codd CA RepeaterEmitter.gif.* A simple configuration in Codd's cellular automaton. Signals pass along wire made of cells in state 1 (blue) sheathed by cells in state 2 (red). Two signal trains circulate a loop and are duplicated at a T-junction onto an open-ended section of wire. The first (7-0) causes the sheathed end of the wire to become exposed. The second (6-0) re-sheathes the exposed end, leaving the wire one cell longer than before.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Codd's cellular automaton"}

**Codd's cellular automaton** is a [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342) (CA) devised by the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) [Edgar F. Codd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_F._Codd) in 1968. It was designed to recreate the computation- and construction-universality of [von Neumann's CA](wikipedia://en/2234009) but with fewer states: 8 instead of 29. Codd showed that it was possible to make a self-reproducing machine in his CA, in a similar way to von Neumann's [universal constructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_Universal_Constructor), but never gave a complete implementation.

:::

## History {#History}

In the 1940s and '50s, [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) posed the following problem:[^o3]

- What kind of logical organization is sufficient for an automaton to be able to reproduce itself?

He was able to construct a [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/2234009) with 29 states, and with it a [universal constructor](wikipedia://en/8597086). Codd, building on von Neumann's work, found a simpler machine with eight states.[^o4] This modified von Neumann's question:

- What kind of logical organization is *necessary* for an automaton to be able to reproduce itself?

Three years after Codd's work, Edwin Roger Banks showed a 4-state CA in his PhD thesis that was also capable of universal computation and construction, but again did not implement a self-reproducing machine.[^o5] John Devore, in his 1973 masters thesis, tweaked Codd's rules to greatly reduce the size of Codd's design. A simulation of Devore's design was demonstrated at the third Artificial Life conference in 1992, showing the final steps of construction and activation of the offspring pattern, but full self-replication was not simulated until the 2000s using [Golly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golly_%28program%29). [Christopher Langton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Langton) made another tweak to Codd's cellular automaton in 1984 to create [Langton's loops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton's_loops), exhibiting self-replication with far fewer cells than that needed for self-reproduction in previous rules, at the cost of removing the ability for universal computation and construction.[^o6]

### Comparison of CA rulesets {#Comparison_of_CA_rulesets}

::: {#o8 .table cue=table}

| CA | number of states | symmetries | computation- and construction-universal | size of self-reproducing machine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| von Neumann | 29 | none | yes | 130,622 cells |
| Codd | 8 | rotations | yes | 283,126,588 cells[^o9] |
| Devore | 8 | rotations | yes | 94,794 cells |
| Banks IV ([Banks IV Cellular Automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banks_IV_Cellular_Automaton)) | 2 - 4 [^o11][^o5] | rotations and reflections | yes | Somewhere around 100,000,000,000 cells |
| Langton's loops | 8 | rotations | no | 86 cells |

:::

## Specification {#Specification}

*Image: Codd CA ConstructionArm.gif.* The construction arm in Codd's CA can be moved into position using the commands listed in the text. Here the arm turns left, then right, then writes a cell before retracting along the same path.

Codd's CA has eight states determined by a [von Neumann neighborhood](wikipedia://en/8485219) with rotational symmetry.

## Universal computer-constructor {#Universal_computer-constructor}

Codd designed a self-replicating computer in the cellular automaton, based on [Wang's W-machine](wikipedia://en/6640489). However, the design was so colossal that it evaded implementation until 2009, when Tim Hutton constructed an explicit configuration.[^o9] There were some minor errors in Codd's design, so Hutton's implementation differs slightly, in both the configuration and the ruleset.
