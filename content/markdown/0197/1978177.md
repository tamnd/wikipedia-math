# William Alvin Howard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Alvin Howard"}

*Image: William Alvin Howard May 2004.jpg.* Howard in 2004

**William Alvin Howard** (December 11, 1926 – March 13, 2026) was a Canadian-born American mathematician and [proof theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_theorist) best known for his work demonstrating formal similarity between [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) and the [simply typed lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/1986011) that has come to be known as the [Curry–Howard correspondence](wikipedia://en/254299). He was also active in the theory of [proof-theoretic ordinals](wikipedia://en/16084282).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

William Alvin Howard was born in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver), [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia), Canada on December 11, 1926.[^o3] He received an undergraduate degree from the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) and later received a master’s degree from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago).[^o3]

Howard earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1956 for his dissertation "k-fold recursion and well-ordering".[^o4] He was a student of [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574).

The [Howard ordinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_ordinal) (also known as the Bachmann–Howard ordinal) was named after him.

Howard was the first to carry out an ordinal analysis of the intuitionistic theory $\mathbf{ID}^i_1(\mathcal O)$ of inductive definitions.[^o5]<sup>p.27</sup>

He was elected to the 2018 class of [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6]

Howard died in Chicago on March 13, 2026, at the age of 99.[^o3]
