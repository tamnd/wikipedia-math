# Conic constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conic constant"}

*Image: Conic constant.svg.* An illustration of various conic constants

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **conic constant** (or **Schwarzschild constant**,[^o2] after [Karl Schwarzschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Schwarzschild)) is a quantity describing [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673), and is represented by the letter *K*. The constant is given by

$$K = -e^2,$$

where $e$ is the [eccentricity](wikipedia://en/1239472) of the conic section.

:::

The equation for a conic section with apex at the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) and tangent to the y axis is

$$y^2-2Rx+(K+1)x^2 = 0$$

or alternately

$$x = \dfrac{y^2}{R+\sqrt{R^2-(K+1)y^2}}$$

where *R* is the [radius of curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius_of_curvature_%28optics%29) at $x = 0$.

This formulation is used in [geometric optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_optics) to specify [oblate elliptical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblate_spheroid) ($K > 0$), [spherical](wikipedia://en/27859) ($K = 0$), [prolate elliptical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolate_spheroid) ($0 > K > -1$), [parabolic](wikipedia://en/145845) ($K = -1$), and [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/145844) ($K < -1$) lens and mirror surfaces. When the [paraxial approximation](wikipedia://en/2643603) is valid, the optical surface can be treated as a spherical surface with the same radius.
