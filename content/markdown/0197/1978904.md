# Latin letters used in mathematics, science, and engineering

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Latin letters used in mathematics, science, and engineering"}

Many letters of the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), both capital and small, are used in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science), and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) to denote by convention specific or abstracted constants, variables of a certain type, units, multipliers, or physical entities. Certain letters, when combined with special formatting, take on special meaning.

:::

Below is an alphabetical list of the letters of the alphabet with some of their uses. The field in which the convention applies is mathematics unless otherwise noted.

## Typographical variation {#Typographical_variation}

Some common conventions:

- Most symbols are written in [italics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italic_type).
- [Vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometry%29) can be denoted in [boldface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boldface).
- Sets of [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) are typically bold or [blackboard bold](wikipedia://en/40276).

## Aa {#Aa}

- A represents:
  - the first point of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654)[^o7]
  - the digit "ten" in [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263)[^o8] and other positional numeral systems with a radix of 11 or greater[^o9]
  - the unit [ampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere) for electric current in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939)[^o10]
  - the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a figure[^o11]
  - the [mass number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_number) or [nucleon number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_number) of an element in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry)[^o12]
  - the [Helmholtz free energy](wikipedia://en/255447) of a closed thermodynamic system of constant pressure and temperature[^o13]
  - a [vector potential](wikipedia://en/422936), in electromagnetics it can refer to the [magnetic vector potential](wikipedia://en/1191067)[^o14]
  - with a subscript, an [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) on that many objects
  - an [Abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) in abstract algebra
  - the [Glaisher–Kinkelin constant](wikipedia://en/2076831)[^o15]
  - [atomic weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_Weight), denoted by *A*<sub>r</sub> [^o26]
  - [work](wikipedia://en/149861) in [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586)[^o26]
  - the [pre-exponential factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-exponential_factor)[^o26] in the [Arrhenius Equation](wikipedia://en/47011)[^o16]
  - [electron affinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_affinity)[^o26]
  - A [blood type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_type)[^o17]
- $\mathbb{A}$ represents the [algebraic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_numbers)[^o18] or affine space in [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_Geometry).
  - A [spectral type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_type)
- a represents:
  - the first side of a triangle (opposite point A)[^o7]
  - the [scale factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_factor_%28Universe%29) of the expanding universe in [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology)[^o19]
  - the [acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443) in [mechanics equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equations_of_motion)[^o11]
  - the first constant in a [linear equation](wikipedia://en/17570)
  - a constant in a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000)
  - the unit [are](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hectare#Are) for area (100 m<sup>2</sup>)[^o20]
  - the unit prefix [atto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atto-) (10<sup>−18</sup>)[^o21]
  - the first term in a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) or [series](wikipedia://en/15287)[^o22]
  - [Reflectance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflectance)

## Bb {#Bb}

- B represents:
  - the digit "11" in [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263)[^o8] and other positional numeral systems with a radix of 12 or greater[^o9]
  - the second point of a triangle[^o7]
  - a [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) (also denoted by ℬ ($\mathcal{B}$) or $\mathbb{B}$)[^o24]
  - a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) of a vector space or of a [filter](wikipedia://en/19719) (both also denoted by ℬ ($\mathcal{B}$))
  - in econometrics and time-series statistics it is often used for the backshift or [lag operator](wikipedia://en/1481533), the formal parameter of the lag polynomial
  - the [magnetic field](wikipedia://en/36563), denoted $\textbf{B}$ or $\vec{B}$
- *B* with various [subscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscript) represents several variations of [Brun's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brun's_constant) and [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262); it can also be used to mean the [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964).
  - [B meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_meson)
  - A [blood type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_type)
  - [Boron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boron)
  - [Buoyancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buoyancy)
  - [Bulk modulus](wikipedia://en/1092110)
  - [Luminance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminance)
  - A [spectral type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_type)
- b represents:
  - the second side of a triangle (opposite point B)
  - the [impact parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_parameter) in nuclear [scattering](wikipedia://en/164483)
  - the second constant in a [linear equation](wikipedia://en/17570)
  - usually with an index, sometimes with an arrow over it, a [basis vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_vector)
  - a [breadth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadth)[^o26]
  - the [molality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molality) of a solution[^o26]
  - [Bottom quark](wikipedia://en/47641)
  - [Barn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barn_%28unit%29) ($10^{-24}$ cm${}^{2}$)

## Cc {#Cc}

- C represents:
  - the third point of a triangle
  - the digit "12" in [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263) and other positional numeral systems with a radix of 13 or greater
  - the unit [coulomb](wikipedia://en/47719) of electrical charge[^o26]
  - [capacitance](wikipedia://en/140711) in electrical theory
  - with indices denoting the number of combinations, a binomial coefficient
  - together with a degree symbol (°), the [Celsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) measurement of temperature = °C[^o26]
  - the [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of a circle or other closed curve[^o27]
  - with a subscript, a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) on that many vertices
  - with a subscript, a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of that order
  - the [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) of a set (lowercase *c* and the symbol ∁ are also used)
  - an arbitrary [category](wikipedia://en/53759)
  - the [number concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_concentration)[^o26]\*
  - [Carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon)
  - [Heat capacity](wikipedia://en/194227)
  - The [C programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29)
  - [Cunningham correction factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunningham_correction_factor)
- $\mathbb{C}$ represents the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers).
- A vertically elongated C with an integer subscript n sometimes denotes the n-th coefficient of a formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958).
- c represents:
  - the unit prefix [centi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centi-) (10<sup>−2</sup>)[^o26]
  - the [amount concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amount_concentration) in chemistry[^o26]
  - the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light) in vacuum[^o28]
  - the third side of a triangle (opposite corner C)
  - the third constant in a [linear equation](wikipedia://en/17570)

  - [Charm quark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charm_quark)
  - [Speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853)
  - [Specific heat capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat_capacity)
- Lowercase [Fraktur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraktur) $\mathfrak{c}$ denotes the [cardinality of the set of real numbers](wikipedia://en/1574901) (the "continuum"), or, equivalently, of the power set of natural numbers.

## Zz {#Zz}

- Z represents:
  - the unit prefix [zetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zetta) (10<sup>21</sup>)[^o26]
  - the [atomic number](wikipedia://en/673) or [proton number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_number) of an element in chemistry[^o26]
  - a standardized normal [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685)
  - [Partition function](wikipedia://en/16846849)
  - in meteorology, the [radar reflectivity factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DBZ_%28meteorology%29)
  - [Electrical impedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_impedance)
  - [Z boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_and_Z_bosons)
  - [Compressibility factor](wikipedia://en/2131266)
- $\mathbb{Z}$ represents the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563)[^o67]
- z represents:
  - the unit prefix zepto (10<sup>−21</sup>)[^o26]
  - the coordinate on the third or vertical axis in three [dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) space[^o26]
    - The [vertical axis](wikipedia://en/9023486) or [altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude) in an aerospace vehicle
  - the view depth in [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics), see also "[z-buffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z-buffering)"
  - the argument of a [complex function](wikipedia://en/5759), or any other variable used to represent a [complex value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_value)
  - in astronomy, wavelength [redshift](wikipedia://en/26262)[^o68]p. 9
  - a third unknown [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109)
  - the [collision frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision_frequency) of A with A is denoted z<sub>A</sub>(A)[^o69]
  - the [collision frequency factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision_frequency_factor) is denoted z<sub>AB</sub>[^o26]
