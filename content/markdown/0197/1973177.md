# Arithmetic geometry

branches

*Image: Example of a hyperelliptic curve.svg.* The [hyperelliptic curve](wikipedia://en/499361) defined by $y^{2} = x(x + 1)(x - 3)(x + 2)(x - 2)$ has only finitely many [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) (such as the points $(-2, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$) by [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic geometry"}

In mathematics, **arithmetic geometry** is roughly the application of techniques from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) to problems in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o2] Arithmetic geometry is centered around [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), the study of [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) of [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808).[^o3][^o4]

:::

In more abstract terms, arithmetic geometry can be defined as the study of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) of [finite type](wikipedia://en/2139357#Morphisms_of_finite_type) over the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613).

## Overview {#Overview}

The classical objects of interest in arithmetic geometry are rational points: [sets of solutions](wikipedia://en/567840) of a [system of polynomial equations](wikipedia://en/27420015) over [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615), [$p$-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field), or [function fields](wikipedia://en/447645), i.e. [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) that are not [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed) excluding the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). Rational points can be directly characterized by [height functions](wikipedia://en/3141855) which measure their arithmetic complexity.[^o7]

The structure of algebraic varieties defined over non-algebraically closed fields has become a central area of interest that arose with the modern abstract development of algebraic geometry. Over finite fields, [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) provides [topological invariants](wikipedia://en/1337282) associated to algebraic varieties.[^o20] [$p$-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853) gives tools to examine when cohomological properties of varieties over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) extend to those over [$p$-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field).[^o8]

## History {#History}

### 19th century: early arithmetic geometry {#19th_century:_early_arithmetic_geometry}

In the early 19th century, [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) observed that non-zero [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) solutions to [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) equations with [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) coefficients exist if non-zero rational solutions exist.[^o11]

In the 1850s, [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) formulated the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](wikipedia://en/1082550), introduced the theory of [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055), and made numerous other connections between number theory and [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). He then conjectured his "[liebster Jugendtraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker's_Jugendtraum)" ("dearest dream of youth"), a generalization that was later put forward by Hilbert in a modified form as his [twelfth problem](wikipedia://en/154584), which outlines a goal to have number theory operate only with rings that are quotients of [polynomial rings](wikipedia://en/373065) over the integers.[^o12]

### Early-to-mid 20th century: algebraic developments and the Weil conjectures {#Early-to-mid_20th_century:_algebraic_developments_and_the_Weil_conjectures}

In the late 1920s, [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) demonstrated profound connections between algebraic geometry and number theory with his doctoral work leading to the [Mordell–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/23087727) which demonstrates that the set of rational points of an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) is a [finitely generated abelian group](wikipedia://en/58527).[^o15]

Modern foundations of algebraic geometry were developed based on contemporary [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), including [valuation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_theory) and the theory of [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) by [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) and others in the 1930s and 1940s.[^o16]

In 1949, [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) posed the landmark [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705) about the [local zeta-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_zeta-function) of algebraic varieties over finite fields.[^o17] These conjectures offered a framework between algebraic geometry and number theory that propelled [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) to recast the foundations making use of [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory) (together with [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483)), and later scheme theory, in the 1950s and 1960s.[^o18] [Bernard Dwork](wikipedia://en/1678313) proved one of the four Weil conjectures (rationality of the local zeta function) in 1960.[^o19] Grothendieck developed étale cohomology theory to prove two of the Weil conjectures (together with [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821) and [Jean-Louis Verdier](wikipedia://en/9168893)) by 1965.[^o20][^o21] The last of the Weil conjectures (an analogue of the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125)) would be finally proven in 1974 by [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544).[^o22]

:::

### Mid-to-late 20th century: developments in modularity, *$p$*-adic methods, and beyond {#Mid-to-late_20th_century:_developments_in_modularity,_-adic_methods,_and_beyond}

Between 1956 and 1957, [Yutaka Taniyama](wikipedia://en/1077843) and [Goro Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724) posed the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](wikipedia://en/174475) (now known as the modularity theorem) relating [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) to [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms).[^o24][^o25] This connection would ultimately lead to [the first proof](wikipedia://en/21950759) of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) in number theory through algebraic geometry techniques of [modularity lifting](wikipedia://en/10504570) developed by [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) in 1995.[^o26]

In the 1960s, Goro Shimura introduced [Shimura varieties](wikipedia://en/10389861) as generalizations of [modular curves](wikipedia://en/669552).[^o27] Since the 1979, Shimura varieties have played a crucial role in the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) as a natural realm of examples for testing conjectures.[^o28]

In papers in 1977 and 1978, [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) proved the [torsion conjecture](wikipedia://en/35173610) giving a complete list of the possible torsion subgroups of elliptic curves over the rational numbers. Mazur's first proof of this theorem depended upon a complete analysis of the rational points on certain [modular curves](wikipedia://en/669552).[^o29][^o30] In 1996, the proof of the torsion conjecture was extended to all number fields by [Loïc Merel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loïc_Merel).[^o31]

In 1983, [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) proved the [Mordell conjecture](wikipedia://en/172199), demonstrating that a curve of genus greater than 1 has only finitely many rational points (where the Mordell–Weil theorem only demonstrates [finite generation](wikipedia://en/58527) of the set of rational points as opposed to finiteness).[^o32][^o33]

### Early 21st century {#Early_21st_century}

In 2001, the proof of the [local Langlands conjectures for $\operatorname{GL}_{n}$](wikipedia://en/7676971#Local_Langlands_conjectures_for_GLn) was based on the geometry of certain Shimura varieties.[^o35]

In the 2010s, [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306) developed [perfectoid spaces](wikipedia://en/39197973) and new cohomology theories in arithmetic geometry over $p$-adic fields with application to [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_representations) and certain cases of the [weight-monodromy conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight-monodromy_conjecture).[^o36][^o37]
