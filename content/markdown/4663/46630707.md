# Perfect complex

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perfect complex"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **perfect complex** of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *A* is an object in the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of *A*-modules that is [quasi-isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-isomorphic) to a [bounded complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_complex) of finite [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) *A*-modules. A **perfect module** is a module that is perfect when it is viewed as a complex concentrated at degree zero. For example, if *A* is [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129), a module over *A* is perfect if and only if it is finitely generated and of finite [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension).

:::

## Other characterizations {#Other_characterizations}

Perfect complexes are precisely the [compact objects](wikipedia://en/57633087) in the unbounded derived category $D(A)$ of *A*-modules.[^o3] They are also precisely the [dualizable objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualizable_object) in this category.[^o4]

A compact object in the [∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category) of (say right) [module spectra](wikipedia://en/41276205) over a [ring spectrum](wikipedia://en/21118494) is often called perfect;[^o5] see also [module spectrum](wikipedia://en/41276205).

## Pseudo-coherent sheaf {#Pseudo-coherent_sheaf}

When the structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X$ is not [coherent](wikipedia://en/603780), working with coherent sheaves has awkwardness (namely the kernel of a finite presentation can fail to be coherent). Because of this, [SGA 6 Expo I](wikipedia://en/394508) introduces the notion of a **pseudo-coherent sheaf**.

By definition, given a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014) $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$, an [$\mathcal{O}_X$-module](wikipedia://en/44887605) is called pseudo-coherent if for every integer $n \ge 0$, locally, there is a [free presentation](wikipedia://en/46394651) of finite type of length *n*; i.e.,

$$L_n \to L_{n-1} \to \cdots \to L_0 \to F \to 0.$$

A complex *F* of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules is called pseudo-coherent if, for every integer *n*, there is locally a quasi-isomorphism $L \to F$ where *L* has degree bounded above and consists of finite free modules in degree $\ge n$. If the complex consists only of the zero-th degree term, then it is pseudo-coherent if and only if it is so as a module.

Roughly speaking, a pseudo-coherent complex may be thought of as a limit of perfect complexes.
