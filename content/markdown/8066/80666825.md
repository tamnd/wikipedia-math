# Sergei Mikhailovitch Voronin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sergei Mikhailovitch Voronin"}

**Sergei Mikhailovitch Voronin** (Сергей Михайлович Воронин; March 11, 1946 – October 18, 1997)[^o13] was a Russian mathematician who specialized in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is known for a theorem showing that the critical strip of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) has the property of [universality](wikipedia://en/25809#Universality).[^o10]

:::

He also did work in [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333) and applications of number theory to [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o12] He was also interested in the history of mathematics.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Voronin's father was a petroleum engineer and his mother a teacher. He grew up in [Buguruslan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buguruslan) in the [Orenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orenburg) region. He studied piano at a music school, successfully participated in mathematics competitions as a student and attended mathematics summer schools in Moscow. In 1963 he transferred to a special boarding school for mathematics in Moscow. In 1964 he entered [Lomonosov University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_University), where he studied [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) under [Anatoly Karatsuba](wikipedia://en/20250709). In 1972, he received his PhD with the dissertation *The Study of the Behavior of the Riemann Zeta Function* under advisor Advisor [Yulij Ilyashenko](wikipedia://en/56631455).[^o4]

During the 1990s Voronin was a professor of number theory at the [Moscow State Pedagogical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Pedagogical_University).

## Career {#Career}

In his dissertation, Voronin proved that the Riemann zeta function does not obey a continuous differential equation.[^o14] In 1975, as part of his habilitation thesis, he proved his universality theorem that any continuous non-vanishing analytic function in a circular disc can be approximated by the Riemann zeta function within the critical strip $\tfrac {1}{2}< \mathrm{Re} \, s < 1$.[^o18]

The theorem shows the chaotic behavior of the Riemann zeta function in the critical strip. He also dealt with the distribution of zeros of other zeta functions (Dirichlet, Epstein).[^o11] For example, in 1980 he showed that certain functions (in this case the Davenport-Heilbronn function, soon followed by some [Epstein zeta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_zeta_function))[^o17] that are defined in the right half-plane by a [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) and satisfy a functional equation like the Riemann zeta function, but for which the Riemann hypothesis does not hold, nevertheless have an abnormal accumulation of zeros on the critical line.

## Voronin's universality theorem {#Voronin's_universality_theorem}

Let $f$ be a continuous function that has no zeros and is analytic inside the circular disc $D(r)$ with $0 <r < \tfrac {1}{4}$. Then, for every real number $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a real number $\tau > 0$ such that, for $| s | \leq r$

$$| \zeta (s + \tfrac {3}{4} + i \tau) - f (s) | < \epsilon.$$

The theorem also holds for general [Dirichlet L-functions](wikipedia://en/400092). The theorem can also be formulated such that continuous, non-vanishing and analytic functions $f$ in circular disks $D$, which lie in the strip $\tfrac{1}{2} < \mathrm{Re} \, s < 1$, can be approximated uniformly with arbitrary precision in $D$ by translations of the Riemann zeta function along the imaginary axis $\zeta (s + i \tau)$. For example, Bhaskar Bagchi generalized it from circular disks to domains $D$ that are simply connected and compact and lie in the strip $\tfrac{1}{2} < \mathrm{Re} \, s < 1$.[^o16]

The [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) is equivalent to the theorem that the Riemann zeta function itself can also be approximated uniformly in the sense of Voronin's universality theorem.[^o15]
