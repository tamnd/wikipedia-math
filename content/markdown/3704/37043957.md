# Neovius surface

*Image: Neovius' minimal surface.png.* Neovius' minimal surface in a unit cell.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neovius surface"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Neovius surface** is a [triply periodic](wikipedia://en/31526142) [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) originally discovered by Finnish mathematician Edvard Rudolf Neovius, an uncle of [Rolf Nevanlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Nevanlinna).[^o2][^o3]

:::

The surface has [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 9, dividing space into two infinite non-equivalent labyrinths. Like many other triply periodic minimal surfaces it has been studied in relation to the microstructure of [block copolymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_copolymers), [surfactant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfactant)-water mixtures,[^o4] and crystallography of soft materials.[^o5]

It can be approximated with the [level set](wikipedia://en/559622) surface[^o6]

$$3(\cos x + \cos y + \cos z) + 4 \cos x \cos y \cos z = 0$$

In [Schoen's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Schoen) categorisation it is called the C(P) surface, since it is the "complement" of the [Schwarz P surface](wikipedia://en/36763333#Schwarz_P_.28.22Primitive.22.29). It can be extended with further handles, converging towards the expanded regular octahedron (in Schoen's categorisation)[^o7][^o8]
