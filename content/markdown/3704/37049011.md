# Richmond surface

*Image: Richmond Surface.png.* Richmond surface for m=2.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richmond surface"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Richmond surface** is a [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) first described by [Herbert William Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_William_Richmond) in 1904.[^o2] It is a family of surfaces with one planar [end](wikipedia://en/6862105) and one [Enneper surface](wikipedia://en/4688830)-like self-intersecting end.

:::

It has [Weierstrass–Enneper parameterization](wikipedia://en/2996193) $f(z)=1/z^2, g(z)=z^m$. This allows a parametrization based on a complex parameter as

$$\begin{aligned} X(z) &= \Re[(-1/2z) - z^{2m+1}/(4m+2)]\\ Y(z) &= \Re[(-i/2z) + i z^{2m+1}/(4m+2)]\\ Z(z) &= \Re[z^m / m] \end{aligned}$$

The [associate family](wikipedia://en/37016612) of the surface is just the surface rotated around the z-axis.

Taking *m* = 2 a real parametric expression becomes:[^o3]

$$\begin{aligned} X(u,v) &= (1/3)u^3 - uv^2 + \frac{u}{u^2+v^2}\\ Y(u,v) &= -u^2v + (1/3)v^3 - \frac{v}{u^2+v^2}\\ Z(u,v) &= 2u \end{aligned}$$
