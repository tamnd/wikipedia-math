# Bour's minimal surface

*Image: Bour's Surface 01.jpg.* Bour's surface.

*Image: Bour's Surface annulus.jpg.* Bour's surface, leaving out the points with *r* \< 0.5 to show the self-crossings more clearly.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bour's minimal surface"}

In mathematics, **Bour's minimal surface** is a two-dimensional [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734), embedded with self-crossings into three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). It is named after [Edmond Bour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond_Bour), whose work on minimal surfaces won him the 1861 mathematics prize of the French Academy of Sciences.[^o2]

:::

## Description {#Description}

Bour's surface crosses itself on three coplanar rays, meeting at equal angles at the origin of the space. The rays partition the surface into six sheets, topologically equivalent to half-planes; three sheets lie in the halfspace above the plane of the rays, and three below. Four of the sheets are mutually tangent along each ray.

## Equation {#Equation}

The points on the surface may be parameterized in [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates) by a pair of numbers $(r, \theta)$. Each such pair corresponds to a point in three dimensions according to the [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108)[^o5]

$$\begin{aligned} x(r,\theta) &= r\cos(\theta) - \tfrac{1}{2}r^2 \cos(2\theta) \\ y(r,\theta) &= -r\sin(\theta)(r \cos(\theta) + 1) \\ z(r,\theta) &= \tfrac{4}{3}r^{3/2} \cos\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\theta\right). \end{aligned}$$

The surface can also be expressed as the solution to a polynomial equation of order 16 in the [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) of the three-dimensional space.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The [Weierstrass–Enneper parameterization](wikipedia://en/2996193), a method for turning certain pairs of functions over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) into minimal surfaces, produces this surface for the two functions $f(z)=1, g(z)=\sqrt{z}$. It was proved by Bour that surfaces in this family are [developable](wikipedia://en/2878111) onto a [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549).[^o8]
