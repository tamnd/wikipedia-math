# Elementary theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elementary theory"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), an **elementary theory** is a [theory](wikipedia://en/3512103) that involves axioms using only [finitary](wikipedia://en/404456) [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983), without reference to [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) or using any axioms that have [consistency strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_strength) equal to set theory.

:::

Saying that a theory is elementary is a weaker condition than saying it is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/24497685).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of elementary theories include:

- The theory of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447)
  - The theory of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707)
  - The theory of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974)
- The theory of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603)
  - The theory of [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_fields)
  - The theory of [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093)
- [Axiomization of Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/2181360)

:::
