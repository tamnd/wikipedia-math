# Roxana Vivian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roxana Vivian"}

**Roxana Hayward Vivian** (December 9, 1871 – May 31, 1961) was an American [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) professor.[^o2] She was the first female recipient of a doctorate in mathematics from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Roxana Hayward Vivian was born to Roxana Nott and Robert Hayward Vivian on December 9, 1871, in [Hyde Park, Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Boston), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts). She went to [Hyde Park High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park_High_School_%28Massachusetts%29) and then, from 1890 to 1894, to [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) where she [graduated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation) in Greek and mathematics.[^o4][^o2]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After four years as a high school teacher in suburban [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston),[^o2] Vivian started post-graduate study at the University of Pennsylvania, where she took a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics in 1901 with a thesis on "Poles of a Right Line with Respect to a Curve of the Order *n*". She returned to Wellesley College as a mathematics teacher, the first in her department to hold a doctorate. In 1906 she went to teach at the [American College for Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_College) in [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul), Turkey, where for two years she was the acting president. In 1908 she became an associate professor at Wellesley, and was made a full professor in 1918; from 1918 to 1921 she was also director of the Graduate Department of Hygiene and Physical Education. In 1925–26 Vivian took a one-year professorship at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). She again returned to Wellesley, but soon resigned. In 1929, after a year in [Vassalboro, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassalboro,_Maine), she became a mathematics professor and dean of women at [Hartwick College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartwick_College) of [Oneonta, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneonta,_New_York). From 1931 to 1935 she was a mathematics teacher and dean of girls at [Rye Public High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye_High_School_%28Rye,_New_York%29) in New York.[^o4][^o2] She died in 1961 in Boston.[^o2]
