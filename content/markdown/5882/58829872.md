# Samuel Lattès

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Lattès"}

**Samuel Lattès** (21 February 1873 ([Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice)) – 5 July 1918) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o2]

:::

From 1892 to 1895 he studied at the [École Normale Superieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29). After this he was a teacher in [Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers), [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon) and Nice. After a promotion to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) in 1906 he moved first to [Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier) in 1908 and then to [Besançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Besançon), before he took up a professorship at the [University of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toulouse) in 1911.

In 1910, he married physicist [Jeanne Ferrier](wikipedia://en/75198597), a former student of his, and they had a daughter together.[^o3]

He died of [typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus) in 1918.

Today Lattès is best known for his work on complex sets, particularly for examples of rational functions including the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) in its [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434). Today these examples are described as [Lattès maps](wikipedia://en/28886032) or Lattès examples.[^o4]
