# Fourier–Mukai transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fourier–Mukai transform"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **Fourier–Mukai transform** *Φ*<sub>*K*</sub> is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) between [derived categories](wikipedia://en/985897) of [coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/603780) D(*X*) → D(*Y*) for [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* and *Y*, which is, in a sense, an [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912) along a kernel object *K* ∈ D(*X*×*Y*). Most natural functors, including basic ones like [pushforwards](wikipedia://en/3022744) and [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/7086661), are of this type.

:::

These kinds of functors were introduced by (txt) in order to prove an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) between the derived categories of coherent sheaves on an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) and its [dual](wikipedia://en/7045490). That equivalence is analogous to the classical [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) that gives an isomorphism between [tempered distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) on a finite-dimensional real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) and its [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *X* and *Y* be [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) [projective varieties](wikipedia://en/320469), *K* ∈ D<sup>b</sup>(*X*×*Y*) an object in the derived category of coherent sheaves on their product. Denote by *q* the projection *X*×*Y*→*X*, by *p* the projection *X*×*Y*→*Y*. Then the Fourier-Mukai transform *Φ*<sub>*K*</sub> is a functor D<sup>b</sup>(*X*)→D<sup>b</sup>(*Y*) given by

$$\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathrm{R}p_*\left(q^*\mathcal{F} \otimes^{L} K\right)$$

where R*p*<sub>\*</sub> is the [derived](wikipedia://en/433875) [direct image functor](wikipedia://en/3022744) and $\otimes^L$ is the derived [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836).

Fourier-Mukai transforms always have left and right [adjoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor), both of which are also kernel transformations. Given two kernels *K*<sub>1</sub> ∈ D<sup>b</sup>(*X*×*Y*) and *K*<sub>2</sub> ∈ D<sup>b</sup>(*Y*×*Z*), the composed functor *Φ*<sub>*K*<sub>2</sub></sub> $\circ$ *Φ*<sub>*K*<sub>1</sub></sub> is also a Fourier-Mukai transform.

The structure sheaf of the diagonal $\mathcal{O}_{\Delta} \in \mathrm{D}^b(X \times X)$, taken as a kernel, produces the identity functor on D<sup>b</sup>(*X*). For a morphism *f*:*X*→*Y*, the structure sheaf of the graph Γ<sub>*f*</sub> produces a [pushforward](wikipedia://en/3022744) when viewed as an object in D<sup>b</sup>(*X*×*Y*), or a [pullback](wikipedia://en/7086661) when viewed as an object in D<sup>b</sup>(*Y*×*X*).

:::

## On abelian varieties {#On_abelian_varieties}

Let $X$ be an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) and $\hat X$ be its [dual variety](wikipedia://en/7045490). The [Poincaré bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_bundle) $\mathcal P$ on $X \times \hat X$, normalized to be trivial on the fiber at zero, can be used as a Fourier-Mukai kernel. Let $p$ and $\hat p$ be the canonical projections. The corresponding Fourier–Mukai functor with kernel $\mathcal P$ is then

$$R\mathcal S: \mathcal F \in D(X) \mapsto R\hat p_\ast (p^\ast \mathcal F \otimes \mathcal P) \in D(\hat X)$$

There is a similar functor

$$R\widehat{\mathcal S} : D(\hat X) \to D(X). \,$$

If the [canonical class](wikipedia://en/734893) of a variety is [ample](wikipedia://en/1186896) or anti-ample, then the derived category of coherent sheaves determines the variety.[^o6] In general, an abelian variety is not isomorphic to its dual, so this Fourier–Mukai transform gives examples of different varieties (with trivial canonical bundles) that have equivalent derived categories.

Let *g* denote the dimension of *X*. The Fourier–Mukai transformation is nearly involutive :

$$R\mathcal S \circ R\widehat{\mathcal S} = (-1)^\ast [-g]$$

It interchanges [Pontrjagin product](wikipedia://en/34640104) and [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844).

$$R\mathcal S(\mathcal F \ast \mathcal G) = R\mathcal S(\mathcal F) \otimes R\mathcal S(\mathcal G)$$

$$R\mathcal S(\mathcal F \otimes \mathcal G) = R\mathcal S(\mathcal F) \ast R\mathcal S(\mathcal G)[g]$$

(Deninger Murre 1991) have used the Fourier-Mukai transform to prove the [Künneth decomposition](wikipedia://en/10470079) for the [Chow motives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_motive) of abelian varieties.

## Applications in string theory {#Applications_in_string_theory}

In [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), [T-duality](wikipedia://en/432615) (short for *target space duality*), which relates two [quantum field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theories) or string theories with different spacetime geometries, is closely related with the Fourier-Mukai transformation.[^o9][^o10]
