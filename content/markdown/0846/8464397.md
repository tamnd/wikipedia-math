# Uniform star polyhedron

*Image: Uniform-Polyhedra-at-the-Science-Museum.jpg.* A display of uniform polyhedra at the [Science Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Museum_%28London%29) in London

*Image: Small snub icosicosidodecahedron.png.* The [small snub icosicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157567) is a *uniform star polyhedron*, with [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) $3_{5}.\tfrac{5}{2}$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform star polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **uniform star polyhedron** is a self-intersecting [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223). They are also sometimes called **nonconvex polyhedra** to imply self-intersecting. Each polyhedron can contain either [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303) faces, star polygon [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719), or both.

:::

The complete set of 57 nonprismatic uniform star polyhedra includes the 4 regular ones, called the [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](wikipedia://en/16814), 14 [quasiregular](wikipedia://en/8810651#Nonconvex_examples) ones, and 39 semiregular ones.

There are also two infinite sets of [*uniform star prisms* and *uniform star antiprisms*](wikipedia://en/2537223#.28p_2_2.29_Prismatic_.5Bp.2C2.5D.2C_I2.28p.29_family_.28Dph_dihedral_symmetry.29).

Just as (nondegenerate) star polygons (which have [polygon density](wikipedia://en/34257549) greater than 1) correspond to circular polygons with overlapping [tiles](wikipedia://en/321671), star polyhedra that do not pass through the center have polytope density greater than 1, and correspond to [spherical polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_polyhedra) with overlapping tiles; there are 47 nonprismatic such uniform star polyhedra. The remaining 10 nonprismatic uniform star polyhedra, those that pass through the center, are the [hemipolyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemipolyhedra) as well as [Miller's monster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller's_monster), and do not have well-defined densities.

The nonconvex forms are constructed from [Schwarz triangles](wikipedia://en/2069325).

All the uniform polyhedra are listed below by their [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186) and subgrouped by their vertex arrangements.

Regular polyhedra are labeled by their [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394). Other nonregular uniform polyhedra are listed with their [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745).

An additional figure, the [pseudo great rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/35694594), is usually not included as a truly uniform star polytope, despite consisting of regular faces and having the same vertices.

Note: For nonconvex forms below an additional descriptor **nonuniform** is used when the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) has same topology as one of these, but has nonregular faces. For example an *nonuniform cantellated* form may have [rectangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangles) created in place of the edges rather than [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29).

## Tetrahedral symmetry {#Tetrahedral_symmetry}

*Image: Disdyakis 6 spherical.png.* (3 3 2) triangles on sphere

There is one nonconvex form, the [tetrahemihexahedron](wikipedia://en/2851834) which has *[tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472)* (with fundamental domain [Möbius triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_triangle) (3 3 2)).

There are two [Schwarz triangles](wikipedia://en/2069325#A_complete_list_of_Schwarz_triangles_grouped_by_symmetry) that generate unique nonconvex uniform polyhedra: one right triangle ($\tfrac{3}{2}$ 3 2), and one general triangle ($\tfrac{3}{2}$ 3 3). The general triangle ($\tfrac{3}{2}$ 3 3) generates the [octahemioctahedron](wikipedia://en/4157168) which is given further on with its full [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805).

## Degenerate cases {#Degenerate_cases}

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) identified a number of degenerate star polyhedra by the Wythoff construction method, which contain overlapping edges or vertices. These degenerate forms include:

- [Small complex icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/25923673) (looks like an icosahedron and a [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441) sharing edges and vertices)
- [Great complex icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/25923721) (looks like a [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898) and a [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843) sharing edges and vertices)
- [Small complex rhombicosidodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_complex_rhombicosidodecahedron) (looks like a [small ditrigonal icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157350) and a [compound of five cubes](wikipedia://en/8010502) sharing edges and vertices)
- [Great complex rhombicosidodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_complex_rhombicosidodecahedron) (looks like a [great ditrigonal icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4156959) and a compound of five cubes sharing edges and vertices)
- [Complex rhombidodecadodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_rhombidodecadodecahedron) (looks like a [ditrigonal dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157586) and a compound of five cubes sharing edges and vertices)

### Skilling's figure {#Skilling's_figure}

One further nonconvex degenerate polyhedron is the [great disnub dirhombidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4145225), also known as *Skilling's figure*, which is vertex-uniform, but has pairs of edges which coincide in space such that four faces meet at some edges. It is counted as a degenerate uniform polyhedron rather than a uniform polyhedron because of its double edges. It has I<sub>h</sub> symmetry.
