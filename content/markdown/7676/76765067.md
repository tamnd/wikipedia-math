# Glossary of logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glossary of logic"}

This is a **glossary of logic**. [Logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) is the study of the principles of valid reasoning and argumentation.

:::

## A {#A}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=term name="A-proposition"}

**A-proposition**

A type of standard-form categorical proposition, asserting that all members of the subject category are included in the predicate category; symbolized as "All S are P".[^o4][^o5]

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=term name="abduction"}

**[abduction](wikipedia://en/60459)**

A form of reasoning characterized by drawing a conclusion based on the best available explanation for a set of premises. Often used in hypothesis formation.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=term name="Abelian logic"}

**Abelian logic**

A type of [relevance logic](wikipedia://en/185076) that rejects contraction and accepts that ((A → B) → B) → A.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=term name="absorption"}

**[absorption](wikipedia://en/34620850)**

A logical rule stating that if a proposition implies another, then adding any additional conjunction to the first proposition does not change the implication. Symbolized as $P \rightarrow Q \equiv (P \land R) \rightarrow Q$.

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=term name="abstract object"}

**[abstract object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_object)**

An object that does not exist at any particular time or place but rather exists as a type of thing—i.e., an idea, or abstraction.

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=term name="abstraction"}

**[abstraction](wikipedia://en/2972)**

The process or result of generalization by reducing the information content of a concept or an observable phenomenon, typically to retain only information which is relevant for a particular purpose.

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=term name="abstraction operator"}

**abstraction operator**

The function implicitly defined by an abstraction principle.[^o15]

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=term name="abstraction principle"}

**abstraction principle**

A formula of the form (∀α)(∀β)(Abst(α) = Abst(β) ↔ Equ(α, β)), where Abst is an abstraction operator mapping the type of entities ranged over by α and β to objects, and “Equ” is an equivalence relation on the type of entities ranged over by α and β.[^o15] For instance, Hume's principle, and Basic Law V.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=term name="accessibility relation"}

**[accessibility relation](wikipedia://en/3708585)**

In modal logic, a relation that describes which worlds are accessible from other worlds in the semantics of possible world models.

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=term name="actual world"}

**[actual world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actual_world)**

In modal logic, the world that is considered to be the real world, as opposed to possible worlds which are considered in modal reasoning.

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=term name="addition"}

**[addition](wikipedia://en/8528)**

A rule of inference in formal logic where from any proposition, a disjunction can be formed by disjoining it with any other proposition. Symbolized as $P \vdash P \lor Q$.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=term name="ad hominem"}

**[ad hominem](wikipedia://en/2226)**

A fallacy in argumentation that targets the person making an argument rather than the argument itself.

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=term name="ad ignorantium"}

**[ad ignorantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_ignorantium)**

A logical fallacy where a proposition is considered true because it has not been proven false or vice versa.

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=term name="ad infinitum"}

**[ad infinitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_infinitum)**

An argument or process that is supposed to continue indefinitely, without ever reaching an end or conclusion.

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=term name="adjunction"}

**adjunction**

See conjunction introduction.

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=term name="affine logics"}

**[affine logics](wikipedia://en/693233)**

A subfield of linear logic focusing on the study of affine transformations and their implications in logical reasoning.

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=term name="affirmative proposition"}

**affirmative proposition**

A proposition that asserts the truth of a statement, as opposed to negating it.[^o26][^o27][^o28]

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=term name="affirming the consequent"}

**[affirming the consequent](wikipedia://en/675)**

A logical fallacy in which a conditional statement is incorrectly used to infer its converse. For example, from "If P then Q" and "Q", concluding "P".

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=term name="alethic modal logic"}

**[alethic modal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alethic_modal_logic)**

A type of modal logic that deals with modalities of truth, such as necessity and possibility.

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=term name="ambiguity"}

**[ambiguity](wikipedia://en/677)**

The property of a statement or phrase having more than one meaning or interpretation.

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=term name="amphiboly"}

**[amphiboly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphiboly)**

A type of ambiguity resulting from ambiguous grammatical structure, rather than the ambiguity of individual words.

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=term name="analethic logic"}

**analethic logic**

A three-valued logic where the third truth value is the truth-value gap "neither true nor false" ("N"), and the designated value are "true" and "neither true nor false."[^o34]

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=term name="analysis"}

**analysis**

[Analysis](wikipedia://en/1134), the process of breaking a concept down into more simple parts, so that its logical structure is displayed.

[Mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396)

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=term name="analytic"}

**[analytic](wikipedia://en/6395956)**

A statement that is true by virtue of its meaning, such as definitions or statements grounded in logical laws.

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=term name="anaphora"}

**[anaphora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaphora_%28linguistics%29)**

A rhetorical device that consists of repeating a sequence of words at the beginnings of neighboring clauses, thereby lending them emphasis.

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=term name="ancestral"}

**[ancestral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_relation)**

In logic and mathematics, the [transitive closure](wikipedia://en/292239) of a relation, capturing the idea of indirect relationships across generations or iterations.

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=term name="antecedent"}

**[antecedent](wikipedia://en/804755)**

The first part of a conditional statement, the "if" clause, which specifies a condition for the consequent.

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=term name="anti-extension"}

**anti-extension**

In set theory and logic, the complement of the extension of a concept or predicate, consisting of all objects that do not fall under the concept.[^o41][^o42][^o43]

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=term name="antilogism"}

**antilogism**

A syllogism with three premises leading to a contradiction, showing the inconsistency of the premises.[^o45][^o46][^o47]

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=term name="antinomy"}

**[antinomy](wikipedia://en/3175)**

A contradiction between two beliefs or conclusions that are in themselves reasonable; a paradox.

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=term name="antisymmetry"}

**[antisymmetry](wikipedia://en/1176)**

A property of a relation $R$, where for any two elements $a$ and $b$, if $aRb$ and $bRa$, then $a = b$.

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=term name="a posteriori"}

**[a posteriori](wikipedia://en/6679056)**

Knowledge or justification is said to be a posteriori if it depends on empirical evidence or experience.

:::

::: {#o51 .entry cue=term name="a priori"}

**[a priori](wikipedia://en/6679056)**

Knowledge or justification is said to be a priori if it is independent of empirical evidence or experience, such as logic or mathematics.

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=term name="argument"}

**[argument](wikipedia://en/13018310)**

A series of statements typically used to persuade someone of something or to present reasons for accepting a conclusion.

:::

::: {#o53 .entry cue=term name="Aristotelian comprehension schema"}

**Aristotelian comprehension schema**

This formula in [second-order logic](wikipedia://en/341428): (∃x)Φ → (∃Y)(∀x)(Yx ↔ Φ).[^o15]

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=term name="Aristotelian logic"}

**[Aristotelian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_logic)**

The traditional logic developed by Aristotle, based on the study of syllogism and the principle of non-contradiction.

:::

::: {#o55 .entry cue=term name="Aristotle's sea battle"}

**[Aristotle's sea battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle's_sea_battle_argument)**

A thought experiment by Aristotle to explore the concept of future contingents and the problem of determinism and free will.

:::

::: {#o56 .entry cue=term name="Aristotle's theses"}

**Aristotle's theses**

The formulas ¬ (¬ A → A) and ¬ (A → ¬A) in [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154); they are theorems in [connexive logic](wikipedia://en/12859904) but not in [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868).[^o57][^o58][^o59] See also Boethius' theses.

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=term name="arity"}

**[arity](wikipedia://en/42301)**

The number of arguments or operands that a function, operation, or relation takes. In logic, it refers to the number of terms that a predicate has.

:::

::: {#o61 .entry cue=term name="assertion"}

**assertion**

The principle, or axiom, that (A ∧ (A → B)) → B.[^o62][^o63] Also called pseudo modus ponens.

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=term name="associativity"}

**[associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity)**

A property of some binary operations in which the grouping of operations does not affect the result. For example, in arithmetic, addition and multiplication are associative.

:::

::: {#o65 .entry cue=term name="asymmetry"}

**[asymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetry)**

A relation between two entities where if the first is related to the second, the second is not related to the first.

:::

::: {#o66 .entry cue=term name="atomic formula"}

**[atomic formula](wikipedia://en/4472066)**

The simplest type of formula in logic, consisting of a single predicate applied to a sequence of terms without any logical connectives.

:::

::: {#o67 .entry cue=term name="atomic sentence"}

**[atomic sentence](wikipedia://en/943457)**

A sentence that contains no logical connectives or quantifiers, expressing a basic statement about objects.

:::

::: {#o68 .entry cue=term name="autological"}

**[autological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autological_word)**

A term that describes itself. For example, the word "short" is autological because it is a short word.

:::

::: {#o69 .entry cue=term name="automaton"}

**[automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automaton)**

A self-operating machine or, in computer science, a theoretical model of computation that performs tasks according to a set of rules or a program.

:::

::: {#o70 .entry cue=term name="automorphism"}

**[automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160)**

An isomorphism from a mathematical object to itself, preserving all the structure of the object. In logic, it often refers to symmetries within logical structures.

:::

::: {#o71 .entry cue=term name="axiological logic"}

**axiological logic**

A branch of logic that deals with the study of value, including ethical and aesthetic values, often in the context of modal logic.[^o72][^o73][^o74]

:::

::: {#o75 .entry cue=term name="axiom"}

**[axiom](wikipedia://en/928)**

A statement or proposition that is accepted as true without proof, serving as a starting point for further reasoning and arguments.

:::

## B {#B}

::: {#o77 .entry cue=term name="bad company objection"}

**bad company objection**

An objection to mathematical abstractionism that points out the difficulty in distinguishing between legitimate and illegitimate forms of abstraction, particularly in the context of Frege's Basic Law V and the paradoxes it generates.[^o78][^o79][^o80]

:::

::: {#o81 .entry cue=term name="barbara"}

**barbara**

A standard form of categorical syllogism in Aristotelian logic, where all three propositions (major premise, minor premise, and conclusion) are universal affirmatives, symbolized as AAA. The form is: All M are P, All S are M, therefore All S are P.[^o82][^o83][^o84]

:::

::: {#o85 .entry cue=term name="Barcan formula"}

**[Barcan formula](wikipedia://en/245298)**

A principle in modal logic that asserts the interchangeability of quantification and possibility: necessarily, if there exists something, then there necessarily exists something.

:::

::: {#o86 .entry cue=term name="Basic Law V"}

**[Basic Law V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_V)**

A principle proposed by Gottlob Frege in his attempt to reduce arithmetic to logic, stating that the extension of a concept is determined by the objects falling under the concept. It leads to Russell's paradox.

:::

::: {#o87 .entry cue=term name="basis"}

**[basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_step)**

The initial case in a proof by mathematical induction.

:::

::: {#o88 .entry cue=term name="Bayes' theorem"}

**[Bayes' theorem](wikipedia://en/49569)**

A theorem in probability theory used to update the probability for a hypothesis as more evidence or information becomes available.

:::

::: {#o89 .entry cue=term name="begging the question"}

**[begging the question](wikipedia://en/43582)**

A logical fallacy where the conclusion of an argument is assumed in the premise, making the argument circular.

:::

::: {#o90 .entry cue=term name="Bew"}

**[Bew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bew_%28mathematical_logic%29)**

See provability predicate.

:::

::: {#o91 .entry cue=term name="BHK-interpretation"}

**[BHK-interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BHK_interpretation)**

The Brouwer-Heyting-Kolmogorov interpretation, a constructivist interpretation of intuitionistic logic, where the truth of a statement is equated with the existence of a proof for it.

:::

::: {#o92 .entry cue=term name="bias"}

**[bias](wikipedia://en/184099)**

A systematic deviation from neutrality, objectivity, or fairness, often resulting from a particular tendency or inclination, especially in statistical or cognitive contexts.

:::

::: {#o93 .entry cue=term name="biconditional"}

**[biconditional](wikipedia://en/228783)**

A logical connective between statements, where both statements imply each other; often denoted as $P \leftrightarrow Q$, meaning "P if and only if Q".

:::

::: {#o94 .entry cue=term name="bijective"}

**[bijective](wikipedia://en/3942)**

A function that is both injective (no two elements of the domain map to the same element of the codomain) and surjective (every element of the codomain is mapped to by some element of the domain), establishing a one-to-one correspondence between the domain and codomain.

:::

::: {#o95 .entry cue=term name="binary function"}

**[binary function](wikipedia://en/3943)**

A function that takes two arguments. In logic and mathematics, this is often a function that combines two values to produce a third value, such as addition or multiplication in arithmetic.

:::

::: {#o96 .entry cue=term name="binary relation"}

**[binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931)**

A relation involving two terms or elements, defining a particular relationship between pairs of objects from two sets (or from one set to itself).

:::

::: {#o97 .entry cue=term name="bivalence"}

**[bivalence](wikipedia://en/24097)**

The principle that any proposition is either true or false, with no middle ground; foundational to classical logic.

:::

::: {#o98 .entry cue=term name="Boethius' theses"}

**Boethius' theses**

The formulas (A → B) → ¬ (A → ¬ B) and (A → ¬ B) → ¬ (A → B) in [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154); they are theorems in [connexive logic](wikipedia://en/12859904) but not in [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868).[^o99][^o100][^o101] See also Aristotle's theses.

:::

::: {#o102 .entry cue=term name="Boolean algebra"}

**[Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/54476844)**

An area of algebra in which the values of the variables are the truth values true and false, typically used in computer science, logic, and mathematical logic.

:::

::: {#o103 .entry cue=term name="Boolean negation"}

**Boolean negation**

A form of [negation](wikipedia://en/161019) where the negation of a non-true proposition is true, and the negation of a non-false proposition is false.[^o104][^o105][^o106]

:::

::: {#o107 .entry cue=term name="Boolean operator"}

**[Boolean operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_operator_%28Boolean_algebra%29)**

An operator used in logic and computer science that performs logical operations on its operands, such as AND, OR, and NOT.

:::

::: {#o108 .entry cue=term name="borderline case"}

**borderline case**

A situation or instance that falls at the boundary between categories or classifications, often challenging strict definitions or distinctions.[^o109][^o110][^o111][^o112]

:::

::: {#o113 .entry cue=term name="bottom"}

**[bottom](wikipedia://en/25016871)**

In logic, the symbol used to represent a contradiction or an absurdity; often denoted by the symbol $\bot$.

:::

::: {#o114 .entry cue=term name="bound variable"}

**[bound variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_variable)**

A variable that is quantified over in a logical expression, as opposed to a free variable, which is not bound by a quantifier.

:::

::: {#o115 .entry cue=term name="bounded quantifier"}

**[bounded quantifier](wikipedia://en/5824808)**

A quantifier that operates within a specific domain or set, as opposed to an unbounded or universal quantifier that applies to all elements of a particular type.

:::

::: {#o116 .entry cue=term name="branching quantifier"}

**[branching quantifier](wikipedia://en/3573834)**

A type of quantifier in formal logic that allows for the expression of dependencies between different quantified variables, representing more complex relationships than can be expressed with standard linear quantification.

:::

::: {#o117 .entry cue=term name="Brouwerian modal logic"}

**Brouwerian modal logic**

A form of modal logic that incorporates principles of intuitionism, as developed by [L.E.J. Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.E.J._Brouwer), focusing on the notion of possibility grounded in constructivist or intuitionist mathematics.[^o118][^o119][^o120]

:::

::: {#o121 .entry cue=term name="Buridan's sophismata"}

**Buridan's sophismata**

A collection of paradoxes and logical exercises attributed to the medieval philosopher Jean Buridan, designed to challenge logical and linguistic intuitions.[^o122][^o123][^o124]

:::

::: {#o125 .entry cue=term name="busy beaver problem"}

**[busy beaver problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busy_beaver_problem)**

A problem in computability theory that seeks the Turing machine with the largest possible behavior (e.g., producing the most output, running the longest) among all Turing machines of a certain size, illustrating limits of computability.

:::

## C {#C}

::: {#o127 .entry cue=term name="Caesar problem"}

**Caesar problem**

A problem in the philosophy of language and logic regarding the applicability of mathematical concepts to non-mathematical objects, famously illustrated by Gottlob Frege's question of whether the concept of being a successor in number applies to Julius Caesar.[^o128][^o129][^o130]

:::

::: {#o131 .entry cue=term name="Carnap-Ramsey sentence"}

**[Carnap-Ramsey sentence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnap-Ramsey_sentence)**

A sentence formulated to express the empirical content of a theory in logical positivism, named after Rudolf Carnap and Frank P. Ramsey, aimed at separating theoretical terms from observational terms.

:::

::: {#o132 .entry cue=term name="categorical"}

**[categorical](wikipedia://en/1096396)**

A theory is categorical if all its models are isomorphic, meaning they describe the same mathematical structures in different ways.

:::

::: {#o133 .entry cue=term name="categorical logic"}

**[categorical logic](wikipedia://en/1063799)**

A branch of logic that studies the categorization of objects and the logical foundations of categories, often using the framework of category theory.

:::

::: {#o134 .entry cue=term name="categorical proposition"}

**[categorical proposition](wikipedia://en/2418429)**

A proposition that asserts or denies that all or some of the members of one category are included in another category, fundamental in syllogistic reasoning.

:::

::: {#o135 .entry cue=term name="categorical syllogism"}

**[categorical syllogism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_syllogism)**

A form of deductive reasoning in Aristotelian logic consisting of three categorical propositions that involve three terms and deduce a conclusion from two premises.

:::

::: {#o136 .entry cue=term name="category"}

**[category](wikipedia://en/53759)**

In mathematics and logic, a collection of objects and morphisms between them that satisfies certain axioms, fundamental to category theory.

:::

::: {#o137 .entry cue=term name="category theory"}

**[category theory](wikipedia://en/5869)**

A branch of mathematics that deals with abstract algebraic structures and relationships between them, providing a unifying framework for various areas of mathematics.

:::

::: {#o138 .entry cue=term name="causal logic"}

**causal logic**

A branch of logic concerned with the study of causal relationships, including the representation and reasoning about causes and effects.[^o139][^o140]

:::

::: {#o141 .entry cue=term name="causal modal logic"}

**causal modal logic**

An extension of modal logic that includes modalities for necessity and possibility along with causal relations, allowing for the formal analysis of causal statements.[^o142][^o143]

:::

::: {#o144 .entry cue=term name="chronological logic"}

**chronological logic**

See temporal modal logic.[^o145][^o146]

:::

::: {#o147 .entry cue=term name="Church's theorem"}

**[Church's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church's_theorem)**

A theorem establishing the undecidability of certain decision problems in logic, such as the Entscheidungsproblem, proving that there is no consistent, complete, and effectively calculable logic.

:::

::: {#o148 .entry cue=term name="Church–Turing thesis"}

**[Church–Turing thesis](wikipedia://en/6854)**

A hypothesis proposing that any function that can be naturally regarded as computable by a human being can be computed by a Turing machine, thereby defining the limits of what can be computed.

:::

::: {#o149 .entry cue=term name="classical dilemma"}

**[classical dilemma](wikipedia://en/359970)**

A form of argument presenting two alternatives, both leading to the same conclusion, often used in classical rhetoric and logic to demonstrate inevitability.

:::

::: {#o150 .entry cue=term name="classical logic"}

**[classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868)**

The traditional framework of logic based on principles of bivalence, non-contradiction, and excluded middle, primarily focusing on propositional and predicate logic.

:::

::: {#o151 .entry cue=term name="classical reductio ad absurdum"}

**classical reductio ad absurdum**

A stronger form of [reductio ad absurdum](wikipedia://en/22456146),[^o152] where instead of only deriving $\neg P$ from showing that $P$ leads to a contradiction, one can also derive $P$ from showing that $\neg P$ leads to a contradiction.

:::

::: {#o153 .entry cue=term name="coextensive"}

**coextensive**

Having the same scope or range, especially referring to two terms or concepts that apply to the same set of objects.[^o154][^o155]

:::

::: {#o156 .entry cue=term name="cogent inductive argument"}

**cogent inductive argument**

A strong inductive argument where all the premises are true.[^o157][^o158][^o159][^o160]

:::

::: {#o161 .entry cue=term name="coherence theory of truth"}

**[coherence theory of truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_theory_of_truth)**

A theory that holds truth as coherence among a set of beliefs or statements, rather than correspondence with reality or facts.

:::

::: {#o162 .entry cue=term name="combinator"}

**[combinator](wikipedia://en/149848)**

A function or expression in combinatory logic that acts on arguments to produce results without the need for variable bindings.

:::

::: {#o163 .entry cue=term name="combinatorialism"}

**combinatorialism**

Combinatorialism is the view that any arbitrary combination of elements constitutes a legitimate mathematical structure, whether that structure is definable or not.[^o164][^o165][^o166]

:::

::: {#o167 .entry cue=term name="combinatory logic"}

**[combinatory logic](wikipedia://en/149848)**

A branch of mathematical logic that seeks to eliminate the need for variables in mathematical expressions by using combinators.

:::

::: {#o168 .entry cue=term name="combinatory terms"}

**combinatory terms**

See combinator.

:::

::: {#o169 .entry cue=term name="commutativity"}

**[commutativity](wikipedia://en/294390)**

A property of binary operations where the order of the operands does not change the result, as in addition and multiplication in arithmetic.

:::

::: {#o170 .entry cue=term name="compactness theorem"}

**[compactness theorem](wikipedia://en/152207)**

A theorem in logic stating that if every finite subset of a set of sentences has a model, then the entire set has a model.

:::

::: {#o171 .entry cue=term name="complete infinity"}

**[complete infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completed_infinity)**

A concept in philosophy and mathematics referring to an actual infinity that is considered as a completed whole, contrasting with potential infinities that are indefinitely extendable.

:::

::: {#o172 .entry cue=term name="completeness"}

**[completeness](wikipedia://en/31271451)**

In logic, the property of a logical system whereby every logically valid formula can be derived using the system's axioms and inference rules.

:::

::: {#o173 .entry cue=term name="complexity class"}

**[complexity class](wikipedia://en/502426)**

A classification of decision problems based on their inherent computational complexity, grouping problems that can be solved within similar resource constraints.

:::

::: {#o174 .entry cue=term name="complexity theory"}

**[complexity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity_theory_%28computation%29)**

The study of the inherent difficulty of computational problems, focusing on the resources needed to solve them, such as time and memory.

:::

::: {#o175 .entry cue=term name="composition"}

**[composition](wikipedia://en/195947)**

The act of combining simple objects or ideas into a complex whole, or the resulting combination itself.

:::

::: {#o176 .entry cue=term name="compositionality"}

**[compositionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositionality)**

The principle in semantics that the meaning of a complex expression is determined by the meanings of its constituent expressions and the rules used to combine them.

:::

::: {#o177 .entry cue=term name="compound formula"}

**compound formula**

A formula representing a compound statement.[^o178]

:::

::: {#o179 .entry cue=term name="compound statement"}

**compound statement**

A statement in logic that is formed by combining two or more statements with logical connectives, allowing for the construction of more complex statements from simpler ones.[^o180][^o181]

:::

::: {#o182 .entry cue=term name="comprehension schema"}

**[comprehension schema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrestricted_comprehension)**

A principle in set theory and logic allowing for the formation of sets based on a defining property or condition.

:::

::: {#o183 .entry cue=term name="computable function"}

**[computable function](wikipedia://en/1139338)**

A function for which there exists an algorithm that can compute the function's value for any valid input within a finite amount of time.

:::

::: {#o184 .entry cue=term name="computation"}

**[computation](wikipedia://en/5926)**

The process of performing a sequence of calculations or logical operations systematically to obtain a result.

:::

::: {#o185 .entry cue=term name="concept"}

**[concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept)**

An abstract idea representing the fundamental characteristics of what it describes.

:::

::: {#o345 .entry cue=term name="dual"}

**dual**

The dual of a truth-table is obtained by interchanging the truth values "true" and "false" (or 0 and 1) throughout the table. Connectives are dual if their truth-tables are dual: conjunction and disjunction are dual, and negation is self-dual.[^o346] The dual of a formula is obtained by replacing each connective by its dual,[^o346][^o347] e.g., for a formula containing only conjunction, disjunction, and negation (such as a formula in [disjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73341)), its dual is the result of replacing each conjunction with a disjunction, and each disjunction with a conjunction. (For a formula in disjunctive normal form, its dual is a formula in [conjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73342).)[^o348][^o349]

The relation that holds between premises and the conclusions derivable from them based on [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) and [inference rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference_rules) within a [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102).[^o1131] Contrast semantic consequence.

:::

::: {#o1132 .entry cue=term name="syntax"}

**[syntax](wikipedia://en/546039)**

The set of rules, principles, and processes that govern the structure of sentences in a given language, distinguishing between correct and incorrect forms of expression.

:::

## T {#T}

::: {#o1134 .entry cue=term name="Tarskian hierarchy"}

**Tarskian hierarchy**

A hierarchical structure of languages proposed by Alfred Tarski to avoid paradoxes in semantic theories, where each level of language can only reference levels below it, preventing self-reference.[^o815]

:::

::: {#o1135 .entry cue=term name="Tarski's indefinability theorem"}

**[Tarski's indefinability theorem](wikipedia://en/583785)**

A theorem stating that truth cannot be consistently defined within the same language it applies to, requiring a meta-language for a definition of truth to avoid paradoxes.

:::

::: {#o1136 .entry cue=term name="tautology"}

**[tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335)**

A statement or formula that is true in every possible interpretation, often used in propositional logic to refer to formulas that are logically true.

:::

::: {#o1137 .entry cue=term name="temperature paradox"}

**[temperature paradox](wikipedia://en/68292003)**

A paradox where, from "the temperature is ninety" and "the temperature is rising", it is concluded that "ninety is rising", which seems invalid but can actually be valid under some formalization schemes.

:::

::: {#o1138 .entry cue=term name="temporal modal logic"}

**temporal modal logic**

A branch of modal logic that deals with modalities related to time, such as 'always', 'sometimes', and 'never', allowing for reasoning about temporal aspects of propositions.[^o1139]

:::

::: {#o1140 .entry cue=term name="term logic"}

**[term logic](wikipedia://en/321652)**

An approach to logic focusing on the relations between terms in propositions and the inferences that can be drawn from them, characteristic of Aristotelian logic.

:::

::: {#o1141 .entry cue=term name="ternary function"}

**[ternary function](wikipedia://en/459413)**

A function that takes three arguments or inputs, in contrast to unary and binary functions.

:::

::: {#o1142 .entry cue=term name="ternary relation"}

**[ternary relation](wikipedia://en/3784565)**

A relation involving three elements, where the relation specifies how the elements are connected or related.

:::

::: {#o1143 .entry cue=term name="ternary semantics"}

**ternary semantics**

A ternary semantics is a [possible worlds semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possible_world_semantics) that makes use of a [ternary relation](wikipedia://en/3784565) on [possible worlds](wikipedia://en/780566) instead of, or in addition to, the more standard binary [accessibility relation](wikipedia://en/3708585). Ternary semantics have been most widely applied in the development of [relevant logics](wikipedia://en/185076).[^o1144]

:::

::: {#o1145 .entry cue=term name="theorem"}

**[theorem](wikipedia://en/30977)**

A statement or proposition that has been formally proven on the basis of previously established statements or axioms within a logical or mathematical system.

:::

::: {#o1146 .entry cue=term name="theory"}

**[theory](wikipedia://en/30746)**

A coherent set of propositions or statements, especially one that forms a comprehensive explanation of some aspect of the natural world or an abstract concept.

:::

::: {#o1147 .entry cue=term name="three-valued logic"}

**[three-valued logic](wikipedia://en/249904)**

A logical system that introduces a third truth value (such as 'unknown', 'indeterminate', or 'both true and false') in addition to the classic binary true and false values.

:::

::: {#o1148 .entry cue=term name="tilde"}

**[tilde](wikipedia://en/212390)**

The symbol '~' used in logic to denote negation, indicating that the proposition it precedes is false.

:::

::: {#o1149 .entry cue=term name="token"}

**[token](wikipedia://en/14934822)**

An instance of a type, such as a particular occurrence of a word or phrase, in contrast to the abstract concept or category it represents.

:::

::: {#o1150 .entry cue=term name="tolerant"}

**tolerant**

In the theory of [vagueness](wikipedia://en/32369), a predicate is considered tolerant if, and only if, small changes in the relevant underlying properties of an object do not affect the justice with which the predicate applies to it. Thus, the predicate "bald" is tolerant, since one hair more or less does not transform a clear instance of baldness into a clear instance of non-baldness.[^o380]

:::

::: {#o1151 .entry cue=term name="tonk"}

**tonk**

A fictional [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) introduced to illustrate the importance of preserving inference rules in defining logical operators, showing that arbitrary rules can lead to absurdity.[^o1152][^o1153][^o34]

:::

::: {#o1154 .entry cue=term name="top"}

**[top](wikipedia://en/19145299)**

In logic, a symbol (⊤) representing the highest or maximal element in a lattice or order, often used to denote a [tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335) or universally true proposition in propositional logic.

:::

::: {#o1155 .entry cue=term name="topos"}

**[topos](wikipedia://en/21391464)**

See *elementary topos*

:::

::: {#o1156 .entry cue=term name="topos theory"}

**[topos theory](wikipedia://en/21391464)**

The study of toposes, which are categories that behave like the category of sets and provide a foundation for much of mathematics, allowing for generalized notions of computation and logic.

:::

::: {#o1157 .entry cue=term name="total function"}

**[total function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_function)**

A function that is defined for every element of its domain, ensuring that there is an output for each input.

:::

::: {#o1158 .entry cue=term name="transition function"}

**transition function**

See action table.

:::

::: {#o1159 .entry cue=term name="transitive closure"}

**[transitive closure](wikipedia://en/292239)**

The smallest transitive relation that contains a given relation, effectively adding the minimum necessary elements to make the original relation transitive.

:::

::: {#o1160 .entry cue=term name="transitivity"}

**[transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463)**

A property of a relation where if the relation holds between A and B, and between B and C, then it also holds between A and C, ensuring a kind of consistency or continuity in the relation across elements.

:::

::: {#o1161 .entry cue=term name="translation"}

**[translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation)**

A translation is a function from the expressions of one language to the expressions of another language. Translations are typically intended to preserve either the meanings or the truth conditions of the translated expressions.

:::

::: {#o1162 .entry cue=term name="translation lemma"}

**translation lemma**

Synonym for the [S theorem](wikipedia://en/2330936).

:::

::: {#o1163 .entry cue=term name="transposition"}

**[transposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28logic%29)**

A logical rule that allows the equivalent transformation of a conditional statement $P \rightarrow Q$ into its contrapositive $\neg Q \rightarrow \neg P$, preserving truth.

:::

::: {#o1164 .entry cue=term name="trans-world identity"}

**[trans-world identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transworld_identity)**

A concept in modal logic and metaphysics concerning the identity of individuals across different possible worlds, addressing questions of persistence and change.

:::

::: {#o1165 .entry cue=term name="tribar"}

**tribar**

A name for the symbol ≡, used for [logical equivalence](wikipedia://en/228783).[^o1166]

:::

::: {#o1167 .entry cue=term name="trichotomy"}

**trichotomy**

A relation R is trichotomous (or comparable) if and only if, for any objects x and y, either Rxy, or Ryx, or x = y.[^o1168]

:::

::: {#o1169 .entry cue=term name="trichotomy law"}

**[trichotomy law](wikipedia://en/373956)**

A law in order theory and mathematics stating that for any two elements in a certain set, exactly one of three relationships (greater than, less than, or equal to) must hold.

:::

::: {#o1170 .entry cue=term name="triviality"}

**[triviality](wikipedia://en/193053)**

The state or quality of being trivial, in logic and mathematics, often referring to statements, propositions, or problems that are oversimplified or of little interest or importance.

:::

::: {#o1171 .entry cue=term name="truth"}

**[truth](wikipedia://en/29920)**

A concept in logic and philosophy concerning the property of statements, beliefs, or propositions corresponding to reality or fact, or being in accord with the actual state of affairs.

:::

::: {#o1172 .entry cue=term name="truth conditions"}

**[truth conditions](wikipedia://en/45132)**

The conditions under which a statement or proposition is considered to be true, playing a central role in the semantic analysis of language.

:::

::: {#o1173 .entry cue=term name="truth function"}

**[truth function](wikipedia://en/604707)**

A function that takes truth values as input and produces a truth value as output, used in logic to model the truth conditions of logical connectives.

:::

::: {#o1174 .entry cue=term name="truth-functional"}

**truth-functional**

Pertaining to an operator or connective in logic whose output truth value depends solely on the input truth values, without regard to the content of the propositions involved.[^o1175]

:::

::: {#o1176 .entry cue=term name="truth-in-a-model"}

**truth-in-a-model**

A concept in [semantic theories of truth](wikipedia://en/601240) indicating that a statement or proposition is true relative to a particular [model or interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) of the [language](wikipedia://en/10939) in which it is expressed.[^o1177]

:::

::: {#o1178 .entry cue=term name="truthmaker"}

**[truthmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truthmaker_theory)**

An entity or set of entities in the world that makes a proposition true, grounding truth in aspects of reality.

:::

::: {#o1179 .entry cue=term name="truth predicate"}

**[truth predicate](wikipedia://en/4807326)**

A predicate that assigns the property of being true to propositions, often discussed in relation to Tarski's semantic conception of truth and the liar paradox.

:::

::: {#o1180 .entry cue=term name="truth table"}

**[truth table](wikipedia://en/25512250)**

A table used in logic to show the truth value of a compound statement for every possible combination of truth values of its components, instrumental in analyzing logical expressions.

:::

::: {#o1181 .entry cue=term name="truth-teller"}

**truth-teller**

The converse of the liar paradox, a statement that asserts its own truth, raising questions about self-reference and the nature of truth.[^o1182]

:::

::: {#o1183 .entry cue=term name="truth-tree"}

**[truth-tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth-tree)**

Synonym of [analytic tableaux](wikipedia://en/1027229).

:::

::: {#o1184 .entry cue=term name="truth value"}

**[truth value](wikipedia://en/161711)**

The value indicating the truth or falsity of a proposition or statement, typically represented as true or false in classical logic, but possibly more varied in many-valued logics.

:::

::: {#o1185 .entry cue=term name="truth-value gap"}

**truth-value gap**

A situation where a statement or proposition cannot be assigned a traditional truth value of true or false, often due to vagueness or undefined terms.[^o1186]

:::

::: {#o1187 .entry cue=term name="truth-value glut"}

**truth-value glut**

A condition in which a statement or proposition is paradoxically both true and false simultaneously, associated with dialetheism and contradictions.[^o1188]

:::

::: {#o1189 .entry cue=term name="T-schema"}

**[T-schema](wikipedia://en/1063436)**

The Tarski schema for defining truth, stating that 'P' is true if and only if P, where 'P' is a placeholder for a proposition and P is the proposition itself.

:::

::: {#o1190 .entry cue=term name="tu quoque"}

**[tu quoque](wikipedia://en/1090534)**

A logical fallacy that attempts to discredit an opponent's position by asserting the opponent's failure to act consistently with that position, essentially accusing them of hypocrisy.

:::

::: {#o1191 .entry cue=term name="Turing computable function"}

**[Turing computable function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing-computable_function)**

A function that can be calculated by a [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403), representing the class of functions that are computable in principle, according to the Church–Turing thesis.

:::

::: {#o1192 .entry cue=term name="Turing thesis"}

**Turing thesis**

See Church–Turing thesis.

:::

::: {#o1193 .entry cue=term name="turnstile"}

**[turnstile](wikipedia://en/7954712)**

A symbol used in logic ($\vdash$) to denote syntactic entailment or provability, indicating that the statement or set of statements to the right is a logical consequence of the statements to the left within a given formal system.

:::

::: {#o1194 .entry cue=term name="type"}

**type**

(In [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282).) A category or class of entities that share certain characteristics, used in logic and mathematics to distinguish between different kinds of objects, expressions, or variables, preventing certain kinds of logical paradoxes.

(In the [type-token distinction](wikipedia://en/14934822).) A universal, as opposed to a particular..

:::

::: {#o1195 .entry cue=term name="type theory"}

**[type theory](wikipedia://en/40282)**

A framework in mathematical logic and computer science that uses types to classify expressions and objects, aiming to avoid paradoxes like Russell's paradox by organizing objects into hierarchies or levels and restricting operations to objects of the same type.

:::

## U {#U}

::: {#o1197 .entry cue=term name="unary function"}

**[unary function](wikipedia://en/161006)**

A function that operates on a single input or argument, common in mathematics and logic for representing operations like negation or the absolute value function.

:::

::: {#o1198 .entry cue=term name="unary relation"}

**[unary relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unary_relation)**

A relation that applies to a single element, used to characterize properties or attributes that an element may or may not possess.

:::

::: {#o1199 .entry cue=term name="universal elimination"}

**[universal elimination](wikipedia://en/1564194)**

A rule of inference in predicate logic that allows for the derivation of a specific statement about an individual from a general statement that applies to all members of a category.

:::

::: {#o1200 .entry cue=term name="universal introduction"}

**[universal introduction](wikipedia://en/939333)**

A rule of inference in predicate logic that allows for the generalization of a statement to all members of a category if the statement is shown to hold for an arbitrary but specific individual.

:::

::: {#o1201 .entry cue=term name="universal proposition"}

**universal proposition**

A statement in logic that asserts something about all members of a certain category, typically formulated using a [universal quantifier](wikipedia://en/76174).

:::

::: {#o1202 .entry cue=term name="universal quantifier"}

**[universal quantifier](wikipedia://en/76174)**

A symbol in predicate logic ($\forall$) used to indicate that the statement it precedes holds for all individuals in the domain of discourse.

:::

::: {#o1203 .entry cue=term name="universal variable"}

**universal variable**

A variable in predicate logic that is bound by a [universal quantifier](wikipedia://en/76174), representing any and all individuals in the domain of discourse.

:::

::: {#o1204 .entry cue=term name="universe of discourse"}

**[universe of discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe_of_discourse)**

The set of all objects, individuals, or values that are relevant in a particular logical or mathematical discussion, serving as the domain over which quantifiers range.

:::

::: {#o1205 .entry cue=term name="upward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem"}

**upward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem**

Part of the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem.

:::

::: {#o1206 .entry cue=term name="unsound deductive argument"}

**[unsound deductive argument](wikipedia://en/26819)**

An argument that is either invalid in its logical form or contains at least one false premise, and therefore does not guarantee the truth of its conclusion.

:::

::: {#o1207 .entry cue=term name="use"}

**use**

In philosophy, particularly in the analysis of language, "use" refers to the actual application of a word or a phrase in a sentence to convey meaning. In the [use-mention distinction](wikipedia://en/172990), "use" involves employing words to refer to things, actions, qualities, or concepts in the world. For example, in the sentence "I enjoy reading books," the word "books" is used to refer to objects that can be read; it is not merely mention.

:::

## V {#V}

::: {#o1209 .entry cue=term name="vacuous quantifier"}

**vacuous quantifier**

A vacuous [quantifier](wikipedia://en/43507260) is one that doesn't bind any variables, such as the second quantifier in $\forall x \forall y Rxx$.[^o1210]

:::

::: {#o1211 .entry cue=term name="vagueness"}

**vagueness**

The characteristic of terms, concepts, or propositions that lack clear boundaries or precision in meaning, leading to indeterminate or borderline cases.[^o109]

:::

::: {#o1212 .entry cue=term name="valid"}

**[valid](wikipedia://en/21304742)**

A deductive argument whose structure ensures that if all the premises are true, then the conclusion must also be true, demonstrating logical validity.

In [model-theoretic semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics_of_logic), a formula that is true under all [interpretations](wikipedia://en/14511671).

In [proof-theoretic semantics](wikipedia://en/910505), a formula that is either an explicit rule of inference of a system, or that does not allow one to prove anything that could not be proved using the explicit rules of inference.[^o1213]

:::

::: {#o1214 .entry cue=term name="valuation"}

**valuation**

Another name for an [interpretation of a formal language](wikipedia://en/14511671), coming from the fact that it assigns semantic values (such as [truth values](wikipedia://en/161711)) to the language's formulas.[^o1215]

:::

::: {#o1216 .entry cue=term name="variable assignment"}

**variable assignment**

Sometimes, an [interpretation of a formal language](wikipedia://en/14511671) for [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) or [higher-order logic](wikipedia://en/562883) is called simply a "variable assignment",[^o1217] or "variable assignment function".[^o1218]

:::

::: {#o1219 .entry cue=term name="vel"}

**[vel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vel_%28symbol%29)**

The Latin word for "or", used in logic as a name for ∨, the [descending wedge](wikipedia://en/37701611) symbol. The symbol is used to denote a disjunction that is inclusive, meaning at least one of the disjuncts must be true for the whole expression to be true.

:::

::: {#o1220 .entry cue=term name="verification constraint"}

**verification constraint**

See epistemic constraint.

:::

::: {#o1221 .entry cue=term name="verity"}

**verity**

The verity (or "degree of truth") of a statement is the semantic value of that statement within degree-theoretic semantics, which assigns degrees between 0 and 1 to statements.[^o1222]

:::

::: {#o1223 .entry cue=term name="verum"}

**[verum](wikipedia://en/19145299)**

*Verum* ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for "true") is another name for the $\top$ symbol,[^o1224] which represents a primitive, necessarily true statement, and is sometimes considered a [nullary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullary) [connective](wikipedia://en/18153).

:::

::: {#o1225 .entry cue=term name="vicious circle principle"}

**[vicious circle principle](wikipedia://en/24709966)**

A principle against definitions or arguments that are circular, ensuring that the thing being defined is not used in its own definition or premise in a way that presupposes its conclusion.

:::

## W {#W}

::: {#o1227 .entry cue=term name="weak completeness"}

**[weak completeness](wikipedia://en/31271451#Semantic_completeness)**

The property of a logical system where if a statement is semantically valid (true under all interpretations), then there is a proof of the statement within the system.[^o1228]

:::

::: {#o1229 .entry cue=term name="weak counterexample"}

**weak counterexample**

Within [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) and [intuitionistic mathematics](wikipedia://en/19513), a weak counterexample is a situation in which we have no positive evidence for the (intuitionistic) truth of some instance of the law of excluded middle, $p \lor \neg p$.[^o1230][^o1231]

:::

::: {#o1232 .entry cue=term name="weak excluded middle"}

**[weak excluded middle](wikipedia://en/17637#Law_of_the_weak_excluded_middle)**

A principle in intuitionistic logic stating that for any proposition P, either P is provable or not-P is provable, but not necessarily both, reflecting a more nuanced view of truth than the classical law of excluded middle.

:::

::: {#o1233 .entry cue=term name="weak Kleene connectives"}

**weak Kleene connectives**

[Connectives defined in the three-valued logic of Kleene](wikipedia://en/249904#Kleene_and_Priest_logics), which do not fully determine the truth value of compound statements if any component statement is undefined.

:::

::: {#o1234 .entry cue=term name="weak mathematical induction"}

**weak mathematical induction**

A form of [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) that only assumes the truth of the statement for the immediately preceding case to prove its truth for any natural number, as opposed to strong induction, which assumes the statement for all smaller numbers.

:::

::: {#o1235 .entry cue=term name="weak negation"}

**[weak negation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_negation)**

A form of negation in some non-classical logics where the negation of a proposition does not assert the truth of the opposite proposition but rather the absence of truth of the original proposition.

:::

::: {#o1236 .entry cue=term name="weak paraconsistency"}

**weak paraconsistency**

Weak paraconsistency is the view that true contradictions, and worlds that contain them, are merely a formal tool used to study reasoning. Compare strong paraconsistency, the view that there are [possible worlds](wikipedia://en/780566) where contradictions are true, or where some statements are both true and false.[^o1090]

:::

::: {#o1237 .entry cue=term name="weakening"}

**[weakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakening_%28logic%29)**

A rule in both propositional and predicate logic allowing the addition of propositions to a derivation without affecting its validity, reflecting the idea that if something follows from a set of premises, it also follows from any larger set of premises.

:::

::: {#o1238 .entry cue=term name="wedge"}

**[wedge](wikipedia://en/37702563)**

A symbol used in logic ($\wedge$) to represent logical conjunction, indicating that both connected statements are true.

:::

::: {#o1239 .entry cue=term name="well-formed formula"}

**[well-formed formula](wikipedia://en/404582)**

A string of symbols in a formal language that follows the syntactic rules of the language, making it a meaningful or grammatically correct expression within the context of that system.

:::

::: {#o1240 .entry cue=term name="wff"}

**wff**

Short for well-formed formula. Pronounced "woof",[^o1241][^o1242][^o1243][^o1244] or sometimes "wiff",[^o1245][^o1246][^o1247] "weff",[^o1248][^o1249] or "whiff".[^o1250] (All sources supported "woof". The sources cited for "wiff", "weff", and "whiff" gave these pronunciations as alternatives to "woof". Gensler[^o1241] gives "wood" and "woofer" as examples of how to pronounce the vowel in "woof".) Plural "wffs".[^o1241]

:::

## Y {#Y}

::: {#o1252 .entry cue=term name="Yablo paradox"}

**[Yablo paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yablo's_paradox)**

A paradox involving an infinite sequence of sentences, each of which states that all following sentences in the sequence are false. Unlike the [liar paradox](wikipedia://en/17967), it does not rely on self-reference, raising questions about the nature of paradoxes and infinity.

:::

## Z {#Z}

::: {#o1254 .entry cue=term name="Zeno paradoxes"}

**[Zeno paradoxes](wikipedia://en/34535)**

A series of paradoxes proposed by the ancient Greek philosopher Zeno of Elea to challenge the coherence of the concepts of plurality, motion, and the continuum, including the famous paradoxes of Achilles and the tortoise, and the dichotomy.
