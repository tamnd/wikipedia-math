# Nicholas Franklin Roberts

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicholas Franklin Roberts"}

**Nicholas Franklin Roberts** (October 13, 1849 – June 25, 1934) was a leading Baptist minister and educator in Raleigh, North Carolina. He was a faculty member and for a short time acting president at [Shaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_University). He was also a city alderman in [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh) and served on the county board of education.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Nicholas Franklin Roberts was born in Seaboard, Northampton County, North Carolina on October 13, 1849. As a child, he worked on a farm. He showed aptitude for mathematics at a young age and entered Shaw University (then Shaw Collegiate Institute) on October 10, 1871. He graduated in May 1878 and was then hired as a professor of Mathematics at Shaw.[^o6]

Roberts was active in Republican politics, even as a student, serving as secretary of a Republican county meeting in July 1872.[^o7] Along with Shaw University president, [Henry Martin Tupper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Martin_Tupper), and fellow student, [Edward Hart Lipscombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Hart_Lipscombe), Roberts was an editor of the quarterly journal, *African Expositor*, founded in 1878[^o8] and was later the business manager of the *Baptist Sentinel*.[^o9]

## Baptist church service {#Baptist_church_service}

In March 1872, he converted to the Baptist religion and on May 20, 1877 he was ordained a minister. On July 2, 1882, he was made pastor of [Blount Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blount_Street_Historic_District) Baptist Church in Raleigh, North Carolina. From 1873 to 1883, he was president of the State Sunday School convention,[^o6] a position he served in many times.[^o11] He was also elected president of the State Baptist Convention numerous times,[^o11] first in October 1885.[^o6] He was made general missionary among the poor in North Carolina by the [American Baptist Home Mission Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Baptist_Home_Mission_Society) in 1881.[^o12] He resigned as pastor of the Blount Street Baptist Church in August 1891 to focus on his other activities.[^o11]

## Public service {#Public_service}

Roberts was heavily involved in political, educational, and religious organizations and served as an officer of many bodies. He was vice president of the state Colored Education Convention in 1877.[^o14] In 1886, he was an alderman in Raleigh and a member of the street committee.[^o15] He was elected to the county school board in 1897.[^o16] He also led the Institute for Colored Teachers in the early 1900s.[^o17]

In late 1897 and early 1898, Roberts hosted [South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa) tribal nobility, including the grandson of Chief Kama (also called King Kama) of [Kaffraria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaffraria) and the nephew of Chief William Shaw Kama, [Alfred Impey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Impey), as Alfred attended Shaw University.[^o18] However, Impey died of consumption in North Carolina in April 1898.[^o19]

Shaw served as acting head of Shaw University for five months after Tupper died on November 12, 1893, serving from that date until March 14, 1894.[^o20] He later held the positions of vice-president,[^o21] dean of faculty,[^o22] and dean of the theology department.[^o23]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

He married Mary S. Chavis of Union, North Carolina in 1904.[^o25] He died on June 25, 1934.[^o23] Roberts Lane in [Oberlin Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_Village) was named for him.[^o26]
