# Tanaka's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tanaka's formula"}

In the [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001), **Tanaka's formula** for the [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) states that

:::

$$|B_t| = \int_0^t \operatorname{sgn}(B_s)\, dB_s + L_t$$

where *B*<sub>*t*</sub> is the standard Brownian motion, sgn denotes the [sign function](wikipedia://en/505449)

$$\operatorname{sgn} (x) = \begin{cases} +1, & x > 0; \\0,& x=0 \\-1, & x < 0. \end{cases}$$

and *L*<sub>*t*</sub> is its [local time](wikipedia://en/5678101) at 0 (the local time spent by *B* at 0 before time *t*) given by the [*L*<sup>2</sup>-limit](wikipedia://en/45194)

$$L_{t} = \lim_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \frac1{2 \varepsilon} | \{ s \in [0, t] | B_{s} \in (- \varepsilon, + \varepsilon) \} |.$$

One can also extend the formula to [semimartingales](wikipedia://en/9653775).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Tanaka's formula is the explicit [Doob–Meyer decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doob&ndash;Meyer_decomposition_theorem) of the submartingale |*B*<sub>*t*</sub>| into the [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536) part (the [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) on the right-hand side, which is a Brownian motion[^o4]), and a continuous increasing process (local time). It can also be seen as the analogue of [Itō's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō's_lemma) for the (nonsmooth) absolute value function $f(x)=|x|$, with $f'(x) = \operatorname{sgn}(x)$ and $f''(x) = 2\delta(x)$; see [local time](wikipedia://en/5678101) for a formal explanation of the Itō term.

:::

## Outline of proof {#Outline_of_proof}

The [function](wikipedia://en/185427) |*x*| is not [*C*<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) in *x* at *x* = 0, so we cannot apply [Itō's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō's_formula) directly. But if we approximate it near zero (i.e. in \[−*ε*, *ε*\]) by [parabolas](wikipedia://en/23231)

$$\frac{x^2}{2|\varepsilon|}+\frac{|\varepsilon|}{2}.$$

and use [Itō's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō's_formula), we can then take the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602) as *ε* → 0, leading to Tanaka's formula.
