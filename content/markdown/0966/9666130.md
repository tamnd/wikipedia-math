# Richard Fletcher (American politician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Fletcher"}

**Richard Fletcher** (January 8, 1788 – June 21, 1869) was a member of the [United States House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) from [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts). The brother of [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Vermont) [Ryland Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryland_Fletcher), he was born in [Cavendish, Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish,_Vermont) on January 8, 1788. He pursued classical studies and graduated from [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) in 1806. He taught school in [Salisbury, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury,_New_Hampshire), studied law, was admitted to the bar and commenced practice there.

:::

He moved to [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston,_Massachusetts) in 1819 and was elected as a [Whig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_Party_%28United_States%29) to the Twenty-fifth Congress (March 4, 1837 – March 3, 1839). Fletcher was not a candidate for renomination to the Twenty-sixth Congress. He served as a judge of the [Massachusetts Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Supreme_Court) 1848–1853, and died in Boston on June 21, 1869. His interment was in [Mount Auburn Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Auburn_Cemetery) in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts).

Fletcher was elected as the first president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), although by the ASA's own admission, he was "little more than a figurehead".[^o3]
