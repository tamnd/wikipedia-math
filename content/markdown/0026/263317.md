# Spectrogram

*Image: Spectrogram-19thC.png.* Spectrogram of the spoken words "nineteenth century". Frequencies are shown increasing up the vertical axis, and time on the horizontal axis. The legend to the right shows that the color intensity increases with the density.

*Image: 3D battery charger RF spectrum over time.jpg.* A 3D spectrogram: The RF spectrum of a battery charger is shown over time

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spectrogram"}

A **spectrogram** is a visual representation of the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/202672) of [frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequencies) of a signal as it varies with time. When applied to an [audio signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal), spectrograms are sometimes called **sonographs**, **voiceprints**, or **voicegrams**. When the data are represented in a 3D plot they may be called *[waterfall displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall_display)*.

:::

Spectrograms are used extensively in the fields of [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar), [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar), [speech processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_processing),[^o2] [seismology](wikipedia://en/28935), [ornithology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithology), and others. Spectrograms of audio can be used to identify spoken words [phonetically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonetics), and to analyse the [various calls of animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_communication).

A spectrogram can be generated by an [optical spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_spectrometer), a bank of [band-pass filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band-pass_filter), by [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) or by a [wavelet transform](wikipedia://en/3352292) (in which case it is also known as a **scaleogram** or **scalogram**).[^o3]

*Image: Scaleogram.png.* Scaleograms from the [DWT](wikipedia://en/857564) and [CWT](wikipedia://en/679596) for an audio sample

A spectrogram is usually depicted as a [heat map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_map), i.e., as an image with the intensity shown by varying the [colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colour) or [brightness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightness).

## Format {#Format}

A common format is a graph with two geometric dimensions: one axis represents [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time), and the other axis represents [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779); a third dimension indicating the [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude) of a particular frequency at a particular time is represented by the [intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightness) or color of each point in the image.

There are many variations of format: sometimes the vertical and horizontal axes are switched, so time runs up and down; sometimes as a [waterfall plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall_plot) where the amplitude is represented by height of a 3D surface instead of color or intensity. The frequency and amplitude axes can be either [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear) or [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/17860), depending on what the graph is being used for. Audio would usually be represented with a logarithmic amplitude axis (probably in [decibels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel), or dB), and frequency would be linear to emphasize harmonic relationships, or logarithmic to emphasize musical, tonal relationships.

*Image: Sound spectrography of infrasound recording 30301.webm.* Sound spectrography of infrasound recording 30301

## Generation {#Generation}

Spectrograms of light may be created directly using an [optical spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_spectrometer) over time.

Spectrograms may be created from a [time-domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-domain) signal in one of two ways: approximated as a filterbank that results from a series of [band-pass filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band-pass_filter) (this was the only way before the advent of modern digital signal processing), or calculated from the time signal using the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247). These two methods actually form two different [time–frequency representations](wikipedia://en/857710), but are equivalent under some conditions.

The bandpass filters method usually uses [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal) processing to divide the input signal into frequency bands; the magnitude of each filter's output controls a transducer that records the spectrogram as an image on paper.[^o6]

Creating a spectrogram using the FFT is a [digital process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processing). Digitally [sampled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_%28signal_processing%29) data, in the [time domain](wikipedia://en/406624), is broken up into chunks, which usually overlap, and Fourier transformed to calculate the magnitude of the frequency spectrum for each chunk. Each chunk then corresponds to a vertical line in the image; a measurement of magnitude versus frequency for a specific moment in time (the midpoint of the chunk). These spectrums or time plots are then "laid side by side" to form the image or a three-dimensional surface,[^o7] or slightly overlapped in various ways, i.e. [windowing](wikipedia://en/244097#Overlapping_windows). This process essentially corresponds to computing the squared [magnitude](wikipedia://en/577301) of the [short-time Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/436912) (STFT) of the signal $s(t)$ — that is, for a window width $\omega$, $\mathrm{spectrogram}(t,\omega)=\left|\mathrm{STFT}(t,\omega)\right|^2$.[^o8]

## Limitations and resynthesis {#Limitations_and_resynthesis}

From the formula above, it appears that a spectrogram contains no information about the exact, or even approximate, [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) of the signal that it represents. For this reason, it is not possible to reverse the process and generate a copy of the original signal from a spectrogram, though in situations where the exact initial phase is unimportant it may be possible to generate a useful approximation of the original signal. The Analysis & Resynthesis Sound Spectrograph[^o10] is an example of a computer program that attempts to do this. The [pattern playback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_playback) was an early speech synthesizer, designed at [Haskins Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskins_Laboratories) in the late 1940s, that converted pictures of the acoustic patterns of speech (spectrograms) back into sound.

In fact, there is some phase information in the spectrogram, but it appears in another form, as time delay (or [group delay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_delay)) which is the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28mathematics%29) of the [instantaneous frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instantaneous_frequency).[^o11]

The size and shape of the analysis window can be varied. A smaller (shorter) window will produce more accurate results in timing, at the expense of precision of frequency representation. A larger (longer) window will provide a more precise frequency representation, at the expense of precision in timing representation. This is an instance of the [Heisenberg uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_uncertainty_principle), that the product of the precision in two [conjugate variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_variables) is greater than or equal to a constant (B\*T>=1 in the usual notation).[^o12]

## Applications {#Applications}

- Early analog spectrograms were applied to a wide range of areas including the study of bird calls, with current research continuing using modern digital equipment[^o15] and applied to all animal sounds. Contemporary use of the digital spectrogram is especially useful for studying [frequency modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation) (FM) in animal calls. Specifically, the distinguishing characteristics of FM chirps, broadband [clicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clicking_noise), and social harmonizing are most easily visualized with the spectrogram.
- Spectrograms are useful in assisting in overcoming speech deficits and in speech training for the portion of the population that is profoundly [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_impairment).[^o16]
- The studies of [phonetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonetics) and [speech synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_synthesis) are often facilitated through the use of spectrograms.[^o17][^o18]
- In deep learning-keyed speech synthesis, spectrogram (or spectrogram in [mel scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_scale)) is first predicted by a seq2seq model, then the spectrogram is fed to a neural vocoder to derive the synthesized raw waveform.
- By reversing the process of producing a spectrogram, it is possible to create a signal whose spectrogram is an arbitrary image. This technique can be used to hide a picture in a piece of audio and has been employed by several [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music) artists.[^o19] See also [Steganography](wikipedia://en/28733).
- Some modern music is created using spectrograms as an intermediate medium; changing the intensity of different frequencies over time, or even creating new ones, by drawing them and then inverse transforming. See [Audio timescale-pitch modification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_timescale-pitch_modification) and [Phase vocoder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_vocoder).
- Spectrograms can be used to analyze the results of passing a test signal through a signal processor such as a filter in order to check its performance.[^o20]
- High definition spectrograms are used in the development of RF and microwave systems.[^o21]
- Spectrograms are now used to display [scattering parameters](wikipedia://en/1972332) measured with vector network analyzers.[^o22]
- The [US Geological Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Geological_Survey) and the [IRIS Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS_Consortium) provide near real-time spectrogram displays for monitoring seismic stations.[^o23][^o24]
- Spectrograms can be used with [recurrent neural networks](wikipedia://en/1706303) for [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition).[^o25][^o26]
- For a vibration signal, a spectrogram's color scale identifies the frequencies of a waveform's amplitude peaks over time. Unlike a time or frequency graph, a spectrogram correlates peak values to time and frequency. Vibration test engineers use spectrograms to analyze the frequency content of a continuous waveform, locating strong signals and determining how the vibration behavior changes over time.[^o27]
- Spectrograms can be used to analyze speech in two different applications: automatic detection of speech deficits in cochlear implant users and phoneme class recognition to extract phone-attribute features.[^o28]
- In order to obtain a speaker's pronunciation characteristics, some researchers proposed a method based on an idea from bionics, which uses spectrogram statistics to achieve a characteristic spectrogram to give a stable representation of the speaker's pronunciation from a linear superposition of short-time spectrograms.[^o29]
- Researchers explore a novel approach to ECG signal analysis by leveraging spectrogram techniques, possibly for enhanced visualization and understanding. The integration of MFCC for feature extraction suggests a cross-disciplinary application, borrowing methods from [audio processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal_processing) to extract relevant information from biomedical signals.[^o30]
- Accurate interpretation of temperature indicating paint (TIP) is of great importance in aviation and other industrial applications. 2D spectrogram of TIP can be used in temperature interpretation.[^o31]
- The spectrogram can be used to process the signal for the rate of change of the human thorax. By visualizing respiratory signals using a spectrogram, the researchers have proposed an approach to the classification of respiration states based on a neural network model.[^o32]
