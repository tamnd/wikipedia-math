# Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem** in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) states that the [ring of invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_invariants) of a finite group [acting](wikipedia://en/12781) on a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) if and only if the group is [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) by [pseudoreflections](wikipedia://en/20084924). In the case of [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of the complex [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) the theorem was first [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by () who gave a case-by-case proof. () soon afterwards gave a uniform proof. It has been extended to finite [linear groups](wikipedia://en/3983656) over an arbitrary [field](wikipedia://en/10603) in the non-modular case by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *V* be a [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) vector space over a field *K* and let *G* be a finite subgroup of the general linear group *GL*(*V*). An element *s* of *GL*(*V*) is called a **pseudoreflection** if it fixes a codimension 1 [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of *V* and is not the [identity transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_transformation) *I*, or equivalently, if the [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28linear_operator%29) Ker(*s* − *I*) has [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) one in *V*. Assume that the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of *G* is [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of *K* (the so-called non-modular case). Then the following properties are equivalent:[^o6]

- (A) The group *G* is generated by pseudoreflections.
- (B) The algebra of invariants *K*\[*V*\]<sup>*G*</sup> is a (free) [polynomial algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_algebra).
- (B') The algebra of invariants *K*\[*V*\]<sup>*G*</sup> is a [regular ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_ring).
- (C) The algebra *K*\[*V*\] is a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) over *K*\[*V*\]<sup>*G*</sup>.
- (C') The algebra *K*\[*V*\] is a [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) over *K*\[*V*\]<sup>*G*</sup>.

In the case when *K* is the field **C** of complex numbers, the first condition is usually stated as "*G* is a [complex reflection group](wikipedia://en/5381096)". Shephard and Todd derived a full classification of such groups.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Let *V* be one-dimensional. Then any finite group faithfully acting on *V* is a subgroup of the multiplicative group of the field *K*, and hence a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327). It follows that *G* consists of [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) of order dividing *n*, where *n* is its order, so *G* is generated by pseudoreflections. In this case, *K*\[*V*\] = *K*\[*x*\] is the polynomial ring in one variable and the algebra of invariants of *G* is the subalgebra generated by *x*<sup>*n*</sup>, hence it is a polynomial algebra.
- Let *V* = *K*<sup>*n*</sup> be the standard *n*-dimensional vector space and *G* be the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>*n*</sub> acting by permutations of the elements of the standard [basis](wikipedia://en/18420). The symmetric group is generated by [transpositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28mathematics%29) (*ij*), which act by reflections on *V*. On the other hand, by the main theorem of [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359), the algebra of invariants is the polynomial algebra generated by the [elementary symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_symmetric_function) *e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e<sub>n</sub>*.
- Let *V* = *K*<sup>2</sup> and *G* be the cyclic group of order 2 acting by ±*I*. In this case, *G* is not generated by pseudoreflections, since the nonidentity element *s* of *G* acts without fixed points, so that dim Ker(*s* − *I*) = 0. On the other hand, the algebra of invariants is the subalgebra of *K*\[*V*\] = *K*\[*x*, *y*\] generated by the homogeneous elements *x*<sup>2</sup>, *xy*, and *y*<sup>2</sup> of degree 2. This subalgebra is not a polynomial algebra because of the relation *x*<sup>2</sup>*y*<sup>2</sup> = (*xy*)<sup>2</sup>.

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

(Broer 2007) gave an extension of the Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem to positive characteristic.

There has been much work on the question of when a reductive algebraic group acting on a vector space has a polynomial ring of invariants. In the case when the algebraic group is simple all cases when the invariant ring is polynomial have been classified by (Schwarz 1978)

In general, the ring of invariants of a finite group acting linearly on a complex vector space is [Cohen-Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen-Macaulay), so it is a finite-rank free module over a polynomial subring.
