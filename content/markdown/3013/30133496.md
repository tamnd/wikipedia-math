# Hans Duistermaat

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Duistermaat"}

**Johannes Jisse (Hans) Duistermaat** ([The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague), December 20, 1942 – [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht), March 19, 2010) was a Dutch mathematician.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Duistermaat attended primary school in [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta), at the time the capital of the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies), where his family moved after the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). In 1957, a few years after the [Indonesian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch–Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference), they came back to the Netherlands and Duistermaat completed his high school studies in [Vlaardingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlaardingen).[^o6][^o7]

From 1959 to 1965 he studied mathematics at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University), and he obtained his PhD degree at the same institution in 1968, with a thesis on the mathematical structures of [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952) entitled "*Energy and Entropy as Real Morphisms for Addition and Order*". His original supervisor was the applied mathematician Günther K. Braun, who died one year before the thesis defense, so the official supervision was taken over by geometer [Hans Freudenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Freudenthal).[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12]

After a postdoctoral stay in [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) (1969–70), Duistermaat returned to the Netherlands in 1971 and became in 1972 full professor in [Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen). In 1974 he returned to Utrecht, where he was offered the chair of Freudenthal.

In 2004 he was awarded a special [academy professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_professor) by the [KNAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), allowing him to focus exclusively on research, even after his official retirement in 2007.[^o9][^o11][^o13][^o7] He died in March 2010, due to [non-Hodgkin lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hodgkin_lymphoma) and [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia).[^o14]

He was elected a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1982,[^o15] and of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 1993.[^o16] In 2007 he became a Knight of the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion).[^o17][^o11][^o18]

Apart from being an eminent mathematician, Duistermaat was also a [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) player. In a simultaneous match of 10 against [Anatoly Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Karpov) in 1977, Duistermaat was the only one who did not lose.[^o7][^o10][^o19]

## Research {#Research}

Duistermaat defined himself a [geometric analyst](wikipedia://en/4325474),[^o11] but his research covered many different areas in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), including [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), [Fourier integral operators](wikipedia://en/10138003), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems).

Duistermaat dropped thermodynamics after his PhD, due to dissents between mathematicians and physicists in Utrecht. Nevertheless, during his PhD research he read the work by [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010), and in particular encountered [contact transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_Transformation). These topics exerted an important influence for his future pioneering works in [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841),[^o9][^o11][^o13] most prominently during his collaboration with [Lars Hörmander](wikipedia://en/500958), when they developed the theory of [Fourier integral operators](wikipedia://en/10138003) and proved the [Propagation of singularities theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propagation_of_singularities_theorem).[^o21] This led him also to the work with [Victor Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636) on the link between spectra of [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) and periodic bicharacteristics.[^o22]

Duistermaat introduced the notion of monodromy in [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531)[^o11][^o12][^o10] as obstruction to the existence of global [action-angle coordinates](wikipedia://en/5435566),[^o23] later, together with Richard Cushman, he extended it to the [quantum systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_systems).[^o24]

In symplectic geometry he is well known[^o12][^o18][^o10] for his article with his PhD student Gert Heckman on the [Duistermaat–Heckman formula](wikipedia://en/18916965),[^o25] which will later be placed in the more general framework of [equivariant cohomology](wikipedia://en/2976342),[^o26] independently by [Berline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Berline) and [Vergne](wikipedia://en/40970117)[^o27] and by [Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Bott](wikipedia://en/337457).[^o28] Other contributions in this field include a generalisation of the Morse index theorem[^o29] and a contribution to the problem of [quantisation commutes with reduction](wikipedia://en/44977826).[^o30]

In his book on [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), together with his PhD student Johan Kolk,[^o31] he provided an alternative proof of the [third Lie theorem](wikipedia://en/10875756), which will turn out to be crucial for proving the analogous theorem for [Lie groupoids](wikipedia://en/310923) and for its applications to [Poisson geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_geometry).[^o9][^o11][^o10]

His work with Alberto Grünbaum on the bispectral problem[^o32] was influential for integrable systems and noncommutative algebraic geometry.[^o10] In the last stages of his life he became interested in algebraic geometry[^o11][^o18] and wrote a book on QRT maps and [elliptic surfaces](wikipedia://en/3073462).[^o33]

Duistermaat contributed also to [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).[^o9][^o11][^o13] He was a consultant to [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch-Shell), which led to the thesis of Christiaan Stolk on the [inversion of seismic data](wikipedia://en/21495178).[^o34] He also worked on [barrier functions](wikipedia://en/3382647) in [convex programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_programming),[^o35] collaborated with biomedical technologists on [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision)[^o36] and with [geophysicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics) on modeling the [polarity reversals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarity_reversal) of the [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field).[^o37]

He supervised 25 PhD students.[^o8]
