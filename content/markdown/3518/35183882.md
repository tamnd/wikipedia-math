# Projective bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Projective bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **projective bundle** is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) whose fibers are [projective spaces](wikipedia://en/242135).

:::

By definition, a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* over a [Noetherian scheme](wikipedia://en/2783891) *S* is a **P**<sup>*n*</sup>-bundle if it is locally a projective *n*-space; i.e., $X \times_S U \simeq \mathbb{P}^n_U$ and transition automorphisms are linear. Over a [regular scheme](wikipedia://en/43016199) *S* such as a [smooth variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety), every projective bundle is of the form $\mathbb{P}(E)$ for some vector bundle ([locally free sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaf)) *E*.[^o2]

## The projective bundle of a vector bundle {#The_projective_bundle_of_a_vector_bundle}

Every [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over a [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *X* gives a projective bundle by taking the projective spaces of the fibers, but not all projective bundles arise in this way: there is an [obstruction](wikipedia://en/2179639) in the [cohomology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) *H*<sup>2</sup>(*X*,O\*). To see why, recall that a projective bundle comes equipped with transition functions on double intersections of a suitable open cover. On triple overlaps, any lift of these transition functions satisfies the cocycle condition up to an invertible function. The collection of these functions forms a 2-cocycle which vanishes in *H*<sup>2</sup>(*X*,O\*) only if the projective bundle is the projectivization of a vector bundle. In particular, if *X* is a compact [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) then *H*<sup>2</sup>(*X*,O\*)=0, and so this obstruction vanishes.

The projective bundle of a vector bundle *E* is the same thing as the [Grassmann bundle](wikipedia://en/50406093) $G_1(E)$ of 1-planes in *E*.

The projective bundle **P**(*E*) of a vector bundle *E* is characterized by the universal property that says:[^o4]

Given a morphism *f*: *T* → *X*, to factorize *f* through the projection map *p*: **P**(*E*) → *X* is to specify a line subbundle of *f*<sup>\*</sup>*E*.

For example, taking *f* to be *p*, one gets the line subbundle *O*(-1) of *p*<sup>\*</sup>*E*, called the [tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tautological_line_bundle) on **P**(*E*). Moreover, this *O*(-1) is a [universal bundle](wikipedia://en/2179570) in the sense that when a line bundle *L* gives a factorization *f* = *p* ∘ *g*, *L* is the pullback of *O*(-1) along *g*. See also [Cone#*O*(1)](wikipedia://en/50394182#O.281.29) for a more explicit construction of *O*(-1).

On **P**(*E*), there is a natural [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) (called the tautological exact sequence):

$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(E)}(-1) \to p^* E \to Q \to 0$$

where *Q* is called the tautological quotient-bundle.

Let *E* ⊂ *F* be vector bundles (locally free sheaves of finite rank) on *X* and *G* = *F*/*E*. Let *q*: **P**(*F*) → *X* be the projection. Then the natural map *O*(-1) → *q*<sup>\*</sup>*F* → *q*<sup>\*</sup>*G* is a global section of the [sheaf hom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_hom) Hom(*O*(-1), *q*<sup>\*</sup>G) = *q*<sup>\*</sup> *G* ⊗ *O*(1). Moreover, this natural map vanishes at a point exactly when the point is a line in *E*; in other words, the zero-locus of this section is **P**(*E*).

A particularly useful instance of this construction is when *F* is the direct sum *E* ⊕ 1 of *E* and the trivial line bundle (i.e., the structure sheaf). Then **P**(*E*) is a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) in **P**(*E* ⊕ 1), called the hyperplane at infinity, and the complement of **P**(*E*) can be identified with *E*. In this way, **P**(*E* ⊕ 1) is referred to as the projective completion (or "compactification") of *E*.

The projective bundle **P**(*E*) is stable under twisting *E* by a line bundle; precisely, given a line bundle *L*, there is the natural isomorphism:

$$g: \mathbf{P}(E) \overset{\sim}\to \mathbf{P}(E \otimes L)$$

such that $g^*(\mathcal{O}(-1)) \simeq \mathcal{O}(-1) \otimes p^* L.$[^o5] (In fact, one gets *g* by the universal property applied to the line bundle on the right.)

## Examples {#Examples}

Many non-trivial examples of projective bundles can be found using fibrations over $\mathbb{P}^1$ such as [Lefschetz fibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_fibrations). For example, an elliptic [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116) $X$ is a K3 surface with a fibration

> $$\pi:X \to \mathbb{P}^1$$

such that the fibers $E_p$ for $p \in \mathbb{P}^1$ are generically elliptic curves. Because every elliptic curve is a genus 1 curve with a distinguished point, there exists a global section of the fibration. Because of this global section, there exists a model of $X$ giving a morphism to the projective bundle[^o8]

> $$X \to \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(4)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(6)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1})$$

defined by the [Weierstrass equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_equation)

> $$y^2z + a_1xyz + a_3yz^2 = x^3 + a_2x^2z + a_4xz^2 + a_6z^3$$

where $x,y,z$ represent the local coordinates of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(4), \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(6), \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}$, respectively, and the coefficients

> $$a_i \in H^0(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(2i))$$

are sections of sheaves on $\mathbb{P}^1$. Note this equation is well-defined because each term in the Weierstrass equation has total degree $12$ (meaning the degree of the coefficient plus the degree of the monomial. For example, $\text{deg}(a_1xyz) = 2 + (4 + 6 + 0) = 12$).

The [contact bundles](wikipedia://en/81359548), and more generally [Grassmann bundles](wikipedia://en/50406093), are projective bundles. The [unit tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/7932644) of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) is a [double cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_cover_%28topology%29) of its projectivized [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794).

## Cohomology ring and Chow group {#Cohomology_ring_and_Chow_group}

Let *X* be a complex smooth [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) and *E* a complex vector bundle of rank *r* on it. Let *p*: **P**(*E*) → *X* be the projective bundle of *E*. Then the [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) H<sup>\*</sup>(**P**(*E*)) is an [algebra over](wikipedia://en/2112) H<sup>\*</sup>(*X*) through the pullback *p*<sup>\*</sup>. Then the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) ζ = *c*<sub>1</sub>(*O*(1)) generates H<sup>\*</sup>(**P**(*E*)) with the relation

$$\zeta^r + c_1(E) \zeta^{r-1} + \cdots + c_r(E) = 0$$

where *c*<sub>*i*</sub>(*E*) is the *i*-th Chern class of *E*. One interesting feature of this description is that one can *define* Chern classes as the coefficients in the relation; this is the approach taken by Grothendieck.

Over fields other than the complex field, the same description remains true with [Chow ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_ring) in place of cohomology ring (still assuming *X* is smooth). In particular, for Chow groups, there is the direct sum decomposition

$$A_k(\mathbf{P}(E)) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1} \zeta^i A_{k-r+1+i}(X).$$

As it turned out, this decomposition remains valid even if *X* is not smooth nor projective.[^o10] In contrast, *A*<sub>*k*</sub>(*E*) = *A*<sub>*k*-*r*</sub>(*X*), via the [Gysin homomorphism](wikipedia://en/7095671), morally because that the fibers of *E*, the vector spaces, are contractible.
