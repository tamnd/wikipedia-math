# Friedrich Schur

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Friedrich Schur"}

*Image: Friedrich Schur.png.* Friedrich Schur

**Friedrich Heinrich Schur** (27 January 1856, Maciejewo, [Krotoschin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krotoschin), [Province of Posen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Posen) – 18 March 1932, [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who studied [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Schur's family was originally [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish), but converted to Protestantism. His father owned an estate. He attended high school in [Krotoschin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krotoschin) and in 1875 studied at [University of Wroclaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wroclaw) astronomy and mathematics under [Heinrich Schröter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Schröter) and [Jacob Rosanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Rosanes). He then went to the [Berlin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_University), where he studied under [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), [Ernst Eduard Kummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Eduard_Kummer), [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) and [Gustav Kirchhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Kirchhoff) and received his doctorate in 1879 from Kummer: *Geometrische Untersuchungen über Strahlenkomplexe ersten und zweiten Grades*. In 1880, he passed the exam and the same year qualified as a teacher at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig). After that, he was an assistant professor and in 1884 became an assistant to [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig). In 1885 he was an associate professor there in 1888 and professor at the [University of Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tartu). In 1892, he was a professor of [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891) at the [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University) and in 1897 was a professor at the [University of Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Karlsruhe), where he was also rector in 1904/1905. In 1909, he became a professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg). After the loss of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), he was sacked by the French in 1919 and became a professor in Breslau, where he retired in 1924.

Friedrich Schur studied [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), transformation groups ([Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945)) after [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010). Many of his results, which he summarized in his book *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (Foundation of Geometry) of 1909, can also be found in the work of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) without reference to Schur. He also wrote a textbook of analytical geometry (1898) and the graphical statics (1915).

In 1912, he received the [Lobachevsky Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky_Prize) for his book *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, a Russian prize. In 1910, he was chairman of the German Mathematical Society. He holds honorary doctorates from the University of Karlsruhe. In 1927, he was selected as a corresponding member of the [Bavarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences).

Among his students was [Theodor Molien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Molien) and Julius Wellstein.
