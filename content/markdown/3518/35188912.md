# Sphinx tiling

*Image: Self-replication of sphynx hexidiamonds.svg.* Four 'sphinx' hexiamonds can be put together to form another sphinx.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphinx tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **sphinx tiling** is a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of the plane using the "sphinx", a [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/20741014) [hexiamond](wikipedia://en/185271) formed by gluing six [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) together. The resultant shape is named for its reminiscence to the [Great Sphinx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Sphinx_of_Giza) at [Giza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giza). A sphinx can be [dissected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) into any square number of copies of itself,[^o2] some of them [mirror images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image), and repeating this process leads to a [non-periodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145) of the plane. The sphinx is therefore a [rep-tile](wikipedia://en/33478101) (a [self-replicating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-replication) tessellation).[^o3] It is one of few known [pentagonal rep-tiles](wikipedia://en/33478101#Pentagonal_rep-tiles) and is the only known pentagonal rep-tile whose sub-copies are equal in size.[^o4]

:::

## General tilings {#General_tilings}

An outer boundary ("frame") in the shape of a sphinx can also be tiled in a non-recursive way for all orders. We define the order of a sphinx frame on a triangular lattice by the number of triangles at the "tail" end. An order-2 frame can be tiled by four sphinxes in exactly one way (as shown in the figure), an order-3 frame can be tiled by 9 sphinxes in 4 ways, etc. The number of tilings grows exponentially as $e^{c n^2}$ with the order $n$ of the frame, where $c \approx 0.425$[^o6]
