# Section formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Section formula"}

In [coordinate geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_geometry), the **Section formula** is a formula used to find the ratio in which a line segment is divided by a point internally or externally.[^o2] It is used to find out the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926), [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) and [excenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excenter) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), it is used to find the [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) of systems, [equilibrium points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_point), etc.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Internal divisions {#Internal_divisions}

*Image: Internal division.png.* Internal division with section formula

If point P (lying on AB) divides the line segment AB joining the points $\mathrm{A}(x_1,y_1)$ and $\mathrm{B}(x_2,y_2)$ in the ratio m:n, then

$P = \left(\frac{mx_2 + nx_1}{m + n},\frac{my_2 + ny_1}{m + n}\right)$[^o8]

The ratio m:n can also be written as $m/n:1$, or $k:1$, where $k=m/n$. So, the coordinates of point $P$ dividing the line segment joining the points $\mathrm{A}(x_1,y_1)$ and $\mathrm{B}(x_2,y_2)$ are:

$$\left(\frac{mx_2 + nx_1}{m + n}, \frac{my_2 + ny_1}{m + n}\right)$$

$$=\left(\frac{\frac{m}{n}x_2 +x_1}{\frac{m}{n}+1},\frac{\frac{m}{n}y_2 +y_1}{\frac{m}{n}+1} \right )$$

$=\left ( \frac{kx_2 +x_1}{k +1},\frac{ky_2 + y_1}{k +1} \right )$[^o5][^o6]

Similarly, the ratio can also be written as $k:(1-k)$, and the coordinates of P are $((1-k)x_1 + kx_2, (1-k)y_1 + ky_2)$.[^o2]

::: {#o10 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Triangles $PAQ\sim BPC$.

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{AP}{BP}=\frac{AQ}{CP}=\frac{PQ}{BC}\\ \frac{m}{n}=\frac{x-x_1}{x_2-x}=\frac{y-y_1}{y_2-y}\\ mx_2-mx=nx-nx_1,my_2-my=ny-ny_1\\ mx+nx=mx_2+nx_1, my+ny=my_2+ny_1\\ (m+n)x=mx_2+nx_1, (m+n)y=my_2+ny_1\\ x=\frac{mx_2 + nx_1}{m + n}, y=\frac{my_2 + ny_1}{m + n}\\ \end{aligned}$$

:::

## External divisions {#External_divisions}

*Image: External division.png.* External division with section formula

If a point P (lying on the extension of AB) divides AB in the ratio m:n then

$P = \left(\dfrac{mx_2 - nx_1}{m - n}, \dfrac{my_2 - ny_1}{m - n}\right)$[^o8]

::: {#o13 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Triangles $PAC\sim PBD$ (Let C and D be two points where A & P and B & P intersect respectively). Therefore ∠ACP = ∠BDP

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{AB}{BP}=\frac{AC}{BD}=\frac{PC}{PD}\\ \frac{m}{n}=\frac{x-x_1}{x-x_2}=\frac{y-y_1}{y-y_2}\\ mx-mx_2=nx-nx_1,my-my_2=ny-ny_1\\ mx-nx=mx_2-nx_1, my-ny=my_2-ny_1\\ (m-n)x=mx_2-nx_1, (m-n)y=my_2-ny_1\\ x=\frac{mx_2 - nx_1}{m - n}, y=\frac{my_2 - ny_1}{m - n}\\ \end{aligned}$$

:::

::: {#o15 .statement cue=named-heading name="Midpoint formula"}

## Midpoint formula {#Midpoint_formula}

The midpoint of a line segment divides it internally in the ratio $1:1$. Applying the Section formula for internal division:[^o5][^o6]

$$P = \left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$

### Derivation {#Derivation}

$$P = \left(\dfrac{mx_2 + nx_1}{m + n}, \dfrac{my_2 + ny_1}{m + n}\right)$$

$$= \left ( \frac{1\cdot x_1 + 1\cdot x_2}{1+1},\frac{1 \cdot y_1 + 1\cdot y_2}{1+1} \right )$$

$$=\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)$$

:::

## Centroid {#Centroid}

*Image: Centroid.svg.* Centroid of a triangle

The centroid of a triangle is the intersection of the [medians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medians_of_a_triangle) and divides each median in the ratio $2:1$. Let the vertices of the triangle be $A(x_1, y_1)$, $B(x_2, y_2)$ and $C(x_3, y_3)$. So, a median from point A will intersect BC at $\left(\frac{x_2 + x_3}{2}, \frac{y_2 + y_3}{2}\right)$. Using the section formula, the centroid becomes:

$$\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3},\frac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3} \right)$$

## In three dimensions {#In_three_dimensions}

Let A and B be two points with [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) *(x<sub>1</sub>, y<sub>1</sub>, z<sub>1</sub>)* and *(x<sub>2</sub>, y<sub>2</sub>, z<sub>2</sub>)* and P be a point on the line through A and B. If $AP:PB =m:n$. Then the section formula gives the coordinates of P as

$\left ( \frac{mx_2 + nx_1}{m +n} ,\frac{my_2 + ny_1}{m+n}, \frac{mz_2 + nz_1}{m+n} \right )$[^o2]

If, instead, P is a point on the line such that $AP:PB = k:1-k$, its coordinates are $((1-k)x_1 + kx_2, (1-k)y_1 + ky_2, (1-k)z_1 + kz_2)$.[^o2]

## In vectors {#In_vectors}

The position vector of a point P dividing the line segment joining the points A and B whose position vectors are $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$

1. in the ratio $m:n$ internally, is given by $\frac{n\vec{a} + m\vec{b}}{m+n}$[^o20][^o2]
1. in the ratio $m:n$ externally, is given by $\frac{m\vec{b} - n\vec{a}}{m-n}$[^o20]
