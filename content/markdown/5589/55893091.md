# Muriel Nissel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Muriel Nissel"}

**Muriel Nissel** (née Griffiths; 30 January 1921 – 2010) was a British statistician and civil servant. Together with [Claus Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_Moser), she created "a national survey analysing trends in social welfare", that was to become *[Social Trends](wikipedia://en/16816239)*, first published in 1970, and considered to be the "statistician's bible", before working on the "distribution and [redistribution of wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistribution_of_wealth)". Nissel also wrote well-regarded books, including *People Count – a history of the General Register Office*, and *Married to the Amadeus: Life with a String Quartet*.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

She was born Muriel Griffiths on 30 January 1921 at 38 Perham Road, [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham), London, the only child of Evan Griffiths, a civil servant who worked for the [General Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Post_Office), and his wife, Bessie May Griffiths, née Phillips, a [governess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governess).[^o4]

She grew up in [Potters Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potters_Bar), Middlesex.[^o4] She was educated at [Queenswood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queenswood_School), in [Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield,_Hertfordshire), having won a boarding scholarship, followed by [St Hugh's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Hugh's_College,_Oxford), where she earned a first-class degree in [philosophy, politics, and economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy,_politics,_and_economics) in 1942.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

After Oxford, she joined the [Ministry of Fuel and Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Fuel_and_Power) in 1942, working as an economic statistician for [Harold Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson) the future Prime Minister.[^o4]

In 1964 Nissel joined the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) as a research assistant, working there with Professor [Claus Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_Moser), who became her mentor.[^o6] Later in 1964, Nissel also joined the government's [Central Statistical Office](wikipedia://en/3597408) (CSO) as a financial statistician.[^o4][^o6]

In 1967 she and Moser started work on "a national survey analysing trends in social welfare", that was to become *[Social Trends](wikipedia://en/16816239)*, first published in 1970, and considered to be the "statistician's bible".[^o4][^o6] She edited *Social Trends* until 1975, when she began work on the "distribution and redistribution of wealth".[^o6]

Nissel also wrote well-regarded books, including *People Count – a history of the General Register Office*, and *Married to the Amadeus: Life with a String Quartet*.[^o6]

## Publications {#Publications}

- *People Count: A History of the General Register Office*, HMSO, 1987.
- *Married to the Amadeus: Life with a String Quartet*, , Giles de la Mare Publishers Limited

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

On 26 January 1946, she married William Hans Barbour (1913–2000), and they later divorced.[^o4] On 5 April 1957, having changed her name to Muriel Morley, she married [Siegmund Walter "Sigi" Nissel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegmund_Nissel) (1922–2008), the violinist and member of the [Amadeus Quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadeus_Quartet).[^o4] They had two children, Claire (born 1958) and Daniel (born 1962).[^o4]
