# Dorota Dabrowska

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dorota Dabrowska"}

**Dorota Maria Dabrowska** is a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) statistician known for applying [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369) and [semiparametric models](wikipedia://en/4415208) to [counting processes](wikipedia://en/20814853) and [survival analysis](wikipedia://en/419259). Dabrowska's estimator, from her paper "Kaplan–Meier estimate on the plane" (*[Annals of Statistics](wikipedia://en/22160768)*, 1988) is a widely used tool for bivariate survival under random [censoring](wikipedia://en/11548952).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Dąbrowska earned a master's degree in mathematics from the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw). She completed her Ph.D. in statistics in 1984 at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Her dissertation, supervised by Kjell Doksum, was *Rank Tests for Independence for Bivariate Censored Data*.

## Career {#Career}

After completing her doctorate, she joined the faculty at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), where she is a professor of [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) and statistics. At UCLA, she made fundamental contributions to the estimation and asymptotic theory in semi-Markov and Markov renewal models.

As well as being a researcher in statistics, Dabrowska is also one of the translators of an influential 1923 paper on [randomized experiments](wikipedia://en/6033300) by [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106), originally written in [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language).

Dabrowska is a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).
