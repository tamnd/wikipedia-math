# Gosset–Elte figures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gosset–Elte figures"}

*Image: E8Petrie.svg.* The [*4*<sub>*21*</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236362) polytope of 8-space

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Gosset–Elte figures**, named by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) after [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) and [E. L. Elte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._L._Elte), are a group of [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) which are not [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polytopes), generated by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) with mirrors all related by order-2 and order-3 dihedral angles. They can be seen as *one-end-ringed* [Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/5358554#Finite_Coxeter_groups).

:::

The **Coxeter symbol** for these figures has the form ***k*<sub>*i,j*</sub>**, where each letter represents a length of order-3 branches on a Coxeter–Dynkin diagram with a single ring on the end node of a *k*-length sequence of branches. The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of *k*<sub>*i,j*</sub> is (*k* − 1)<sub>*i,j*</sub>, and each of its facets are represented by subtracting one from one of the nonzero subscripts, i.e. *k*<sub>*i* − 1*,j*</sub> and *k*<sub>*i,j* − 1</sub>.[^o2]

Rectified [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) are included in the list as limiting cases with *k* = 0. Similarly *0*<sub>*i,j,k*</sub> represents a bifurcated graph with a central node ringed.

## History {#History}

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) named these figures as *k*<sub>*i,j*</sub> (or *k*<sub>*ij*</sub>) in shorthand and gave credit of their discovery to Gosset and Elte:[^o4]

- [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) first published a list of *regular and semi-regular figures in space of*n*dimensions*[^o5] in 1900, enumerating polytopes with one or more types of [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) faces. This included the [rectified 5-cell](wikipedia://en/2720430) *0*<sub>*21*</sub> in 4-space, [demipenteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demipenteract) *1*<sub>*21*</sub> in 5-space, [*2*<sub>*21*</sub>](wikipedia://en/19232473) in 6-space, [*3*<sub>*21*</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236353) in 7-space, [*4*<sub>*21*</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236362) in 8-space, and [*5*<sub>*21*</sub>](wikipedia://en/26842937) infinite tessellation in 8-space.
- [E. L. Elte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._L._Elte) independently enumerated a different semiregular list in his 1912 book, *The Semiregular Polytopes of the Hyperspaces*.[^o6] He called them *semiregular polytopes of the first kind*, limiting his search to one or two types of regular or semiregular *k*-faces.

Elte's enumeration included all the *k*<sub>*ij*</sub> polytopes except for the [*1*<sub>*42*</sub>](wikipedia://en/19091290) which has 3 types of 6-faces.

The set of figures extend into honeycombs of (2,2,2), (3,3,1), and (5,4,1) families in 6,7,8 dimensional Euclidean spaces respectively. Gosset's list included the *5*<sub>*21*</sub> honeycomb as the only semiregular one in his definition.

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Simply Laced Dynkin Diagrams.svg.* Simply-laced ADE groups

The polytopes and honeycombs in this family can be seen within [ADE classification](wikipedia://en/648042).

A finite polytope *k*<sub>*ij*</sub> exists if

$$\frac{1}{i+1}+\frac{1}{j+1}+\frac{1}{k+1}>1$$

or equal for Euclidean honeycombs, and less for hyperbolic honeycombs.

The [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) **\[3<sup>i,j,k</sup>\]** can generate up to 3 unique uniform **Gosset–Elte figures** with [Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/5358554) with one end node ringed. By [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter)'s notation, each figure is represented by ***k<sub>ij</sub>*** to mean the end-node on the *k*-length sequence is ringed.

The [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) family can be seen as a limiting case with *k* = 0, and all [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) (single-ring) Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams.

:::
