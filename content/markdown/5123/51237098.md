# Mean square

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mean square"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and its applications, the **mean square** is normally defined as the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) of the [squares](wikipedia://en/659942) of a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of numbers or of a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685).[^o2]

:::

It may also be defined as the arithmetic mean of the squares of the *[deviations](wikipedia://en/9252911)* between a set of numbers and a reference value (e.g., may be a [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) or an [assumed mean](wikipedia://en/24783293) of the data),[^o3] in which case it may be known as *[mean square deviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_square_deviation)*. When the reference value is the assumed [true value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_value), the result is known as *[mean squared error](wikipedia://en/201816)*.

A typical estimate for the [sample variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_variance) from a set of sample values $x_i$ uses a divisor of the number of values minus one, *n-1*, rather than *n* as in a simple [quadratic mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_mean), and this is still called the "mean square" (e.g. in [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634)):

$$s^2=\textstyle\frac{1}{n-1}\sum(x_i-\bar{x})^2$$

The second [moment](wikipedia://en/368684) of a random variable, $E(X^{2})$ is also called the **mean square**. The [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of a mean square is known as the [root mean square](wikipedia://en/66819) (RMS or rms), and can be used as an estimate of the [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590) of a random variable when the random variable is zero-mean.
