# Homomorphic equivalence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Homomorphic equivalence"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), two graphs *G* and *H* are called **homomorphically equivalent** if there exists a [graph homomorphism](wikipedia://en/676328) $f\colon G \to H$ and a graph homomorphism $g\colon H \to G$. An example usage of this notion is that any two [cores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_%28graph_theory%29) of a graph are homomorphically equivalent.

:::

Homomorphic equivalence also comes up in the theory of [databases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database). Given a [database schema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database_schema), two [instances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instance_%28database%29) *I* and *J* on it are called homomorphically equivalent if there exists an instance homomorphism $f\colon I\to J$ and an instance homomorphism $g\colon J\to I$.

Deciding whether two graphs are homomorphically equivalent is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete).[^o2]

In fact for any [category](wikipedia://en/53759) *C*, one can define homomorphic equivalence. It is used in the theory of [accessible categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessible_categories), where "weak universality" is the best one can hope for in terms of injectivity classes; see [^o3]
