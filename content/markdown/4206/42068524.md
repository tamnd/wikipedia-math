# Bretherton equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bretherton equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Bretherton equation** is a [nonlinear partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/16678376) introduced by [Francis Bretherton](wikipedia://en/48499421) in 1964:[^o2]

:::

$$u_{tt}+u_{xx}+u_{xxxx}+u = u^p,$$

with $p$ [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) and $p \ge 2.$ While $u_t, u_x$ and $u_{xx}$ denote [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) of the [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879) $u(x,t).$

The original equation studied by Bretherton has [quadratic](wikipedia://en/187240) nonlinearity, $p=2.$ [Nayfeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_H._Nayfeh) treats the case $p=3$ with two different methods: [Whitham's](wikipedia://en/22078924) [averaged Lagrangian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Averaged_Lagrangian) method and the [method of multiple scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_multiple_scales).[^o3]

The Bretherton equation is a model equation for studying weakly-nonlinear [wave dispersion](wikipedia://en/1744650). It has been used to study the interaction of [harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic) by [nonlinear resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_resonance).[^o4][^o5] Bretherton obtained analytic solutions in terms of [Jacobi elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobi_elliptic_function).[^o2][^o6]

## Variational formulations {#Variational_formulations}

The Bretherton equation derives from the [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) density:[^o8]

$$\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 \left( u_t \right)^2 + \tfrac12 \left( u_x \right)^2 -\tfrac12 \left( u_{xx} \right)^2 - \tfrac12 u^2 + \tfrac{1}{p+1} u^{p+1}$$

through the [Euler–Lagrange equation](wikipedia://en/294995):

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left( \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial u_t} \right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial u_x} \right) - \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} \left( \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial u_{xx}} \right) - \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial u} = 0.$$

The equation can also be formulated as a [Hamiltonian system](wikipedia://en/1197531):[^o9]

$$\begin{aligned} u_t & - \frac{\delta{H}}{\delta v} = 0, \\ v_t & + \frac{\delta{H}}{\delta u} = 0, \end{aligned}$$

in terms of [functional derivatives](wikipedia://en/301504) involving the Hamiltonian $H:$

$H(u,v) = \int \mathcal{H}(u,v;x,t)\; \mathrm{d}x$ 2em and 2em $\mathcal{H}(u,v;x,t) = \tfrac12 v^2 - \tfrac12 \left( u_x \right)^2 +\tfrac12 \left( u_{xx} \right)^2 + \tfrac12 u^2 - \tfrac{1}{p+1} u^{p+1}$

with $\mathcal{H}$ the Hamiltonian density – consequently $v=u_t.$ The Hamiltonian $H$ is the total energy of the system, and is [conserved](wikipedia://en/6956) over time.[^o9][^o10]
