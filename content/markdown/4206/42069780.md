# Affine monoid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine monoid"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **affine monoid** is a [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) that is finitely generated, and is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a submonoid of a [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) $\mathbb{Z}^d, d \ge 0$.[^o2] Affine monoids are closely connected to [convex polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedra), and their associated [algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) are of much use in the algebraic study of these geometric objects.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

- Affine monoids are **finitely generated**. This means for a monoid $M$, there exists $m_1, \dots , m_n \in M$ such that

$$M = m_1\mathbb{Z_+}+\dots + m_n\mathbb{Z_+}.$$

- Affine monoids are **[cancellative](wikipedia://en/490646)**. In other words,

$x + y = x + z$ implies that $y = z$ for all $x,y,z \in M$, where $+$ denotes the [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) on the affine monoid $M$.

- Affine monoids are also **[torsion free](wikipedia://en/11630973)**. For an affine monoid $M$, $nx = ny$ implies that $x = y$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and $x, y \in M$.

### Properties and examples {#Properties_and_examples}

- Every submonoid of $\mathbb{Z}$ is finitely generated. Hence, *every* submonoid of $\mathbb{Z}$ is affine.
- The submonoid $\{(x,y)\in \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z} \mid y > 0\} \cup \{(0,0)\}$ of $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ is *not* finitely generated, and therefore *not* affine.
- The [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of two affine monoids is an affine monoid.

:::

## Affine monoids {#Affine_monoids}

### Group of differences {#Group_of_differences}

If $M$ is an affine monoid, it can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) into a [group](wikipedia://en/19447). More specifically, there is a unique group $gp(M)$, called *the group of differences*, in which $M$ can be embedded.

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

#### Definition {#Definition}

- $gp(M)$ can be viewed as the set of equivalences classes $x - y$, where $x - y = u - v$ if and only if $x + v + z = u + y + z$, for $z \in M$, and

$(x-y) + (u-v) = (x+u) - (y+v)$ defines the addition.[^o2]

- The **rank** of an affine monoid $M$ is the [rank of a group](wikipedia://en/18720088) of $gp(M)$.[^o2]
- If an affine monoid $M$ is given as a submonoid of $\mathbb{Z}^r$, then $gp(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}M$, where $\mathbb{Z}M$ is the subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}^r$.[^o2]

:::

#### Universal property {#Universal_property}

- If $M$ is an affine monoid, then the monoid [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) $\iota : M \to gp(M)$ defined by $\iota(x) = x + 0$ satisfies the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245):

for any monoid homomorphism $\varphi: M \to G$, where $G$ is a group, there is a unique group homomorphism $\psi : gp(M) \to G$, such that $\varphi = \psi \circ \iota$, and since affine monoids are cancellative, it follows that $\iota$ is an embedding. In other words, *every* affine monoid can be embedded into a group.

### Normal affine monoids {#Normal_affine_monoids}

::: {#o13 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

#### Definition {#Definition}

- If $M$ is a submonoid of an affine monoid $N$, then the submonoid

$$\hat{M}_N = \{x\in N \mid mx \in M, m \in \mathbb{N}\}$$

is the **integral closure** of $M$ in $N$. If $M = \hat{M_N}$, then $M$ is **integrally closed**.

- The **normalization** of an affine monoid $M$ is the integral closure of $M$ in $gp(M)$. If the normalization of $M$, is $M$ itself, then $M$ is a **normal** affine monoid.[^o2]
- A monoid $M$ is a normal affine monoid if and only if $\mathbb{R}_+M$ is finitely generated and $M = \mathbb{Z}^r \cap \mathbb{R}_+M$ .

:::

## Affine monoid rings {#Affine_monoid_rings}

Let $M$ be an affine monoid, and $R$ a commutative [ring](wikipedia://en/48404). Then one can form the **affine monoid ring** $R[M]$. This is an $R$-module with a free basis $M$, so if $f \in R[M]$, then

$f = \sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}x_i$, where $f_i \in R, x_i \in M$, and $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

In other words, $R[M]$ is the set of finite sums of elements of $M$ with coefficients in $R$.

$R[M]$ is a [domain](wikipedia://en/487627) since, for some $d \geq 0$, it embeds in $R[\mathbb{Z}^d]$ which is a domain.

## Connection to [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/4165882) {#Connection_to_convex_geometry}

Affine monoids arise naturally from convex polyhedra, [convex cones](wikipedia://en/3558266), and their associated discrete structures.

- Let $C$ be a rational [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) in $\mathbb{R}^n$, and let $L$ be a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) in $\mathbb{Q}^n$. Then $C \cap L$ is an affine monoid.[^o2] (Lemma 2.9, Gordan's lemma)
- If $M$ is a submonoid of $\mathbb{R}^n$, then $\mathbb{R}_+M$ is a cone if and only if $M$ is an affine monoid.
- If $M$ is a submonoid of $\mathbb{R}^n$, and $C$ is a cone generated by the elements of $gp(M)$, then $M \cap C$ is an affine monoid.
- Let $P$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ be a rational polyhedron, $C$ the [recession cone](wikipedia://en/34978152) of $P$, and $L$ a lattice in $\mathbb{Q}^n$. Then $P \cap L$ is a finitely generated [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over the affine monoid $C \cap L$.[^o2] (Theorem 2.12)
