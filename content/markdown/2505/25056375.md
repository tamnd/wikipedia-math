# Contiguity (probability theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Contiguity"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), two sequences of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) are said to be **contiguous** if asymptotically they share the same [support](wikipedia://en/6591796). Thus the notion of **contiguity** extends the concept of [absolute continuity](wikipedia://en/252670) to the sequences of measures.

:::

The concept was originally introduced by (Le Cam 1960) as part of his foundational contribution to the development of [asymptotic theory](wikipedia://en/26024794) in mathematical [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). He is best known for the general concepts of [local asymptotic normality](wikipedia://en/27260158) and contiguity.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(\Omega_n,\mathcal{F}_n)$ be a sequence of [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333), each equipped with two measures *P<sub>n</sub>* and *Q<sub>n</sub>*.

- We say that *Q<sub>n</sub>* is **contiguous** with respect to *P<sub>n</sub>* (denoted *Q<sub>n</sub>* ◁ *P<sub>n</sub>*) if for every sequence *A<sub>n</sub>* of [measurable sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set), *P<sub>n</sub>*(*A<sub>n</sub>*) → 0 implies *Q<sub>n</sub>*(*A<sub>n</sub>*) → 0.
- The sequences *P<sub>n</sub>* and *Q<sub>n</sub>* are said to be **mutually contiguous** or **bi-contiguous** (denoted *Q<sub>n</sub>* ◁▷ *P<sub>n</sub>*) if both *Q<sub>n</sub>* is contiguous with respect to *P<sub>n</sub>* and *P<sub>n</sub>* is contiguous with respect to *Q<sub>n</sub>*.[^o6]

The notion of contiguity is closely related to that of [absolute continuity](wikipedia://en/252670#Absolute_continuity_of_measures). We say that a measure *Q* is *absolutely continuous* with respect to *P* (denoted *Q* ≪ *P*) if for any measurable set *A*, *P*(*A*) = 0 implies *Q*(*A*) = 0. That is, *Q* is absolutely continuous with respect to *P* if the [support](wikipedia://en/6591796) of *Q* is a subset of the support of *P*, except in cases where this is false, including, e.g., a measure that concentrates on an open set, because its support is a [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) and it assigns measure zero to the boundary, and so another measure may concentrate on the boundary and thus have support contained within the support of the first measure, but they will be mutually singular. In summary, this previous sentence's statement of absolute continuity is false. The *contiguity* property replaces this requirement with an asymptotic one: *Q<sub>n</sub>* is contiguous with respect to *P<sub>n</sub>* if the "limiting support" of *Q<sub>n</sub>* is a subset of the limiting support of *P<sub>n</sub>*. By the aforementioned logic, this statement is also false.

It is possible however that each of the measures *Q<sub>n</sub>* be absolutely continuous with respect to *P<sub>n</sub>*, while the sequence *Q<sub>n</sub>* not being contiguous with respect to *P<sub>n</sub>*.

The fundamental [Radon–Nikodym theorem](wikipedia://en/338746) for absolutely continuous measures states that if *Q* is absolutely continuous with respect to *P*, then *Q* has *density* with respect to *P*, denoted as *ƒ* = $\tfrac{dQ}{dP}$, such that for any measurable set *A*

$$Q(A) = \int_A f\,\mathrm{d}P, \,$$

which is interpreted as being able to "reconstruct" the measure *Q* from knowing the measure *P* and the derivative *ƒ*. A similar result exists for contiguous sequences of measures, and is given by the *Le Cam's third lemma*.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- For the case $(P_n,Q_n)= (P,Q)$ for all *n* it applies $Q_n\triangleleft P_n\Leftrightarrow Q\ll P$.
- It is possible that $P_n\ll Q_n$ is true for all *n* without $P_n\triangleleft Q_n$.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Le Cam's first lemma"}

## Le Cam's first lemma {#Le_Cam's_first_lemma}

For two sequences of measures $(P_n)\text{ and }(Q_n)$ on measurable spaces $(\Omega_n,\mathcal{F}_n)$ the following statements are equivalent:[^o12]

- $$P_n\triangleleft Q_n$$
- $$\frac{\mathrm dQ_n}{\mathrm dP_n}\overset{P_n}{\,\longrightarrow\,}U\text{ along a subsequence }\Rightarrow P(U>0)=1$$
- $$\frac{\mathrm dP_n}{\mathrm dQ_n}\overset{Q_n}{\,\longrightarrow\,}V\text{ along a subsequence }\Rightarrow E(V)=1$$
- $T_n\overset{P_n}{\,\longrightarrow\,}0\,\Rightarrow\, T_n\overset{Q_n}{\,\longrightarrow\,}0$ for any statistics $T_n:\Omega_n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$.

where $U$ and $V$ are random variables on $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ and $(\Omega',\mathcal{F}',Q)$.

### Interpretation {#Interpretation}

[Prohorov's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohorov's_theorem) tells us that given a sequence of probability measures, every [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098) has a further subsequence which converges [weakly](wikipedia://en/6811795#Weak_convergence_of_measures). Le Cam's first lemma shows that the properties of the associated limit points determine whether contiguity applies or not. This can be understood in analogy with the non-asymptotic notion of [absolute continuity of measures](wikipedia://en/252670#Absolute_continuity_of_measures).[^o14]
