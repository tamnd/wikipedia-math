# Torelli theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Torelli theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Torelli theorem**, named after [Ruggiero Torelli](wikipedia://en/51289873), is a classical result of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) over the [complex number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_number_field), stating that a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) projective [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) ([compact Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface)) *C* is determined by its [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) *J*(*C*), when the latter is given in the form of a [principally polarized abelian variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principally_polarized_abelian_variety). In other words, the [complex torus](wikipedia://en/3055042) *J*(*C*), with certain 'markings', is enough to recover *C*. The same statement holds over any [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018).[^o2] From more precise information on the constructed [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of the curves it follows that if the canonically principally polarized Jacobian varieties of curves of genus $\geq 2$ are *k*-isomorphic for *k* any [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088), so are the curves.[^o3]

:::

This result has had many important extensions. It can be recast to read that a certain natural [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891), the [period mapping](wikipedia://en/6010542), from the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of curves of a fixed [genus](wikipedia://en/61891), to a moduli space of [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties), is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) (on [geometric points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_point)). Generalizations are in two directions. Firstly, to geometric questions about that morphism, for example the [local Torelli theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Torelli_theorem). Secondly, to other period mappings. A case that has been investigated deeply is for [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116) (by [Viktor S. Kulikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_S._Kulikov), [Ilya Pyatetskii-Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Pyatetskii-Shapiro), [Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815) and [Fedor Bogomolov](wikipedia://en/8823369))[^o4] and [hyperkähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/646120) (by [Misha Verbitsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misha_Verbitsky), [Eyal Markman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyal_Markman) and [Daniel Huybrechts](wikipedia://en/48322758)).[^o5]
