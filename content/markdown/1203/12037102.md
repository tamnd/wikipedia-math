# Dowker space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dowker space"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), a **Dowker space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that is [T<sub>4</sub>](wikipedia://en/48629) but not [countably paracompact](wikipedia://en/48631). They are named after [Clifford Hugh Dowker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Hugh_Dowker).

:::

The non-trivial task of providing an example of a Dowker space (and therefore also proving their existence as mathematical objects) helped mathematicians better understand the nature and variety of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450).

## Equivalences {#Equivalences}

Dowker showed, in 1951, the following:

If *X* is a normal [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721) (that is, a [T<sub>4</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T4_space)), then the following are equivalent:

- *X* is a Dowker space
- The product of *X* with the unit interval is not normal.[^o3]
- *X* is not [countably metacompact](wikipedia://en/5074872).

Dowker conjectured that there were no Dowker spaces, and the conjecture was not resolved until [Mary Ellen Rudin](wikipedia://en/2976742) constructed one in 1971.[^o4] Rudin's counterexample is a very large space (of [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) $\aleph_\omega^{\aleph_0}$). [Zoltán Balogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoltán_Tibor_Balogh) gave the first [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) construction of a small (cardinality [continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901)) example,[^o5] which was more [well-behaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-behaved) than Rudin's. Using [PCF theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCF_theory), M. Kojman and [S. Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) constructed a subspace of Rudin's Dowker space of cardinality $\aleph_{\omega+1}$ that is also Dowker.[^o6]
