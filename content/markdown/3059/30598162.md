# Zorn ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zorn ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **Zorn ring** is an [alternative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_ring) in which for every non-[nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235) *x* there exists an element *y* such that *xy* is a non-zero [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent_element_%28ring_theory%29).[^n1] They are named after [Max August Zorn](wikipedia://en/19740), who studied a similar condition.[^n2][^n3]

:::

For [associative rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_rings), an equivalent definition is that the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) J(*R*) is a [nil ideal](wikipedia://en/3100140) and every right [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of *R* which is not contained in J(*R*) contains a nonzero idempotent. Replacing "right ideal" with "left ideal" yields an equivalent definition. Left or right [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273), left or right [perfect rings](wikipedia://en/22658615), [semiprimary rings](wikipedia://en/29435296) and [von Neumann regular rings](wikipedia://en/907524) are all examples of associative Zorn rings.
