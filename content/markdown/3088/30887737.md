# Jill Pipher

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jill Pipher"}

**Jill Catherine Pipher** (born December 14, 1955, in [Harrisburg, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg,_Pennsylvania)) is an American mathematician. She served as president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS, 2019–2020)[^o3] and president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM, 2011–2013). She was the first director of the [Institute for Computational and Experimental Research in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31112547) (ICERM, 2011–2016), an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation)-funded mathematics institute based in [Providence, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island).[^o4]

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Pipher's research areas include [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432). She has published more than 60 research articles and has coauthored with Jeffrey Hoffstein and Joseph Silverman a textbook titled *An Introduction to Mathematical Cryptography*.[^o6]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Pipher is currently the [Elisha Benjamin Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Andrews) Professor of Mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). She received a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1979 and a PhD from UCLA in 1985 under the direction of [John B. Garnett](wikipedia://en/21917650). She taught at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) (1985–1990) before taking a position at Brown in 1990, where she served as chair of the Mathematics Department from 2005 to 2008.

In 1996, Pipher, along with [Jeffrey Hoffstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Hoffstein), [Daniel Lieman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Lieman) and [Joseph Silverman](wikipedia://en/2288703), founded NTRU Cryptosystems, Inc., now part of Security Innovation, Inc. to market their cryptographic algorithms, [NTRUEncrypt](wikipedia://en/481582) and [NTRUSign](wikipedia://en/3513327).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2012 she became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o10] In 2017, she was selected as a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the inaugural class.[^o11] In 2019 she was named a [SIAM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Fellow) "for her profound contributions in analysis and partial differential equations, groundbreaking work in public key cryptography, and outstanding scientific leadership".[^o12] She was named to the 2021 class of Fellows of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o13]

In 2014 Pipher was a [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) Invited Lecturer at the [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143), speaking on *The Mathematics of Lattice-based Cryptography*[^o14] The [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) named her as their [Noether Lecturer](wikipedia://en/11566873) for 2018.[^o15]

In 2019–2020, she served as the 65th president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16] She was the third woman to be elected to this position, following [Julia Robinson](wikipedia://en/952987) (1983–1984) and [Cathleen Synge Morawetz](wikipedia://en/1254108) (1995–1996).
