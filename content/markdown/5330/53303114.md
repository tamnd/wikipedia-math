# Orthopole

*Image: Orthopole.svg.* solid green 2px solid #cc9900 2px dashed #cc9900 1px solid #00A6A6 2px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthopole"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **orthopole** of a system consisting of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) $\triangle ABC$ and a line $\ell$ in the same plane is a point determined as follows:[^o2]

:::

- Let $A'$, $B'$, $C'$ be the feet of perpendiculars dropped on $\ell$ from $A$, $B$, $C$ respectively.
- Let $A''$, $B''$, $C''$ be the feet of perpendiculars dropped from $A'$, $B'$, $C'$ to the sides opposite $A$, $B$, $C$ (respectively) or to those sides' extensions.
- Then the three lines $A'A''$, $B'B''$, $C'C''$, are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157).[^o3] The point at which they concur is the orthopole.

Due to their many properties,[^o4] orthopoles have been the subject of a large literature.[^o5] Some key topics are determination of the lines having a given orthopole[^o6] and orthopolar circles.[^o7]
