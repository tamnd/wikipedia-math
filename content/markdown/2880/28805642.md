# Morgan Ward

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morgan Ward"}

**Henry Morgan Ward**[^o3] (August 20, 1901 – June 26, 1963) was an American mathematician, a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Ward was born in New York City.[^o3][^o6] He studied at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), receiving his BA in 1924. He obtained his Ph.D. in mathematics from Caltech in 1928,[^o6] with a dissertation titled *The Foundations of General Arithmetic*; his advisor was [Eric Temple Bell](wikipedia://en/255236).[^o7] He became a research fellow at Caltech, and then in 1929 a member of the faculty; he remained at Caltech until his death in 1963.[^o8] Among his doctoral students was [Robert P. Dilworth](wikipedia://en/9581646), who also became a Caltech professor.[^o7] Ward is the [academic ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy) of over 500 mathematicians and computer scientists through Dilworth and another of his students, [Donald A. Darling](wikipedia://en/38298648).[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

Ward's research interests included the study of [recurrence relations](wikipedia://en/146806) and the [divisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility) properties of their solutions, [diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) including [Euler's sum of powers conjecture](wikipedia://en/9660) and equations between [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416), [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory) and [residuated lattices](wikipedia://en/2483542), [functional equations](wikipedia://en/364377) and [functional iteration](wikipedia://en/1448702), and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o10] He also worked with the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) on the reform of the elementary school mathematics curriculum,[^o8] and with Clarence Ethel Hardgrove[^o11] he wrote the textbook *Modern Elementary Mathematics* (Addison-Wesley, 1962).

Ward's works are collected in the Caltech library.[^o8] A [symposium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_conference) in his memory was held at Caltech on November 21–22, 1963.[^o10] *Ward [quasigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasigroups)* are named after him, following his paper on alternative set of [group](wikipedia://en/19447) [axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axioms).[^o12][^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Ward died of a heart attack in [Duarte, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte,_California).[^o6]
