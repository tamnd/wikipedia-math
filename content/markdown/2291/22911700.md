# Olaf Helmer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Olaf Helmer"}

**Olaf Helmer** (June 4, 1910 – April 14, 2011) was a German-American [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065) and futurologist. He was a researcher at the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation) from 1946 to 1968 and a co-founder of the [Institute for the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Future).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), Helmer studied mathematics and logic at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). He earned his doctorate there in 1934, under direction from philosopher [Hans Reichenbach](wikipedia://en/1546863). That year he moved to London where he began a second doctoral study, on [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095), under direction from [Susan Stebbing](wikipedia://en/3783415) at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London). [Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) himself was one of Helmer's examiners.

Helmer moved to the United States in 1937, first working as a research assistant to [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), then as a teacher of mathematics.

Beginning 1944, Helmer was involved in work for the National Defense Research Council under John Williams\<ref>John Davis Williams (1909-1964). He would join Williams at the newly formed RAND Corporation in 1946.

Helmer's interests later turned towards forecasting and prediction. Collaborating with colleagues Norman Dalkey\<ref>Norman Crolee Dalkey (1915-2004) and [Nicholas Rescher](wikipedia://en/576633), his work led to the development of the [Delphi method](wikipedia://en/337523) forecasting technique, also known as ETE (Estimate/Talk/Estimate).

In 1968, Helmer left RAND to co-found the [Institute for the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Future). In 1973 he was appointed Professor of Futuristics at the [School of Business Administration at the University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Marshall_School_of_Business).
