# Dale L. Compton

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dale L. Compton"}

**Dale Leonard Compton** (born June 1935) is an American [aerospace engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineer). He was the sixth director of [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)'s [Ames Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_Research_Center), serving from 1989 to 1994.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Compton earned a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in aeronautical engineering from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1957, where he was a member of [Sigma Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Nu).[^o5] He also earned a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in 1958 as one of the first students taught by former Ames aerodynamicist [Walter G. Vincenti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_G._Vincenti).[^o12]

He earned a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate_of_Philosophy) from Stanford in 1969.[^o4] He was a [Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellows) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1974 to 1975 and attended the Advanced Management Program at [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School) in 1986.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Compton joined Ames Research Center as a [research scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_scientist) studying [aerodynamics](wikipedia://en/2819).[^o7] His early work studied [ablation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ablation) and [aerothermodynamic](wikipedia://en/29952) effects on [ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile).[^o8] He was a scientist for fifteen years, contributing to [Project Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Mercury), [Project Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Gemini), and the [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program).

In the mid-1970s, Compton entered management, serving as deputy director for astronautics. He was later appointed chief of the space science division and manager of the [IRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRAS) telescope project office.[^o9] He was also made director of engineering and [computer systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_system) at Ames and named deputy director to [William F. Ballhaus Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Ballhaus_Jr.) in 1985.[^o5]

On February 1, 1988, Compton became acting center director of Ames until February 1, 1989, succeeding Ballhaus.[^o10] He resumed the position on July 15, 1989, and was formally appointed director on December 20, 1989, the fiftieth anniversary of the center's founding by [Smith J. DeFrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_J._DeFrance).[^o11]

He was considered conservative in his vision for Ames by some at NASA, and even a "tunnel hugger" for believing that Ames' standing at NASA was dependent on its [wind tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_tunnel) flight research.[^o12] In his view, project-oriented centers went through [boom and bust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_and_bust) cycles as [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress) approved or rejected major projects, and Ames could be protected by its specialization. Ames received \$300 million in the late 1980s for wind tunnel and simulator restoration projects, which Compton ensured received the highest priority.[^o12] Compton also fought for projects conceived at Ames, including the [Space Infrared Telescope Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Infrared_Telescope_Facility), [Mars Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Observer), and [Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_%28spacecraft%29), to be executed at Ames, but ultimately lost to the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory).[^o12]

In the early 1990s, with the fall of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and reduction in budget for space research, Compton led the center against efforts to have Ames shut down. He said "Ames has never had a secure place \[at NASA\]. All directors have tried to secure a place; some have succeeded, some not. My years as director were not easy."[^o12] He served as director until his retirement on January 28, 1994, and was succeeded by [Ken Munechika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Munechika).[^o11]

He was recognized by the city of [Mountain View, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_View,_California) in 1994 for his service.[^o13] He is a fellow of the [American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics).[^o5]
