# Martin A. Lindquist

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin A. Lindquist"}

**Martin A. Lindquist** is a statistician and biostatistician whose work focuses on functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), neuroimaging methodology, brain connectivity, causal inference, and pain neuroscience. He is a professor in the Department of Biostatistics[^o4] at the [Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Bloomberg_School_of_Public_Health). His research has included statistical modeling of the hemodynamic response in fMRI, dynamic functional connectivity, mediation analysis in neuroimaging, and methodological work on reliability, preprocessing, and biomarkers in brain imaging.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Lindquist was born in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden). He completed an MSc in engineering physics at the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTH_Royal_Institute_of_Technology) (KTH) in Stockholm in 1997 and earned a PhD in statistics from [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in 2001. His doctoral dissertation was titled *Fast Functional MRI Using Two-Dimensional Prolate Spheroidal Wavefunctions*. His thesis advisors were [Lawrence Shepp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Shepp) and Cun-Hui Zhang.

## Career {#Career}

After completing his doctorate, Lindquist was a postdoctoral associate at the Center for Magnetic Resonance Research at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) from 2001 to 2002. He joined [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 2002 as an assistant professor of statistics and became an associate professor in 2008. In 2012 he moved to [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) as an associate professor of biostatistics, and in 2015 he became a full professor.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

Lindquist's published work spans several areas of neuroimaging statistics. His early research included methods for rapid fMRI acquisition and the statistical analysis of high-temporal-resolution imaging data.[^o9] He later published on hemodynamic response modeling,[^o10] dynamic functional connectivity,[^o11] and reliability in neuroimaging research.[^o12] He has also published on causal inference and mediation analysis for neuroimaging data, including functional causal mediation[^o13] and high-dimensional mediation models.[^o14] His more recent work has addressed pain neuroimaging[^o15] and neuroimaging-based biomarkers.[^o16] His co-authored publications include work on neurologic signatures of physical pain.[^o17][^o18] He is a principal investigator on the Acute to Chronic Pain Signatures (A2CPS) project, which seeks to identify biomarkers and advance the study of pain.[^o19]

Lindquist also developed online courses, including *Principles of fMRI I*, *Principles of fMRI II*, and *The Statistical Analysis of fMRI Data*, offered through [Coursera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coursera), which have reached 100,000 students world-wide.[^o20][^o21][^o22]

Together with [Tor Wager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_Wager), he co-authored Principles of fMRI,[^o23] a low-cost book on fMRI data analysis.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Lindquist was elected a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 2016.[^o25] In 2018 he received the Organization for Human Brain Mapping Education in Neuroimaging Award.[^o3]
