# Magic triangle (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Magic triangle"}

A **magic triangle** is a magic arrangement of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) from 1 to $n$ in a triangular figure.

:::

## Perimeter magic triangle {#Perimeter_magic_triangle}

A **magic triangle** or **perimeter magic triangle**[^o3] is an arrangement of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) from 1 to $n$ on the sides of a triangle with the same number of integers on each side, called the *order* of the triangle, so that the sum of integers on each side is a constant, the [magic sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_sum) of the triangle.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6] Unlike [magic squares](wikipedia://en/52202), there are different magic sums for magic triangles of the same order.[^o3] Any magic triangle has a complementary triangle obtained by replacing each integer $x$ in the triangle with $1 + n - x$.[^o3]

## Other magic triangles {#Other_magic_triangles}

Other magic triangles use a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) or [square number](wikipedia://en/213919) of vertices to form magic figure. Matthew Wright and his students in [St. Olaf College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Olaf_College) developed magic triangles with square numbers. In their magic triangles, the sum of the $k$th row and the $(n - k + 1)$th row is same for all $k$[^o10] . Its one modification uses triangular numbers instead of square numbers . Another magic triangle form is magic triangles with triangular numbers with different summation. In this magic triangle, the sum of the $k$th row and the $(n - k)$th row is the same for all $k$ .

Another magic triangle form is magic triangles with square numbers with different summation. In this triangle, the sum of the 2×2 subtriangles is the same for all subtriangles .

Another form is the trihexagonal magic figure, based on a triangular portion of the [trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397). All triangular faces have the same sum, while all hexagonal faces have another common sum equal to twice the triangular sum . It has been conjectured that solutions to both of these forms may eventually cease to exist as the order *n* increases, due to their high constraint density.[^o11]

Generalized perimeter-magic types are defined which do not require a consecutive set of integers starting at 1 along the sides , .
