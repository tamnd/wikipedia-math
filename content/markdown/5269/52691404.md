# Matthew O'Brien (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Matthew O'Brien"}

**Matthew O'Brien** (1814–1855) was an Irish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

O'Brien was born at [Ennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennis) ([county Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Clare)) son of a medical doctor.[^o4] In 1830 he was admitted in the [Trinity College, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Dublin), and in 1834 in the [Caius College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caius_College) ([university of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge)) where he graduated in 1838 as third *wrangler*,[^o5] as pupil of [William Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hopkins).[^o6] During a brief period (1840–1841) he was fellow of Caius College.[^o7]

From 1844 to 1854 he was lecturer on Natural Philosophy and Mathematics at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London), he simultaneously held the post of lecturer on Astronomy in the [Royal Military Academy, Woolwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy,_Woolwich).[^o8]

O'Brien was the author of twenty mathematical papers and some elementary textbooks. His most notable contribution was in theory and application of the vector method,[^o9] in a set of papers published between 1846 and 1852.[^o10] However, he did not finish fully developing the method because some of his theories were unsatisfactory,[^o11] and because he failed to include a treatment of associativity.[^o12] His work was very innovative, but his ideas were almost completely ignored by his contemporaries.[^o13]
