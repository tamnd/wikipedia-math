# Carl S. Herz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carl S. Herz"}

**Carl Samuel Herz** (10 April 1930 – 1 May 1995) was an American-Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specializing in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). His name is attached to the [Herz–Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/22616744). He held professorships at Cornell University and McGill University, where he was Peter Redpath Professor of Mathematics at the time of his death.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Herz received his bachelor's degree from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1950 and continued on as a mathematics graduate student at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). There he received a Ph.D. under the supervision of [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847) in 1953 with the dissertation "Bessel Functions of Matrix Argument". According to [Tom H. Koornwinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_H._Koornwinder), Herz's dissertation (published in the [Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785) in May 1955) "was a pioneering paper in the field of [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function) in several variables associated with [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and with [root systems](wikipedia://en/277087)."[^o5] Herz returned to Cornell as an instructor, rising in rank to assistant professor in 1955, associate professor in 1958, and full professor in 1963. He remained at Cornell until 1969. During the academic year 1969–1970 he worked at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) and then in 1970 joined the faculty of [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) as full professor, where he remained until his death in 1995. During the academic year 1962–1963 Herz was a Sloan Fellow at [Université de Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud) at [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay), where he established close ties with mathematicians there that led to frequent academic visits at Orsay of a month or two each year. In the academic years 1957–1958 and 1976–1977 he was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).

Herz did mathematical research on spectral synthesis, [positive-definite functions](wikipedia://en/649616), [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247) on [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292), [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), H<sub>p</sub>, and [BMO](wikipedia://en/4694026). According to [Nicholas Varopoulos](wikipedia://en/37551761), Herz made contributions "to the theory of symmetric spaces, Lie groups and the [heat kernel](wikipedia://en/3904336) on these; among other things he succeeded in classifying all faithful representations of Lie groups by [contact transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_transformation) of a [compact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_manifold)."[^o6]

In 1978 he was elected of a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada). In 1986 he was awarded the [Jeffery–Williams Prize](wikipedia://en/18739069) by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055). He was the president of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) in 1987–1989.[^o7] The [Institut des sciences mathématiques](http://ism.uqam.ca/), a consortium of eight [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec) universities of which Herz was Director at the time of his death, established the Carl Herz Prize in his honor.[^o8]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Herz met Judith Scherer, a Professor of English, while teaching at Cornell. They were married in 1960. They had two children, Rachel and Nathaniel. Rachel is a psychologist and cognitive neuroscientist, specializing in the psychology of smell. Nathaniel is a trained lawyer working as web developer.
