# René Schoof

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="René Schoof"}

**René Schoof** (born 8 May 1955 in [Den Helder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_Helder))[^o3] is a mathematician from the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands) who works in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), and [coding theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coding_Theory).

:::

He received his PhD in 1985 from the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) with [Hendrik Lenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lenstra) (*Elliptic Curves and Class Groups*).[^o3][^o4] He is now a professor at the [University Tor Vergata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_Tor_Vergata) in Rome.[^o5]

In 1985, Schoof discovered an algorithm which enabled him to count points on [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time).[^o6] This was important for the use of elliptic curves in [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), and represented a theoretical breakthrough, as it was the first deterministic polynomial time algorithm for counting points on elliptic curves. The algorithms known before (e.g. the [baby-step giant-step](wikipedia://en/563928) algorithm) were of [exponential running time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_time). His algorithm was improved by [A. O. L. Atkin](wikipedia://en/3721430) (1992) and [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999) (1990).

He obtained the best-known result extending [Deligne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne)'s Theorem for finite flat group schemes to the non-commutative setting, over certain local [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273). His interests range throughout Algebraic Number Theory, [Arakelov theory](wikipedia://en/16364229), [Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/396150), problems related to the existence and classification of [Abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties) over the rationals with bad reduction in one prime only, and algorithms.

In the past, René has also worked with [Rubik's Cubes](wikipedia://en/25971) by creating a common strategy in [speedsolving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedsolving) used to set many world records known as F2L Pairs, in which the solver creates four 2-piece "pairs" with one edge and corner piece which are each "inserted" into F2L slots in the [CFOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFOP) method to finish the first two layers of a 3x3x3 Rubik's cube. This strategy is also used for all cubes of higher order (4x4x4 and up) in the Reduction, Yau, and Hoya methods if CFOP is used for their 3x3x3 stages.

He also wrote a book on [Catalan's conjecture](wikipedia://en/62382).[^o7]
