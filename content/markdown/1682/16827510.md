# First variation of area formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="First variation of area formula"}

In the mathematical field of [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), every [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) has a [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725). The **first variation of area formula** is a fundamental computation for how this quantity is affected by the deformation of the submanifold. The fundamental quantity is to do with the [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210).

:::

Let $(M, g)$ denote a Riemannian manifold, and consider an oriented smooth manifold $S$ (possibly with boundary) together with a one-parameter family of smooth [immersions](wikipedia://en/6993953) $f_{t}$ of $S$ into $M$. For each individual value of the parameter $t$, the immersion $f_{t}$ induces a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on $S$, which itself induces a [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) on $S$ known as the [Riemannian volume form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_volume_form) $\omega_{t}$. The *first variation of area* refers to the computation

$$\frac{d}{dt}\omega_t=-\left\langle W_t,H(f_t)\right\rangle_g\omega_t+\text{d}\left(\iota_{W_t^\parallel}\omega_t\right)$$

in which $H(f_{t})$ is the [mean curvature vector](wikipedia://en/684210) of the immersion $f_{t}$ and $W_{t}$ denotes the *variation vector field* $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f_t.$ Both of these quantities are [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) along the map $f_{t}$. The second term in the formula represents the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) of the [interior product](wikipedia://en/2154963) of the volume form with the vector field $W_t^\parallel$ on $S$, defined as the tangential projection of $W_{t}$. Via [Cartan's magic formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_magic_formula), this term can also be written as the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437) of the volume form relative to the tangential projection. As such, this term vanishes if each $f_{t}$ is reparametrized by the corresponding one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms of $S$.

Both sides of the first variation formula can be integrated over $S$, provided that the variation vector field has [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support). In that case it is immediate from [Stokes' theorem](wikipedia://en/25441497) that

$$\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{vol}(f_t)=-\int_S \left\langle W_t,H(f_t)\right\rangle_g\omega_t+\int_{\partial S}\iota_{W_t^\parallel}\omega_t.$$

In many contexts, $S$ is a [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) or the variation vector field is every orthogonal to the submanifold. In either case, the second term automatically vanishes. In such a situation, the mean curvature vector is seen as entirely governing how the surface area of a submanifold is modified by a deformation of the surface. In particular, the vanishing of the mean curvature vector is seen as being equivalent to submanifold being a [critical point](wikipedia://en/1951424) of the volume functional. This shows how a [minimal submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_submanifold) can be characterized either by the critical point theory of the volume functional or by an explicit [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) for the immersion.

The special case of the first variation formula arising when $S$ is an interval on the [real number line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_number_line) is particularly well-known. In this context, the volume functional is known as the *length functional* and its variational analysis is fundamental to the study of [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) in Riemannian geometry.
