# Polygon with holes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polygon with holes"}

*Image: Polygons with holes.png.* Polygons with holes, with simply connected brown regions and interior boundaries, including degenerate cases of single vertices and edges, (a,b,f).

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **polygon with holes** is an area-connected planar [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with one external boundary and one or more interior boundaries (holes).[^o2] Polygons with holes can be [dissected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) into multiple polygons by adding new edges, so they are not frequently needed.

:::

An ordinary polygon can be called [simply-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply-connected), while a polygon-with-holes is *multiply-connected*. An *H*-holed-polygon is *H*-*connected*.[^o3]

## Degenerate holes {#Degenerate_holes}

[Degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) cases may be considered, but a well-formed holed-polygon must have no contact between exterior and interior boundaries, or between interior boundaries. Nondegenerate holes should have 3 or more sides, excluding internal point boundaries ([monogons](wikipedia://en/5483767)) and single edge boundaries ([digons](wikipedia://en/5442728)).

## Boundary orientation {#Boundary_orientation}

[Computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) algorithms which process polygons with holes, such as in order to draw their interiors or to perform a [point in polygon test](wikipedia://en/504484), may require that the exterior boundary is distinguished from the interior boundaries by their [orientation](wikipedia://en/3633706), the order in which the points are listed;[^o6] commonly, the exterior boundary is listed in counter-clockwise order, and interior boundaries in clockwise order. This allows the interior area to be defined as *left* of each edge, regardless of whether that edge is part of an exterior or interior boundary.

## Conversion to ordinary polygon {#Conversion_to_ordinary_polygon}

A *polygon with holes* can be transformed into an ordinary unicursal boundary path by adding (degenerate) connecting double-edges between boundaries, or by [dissecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) or [triangulating](wikipedia://en/1440970) it into 2 or more simple polygons.

*Image: Holed-polygon-dissections.png.* Example conversion of a single-holed polygon by connecting edges, or [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29)

## In polyhedra {#In_polyhedra}

*Polygons with holes* can be seen as [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) in [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra), like a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) with a smaller cube externally placed on one of its [square](wikipedia://en/659939) [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) (augmented), with their common surfaces removed. A [toroidal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/24804975) can also be defined connecting a holed-face to a holed-faced on the opposite side (excavated). The [1-skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-skeleton) (vertices and edges) of a polyhedron with holed-faces is not a connected graph. Each set of connected edges will make a separate polyhedron if their edge-connected holes are replaced with faces.

The [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of hole-faced polyhedron is χ = *V* − *E* + *F* = 2(1−*g*) + *H*, [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g*, for *V* vertices, *E* edges, *F* faces, and *H* holes in the faces.

**Examples**

**Examples with degenerate holes**

A face with a point hole is considered a [monogonal](wikipedia://en/5483767) hole, adding one vertex, and one edge, and can attached to a degenerate [monogonal hosohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogonal_hosohedron) hole, like a [cylinder](wikipedia://en/859283#Right_circular_cylinders) hole with zero radius. A face with a degenerate [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) hole adds 2 vertices and 2 coinciding edges, where the two edges attach to two coplanar faces, as a [dihedron](wikipedia://en/6109457) hole.
