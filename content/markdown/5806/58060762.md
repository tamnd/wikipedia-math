# Annie Leuch-Reineck

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Annie Leuch-Reineck"}

**Annie Leuch-Reineck** (born **Annie Reineck**: 26 November 1880 - 21 December 1978) was a Swiss mathematician and women's rights activist. She was one of the most influential participants in the Swiss women's movement during the 1920s and 1930s.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

### Provenance and early years {#Provenance_and_early_years}

Annie Reineck was born in [Kannawurf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannawurf), a village in the countryside between [Erfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erfurt) and [Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg) in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_empire). Erhard Reineck (1841–1932), her father, was a protestant [church minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parson) and [superintentant (church administrator)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_%28ecclesiastical%29) originally from [Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg). Her mother, born Marie Godet (1847–1936), was from [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuchâtel) in [francophone western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suisse_Romande) [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland). Annie grew up in [Kannawurf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannawurf) and then in nearby [Heldrungen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heldrungen). She received her early education at the home of her elder sister, Theodora (Theodora Reineck, 1874–1963). Later, between 1895 and 1898, she attended the "École Vinet", a secondary school in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne).[^o3][^o7] The school was headed up by her aunt, Sophie Godet.[^o8][^o9]

### Student {#Student}

Reineck enrolled at the [University of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bern) in 1901 or 1902. She studied Mathematics and various Natural Sciences, notably Physics and Earth Sciences. In 1905 she received a secondary school teaching certificate.[^o3] There were still very few female students at the university, and several of the members of the small "Bern Female Students' Association" (*"Berner Studentinnenverein"*) became, in different ways, pioneering feminists and lifelong friends. For Annie Reineck these included [Gertrud Woker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrud_Woker) (1878 - 1968) and the physician [Ida Hoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Hoff) (1880 - 1952).[^o11]

### Teacher {#Teacher}

In 1907 Annie Reineck became the first German-language woman at the [University of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bern) to receive a doctorate in Mathematics. Her dissertation concerned "The relationship between [spherical functions](wikipedia://en/203056) and [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700)" (*"Die Verwandtschaft zwischen Kugelfunktionen und Besselschen Funktionen"*).[^o13] From this point till 1925 she taught at the Girls' Secondary School in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern) (where [Ida Hoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Hoff) was appointed school doctor in 1913) and at the city's training college for women teachers.[^o14]

In 1913 she married the commercial lawyer Georg Leuch (1888 - 1959). He became a [cantonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern) high court judge in 1920 and a [Federal Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Supreme_Court_of_Switzerland) Judge in 1925, which involved relocating to [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne).[^o15] By this time Annie Leuch-Reineck's time and energies had become divided between mathematics teaching and political campaigning: she now resigned from her teaching jobs in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern).[^o3]

### Women's rights {#Women's_rights}

In 1916 she took on the leadership of the [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern) section of the "Swiss Association for Women's Voting Rights" (*"Schweizerischer Verband für Frauenstimmrecht"*). In 1919 or 1921 she was a co-founder of the "Bernese Women's League" (*"Berner Frauenbund"*).[^o3] Between 1920 and 1933 she campaigned for the rights of Swiss women o retain their Swiss citizenship rights if they married a foreigner. She was a member of various legal commissions established by the [League of Swiss Women's Associations (*"Bund Schweizerischer Frauenvereine"*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_F). In 1921 she co-organised the "Second Swiss Congress for Women's Interests" (*"2. Schweizer Kongresses für Fraueninteressen"*), held that year in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern). In 1928 she was appointed to the national presidency of the "Swiss Association for Women's Voting Rights" (*"Schweizerischer Verband für Frauenstimmrecht"*), a post she retained till 1940.[^o3]

She was also, from 1928, one of those working on the 1929 "Swiss Exhibition for Women's Work" (*"Schweizer Ausstellung für Frauenarbeit"* / SAFFA). In 1929 she led that year's petition demanding for women the right to vote.[^o17] She was a member of the national executive (*"Vorstand"*) of the "Swiss Social Policy Union" (*"Schweizer Vereinigung für Sozialpolitik"*) and a member of the commission set up by the [Weltbund für Frauenstimmrecht (*"International Alliance of Women"*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Alliance_of_Women) to enquire into the citizenship rights of wives.

## Family {#Family}

Annie Leuch-Reineck was a grand daughter of the [reformist theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Reformed_church) Frédéric Louis Godet (1812–1900) from [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuchâtel).

Her sister, Eva Reineck, was a professional violinist who built her career in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) and married a graphic artist of Jewish provenance called David Spear. Annie Leuch-Reineck was thereby an aunt to this couple's son, [Walter Eric Spear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Eric_Spear), a notable physicist who built his career in England and Scotland after the family managed to escape from Nazi Germany in 1938.
