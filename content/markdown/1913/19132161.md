# Ordered semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ordered semigroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **ordered semigroup** is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) (*S*,•) together with a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) ≤ that is **compatible** with the semigroup operation, meaning that *x* ≤ *y* implies z•x ≤ z•y and x•z ≤ y•z for all *x*, *y*, *z* in *S*.

:::

An **ordered monoid** and an [ordered group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_group) are, respectively, a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) or a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that are endowed with a partial order that makes them ordered semigroups. The terms *posemigroup*, *pogroup* and *pomonoid* are sometimes used, where "po" is an abbreviation for "partially ordered".

The [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer), the [nonnegative integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonnegative_integer) and the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) form respectively a posemigroup, a pomonoid, and a pogroup under addition and the natural ordering.

Every semigroup can be considered as a posemigroup endowed with the trivial (discrete) partial order "=".

A **morphism** or **homomorphism** of posemigroups is a [semigroup homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_homomorphism) that *preserves* the order (equivalently, that is [monotonically increasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonically_increasing)).

## Category-theoretic interpretation {#Category-theoretic_interpretation}

A pomonoid $(M, \bullet, 1, \le)$ can be considered as a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) that is both [skeletal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_category) and [thin](wikipedia://en/31353487), with an object of for each element of $M$, a unique morphism from $m$ to $n$ if and only if $m \le n$, the tensor product being given by $\bullet$, and the unit by $1$.
