# Borel isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Borel isomorphism"}

In mathematics, a **Borel isomorphism** is a [measurable](wikipedia://en/44055) [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) function between two [standard Borel spaces](wikipedia://en/29819325). By [Souslin's theorem](wikipedia://en/634261) in standard Borel spaces (which says that a set that is both [analytic](wikipedia://en/634261) and [coanalytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coanalytic) is necessarily Borel), the [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) of any such measurable bijective function is also measurable. Borel isomorphisms are closed under composition and under taking of inverses. The set of Borel isomorphisms from a space to itself clearly forms a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) under composition. Borel isomorphisms on standard Borel spaces are analogous to [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) on [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450): both are bijective and closed under composition, and a homeomorphism and its inverse are both [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28topology%29), instead of both being only Borel measurable.

:::

## Borel space {#Borel_space}

A [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) that is Borel isomorphic to a [measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set) subset of the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is called a Borel space.[^o5]
