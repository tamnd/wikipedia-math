# Mikhail Subbotin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mikhail Subbotin"}

**Mikhail Fedorovich Subbotin** (Михаил Фёдорович Субботин, 29 June 1893 – 26 December 1966) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) who [calculated orbits](wikipedia://en/102182) of planets and [comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet). He worked on general properties of motion in the [*n*-body problem](wikipedia://en/23859945).

:::

## Biography and education {#Biography_and_education}

Subbotin was born on 29 June 1893 in Ostrolenka, Russian Empire (now [Ostrołęka, Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrołęka)).[^o4] His father was Fedor Subbotin, who was an army officer.

Mikhail Fedorovich Subbotin studied in the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in 1910 and graduated in 1914. He had an interest in astronomy, and worked as a calculator at the university observatory. After graduating he continued on as a junior astronomer.

In 1915, the University of Warsaw was evacuated to [Rostov-on-Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov-on-Don) after the German army invaded Poland, and Subbotin completed his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) there in 1917. During this time he also published two papers, “On the determination of singular points of analytic functions” and another on singular points of certain [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). He then moved to the Donskoy Polytechnic Institute ([Novocherkassk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novocherkassk)) where he was appointed a professor of mathematics. In 1922, he accepted an offer to work at the [Central Astronomical Observatory of the Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulkovo_Observatory) as Director in [Tashkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent).[^o5]

Preceding and during World War II, Subbotin worked at various astronomical institutions in [Leningrad (Saint Petersburg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg). Subbotin almost starved to death during the [Siege of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad), and was evacuated in February 1942 to [Sverdlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg) to recover. Near the end of 1942 Subbotin became the Director of the Leningrad Astronomical Institute, and relocated to [Saratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov) before it was brought back to Leningrad after the German withdrawal.[^o6] On 6 June 1945 Subbotin received the [Order of the Red Banner of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Red_Banner_of_Labor). In 1963 he was awarded the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin).[^o11]

Subbotin died on 26 December 1966 in then Leningrad, USSR (now [Saint Petersburg, Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg)). A memorial plaque was installed at his house at Moskovsky Prospect 206 in 1971 (architect V. V. Isaeva)[^o7]

## Works {#Works}

Subbotin started his career working on the [theory of functions](wikipedia://en/26478) and probability. He worked on the creation of a catalog of faint stars.[^o11] After his studies moved to astronomy, he concentrated on [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182) to devise new methods to calculate orbits from three observations based on solving the [Euler–Lambert equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Lambert_equations).[^o5][^o9][^o10]

> “... Subbotin not only showed the possibility of improving the convergence of the trigonometric series by which the behaviour of perturbing forces is represented, but also gave an expression for determining [Laplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace) coefficients and presented formulas for computing the coefficients of the necessary members of the trigonometric series.”[^o11]

Subbotin wrote a three-volume work called “Course in Celestial Mechanics" (1933–49), in which for the first time in Russian the main questions of celestial mechanics were described in detail.[^o11] He was the author of a number of fundamental studies on the history of astronomy. He was the editor-in-chief of the Astronomical Yearbook of the USSR, published by the Institute of Theoretical Astronomy of the [Academy of Sciences of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences).

## Celestial objects named after Subbotin {#Celestial_objects_named_after_Subbotin}

- [1692 Subbotina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1692_Subbotina), is a carbonaceous asteroid from the middle region of the asteroid belt, approximately 37 kilometers in diameter.
- *[Subbotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subbotin_%28crater%29)* is a 67 km-wide lunar crater on the far side of the Moon.[^o13][^o14]
