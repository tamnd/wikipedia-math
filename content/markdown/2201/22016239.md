# Size theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Size theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **size theory** studies the properties of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) endowed with $\mathbb{R}^k$-valued [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), with respect to the change of these functions. More formally, the subject of size theory is the study of the [natural pseudodistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_pseudodistance) between [size pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Size_pair). A survey of size theory can be found in .[^o2]

:::

## History and applications {#History_and_applications}

The beginning of size theory is rooted in the concept of [size function](wikipedia://en/16637535), introduced by Frosini.[^o4] Size functions have been initially used as a mathematical tool for shape comparison in [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) and [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706).[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12]

An extension of the concept of size function to [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) was made in the 1999 Frosini and Mulazzani paper [^o13] where [size homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/16683017) were introduced, together with the [natural pseudodistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_pseudodistance) for $\mathbb{R}^k$-valued functions. An extension to [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) (the [size functor](wikipedia://en/16845636)) was introduced in 2001.[^o14] The [size homotopy group](wikipedia://en/16683017) and the [size functor](wikipedia://en/16845636) are strictly related to the concept of persistent homology group [^o15] studied in [persistent homology](wikipedia://en/31069689). It is worth to point out that the size function is the rank of the $0$-th persistent homology group, while the relation between the persistent homology group and the size homotopy group is analogous to the one existing between [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) and [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506).

In size theory, [size functions](wikipedia://en/16637535) and [size homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/16683017) are seen as tools to compute lower bounds for the [natural pseudodistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_pseudodistance). Actually, the following link exists between the values taken by the size functions $\ell_{(N,\psi)}(\bar x,\bar y)$, $\ell_{(M,\varphi)}(\tilde x,\tilde y)$ and the [natural pseudodistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_pseudodistance) $d((M,\varphi),(N,\psi))$ between the size pairs $(M,\varphi),\ (N,\psi)$ ,[^o16][^o17]

$$\text{If }\ell_{(N,\psi)}(\bar x,\bar y)>\ell_{(M,\varphi)}(\tilde x,\tilde y)\text{ then }d((M,\varphi),(N,\psi))\ge \min\{\tilde x-\bar x,\bar y-\tilde y\}.$$

An analogous result holds for [size homotopy group](wikipedia://en/16683017).[^o13]

The attempt to generalize size theory and the concept of [natural pseudodistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_pseudodistance) to norms that are different from the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm) has led to the study of other reparameterization invariant norms.[^o18]
