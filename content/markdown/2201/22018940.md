# Graph equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph equation"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401),**Graph equations** are [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) in which the unknowns are [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806). One of the central questions of graph theory concerns the notion of [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/247577). We ask: When are two graphs the same? (i.e., graph isomorphism) The graphs in question may be expressed differently in terms of graph equations.

:::

What are the graphs ([solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solution_%28equation%29)) *G* and *H* such that the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of *G* is same as the [total graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_graph) of *H*? (What are *G* and *H* such that *L*(*G*) = *T*(*H*) ?).

For example, *G* = *K*<sub>3</sub>, and *H* = *K*<sub>2</sub> are the solutions of the graph equation *L*(*K*<sub>3</sub>) = *T*(*K*<sub>2</sub>) and *G* = *K*<sub>4</sub>, and *H* = *K*<sub>3</sub> are the solutions of the graph equation *L*(*K*<sub>4</sub>) = *T*(*K*<sub>3</sub>).

Note that *T*(*K*<sub>3</sub>) is a 4-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) on 6 vertices.
