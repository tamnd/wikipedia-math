# Rectification (geometry)

*Image: Cuboctahedron.png.* A rectified cube is a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) – edges reduced to vertices, and vertices expanded into new faces

*Image: Dual Cube-Octahedron.svg.* A *birectified* cube is an octahedron – faces are reduced to points and new faces are centered on the original vertices.

*Image: Rectified cubic honeycomb.jpg.* A [rectified cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_cubic_honeycomb) – edges reduced to vertices, and vertices expanded into new cells.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rectification"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), **rectification**, also known as **critical truncation** or **complete-truncation**, is the process of truncating a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) by marking the midpoints of all its [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), and cutting off its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at those points. The resulting polytope will be bounded by [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) facets and the rectified facets of the original polytope.

:::

A rectification operator is sometimes denoted by the letter $r$ with a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394). For example, $r\{4,3\}$ is the rectified [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), also called a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), and also represented as $\begin{Bmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$. And a rectified cuboctahedron $\operatorname{rr}\{4,3\}$ is a [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213), and also represented as $r\begin{Bmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$.

[Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705) uses $a$ for **ambo** as this operator. In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) this operation creates a [medial graph](wikipedia://en/28280731).

The rectification of any regular [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual) polyhedron or tiling will result in another regular polyhedron or tiling with a [tiling order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_order) of 4, for example the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) $\{3,3\}$ becoming an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) $\{3,4\}.$ As a special case, a [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) $\{4,4\}$ will turn into another square tiling $\{4,4\}$ under a rectification operation.

## Higher degree rectifications {#Higher_degree_rectifications}

Higher degree rectification can be performed on higher-dimensional regular polytopes. The highest degree of rectification creates the [dual polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope). A rectification truncates edges to points. A birectification truncates faces to points. A trirectification truncates cells to points, and so on.

:::

## In polygons {#In_polygons}

The dual of a polygon is the same as its rectified form. New vertices are placed at the center of the edges of the original polygon.

## In polyhedra and plane tilings {#In_polyhedra_and_plane_tilings}

Each [platonic solid](wikipedia://en/23905) and its [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) have the same rectified polyhedron. (This is not true of polytopes in higher dimensions.)

The rectified polyhedron turns out to be expressible as the intersection of the original platonic solid with an appropriately scaled concentric version of its dual. For this reason, its name is a combination of the names of the original and the dual:

- The [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) is its own dual, and its rectification is the *tetratetrahedron*, better known as the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635).
- The [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) and the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) are each other's dual, and their rectification is the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280).
- The [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) and the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) are duals, and their rectification is the [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023).

### In nonregular polyhedra {#In_nonregular_polyhedra}

If a polyhedron is not regular, the edge midpoints surrounding a vertex may not be coplanar. However, a form of rectification is still possible in this case: every polyhedron has a [polyhedral graph](wikipedia://en/24656270) as its [1-skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910), and from that graph one may form the [medial graph](wikipedia://en/28280731) by placing a vertex at each edge midpoint of the original graph, and connecting two of these new vertices by an edge whenever they belong to consecutive edges along a common face. The resulting medial graph remains polyhedral, so by [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817) it can be represented as a polyhedron.

The [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705) equivalent to rectification is **ambo**, represented by **a**. Applying twice **aa**, (rectifying a rectification) is Conway's [**expand**](wikipedia://en/6341070) operation, **e**, which is the same as Johnson's [cantellation](wikipedia://en/6317514) operation, t<sub>0,2</sub> generated from regular polyhedral and tilings.

## In 4-polytopes and 3D honeycomb tessellations {#In_4-polytopes_and_3D_honeycomb_tessellations}

Each [Convex regular 4-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_regular_4-polytope) has a rectified form as a [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899).

A regular 4-polytope {p,q,r} has cells {p,q}. Its rectification will have two cell types, a rectified {p,q} polyhedron left from the original cells and {q,r} polyhedron as new cells formed by each truncated vertex.

A rectified {p,q,r} is not the same as a rectified {r,q,p}, however. A further truncation, called [bitruncation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitruncation_%28geometry%29), is symmetric between a 4-polytope and its dual. See [Uniform 4-polytope#Geometric derivations](wikipedia://en/1645899#Geometric_derivations).

## Degrees of rectification {#Degrees_of_rectification}

A first rectification truncates edges down to points. If a polytope is [regular](wikipedia://en/518693), this form is represented by an extended [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) notation **t**<sub>1</sub>{p,q,...} or **r**{p,q,...}.

A second rectification, or **birectification**, truncates [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) down to points. If regular it has notation **t**<sub>2</sub>{p,q,...} or 2**r**{p,q,...}. For [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470), a birectification creates a [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815).

Higher degree rectifications can be constructed for higher dimensional polytopes. In general an n-rectification truncates *n-faces* to points.

If an n-polytope is (n-1)-rectified, its [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) are reduced to points and the polytope becomes its [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope).
