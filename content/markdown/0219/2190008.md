# Shephard's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shephard's lemma"}

**Shephard's lemma** is a result in [microeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microeconomics) having applications in the [theory of the firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_the_firm) and in [consumer choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_choice).[^o2] The [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) states that if [indifference curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indifference_curves) of the expenditure or [cost function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_curve) are [convex](wikipedia://en/245568), then the cost-minimizing point of a given good ($i$) with [price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price) $p_i$ is unique. The idea is that a [consumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer) will buy a unique ideal amount of each item to minimize the price for obtaining a certain level of [utility](wikipedia://en/45479) given the price of goods in the [market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_%28economics%29).

:::

The lemma is named after [Ronald Shephard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Shephard), who proved it using the distance formula in his book *Theory of Cost and Production Functions* in 1953. The equivalent result in the context of consumer theory was first derived by [Lionel W. McKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_W._McKenzie) in 1957.[^o3] It states that the partial derivatives of the expenditure function with respect to the prices of goods equal the [Hicksian demand functions](wikipedia://en/1998026) for the relevant goods. Similar results had already been derived by [John Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hicks) (1939) and [Paul Samuelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Samuelson) (1947).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In [consumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer) theory, Shephard's lemma states that the [demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand) for a particular good $i$ for a given level of utility $u$ and given prices $\mathbf{p}$, equals the derivative of the [expenditure function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expenditure_function) with respect to the price of the relevant good:

$$h_i(\mathbf{p}, u) = \frac{\partial e (\mathbf{p}, u)}{ \partial p_i}$$

where $h_i(\mathbf{p}, u)$ is the [Hicksian demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hicksian_demand) for good $i$, $e (\mathbf{p}, u)$ is the [expenditure function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expenditure_function), and both functions are in terms of prices (a [vector](wikipedia://en/149968) $\mathbf{p}$) and utility $u$.

Likewise, in the [theory of the firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_the_firm), the lemma gives a similar formulation for the [conditional factor demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_factor_demands) for each input factor: the derivative of the cost function $c (\mathbf{w}, y)$ with respect to the factor price:

$$x_i(\mathbf{w}, y) = \frac{\partial c (\mathbf{w}, y)}{ \partial w_i}$$

where $x_i(\mathbf{w}, y)$ is the [conditional factor demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_factor_demands) for input $i$, $c (\mathbf{w}, y)$ is the cost function. Both functions are in terms of factor prices (a [vector](wikipedia://en/149968) $\mathbf{w}$) and output $y$.

Although Shephard's original proof used the distance formula, modern proofs of Shephard's lemma use the [envelope theorem](wikipedia://en/3394695).[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof for the differentiable case"}

## Proof for the differentiable case {#Proof_for_the_differentiable_case}

The proof is stated for the two good cases for ease of notation. The expenditure function $e(p_{1},p_{2},u)$ is the value function of the constrained optimization problem characterized by the following Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L}=p_{1}x_{1} + p_{2}x_{2} + \lambda(u-U(x_{1},x_{2}))$$

By the [envelope theorem](wikipedia://en/3394695) the derivatives of the value function $e(p_{1},p_{2},u)$ with respect to the parameter $p_{1}$ are:

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial p_{1}}=\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_{1}}=x_{1}^{h}$$

where $x_{1}^{h}$ is the minimizer (i.e. the Hicksian demand function for good 1). This completes the Proof.

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

Shephard's lemma gives a relationship between expenditure (or cost) functions and Hicksian demand. The lemma can be re-expressed as [Roy's identity](wikipedia://en/2917162), which gives a relationship between an [indirect utility function](wikipedia://en/1998025) and a corresponding [Marshallian demand function](wikipedia://en/1018783).
