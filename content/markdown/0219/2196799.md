# Gauss–Kuzmin distribution

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Gauss–Kuzmin distribution"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Gauss–Kuzmin distribution** is a [discrete probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution) that arises as the limit [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the coefficients in the [continued fraction](wikipedia://en/739199) expansion of a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) [uniformly distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29) in (0, 1). The distribution is named after [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), who derived it around 1800,[^o6] and [Rodion Kuzmin](wikipedia://en/31855224), who gave a bound on the rate of convergence in 1929.[^o7][^o8] It is given by the [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725)

:::

$$p(k) = - \log_2 \left( 1 - \frac{1}{(k+1)^2}\right)~.$$

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Gauss–Kuzmin theorem"}

## Gauss–Kuzmin theorem {#Gauss–Kuzmin_theorem}

Let

$$x = \cfrac{1}{k_1 + \cfrac{1}{k_2 + \cdots}}$$

be the continued fraction expansion of a number *x* uniformly distributed in (0, 1). Then

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{P} \left\{ k_n = k \right\} = - \log_2\left(1 - \frac{1}{(k+1)^2}\right)~.$$

Equivalently, let

$$x_n = \cfrac{1}{k_{n+1} + \cfrac{1}{k_{n+2} + \cdots}}~;$$

then

$$\Delta_n(s) = \mathbb{P} \left\{ x_n \leq s \right\} - \log_2(1+s)$$

tends to zero as *n* tends to infinity.

:::

## Rate of convergence {#Rate_of_convergence}

In 1928, Kuzmin gave the bound

$$|\Delta_n(s)| \leq C \exp(-\alpha \sqrt{n})~.$$

In 1929, [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462)[^o12] improved it to

$$|\Delta_n(s)| \leq C \, 0.7^n~.$$

Later, [Eduard Wirsing](wikipedia://en/57927311) showed[^o13] that, for *λ* = 0.30366... (the [Gauss–Kuzmin–Wirsing constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss–Kuzmin–Wirsing_constant)), the limit

$$\Psi(s) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\Delta_n(s)}{(-\lambda)^n}$$

exists for every *s* in \[0, 1\], and the function *Ψ*(*s*) is analytic and satisfies *Ψ*(0) = *Ψ*(1) = 0. Further bounds were proved by [K. I. Babenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._I._Babenko).[^o14]
