# Tom M. Apostol

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tom M. Apostol"}

**Tom Mike Apostol** ( ;[^o3] August 20, 1923 – May 8, 2016)[^o4] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18831) and professor at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) specializing in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), best known as the author of widely used mathematical textbooks, including [*Calculus*](wikipedia://en/81437851) in two volumes.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Apostol was born on August 20, 1923, in [Helper, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helper,_Utah). His parents, Emmanouil Apostolopoulos and Efrosini Papathanasopoulos, were Greek immigrants.[^o6] Apostolopoulos's name was shortened to Mike Apostol when he obtained his United States citizenship, and Tom Apostol inherited this Americanized surname.[^o6]

Apostol received his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) in 1944, [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1946, and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1948. Thereafter Apostol was a faculty member at UC Berkeley, [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), and [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech). He was the author of several influential graduate and undergraduate level textbooks.

Apostol was the creator and project director for [Project MATHEMATICS!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_MATHEMATICS!) producing videos which explore basic topics in high school mathematics. He helped popularize the [visual calculus](wikipedia://en/10458716) devised by [Mamikon Mnatsakanian](wikipedia://en/29819533) with whom he also wrote a number of papers, many of which appeared in the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)*. He also provided academic content for an acclaimed video lecture series on introductory physics, *[The Mechanical Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mechanical_Universe)*.

In 2001, Apostol was elected into the [Academy of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Athens_%28modern%29) as a Corresponding Member.[^o8][^o9] He received the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) for expository excellence in 2005,[^o10][^o11][^o12] 2008,[^o13] and 2010.[^o14] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o15]
