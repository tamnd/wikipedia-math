# María del Carmen Martínez Sancho

Martínez

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="María del Carmen Martínez Sancho"}

**María del Carmen Martínez Sancho** (8 July 1901 in Toledo – 15 October 1995 in Malaga) was the first woman in Spain to gain a PhD in Mathematics. She was the first Professor of Mathematics to work in secondary school education in Spain, and the first woman to be appointed to the *Board of Extension of Studies* at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). She was part of Madrid and Seville's *Instituto-Escuela.*[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Martínez was born on 8 July 1901 in Toledo.[^o5][^o6] She was the second of six children in the family of José Martínez and Emilia Sancho.[^o5] Her father was an engineer and public worker, which resulted in the family living in several Spanish cities before they settled in Madrid.[^o5] Her father was keen for his children to receive their education at the *[Institución Libre de Enseñanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institución_Libre_de_Enseñanza)*.[^o7] He believed that boys and girls should have the same education, and supported her to complete high school classes at the [University of Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alcalá)'s Cardenal Cisneros Associated School.[^o5] Here she became interested in mathematics and literature, and her parents encouraged her toward mathematics.[^o5] Martínez herself was unhappy with the school: all students scored highly and there were no clear criteria for success. She finished her high school studies on 1 July 1918.[^o5]

In 1918 she began to study in sciences the [Central University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid).[^o8] In the first year of her degree, she was taught by extraordinary teachers, including Cecilio Jiménez Rueda and [Julio Rey Pastor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Rey_Pastor).[^o5] Throughout her career, her greatest influence would be [Julio Rey Pastor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Rey_Pastor) who taught her [Mathematical Analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).[^o3] Carmen was a prestigious student since she got the highest marks in the year. Whist still an undergraduate student she was asked to teach at the *Instituto-Escuela* of Madrid where her younger siblings studied. She gave classes simultaneously with her university studies. She graduated on 7 December 1926.[^o5]

She began her doctorate studies under the supervision of José María Plans y Freyre who was the first person to introduce relativity to Spain. Her thesis was titled: *The concept of function, continuous and semicontinuous functions, and their properties*.[^o5] She was awarded the *Extraordinary Price of Doctorate* for her doctoral thesis and became the first Spanish woman to earn a PhD in mathematics.[^o8][^o5][^o9]

## Career {#Career}

In 1928 Martínez was appointed Professor of Mathematics in the High-School Institute of Ferrol (La Coruña).[^o5] She would go on to become a teacher of the *El Instituto Femenino Infanta Beatriz* with her colleague Carmen Vielva Otorel.[^o5]

### Germany {#Germany}

On 4 November 1930 the Board of Extension of Studies granted her an eight-month grant to move to Germany to continue her studies on Multidimensional Geometry.[^o5] She attended classes given by [Kurt Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565) at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). She extended her grant for two extra months, eventually finishing her work on 2 September 1932.[^o5] She presented a memorial project that detailed Hammerstein's and Bieberbach's classes.[^o5]

### Seville {#Seville}

Immediately after returning to Spain from Germany, Martínez began to teach at *Instituto-Escuela.* She describes the time in Seville as one of the highlights of her career.[^o5] Whilst in Seville she met Alberto Meléndez, who would become her husband and father of her child, Alberto Meléndez Martínez.[^o5]

Martínez moved to the Murillo Institute.[^o13] She taught the first, third and fifth grade in 1938 and was promoted to Professor in 1939. Martínez organised a campaign within the Institute to build a canteen to provide food for disadvantaged students.[^o5] Her campaign suffered due to the rise of [Francoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain).[^o5] Her high school colleague Patricio Peñalver Bachiller, Professor and Dean in the Faculty of Sciences of the [University of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Seville), encouraged her to apply for faculty position.[^o5] She was appointed a Professor of Mathematics in 1957.[^o5]

### Return to Madrid {#Return_to_Madrid}

Martínez retired from the Murillo Institute in 1974, returned to Madrid and taught mathematics in the Jesús María School of Vallecas.[^o5] Again she made efforts to engage students of low socioeconomic status.[^o5]

### Recognition {#Recognition}

As an acknowledgement to her teaching labour, Seville's town hall named one of their streets of the Santa Justa district in her honour.[^o3]
