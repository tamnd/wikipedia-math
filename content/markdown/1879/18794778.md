# Gustav A. Hedlund

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gustav A. Hedlund"}

**Gustav Arnold Hedlund** (May 7, 1904 – March 15, 1993), an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), was one of the founders of [symbolic](wikipedia://en/1986263) and [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hedlund was born May 7, 1904, in [Somerville, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville,_Massachusetts). He did his undergraduate studies at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), earned a master's degree from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), and returned to Harvard for his doctoral studies. He was a student of [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523), under whose supervision he received a Ph.D. in 1930 with thesis entitled "I. Geodesics on a Two-Dimensional Riemannian Manifold with Periodic Coefficients II. Poincare's Rotation Number and Morse's Type Number".[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

While still studying at Columbia, Hedlund taught at [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College), and after receiving his doctorate he took a position at [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College), where he remained for nine years. From 1939 to 1948 he taught at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia), after which he moved to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). At Yale, he became the Philip Schuyler Beebe Professor of Mathematics, and chaired the mathematics department for ten years. He was also a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), which he visited in 1933, 1938, and 1953.[^o8] He was the Director of [IDA's Communications Research Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses#Center_for_Communications_and_Computing) in Princeton 1962-1963.[^o9] He retired from Yale in 1972, but afterwards held a visiting professorship at [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University).[^o4][^o5]

Hedlund died in 1993.[^o5] He has over 200 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy), many of them through two of his students at Virginia, [Walter Gottschalk](wikipedia://en/37710667) and W. Roy Utz, Jr.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

One of Hedlund's early results was an important theorem about the [ergodicity](wikipedia://en/5456824) of [geodesic flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_flow).[^o11] He also made significant contributions to [symbolic dynamics](wikipedia://en/1986263), whose origins as a field of modern mathematics can be traced to a 1944 paper of Hedlund, and to [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713).[^o4][^o5]

The [Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon theorem](wikipedia://en/18903091), a topological characterization of [cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata), is named after Hedlund. Hedlund first published this theorem in 1969, crediting [Morton L. Curtis](wikipedia://en/32407013) and [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon) as co-discoverers.[^o12]

Hedlund was the co-author of the book *Topological Dynamics* (with [Walter Gottschalk](wikipedia://en/37710667), American Mathematical Society, 1955).[^o13][^o14]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Hedlund was elected to [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi) in 1943.[^o16]

In 1972, a conference on topological dynamics was held to honor Hedlund on the occasion of his retirement from Yale. The editor of the [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) from the conference, [Anatole Beck](wikipedia://en/34858154), wrote that it was "our token of respect to the man who did so much to foster and build this field".[^o17]
