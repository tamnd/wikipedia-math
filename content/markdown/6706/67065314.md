# Almgren's isomorphism theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Almgren's isomorphism theorem"}

**Almgren isomorphism theorem** is a result in [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) about the topology of the space of [flat cycles](wikipedia://en/36180950) in a Riemannian manifold.

:::

The theorem plays a fundamental role in the [Almgren–Pitts min-max theory](wikipedia://en/46587298) as it establishes existence of topologically non-trivial families of cycles, which were used by [Frederick J. Almgren Jr.](wikipedia://en/12998276), [Jon T. Pitts](wikipedia://en/46721841) and others to prove existence of (possibly singular) [minimal submanifolds](wikipedia://en/276734) in every Riemannian manifold. In the special case of the space of null-homologous codimension 1 cycles with mod 2 coefficients on a closed Riemannian manifold Almgren isomorphism theorem implies that it is weakly homotopy equivalent to the infinite [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let M be a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652). Almgren isomorphism theorem asserts that the m-th [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) of the space of [flat](wikipedia://en/36180950) k-dimensional cycles in M is isomorphic to the (m+k)-th dimensional [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) group of M. This result is a generalization of the [Dold–Thom theorem](wikipedia://en/31336432), which can be thought of as the k=0 case of Almgren's (1962a (ver. PhD thesis),[^o5]1962b (ver. Topology (Elsevier)[^o6])[^o7] theorem. The isomorphism is defined as follows. Let G be an abelian group and $Z_k(M;G)$ denote the space of flat cycles with coefficients in group G. To each family of cycles $f: S^m \rightarrow Z_k(M;G)$ we associate an (m+k)-cycle C as follows. Fix a fine [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) T of $S^m$. To each vertex v in the 0-skeletion of T we associate a cycle f(v). To each edge E in the 1-skeleton of T with ∂E=v-w we associate a (k+1)-chain with boundary f(v)-f(w) of minimal mass. We proceed this way by induction over the skeleton of T. The sum of all chains corresponding to m-dimensional faces of T will be the desired (m+k)-cycle C. Even though the choices of triangulation and minimal mass fillings were not unique, they all result in an (m+k)-cycle in the same homology class.[^o8]

:::
