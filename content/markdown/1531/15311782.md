# Accessible category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Accessible category"}

The theory of **accessible categories** is a part of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869). It attempts to describe categories in terms of the "size" (a [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173)) of the operations needed to generate their objects.

:::

The theory originates in the work of [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) completed by 1969,[^o2] and Gabriel and Ulmer (1971).[^o3] It has been further developed in 1989 by [Michael Makkai](wikipedia://en/22629410) and Robert Paré, with motivation coming from [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a branch of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).[^o4] A standard text book by Adámek and Rosický appeared in 1994.[^o5] Accessible categories also have applications in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831).[^o6][^o7] Grothendieck continued the development of the theory for homotopy-theoretic purposes in his (still partly unpublished) 1991 manuscript *Les dérivateurs*.[^o8] Some properties of accessible categories depend on the [set universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) in use, particularly on the [cardinal](wikipedia://en/6173) properties and [Vopěnka's principle](wikipedia://en/3995195).[^o9]

## $\kappa$-directed colimits and $\kappa$-presentable objects {#-directed_colimits_and_-presentable_objects}

Let $\kappa$ be an infinite [regular cardinal](wikipedia://en/374128), i.e. a [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) that is not the sum of a smaller number of smaller cardinals; examples are $\aleph _{0}$ ([aleph-0](wikipedia://en/293961)), the first infinite cardinal number, and $\aleph_{1}$, the first uncountable cardinal). A [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) $(I, \leq)$ is called **$\kappa$-directed** if every subset $J$ of $I$ of cardinality less than $\kappa$ has an upper bound in $I$. In particular, the ordinary [directed sets](wikipedia://en/9165) are precisely the $\aleph_0$-directed sets.

Now let $C$ be a [category](wikipedia://en/53759). A [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) (also known as a directed colimit) over a $\kappa$-directed set $(I, \leq)$ is called a **$\kappa$-directed colimit**. An object $X$ of $C$ is called $\kappa$**\-presentable** if the [Hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037) $\operatorname{Hom}(X,-)$ preserves all $\kappa$-directed colimits in $C$. It is clear that every $\kappa$-presentable object is also $\kappa'$-presentable whenever $\kappa\leq\kappa'$, since every $\kappa'$-directed colimit is also a $\kappa$-directed colimit in that case. A $\aleph_0$-presentable object is called **finitely presentable**.

::: {#o12 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- In the category **[Set](wikipedia://en/26829)** of all sets, the finitely presentable objects coincide with the finite sets. The $\kappa$-presentable objects are the sets of cardinality smaller than $\kappa$.
- In the [category of all groups](wikipedia://en/800092), an object is finitely presentable if and only if it is a [finitely presented group](wikipedia://en/99494#finitely_presented), i.e. if it has a presentation with finitely many generators and finitely many relations. For uncountable regular $\kappa$, the $\kappa$-presentable objects are precisely the groups with cardinality smaller than $\kappa$.
- In the [category of left $R$-modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over some (unitary, associative) [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $R$, the finitely presentable objects are precisely the [finitely presented modules](wikipedia://en/326430#Finitely_presented,_finitely_related,_and_coherent_modules).

:::

## $\kappa$-accessible and locally presentable categories {#-accessible_and_locally_presentable_categories}

The category $C$ is called **$\kappa$-accessible** provided that:

- $C$ has all $\kappa$-directed colimits
- $C$ contains a set $P$ of $\kappa$-presentable objects such that every object of $C$ is a $\kappa$-directed colimit of objects of $P$.

An $\aleph_0$-accessible category is called **finitely accessible**. A category is called **accessible** if it is $\kappa$-accessible for some infinite regular cardinal $\kappa$. When an accessible category is also [cocomplete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocomplete), it is called **locally presentable**.

A functor $F : C \to D$ between $\kappa$-accessible categories is called **$\kappa$-accessible** provided that $F$ preserves $\kappa$-directed colimits.

::: {#o15 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- The category **Set** of all sets and functions is locally finitely presentable, since every set is the direct limit of its finite subsets, and finite sets are finitely presentable.
- The category $R$-Mod of (left) $R$-modules is locally finitely presentable for any ring $R$.
- The category of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784) is finitely accessible.
- The category Mod(T) of models of some [first-order theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theory) T with countable signature is $\aleph_1$ -accessible. $\aleph_1$ -presentable objects are models with a countable number of elements.
- Further examples of locally presentable categories are finitary algebraic categories (i.e. the categories corresponding to [varieties of algebras](wikipedia://en/1021753) in [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200)) and [Grothendieck categories](wikipedia://en/30051538).

:::

::: {#o17 .statement cue=heading name="Theorems"}

## Theorems {#Theorems}

One can show that every locally presentable category is also [complete](wikipedia://en/62781).[^o18] Furthermore, a category is locally presentable if and only if it is equivalent to the category of models of a limit [sketch](wikipedia://en/20494787).[^o19]

[Adjoint functors](wikipedia://en/53991) between locally presentable categories have a particularly simple characterization. A functor $F : C \to D$ between locally presentable categories:

- is a left adjoint if and only if it preserves small colimits,
- is a right adjoint if and only if it preserves small limits and is accessible.

:::
