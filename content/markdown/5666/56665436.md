# Romano Scozzafava

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Romano Scozzafava"}

**Romano Scozzafava** (born November 12, 1935) is an Italian mathematician known for his contributions to subjective probability along the lines of [Bruno de Finetti](wikipedia://en/180825), based on the concept of coherence. He taught Probability Calculus at the Engineering Faculty of the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) from 1979 to his retirement (at the end of 2009).

:::

Scozzafava has conducted significant research on [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571), [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence), and [fuzzy set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzzy_set_theory) in terms of coherent conditional probability.[^o3][^o4] He has written six books and over 200 papers on these subjects.[^o5][^o6] Throughout his career, he actively participated in politics as a supporter of the Italian Radical Party and of “Associazione Luca Coscioni” for Freedom of Scientific Research.[^o7][^o8]

## Education and early career {#Education_and_early_career}

Scozzafava graduated in mathematics in 1961 at the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome). He was given a fellowship at Istituto Superiore Poste Telecomunicazioni and then one at CNEN (Comitato Nazionale Energia Nucleare) in 1962. For the next five years, he conducted research at CNEN and during this time, he wrote several articles on the application of Mathematics in Physics. In 1967, he received his academic teaching [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) ("libera docenza") in Mathematical Methods in Physics, which was confirmed in 1973.

## Later career {#Later_career}

In 1967, Scozzafava began teaching at the [University of Perugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Perugia). He taught there until 1969, when he left to join [University of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florence) as assistant professor of Mathematical Analysis. At this time, the focus of his research began shifting towards Algebra,[^o11] and mainly towards Statistics and Probability.[^o12]

Scozzafava joined [University of Lecce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lecce) as full professor in 1976. After teaching at University of Lecce for three years, he left to join Sapienza University of Rome in 1979. Over two decades of career, he received several research grants from Ministry of Education and Research and National Council of Research to conduct research and write papers in the field of Bayesian Statistics, Probability and Artificial Intelligence. While teaching at Sapienza, he taught also at the Universities of Ancona, L'Aquila and Perugia. In 1994 and 1997 he served as the director of the International School of Mathematics G. Stampacchia of the [Ettore Majorana Foundation and Centre for Scientific Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Majorana_Foundation_and_Centre_for_Scientific_Culture) in [Erice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erice), [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily).[^o13][^o14] From 2001 to 2009 he organized the international school ReasonPark (Reasoning under Partial Knowledge).[^o15]

He has been a visiting professor in [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology), [Karl Marx University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvinus_University_of_Budapest) of Budapest, [Somali National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_National_University), [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) (UK), [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina), Chapel Hill (USA), [Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Tech) (USA), [University of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury) (New Zealand) and [University of Economics, Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Economics,_Prague).[^o16]

He has been the editor of *Rendiconti di matematica*, *Pure Mathematics and Applications*, *Induzioni* and *Cognitive Processing*. He has been Elected Member of the International Statistical Institute and Coordinator of the *Dottorato di Ricerca* (Ph.D.) in *Modelli e Metodi Matematici per la Tecnologia e la Società*, University *La Sapienza*, Roma.[^o17] He has been Guest Editor of special issues of *Soft Computing* in 1999[^o18] and of *Annals of Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence* in 2002.[^o19]

A special issue of the *International Journal of Approximate Reasoning* was dedicated to Scozzafava to celebrate his 70th birthday.[^o20] An international workshop was also organized on the same occasion in his honour.[^o21]

Due to his involvement in politics, he has also written some papers on the connections among mathematics, politics, elections and scientific reasoning.[^o22][^o23][^o24][^o25]
