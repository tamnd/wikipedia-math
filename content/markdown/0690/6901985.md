# James Glimm

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James Glimm"}

**James Gilbert Glimm** (born March 24, 1934) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), former president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), and distinguished professor at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University). He has made many contributions in the areas of pure and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

*Image: James Glimm - National Medal of Science, 2002.webm.* Glimm discusses his contributions to the world of computer science through mathematical analysis and physics.

James Glimm was born in [Peoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Illinois), [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois), United States on March 24, 1934.[^o4] He received his BA in engineering from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1956. He continued on to graduate school at Columbia where he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics in 1959; his advisor was [Richard V. Kadison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_V._Kadison).[^o5] Glimm was at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), and at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University), before arriving at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) in 1989.[^o4]

He has been noted for contributions to [C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/7184), [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), [scientific computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computing), and the modeling of petroleum reservoirs. Together with [Arthur Jaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Jaffe), he has founded a subject called [constructive quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/1231204). His early work in the theory of operator algebras was seminal, and today the "Glimm algebras" that bear his name continue to play an important role in this area of research.[^o6] More recently, the [United States Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy) adopted Glimm's front-track methodology for shock-wave calculations, e.g., simulating weapons performance.[^o7]

Glimm was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1984. He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1970 at Nice[^o8] and a Plenary Speaker of the ICM in 1974 at Vancouver.[^o9] In 1993, Glimm was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for his contribution to solving hyperbolic systems of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).[^o10] He won the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) in 2002 "For his original approaches and creative contribution to an array of disciplines in mathematical analysis and mathematical physics".[^o11] Starting January 1, 2007, he served a 2-year term as president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). In 2012 he became a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.[^o12]
