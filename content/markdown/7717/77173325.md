# Equivalent radius

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equivalent radius"}

In [applied sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_science), the **equivalent radius** (or **mean radius**) is the radius of a circle or sphere with the same perimeter, area, or volume of a non-circular or non-spherical object. The **equivalent diameter** (or **mean diameter**) ($D$) is twice the equivalent radius.

:::

## Perimeter equivalent {#Perimeter_equivalent}

*Image: Using a DTApe.JPG.* Measurement of tree circumference. The tape is calibrated to show diameter at breast height, assuming a circular shape.

The [perimeter of a circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perimeter_of_a_circle) of radius *R* is $2 \pi R$. Given the perimeter of a non-circular object *P*, one can calculate its **perimeter-equivalent radius** by setting

$$P = 2\pi R_\text{eq}$$

or, alternatively:

$$R_\text{eq} = \frac{P}{2\pi}$$

For example, a square of side *L* has a perimeter of $4L$. Setting that perimeter to be equal to that of a circle imply that

$$R_\text{eq} = \frac{4L}{2 \pi} = \frac{2L}{\pi} \approx 0.6366 L$$

Applications:

- [US hat size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_hat_size) is the circumference of the head, measured in inches, divided by [pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_%28constant%29), rounded to the nearest 1/8 inch. This corresponds to the 1D mean diameter.[^o3]
- [Diameter at breast height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diameter_at_breast_height) is the circumference of [tree trunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_trunk), measured at height of 4.5 feet, divided by pi. This corresponds to the 1D mean diameter. It can be measured directly by a [girthing tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girthing_tape).[^o4]

## Area equivalent {#Area_equivalent}

*Image: Area-equivalent radius and diameter.svg.* The area-equivalent radius of a 2D object is the radius of a circle with the same area as the object

*Image: Wetted Perimeter.svg.* Cross sectional area of a trapezoidal open channel, red highlights the [wetted perimeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetted_perimeter), where water is in contact with the channel. The [hydraulic diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_diameter) is the equivalent circular configuration with the same circumference as the wetted perimeter.

The [area of a circle](wikipedia://en/1896782) of radius *R* is $\pi R^2$. Given the area of a non-circular object *A*, one can calculate its **area-equivalent radius** by setting

$$A = \pi R^2_\text{eq}$$

or, alternatively:

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}$$

Often the area considered is that of a [cross section](wikipedia://en/1337370).

For example, a square of side length *L* has an area of $L^2$. Setting that area to be equal that of a circle imply that

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt{\frac{L^2}{\pi}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi}} L \approx 0.5642 L$$

Similarly, an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) with [semi-major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) $a$ and [semi-minor axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-minor_axis) $b$ has area of $\pi ab$, and therefore

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi ab}{\pi}} = \sqrt{a b}.$$

Applications:

- The [hydraulic diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_diameter) is similarly defined as 4 times the [cross-sectional area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-sectional_area) of a pipe *A*, divided by its ["wetted" perimeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetted_perimeter) *P*. For a circular pipe of radius *R*, at full flow, this is

$$D_\text{H} = \frac{4 \pi R^2}{2 \pi R} = 2R$$

as one would expect. This is equivalent to the above definition of the 2D mean diameter. However, for historical reasons, the [hydraulic radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_radius) is defined as the cross-sectional area of a pipe *A*, divided by its wetted perimeter *P*, which leads to $D_\text{H} = 4 R_\text{H}$, and the hydraulic radius is *half* of the 2D mean radius.[^o6]

- In [aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_aggregate) classification, the equivalent diameter is the "diameter of a circle with an equal aggregate sectional area", which is calculated by $D = 2 \sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}$. It is used in many digital image processing programs.[^o7]

## Volume equivalent {#Volume_equivalent}

*Image: Ellipsoide.svg.* A sphere (top), rotational ellipsoid (left) and triaxial ellipsoid (right)

The [volume of a sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_of_a_sphere) of radius *R* is $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$. Given the volume of a non-spherical object *V*, one can calculate its **volume-equivalent radius** by setting

$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3_\text{eq}$$

or, alternatively:

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3V}{4\pi}}$$

For example, a cube of side length *L* has a volume of $L^3$. Setting that volume to be equal that of a sphere imply that

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3L^3}{4\pi}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3}{4\pi}} L \approx 0.6204 L$$

Similarly, a [tri-axial ellipsoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-axial_ellipsoid) with axes $a$, $b$ and $c$ has a volume of $\frac{4}{3}\pi abc$, and therefore[^o9]

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3\frac{4}{3}\pi abc}{4\pi}} =\sqrt[3]{abc}.$$

The formula for a [rotational ellipsoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_ellipsoid) is the special case where $a=b$

$R_\text{eq}=\sqrt[3]{a^{2} \cdot c }$.[^o10]

Applications:

- For planet [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228), which can be approximated as an oblate spheroid with radii \6378.1 km and \6356.8 km, the 3D mean radius is $R=\sqrt[3]{6378.1^{2}\cdot6356.8}=6371.0\text{ km}$.[^o10]

## Other equivalences {#Other_equivalences}

### Surface-area equivalent radius {#Surface-area_equivalent_radius}

The [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) of a sphere of radius *R* is $4\pi R^2$. Given the surface area of a non-spherical object *A*, one can calculate its surface area-equivalent radius by setting

$$4\pi R^2_\text{eq} = A$$

or equivalently

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt{\frac{A}{4\pi}}$$

For example, a cube of length *L* has a surface area of $6L^2$. A cube therefore has an surface area-equivalent radius of

$$R_\text{eq} = \sqrt{\frac{6L^2}{4\pi}} \approx 0.6910 L$$

### Curvature-equivalent radius {#Curvature-equivalent_radius}

*Image: Osculating circle.svg.* An osculating circle

The [osculating circle](wikipedia://en/1896705) and [osculating sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osculating_sphere) define [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770)-equivalent radii at a particular [point of tangency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_tangency) for [plane figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_figure) and solid figures, respectively.
