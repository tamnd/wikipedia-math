# Theodore Motzkin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theodore Motzkin"}

**Theodore Samuel Motzkin** (תיאודור מוצקין; 26 March 1908 – 15 December 1970) was an [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Motzkin's father [Leo Motzkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Motzkin), a [Ukrainian Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Jews), went to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) at the age of thirteen to study mathematics. He pursued university studies in the topic and was accepted as a graduate student by [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450), but left the field to work for the [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) movement before finishing a dissertation.

Motzkin grew up in Berlin and started studying mathematics at an early age as well, entering university when he was only 15.[^o5] He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1934 from the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel) under the supervision of [Alexander Ostrowski](wikipedia://en/5827091) for a thesis on the subject of [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730)[^o5] (*Beiträge zur Theorie der linearen Ungleichungen*, "Contributions to the Theory of Linear Inequalities", 1936[^o7]).

In 1935, Motzkin was appointed to the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem), contributing to the development of [mathematical terminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_terminology) in [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language).[^o7] In 1936 he was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Oslo.[^o8] During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he worked as a [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) for the British government.[^o5]

Motzkin married Naomi Orenstein in Jerusalem. They had three sons there.[^o5]

In 1948, Motzkin moved to the United States. After two years at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College), he was appointed at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) in 1950, becoming a professor in 1960.[^o7] He worked there until his retirement.[^o5]

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

Motzkin's dissertation contained an important contribution to the nascent theory of linear programming (LP), but its importance was only recognized after an English translation appeared in 1951. He would continue to play an important role in the development of LP while at UCLA.[^o7] Apart from this, Motzkin published about diverse problems in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o7]

The [Motzkin transposition theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motzkin_transposition_theorem), [Motzkin numbers](wikipedia://en/853141), [Motzkin–Taussky theorem](wikipedia://en/73605210) and the [Fourier–Motzkin elimination](wikipedia://en/9203671) are named after him. He first developed the "double description" algorithm of [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519) and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927).[^o10] He was the first to prove the existence of [principal ideal domains](wikipedia://en/25039) that are not [Euclidean domains](wikipedia://en/10376), $\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{-19}}{2}\right]$ being his first example.[^o11]

He found the first explicit example of a [nonnegative polynomial](wikipedia://en/28416554) which is not a [sum of squares](wikipedia://en/17001761), known as the Motzkin polynomial $X^4Y^2+X^2Y^4-3X^2Y^2+1$, and showed that it was positive using the [AM–GM inequality](wikipedia://en/605011#Motzkin).[^o12]

The quote "complete disorder is impossible," describing [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175), is attributed to him.[^o13]
