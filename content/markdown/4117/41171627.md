# Shoichiro Sakai

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shoichiro Sakai"}

**Shoichiro Sakai** is a Japanese mathematician.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Sakai studied mathematics at the Tohoku University (Sendai). He there received the B. A. degree in 1953 and a doctorate at the same University in 1961. From 1960 to 1964, he was a faculty member of [Waseda University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waseda_University). He then went to the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he became a professor in 1966 and remained until 1979. He then returned to Japan and went to the [Nihon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihon_University). In 1992, he received the [Japanese Mathematical Society Autumn Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Mathematical_Society_Autumn_Prize).[^o3] He is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4]

Sakai's main field is [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). His textbook published in the Springer series in C \*-algebras and W \*-algebras, in which W \*-algebras as C \*-algebras are introduced with a predual, is widely used. That fact the W \*-algebras may be defined in this way is known as a theorem of Sakai[^o5] (cf. a [theorem of Kadison-Sakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theorem_of_Kadison-Sakai).)[^o6]
