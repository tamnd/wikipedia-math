# Craig Huneke

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Craig Huneke"}

**Craig Lee Huneke** (born August 27, 1951) is an American mathematician specializing in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990). He is a professor at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia).

:::

Huneke graduated from [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College) with a bachelor's degree in 1973 and in 1978 earned a Ph.D. from the [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) under [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) and [David Eisenbud](wikipedia://en/3649845) (*Determinantal ideal and questions related to factoriality*).[^o3] As a post-doctoral fellow, he was at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). In 1979 he became an assistant professor and was at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and the University of Bonn (1980). In 1981 he became an assistant professor at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), where in 1984 he became an associate professor and became a professor in 1987. From 1994 to 1995 he was a visiting professor at the University of Michigan and in 1999 was at the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) in Bonn (as a [Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Scholar)). In 1999, he was Henry J. Bischoff professor at the University of Kansas. In 2002 he was at MSRI. Since 2012 he has been Marvin Rosenblum professor at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia).

With [Melvin Hochster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Hochster) and others, he developed the theory of [tight closure](wikipedia://en/6921893), a device in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424) that is used to study rings containing a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_%28field_theory%29) *p* in which [Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270) figures prominently. He also studies [linkage theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkage_theory), [Rees algebras](wikipedia://en/25362831), homological theory of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129), [local cohomology](wikipedia://en/3212091), [symbolic powers of ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_powers_of_ideal), [Cohen-Macaulay rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen-Macaulay_ring), [Gorenstein rings](wikipedia://en/515613) and [Hilbert-Kunz functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert-Kunz_function).

He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1990 in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) (*Absolute Integral Closure and Big Cohen-Macaulay Algebras*). He is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4]

Huneke's son is historian [Samuel Clowes Huneke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Clowes_Huneke).[^o5]
