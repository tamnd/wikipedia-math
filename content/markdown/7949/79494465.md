# Trajectoid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trajectoid"}

*Image: rolling\_sphericon.gif.* Animation of a rolling sphericon which makes a sinusoidal path.

A **trajectoid** is a geometric shape designed to trace a predetermined general periodic trajectory on the plane.[^o2] Unlike more familiar rolling bodies such as [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) or [cylinders](wikipedia://en/859283), which follow linear or sinusoidal paths, trajectoids can trace even very complex, custom trajectories. The concept extends earlier studies on rolling objects like [sphericons](wikipedia://en/572813) and [oloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oloid), introducing much greater diversity in possible rolling paths.

:::

Physicists and mathematicians from the [Institute for Basic Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Basic_Science), [Ulsan National Institute of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_National_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology) and the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva), developed an algorithm that defines 3D shapes that will roll under gravity along a general periodic trajectory on an inclined plane (or equivalently can be rolled by hand on a horizontal plane).[^o3] This algorithm was realized through 3D printing: Trajectoids were produced by printing two halves and a spherical hollow center which would contain a metal sphere for mass.

The authors conjectured that trajectoids can be generated for any planar periodic trajectory as long as the trajectoid returns to its original orientation after **two** or more periods.[^o4] This conjecture was later proved as part of a more general theorem that applies to any system governed by the [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789) group [SO(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%283%29) or the unitary group [SU(2)](wikipedia://en/173997),[^o5] such as spins, qubits and gyroscopes. The theorem states any series of rotations operating on such a physical system (except for a zero-measure set) can be made to return the system to its original state by uniformly scaling and repeating the series **twice**. The existence of trajectoids follows from [Minkowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/53455) and the [Rodrigues](wikipedia://en/1525933) formula for the product of rotation matrices.
