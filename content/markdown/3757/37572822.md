# Leonhard Sohncke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leonhard Sohncke"}

**Leonhard Sohncke** (22 February 1842 in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_%28Saale%29) ([Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia)) – 1 November 1897 in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) ([German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire))) was a German mathematician, physicist, and mineralogist.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Leonhard Sohncke was born as son of [Ludwig Adolf Sohncke](wikipedia://en/35218590), a professor for mathematics in Halle.[^n1] Leonhard Sohncke studied mathematics and sciences at the [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_Halle-Wittenberg) in his native town,[^n1] and passed the examination for high school teachers in 1862. With this qualification he became teacher at the [Collegium Fridericianum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Fridericianum) in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) in the Prussian province of [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia),[^n2][^o7] and worked in this position until 1871. In this time he wrote his doctoral thesis in mathematics, which he submitted to Halle University, where he was in contact with [Eduard Heine](wikipedia://en/608289) and [Carl Neumann](wikipedia://en/915551).[^n2] With the duty of twenty-two lessons a week as teacher,[^n2] he additionally continued his studies at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg), where he attended courses at the Königsberg Seminar for Physics, once founded by his father L. A. Sohncke together with [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340) and [Franz Ernst Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Ernst_Neumann), the father of Carl Neumann.[^n2] Franz Neumann interested Sohncke for mineralogical problems, and he eventually qualified himself as [private lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) at the university in 1869 with a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) thesis in mineralogy.

Two years later he was claimed as professor of physics to the Polytechnical School in [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe),[^n1] which was the first German [Technische Hochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule) and the preceding institution of today's [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology). He was also appointed head of the meteorological observatory with responsibility for the meteorological service of the [Grand Duchy of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Baden),[^n3][^n1] and started research on meteorology.[^n4] In the academic year 1878/79 Sohncke was elected Director of the Karlsruhe Institute.[^o15] In 1883, he followed a claim to the [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) of [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena) as first director of the new founded institute of physics, after [Emil Warburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Warburg) had declined;[^n5] there he drew his attention mainly to experimental physics.[^o17][^n5] Finally in 1886, he moved to the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), where he worked until his death. Sohncke was a successful academic teacher. His lectures in Jenas had the greatest frequency of students.[^n6]

In 1866, Sohncke married his cousin Elise Berhardi; they had two daughters.[^n1] He died from a kidney disease on 1 November 1897 in Munich.[^n4]

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

*Image: Sohncke Modell.jpg.* One of Sohncke's teaching models for space groups

Leonhard Sohncke wanted to understand the relations between the structure of crystals and their physical properties.[^n7] A major experimental study dealt with the [cohesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohesion_%28chemistry%29) of [rock salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_salt) by measuring its [ultimate tensile strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_tensile_strength) in the different crystallographic directions (1869). He investigated the influence of temperature on the [optical rotation](wikipedia://en/39774) of crystalline material both theoretically and experimentally (1875),[^n8] and found that natural light is affected by electromagnetic forces in the same way as polarized light (1886).[^n9] Leonhard Sohncke is mainly known for his mathematical studies on the structure of crystals that kept him for more than a decade. He combined the 14 [Bravais lattices](wikipedia://en/661808) with the [rotation axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_axis) and the [screw axes](wikipedia://en/3656192) and thereby got the totally 65 [space groups](wikipedia://en/463721) that are commonly called "Sohncke groups", in which [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral) crystal structures form (1879).[^n10] Sohncke discussed thoroughly the results of previous researches on that matter, especially [Auguste Bravais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Bravais) and [Jordan](wikipedia://en/355931).[^n11] This sense for history of science led him write a special publication on [Johann F. C. Hessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_F._C._Hessel) (1891), who had discovered the 32 [crystallographic point groups](wikipedia://en/663786) in 1830, but was nearly completely ignored by his colleagues.[^n12]

Early in Königsberg (1867), he demonstrated a presumption of the Göttingen astronomer [Wilhelm Klinkerfues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Klinkerfues) on an influence of a star's motion to the [refraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refraction) of its light, with consequence of breaking the [Doppler effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_effect), was in error.

In Jena, he researched on [Newton's rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_rings) and [thin-film interference](wikipedia://en/27301235), in cooperation with [Albert Wangerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Heinrich_Albert_Wangerin) in Halle, whom he knew from the Königsberg Seminar of Physics; Wangerin took the theoretical part, and Sohncke the experimental one. They found errors in the previous concepts on this matter; in contrary to preceding research they took into account the thickness of the plane glass and the extension of the source of light.[^o29][^n13][^n14] Other cooperation partners in Jena were [Ernst Abbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Abbe) and [Siegfried Czapski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegfried_Czapski) on the field of optical polarization.[^n15]

Sohncke developed a theory on atmosperical electricity applying [Faraday](wikipedia://en/19727)'s discovery, that friction of water on ice causes static electricity. to the atmosphere.[^n3][^n15]

Sohncke was engaged in the [popularization of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popularization_of_science), and gave general comprehensible lectures, most of them in his Jena and Munich time, some of them were edited in 1892.[^n1][^n16] In Karlsruhe, he was co-founder of the "Oberrheinischer Geologischer Verein" (*Upper Rhenanian Geological Association*).[^o37] In Jena, he was member of the "Medizinisch-naturwissenschaftliche Gesellschaft" (*Association for Medicin and Science*).[^n17] In Munich, his interest in meteorology let him come in contact to the [airship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airship) and [balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon_%28aeronautics%29) community. He was co-founder and chairman of the "Münchener Vereinigung für Luftschiffahrt" (*Munich Association for Airshipping*), and published meteorological results taken from balloon journeys together with [Sebastian Finsterwalder](wikipedia://en/41045441).[^n1] Sohncke and Finsterwalder were the scientific observers during the first balloon night flight of the Munich Association.[^o40]

## Honours {#Honours}

Leonhard Sohncke was awarded the following orders, titles, and memberships of scientific institutions.[^o42]

- Member of the [Royal Bavarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities)[^o43]
- Honorary member of the "Naturwissenschaftlicehr Verein Karlsruhe"
- Member of the "Physikalisch-medizinische Societät Erlangen"
- Honorary doctorate of the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua)
- Knight 1st class, of the [Order of the Zähringer Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Zähringer_Lion) (Grand Duchy of Baden)
- [Order of Saint Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Michael), 4th class (Kingdom of Bavaria)
- Honorary title "[Hofrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofrath)" (Grand Duchy of Baden)
