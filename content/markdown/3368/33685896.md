# Jan Karel Lenstra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jan Karel Lenstra"}

**Jan Karel Lenstra** (born 19 December 1947, in [Zaandam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaandam)) is a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [operations researcher](wikipedia://en/43476), known for his work on scheduling problems, the [travelling salesman problem](wikipedia://en/31248), complexity, approximation, and [local search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_search_%28optimization%29).

:::

Lenstra received his Ph.D. from the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) in 1976, advised by [Gijsbert de Leve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gijsbert_de_Leve).[^o3] He then became a researcher at the [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063) (CWI), where he remained until 1989. After taking positions at the [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology) (where he became Dean of the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science) and the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology), he returned to CWI as its director in 2003. He stepped down in 2011, and at that time became a CWI Fellow.[^o4][^o5] He was editor-in-chief of *[Mathematics of Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_Operations_Research)* from 1993 to 1998, and of *[Operations Research Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research_Letters)* from 2002 to 2021.

Lenstra became an [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFORMS) Fellow in 2004.[^o6] In 1997, he was awarded the [EURO Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EURO_Gold_Medal), the highest distinction within [Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research) in Europe. In 2011, he was made a Knight of the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion),[^o4] and the CWI organized a symposium in his honor.[^o7]

Lenstra was chair of the [Mathematical Optimization Society](wikipedia://en/5751407), of the [Royal Dutch Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/43504918), and of advisory committees of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) on mathematics in primary education and on digital literacy in secondary education. He chaired the committees for the [Spinoza Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinoza_Prize) and the [Stevin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevin_Prize) of the Dutch Science Council.

Lenstra is the brother of [Arjen Lenstra](wikipedia://en/6850292), Andries Lenstra, and [Hendrik Lenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lenstra), all of whom are also mathematicians. His daughter Catrien (1976) is CEO of the City of Amsterdam. He is married to [Karen Aardal](wikipedia://en/59395678), who is professor of optimization at [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology).[^o8] They have two children, Lisa (1999) and Jacob (2003).
