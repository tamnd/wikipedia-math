# Thomas' cyclically symmetric attractor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas' cyclically symmetric attractor"}

*Image: Thomas' cyclically symmetric attractor.png.* Thomas' cyclically symmetric attractor.

In the [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632), **Thomas' cyclically symmetric attractor** is a 3D [strange attractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_attractor) originally proposed by [René Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Thomas_%28biologist%29).[^o2] It has a simple form which is cyclically symmetric in the x, y, and z variables and can be viewed as the trajectory of a frictionally dampened particle moving in a 3D lattice of forces.[^o3] The simple form has made it a popular example.

:::

It is described by the differential equations

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = \sin(y)-bx$$

$$\frac{dy}{dt} = \sin(z)-by$$

$$\frac{dz}{dt} = \sin(x)-bz$$

where $b$ is a constant.

$b$ corresponds to how [dissipative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissipative) the system is, and acts as a [bifurcation](wikipedia://en/2326042) parameter. For $b>1$ the origin is the single stable equilibrium. At $b=1$ it undergoes a [pitchfork bifurcation](wikipedia://en/4850474), splitting into two attractive fixed points. As the parameter is decreased further they undergo a [Hopf bifurcation](wikipedia://en/3626981) at $b\approx 0.32899$, creating a stable limit cycle. The limit cycle then undergoes a [period doubling cascade](wikipedia://en/4457659) and becomes chaotic at $b\approx 0.208186$. Beyond this the attractor expands, undergoing a series of [crises](wikipedia://en/33888206) (up to six separate attractors can coexist for certain values). The [fractal dimension](wikipedia://en/285907) of the attractor increases towards 3.[^o3]

In the limit $b=0$ the system lacks dissipation and the trajectory [ergodically](wikipedia://en/258986) wanders the entire space (with an exception for 1.67%, where it drifts parallel to one of the coordinate axes: this corresponds to [quasiperiodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiperiodic) torii). The dynamics has been described as deterministic fractional [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), and exhibits [anomalous diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalous_diffusion).[^o3][^o4]
