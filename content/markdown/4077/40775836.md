# EdDSA

ECDSA

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="EdDSA"}

In [public-key cryptography](wikipedia://en/24222), **Edwards-curve Digital Signature Algorithm** (**EdDSA**) is a [digital signature](wikipedia://en/59644) scheme using a variant of [Schnorr signature](wikipedia://en/450536) based on [twisted Edwards curves](wikipedia://en/25889726).[^o3] It is designed to be faster than existing digital signature schemes without sacrificing security. It was developed by a team including [Daniel J. Bernstein](wikipedia://en/198983), Niels Duif, [Tanja Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanja_Lange), Peter Schwabe, and [Bo-Yin Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo-Yin_Yang).[^o4] The [reference implementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_implementation) is [public-domain software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public-domain_software).[^o5]

:::

## Summary {#Summary}

The following is a simplified description of EdDSA, ignoring details of encoding integers and curve points as bit strings; the full details are in the papers and RFC.[^o7][^o4][^o3]

An **EdDSA signature scheme** is a choice:[^o7][^o4][^o3]

- of [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbb{F}_q$ over odd prime power $q$;
- of [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/9966) $E$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ whose group $E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ of [$\mathbb{F}_q$-rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) has order $\#E(\mathbb{F}_q) = 2^c \ell$, where $\ell$ is a large prime and $2^c$ is called the cofactor;
- of base point $B \in E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ with order $\ell$; and
- of [cryptographic hash function](wikipedia://en/439526) $H$ with $2b$-bit outputs, where $2^{b - 1} > q$ so that elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$ and curve points in $E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ can be represented by strings of $b$ bits.

These parameters are common to all users of the EdDSA signature scheme. The security of the EdDSA signature scheme depends critically on the choices of parameters, except for the arbitrary choice of base point—for example, [Pollard's rho algorithm for logarithms](wikipedia://en/1630817) is expected to take approximately $\sqrt{\ell\pi/4}$ curve additions before it can compute a discrete logarithm,[^o8] so $\ell$ must be large enough for this to be infeasible, and is typically taken to exceed $2^{200}$.[^o9] The choice of $\ell$ is limited by the choice of $q$, since by [Hasse's theorem](wikipedia://en/1108758), $\#E(\mathbb{F}_q) = 2^c \ell$ cannot differ from $q + 1$ by more than $2\sqrt{q}$. The hash function $H$ is normally modelled as a [random oracle](wikipedia://en/451286) in formal analyses of EdDSA's security.

Within an EdDSA signature scheme,

**Public key**

An EdDSA public key is a curve point $A \in E(\mathbb{F}_q)$, encoded in $b$ bits.

**Signature verification**

An EdDSA signature on a message $M$ by public key $A$ is the pair $(R, S)$, encoded in $2b$ bits, of a curve point $R \in E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ and an integer $0 < S < \ell$ satisfying the following verification equation, where $\parallel$ denotes [concatenation](wikipedia://en/64474):

$$2^c S B = 2^c R + 2^c H(R \parallel A \parallel M) A.$$

**Private key**

An EdDSA private key is a $b$-bit string $k$ which should be chosen uniformly at random. The corresponding public key is $A = s B$, where $s = H_{0,\dots,b - 1}(k)$ is the least significant $b$ bits of $H(k)$ interpreted as an integer in [little-endian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little-endian).

**Signing**

The signature on a message $M$ is deterministically computed as $(R, S),$ where $R = r B$ for $r = H(H_{b,\dots,2b - 1}(k) \parallel M)$, and

$$S \equiv r + H(R \parallel A \parallel M) s \pmod \ell.$$

This satisfies the verification equation

$$\begin{aligned} 2^c S B &= 2^c (r + H(R \parallel A \parallel M) s) B \\ &= 2^c r B + 2^c H(R \parallel A \parallel M) s B \\ &= 2^c R + 2^c H(R \parallel A \parallel M) A. \end{aligned}$$

## Ed25519 {#Ed25519}

**Ed25519** is the EdDSA signature scheme using [SHA-512](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA-512) (SHA-2) and an elliptic curve related to [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444)[^o4] where

- $$q = 2^{255} - 19,$$
- $E/\mathbb{F}_q$ is the [twisted Edwards curve](wikipedia://en/25889726)

$$-x^2 + y^2 = 1 - \frac{121665}{121666}x^2y^2,$$

- $\ell = 2^{252} + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493$ and $c = 3$
- $B$ is the unique point in $E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ whose $y$ coordinate is $4/5$ and whose $x$ coordinate is positive. "positive" is defined in terms of bit-encoding:
  - "positive" coordinates are even coordinates (least significant bit is cleared)
  - "negative" coordinates are odd coordinates (least significant bit is set)
- $H$ is [SHA-512](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA-512), with $b = 256$.

The [twisted Edwards curve](wikipedia://en/25889726) $E/\mathbb{F}_q$ is known as **edwards25519**,[^o12][^o3] and is [birationally equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birationally_equivalent) to the [Montgomery curve](wikipedia://en/25780671) known as [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444). The equivalence is[^o4][^o12][^o13]

$$x = \frac{u}{v}\sqrt{-486664}, \quad y = \frac{u - 1}{u + 1}.$$

### Performance {#Performance}

The original team has optimized Ed25519 for the [x86-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86-64) [Nehalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehalem_%28microarchitecture%29)/[Westmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmere_%28microarchitecture%29) processor family. Verification can be performed in batches of 64 signatures for even greater throughput. Ed25519 is intended to provide attack resistance comparable to quality 128-bit [symmetric ciphers](wikipedia://en/53042).[^o15]

Public keys are 256 bits long and signatures are 512 bits long.[^o16]

### Secure coding {#Secure_coding}

Ed25519 is designed to avoid implementations that use branch conditions or array indices that depend on secret data,[^o4][^o3] in order to mitigate [side-channel attacks](wikipedia://en/667678).

As with other discrete-log-based signature schemes, EdDSA uses a secret value called a [nonce](wikipedia://en/6988390) unique to each signature. In the signature schemes [DSA](wikipedia://en/59470) and [ECDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECDSA), this nonce is traditionally generated randomly for each signature—and if the random number generator is ever broken and predictable when making a signature, the signature can leak the private key, as happened with the [Sony PlayStation 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_PlayStation_3) firmware update signing key.[^o18][^o19][^o20][^o21]

In contrast, EdDSA chooses the nonce deterministically as the hash of a part of the private key and the message. Thus, once a private key is generated, EdDSA has no further need for a random number generator in order to make signatures, and there is no danger that a broken random number generator used to make a signature will reveal the private key.[^o4]

### Standardization and implementation inconsistencies {#Standardization_and_implementation_inconsistencies}

Note that there are two standardization efforts for EdDSA, one from IETF, an informational 8032 and one from NIST as part of FIPS 186-5.[^o45] The differences between the standards have been analyzed,[^o23][^o24] and test vectors are available.[^o25]

### Software {#Software}

Notable uses of Ed25519 include [OpenSSH](wikipedia://en/25490426),[^o27] [GnuPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GnuPG)[^o28] and various alternatives, and the [signify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signify_%28OpenBSD%29) tool by [OpenBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenBSD).[^o29] Usage of Ed25519 (and Ed448) in the SSH protocol has been standardized.[^o30] In 2023 the final version of the [FIPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Information_Processing_Standards) 186-5 standard included deterministic Ed25519 as an approved signature scheme.[^o45]

- [Apple Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Watch) and [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone) use Ed25519 keys for [IKEv2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEv2) mutual authentication[^o31]
- [Botan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botan_%28programming_library%29)
- [Crypto++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto%2B%2B)
- [CryptoNote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CryptoNote) [cryptocurrency](wikipedia://en/36662188) [protocol](wikipedia://en/591994)
- [Dropbear SSH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dropbear_%28software%29)[^o32]
- I2Pd implementation of EdDSA[^o33]
- [Java Development Kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_Development_Kit) 15
- [Libgcrypt](wikipedia://en/29870579)
- Minisign[^o34] and Minisign Miscellanea[^o35] for [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS)
- [NaCl / libsodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NaCl_%28software%29)[^o36]

## Ed448 {#Ed448}

**Ed448** is the EdDSA signature scheme defined in 8032 using the hash function [SHAKE256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHAKE256) and the elliptic curve **edwards448**, an (untwisted) [Edwards curve](wikipedia://en/25505011) related to [Curve448](wikipedia://en/56665655) in 7748. Ed448 has also been approved in the final version of the FIPS 186-5 standard.[^o45]
