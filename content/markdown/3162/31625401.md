# Jay Kappraff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jay Kappraff"}

**Jay Kappraff** was an American author and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [New Jersey Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Institute_of_Technology).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kappraff was trained in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [physical sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_sciences) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), earning a B.Ch.E. in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) at New York Polytechnic in 1958. He went on to be awarded a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in applied mathematics in 1974 from the Courant Institute of Mathematical Science, [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) and a M.S. in chemical engineering in 1960 from [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University). He began work for [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont) DeNemours as a chemical engineer from 1961 to 1962 going on to teach mathematics for a brief period before obtaining a position at [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) as an [aerospace engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineer) from 1962 until 1965. He went on to be an instructor of mathematics at the [Cooper Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union_College), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) from 1968 until 1974.

Following this, he joined the New Jersey Institute of Technology, where he worked until his death. He was a consultant for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy) in 1976. In 1978, he developed a course in the mathematics of design for [computer scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) and [architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architect).[^o6] In bringing together such an [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary) range of subjects, he began to study what he termed a *common language* of design and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).[^o7] He has been a lecturer on the relationship between [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art) and [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) and published a large number or articles on subjects ranging from [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics), [solar heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_heating), [aerospace engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering) and [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals). He has also published a number of books on these and related subjects and compiled a series of video lectures on the science of design.

In 1991, his book *Connections* won a prize for the best book in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and reference from the [Association of American Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Publishers).[^o4]

At the NJIT, Kappraff has organized various forums and tuition programs on subjects from [Nuclear war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_war) and ancient [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) to [experimental mathematics](wikipedia://en/256363). he was a member of the faculty council and chairman of the NJIT [Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology) and [Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society) Forum committee. He is a member of the [Mathematics Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_Association_of_America) and on the editorial board of a new interdisciplinary journal, the *International Journal of Biological Systems*.
