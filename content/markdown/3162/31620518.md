# John Rhodes (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Rhodes"}

**John Lewis Rhodes** (born July 16, 1937) is an American mathematician known for work in the theory of [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799), [finite-state automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-state_automata), and algebraic approaches to [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rhodes was born in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio), on July 16, 1937, and grew up in [Wooster, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooster,_Ohio), where he founded the Wooster Rocket Society as a teenager. In the fall of 1955, Rhodes entered the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) intending to major in physics, but he soon switched to mathematics, earning his B.S. in 1960 and his Ph.D. in 1962. His Ph.D. thesis, co-written with a graduate student from Harvard, Kenneth Krohn, became known as the Prime Decomposition Theorem, or more simply the [Krohn–Rhodes Theorem](wikipedia://en/513506).[^o6] After a year on an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) fellowship in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), he became a member of the Department of Mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he spent his entire teaching career.

In the late 1960s, Rhodes wrote *Applications of Automata Theory and Algebra: Via the Mathematical Theory of Complexity to Biology, Physics, Psychology, Philosophy, and Games*, informally known as *The Wild Book*,[^o7] which quickly became an underground classic, but remained in typescript until its revision and editing by [Chrystopher L. Nehaniv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrystopher_L._Nehaniv) in 2009.[^o8] The following year Springer published his and his student Benjamin Steinberg's book, *The q-Theory of Finite Semigroups*, both a history of the field and the fruit of eight years' development of finite semigroup theory.[^o9]

In recent years Rhodes brought semigroups into [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory). In 2015, he published, with Pedro V. Silva, the results of his current work in another monograph with Springer, *Boolean Representations of Simplicial Complexes and Matroids*.[^o10]
