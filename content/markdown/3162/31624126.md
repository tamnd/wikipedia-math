# Aleksandr Khazanov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aleksandr Khazanov"}

**Aleksandr Leonidovich Khazanov** (May 4, 1979 – disappeared June 10, 2001) is a [Russian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). A [child prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy), he wrote a perfect paper at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) 1994,[^o3] [one of the youngest ever to do so](wikipedia://en/15712699#Exceptionally_young_medalists). Khazanov was reported missing in June 2001.[^o4] He suffered from depression or bipolar disorder at the time of his disappearance.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born to Anna and Leonid Khazanov, a math professor, Aleksandr had attended a special school for math students several years older in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (now [Saint Petersburg, Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg,_Russia)), before his family fled [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) threats and moved to [Brooklyn, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn,_New_York), United States as refugees in 1992. He attended [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) and showed his exceptional talent in mathematics. In the summer of 1994, he passed qualifying exams for [Penn State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_University)'s doctoral program in math, and he was the youngest member on the United States team for the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) of which all six members received perfect scores. In the following year, he was named a finalist and eventually placed 7th at the 54th [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search) for a paper dealing with a variant of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953), mentored by [Leonid Vaserstein](wikipedia://en/16636593), a math professor at Penn State University, and also from Russia.[^o6][^o7] He represented the United States for the second time in the 1995 International Mathematical Olympiad, and he entered the PhD math program at [Penn State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_University) right after high school in fall 1995.[^o8][^o9]

## Disappearance {#Disappearance}

In the morning of June 10, 2001, he left home with his mountain bike and went missing, leaving a note in Russian saying "I went to a library." He was taking a semester leave due to personal problems, and had been taking drugs for depression, but he did not bring them when he left home.[^o8][^o4]
