# Killing tensor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Killing tensor"}

In mathematics, a **Killing tensor** or **Killing tensor field** is a generalization of a [Killing vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_vector), for [symmetric](wikipedia://en/23087738) [tensor fields](wikipedia://en/256738) instead of just [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641). It is a concept in [Riemannian](wikipedia://en/195243) and [pseudo-Riemannian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Riemannian_geometry), and is mainly used in the theory of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). Killing tensors satisfy an equation similar to Killing's equation for Killing vectors. Like Killing vectors, every Killing tensor corresponds to a quantity which is conserved along [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096). However, unlike Killing vectors, which are associated with symmetries ([isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometries)) of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/287555), Killing tensors generally lack such a direct geometric interpretation. Killing tensors are named after [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and properties"}

## Definition and properties {#Definition_and_properties}

In the following definition, parentheses around tensor indices are notation for symmetrization. For example:

$$T_{(\alpha\beta\gamma)} = \frac{1}{6}(T_{\alpha\beta\gamma} + T_{\alpha\gamma\beta} + T_{\beta\alpha\gamma} + T_{\beta\gamma\alpha} + T_{\gamma\alpha\beta} + T_{\gamma\beta\alpha})$$

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

A Killing tensor is a [tensor field](wikipedia://en/256738) $K$ (of some order *m*) on a [(pseudo)-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/287555) which is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/23087738) (that is, $K_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m} = K_{(\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m)}$) and satisfies:[^o6][^o7]

$$\nabla_{(\alpha}K_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m)} = 0$$

This equation is a generalization of Killing's equation for [Killing vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_vector):

$$\nabla_{(\alpha}K_{\beta)} = \frac{1}{2} (\nabla_{\alpha}K_{\beta} + \nabla_{\beta}K_{\alpha}) = 0$$

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

Killing vectors are a special case of Killing tensors. Another simple example of a Killing tensor is the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) itself. A [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of Killing tensors is a Killing tensor. A symmetric product of Killing tensors is also a Killing tensor; that is, if $S_{\alpha_1 \cdots \alpha_l}$ and $T_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m}$ are Killing tensors, then $S_{(\alpha_1 \cdots \alpha_l}T_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m)}$ is a Killing tensor too.[^o6]

Every Killing tensor corresponds to a [constant of motion](wikipedia://en/5391037) on [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096). More specifically, for every geodesic with tangent vector $u^\alpha$, the quantity $K_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m} u^{\beta_1} \cdots u^{\beta_m}$ is constant along the geodesic.[^o6][^o7]

## Examples {#Examples}

Since Killing tensors are a generalization of Killing vectors, the examples at Killing vector field are also examples of Killing tensors. The following examples focus on Killing tensors not simply obtained from Killing vectors.

### FLRW metric {#FLRW_metric}

The [Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric](wikipedia://en/343031), widely used in [cosmology](wikipedia://en/5378), has spacelike Killing vectors corresponding to its spatial symmetries, in particular rotations around arbitrary axes and in the flat case for $k=1$ translations along $x$, $y$, and $z$. It also has a Killing tensor

$$K_{\mu\nu} = a^2 (g_{\mu\nu} + U_{\mu}U_{\nu})$$

where *a* is the [scale factor](wikipedia://en/921168), $U^{\mu} = (1,0,0,0)$ is the *t*-coordinate [basis vector](wikipedia://en/18420), and the −+++ [signature](wikipedia://en/463225) convention is used.[^o13]

### Kerr metric {#Kerr_metric}

The [Kerr metric](wikipedia://en/456715), describing a rotating black hole, has two independent Killing vectors. One Killing vector corresponds to the [time translation symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_translation_symmetry) of the metric, and another corresponds to the [axial symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673) about the axis of rotation. In addition, as shown by Walker and [Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193) (1970), there is a nontrivial Killing tensor of order 2.[^o15][^o16][^o17] The constant of motion corresponding to this Killing tensor is called the [Carter constant](wikipedia://en/21761927).

## Conformal Killing tensor {#Conformal_Killing_tensor}

**Conformal Killing tensors** are a generalization of Killing tensors and [conformal Killing vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_Killing_vector). A conformal Killing tensor is a tensor field $K$ (of some order *m*) which is symmetric and satisfies[^o15]

$$\nabla_{(\alpha}K_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m)} = k_{(\beta_1 \cdots \beta_{m-1}} g_{\beta_m \alpha)}$$

for some symmetric tensor field $k$. This generalizes the equation for conformal Killing vectors, which states that

$$\nabla_\alpha K_\beta + \nabla_\beta K_\alpha = \lambda g_{\alpha \beta}$$

for some scalar field $\lambda$.

Every conformal Killing tensor corresponds to a constant of motion along [null geodesics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_geodesic). More specifically, for every null geodesic with tangent vector $v^\alpha$, the quantity $K_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m} v^{\beta_1} \cdots v^{\beta_m}$ is constant along the geodesic.[^o15]

The property of being a conformal Killing tensor is preserved under conformal transformations in the following sense. If $K_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m}$ is a conformal Killing tensor with respect to a metric $g_{\alpha \beta}$, then $\tilde{K}_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m} = u^m K_{\beta_1 \cdots \beta_m}$ is a conformal Killing tensor with respect to the conformally equivalent metric $\tilde{g}_{\alpha \beta} = u g_{\alpha \beta}$, for all positive-valued $u$.[^o20]

:::

## Killing–Yano tensor {#Killing–Yano_tensor}

An [antisymmetric tensor](wikipedia://en/696955) of order *p*, $f_{a_1 a_2 ... a_p}$, is a Killing–Yano tensor fr:Tenseur de Killing-Yano if it satisfies the equation

$$\nabla_b f_{c a_2 ... a_p} + \nabla_c f_{b a_2 ... a_p} = 0\,.$$

While also a generalization of the [Killing vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_vector), it differs from the usual Killing tensor in that the [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) is only contracted with one tensor index.

Killing–Yano tensors are the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of Killing tensors because of satisfying certain theorems[^o22] which are put below:

- For a Killing–Yano tensor, $f_{\mu_1\ldots\mu_p}$, the Killing tensor of rank 2 is $k_{\mu\nu} = f_{\mu\mu_2\ldots\mu_p} f_\nu^{{\mu_2}\ldots\mu_p}$
- $f_{\mu\mu_2\ldots\mu_p} p^{\mu_p}$ is parallel transported along the geodesic with tangent vector, $p^{\mu_p}$

**Conformal Killing–Yano tensors** are a generalization of Killing–Yano tensors.[^o22] It states that a Conformal Killing–Yano tensor of rank *p* is totally antisymmetric tensor $k_{\mu_2...\mu_p}$ with p-form if it fulfills:

$$\nabla_\mu k_{\mu_1\ldots\mu_p} = \nabla_{[\mu k_{\mu_1\ldots\mu_p}]} +\, }\bar{ p\, g_{\mu[\mu_1} {k}_{\mu_2\ldots\mu_p]}$$

where $\bar k_{\mu_2\ldots\mu_p}$ is an asymmetrical tensor of rank *p - 1.* By doing a contraction of $\mu$ and $\mu_1$, we get:

$$\bar k_{\mu_2\ldots\mu_p} = \frac 1 {D - p + 1} \nabla_\mu k_{\mu_2\ldots\mu_p}^\mu$$

**Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensors** are a special case of Conformal Killing–Yano tensor when $\nabla_\mu k_{\mid{\mu_2\ldots\mu_p}\mid} = 0$ where $k = db$ and *b* is some p - 1 form.[^o22] This follows the [Hodge duality](wikipedia://en/310914) transformation result which is:

The Hodge dual $k\star$ of a rank *p* Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor $k$ is a Killing–Yano tensor $f \equiv \star k$ of rank *D - p* and vice-versa.

An important property of Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor is that their [wedge product](wikipedia://en/221537) is a Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor of higher rank. In other words, $k \equiv a \land b$ is a Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor of rank p + q, where $a$ is a Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor of rank p and $b$ is a Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor of rank q.

::: {#o24 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

#### Kerr spacetime {#Kerr_spacetime}

A particle moving in Kerr [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) is closely related to Conformal Killing–Yano tensors of rank two. There is one solution for a rotating blackhole described by Kerr Metric in which the asymptomatic tensor looks like as follows:[^o26]

$$Y = r^3 sin\,\theta\,d\theta \,\land d\phi + O(1) = \star(\tau_0 \land D)$$

where $D$ is a dilation vector field with value $x^\mu {\partial\over\partial x^\mu}$ and $\tau$ is a Killing field with value ${\partial\over\partial x^\mu}$

#### Killing–Yano towers {#Killing–Yano_towers}

From the wedge product property of Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensors, many Conformal Killing–Yano tensors can be constructed which is known as Killing–Yano tensor tower. For a $n^{th}$ Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor, the Killing–Yano tensor tower is defined as:

$$h^{(j)} \equiv h^{\land j} = \land_{n=1}^j h$$

where $h^{(j)}$ is a Closed Conformal Killing–Yano tensor of rank $2j$.[^o22]

#### Bosonic and spinning string {#Bosonic_and_spinning_string}

In the invariances of tensionless [Bosonic string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosonic_string_theory), the expression for the field equation for tension in the string vanishes when $K$ is a killing vector.[^o29]

$$\nabla_{(\mu} K_{\nu)} = \lambda G_{\mu\nu}$$

The [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) of the tensionless spinning strings also involves super conformal Killing–Yano tensors.[^o29]

#### Supersymmetries {#Supersymmetries}

For a bosonic particle falling in a geodesic background, the [supersymmetric](wikipedia://en/224636) transformation with respect to Killing–Yano tensor can be derived.[^o31] One such supersymmetry transform equation is as follows:

$$\delta x^\mu = -i \in \xi^\mu$$

#### G-Structures {#G-Structures}

Killing–Yano tensors are also studied in [G-Structures](wikipedia://en/1277699) which are used in constructing [supergravity](wikipedia://en/432632) solutions and also in [holonomy manifolds](wikipedia://en/644921).[^o31]
