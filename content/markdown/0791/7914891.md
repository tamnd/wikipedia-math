# Hitchin functional

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hitchin functional"}

The **Hitchin functional** is a [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) concept with applications in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) that was introduced by the British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164). (Hitchin 2000) and (Hitchin 2001) are the original articles of the Hitchin functional.

:::

As with Hitchin's introduction of [generalized complex manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_complex_manifold), this is an example of a mathematical tool found useful in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

This is the definition for 6-manifolds. The definition in Hitchin's article is more general, but more abstract.[^o6]

Let $M$ be a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriented) 6-[manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with trivial [canonical bundle](wikipedia://en/734893). Then the **Hitchin functional** is a [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) on [3-forms](wikipedia://en/221519) defined by the formula:

$$\Phi(\Omega) = \int_M \Omega \wedge * \Omega,$$

where $\Omega$ is a 3-form and \* denotes the [Hodge star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_star) operator.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The Hitchin functional is analogous for six-manifold to the [Yang-Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang-Mills) functional for the four-manifolds.
- The Hitchin functional is manifestly [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) under the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of orientation-preserving [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564).
- **Theorem.** Suppose that $M$ is a three-dimensional [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) and $\Omega$ is the real part of a non-vanishing [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) 3-form, then $\Omega$ is a [critical point](wikipedia://en/1951424) of the functional $\Phi$ restricted to the [cohomology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_class) $[\Omega] \in H^3(M,R)$. Conversely, if $\Omega$ is a critical point of the functional $\Phi$ in a given comohology class and $\Omega \wedge * \Omega < 0$, then $\Omega$ **defines** the structure of a complex manifold, such that $\Omega$ is the real part of a non-vanishing holomorphic 3-form on $M$.

The proof of the theorem in Hitchin's articles (txt) and (txt) is relatively straightforward. The power of this concept is in the converse statement: if the exact form $\Phi(\Omega)$ is known, we only have to look at its critical points to find the possible complex structures.

:::

## Stable forms {#Stable_forms}

Action functionals often determine geometric structure[^o12] on $M$ and geometric structure are often characterized by the existence of particular differential forms on $M$ that obey some integrable conditions.

If an *2*-form $\omega$ can be written with local coordinates

$$\omega=dp_1\wedge dq_1+\cdots+dp_m\wedge dq_m$$

and

$$d\omega=0,$$

then $\omega$ defines *[symplectic structure](wikipedia://en/28356)*.

A *p*-form $\omega\in\Omega^p(M,\mathbb{R})$ is *stable* if it lies in an open orbit of the local $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$ action where n=dim(M), namely if any small perturbation $\omega\mapsto\omega+\delta\omega$ can be undone by a local $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$ action. So any *1*-form that don't vanish everywhere is stable; *2*-form (or *p*-form when *p* is even) stability is equivalent to non-degeneracy.

What about *p*=3? For large *n* *3*-form is difficult because the dimension of $\wedge^3(\mathbb{R}^n)$, is of the order of $n^3$, grows more fastly than the dimension of $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$ which is $n^2$. But there are some very lucky exceptional case, namely, $n=6$, when dim $\wedge^3(\mathbb{R}^6)=20$, dim $GL(6,\mathbb{R})=36$. Let $\rho$ be a stable real *3*-form in dimension *6*. Then the stabilizer of $\rho$ under $GL(6,\mathbb{R})$ has real dimension *36-20=16*, in fact either $SL(3,\mathbb{R})\times SL(3,\mathbb{R})$ or $SL(3,\mathbb{C})$.

Focus on the case of $SL(3,\mathbb{C})$ and if $\rho$ has a stabilizer in $SL(3,\mathbb{C})$ then it can be written with local coordinates as follows:

$$\rho=\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_1\wedge\zeta_2\wedge\zeta_3+\bar{\zeta_1}\wedge\bar{\zeta_2}\wedge\bar{\zeta_3})$$

where $\zeta_1=e_1+ie_2,\zeta_2=e_3+ie_4,\zeta_3=e_5+ie_6$ and $e_i$ are bases of $T^*M$. Then $\zeta_i$ determines an [almost complex structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure) on $M$. Moreover, if there exist local coordinate $(z_1,z_2,z_3)$ such that $\zeta_i=dz_i$ then it determines fortunately a [complex structure](wikipedia://en/509742) on $M$.

Given the stable $\rho\in\Omega^3(M,\mathbb{R})$:

$$\rho=\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_1\wedge\zeta_2\wedge\zeta_3+\bar{\zeta_1}\wedge\bar{\zeta_2}\wedge\bar{\zeta_3}).$$

We can define another real *3*-from

$$\tilde{\rho}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_1\wedge\zeta_2\wedge\zeta_3-\bar{\zeta_1}\wedge\bar{\zeta_2}\wedge\bar{\zeta_3}).$$

And then $\Omega=\rho+i\tilde{\rho}(\rho)$ is a holomorphic *3*-form in the almost complex structure determined by $\rho$. Furthermore, it becomes to be the complex structure just if $d\Omega=0$ i.e. $d\rho=0$ and $d\tilde{\rho}(\rho)=0$. This $\Omega$ is just the *3*-form $\Omega$ in formal definition of *Hitchin functional*. These idea induces the [generalized complex structure](wikipedia://en/6871218).

## Use in string theory {#Use_in_string_theory}

Hitchin functionals arise in many areas of string theory. An example is the [compactifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_%28physics%29) of the 10-dimensional string with a subsequent [orientifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientifold) projection $\kappa$ using an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) $\nu$. In this case, $M$ is the internal 6 (real) dimensional [Calabi-Yau space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabi-Yau_space). The couplings to the complexified [Kähler coordinates](wikipedia://en/390538) $\tau$ is given by

$$g_{ij} = \tau \text{im} \int \tau i^*(\nu \cdot \kappa \tau).$$

The potential function is the functional $V[J] = \int J \wedge J \wedge J$, where J is the [almost complex structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure). Both are Hitchin functionals.(Grimm Louis 2005)

As application to string theory, the famous OSV conjecture (Ooguri Strominger Vafa 2004) used *Hitchin functional* in order to relate topological string to 4-dimensional black hole entropy. Using similar technique in the $G_2$ holonomy (Dijkgraaf Gukov Neitzke Vafa 2005) argued about [topological M-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_string_theory#Topological_M-theory) and in the $Spin(7)$ [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) topological [F-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-theory) might be argued.

More recently, [E. Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Witten) claimed the mysterious superconformal field theory in six dimensions, called [6D (2,0) superconformal field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6D_%282,0%29_superconformal_field_theory) (Witten 2007). Hitchin functional gives one of the bases of it.
