# Structurable algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Structurable algebra"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **structurable algebra** is a certain kind of unital [involutive](wikipedia://en/293450) [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/6533841) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). For example, all [Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371) are structurable algebras (with the trivial involution), as is any [alternative algebra](wikipedia://en/3160) with involution, or any [central simple algebra](wikipedia://en/404082) with involution. An *involution* here means a linear anti-homomorphism whose square is the identity.[^o2]

:::

Assume *A* is a unital non-associative [algebra over a field](wikipedia://en/191788), and $x \mapsto \bar{x}$ is an involution. If we define $V_{x,y}z:=(x\bar{y})z+(z\bar{y})x-(z\bar{x})y$, and $[x,y]=xy-yx$, then we say *A* is a *structurable algebra* if:[^o3]

$$[V_{x,y} , V_{z,w}] = V_{V_{x,y}z,w} - V_{z,V_{y,x}w}.$$

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

Structurable algebras were introduced by Allison in 1978.[^o6] The [Kantor–Koecher–Tits construction](wikipedia://en/30230650) produces a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) from any [Jordan algebra](wikipedia://en/936371), and this construction can be generalized so that a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) can be produced from a structurable algebra. Moreover, Allison proved over fields of characteristic zero that a structurable algebra is central simple if and only if the corresponding Lie algebra is central simple.[^o2]

Another example of a structurable algebra is a 56-dimensional non-associative algebra originally studied by Brown in 1963, which can be constructed out of an [Albert algebra](wikipedia://en/9153180).[^o7] When the base field is algebraically closed over characteristic not 2 or 3, the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of such an algebra has [identity component](wikipedia://en/1088787) equal to the simply connected exceptional [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) of type [E<sub>6</sub>](wikipedia://en/293725).[^o8]
