# Friedrich Schilling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Friedrich Schilling"}

*Image: Friedrich Schilling.jpg.* Friedrich Schilling

**Friedrich Georg Schilling** (9 April 1868, [Hildesheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildesheim) – 25 May 1950, [Gladbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladbeck)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

From 1887 Schilling studied mathematics at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he received his doctorate in 1893. His doctoral thesis *Beiträge zur geometrischen Theorie der Schwarzschen s-Funktion* (Contributions to the geometric theory of the Schwarz s-function) was supervised by [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887). At the University of Göttingen, Schilling was from 1891 to 1893 an assistant for the physical model and instrument collection. He habilitated in 1896 in Aachen and was, from August 1897 to April 1899, an adjunct professor ([*außerplanmäßiger Professor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_Germany)) at the [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology). From 1899 he was an adjunct professor at the University of Göttingen, where he taught [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891) and oversaw the collection of mathematical equipment. In 1904 he became a professor at the [TH Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdańsk_University_of_Technology), where he was rector from 1917 to 1919. He retired in 1936.

In his dissertation, he developed a new interpretation of the formulas of [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405) as a relationship between the invariants of three quadratic forms and their functional determinants.[^o3] Schilling's theory was presented by Felix Klein in his lectures on [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223).[^o4]

Schilling also did research on [Reuleaux tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/5158942).

He took notes on and edited the lectures on higher geometry by Felix Klein from 1892/93, which were initially distributed in [autographed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autographed) form. In 1926 Felix Klein's book *Vorlesungen über nichteuklidische Geometrie* (Lectures on non-Euclidean geometry) was published posthumously by Springer Verlag.[^o5] Schilling himself wrote several books on non-Euclidean geometry, which were strongly influenced by his geometric intuition. Felix Klein and Friedrich Schilling also designed geometric models that were manufactured by the Martin Schilling company in Leipzig.[^o6][^o7]

In 1927 Friedrich Schilling was president of the *[Deutsche Mathematiker-Vereinigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mathematiker-Vereinigung)*. In November 1933, he signed the [*Bekenntnis der Professoren an den deutschen Universitäten und Hochschulen zu Adolf Hitler*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State).[^o8]
