# LEA (cipher)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="LEA"}

The **Lightweight Encryption Algorithm** (also known as **LEA**) is a 128-bit [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) developed by [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea) in 2013 to provide confidentiality in high-speed environments such as [big data](wikipedia://en/27051151) and [cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing), as well as lightweight environments such as [IoT devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_things) and [mobile devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_computing).[^o3] LEA has three different key lengths: 128, 192, and 256 bits. LEA encrypts data about 1.5 to 2 times faster than [AES](wikipedia://en/1260), the most widely used block cipher in various software environments.

:::

LEA is one of the cryptographic algorithms approved by the Korean Cryptographic Module Validation Program (KCMVP) and is the national standard of Republic of Korea (KS X 3246). LEA is included in the ISO/IEC 29192-2:2019 standard ([Information security](wikipedia://en/15036) - Lightweight cryptography - Part 2: Block ciphers).

## Specification {#Specification}

The block cipher LEA consisting of ARX operations (modular Addition: $\boxplus$, bitwise Rotation: $\lll$, $\ggg$, and bitwise XOR $\oplus$) for 32-bit words processes data blocks of 128 bits and has three different key lengths: 128, 192, and 256 bits. LEA with a 128-bit key, LEA with a 192-bit key, and LEA with a 256-bit key are referred to as “LEA-128”, “LEA-192”, and “LEA-256”, respectively. The number of rounds is 24 for LEA-128, 28 for LEA-192, and 32 for LEA-256.

### Encryption {#Encryption}

Let $P = P[0] \| P[1] \| P[2] \| P[3]$ be a 128-bit block of plaintext and $C = C[0] \| C[1] \| C[2] \| C[3]$ be a 128-bit block of ciphertext, where $P[i]$ and $C[i]$ ($0 \le i < 4$) are 32-bit blocks. Let $K_{i} = K_{i}[0] \| K_{i}[1] \| K_{i}[2] \| K_{i}[3] \| K_{i}[4] \| K_{i}[5]$ ($0 \le i < Nr$) be 192-bit round keys, where $K_{i}[j]$ ($0 \le j < 6$) are 32-bit blocks. Here $Nr$ is the number of rounds for the LEA algorithm. The encryption operation is described as follows:

1. $$X_{0}[0] \| X_{0}[1] \| X_{0}[2] \| X_{0}[3] \leftarrow P[0] \| P[1] \| P[2] \| P[3]$$
1. for $i=0$ to $Nr - 1$
   1. $$X_{i+1}[0] \leftarrow \left( \left( X_{i}[0] \oplus K_{i}[0] \right) \boxplus \left( X_{i}[1] \oplus K_{i}[1] \right) \right) \lll 9$$
   1. $$X_{i+1}[1] \leftarrow \left( \left( X_{i}[1] \oplus K_{i}[2] \right) \boxplus \left( X_{i}[2] \oplus K_{i}[3] \right) \right) \ggg 5$$
   1. $$X_{i+1}[2] \leftarrow \left( \left( X_{i}[2] \oplus K_{i}[4] \right) \boxplus \left( X_{i}[3] \oplus K_{i}[5] \right) \right) \ggg 3$$
   1. $$X_{i+1}[3] \leftarrow X_{i}[0]$$
1. $$C[0] \| C[1] \| C[2] \| C[3] \leftarrow X_{Nr}[0] \| X_{Nr}[1] \| X_{Nr}[2] \| X_{Nr}[3]$$

### Decryption {#Decryption}

The decryption operation is as follows:

1. $$X_{Nr}[0] \| X_{Nr}[1] \| X_{Nr}[2] \| X_{Nr}[3] \leftarrow C[0] \| C[1] \| C[2] \| C[3]$$
1. for $i=(Nr-1)$ down to $0$
   1. $$X_{i}[0] \leftarrow X_{i+1}[3]$$
   1. $$X_{i}[1] \leftarrow \left( \left( X_{i+1}[0] \ggg 9 \right) \boxminus \left( X_{i}[0] \oplus K_{i}[0] \right) \right) \oplus K_{i}[1]$$
   1. $$X_{i}[2] \leftarrow \left( \left( X_{i+1}[1] \lll 5 \right) \boxminus \left( X_{i}[1] \oplus K_{i}[2] \right) \right) \oplus K_{i}[3]$$
   1. $$X_{i}[3] \leftarrow \left( \left( X_{i+1}[2] \lll 3 \right) \boxminus \left( X_{i}[2] \oplus K_{i}[4] \right) \right) \oplus K_{i}[5]$$
1. $$P[0] \| P[1] \| P[2] \| P[3] \leftarrow X_{0}[0] \| X_{0}[1] \| X_{0}[2] \| X_{0}[3]$$

### Key schedule {#Key_schedule}

The key schedule of LEA supports 128, 192, and 256-bit keys and outputs 192-bit round keys $K_{i}$ ($0 \le i < Nr$) for the data processing part.

#### Key schedule for LEA-128 {#Key_schedule_for_LEA-128}

Let $K = K[0] \| K[1] \| K[2] \| K[3]$ be a 128-bit key, where $K[i]$ ($0 \le i < 4$) are 32-bit blocks. The key schedule for LEA-128 takes $K$ and four 32-bit constants $\delta[i]$ ($0 \le i < 4$) as inputs and outputs twenty-four 192-bit round keys $K_{i}$ ($0 \le i < 24$). The key schedule operation for LEA-128 is as follows:

1. $$T[0] \| T[1] \| T[2] \| T[3] \leftarrow K[0] \| K[1] \| K[2] \| K[3]$$
1. for $i=0$ to $23$
   1. $$T[0] \leftarrow \left( T[0] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 4] \lll i \right) \right) \lll 1$$
   1. $$T[1] \leftarrow \left( T[1] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 4] \lll \left( i + 1 \right) \right) \right) \lll 3$$
   1. $$T[2] \leftarrow \left( T[2] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 4] \lll \left( i + 2 \right) \right) \right) \lll 6$$
   1. $$T[3] \leftarrow \left( T[3] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 4] \lll \left( i + 3 \right) \right) \right) \lll 11$$
   1. $$K_{i} \leftarrow T[0] \| T[1] \| T[2] \| T[1] \| T[3] \| T[1]$$

#### Key schedule for LEA-192 {#Key_schedule_for_LEA-192}

Let $K = K[0] \| K[1] \| K[2] \| K[3] \| K[4] \| K[5]$ be a 192-bit key, where $K[i]$ ($0 \le i < 6$) are 32-bit blocks. The key schedule for LEA-192 takes $K$ and six 32-bit constants $\delta[i]$ ($0 \le i < 6$) as inputs and outputs twenty-eight 192-bit round keys $K_{i}$ ($0 \le i < 28$). The key schedule operation for LEA-192 is as follows:

1. $$T[0] \| T[1] \| T[2] \| T[3] \| T[4] \| T[5] \leftarrow K[0] \| K[1] \| K[2] \| K[3] \| K[4] \| K[5]$$
1. for $i=0$ to $27$
   1. $$T[0] \leftarrow \left( T[0] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 6] \lll i \right) \right) \lll 1$$
   1. $$T[1] \leftarrow \left( T[1] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 6] \lll \left( i + 1 \right) \right) \right) \lll 3$$
   1. $$T[2] \leftarrow \left( T[2] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 6] \lll \left( i + 2 \right) \right) \right) \lll 6$$
   1. $$T[3] \leftarrow \left( T[3] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 6] \lll \left( i + 3 \right) \right) \right) \lll 11$$
   1. $$T[4] \leftarrow \left( T[4] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 6] \lll \left( i + 4 \right) \right) \right) \lll 13$$
   1. $$T[5] \leftarrow \left( T[5] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 6] \lll \left( i + 5 \right) \right) \right) \lll 17$$
   1. $$K_{i} \leftarrow T[0] \| T[1] \| T[2] \| T[3] \| T[4] \| T[5]$$

#### Key schedule for LEA-256 {#Key_schedule_for_LEA-256}

Let $K = K[0] \| K[1] \| K[2] \| K[3] \| K[4] \| K[5] \| K[6] \| K[7]$ be a 256-bit key, where $K[i]$ ($0 \le i < 8$) are 32-bit blocks. The key schedule for LEA-192 takes $K$ and eight 32-bit constants $\delta[i]$ ($0 \le i < 8$) as inputs and outputs thirty-two 192-bit round keys $K_{i}$ ($0 \le i < 32$). The key schedule operation for LEA-256 is as follows:

1. $$T[0] \| T[1] \| T[2] \| T[3] \| T[4] \| T[5] \| T[6] \| T[7] \leftarrow K[0] \| K[1] \| K[2] \| K[3] \| K[4] \| K[5] \| K[6] \| K[7]$$
1. for $i=0$ to $31$
   1. $$T[6i \mod 8] \leftarrow \left( T[6i \mod 8] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 8] \lll i \right) \right) \lll 1$$
   1. $$T[6i + 1 \mod 8] \leftarrow \left( T[6i + 1 \mod 8] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 8] \lll \left( i + 1 \right) \right) \right) \lll 3$$
   1. $$T[6i + 2 \mod 8] \leftarrow \left( T[6i + 2 \mod 8] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 8] \lll \left( i + 2 \right) \right) \right) \lll 6$$
   1. $$T[6i + 3 \mod 8] \leftarrow \left( T[6i + 3 \mod 8] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 8] \lll \left( i + 3 \right) \right) \right) \lll 11$$
   1. $$T[6i + 4 \mod 8] \leftarrow \left( T[6i + 4 \mod 8] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 8] \lll \left( i + 4 \right) \right) \right) \lll 13$$
   1. $$T[6i + 5 \mod 8] \leftarrow \left( T[6i + 5 \mod 8] \boxplus \left( \delta[i \mod 8] \lll \left( i + 5 \right) \right) \right) \lll 17$$
   1. $$K_{i} \leftarrow T[6i \mod 8] \| T[6i + 1 \mod 8] \| T[6i + 2 \mod 8] \| T[6i + 3 \mod 8] \| T[6i + 4 \mod 8] \| T[6i + 5 \mod 8]$$

## Security {#Security}

As of 2019, no successful attack on full-round LEA is known. As is typical for iterated block ciphers, reduced-round variants have been attacked. The best published attacks on LEA in the standard attack model (CPA/CCA with unknown key) are boomerang attacks and differential linear attacks. The security margin to the whole rounds ratio is greater than 37% against various existing cryptanalytic techniques for block ciphers.

*Security of LEA-128 (24 rounds)*

::: {#o14 .table cue=table name="Security of LEA-128 (24 rounds)"}

|---|---|

| Truncated differential[^o15] | 14 |
| Linear[^o3] | 13 |
| Zero correlation[^o3] | 10 |
| Boomerang[^o3] | 15 |
| Impossible differential[^o3] | 12 |
| Integral[^o3] | 9 |
| Differential linear[^o3] | 15 |
| Related-key differential[^o3] | 13 |

:::

## Performance {#Performance}

LEA has very good performance in a general-purpose software environment. In particular, it is possible to encrypt at a rate of about 1.5 to 2 times on average, compared to AES, the most widely used block cipher in various software environments. ## Implementations {#Implementations}

LEA is free for any use: public or private, commercial or non-commercial. The source code for distribution of LEA implemented in [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29), [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29), and [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) can be downloaded from KISA's website.[^o25] In addition, LEA is contained in Crypto++ library, a free [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++) class library of cryptographic schemes.[^o26]

:::

## KCMVP {#KCMVP}

LEA is one of the cryptographic algorithms approved by the Korean Cryptographic Module Validation Program (KCMVP).[^o28]
