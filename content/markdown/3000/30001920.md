# Random modulation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Random modulation"}

In the theories of [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation) and of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), **random modulation** is the creation of a new signal from two other signals by the process of [quadrature amplitude modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrature_amplitude_modulation). In particular, the two signals are considered as being [random processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_process). For applications, the two original signals need have a limited frequency range, and these are used to modulate a third sinusoidal [carrier signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_signal) whose frequency is above the range of frequencies contained in the original signals.

:::

## Details {#Details}

The random modulation procedure starts with two stochastic [baseband signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseband_signal#Baseband_signal), $x_c(t)$ and $x_s(t)$, whose [frequency spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_spectrum) is non-zero only for $f \in [-B/2,B/2]$. It applies [quadrature modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrature_modulation) to combine these with a carrier frequency $f_0$ (with $f_0 > B/2$) to form the signal $x(t)$ given by

$$x(t)=x_c(t)\cos(2 \pi f_0 t)-x_s(t)\sin(2 \pi f_0 t)= \Re \left \{ \underline{x}(t)e^{j 2 \pi f_0 t}\right \} ,$$

where $\underline{x}(t)$ is the [equivalent baseband representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseband_signal#Equivalent_baseband_signal) of the modulated signal $x(t)$

$$\underline{x}(t)=x_c(t)+j x_s(t).$$

In the following it is assumed that $x_c(t)$ and $x_s(t)$ are two real jointly [wide sense stationary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_sense_stationary#Weak_or_wide-sense_stationarity) processes. It can be shown that the new signal $x(t)$ is wide sense stationary [if and only if](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) $\underline{x}(t)$ is circular complex, i.e. if and only if $x_c(t)$ and $x_s(t)$ are such that

$$R_{x_c x_c}(\tau)=R_{x_s x_s}(\tau) \qquad \text{and }\qquad R_{x_c x_s}(\tau)=-R_{x_s x_c}(\tau).$$
