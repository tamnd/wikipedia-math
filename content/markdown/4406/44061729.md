# Van Lamoen circle

*Image: Van Lamoen circle.svg.* The van Lamoen circle through six circumcenters $A_b$, $A_c$, $B_c$, $B_a$, $C_a$, $C_b$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Van Lamoen circle"}

In [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **van Lamoen circle** is a special [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) associated with any given [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) $T$. It contains the [circumcenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of the six triangles that are defined inside $T$ by its three [medians](wikipedia://en/1072006).[^o13][^o7]

:::

Specifically, let $A$, $B$, $C$ be the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of $T$, and let $G$ be its [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) (the intersection of its three medians). Let $M_a$, $M_b$, and $M_c$ be the midpoints of the sidelines $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$, respectively. It turns out that the circumcenters of the six triangles $AGM_c$, $BGM_c$, $BGM_a$, $CGM_a$, $CGM_b$, and $AGM_b$ lie on a common circle, which is the van Lamoen circle of $T$.[^o7]

## History {#History}

The van Lamoen circle is named after the mathematician Floor van Lamoen who posed it as a problem in 2000.[^o8][^o10] A proof was provided by [Kin Y. Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kin_Y._Li) in 2001,[^o10] and the editors of the Amer. Math. Monthly in 2002.[^o13][^o9]

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The center of the van Lamoen circle is point $X(1153)$ in [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [comprehensive list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberling_center) of [triangle centers](wikipedia://en/12401488).[^o13]

In 2003, [Alexey Myakishev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Myakishev) and [Peter Y. Woo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Y._Woo) proved that the converse of the theorem is nearly true, in the following sense: let $P$ be any point in the triangle's interior, and $AA'$, $BB'$, and $CC'$ be its [cevians](wikipedia://en/6135790), that is, the [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) that connect each vertex to $P$ and are extended until each meets the opposite side. Then the circumcenters of the six triangles $APB'$, $APC'$, $BPC'$, $BPA'$, $CPA'$, and $CPB'$ lie on the same circle if and only if $P$ is the centroid of $T$ or its [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) (the intersection of its three [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241)), at which point the six circumcenters degenerate into the three Euler points of the [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243).[^o11] A simpler proof of this result was given by [Nguyen Minh Ha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguyen_Minh_Ha) in 2005.[^o12]
