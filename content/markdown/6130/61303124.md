# Nadeschda Gernet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nadeschda Gernet"}

Nikolayevna

**Nadeschda Nikolayevna Gernet** (Надежда Николаевна Гернет; April 18, 1877 – January 1, 1943) was a Russian mathematician, [academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_staff), and the second Russian woman to earn a doctorate.[^o3][^o4][^o12][^o7] Gernet extended the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) to further functions on the basis developed by her instructor, [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), and was one of the first to include inequalities in the calculus of variations.[^o11][^o6]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Gernet was born on April 18, 1877 in [Simbirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simbirsk) (now Ulyanovsk), to Nikolai V. Gernet, a state councillor and his wife Nadezhda.[^o3][^o4][^o6]

In the spring of 1894, Gernet graduated from high school in Simbirsk with a gold medal.[^o6] That fall, she began her higher education at [Women's University of Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bestuzhev_Courses), where she took science and math-related classes.[^o6] She was the first to graduate from the Women's University of Petersburg.[^o7] Gernet's academic interests included, but were not limited to, mathematics, astronomy, and physics.[^o6]

In 1902, Gernet received a PhD for her thesis *Untersuchung zur Variationsrechnung* ("On One New Method in the Variation of Calculus"), written at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen)[^o6] in Germany. She was the second female doctoral candidate to study under the instruction of David Hilbert,[^o6][^o8][^o9] [Anne Bosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Bosworth) having been the first. In her thesis, Gernet extended the calculus of variations and generalized Hilbert's independence theorem to the case of two unknown functions.[^o6] After defending her thesis, Gernet became the second woman in Russia to hold a doctorate, and the *[International Catalogue of Scientific Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Catalogue_of_Scientific_Literature): First Volume* indexed Gernet's book within the same year.[^o10]

As her German doctorate was not recognized in Russia, in 1915 Gernet submitted her thesis *On the Fundamental Simplest Problems of Variation Calculus* to [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University) in order to obtain a Russian master's degree which would also enable her to take up a university position.[^o12][^o11] Gernet included a summary of the past achievements within the field of calculus of variations, and she was the first to attempt the inclusion of inequalities in the calculus of variations.[^o11]

*Image: CimetierePetersbourg5.JPG.* [Smolensky Lutheran Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolensky_Lutheran_Cemetery)

After defending her master's thesis and obtaining her degree with outstanding results from [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University), Gernet began her teaching career at the [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) where she taught until 1929.[^o12] Her curriculum focused on educating female students in the Women's Higher Courses.[^o11][^o9] Soon after, she began teaching at the [Leningrad Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Polytechnic_Institute).[^o11][^o13] On January 1, 1943, Gernet died in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad) (now St. Petersburg) during the blockades of the [Siege of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad) and was buried in the [Smolensky Lutheran Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolensky_Lutheran_Cemetery).[^o11][^o9][^o14]

Gernet's publications raised new discussions, and her work encouraged further development in the calculus of variations.[^o11]

- *On the Fundamental Simplest Problems of Variation Calculus*[^o11]
