# Component (group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Component"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **component** of a [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is a [quasisimple](wikipedia://en/1642729) [subnormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774415). Any two distinct components [commute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity). The product of all the components is the [layer](wikipedia://en/1416042#The_generalized_Fitting_subgroup) of the group.

:::

For finite [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) (or [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569)) groups, ***p*-component** is used in a different sense to mean the [Sylow *p*-subgroup](wikipedia://en/53993), so the abelian group is the product of its *p*-components for [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*. These are not components in the sense above, as abelian groups are not quasisimple.

A quasisimple subgroup of a finite group is called a **standard component** if its [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) has even order, it is [normal](wikipedia://en/21918) in the centralizer of every [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) centralizing it, and it commutes with none of its [conjugates](wikipedia://en/49176#Conjugacy_of_subgroups_and_general_subsets). This concept is used in the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), for instance, by showing that under mild restrictions on the standard component, one of the following always holds:

- a standard component is normal (so a component as above),
- the whole group has a nontrivial [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) normal subgroup,
- the subgroup generated by the conjugates of the standard component is on a short list,
- or the standard component is a previously unknown quasisimple group (Aschbacher Seitz 1976).
