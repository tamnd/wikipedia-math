# Transitively normal subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transitively normal subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **transitively normal** in the group if every [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of the subgroup is also normal in the whole group. In symbols, $H$ is a transitively normal subgroup of $G$ if for every $K$ normal in $H$, we have that $K$ is normal in $G$.[^o2]

:::

An alternate way to characterize these subgroups is: every *normal subgroup preserving [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160)* of the whole group must restrict to a *normal subgroup preserving automorphism* of the subgroup.

Here are some facts about transitively normal subgroups:

- Every [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of a transitively normal subgroup is normal.
- Every [direct factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_factor), or more generally, every [central factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_factor) is transitively normal. Thus, every [central subgroup](wikipedia://en/4938766) is transitively normal.
- A transitively normal subgroup of a transitively normal subgroup is transitively normal.
- A transitively normal subgroup is normal.
