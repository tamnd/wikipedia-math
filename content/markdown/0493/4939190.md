# Perfect core

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perfect core"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **perfect core** (or **perfect radical**) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is its largest [perfect](wikipedia://en/585271) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011).[^o2] Its existence is guaranteed by the fact that the subgroup [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) by a family of perfect subgroups is again a perfect subgroup. The perfect core is also the point where the [transfinite derived series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfinite_derived_series) stabilizes for any group.

:::

A group whose perfect core is trivial is termed a **hypoabelian group**. Every [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012) is hypoabelian, and so is every [free group](wikipedia://en/59735). More generally, every [residually solvable group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residually_solvable_group) is hypoabelian.

The [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of a group *G* by its perfect core is hypoabelian, and is called the **hypoabelianization** of *G*.
