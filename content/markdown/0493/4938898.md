# A-group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="A-group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), an **A-group** is a type of group that is similar to [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974). The groups were first studied in the 1940s by [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033), and are still studied today. A great deal is known about their structure.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

An **A-group** is a finite [group](wikipedia://en/19447) with the property that all of its [Sylow subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_subgroup) are [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974).

:::

## History {#History}

The term A-group was probably first used by [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) in 1940,[^n1] where attention was restricted to [soluble](wikipedia://en/105012) A-groups. Hall's presentation was rather brief without proofs, but his remarks were soon expanded with proofs by D. R. Taunt.[^n2] The [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of A-groups was studied by Noboru Itô.[^n3] Roger W. Carter then published an important relationship between [Carter subgroups](wikipedia://en/5456815) and Hall's work.[^n4] The work of Hall, Taunt, and Carter was presented in textbook form in 1967.[^n5] The focus on soluble A-groups broadened, with the classification of finite simple A-groups in 1969[^n6] which allowed generalizing Taunt's work to finite groups in 1971.[^n7] Interest in A-groups also broadened due to an important relationship to [varieties of groups](wikipedia://en/1021753).[^n8] Modern interest in A-groups was renewed when new enumeration techniques enabled tight asymptotic bounds on the number of distinct [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) classes of A-groups.[^n9]

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The following can be said about A-groups:

- Every [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011), [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526), and [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of A-groups are A-groups.
- Every finite abelian group is an A-group.
- A finite [nilpotent group](wikipedia://en/144569) is an A-group if and only if it is abelian.
- The [symmetric group on three points](wikipedia://en/2375824) is an A-group that is not abelian.
- Every group of [cube-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube-free) order is an A-group.
- The derived length of an A-group can be arbitrarily large, but no larger than the number of distinct prime divisors of the order.[^n10][^n11]
- The [lower nilpotent series](wikipedia://en/14943054) coincides with the [derived series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_series)[^n10].
- A soluble A-group has a unique maximal abelian normal subgroup[^n10].
- The [Fitting subgroup](wikipedia://en/1416042) of a [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) A-group is equal to the direct product of the [centers](wikipedia://en/7125) of the terms of the [derived series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_series), first stated by Hall,[^n10] then proven by Taunt.[^n2][^n12]
- A non-abelian finite [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) is an A-group if and only if it is isomorphic to the [first Janko group](wikipedia://en/10796779) or to [PSL(2,*q*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) where *q* > 3 and either *q* = 2<sup>n</sup> or *q* ≡ 3,5 mod 8.[^n6]
- All the groups in the variety generated by a finite group are [finitely approximable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_approximable_group) if and only if that group is an A-group.[^n8]
- Like [Z-groups](wikipedia://en/15757039#Groups_whose_Sylow_subgroups_are_cyclic), whose Sylow subgroups are cyclic, A-groups can be easier to study than general finite groups because of the restrictions on the local structure. For instance, a more precise enumeration of soluble A-groups was found after an enumeration of [soluble groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble_group) with fixed, but arbitrary Sylow subgroups.[^n9][^n13]
