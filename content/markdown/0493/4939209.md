# Imperfect group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Imperfect group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the area of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), an **imperfect group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) with no nontrivial [perfect](wikipedia://en/585271) [quotients](wikipedia://en/11526). Some of their basic properties were established in (Berrick Robinson 1993). The study of imperfect groups apparently began in (Robinson 1972).[^o4]

:::

The class of imperfect groups is closed under [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) and quotient groups, but not under [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011). If *G* is a group, *N*, *M* are normal subgroups with *G*/*N* and *G*/*M* imperfect, then *G*/(*N*∩*M*) is imperfect, showing that the class of imperfect groups is a [formation](wikipedia://en/5456815#Generalizations). The (restricted or unrestricted) [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of imperfect groups is imperfect.

Every [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012) is imperfect. Finite [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) are also imperfect. The [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564) PGL(2,*q*) are imperfect for *q* an odd prime power. For any group *H*, the [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625) *H* wr *Sym*<sub>2</sub> of *H* with the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on two points is imperfect. In particular, every group can be embedded as a two-step [subnormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774415) of an imperfect group of roughly the same cardinality (2|*H*|<sup>2</sup>).
