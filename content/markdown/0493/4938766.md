# Central subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Central subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is termed **central** if it lies inside the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) of the group.

:::

Given a group $G$, the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) of $G$, denoted as $Z(G)$, is defined as the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of those elements of the group which commute with every element of the group. The center is a [characteristic subgroup](wikipedia://en/7198). A subgroup $H$ of $G$ is termed *central* if $H \leq Z(G)$.

Central subgroups have the following properties:

- They are abelian groups (because, in particular, all elements of the center must commute with each other).
- They are [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918). They are [central factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_factor), and are hence [transitively normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/4938714).
