# Lomonosov's invariant subspace theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lomonosov's invariant subspace theorem"}

**Lomonosov's invariant subspace theorem** is a mathematical theorem from [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) concerning the existence of [invariant subspaces](wikipedia://en/585826) of a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) on some complex [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). The theorem was proved in 1973 by the Russian–American mathematician [Victor Lomonosov](wikipedia://en/57016901).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Lomonosov's invariant subspace theorem"}

## Lomonosov's invariant subspace theorem {#Lomonosov's_invariant_subspace_theorem}

### Notation and terminology {#Notation_and_terminology}

Let $\mathcal{B}(X):=\mathcal{B}(X,X)$ be the space of bounded linear operators from some space $X$ to itself. For an operator $T\in\mathcal{B}(X)$ we call a closed subspace $M\subset X,\;M\neq \{0\}$ an invariant subspace if $T(M)\subset M$, i.e. $Tx\in M$ for every $x\in M$.

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

### Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $X$ be an infinite dimensional complex Banach space, $T\in\mathcal{B}(X)$ be [compact](wikipedia://en/577441) and such that $T\neq 0$. Further let $S\in\mathcal{B}(X)$ be an operator that commutes with $T$. Then there exist an invariant subspace $M$ of the operator $S$, i.e. $S(M)\subset M$.[^o8]
