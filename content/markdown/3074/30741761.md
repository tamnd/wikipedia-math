# Howard Wainer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Howard Wainer"}

**Howard Charles Wainer** (born October 26, 1943)[^o5] is an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), past principal research scientist at the [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service), adjunct professor of statistics at the [Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania), and author, known for his contributions in the fields of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [psychometrics](wikipedia://en/24982), and [statistical graphics](wikipedia://en/15934463).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Howard Wainer was born Howard Charles Goldhaber in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29) on October 26, 1943. In 1948 his father Meyer Goldhaber, an [anatomist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomist) by education and a dentist by profession, died of complications from a bleeding ulcer at the age of 35. Howard, his brother and his mother moved in with his mother's parents. After two years his mother married Sam Wainer, a local businessman, and the family relocated to Long Island. Howard was formally adopted by his mother's new husband and took the surname Wainer.[^o5]

### Education {#Education}

Early on Wainer showed an aptitude for science and mathematics. In 1960, at the end of his junior year in high school, he was accepted into a National Science Foundation honors program at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). He spent two hours traveling on subway and bus each way to and from Columbia, learning about [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) in the morning and working on the [IBM 650](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_650) computer in the afternoon.[^o5]

Wainer's experiences at Columbia motivated him to continue his studies along similar lines. He matriculated at [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute) in 1961 to study mathematics. It was at R.P.I. that Wainer first encountered [psychometrics](wikipedia://en/24982). There, Professor George Boguslavsky was so impressed with his abilities and enthusiasm that he recommended Wainer for a Psychometric Fellowship at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under [Harold Gulliksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Gulliksen).[^o7] Wainer received his B.S. from R.P.I. in mathematics in 1965 and a Ph.D. from Princeton in psychometrics in 1968.[^o8]

### Career {#Career}

Howard Wainer began his teaching career at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) in 1968, staying on as an assistant professor until 1970.[^o10] After Temple he taught at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), as a member of the Committee on Methodology in the department of Behavioral Sciences until 1977.[^o11] Wainer then moved to [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), to join the [Bureau of Social Science Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Social_Science_Research), a nonprofit organization that focused on policy research. During his time in DC Wainer also joined with Richard Roistacher and Barbara Noble in founding Multiple Technical Services, a small firm that provided statistical and computational advice to the DC research community. In 1980 he moved to Princeton NJ to become a principal research scientist at the [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service), a position he held for 21 years. In 2001 he assumed the position of Distinguished Research Scientist at the [National Board of Medical Examiners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_of_Medical_Examiners), from which he retired on December 2, 2016. Wainer was also an adjunct professor of statistics at the [Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania) from 2002 until 2013.[^o8]

### Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Howard Wainer is the recipient of numerous awards and honors: He is a fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) and the [American Educational Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Educational_Research_Association). He was given a Career Achievement Award for Contributions to Educational Measurement by the [National Council on Measurement in Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_on_Measurement_in_Education) in 2007, the Samuel J. Messick Award for Distinguished Scientific Contributions from Division 5 of the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association) in 2009, and the Lifetime Achievement Award from the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500) in 2013. He also received the ACT/AERA E. F. Lindquist Award for Outstanding Research in Testing & Measurement in 2015. His work on testlets was recognized when he received the Award for Scientific Contribution to a Field of Educational Measurement from the National Council on Measurement in Education in 2006. His book *Graphic Discovery* was named by Choice as the “Best Math book of 2005”. He was a Distinguished Visiting Lecturer at the [Hebrew University in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_in_Jerusalem), the [University of Twente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Twente), Enschede, The Netherlands, and the American College Testing organization. He also received the [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service)’s Senior Scientist Award in 1990.

### Current status {#Current_status}

Howard Wainer lives with his wife, Linda Steinberg, in [Pennington, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennington,_New_Jersey).

## Work {#Work}

### Contributions to statistics {#Contributions_to_statistics}

Since 1974 when he published his first article on statistical graphics, an empirical verification of the efficacy of the suspended Rootogram, Howard Wainer has been a tireless advocate for the efficacy of [graphics for communicating quantitative phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infographics). He is one of the principals responsible for the renewed importance of graphics in statistics. In addition to the three books he authored on graphical methods: *Picturing the Uncertain World*,[^o16] *Graphic Discovery*[^o17] and *Visual Revelations*[^o18] he was also responsible for the English translation of two of the masterworks in the field by the French semiologist [Jacques Bertin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Bertin).[^o19][^o20]

Wainer’s approach to the study of graphics has always shown a deep respect for the work of those who had preceded him. In 2007 he arranged for the publication of replica volumes of [William Playfair](wikipedia://en/1411831)'s *Atlas* as well as his *Statistical Breviary*, the first books on the subject. In them he collaborated with [Ian Spence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Spence_%28psychologist%29) on an extended introduction to Playfair and a biography of him.[^o21]

Wainer has done extensive work on problems in [psychometrics](wikipedia://en/24982). He has authored, co-authored or edited the principal texts in five of the major areas of the subject: test scoring,[^o22] [test validity](wikipedia://en/21526661),[^o23] [computerized adaptive testing](wikipedia://en/1743830),[^o24] test fairness,[^o25] and, most recently, on a theory of [testlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testlet).[^o26]

Wainer has published more than 450 articles, chapters and books. His latest book *Truth or Truthiness*[^o27] explains how to use evidence to debunk baseless claims. Since 1990 Wainer has written the popular column “Visual Revelations” for *[Chance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chance_%28magazine%29)* magazine. Wainer edited the Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics from 2002 through 2004 as well as being an associate editor of a handful of statistical and psychometric journals. He is currently on the Board of Editors of *Significance*, the new joint publication of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) and the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664).

He has also served on the front lines of educational practice by working for many years as a consultant for teachers’ unions, with a five-year hiatus when he served on the Princeton Board of Education. He has also served, in many capacities, as a consultant and advisor to government and industry.
