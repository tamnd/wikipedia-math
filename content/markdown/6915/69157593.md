# Heinrich Wieleitner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heinrich Wieleitner"}

**Heinrich Wieleitner** (31 October 1874, [Wasserburg am Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserburg_am_Inn), Germany – 27 December 1931) was a German mathematician and historian of mathematics. He became an honorary professor of mathematics at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) but for much of his career worked in school- and college-level education.

:::

Wieleitner was born in [Wasserburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserburg_am_Inn) and was educated at the Catholic seminaries at [Scheyern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheyern) and [Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freising) in theology but took an interest in mathematics, joining the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München). He received a Lamont scholarship proposed by [C. L. F. Lindemann](wikipedia://en/335736) and went on to receive a doctorate on third order surfaces (*Über die Flächen dritter Ordnung mit Ovalpunkten*) in 1901. He then became a mathematics teacher at the [Speyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speyer) Gymnasium and in 1909 moved to [Pirmasens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirmasens) before returning to Speyer as a headmaster of the Realschule. In 1926, he was promoted Oberstudiendirektor in Munich. He became interested in Italian work on geometry following his attendance of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held in Heidelberg (1904) and Rome (1908), leading to translations of an article by [Gino Loria](wikipedia://en/36957490) and he worked on a German edition of [Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068)'s work along with [Edgardo Ciani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgardo_Ciani). [Arnold Sommerfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Sommerfeld) suggested that he do his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation), and so he held lectures on the history of mathematics at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München from 1928. In 1930, he was made an honorary professor. Wieleitner worked on an unfinished manuscript by [Anton von Braunmühl](wikipedia://en/35217283) (died in 1908[^o2]) on the history of mathematics after Braunmühl and [Siegmund Günther](wikipedia://en/47154330) had worked on a history, Geschichte der Mathematik, the first volume of which came out in 1908. Wieleitner later published a history of mathematical ideas that did not spend too much space on the biographies of people involved which was published by [Sammlung Göschen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sammlung_Göschen) between 1922 and 1923. He also contributed to a translation of the trigonometry of [Al-Biruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Biruni) by [Julius Ruska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Ruska).[^o3]
