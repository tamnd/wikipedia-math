# C. J. Eliezer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="C. J. Eliezer"}

**Christie Jayaratnam Eliezer** (கிரிஸ்டி ஜெயரத்தினம் எலியேசர்; 12 June 1918 – 10 March 2001) was a [Ceylon Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon_Tamil) mathematician, physicist and academic.

:::

## Early life and family {#Early_life_and_family}

Eliezer was born on 12 June 1918 in [Navatkuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navatkuli) in northern [Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ceylon).[^o4][^o5] He was the son of Jacob Richard Eliezer and Elizabeth Ponnammah Vairakiam.[^o4] Both of his parents died when he was young.[^o6] Eliezer was educated at the Wesleyan Mission School, [Puloly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puloly) and [Hartley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartley_College), [Point Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Pedro) (1926–33) where he passed the Cambridge Local Examinations with honours and distinction.[^o4][^o6][^o7] He then spent a year studying at [St. Joseph's College, Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph's_College,_Colombo) before joining [Ceylon University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon_University_College) in 1935, graduating with a first class honours [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) special degree in mathematics.[^o4][^o7][^o8]

Eliezer married Ranee, daughter of Rev. John Handy.[^o4] They had five children (Dhamayanthi, Ratna, Anandhi, Renuka and Tamara).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Eliezer worked at Ceylon University College as a [visiting lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_lecturer) in 1938 before proceeding to [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge) (1939–43) on a [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) to study mathematics and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).[^o4][^o7] He received a first class mathematics [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos) from Christ's College in 1941.[^o4][^o7] He received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree from Cambridge in 1946 after producing a [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis), supervised by [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742), on spinning electron and electromagnetic field.[^o4][^o6][^o7][^o10][^o11] Returning to Ceylon Eliezer lectured at the [University of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ceylon) for a brief period before rejoining Christ's College as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) (1946–49).[^o4][^o5][^o7] He received a [D.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) degree in 1949.[^o4][^o11] He was [called to the bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Called_to_the_bar) at the [Middle Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Temple) in 1949.[^o5][^o11]

Eliezer was appointed professor of mathematics at University of Ceylon in 1949.[^o4][^o5][^o7] During his ten years at the university he was dean of the Faculty of Science from 1954 to 1957 and deputy pro-vice chancellor in 1955.[^o4][^o5][^o7] Eliezer was a scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1955 to 1956, working with [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer).[^o7][^o10] He spent some time at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o7] Following the passing of the [Sinhala Only Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_Only_Act) in 1956, [Sinhalese nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_nationalists) at the University of Ceylon, led by [vice-chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-chancellor) [Nicholas Attygalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Attygalle) and chancellor [Dudley Senanayake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Senanayake), attempted to remove [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) as a [medium of instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_of_instruction) at the university but this was thwarted by Eliezer and [A. M. A. Azeez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._M._A._Azeez), a member of the university's [council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_council).[^o7]

Eliezer was appointed foundation professor of mathematics at the [University of Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malaya) in 1959.[^o4][^o5][^o7] The appointment was only meant be for two years but the deteriorating situation in Ceylon meant that Eliezer decided to stay in [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya).[^o7] During his nine years at the university he was dean of the Faculty of Science from 1959 to 1963 and deputy principal and vice-chancellor for another three years.[^o4][^o5] Eliezer became the first professor of applied mathematics at [La Trobe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trobe_University) in 1968.[^o4][^o5][^o7] During his 15 years at the university he was dean of the School of Physical Sciences (1969–71 and 1982–83) and deputy vice-chancellor for a period.[^o4][^o5][^o7] After retiring in 1983 he was appointed [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor) at La Trobe.[^o4][^o5][^o11]

Eliezer had been president of the Ceylon Association for the Advancement of Science.[^o10] He was a [Fellow of the Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Institute_of_Mathematics_and_its_Applications).[^o11] He received an [honorary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) D.Sc.Inf. degree from the [University of Jaffna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jaffna) in 1981.[^o10][^o11] He was made a [Member of the Order of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Australia) in 1996.[^o5][^o7][^o10] He was awarded the [Maamanithar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maamanithar) (Great Man) honour by the rebel [Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Tigers_of_Tamil_Eelam) on 19 October 1997.[^o6][^o7][^o10]

Eliezer was vice-president of the [Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo) branch of the [Young Men’s Christian Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Men’s_Christian_Association) and a member of the [Jaffna College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffna_College) board.[^o4] He was president of the Ceylon Tamil Association of Victoria and chairman of the Australian Federation of Tamil Associations (1984-2001).[^o4][^o10] Eliezer helped Tamil refugees fleeing to Australia following the 1983 anti-Tamil [Black July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_July) riots.[^o7] He co-hosted a Tamil language programme on the [Special Broadcasting Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Broadcasting_Service).[^o12] Eliezer died on 10 March 2001 in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne).[^o5][^o13]

## Works {#Works}

Eliezer wrote several books and articles including:[^o4][^o6][^o7]

- *Concise Vector Analysis* (1963, [Pergamon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon_Press))
- *A Modern Text-book on Statics: For Students of Applied Mathematics, Physics and Engineering* (1964, Pergamon Press)
- *Mathematics : Queen of the Arts, Handmaiden of the Sciences* (1969, La Trobe University)
- *The First Integrals of Some Differential Equations of Dynamics* (1978, La Trobe University)
- *Mechanics for Year Eleven* (1988, co-author J. G. Barton)
