# Shizuo Kakutani

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shizuo Kakutani"}

**Shizuo Kakutani** was a Japanese and American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), best known for his [eponymous fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/2658571).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kakutani attended [Tohoku University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohoku_University) in [Sendai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendai,_Miyagi), where his advisor was [Tatsujiro Shimizu](wikipedia://en/71174005). At one point he spent two years at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) at the invitation of the mathematician [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544). While there, he also met [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942).

Kakutani received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1941 from [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University) and taught there through [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). He returned to the Institute for Advanced Study in 1948, and was given a professorship by [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1949, where he won a students' choice award for excellence in teaching.[^o5]

Kakutani received two awards of the [Japan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy), the [Imperial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Prize_of_the_Japan_Academy) and the [Academy Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy_Prize_%28academics%29) in 1982, for his scholarly achievements in general and his work on functional analysis in particular. He was a Plenary Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1950 in Cambridge, Massachusetts.[^o6]

Kakutani was married to Keiko ("Kay") Uchida, who was a sister to author [Yoshiko Uchida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshiko_Uchida). His daughter, [Michiko Kakutani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michiko_Kakutani), is a [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize)-winning former literary critic for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*.

## Work {#Work}

The [Kakutani fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/2658571) is a generalization of [Brouwer's fixed-point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brouwer's_fixed-point_theorem), holding for generalized [correspondences](wikipedia://en/12506030) instead of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427). Its most important uses are in proving the existence of [Nash equilibria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_equilibria) in [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), and the [Arrow–Debreu–McKenzie model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow–Debreu–McKenzie_model) of [general equilibrium theory](wikipedia://en/45938) in microeconomics.

Kakutani's other mathematical contributions include [Markov–Kakutani fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/36205212), another fixed point theorem; the [Kakutani skyscraper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakutani_skyscraper), a concept in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) (a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) that studies [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) with an [invariant measure](wikipedia://en/7635201) and related problems); his solution of the [Poisson equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_equation) [using the methods of stochastic analysis](wikipedia://en/12195613).

The [Collatz conjecture](wikipedia://en/37895) is also known as the Kakutani conjecture.
