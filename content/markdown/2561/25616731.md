# Vinzenz Bronzin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vinzenz Bronzin"}

**Vinzenz Bronzin** (born 1872 in Rovigno – died 1970 in Trieste) was an Italian mathematics professor, known today for an early (rediscovered) [option pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_of_options) formula, similar to, and predating, the [Black–Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black–Scholes) 1973 formula; [^o2] he also provided a formulation of [put–call parity](wikipedia://en/207387), [^o3] written up formally only in 1969 by [Stoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Stoll). [^o4]

:::

Bronzin was born in [Rovigno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovigno) (now Rovinj), [Istria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istria). He studied engineering at the [Vienna Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Polytechnic_Institute), and then mathematics and pedagogics at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna). He was made a professor at the [Accademia di Commercio e Nautica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Trieste#History), [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), in 1900; his focus was "Political and [Commercial Arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3593875#University_level)", which included [actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). In 1910 he accepted the position of director. In 1937 he resigned from all of his positions at the Academia at the age of 65.[^o5]

In 1908 Bronzin published his [*Theorie der Prämiengeschäfte*](https://doi.org/10.1007%2FBF01693247) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): "Theory of Premium Contracts") discussing a then current type of [option contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_%28finance%29#Contract_specifications). Almost every element of [modern option pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_of_options) can be found in Bronzin's book;[^o6] however, like [Louis Bachelier](wikipedia://en/1062753)'s now famous dissertation (1900), the work seems to have been forgotten shortly after it was published. Bronzin's "methodological setup is completely different from Bachelier’s,"[^o7] at least in terms of the underlying [stochastic framework](wikipedia://en/228001); he takes a much more "pragmatic" approach, directly making assumptions on the share price distribution at maturity, and deriving a "rich set of closed form solutions for the value of options."
