# Race-norming

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Race-norming"}

**Race-norming**, more formally called **within-group score conversion** and **score adjustment strategy**, is the practice of adjusting test scores to account for the race or ethnicity of the test-taker.[^o2] In the United States, it was first implemented by the Federal Government in 1981 with little publicity,[^o3] and was subsequently outlawed by the [Civil Rights Act of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Act_of_1991).[^o4]

:::

Prior to being banned by the federal government, race-norming was practiced by 38 U.S. states' employment services.[^o5] The aim of this practice is to counteract alleged racial bias in [aptitude tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptitude_test) administered to job applicants,[^o4] as well as in [neuropsychological tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuropsychological_test).[^o6] The argument was that it guarantees racial balance. The practice converted and compared the raw score of the test according to racial groups. The score of a black candidate is only compared to the scores of those who had the same ethnicity. If the candidate's score, which is reported within a percentile range, fell within a certain percentile when compared to white or all candidates, it would be much higher among other black candidates.[^o7]

## Criticism {#Criticism}

Race-norming has been criticized as racist towards black people and has been compared to [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics) and [pseudoscientific racism](wikipedia://en/23047).[^o9] In 2021, such criticisms surfaced following an announcement by the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League) that they will cease to use the practice in determining settlements for players' injuries.[^o10]

[University of Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Delaware) professor [Linda Gottfredson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Gottfredson) has been very critical of this practice,[^o11][^o12] as have conservative columnist [George Will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Will)[^o13] and law professor [Robert J. Delahunty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Delahunty).[^o14] Criticism was based on the perception that race-norming was biased in favor of blacks.[^o15] In the 1980s, the [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_administration) ordered a study into the unadjusted [General Aptitude Test Battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Aptitude_Test_Battery) (without race-norming); the results, released in 1989, showed that unadjusted test scores were not strongly related to job performance.[^o16]

On June 2, 2021, the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League) (NFL) announced they would stop using race-norming, as part of their press release for a \$1 billion dollar concussion settlement.[^o17] Race-stratified Heaton, Grant, and Matthews norms, originally published for the [Halstead–Reitan Neuropsychological Battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halstead–Reitan_Neuropsychological_Battery) in 1991 and revised in 2004, had been used in the settlement's claims-evaluation process, contributing to the denial of [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia) claims by Black retired players whose scores would have qualified for benefits had White norms been applied.[^o18][^o19][^o9] The issue had been raised in a 2020 federal lawsuit filed in the [Eastern District of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Pennsylvania) by two former players, Najeh Davenport and Kevin Henry, and was the subject of investigative reporting by *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* in August 2021.[^o9] In March 2022, the presiding court approved a modification of the settlement that removed race-based scoring and provided for re-scoring of affected claims.[^o20] By August 2022, more than 300 Black retired players who had originally been denied claims had qualified for benefits or league-funded medical treatment under the revised process.[^o21]

Within [clinical neuropsychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_neuropsychology), the use of race norming has been debated. In a 2009 review in *[Neuropsychology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuropsychology_Review)*, Gasquoine argued that race-stratified norms treat a [socially defined category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_%28human_categorization%29) as if it were biological, and may either mask pathology in patients of one group or exaggerate it in another, depending on the direction of the adjustment.[^o6] In November 2021, the Relevance 2050 Subcommittee of the American Academy of Clinical Neuropsychology issued a position statement supporting the elimination of race as a variable in demographically based normative test interpretation.[^o22]
