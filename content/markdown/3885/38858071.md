# Robert Sinclair MacKay

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Sinclair MacKay"}

**Robert Sinclair MacKay** (born 1956) is a British mathematician and professor at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick). He researches [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [Hamiltonian dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_dynamics) and applications to complex systems in physics, engineering, chemistry, biology and economics.[^o6][^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

MacKay was educated at [Newcastle High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle-under-Lyme_School), leaving in 1974. He completed his Bachelor of Arts degree with first class honours in mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1977, and completed Part III of the [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos) with distinction in 1978. He obtained his PhD in [astrophysical sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics) in 1982 from the [Plasma Physics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Plasma_Physics_Laboratory) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) for research supervised by [John M. Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Greene) and [Martin David Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_David_Kruskal).[^o5][^o8]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Between 1982 and 1995, MacKay held [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research) positions at [Queen Mary College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_College,_London), London,[^o4] the [Institut des Hautes Etudes Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Etudes_Scientifiques), and the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick). From 1995 to 2000 he was Professor of Nonlinear Dynamics in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), Director of the Nonlinear Centre, and Fellow of Trinity College. In 2000 he returned to Warwick as Professor of Mathematics and Director of Mathematical Interdisciplinary Research.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

MacKay was awarded the [Stefanos Pnevmatikos International Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Pnevmatikos_International_Award) in 1992.[^o11] He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2000).[^o12] In 2012 he was elected President of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139).[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

MacKay was born to [Donald MacCrimmon MacKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_MacCrimmon_MacKay) and Valerie MacKay (née Wood) in 1956.[^o3] His younger brother [David J. C. MacKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._C._MacKay) FRS was the Regius Professor of Engineering at the University of Cambridge.
