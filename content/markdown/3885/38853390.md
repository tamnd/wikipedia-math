# Health indicator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Health indicator"}

**Health indicators** are [quantifiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantifiable) characteristics of a population which researchers use as supporting evidence for describing the [health of a population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_health). Typically, researchers will use a [survey methodology](wikipedia://en/272134) to gather information about a population sample, use [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in an attempt to generalize the information collected to the entire population, and then use the statistical analysis to make a statement about the health of the population.[^o2] Health indicators are often used by governments to guide [health care policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_policy) or to make goals for improving population health.[^o2]

:::

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

A health indicator which will be used internationally to describe [global health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_health) should have the following characteristics:

1. It should be defined in such a way that it can be measured uniformly internationally.[^o4]
1. It must have [statistical validity](wikipedia://en/239140).[^o4]
1. The indicator must be data which can feasibly be collected in a reasonable amount of time.[^o4][^o17]
1. The analysis of the data must result in a recommendation on which people can make changes to improve health[^o4]

## Health indicator examples {#Health_indicator_examples}

\*This is not a comprehensive list of health indicators.

### Health status {#Health_status}

- [Life expectancy at birth](wikipedia://en/18669)[^o8]
  - Number of years a newborn is expected to live based on current mortality trends.[^o9]
- [Infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality)[^o8]
  - Number of infant deaths (children under 1 year) per 1,000 live births in a year.[^o9]
- [Neonatal mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_mortality_rate)[^o8]
  - Number of newborn deaths (children under 28 days) per 1,000 births in a year.[^o9]
- [Maternal mortality ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_mortality_ratio)[^o10]
  - Number of women who die due to pregnancy or birth complications per 100,000 births in a year.[^o9]
- Mortality from [cardiovascular diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease)[^o10]
- [Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) incidence[^o10]
- [Diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes) incidence[^o10]
- [Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_%28mood%29) incidence[^o10]
- [Disability adjusted life years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability-adjusted_life_year) (DALY)
  - A measurement of premature death or lower quality of life due to disability.[^o9]
- [Activities of daily living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activities_of_daily_living) (ADL)
  - A measurement of ability to self-sustain based on ability to perform daily activities such as dressing, feeding, and grooming.[^o11]

### Risk factors {#Risk_factors}

- Alcohol consumption[^o8]
- Smoking in adults[^o8]
- Physical exercise habits[^o8]
- Condom use[^o10]
- [Obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity) rate[^o10]
- [Asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma) rate[^o10]
- [High blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_blood_pressure) rate[^o10]
- [Air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution) levels[^o10]
- Exclusive breastfeeding rate[^o10]
  - Number of mothers who only feed their infants breast milk for the first 6 months of life per a unit of measurement.[^o9]
- Child stunting rate[^o10]
  - Number of children who have a low height for their age (more than two standard deviations below the international reference) due to poor nutrition per a unit of measurement.[^o9]
- Child wasting rate[^o10]
  - Number of children who have a low weight for their height (more than two standard deviations below the international reference) due to poor nutrition per a unit of measurement.[^o9]

### Health systems {#Health_systems}

- Healthcare coverage[^o8]
  - Number of people with some type of health care coverage/insurance per unit of measurement.[^o8]
- Hospital beds per capita[^o8]
- Doctors per capita[^o8]
- Nurses per capita[^o8]
- [Hospital readmission rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_readmission)[^o10]
- Health expenditure as percentage of GDP[^o10]
  - Percentage of a nation’s gross domestic product (GDP) used toward healthcare.[^o10]
- People with [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV) aware of their status[^o10]
- [Breast cancer screening rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer_screening)[^o10]
- Birth registration rate[^o10]
  - Number of people with a government- verified birth certificate per unit of measurement.[^o10]
- Death registration rate[^o10]
  - Number of people with a government- verified death certificate per unit of measurement.[^o10]

## Applications {#Applications}

Health indicators are commonly used to make large-scale or community health-related decisions.[^o16] By describing the current health of a population, the areas that need improvement become evident, and policy-makers and health professionals can work to fill these gaps.[^o16][^o17] Once interventions are put in place to try to improve the health of a population, health indicators can then be used to evaluate the success of the intervention.[^o17]

Additionally, health indicators can highlight health disparities in a population.[^o17] Differences in health indicators among genders, races, ethnic groups, socioeconomic classes, and other groups can be used to guide policy and interventions that will bring health equity in the future.[^o17]

Health indicators are used by many institutions, including international organizations such as the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) and [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) (WHO).[^o18][^o19] They are also used by smaller-scale community health organizations, hospitals, and other medical and public health organizations such as the [Center for Disease Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention) (CDC), [National Institute of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) (NIH), [The African Comprehensive HIV/AIDS Partnerships](http://www.achap.org/) (ACHAP), and [Global Alliance for Africa](https://www.globalallianceafrica.org/).[^o19][^o20]
