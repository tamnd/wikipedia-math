# Qualitative property

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Qualitative property"}

**Qualitative properties** are properties that are observed and can generally not be measured with a numerical result, unlike [quantitative properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_property), which have numerical characteristics.

:::

## Description {#Description}

Qualitative properties are properties that are observed and can generally not be measured with a numerical result.[^o3] They are contrasted to [quantitative properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_property) which have numerical characteristics.

### Evaluation {#Evaluation}

Although measuring something in qualitative terms is difficult, most people can (and will) make a judgement about a [behaviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behaviour) on the basis of how they feel treated. This indicates that qualitative properties are closely related to [emotional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotional) impressions.

A [test method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_method) can result in qualitative data about something. This can be a categorical result or a [binary classification](wikipedia://en/205393) (e.g., pass/fail, [go/no go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go/no_go), [conform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformity)/non-conform). It can sometimes be an engineering judgement.

### Categorization {#Categorization}

The data that all share a qualitative property form a [nominal category](wikipedia://en/484817). A variable which codes for the presence or absence of such a property is called a binary [categorical variable](wikipedia://en/821165), or equivalently a [dummy variable](wikipedia://en/285156).

## Types {#Types}

- Some [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) and [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific) [properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_property) are qualitative.
- Some important qualitative properties that concern [businesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business) are:
  - [Human factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_factors), [human work capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital) is important issue that deals with qualitative properties. Some common aspects are work, [motivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivation), general participation, etc. Although all of these aspects are not measurable in terms of quantitative criteria, the general overview of them could be summarized as a [quantitative property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_property).
  - [Environmental issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues) are in some cases quantitatively measurable, but other properties are qualitative, including environmentally friendly [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing), responsibility for the entire [life of a product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_lifetime) (from the raw-material till scrap), attitudes towards [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety), [efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficiency), and minimum [waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste) production.
  - [Ethical issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical_issues) are closely related to environmental and human issues, and may be covered in [corporate governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_governance). [Child labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_labour) and [illegal dumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_dumping) of waste are examples of ethical issues.
  - The way a company deals with its [stockholders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shareholder) (the 'acting' of a company).
