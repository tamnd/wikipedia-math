# Next Dutch general election

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Next Dutch general election"}

General elections are scheduled to be held in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands) on 15 May 2030 to elect the members of the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_%28Netherlands%29), but may be held at an earlier date if a snap election is called.[^o3]

:::

## Background {#Background}

The [2025 Dutch general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Dutch_general_election) resulted in losses for all parties in the [Schoof cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoof_cabinet).[^o5] [Democrats 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrats_66) (D66) became the largest party in the House of Representatives for the first time, winning 26 seats, the same number as the [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV), but receiving 29,668 more votes.[^o6] [GroenLinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GroenLinks) (GL) and the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_%28Netherlands%29) (PvdA) merged in 2026 to form [Progressief Nederland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressief_Nederland) (PRO).[^o7]

## Electoral system {#Electoral_system}

Eligible voters are Dutch nationals who are at least 18 years old on nomination day and have not been deprived of their right to vote following a [criminal conviction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_conviction). Electoral procedures are governed by the Dutch Electoral Act.[^o9]

Elections for the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_%28Netherlands%29) are normally held every four years, unless a [snap election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap_election) is called. Regular general elections in the Netherlands are held in March. However, due to municipal elections already taking place in March 2030, general elections would instead be held in May 2030.[^o10][^o9][^o11]

The 150 members of the House of Representatives are elected by [semi-open list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-open_list) [proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_representation). The number of seats per list is determined using the [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method). A list must receive a number of votes equal to or exceeding the [Hare quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare_quota) (1 full seat) in order to qualify for seat distribution, meaning there is an [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 0.67%.[^o12]

Voters have the option to cast a preferential vote. The seats won by a list are first allocated to the candidates who, in preferential votes, have received at least 25% of the Hare quota (effectively ¼ of a seat or 0.17% of the total votes), regardless of their placement on the electoral list. If multiple candidates from a list pass this threshold, their ordering is determined based on the number of votes received. Any remaining seats are allocated to candidates according to their position on the electoral list.[^o13][^o14]
