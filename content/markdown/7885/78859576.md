# Richard Skemp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Skemp"}

**Richard Rowland Skemp**, (10 March 1919 – 22 June 1995) was a British mathematician, psychologist and academic, who specialised in [mathematical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_education). Having started his career as a school teacher, he became a lecturer in psychology at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) in 1955. He was appointed Professor of [Educational Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Theory) at [Warwick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_University) in 1973 and also served as director of its Mathematics Education Research Centre from 1978 until he retired in 1986.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Skemp was born on 10 March 1919 in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol), England.[^o4] His father, Professor Arthur Rowland Skemp, had been killed in action during the First World War on 1 November 1918.[^o5][^o6] As a foundation scholar, he was educated at [Wellington College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_College,_Berkshire), then an all-boys [independent school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_school_%28UK%29) in Berkshire which had been set up for the sons of deceased army officers.[^o7] He won an open scholarship in mathematics at [Hertford College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford_College,_Oxford), matriculating in 1937.[^o7]

His university studies were interrupted by military service during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).[^o5] On 24 May 1941, having trained at an [Officer Cadet Training Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_Cadet_Training_Unit), he was commissioned in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army) as a [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant) to serve in the [Royal Corps of Signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Corps_of_Signals). He served in India and had reached the rank of captain by the end of the war.[^o7]

After the end of the war, Skemp returned to Oxford to continue his studies.[^o7] He graduated from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) (BA) degree in 1947.[^o5] He then worked as a mathematics teacher at two [private schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_schools_in_the_United_Kingdom); first [Oundle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oundle_School) and then at [Rye St Antony School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye_St_Antony_School).[^o5] After developing an interesting in how children learn, he returned to Oxford to undertake a second undergraduate degree in psychology in 1952.[^o7] He then moved to the Department of Education, [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) to undertake a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (PhD) degree under the [supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_supervisor) of [C. E. M. Hansel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._E._M._Hansel) and Professor John Cohen.[^o4][^o9] He completed his PhD in 1959 with a [doctoral thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_thesis) titled "Investigation into difficulties in learning mathematics by children of good general intelligence".[^o4][^o9]

Skemp joined the academic staff of the University of Manchester as a [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) in psychology in 1955. He was promoted to [senior lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_lecturer) in 1962. In 1973, he joined the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) as Professor of Educational Theory. He also served as director of the Mathematics Education Research Centre at Warwick from 1978 to 1986.[^o4] He retired from full-time academia in 1986, but continued to lecture around the world.[^o7] He was [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) at the [University of Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calgary) from 1987 to 1994.[^o4]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1961, Skemp married Valerie Watts.[^o4] Together they had one son.[^o4] In January 1995, Skemp was diagnosed with [non-Hodgkin's lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hodgkin's_lymphoma).[^o7] He died on 22 June 1995, aged 76, in [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry), England.[^o4]
