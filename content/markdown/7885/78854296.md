# Darboux transformation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Darboux transformation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Darboux transformation**, named after [Gaston Darboux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Darboux) (1842–1917), is a method of generating a new equation and its solution from the known ones. It is widely used in [inverse scattering theory](wikipedia://en/385661), in the theory of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718),[^o2][^o3] and as a way of constructing [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) solutions of the [KdV hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KdV_hierarchy).[^o4] From the [operator-theoretic](wikipedia://en/712675) point of view, this method corresponds to the factorization of the initial second order [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) into a product of first order differential expressions and subsequent exchange of these factors, and is thus sometimes called the **single commutation** method in mathematics literature.[^o5] The Darboux transformation has applications in [supersymmetric quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetric_quantum_mechanics).[^o6][^o7]

:::

## History {#History}

The idea goes back to [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^o9]

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Method"}

## Method {#Method}

Let $y = y(x)$ be a solution of the equation

$$-y''(x) + q(x)y(x) = \lambda y(x)$$

and $y = z(x)$ be a fixed strictly positive solution of the same equation for some $\lambda = \lambda_0$. Then for $\lambda \ne \lambda_0$,

$$Y(x) := y'(x) - \frac{z'(x)}{z(x)}y(x) = z(x) \left( \frac{y(x)}{z(x)} \right)'$$

is a solution of the equation

$$-Y''(x) + Q(x)Y(x) = \lambda Y(x),$$

where $Q(x) = q(x) - 2\left( \frac{z'(x)}{z(x)} \right)'.$ Also, for $\lambda = \lambda_0$, one solution of the latter differential equation is $1/z(x)$ and its general solution can be found by [d'Alembert's method](wikipedia://en/3421971):

$$Y(x) = \frac{1}{z(x)} \left( C_1 + C_2 \int^x z^2(x) dx \right),$$

where $C_1$ and $C_2$ are arbitrary constants.

:::

## Eigenvalue problems {#Eigenvalue_problems}

Darboux transformation modifies not only the differential equation but also the boundary conditions. This transformation makes it possible to reduce [eigenparameter-dependent boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenparameter-dependent_boundary_conditions) to boundary conditions independent of the eigenvalue parameter – one of the [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/423504), [Neumann](wikipedia://en/735443) or [Robin](wikipedia://en/9945920) conditions.[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16] On the other hand, it also allows one to convert inverse square singularities to Dirichlet boundary conditions and vice versa.[^o17][^o18] Thus Darboux transformations relate eigenparameter-dependent boundary conditions with inverse square singularities.[^o19]
