# Jeffrey Schenker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jeffrey Schenker"}

**Jeffrey Hudson Schenker** (born December 22, 1972) is an American professor of mathematics and current chair of the mathematics department at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University). His research focuses on [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), with particular emphasis on [Anderson localization](wikipedia://en/2213942), [disordered quantum systems](wikipedia://en/25202), and [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Schenker grew up in [Laramie, Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laramie,_Wyoming), and completed all of his undergraduate and master's education at the [University of Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wyoming). He was named a [National Merit Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Merit_Scholarship) (1991–1995) and elected to [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) in 1995. He earned a B.A. with honors in chemistry in 1995, a B.S. with honors in mathematics and a B.S. in physics in 1996, and an M.S. in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in 1997.

He received his Ph.D. in mathematics in 2002 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), with the thesis *Schrödinger Evolution: Localization Bounds and Adiabatic Theorems in the Absence of a Gap Condition*, written under the supervision of [Michael Aizenman](wikipedia://en/22411175). He held an NSF Graduate Fellowship from 1997 to 2000 during his doctoral studies.

## Career {#Career}

Schenker was an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Postdoctoral Fellow at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine) from 2002 to 2003, a postdoctoral fellow at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich) from 2003 to 2005, and an NSF Postdoctoral Fellow and member at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 2005 to 2007. He joined [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) as an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) in 2007, was promoted to associate professor in 2010, and has been a full professor since 2016. He served as Director of Graduate Studies from 2017 to 2020 and has been Chair of Mathematics since 2022.

He has held visiting positions at the [Isaac Newton Institute](wikipedia://en/736837), the [Bernoulli Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne#Schools_&_Colleges), the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452), and [Sorbonne Paris North University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_Paris_North_University).

Schenker's research centers on the rigorous mathematical analysis of physical systems subject to disorder or randomness. His work spans [Anderson localization](wikipedia://en/2213942) for random [Schrödinger operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_operator), quantum diffusion in fluctuating media, ergodic quantum processes, and mathematical models of statistical physics. He has published more than 70 research papers and received sustained external funding from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), including a CAREER award.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2019, Schenker was elected as a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society) in the 2020 class of fellows.
