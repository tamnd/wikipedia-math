# Albert Cohen (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Albert Cohen"}

*Image: CohenAlbert2010 MFO12757.jpg.* Albert Cohen

**Albert Cohen** (born 29 June 1965 in Paris) is a French mathematician, specializing in [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), and digital [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He is, through maternal descent, the grand-nephew of the physicist [Jacques Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Solomon). From 1984 to 1987 Albert Cohen was a student at the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique). In 1990 he defended his doctoral thesis at [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University). His thesis, written under the supervision of [Yves Meyer](wikipedia://en/2096465), is entitled *Ondelettes, analyse multi résolution et traitement numérique du signal* (Wavelets, multiresolution analysis and digital signal analysis)[^o3] From 1990 to 1991 Cohen was a postdoc at [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Laboratories) at Murray Hill. He completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1992 at Paris Dauphine University. From 1993 to 1995 he worked at [ENSTA Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENSTA_Paris). Since 1995 he has been a professor at the *Laboratoire Jacques-Louis Lions* of [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) (Paris 6),[^o4] which is a component of [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University). He is the author of 3 books and the author of co-author of over 100 research articles.[^o5]

Cohen developed in 1990, with [Ingrid Daubechies](wikipedia://en/1430989) and Jean-Christophe Feauveau, the first biorthogonal bases for [wavelets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelets);[^o6] this research has an important application in the image compression standard [JPEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG)-2000. With [Wolfgang Dahmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Dahmen_%28mathematician%29) and [Ronald DeVore](wikipedia://en/27314329), Cohen then worked on the analysis of nonlinear and adaptive approximation methods, with a view to applications in learning theory and in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). He is interested in algorithmic problems involving a very large number of variables, which cause a prohibitive increase in the complexity of calculations. These problems arise in [statistical learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_learning) theory, in the treatment of parametric and [stochastic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/12473239), and in the development of response surfaces from computer software involving adaptive numerical methods.[^o4]

In 2000 Cohen received the [*grand prix Jacques Herbrand*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Herbrand_Prize) from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1995 he won the V. A. Popov Prize in approximation theory from the University of South Carolina and in 2004 the *prix Blaise Pascal* from the French Academy of Sciences. He was elected in 1998 a junior member and in 2013 a senior member of the *Institut Universitaire de France*.[^o7] He was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in Beijing in 2002,[^o8] a plenary speaker at the [International Congress on Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13606431) (ICIAM) in Zurich in 2007, and an invited speaker at the conference [Dynamics, Equations and Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics,_Equations_and_Applications) in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) in 2019.[^o9]

In 2013 Cohen was awarded an [ERC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) [Advanced Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council#Schemes) for a project called BREAD (*Breaking the curse of dimensionality in analysis and simulation*).
