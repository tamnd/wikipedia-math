# Chantal David

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Chantal David"}

**Chantal David** (born 1964) is a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works as a professor of mathematics at [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University). Her interests include [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), [arithmetic statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_statistics), and [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory), and she has shown interest in [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) and [Drinfeld modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinfeld_modules). She is the 2013 winner of the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829), given annually by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) to an outstanding female researcher in mathematics.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

David completed her doctorate in mathematics in 1993 at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), under the supervision of [Ram Murty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Murty).

Her thesis was entitled *Supersingular Drinfeld Modules*.

In the same year, she joined the faculty at Concordia University.

She became the deputy director of the [Centre de Recherches Mathématiques](wikipedia://en/3505436) in 2004.

In 2008, David was an invited professor at [Université Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Henri_Poincaré).

She spent September 2009 through April 2010 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).

From January through May 2017, she co-organized a program on analytic number theory at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute).

## Research {#Research}

In 1999, David published a paper with [Francesco Pappalardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Pappalardi) which proved that the [Lang–Trotter conjecture](wikipedia://en/597837#More_precise_questions) holds in most cases.[^o5]

She has shown that for several families of curves over [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_fields), the zeroes of [zeta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_functions) are compatible with the Katz–Sarnak conjectures.

She has also used random matrix theory to study the zeroes in families of elliptic curves. David and her collaborators have exhibited a new [Cohen–Lenstra phenomenon](wikipedia://en/596282#Real_quadratic_fields) for the group of points of elliptic curves over finite fields.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

David was awarded the [Krieger-Nelson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krieger-Nelson_Prize) by the Canadian Mathematical Society in 2013.
