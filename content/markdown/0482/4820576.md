# Ivan Orlov (philosopher)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ivan Orlov"}

**Ivan Efimovich Orlov** (Russian: Иван Ефимович Орлов; October 25, in [Galich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galich,_Russia), [Kostroma Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostroma_Oblast) – [after](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminus_post_quem) 1936 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow)[^o2]) was a Russian [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), a forerunner of [relevant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relevant_logic) and other [substructural logics](wikipedia://en/412909), and an industrial chemist. The date of his death is unknown, but is most likely between 1936 and 1937.

:::

## Education and Scientific Career {#Education_and_Scientific_Career}

Orlov studied at the Natural Sciences Faculty of [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University). His academic career began in 1916, when he published several papers related to the method of [inductive reasoning](wikipedia://en/393736) and the notion of [inductive proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_proof). For 7 years beginning 1916, he published no scientific work, presumably because of the political turmoil of the era. In the 1920s, he taught in the newly established [Communist Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Academy), and was an officer at the Chemical Institute.

In 1923 Orlov resumed his academic activity, becoming very productive. Most of his papers were published in leading Soviet ideological journals, where he waxed polemical in the manner typical of that place and time. Orlov's work bore on the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), specifically on the so-called [dialectical logic](wikipedia://en/43998406), [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) in nature. He wrote on the [theory of probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_probability), [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology), [theory of music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_music), and on [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering).

## Logic {#Logic}

Analyzing the development of the natural sciences, he sought to uncover their specific "logic." According to Orlov, the laws of thought should be treated as formal rules, bounded by the laws of [identity](wikipedia://en/298428) and [contradiction](wikipedia://en/75807). (When Orlov wrote this, the invention of [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072), the [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329), and the [semantic tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_tableau) all lay in the future.) We must seek the [semantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic) relation between [antecedent](wikipedia://en/804755) and [consequent](wikipedia://en/804778). The main "contradiction of logic" manifests itself in the linkage of premise and corollary, and requires a logic different from the traditional one. If we insist that a corollary be a necessary condition of the premises then, according to Orlov, we necessarily arrive at a [non-Aristotelian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aristotelian_logic), [dialectical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialectical) in nature.

### The Logic of Compatibility Propositions {#The_Logic_of_Compatibility_Propositions}

Orlov proposed a logic of just this type in his work "The Logic of Compatibility of Propositions" published in 1928 in a Soviet mathematical journal. This paper analysed the problem of compatibility (noncompatibility) of propositions through the interpretative prism of an [implication](wikipedia://en/37531624) procedure. He also foresaw the translation of systems with intuitionistic [negation](wikipedia://en/169262) into [S4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S4_%28modal_logic%29) [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) with classical [negation](wikipedia://en/161019).

Orlov's work was very little known for a long time, because his publications, all in Russian, were almost totally unknown outside of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). It is only with the post-World War II rise of what have come to be known as [substructural logics](wikipedia://en/412909) (Restall 2000) that Orlov's pioneering role has gradually emerged. [Substructural logics](wikipedia://en/412909), a category including [intuitionistic](wikipedia://en/169262), relevant, [linear logics](wikipedia://en/579675), etc., can be obtained by restricting the [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072) ("structural") rules for [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868). For example, [relevant logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relevant_logic) does not employ the structural rule of weakening (also called the rule of [monotonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonicity)), and this rule is unlike the other structural rules (Dosen).

Orlov believed in a mechanistic reduction of the laws of nature. He castigated the [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) of [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216), the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) (he believed in the existence of [aether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminiferous_aether)), and the [heliobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliobiology) of A. Chizhevsky.

:::

## Industrial Chemistry {#Industrial_Chemistry}

Around 1928, Orlov ceased publishing logical and philosophical work, turning to industrial [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), specifically the production of [bromine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromine) and [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine), and in translating German works on chemistry into Russian.
