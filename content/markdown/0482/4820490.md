# Nicolai A. Vasiliev

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicolai A. Vasiliev"}

**Nicolai Alexandrovich Vasiliev** (Николай Александрович Васильев), also **Vasil'ev**, **Vassilieff**, **Wassilieff** (11 July 1880 – December 31, 1940), was a Russian [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist), [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet). He was a forerunner of [paraconsistent](wikipedia://en/421085) and [multi-valued logics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-valued_logic).

:::

## Early years {#Early_years}

Vasiliev was born on June 29 [O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style), 1880 in [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). His father, Professor Alexander V. Vasiliev, was a fairly well known mathematician, his grandfather was the outstanding sinologist Professor [Vassily P. Vasiliev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassily_P._Vasiliev), and his great grandfather was the prominent astronomer Ivan M. Simonov, who was a close colleague of [Nikolai Lobachevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Ivanovich_Lobachevsky).

Wanting to be a psychologist, Vasiliev studied at the medical faculty and the historico-philological faculty of [Kazan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_University) (1906), where he was offered the position of privat-dozent in 1910.[^o3]

As a university student, Vasiliev was enthusiastic about [symbolist style poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolism_%28arts%29#Literary_world) and published some books of verses of his own (for example,*"The longing for eternity"*) and translations of the poetry of [Emile Verhaeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emile_Verhaeren) and [Algernon Charles Swinburne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algernon_Charles_Swinburne).

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Imaginary logic"}

## Work in logic {#Work_in_logic}

Although Vasiliev outlined an abstract to the article on the "logic of relatives" by [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) as early as in 1897, it was only in 1908 that he entirely devoted himself to logic.

On May 18, 1910 Vasiliev presented a lecture (published in October that same year) *"On Partial Judgements, on the Triangle of Opposites, on the Law of Excluded Third"* in which he put forward for the first time ever the idea of (non-Aristotelian) logic, free of the [laws of excluded middle](wikipedia://en/17637) and [contradiction](wikipedia://en/17636). Reasoning by analogy with the ["imaginary" geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) of Lobachevsky, Vasiliev called his novel logic "imaginary", for he assumed it was valid for the worlds where the above-mentioned laws did not hold, worlds with beings having other types of sensations. He distinguished levels of logical reasoning, and introduced the notion of metalogic.

Vasiliev spent 1912-13 in [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe) (mostly [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany)) and published his salient works *"Logic and Metalogic"* and *"Imaginary (non-Aristotelian) logic"*. Vasiliev constructed [non-Aristotelian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aristotelian_logic) using the concepts, and even the manner of reasoning, common to [Aristotelian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_logic). He was aware of the achievement in mathematical logic (and even carefully studied [Ernst Schröder](wikipedia://en/448015)'s works) but did not make an attempt to formalise "imaginary" logic.

His only work in a foreign language (English) - a concise abstract of his "imaginary logic" - was published in [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples) in 1924.

:::

## Late years {#Late_years}

In 1914, when [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) broke out, Vasiliev was drafted into the army, where he became seriously [mentally ill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_illness). Nevertheless, he returned to teaching at Kazan University, but in 1922 was forcibly retired by the new [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) administration. This act aggravated his ailment: Vasiliev spent most of the following 20 years in a mental hospital, thus rescued from the [Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) regime. He died on December 31, 1940. The place where he was buried is unknown.[^o7]

The pioneer ideas of Vasiliev were rediscovered in the early 1960s by [Vladimir Smirnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Smirnov_%28philosopher%29), and formed a basis mainly for [paraconsistent logic](wikipedia://en/421085). Some well-known scholars in the 1960s considered his work to be the precursor of [multi-valued logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-valued_logic). The informal style and conceptual riches of Vasiliev's works make them especially valuable. In 2012 an international conference on Vasiliev's work was held in Moscow where a number of important modern paraconsistent logicians contributed.[^o8]
