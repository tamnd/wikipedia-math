# Grassmann–Cayley algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grassmann–Cayley algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Grassmann–Cayley algebra** is the [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) with an additional product, which may be called the [shuffle product](wikipedia://en/32234404) or the regressive product. It is the most general structure in which projective properties are expressed in a coordinate-free way. The technique is based on work by German mathematician [Hermann Grassmann](wikipedia://en/99242) on [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537), and subsequently by British mathematician [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034)'s work on [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). It is a form of modeling [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) for use in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849).

:::

The technique uses [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) as basic elements of computation, a formalism which allows the translation of synthetic geometric statements into [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) algebraic statements. This can create a useful framework for the modeling of [conics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic) and [quadrics](wikipedia://en/145570) among other forms, and in [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) mathematics. It also has a number of applications in [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics), particularly for the [kinematical](wikipedia://en/65914) analysis of manipulators.
