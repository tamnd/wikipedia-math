# Edward Burger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Burger"}

Edward Berger (disambiguation)

**Edward Bruce Burger** (born December 10, 1964)[^o4][^o5] is an American mathematician and President Emeritus of [Southwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_University) in Georgetown, Texas.[^o6][^o7] Previously, he was the Francis Christopher Oakley Third Century Professor of Mathematics at [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College), and the Robert Foster Cherry Professor for Great Teaching at [Baylor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_University). He also had been named to a single-year-appointment as vice provost of strategic educational initiatives at Baylor University in February 2011.[^o8] He currently serves as the president and CEO of St. David's Foundation.[^o9]

:::

Burger has been honored as a leader in education. He has been a keynote speaker, invited special session speaker, or the conference chair at a number of [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), and the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691) conferences.

During the late 1980s Burger was featured at a stand-up comedy club in Austin, Texas and also was an 'independent contractor', writing for Jay Leno.[^o10] Today he has a weekly program on higher education, thinking, and learning produced by [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR)'s Austin affiliate KUT called Higher ED.[^o11]

## Education {#Education}

Burger graduated from [Connecticut College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_College) in 1985, where he had earned [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts), *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude)*, with distinction in mathematics.[^o13] In 1990, he was awarded his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics from the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Texas_at_Austin).[^o13] He did his postdoctoral work at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in Canada.[^o14] In 2013 he was awarded an LL.D. from Williams College.[^o15]

## Career {#Career}

### Research {#Research}

His research interests include [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), [Diophantine analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_analysis), [*p*-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153), and the theory of [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199).[^o13][^o18] He teaches [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), "The Art of Creating Mathematics", and Diophantine analysis.[^o18]

### Teaching {#Teaching}

He has taught or has been a visiting scholar at [The University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Texas_at_Austin), [Westminster College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_College,_Salt_Lake_City), [James Madison University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison_University), the [University of Colorado at Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder), [Concordia University Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University_Texas), [Baylor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_University), and the [Macquarie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macquarie_University) in Australia.[^o18]

Burger is a pioneer in rich, multimedia Internet lectures that, together with written material, form an electronic textbook.[^o20] Together with [Thinkwell](http://www.thinkwell.com/), Burger "crafted the first-ever virtual, CD-ROM video, interactive, mathematics texts/courses"[^o21] published over the World Wide Web. Additionally, his lesson tutorial videos earned publisher [Holt, Rinehart and Winston](https://web.archive.org/web/20080502202132/http://www.hrw.com/) one of the 2007 *Awards of Excellence*[^o22] from *Technology & Learning*, an academic publication.

Burger has written and starred in number of educational videos, including the 24-lecture video series *[Zero to Infinity: A History of Numbers](https://web.archive.org/web/20260610143058/https://shop.thegreatcourses.com/zero-to-infinity-a-history-of-numbers)* and *[An Introduction to Number Theory](https://web.archive.org/web/20260610142607/https://shop.thegreatcourses.com/an-introduction-to-number-theory)*. He has delivered more than 400 lectures worldwide and has appeared on more than 40 radio and TV programs including *[ABC News Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_Now)* on [WABC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WABC-TV) in New York and [National Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Radio).[^o18] He starred in the "Mathletes" episode of NBC's "Science of the Winter Olympics" series shown on the Today Show and throughout the 2010 Winter Olympic Games.

In recognition for his work in multimedia education technology, [The Association of Educational Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Educational_Publishers) awarded Burger with the 2007 *Distinguished Achievement Award* for Educational Video Technology.[^o23]

Burger feels that "math trauma" is commonly inflicted upon America's elementary and middle-school students, particularly girls, having received a seventh-grade report card stating: "Eddie is a nice boy, but he'll never do well in math."[^o5] He offers his students "challenging questions for which the solution is by no means apparent".[^o24] For example, when teaching students about [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), he asked students if it is "possible to take a cord of rope 6 ft long and tie it snugly around your right ankle and your left ankle, take off your pants, turn them inside out, and put your pants back on without ever cutting the rope?" He proceeded to demonstrate the solution to that challenge, wearing huge [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox) boxer shorts under his trousers, at the [Boston Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Public_Library) in the summer of 2005.[^o25]

In addition to his math courses, Burger teaches a short course in comedy writing during the winter study program at Williams. Combining math with comedy comes from his days as a stand-up comic at the [Laff Stop Comedy Club](http://www.austinchronicle.com/gyrobase/Issue/story?oid=oid%3A530510) in [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas) in the late-1980s.[^o5]

### Publications {#Publications}

Burger has written 12 books and has had 37 papers published in scholarly journals.[^o27] With [Michael Starbird](wikipedia://en/10592650), he coauthored [*The Heart of Mathematics: An invitation to effective thinking*](http://www.williams.edu/Mathematics/eburger/texts2.html), for which they won a 2001 [Robert W. Hamilton Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Hamilton_Book_Award), and *Coincidences, Chaos, and All That Math Jazz,*, a humorous look at mathematics filed under both *math* and *humor* in the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) catalog.[^o25] Burger is also an associate editor for the [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) and a member of the editorial board for \[AK Peters Publishing\].[^o18]

## Selected honors and awards {#Selected_honors_and_awards}

Some of the honors and awards Burger has received include:

- 2014 Elected to [The Philosophical Society of Texas](http://www.pstx.org).[^o33]
- 2013 Awarded an Honorary Degree (Doctor of Laws) from [Williams College](https://communications.williams.edu/news-releases/3_13_2013_honorarydegrees/).
- 2013 Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o34]
- 2012 "Global Hero in Education", Named by [Microsoft Corporation](http://dailyedventures.com/?p=7321)[^o23]
- 2010 Telly Award, for the "Mathletes" episode of the NBC-Universal television series "The Science of the Olympic Winter Games" (the entire series won a 2011 Emmy Award) [^o23]
- 2010 *Game Changer*, Named by [The Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Huffington_Post); "HuffPost's Game Changers salutes 100 innovators, visionaries, mavericks, and leaders who are reshaping their fields and changing the world."[^o23]
- 2010 *[Robert Foster Cherry Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Foster_Cherry_Award) for Great Teaching* from [Baylor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_University)[^o23]
- 2007 *Award of Excellence*, for "educational mathematics videos that break new ground", *Technology & Learning* magazine[^o23]
- 2007 *Distinguished Achievement Award*, for Educational Video Technology, [The Association of Educational Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Educational_Publishers)[^o23]
- 2006 "100 Best of America", Listed in [Reader's Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader's_Digest) Annual Special Issue as Best Math Teacher[^o23]
- 2006 [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) for the expository article *A Tail of Two Palindromes*[^o35]
- 2003 Residence Life Academic Teaching Award, University of Colorado at Boulder[^o23]
- 2001 Robert W. Hamilton Book Award, for "*The Heart of Mathematics*"[^o23]
- 1988 Mathematics Teaching Award, The University of Texas at Austin[^o23]

Burger is the Francis Christopher Oakley Third Century Professor of Mathematics and was the Lissack Professor for Social Responsibility and Personal Ethics (2010–2012) [^o23] and the Gaudino Scholar (2008–2010)[^o18] at [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College), where he was also awarded the 2007 *[Nelson Bushnell Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Bushnell_Prize)* for Scholarship and Teaching.[^o23]

Burger has been honored by [The Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) on several occasions: 2001, [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics);[^o36][^o37] 2001–2003, [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) lecturer;[^o38] 2004, *[Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize)*;[^o39][^o40] and 2006, *[Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award)*[^o41][^o35]
