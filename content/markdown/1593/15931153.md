# Model complete theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Model complete theory"}

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) theory is called **model complete** if every embedding of its models is an [elementary embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_embedding). Equivalently, every [first-order formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_formula) is equivalent to a universal formula. This notion was introduced by [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024).

:::

## Model companion and model completion {#Model_companion_and_model_completion}

A **companion** of a theory *T* is a theory *T*\* such that every model of *T* can be embedded in a model of *T*\* and vice versa.

A **model companion** of a theory *T* is a companion of *T* that is model complete. Robinson proved that a theory has at most one model companion. Not every theory is model-companionable, e.g. theory of groups. However if *T* is an $\aleph_0$-[categorical theory](wikipedia://en/1096396), then it always has a model companion.[^n1][^n2]

A **model completion** for a theory *T* is a model companion *T*\* such that for any model *M* of *T*, the theory of *T*\* together with the [diagram](wikipedia://en/59681951) of *M* is [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335). Roughly speaking, this means every model of *T* is embeddable in a model of *T*\* in a unique way.

If *T*\* is a model companion of *T* then the following conditions are equivalent:[^n3]

- *T*\* is a model completion of *T*
- *T* has the [amalgamation property](wikipedia://en/11092492).

If *T* also has universal axiomatization, both of the above are also equivalent to:

- *T*\* has [elimination of quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_of_quantifiers)

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any theory with [elimination of quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_of_quantifiers) is model complete.
- The theory of [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018) is the model completion of the theory of fields. It is model complete but not complete.
- The model completion of the theory of [equivalence relations](wikipedia://en/9259) is the theory of equivalence relations with infinitely many equivalence classes, each containing an infinite number of elements.
- The theory of [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093), in the language of [ordered rings](wikipedia://en/525697), is a model completion of the theory of [ordered fields](wikipedia://en/22430) (or even ordered [domains](wikipedia://en/15462)).
- The theory of real closed fields, in the language of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), is the model companion for the theory of [formally real fields](wikipedia://en/1067223), but is not a model completion.

:::

::: {#o9 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Non-examples"}

## Non-examples {#Non-examples}

- The theory of dense linear orders with a first and last element is complete but not model complete.
- The theory of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) (in a language with symbols for the identity, product, and inverses) has the amalgamation property but does not have a model companion.

:::

## Sufficient condition for completeness of model-complete theories {#Sufficient_condition_for_completeness_of_model-complete_theories}

If *T* is a model complete theory and there is a model of *T* that embeds into any model of *T*, then *T* is complete.[^n4]
