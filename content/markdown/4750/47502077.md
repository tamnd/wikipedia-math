# Yujiro Kawamata

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Yujiro Kawamata"}

**Yujiro Kawamata** (born 1952) is a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Kawamata completed the master's course at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1977. He was an Assistant at the [University of Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mannheim) from 1977 to 1979 and a [Miller Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Research_Fellows) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) from 1981 to 1983. Kawamata is now a professor at the University of Tokyo. He won the Mathematical Society of Japan Autumn award (1988) and the Japan Academy of Sciences award (1990) for his work in algebraic geometry.

## Research {#Research}

Kawamata was involved in the development of the [minimal model program](wikipedia://en/15148924) in the 1980s. The program aims to show that every [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) is [birational](wikipedia://en/382733) to one of an especially simple type: either a minimal model or a Fano fiber space. The [Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawamata-Viehweg_vanishing_theorem), strengthening the [Kodaira vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/2988093), is a method. Building on that, Kawamata proved the basepoint-free theorem. The cone theorem and contraction theorem, central results in the theory, are the result of a joint effort by Kawamata, [Kollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janos_Kollar), [Mori](wikipedia://en/794811), [Reid](wikipedia://en/5863755), and [Shokurov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Shokurov).[^o5]

After Mori proved the existence of minimal models in dimension 3 in 1988, Kawamata and [Miyaoka](wikipedia://en/22717073) clarified the structure of minimal models by proving the [abundance conjecture](wikipedia://en/56768957) in dimension 3.[^o6] Kawamata used analytic methods in [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) to prove the [Iitaka conjecture](wikipedia://en/1866873#Application_to_classification) over a base of dimension 1.[^o7]

More recently, a series of papers by Kawamata related the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of [coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/603780) on an algebraic variety to geometric properties in the spirit of minimal model theory.[^o8]
