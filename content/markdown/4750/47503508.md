# Eugenia Cheng

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eugenia Cheng"}

**Eugenia Loh-Gene Cheng** is a British mathematician, educator and concert pianist. Her mathematical interests include [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013), and as a pianist she specialises in [lieder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lied) and [art song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_song).[^o7] She is also known for explaining mathematics to non-mathematicians to combat [math phobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_phobia), often using analogies with food and baking.[^o8] Cheng is a [scientist-in-residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist-in-residence) at the [School of the Art Institute of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_the_Art_Institute_of_Chicago).[^o9][^o10][^o11]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Cheng was born in [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire), England. She moved to [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex) at the age of one.[^o13] Her family is originally from [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong).[^o14] Her interest in mathematics stemmed from a young age thanks largely to her mother who made mathematics a part of life.[^o13][^o15]

Cheng attended [Roedean School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roedean_School).[^o16] She studied the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where she was a student of [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge). Her postgraduate research was supervised by [Martin Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Hyland).[^o4][^o17][^o18]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

As of 2020, Cheng is a scientist-in-residence at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago, where she teaches mathematics to arts students.[^o9][^o11] Cheng formerly held academic appointments at the [University of Nice Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nice_Sophia_Antipolis), the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield) and the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o11]

She has published over a dozen research papers across several journals within her area of category theory.[^o20]

### Mathematics and baking {#Mathematics_and_baking}

Cheng's research interests are in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), which she has written about for a general audience by using analogies from baking. Her vision is to rid the world of mathematics phobia. In *[How to Bake Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Bake_Pi)*, published on May 5, 2015,[^o9] each chapter begins with a recipe for a dessert, to illustrate the commonalities in the methods and principles of mathematics and cooking. The book was well received[^o13][^o22][^o23] and has since been translated into French.[^o24]

Cheng has also written a number of papers with similar themes, such as *On the perfect quantity of cream for a scone*[^o25] and *On the perfect size for a pizza*.[^o26] Cheng has presented similar topics through YouTube in a light-hearted manner and has explored mathematics in other ways such as in her speech *Mathematics and Lego: the untold story*.[^o27]

### Other writing {#Other_writing}

Cheng's second book, *[Beyond Infinity](wikipedia://en/68598568)*, explains [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) for lay audiences using analogies and anecdotes, including [Cantor's diagonal argument](wikipedia://en/51426) and [Zeno's paradoxes](wikipedia://en/34535).[^o29] It was shortlisted for the 2017 Insight Investment Science Book Prize under the [Royal Society Prizes for Science Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Prizes_for_Science_Books).[^o30]

She published her third book, *The Art of Logic in an Illogical World,* in 2018.[^o31] It explores arguments on real-world topics like [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage), [white privilege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_privilege), and [police brutality in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality_in_the_United_States) using methods from [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), including explanations of [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095) and [Euclid's axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_axioms) on the way.[^o32]

Cheng writes the column *Everyday Math* for *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)*[^o33] on topics including [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [Rubik's Cube](wikipedia://en/25971) solutions.

### Music {#Music}

Cheng is a pianist who specialises in [lieder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieder) and [art song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_song). She was awarded the Sheila Mossman Memorial Award from the [Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Board_of_the_Royal_Schools_of_Music) and was the first recipient of the Brighton and Hove Arts Council Award for the Musician of the Year. In Chicago, she gave a recital in the Pianoforte Chicago recital series; she performed *[Schwanengesang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwanengesang)* and *[Winterreise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterreise)* with [Paul Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Giger) at Schubertiade Chicago in 2005 and 2006 respectively, and *[Die Schöne Müllerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Schöne_Müllerin)* with Ryan de Ryke at Schubertiade Chicago 2007. She performed lieder with tenor Nicholas Harkness in the Noontime Recital Series at the University of Chicago, the Salon Series at the Tower Club, and the Maxwell Recital Series, and she gave recitals for the Auxiliary Board Chapter of the Lyric Opera; she also performed *[La Traviata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Traviata)* at the Oak Park Village Players.[^o35]

In 2013, Cheng founded the Liederstube as an oasis for art song in the Fine Arts Building, in downtown Chicago. The mission of the Liederstube is to present and enjoy classical music in an intimate and informal setting. The Liederstube is a Not For Profit [501(c)(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/501%28c%29%283%29) organisation.[^o36]

### Media appearances {#Media_appearances}

Cheng has appeared on *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert)* making [mille-feuille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mille-feuille) with [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert) in 2015 to demonstrate [exponentials](wikipedia://en/9678).[^o38] She was interviewed for the morning magazine show *The Morning Shift* on Chicago's [Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Radio) station *[WBEZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBEZ)* in 2017.[^o39] She was interviewed by [Jim Al-Khalili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Al-Khalili) for *[The Life Scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_Scientific)* on [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4), first broadcast in January 2018.[^o40] She appeared on the [WGBH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WGBH_%28FM%29) podcast *[Innovation Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation_Hub_%28radio_program%29)* in spring 2018.[^o41]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Cheng met her partner, Peter “Pete” Wesoloski, when she was 39. In a 2024 *Wall Street Journal* article, Cheng wrote about her fertility issues and childlessness.[^o43]
