# Gabriel Stolzenberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gabriel Stolzenberg"}

**Gabriel Stolzenberg** (Brooklyn, New York, 1937 – Watertown, Massachusetts, 2019) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who taught at various academic institutions.

:::

## Early years and education {#Early_years_and_education}

Stolzenberg was born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to Aba Stolzenberg (1905–1966),[^o4] a [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish) poet, and Florence Stolzenberg, also known as Bluma.[^o5] His father and other Yiddish artists would often gather around poet Zishe Landau.[^o6] His elder sister, Ethel, participated in a [social club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_club) for young Jewish girls in [Crown Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Heights,_Brooklyn), called Faithful Friends Forever Club. This club was established around 1938. Ethel went on to receive a Ph.D. in [Biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics) from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). Later, she and her husband, Irwin Tessman, were inducted as members of the [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological) sciences [faculty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_staff) at [Purdue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University).[^o7]

Stolzenberg attended [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School). At the age of sixteen, he went to [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel), where he joined a [kibbutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibbutz) for one year.[^o8] Returning to the States, he entered [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) on a [Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation) [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) to study [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He graduated in 1958 and went on to receive his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Mathematics from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1961.

## Teaching {#Teaching}

Stolzenberg was [Benjamin Peirce](wikipedia://en/364270) [Instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutoring) at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard). He also taught at [Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) and other [northeastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_United_States) institutions, such as [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University). He held [visiting positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). His research field included the theory of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) with several [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable) and [Banach algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_algebras). He published primarily in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* and the *[Acta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/6860708)*.[^o5]

## Philosopher of science {#Philosopher_of_science}

Stolzenberg, influenced by the work of [Errett Bishop](wikipedia://en/321995),[^o12] was a [constructivist in the philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/21059), as well as in [science education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_in_science_education).[^o11][^o12]

He was an active participant in the so-called "[science wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_wars)",[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16] defending [post-modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-modernism) and constructivism against the positions held by scientists and philosophers, as well as against accusations of [relativism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativism).[^o17] Solzenberg engaged publicly and for many years in a dialogue with opponents of the postmodernist approach to sciences and of the use of concepts of physics, mathematics, or chemistry in philosophy and the social sciences,[^o18][^o19] attracting commentary across both positive and social sciences[^o20] and drawing wider attention to these issues.[^o21]

## Private life {#Private_life}

Stolzenberg married Judith Levine (b. 1938)[^o29] soon after graduating from Columbia in 1958.[^o8] He met his second wife, mathematician [Nancy Kopell](wikipedia://en/27067060) (b.1942), while they were both serving at the faculty of [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University).[^o30] Stolzenberg and Levine had two children, Nomi and Daniel Stolzenberg.[^o8]

Nomi joined the [USC Gould School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Gould_School_of_Law) faculty in 1988, with her research focusing mainly on the relationship of law with religion, [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism), [psychoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoanalysis), and literature.[^o31] Daniel[^o32] went on to receive a PhD in [History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History) from [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford) and serve as a [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) of knowledge in [UC Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Davis), specializing in early modern Europe. In 2014, Daniel received the [Howard R. Marraro Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_R._Marraro_Prize) for the best book on [Italian History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_History) published that year. He mainly researches the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science) and [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_scholarship) from the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) to the [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment).[^o33]

Gabriel Stolzenberg and [novelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelist) [Cormac McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormac_McCarthy) were close friends.[^o34]

## Death {#Death}

Stolzenberg died on 19 November 2019, at the age of 82, while being treated for a [neurological disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurological_disorder) in [Watertown, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watertown,_Massachusetts).[^o5]
