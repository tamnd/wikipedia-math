# Subexponential distribution (light-tailed)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subexponential distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), in the context of light-tailed distributions, one definition of a **subexponential distribution** is as a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) whose tails decay at an exponential rate, or faster: a real-valued distribution $\cal D$ is called subexponential if, for a random variable $X\sim {\cal D}$,

:::

${\mathbb P}(|X|\ge x)=O(e^{-K x})$, for large $x$ and some constant $K>0$.

Note that this is almost the opposite of the more established meaning of [subexponential](wikipedia://en/8092200#Subexponential_distributions) in the context of [Heavy-tailed distributions](wikipedia://en/8092200), where "sub" means that the rate of decay is *slower* than exponential, rather than that the tail is *lighter* than exponential.

The **subexponential norm**, $\|\cdot\|_{\psi_1}$, of a random variable is defined by

$\|X\|_{\psi_1}:=\inf\ \{ K>0\mid {\mathbb E}(e^{|X|/K})\le 2\},$ where the infimum is taken to be $+\infty$ if no such $K$ exists.

This is an example of a [Orlicz norm](wikipedia://en/17686010#Orlicz_norm_of_a_random_variable). An equivalent condition for a distribution $\cal D$ to be subexponential is then that $\|X\|_{\psi_1}<\infty.$

Subexponentiality can also be expressed in the following equivalent ways:

1. ${\mathbb P}(|X|\ge x)\le 2 e^{-K x},$ for all $x\ge 0$ and some constant $K>0$.
1. ${\mathbb E}(|X|^p)^{1/p}\le K p,$ for all $p\ge 1$ and some constant $K>0$.
1. For some constant $K>0$, ${\mathbb E}(e^{\lambda |X|}) \le e^{K\lambda}$ for all $0\le \lambda \le 1/K$.
1. ${\mathbb E}(X)$ exists and for some constant $K>0$, ${\mathbb E}(e^{\lambda (X-{\mathbb E}(X))})\le e^{K^2 \lambda^2}$ for all $-1/K\le \lambda\le 1/K$.
1. $\sqrt{|X|}$ is [sub-Gaussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Gaussian).
