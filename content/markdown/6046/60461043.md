# LOTS (personality psychology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="LOTS"}

**LOTS** is an acronym, suggested by [Cattell](wikipedia://en/1145683) in 1957 and later elaborated by Block, to provide a broad classification of data source for [personality psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_psychology) assessment.[^o7] Each data source has its advantage and disadvantage. Research on personality commonly employ different data source so as to represent better the pattern of one's distinctive features.[^o3][^o4]

:::

- L-data, refer to the life-outcome data, such as age, education, income,[^o6] student grades at school, criminal and conviction record[^o5]
- O-data, refer to observational data, such as observer rating from friends and family
- T-data, refer to standardised and objective [test](wikipedia://en/59861) measurement, such as scored test, physiological response, [reaction times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_time) (RT), [implicit association test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implicit-association_test) (IAT)
- S-data, refer to [self-reports](wikipedia://en/4144627), such as [questionnaires](wikipedia://en/956483), [personality test](wikipedia://en/609194), [structured interview](wikipedia://en/2474014)[^o6]
