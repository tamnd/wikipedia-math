# Traveling plane wave

*Image: Plane wave wavefronts 3D.svg.* The [wavefronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavefront) of a traveling plane wave in three-dimensional space.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Traveling plane wave"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), a **traveling plane wave**[^o2] is a special case of [plane wave](wikipedia://en/41559), namely a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) whose evolution in time can be described as simple translation of its values at a constant *[wave speed](wikipedia://en/25098)* $c$, along a fixed *[direction of propagation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_of_propagation)* $\vec n$.

:::

Such a field can be written as

$$F(\vec x, t)=G\left(\vec x \cdot \vec n - c t\right)\,$$

where $G(u)$ is a function of a single real parameter $u = d - c t$. The function $G$ describes the **profile** of the wave, namely the value of the field at time $t = 0$, for each displacement $d = \vec x \cdot \vec n$. For each displacement $d$, the moving plane perpendicular to $\vec n$ at distance $d + c t$ from the origin is called a **[wavefront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavefront)**. This plane too travels along the direction of propagation $\vec n$ with velocity $c$; and the value of the field is then the same, and constant in time, at every one of its points.

The wave $F$ may be a [scalar](wikipedia://en/161879) or [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641); its values are the values of $G$.

A [sinusoidal plane wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinusoidal_plane_wave) is a special case, when $G(u)$ is a [sinusoidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinusoidal) function of $u$.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A traveling plane wave can be studied by ignoring the dimensions of space perpendicular to the vector $\vec n$; that is, by considering the wave $F(z\vec n,t) = G(z - ct)$ on a one-dimensional medium, with a single position coordinate $z$.

For a scalar traveling plane wave in two or three dimensions, the gradient of the field is always collinear with the direction $\vec n$; specifically, $\nabla F(\vec x,t) = \vec n G'(\vec x \cdot \vec n - ct)$, where $G'$ is the derivative of $G$. Moreover, a traveling plane wave $F$ of any shape satisfies the [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564)

$$\nabla F = -\frac{\vec n}{c}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}$$

Plane traveling waves are also special solutions of the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) in an homogeneous medium.
