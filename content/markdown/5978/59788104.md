# Von Bertalanffy function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Von Bertalanffy function"}

The **von Bertalanffy growth function** (**VBGF**), or **von Bertalanffy curve,** is a type of [growth curve](wikipedia://en/44286896) for a [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) and is named after [Ludwig von Bertalanffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Bertalanffy). It is a special case of the [generalised logistic function](wikipedia://en/224011). The [growth curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_curve_%28biology%29) is used to model mean length from age in animals.[^o2] The function is commonly applied in [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) to model fish growth[^o3] and in [paleontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontology) to model [sclerochronological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sclerochronology) parameters of shell growth.[^o4]

:::

The model can be written as the following:

$$L(a)= L_\infty(1-\exp(-k(a-t_0)))$$

where $a$ is age, $k$ is the growth coefficient, $t_0$ is the theoretical age when size is zero, and $L_\infty$ is asymptotic size.[^o5] It is the solution of the following [linear differential equation](wikipedia://en/379868):

$$\frac{dL}{da} = k (L_{\infty} - L )$$

## History {#History}

In 1920, August Pütter proposed that growth was the result of a balance between [anabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabolism) and [catabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catabolism).[^o7] von Bertalanffy, citing Pütter, borrowed this concept and published its equation first in 1941,[^o8] and elaborated on it later on.[^o9] The original equation was under the following form:

$$\frac{dW}{dt} = \eta W^m - \kappa W^n$$

with $W$ the weight, $\eta$ and $\kappa$ constants of anabolism and catabolism respectively, and $m$, $n$ constant exponants. Von Bertalanffy gave himself the resulting equation for $W$ as a function of $t$, assuming that $n=1$ and $m \leq 1$ :[^o9]

$$W = \Biggl(\frac{\eta}{\kappa}-\Bigl(\frac{\eta}{\kappa}-W_0^{1-m}\Bigr)e^{-(1-m)\kappa t}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{1-m}}$$

Prior to von Bertalanffy, in 1921, J. A. Murray wrote a similar differential equation,[^o10] with $m = \frac{2}{3}$, according to the then-called "surface law", and $n = 1$, but Murray's article does not appear in von Bertalanffy's sources.

## Seasonally-adjusted von Bertalanffy {#Seasonally-adjusted_von_Bertalanffy}

The seasonally-adjusted von Bertalanffy is an extension of this function that accounts for organism growth that occurs seasonally. It was created by I. F. Somers in 1988.[^o12]
