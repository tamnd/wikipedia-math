# Copenhagen General Population Study

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Copenhagen General Population Study"}

*Image: Herlev\_Hospital,\_with\_the\_2022\_construction\_additions,\_seen\_from\_Herlev\_Ringvej\_2023\_(cropped).jpg.* [Herlev Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herlev_Hospital) (2023), where the population survey takes place and is headquartered.

The **Copenhagen General Population Study** (**CGPS**; Herlev/Østerbroundersøgelsen) is an ongoing Danish [longitudinal](wikipedia://en/1557623) [cohort study](wikipedia://en/397271), that investigates the [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) of a wide range of [diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease), in particular [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease), [respiratory disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_disease), [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer), [infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection) and other [multifactorial diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifactorial_disease), in a representative sample of the [Danish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Denmark).[^o2][^o3] Headquartered at the Department of [Clinical Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_chemistry) at [Herlev Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herlev_Hospital), it was established in 2003 with professor [Børge Nordestgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Børge_Nordestgaard) as [principal investigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_investigator), and it is the world's largest [population study](wikipedia://en/16081079) that follows the long-term health development of a healthy population over time, currently collecting data on over 170,000 enrolled [participants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_participant).[^o4][^o5]

:::

Expanding upon the earlier and less extensive sister study, the **Copenhagen** **City** **Heart Study** (CCHS; Østerbroundersøgelsen), the population survey has been conducted in several phases since 1976, incipiently involving a representative [sample](wikipedia://en/160361) of people in the [Østerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Østerbro) neighbourhood of [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen). In 2003, the CGPS was established to expand the study to cover an increasingly large area in the [Copenhagen metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_metropolitan_area). A second phase is currently in progress for the study, whereas a sixth is underway under the auspices of CCHS.[^o3] All participants are recalled for follow-up examinations, and are followed in the [Danish Civil Registration System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Det_Centrale_Personregister) and [medical records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_record).[^n1] The survey includes a comprehensive [physical examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_examination), preceded by a [questionnaire](wikipedia://en/956483) on lifestyle [factors](wikipedia://en/909227). The examination covers heart and [lung function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirometry), measurements of height, weight, [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure), [ankle-brachial pressure index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankle–brachial_pressure_index), extensive [blood sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venipuncture) [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_test) and other clinical examinations.[^o7]

Since 1976, the study and its precursor have produced over 1,330 [scientific articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_literature) in the [medical press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_literature), including significant research in [general](wikipedia://en/66997) and [genetic epidemiology](wikipedia://en/16017459). Notable findings include the positive impact of regular [physical activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_activity) on lifespan, the health benefits of moderate [red wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wine) consumption, and the role of [triglycerides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglyceride) and [genetic predisposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_predisposition) in developing various diseases.[^o2][^o3]

As of 2023, 27 [doctoral theses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) and 96 [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) dissertations have been based on the study's data, with around 20 PhD students and hundreds of other researchers currently working with the data.[^o3]

## History {#History}

### Precursor {#Precursor}

The Copenhagen General Population Study is an offshoot of the Copenhagen City Heart Study (CCHS), known in Danish as *Østerbroundersøgelsen*. The CCHS was launched in 1976 by Professor [Anders Tybjærg Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Tybjærg_Hansen) and [cardiologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiology) Dr. Peter Schnohr, Dr. Gorm Jensen and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) Jørgen Nyboe, and began as a large [cardiovascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease)-focused study.

The study was launched in response to a sharp [post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war) [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) increase in mortality from [acute myocardial infarction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction) (AMI) in Denmark, particularly in the [capital region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Region_of_Denmark). From the 1950s onwards, cardiovascular disease emerged as a leading [cause of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cause_of_death), yet knowledge about its causes and [risk factors](wikipedia://en/909227) remained limited. Inspired by large-scale [epidemiological initiatives](wikipedia://en/66997#Types_of_studies) such as the [Framingham Heart Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framingham_Heart_Study) (US), the [Seven Countries Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Countries_Study) ([Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancel_Keys)), and the [Whitehall Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehall_Study) (UK), Danish Professor Tybjærg Hansen initiated the national cohort study. It incipiently received funding from the [Danish Heart Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Heart_Foundation) and support from [municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Denmark) health authorities.[^o10]

Although designed with a large emphasis on [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease), specifically [coronary heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease), [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension) and [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke), it quickly expanded to include a wide array of health conditions, such as [pulmonary diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_disease), [heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_failure), [arrhythmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhythmia), [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia), and various genetic and [psychosocial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosocial) factors.[^o11] The initial study population of the Copenhagen City Heart Study consisted of a random sample of nearly 20,000 men and women, aged 20 to 93, selected from approximately 90,000 residents aged 20 and older in ten wards around [Rigshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigshospitalet) in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen), encompassing all of [Østerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Østerbro) and one-third of [Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nørrebro), [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District) of [Copenhagen Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Municipality).[^o12]

### Establishment of the study {#Establishment_of_the_study}

The Copenhagen General Population Study (CGPS) was established on 26 November 2003 at Herlev Hospital under the leadership of Professor [Børge Nordestgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Børge_Nordestgaard). Designed as an expansion of this precursor study, it broadened the scope to include a larger and more diverse cohort from the [Copenhagen metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_metropolitan_area). Initially, the study recruited over 100,000 participants aged 20 to 100, who contributed through [questionnaires](wikipedia://en/956483), [biological samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_%28medicine%29), and [physical examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_examination). In 2010, ethical approval enabled the integration of cardiac [CT imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan), providing insights into cardiovascular [biomarkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomarker).[^o14] The cohort has since expanded to include over 170,000 enrolled participants.

A key feature of the CGPS and its predecessor studies is the integration with Denmark’s comprehensive [national registries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_identification_number_%28Denmark%29). These registries provide detailed and updated information on participants, including hospital [discharge diagnoses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_diagnosis), cancer records, causes of death, [prescription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescription_drug) use, and [demographic](wikipedia://en/45642) data such as education and income. This infrastructure ensures nearly 100% [follow-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follow-up_studies), eliminating a common source of bias in [longitudinal studies](wikipedia://en/1557623). The registries also enable cost-effective management of large cohorts and allow disease-free participants to serve as controls for case-control studies.[^o15]

## Scientific impact and findings {#Scientific_impact_and_findings}

### Scientific findings {#Scientific_findings}

Numerous highly cited [scientific articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_literature) have been published on the results of the studies, for example that 65–79 year olds who start [exercising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise) about 4 hours a week will increase their lifespan by an average of 4 years.[^o2]

One highly publicized result showed that moderate [red wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wine) consumption was associated with a significantly reduced risk of [myocardial infarction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction) and lower [all-cause mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-cause_mortality). The finding was cited by [U.S. President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton) and featured on [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS)’s *[60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_60_Minutes)*, sparking global interest.[^o10]

Other findings from the studies include the clinical implication of elevated [triglyceride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglyceride) levels for the development of [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease) and the importance of [genetic predisposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_predisposition) for the development of cardiovascular disease, [lung disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_disease) and [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer), among others.[^o2]

For example, in cardiovascular health, its findings established that elevated nonfasting [triglyceride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglyceride) levels correlate with a heightened risk of [myocardial infarction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction), [ischaemic heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease), and [mortality](wikipedia://en/191305) in both men and women.[^o18] Additionally, the study revealed nuanced effects of [alcohol consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) on [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure), showing that different types of alcoholic beverages may influence blood pressure differently.[^o19]

In the field of genetic research, CGPS researchers has employed [Mendelian randomization](wikipedia://en/10453294) to uncover the [causal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality) links between long-term [alcohol consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) and [coronary heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease) risk factors.[^o20] In respiratory health, CGPS provided pivotal data on early identification of [chronic obstructive pulmonary disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_obstructive_pulmonary_disease) (COPD), emphasizing the roles of age and smoking exposure in disease progression and associated risks.[^o21]

The study's findings in [metabolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism) health revealed that increased [triglyceride metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_metabolism), characterized by elevated levels of [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cell) [glycerol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycerol) and [β-hydroxybutyrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Β-Hydroxybutyric_acid), is strongly linked to higher risks of [all-cause mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-cause_mortality), including deaths from cardiovascular diseases and cancer. These discoveries underscore the CGPS's broad impact on understanding the interplay of lifestyle, genetics, and metabolic factors in health outcomes.[^o22]
