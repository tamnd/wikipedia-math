# Berkson error model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Berkson error model"}

The **Berkson error model** is a description of [random error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_error) (or misclassification) in [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022). Unlike [classical error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_error), Berkson error causes little or no bias in the measurement. It was proposed by [Joseph Berkson](wikipedia://en/9875090) in an article entitled “Are there two regressions?,”[^o2] published in 1950.

:::

An example of Berkson error arises in [exposure assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposure_assessment) in epidemiological studies. Berkson error may predominate over [classical error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_error) in cases where exposure data are highly aggregated. While this kind of error reduces the [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power) of a study, risk estimates themselves are not themselves attenuated (as would be the case where [random error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_error) predominates).
