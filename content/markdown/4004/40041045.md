# Attilio Palatini

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Attilio Palatini"}

**Attilio Palatini** (18 November 1889 – 24 August 1949) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) born in [Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treviso).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Palatini was the seventh of the eight children of Michele (1855-1914) and Ilde Furlanetto (1856-1895). In 1900, during the celebrations for the election of his father to Parliament, he was blinded by a young man from Treviso, losing the use of one eye. He completed his secondary studies in Treviso. He graduated in mathematics in 1913 at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), where he was a student of [Ricci-Curbastro](wikipedia://en/499223) and of [Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180).

He taught [rational mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) at the [Universities of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Messina), [Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Parma) and [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia). He was mainly involved in [absolute differential calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_differential_calculus) and in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). Within this latter subject he gave a sound generalization of the [variational principle](wikipedia://en/491097).

In 1919, Palatini wrote an important article where he proposed a new approach to the variational formulation of [Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s gravitational field equations.[^o6] In the same paper, Palatini also showed that the variations of [Christoffel symbols](wikipedia://en/1401020) constitute the coordinate components of a [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965).

He wrote the "Rational Mechanics" and "Theory of relativity" entries for the *Hoepli Encyclopedia of Elementary mathematics*.
