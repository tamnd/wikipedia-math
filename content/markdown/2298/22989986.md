# Nicolas de Malézieu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicolas de Malézieu"}

*Image: Nicolas de Malezieu - Coste 14372.jpg.* Nicolas de Malezieu

**Nicolas de Malézieu** (or **Malézieux**)[^o2] (or **Malesieu**)[^o3] (7 September 1650, in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) – 4 March 1727, in Paris) was a French intellectual, [Greek scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_literature) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o4]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Nicolas de Malézieu was a [squire](wikipedia://en/676591) and lord of [Chatenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Châtenay-Malabry). He later became chancellor of [Dombes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dombes) and secretary-general to the [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) and [Grisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Leagues) of France. He was the tutor of [Louis Auguste, Duke of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Auguste,_Duke_of_Maine) (to whom he introduced [Bossuet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Bénigne_Bossuet)) and he declaimed the plays of [Euripides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides) and [Sophocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophocles) to the [duchess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Bénédicte_de_Bourbon) who had made her chateau of [Sceaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Château_de_Sceaux) into a literary salon. Here he became a member of the light-hearted fraternity she founded, the (fr) Knights of the Bee, and organised the festivals she loved, the fr:Grandes Nuits de Sceaux. Later tutor to [duc de Bourgogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis,_Duke_of_Burgundy), he was appointed to the [Académie royale des sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_sciences_%28France%29) in 1699 and to the [Académie française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_française) in 1701.[^o6]

Malézieu collected and published the lessons in mathematics that he gave to the duc de Bourgogne over four years in 1705 as *Élémens de géométrie de Mgr le duc de Bourgogne*. Le *[Journal des savants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_des_savants)* reported in detail the observations he made in this work on geometry and infinitely small numbers. In 1713, this work was translated into Latin as *Serenissimi Burgundiae Ducis Elementa Geometrica, ex Gallico Semone in Latinum translata ad Usum Seminarii Patavini*. A third (posthumous) edition, with corrections and a supplemental treatise on logarithms, appeared 1729.[^o4]

Nicolas de Malézieu also translated [Euripides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides)’ *[Iphigenia in Tauris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iphigenia_in_Tauris)* as well as poems, songs and sketches, which were published in 1712 in *Les Divertissements de Sceaux* and in 1725 in the *Suite des Divertissements*. Among these pieces are *Philémon et Baucis*, *Le Prince de Cathay*, *Les Importuns de Chatenay*, *La Grande Nuit de l'éclipse*, *L'Hôte de Lemnos*, *La Tarentole*[^o7] and *L'Heautontimorumenos*. Often written in a single day, these pieces were set to music and staged for the amusement of the duchess, to whom Malézieu also gave courses in astronomy.

A four-volume work, a *Histoire des fermes du roi* (*History of Royal Farms*) survives only a manuscript version dating from 1746.[^o8]

[Pierre-Édouard Lémontey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Édouard_Lémontey) said of Malézieu "Knowing a bit about everything, he gathered in his servile person all the advantages of universal mediocrity."[^o6]

## Family {#Family}

Malézieu was the son of Nicolas de Malézieu (1612-1652) and Marie des Forges (d.1680).[^o4] His brother Michel Louis de Malézieu married Marie Jérônime Mac Carthy (d.1714)[^o10]

In 1672 Malézieu married Françoise Faudel de Fauveresse (1650-1741) by whom he had the following children:[^o11]

- Nicolas de Malézieu (1674-1748), bishop of Lavaur en 1713.[^o12]
- Pierre de Malézieu (1680-1756), married Louise Marthe Stoppa in 1717 (d.1720), under-secretary to the duc de Maine, secretary-general of the Swiss and Grisons in 1727, brigadier of the infantry, ifnanterie, [maréchal de camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maréchal_de_camp) in 1734, lieutenant general of the artillery and commander of the [Order of Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint-Louis) in 1756.
- Charles-François de Malézieu (d.1763), lieutenant-colonel of a brigade of [carabiniers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabinier), cavalry brigadier in 1745, governor of the harbour and defences of [La Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rochelle).
- Élisabeth de Malézieu (b.1676), married (1699) Antoine des Rioux de Missimy, first president of the parlement and intendant of Dombes
- Marie de Malézieu (b.1682), married (1705) Louis de Guiry, seigneur de Noncourt and la Roncière, [mestre de camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestre_de_camp) of the cavalry, lieutenant general of [Aunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aunis) and [la Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rochelle)
