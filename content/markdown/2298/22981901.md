# Melvin J. Hinich

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Melvin J. Hinich"}

**Melvin Jay "Mel" Hinich** (April 29, 1939 – September 6, 2010) [^o3] was a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government) and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin).[^o4] Hinich was also a [research professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_professor) at UT's Applied Research Laboratories.[^o4] Known as an expert in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) with a long record of distinction in a number of fields, he wrote seven books and published more than 200 papers in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685)/[statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579), [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), economics, political science, [biomedical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_engineering), [pharmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacy), and [library science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_science).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hinich was born on April 29, 1939, in [Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania). In 1966, he married Sonje Gregg and the couple had a daughter, Amy Leksana.

## Education {#Education}

Hinich attended [Carnegie Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) where he earned a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1959 as well as an [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.S.) in Mathematics in 1960.[^o4] In 1963, he earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o4][^o5] Hinich's advisor while at Stanford was [Herman Chernoff](wikipedia://en/1181131).[^o8]

## Editorial experience and professional affiliations {#Editorial_experience_and_professional_affiliations}

At various times throughout his career, Hinich served as editor for *[Macroeconomic Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_Dynamics)*, *[Society for Nonlinear Dynamics and Econometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Electronic_Press)*, *[Journal of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/16179313)*, and *[Journal of Mathematical Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Mathematical_Sociology)*.[^o10] He became a fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1973, a fellow of the [Public Choice Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Choice_Society) in 1988, and was president of that organization from 1992 to 1994.[^o10] Hinich was elected as a fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 2002.[^o11]

## Academic influence {#Academic_influence}

[Michael Munger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Munger) was reported to have been heavily influenced by Hinich.[^o13]

## Death {#Death}

Hinich died on September 6, 2010, after falling down a staircase.[^o5] He is survived by his wife and daughter.[^o5]
