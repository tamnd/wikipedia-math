# Head injury criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Head injury criterion"}

The **head injury criterion** (**HIC**) is a measure of the likelihood of [head injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_injury) arising from an impact. The HIC can be used to assess safety related to vehicles, personal protective gear, and sport equipment.

:::

Normally the variable is derived from the measurements of an accelerometer mounted at the [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) of a [crash test dummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crash_test_dummy)’s head, when the dummy is exposed to crash forces.

It is defined as: $\mathit{H}\mathit{I}\mathit{C} = \max_{t_1,t_2} \bigg\{ (t_{2}-t_{1})\cdot \bigg[\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}} \int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} a(t) dt \bigg]^{2.5} \bigg\}$ [^o2][^o3] where *t*<sub>1</sub> and *t*<sub>2</sub> are the initial and final times (in seconds) chosen to maximize HIC, and [acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443) *a* is measured in *g*s ([standard gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gravity) acceleration). The time duration, *t*<sub>2</sub> – *t*<sub>1</sub>, is limited to a maximum value of 36 ms,[^o2][^o3] usually 15 ms.[^o4]

This means that the HIC includes the effects of head acceleration and the duration of the acceleration. Large accelerations may be tolerated for very short times.

At a HIC of 1000, there is an 18% probability of a severe head injury, a 55% probability of a serious injury and a 90% probability of a moderate head injury to the average adult.[^o5]

## Automobile safety {#Automobile_safety}

HIC is used to determine the U.S. [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_Traffic_Safety_Administration) (NHTSA) star [rating for automobile safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_safety_rating) and to determine ratings given by the [Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_Institute_for_Highway_Safety).[^o7]

According to the [Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_Institute_for_Highway_Safety), head injury risk is evaluated mainly on the basis of head injury criterion. A value of 700 is the maximum allowed under the provisions of the U.S. advanced airbag regulation (NHTSA, 2000) and is the maximum score for an "acceptable" IIHS rating for a particular vehicle.[^o7]

A HIC-15 (meaning a measure of impact over 15 milliseconds) of 700 is estimated to represent a 5 percent risk of a severe injury (Mertz et al., 1997). A "severe" injury is one with a score of 4+ on the Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) [^o8]

Data for specific vehicles can be found on various automotive review websites. Some sample data is as follows, for comparative purposes:

- The 1998 [Ford Windstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Windstar), marketed as one of the safest minivans of that year, tested out to a HIC=305 score for driver [^o9]
- A small car, a 1998 [Dodge Neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_Neon), tested at HIC=265.[^o10]
- A common family sedan, a 1998 [Toyota Camry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry), tested at HIC=288.[^o11]
- A 2007 Camry at HIC=175.[^o12]

[^o13]

A comprehensive searchable database of vehicles and their HIC scores is available at [safercar.gov](http://www.safercar.gov/portal/site/safercar/) (2010-05-22). <https://web.archive.org/web/20100522231124/http://www.safercar.gov/portal/site/safercar/>.

## Athletics and recreation {#Athletics_and_recreation}

[Sport physiologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_physiology) and [biomechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomechanics) experts use the HIC in the research of safety equipment and guidelines for competitive sport and recreation.[^o15] In one study, [concussions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concussion) were found to occur at HIC=250 in most athletes.[^o16] Studies have been conducted in skiing and other sports to test adequacy of helmets [^o17]
