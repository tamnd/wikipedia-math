# Carol Karp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carol Karp"}

**Carol Karp**, born **Carol Ruth Vander Velde** (10 August 1926 in [Forest Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown_Charter_Township,_Michigan), [Ottawa County, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_County,_Michigan) – 20 August 1972 in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Virginia)), was an American mathematician of Dutch ancestry best known for her work on [infinitary logic](wikipedia://en/630478). She also played [viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola) in an all-women orchestra. She is the namesake of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438)'s [Karp Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karp_Prize).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Karp was born Carol Ruth van der Velde on 10 August 1926 in Forest Grove in [Ottawa County, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_County,_Michigan) to a farming supply store manager and a housewife. The family moved to Ohio when she was 11, and she and her siblings graduated from high school there. She graduated from [Alliance High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_High_School_%28Ohio%29) in 1944. Next she attended [Manchester University, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University_%28Indiana%29), earning her [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in 1948. She then went back to Michigan to study at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) (then called Michigan State College), where she earned a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in mathematics in 1950. She then spent time as a [violist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola) in an all-women orchestra before returning to mathematics.

In 1951 she married Arthur Louis Karp and took his last name. She continued her graduate study in mathematics while traveling to California and Japan with her husband, who worked in the US Navy. She completed her Ph.D. in 1959 at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) under the supervision of [Leon Henkin](wikipedia://en/356165). Her dissertation, in [formal language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language_theory) and [infinitary logic](wikipedia://en/630478), was *Languages with Expressions of Infinite Length*; she later published it as a book with the same title (North–Holland Publishing, 1964).

## Career {#Career}

Even before completing her doctorate, Karp had taken a faculty position in 1958 at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), where her husband Arthur also taught. She was promoted to full professor in 1966 after only seven years and she became a "leader in the developing theory of infinitary logic". She was particularly known for applying methods of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) to logic, thus advancing [algebraic logic](wikipedia://en/7870034). In 1969 she was diagnosed with [breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer) but remained active until her death three years later on 20 August 1972 in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Virginia).

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The [Karp Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karp_Prize) of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438) is named in her honor. The cash prize was established in 1973 and is awarded every five years for a "connected body of research, most of which has been completed in the time since the previous prize was awarded."
