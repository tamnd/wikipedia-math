# Matrix determinant lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matrix determinant lemma"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), the **matrix determinant lemma** computes the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the sum of an [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) **A** and the [dyadic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyadic_product), **u v**<sup>T</sup>, of a column [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28mathematics%29) **u** and a row vector **v**<sup>T</sup>.[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose **A** is an invertible [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) and **u**, **v** are column vectors. Then the matrix determinant lemma states that

$$\det(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{uv}^\textsf{T}) = (1 + \mathbf{v}^\textsf{T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u})\,\det(\mathbf{A})\,.$$

Here, **uv**<sup>T</sup> is the [outer product](wikipedia://en/103109) of two vectors **u** and **v**.

The theorem can also be stated in terms of the [adjugate matrix](wikipedia://en/202840) of **A**:

$$\det(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{uv}^\textsf{T}) = \det(\mathbf{A}) + \mathbf{v}^\textsf{T}\mathrm{adj}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{u}\,,$$

in which case it applies whether or not the matrix **A** is invertible.

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

First the proof of the special case **A** = **I** follows from the equality:[^o8]

$$\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{I} & 0 \\ \mathbf{v}^\textsf{T} & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{I} + \mathbf{uv}^\textsf{T} & \mathbf{u} \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{I} & 0 \\ -\mathbf{v}^\textsf{T} & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{I} & \mathbf{u} \\ 0 & 1 + \mathbf{v}^\textsf{T}\mathbf{u} \end{pmatrix}.$$

The determinant of the left hand side is the product of the determinants of the three matrices. Since the first and third matrix are [triangular matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_matrices) with unit diagonal, their determinants are just 1. The determinant of the middle matrix is our desired value. The determinant of the right hand side is simply (1 + **v**<sup>T</sup>**u**). So we have the result:

$$\det(\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{uv}^\textsf{T}) = 1 + \mathbf{v}^\textsf{T}\mathbf{u}.$$

Then the general case can be found by setting **u** to be **A**<sup>−1</sup>**u**:

$$\begin{aligned} \det(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{uv}^\textsf{T}) &= \det(\mathbf{A}) \det(\mathbf{I} + (\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u})\mathbf{v}^\textsf{T})\\ &= \det(\mathbf{A}) \left(1 + \mathbf{v}^\textsf{T} (\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u})\right). \end{aligned}$$

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

If the determinant and inverse of **A** are already known, the formula provides a numerically cheap way to compute the determinant of **A** corrected by the matrix **uv**<sup>T</sup>. The computation is relatively cheap because the determinant of **A** + **uv**<sup>T</sup> does not have to be computed from scratch (which in general is expensive). Using [unit vectors](wikipedia://en/167053) for **u** and/or **v**, individual columns, rows or elements[^o11] of **A** may be manipulated and a correspondingly updated determinant computed relatively cheaply in this way.

When the matrix determinant lemma is used in conjunction with the [Sherman–Morrison formula](wikipedia://en/2556798), both the inverse and determinant may be conveniently updated together.

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

Suppose **A** is an invertible *n*-by-*n* matrix and **U**, **V** are *n*-by-*m* matrices. Then

$$\det(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{UV}^\textsf{T}) = \det(\mathbf{I_m} + \mathbf{V}^\textsf{T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{U})\det(\mathbf{A}).$$

In the special case $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{I_n}$ this is the [Weinstein–Aronszajn identity](wikipedia://en/8641870).

Given additionally an invertible *m*-by-*m* matrix **W**, the relationship can also be expressed as

$$\det(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{UWV}^\textsf{T}) = \det(\mathbf{W}^{-1} + \mathbf{V}^\textsf{T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{U})\det(\mathbf{W})\det(\mathbf{A}).$$

:::

## See also {#See_also}

- The [Sherman–Morrison formula](wikipedia://en/2556798), which shows how to update the inverse, **A**<sup>−1</sup>, to obtain (**A** + **uv**<sup>T</sup>)<sup>−1</sup>.
- The [Woodbury formula](wikipedia://en/430493), which shows how to update the inverse, **A**<sup>−1</sup>, to obtain (**A** + **UCV**<sup>T</sup>)<sup>−1</sup>.
- The [binomial inverse theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_inverse_theorem) for (**A** + **UCV**<sup>T</sup>)<sup>−1</sup>.
