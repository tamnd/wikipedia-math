# Robert Remak (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Remak"}

**Robert Erich Remak** (14 February 1888 – 13 November 1942) was a German mathematician. He is chiefly remembered for his work in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) ([Remak decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remak_decomposition)). His other interests included [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255) and [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153). Robert Remak was the son of the neurologist [Ernst Julius Remak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Julius_Remak) and the grandson of the embryologist [Robert Remak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Remak). He was murdered in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Robert Remak was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin). He studied at [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) under [Ferdinand Georg Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048) and received his doctorate in 1911. His dissertation, *Über die Zerlegung der endlichen Gruppen in indirekte unzerlegbare Faktoren* ("On the decomposition of a finite group into indirect indecomposable factors") established that any two decompositions of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) into a [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) are related by a central automorphism. A weaker form of this statement, uniqueness, was first proved by [Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494) in 1909. Later the theorem was generalized by [Wolfgang Krull](wikipedia://en/964328) and [Otto Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Schmidt) to some classes of infinite groups and became known as the [Krull–Schmidt theorem](wikipedia://en/21100715) or the **Krull–Remak–Schmidt theorem**.

Although the dissertation was first submitted in 1911, it was rejected several times and Remak did not obtain his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) until 1929. In the meantime, he wrote several papers on the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153). Between 1929 and 1933 Remak lectured as a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at Humboldt University. In the 1929 essay *Kann die Volkwirtschaftslehre eine exakte Wissenschaft werden?* ("Can economics become an exact science?"), Remak analyzed price formation in socialist and capitalist economies. He also anticipated the role played by digital [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) in [numerical solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_solution) of [systems of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087). Remak's analysis may have influenced [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), who was a fellow lecturer in Berlin, but most of it has not been translated into English and it remains little known and appreciated in the English-speaking world.[^o4] In 1932 Remak published a paper giving a lower bound for the [regulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulator_%28mathematics%29) of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) in terms of the numbers *r*<sub>1</sub> and *r*<sub>2</sub> of real embeddings and pairs of complex embeddings. He went on to investigate relations between the regulator and the [discriminant of an algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/3022963), isolating an important class of [CM-fields](wikipedia://en/10240442) ("fields with unit defect"). His last two papers on the subject appeared in *[Compositio Mathematica](wikipedia://en/31882322)* in 1952 and 1954, more than ten years after his death.

*Image: Stolperstein Robert Remak, Berlin 05.jpg.* [Stolperstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolpersteine) for Remak in [Berlin-Lichterfelde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Lichterfelde)

After the [Nazis seized power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung) in 1933 and the [Civil Service Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_for_the_Restoration_of_the_Professional_Civil_Service) was passed a few months later, Remak, who was of Jewish ancestry, lost his right to teach in September 1933. He was arrested on [Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht), 9 November 1938, and was interned at [Sachsenhausen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp) for several weeks. After an unsuccessful campaign by his wife to secure a permission for him to emigrate to the United States, he was released and permitted to leave for [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam). In 1942, however, he was arrested by the German occupational authorities in the Netherlands and deported to [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz), where he was murdered.[^o5]
