# Jaakko Hintikka

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jaakko Hintikka"}

**Kaarlo Jaakko Juhani Hintikka** (; ; 12 January 1929 – 12 August 2015) was a Finnish and American [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician). Hintikka is regarded as the founder of formal [epistemic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_logic) and of [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121) for logic. He was awarded the [Rolf Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Schock_Prizes) for philosophy in 2005, and he was chief editor of the philosophical journal *[Synthese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthese)* 1965–2002.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hintikka was born in [Helsingin maalaiskunta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsingin_maalaiskunta) (now Vantaa) outside of Helsinki, Finland on 12 January 1929.[^o4]

In 1953, he received his doctorate from the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki) for a thesis on [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic) entitled *Distributive Normal Forms in the Calculus of Predicates*. He was a student of [Georg Henrik von Wright](wikipedia://en/12250).[^o5] After his doctoral study, he became a [Junior Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Fellow) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) (1956–1959).[^o16]

## Career {#Career}

Hintikka held professorial appointments at the University of Helsinki (1959–1970), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (1965–1982, partially visiting), [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University) (1978–1990), and finally [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) from 1990 until his death in 2015.[^o7][^o16] He was the prolific author or co-author of over 30 books and over 300 scholarly articles. Hintikka contributed to [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439), [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), [language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), and the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science). His works have appeared in over nine languages. In 2005, he won the [Rolf Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Schock_Prize) in logic and philosophy "for his pioneering contributions to the logical analysis of modal concepts, in particular the concepts of knowledge and belief".[^o8]

Hintikka served as chief editor of the philosophical journal *[Synthese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthese)* from 1965 to 2002,[^o16] and he was a consultant editor for more than ten journals. He was a vice president of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438) (1968–1971),[^o9] a member of the [American Philosophical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Association) and president of its Pacific division (1975–1976),[^o16][^o10] a member of the governing board of the [Philosophy of Science Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_Science_Association) (1970–1974),[^o16] president of the [Florida Philosophical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Philosophical_Association) (1984–1985),[^o11] the first vice-president of the [Fédération Internationale des Sociétés de Philosophie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Philosophical_Societies) (1993–1998),[^o16] a vice-president and president of the [Institut International de Philosophie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_International_de_Philosophie) (1993–1996; 1999–2002),[^o16] as well as a president of the [Charles S. Peirce Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_S._Peirce_Society) (1997)[^o12] and the [International Union of History and Philosophy of Science](wikipedia://en/33072433).[^o16]

He was a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o13] On May 26, 2000, Hintikka received an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from the Faculty of History and Philosophy at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden).[^o14]

## Death {#Death}

Hintikka died on 12 August 2015 in [Porvoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porvoo), Finland.[^o16]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Early in his career, he devised a [semantics of modal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics_of_modal_logic) essentially analogous to [Saul Kripke](wikipedia://en/48039)'s [frame semantics](wikipedia://en/911960), and discovered the now widely taught [semantic tableau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_tableau) independently of [Evert Willem Beth](wikipedia://en/2170919).[^o18] Later, he worked mainly on [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121), and on [independence-friendly logic](wikipedia://en/1974007), known for its "[branching quantifiers](wikipedia://en/3573834)", which he believed do better justice to our intuitions about [quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29) than does conventional [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983).[^o19] He did important exegetical work on [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308), [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant), [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), and [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117). Hintikka's work can be seen as a continuation of the analytic tendency in philosophy founded by [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano) and Peirce, advanced by [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416) and [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163), and continued by [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987), [Willard Van Orman Quine](wikipedia://en/33833), and by Hintikka's teacher [Georg Henrik von Wright](wikipedia://en/12250). In 1998, for instance, he wrote *The Principles of Mathematics Revisited*, which takes an exploratory stance comparable to that Russell adopted in his *[The Principles of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Principles_of_Mathematics)* in 1903.
