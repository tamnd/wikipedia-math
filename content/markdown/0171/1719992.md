# Non-perturbative

*Image: expinvsq.svg.* The function *e*<sup>−1/*x*<sup>2</sup></sup>. The [MacLaurin series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacLaurin_series) is identically zero, but the function is not.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-perturbative"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), a **non-perturbative** [function](wikipedia://en/185427) or process is one that cannot be described by [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/102140). An example is the function

:::

$$f(x) = e^{-1/x^2},$$

which does not equal its own [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) in any neighborhood around *x* = 0. Every coefficient of the Taylor expansion around *x* = 0 is exactly zero, but the function is non-zero if *x* ≠ 0.

In physics, such functions arise for phenomena which are impossible to understand by perturbation theory, at any finite order. In [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), ['t Hooft–Polyakov monopoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'t_Hooft–Polyakov_monopole), [domain walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_wall), [flux tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_tube), and [instantons](wikipedia://en/296077) are examples.[^o2] A concrete, physical example is given by the [Schwinger effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwinger_effect),[^o3] whereby a strong electric field may spontaneously decay into electron-positron pairs. For not too strong fields, the rate per unit volume of this process is given by,

$$\Gamma = \frac{ (e E)^2 }{ 4 \pi^3} \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\pi m^2}{eE}}$$

which cannot be expanded in a Taylor series in the electric charge $e$, or the electric field strength $E$. Here $m$ is the mass of an electron and we have used units where $c=\hbar=1$.

In [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), a **non-perturbative** solution is one that cannot be described in terms of perturbations about some simple background, such as empty space. For this reason, non-perturbative solutions and theories yield insights into areas and subjects that perturbative methods cannot reveal.
