# Perverse sheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perverse sheaf"}

The mathematical term **perverse sheaves** refers to the objects of certain [abelian categories](wikipedia://en/45063) associated to [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), which may be a real or complex [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), or more general [topologically stratified spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_stratified_space), possibly singular.

:::

The concept was introduced in the work of [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383), [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613), and [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) and [Ofer Gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Gabber) (1982) as a consequence of the [Riemann-Hilbert correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann-Hilbert_correspondence), which establishes a connection between the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of regular holonomic [D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755) and the derived category of [constructible sheaves](wikipedia://en/26131119). Perverse sheaves are the objects in the latter that correspond to individual D-modules (and not more general complexes thereof); a perverse sheaf *is* in general represented by a complex of sheaves. The concept of perverse sheaves is already implicit in a 75's paper of Kashiwara on the constructibility of solutions of holonomic D-modules.

A key observation was that the [intersection homology](wikipedia://en/705749) of [Mark Goresky](wikipedia://en/10238162) and [Robert MacPherson](wikipedia://en/10238121) could be described using sheaf complexes that are actually perverse sheaves. It was clear from the outset that perverse sheaves are fundamental mathematical objects at the crossroads of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), analysis and [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations). They also play an important role in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), algebra, and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

## Preliminary remarks {#Preliminary_remarks}

The name *perverse sheaf* comes through rough translation of the French "faisceaux pervers".[^o3] The justification is that perverse sheaves are complexes of sheaves which have several features in common with sheaves: they form an abelian category, they have [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060), and to construct one, it suffices to construct it locally everywhere. The adjective "perverse" originates in the [intersection homology](wikipedia://en/705749#Perversities) theory,[^o4] and its origin was explained by (Goresky 2010).

The Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne-Gabber definition of a perverse sheaf proceeds through the machinery of [triangulated categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulated_categories) in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) and has a very strong algebraic flavour, although the main examples arising from Goresky–MacPherson theory are topological in nature because the simple objects in the category of perverse sheaves are the intersection cohomology complexes. This motivated MacPherson to recast the whole theory in geometric terms on a basis of [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560). For many applications in representation theory, perverse sheaves can be treated as a 'black box', a category with certain formal properties.

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and examples"}

## Definition and examples {#Definition_and_examples}

A **perverse sheaf** is an object *C* of the bounded [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of sheaves with [constructible](wikipedia://en/26131119) cohomology on a space *X* such that the set of points *x* with

$H^{-i}(j_x^*C)\ne 0$ or $H^{i}(j_x^!C)\ne 0$

has real dimension at most 2*i*, for all *i*. Here *j*<sub>*x*</sub> is the inclusion map of the point *x*.

If *X* is a smooth complex algebraic variety and everywhere of dimension *d*, then

$$\mathcal F[d]$$

is a perverse sheaf for any [local system](wikipedia://en/3461103) $\mathcal F$.[^o8] If *X* is a flat, locally complete intersection (for example, regular) scheme over a [henselian](wikipedia://en/14623383) [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633), then the constant sheaf shifted by $\dim X+1$ is an étale perverse sheaf.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="A simple example"}

## A simple example {#A_simple_example}

Let *X* be a disk around the origin in $\mathbb{C}$ stratified so that the origin is the unique singular stratum. Then the category of perverse sheaves on *X* is equivalent to the category of diagrams of vector spaces $V \overset{u}\underset{v}\rightleftarrows W$ where $\operatorname{id} - u \circ v$ and $\operatorname{id} - v \circ u$ are invertible.[^o12] More generally, quivers can be used to describe perverse sheaves.

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The category of perverse sheaves is an abelian subcategory of the (non-abelian) derived category of sheaves, equal to the core of a suitable [t-structure](wikipedia://en/1798923), and is preserved by [Verdier duality](wikipedia://en/7190735).

The bounded derived category of perverse l-adic sheaves on a scheme *X* is equivalent to the derived category of constructible sheaves and similarly for sheaves on the complex analytic space associated to a scheme *X*/**C**.[^o15]

:::

::: {#o17 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Perverse sheaves are a fundamental tool for the geometry of singular spaces. Therefore, they are applied in a variety of mathematical areas. In the [Riemann-Hilbert correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann-Hilbert_correspondence), perverse sheaves correspond to regular holonomic [D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755). This application establishes the notion of perverse sheaf as occurring 'in nature'. The [decomposition theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decomposition_theorem), a far-reaching extension of the [hard Lefschetz theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Lefschetz_theorem) decomposition, requires the usage of perverse sheaves. [Hodge modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_module) are, roughly speaking, a [Hodge-theoretic](wikipedia://en/501590) refinement of perverse sheaves. The [geometric Satake equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Satake_equivalence) identifies equivariant perverse sheaves on the [affine Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/16896827) $Gr_G$ with representations of the [Langlands dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual) group of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G* - see (Mirković Vilonen 2007). A proof of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705) using perverse sheaves is given in (Kiehl Weissauer 2001).

:::

## String theory {#String_theory}

Massless fields in [superstring](wikipedia://en/19595676) [compactifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_%28physics%29) have been identified with [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) classes on the target space (i.e. four-dimensional [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) with a six-dimensional [Calabi-Yau (CY) manifold](wikipedia://en/188932)). The determination of the matter and interaction content requires a detailed analysis of the [(co)homology](wikipedia://en/194060) of these spaces: nearly all massless fields in the effective [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) model are represented by certain (co)homology elements.

However, a troubling consequence occurs when the target space is [singular](wikipedia://en/581763). A singular target space means that only the CY manifold part is singular as the Minkowski space factor is smooth. Such a singular [CY manifold](wikipedia://en/188932) is called a [conifold](wikipedia://en/644677) as it is a CY manifold that admits conical [singularities](wikipedia://en/581763).

[Andrew Strominger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Strominger) observed (A. Strominger, 1995) that conifolds correspond to massless [blackholes](wikipedia://en/4650). Conifolds are important objects in string theory: [Brian Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Greene) explains the physics of conifolds in Chapter 13 of his book [The Elegant Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elegant_Universe) —including the fact that the space can tear near the cone, and its [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) can change. These singular target spaces, i.e. conifolds, correspond to certain mild degenerations of [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) which appear in a large class of [supersymmetric](wikipedia://en/224636) theories, including superstring theory (E. Witten, 1982).

Essentially, different cohomology theories on singular target spaces yield different results thereby making it difficult to determine which theory physics may favor. Several important characteristics of the cohomology, which correspond to the massless fields, are based on general properties of field theories, specifically, the (2,2)-supersymmetric 2-dimensional [world-sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldsheet) [field theories](wikipedia://en/25267). These properties, known as the [Kähler](wikipedia://en/383501) package (T. Hubsch, 1992), should hold for singular and smooth target spaces. Paul Green and Tristan Hubsch (P. Green & T. Hubsch, 1988) determined that the manner in which you move between singular CY target spaces require moving through either a [small resolution or deformation of the singularity](wikipedia://en/5961115) (T. Hubsch, 1992) and called it the 'conifold transition'.

Tristan Hubsch (T. Hubsch, 1997) conjectured what this [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) theory should be for singular target spaces. Tristan Hubsch and Abdul Rahman (T. Hubsch and A. Rahman, 2005) worked to solve the Hubsch conjecture by analyzing the non-transversal case of [Witten's](wikipedia://en/54894) gauged linear sigma model (E. Witten, 1993) which induces a [stratification](wikipedia://en/571280) of these [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) (termed the ground state variety) in the case of isolated conical [singularities](wikipedia://en/581763).

Under certain conditions it was determined that this ground state variety was a [conifold](wikipedia://en/644677) (P. Green & T.Hubsch, 1988; T. Hubsch, 1992) with isolated conic [singularities](wikipedia://en/581763) over a certain base with a 1-dimensional exocurve (termed exo-strata) attached at each [singular](wikipedia://en/581763) point. T. Hubsch and A. Rahman determined the (co)-homology of this ground state variety in all dimensions, found it compatible with [Mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671) and [String Theory](wikipedia://en/28305) but found an [obstruction in the middle dimension](wikipedia://en/5961115) (T. Hubsch and A. Rahman, 2005). This [obstruction](wikipedia://en/5961115) required revisiting Hubsch's conjecture of a Stringy Singular Cohomology (T. Hubsch, 1997). In the winter of 2002, T. Hubsch and A. Rahman met with R.M. Goresky to discuss this [obstruction](wikipedia://en/5961115) and in discussions between [R.M. Goresky](wikipedia://en/10238162) and [R. MacPherson](wikipedia://en/10238121), R. MacPherson made the observation that there was such a perverse sheaf that could have the cohomology that satisfied Hubsch's conjecture and [resolved the obstruction](wikipedia://en/5961115). [R.M. Goresky](wikipedia://en/10238162) and T. Hubsch advised A. Rahman's Ph.D. dissertation on the construction of a self-dual perverse sheaf (A. Rahman, 2009) using the zig-zag construction of [MacPherson](wikipedia://en/10238121)-[Vilonen](wikipedia://en/56918693) (R. MacPherson & K. Vilonen, 1986). This perverse sheaf proved the Hübsch conjecture for isolated conic [singularities](wikipedia://en/581763), satisfied [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732), and aligned with some of the properties of the Kähler package. Satisfaction of all of the Kähler package by this Perverse sheaf for higher [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) [strata](wikipedia://en/571280) is still an open problem. Markus Banagl (M. Banagl, 2010; M. Banagl, et al., 2014) addressed the Hubsch conjecture through intersection spaces for higher [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) [strata](wikipedia://en/571280) inspired by Hubsch's work (T. Hubsch, 1992, 1997; P. Green and T. Hubsch, 1988) and A. Rahman's original ansatz (A. Rahman, 2009) for [isolated singularities](wikipedia://en/581763).
