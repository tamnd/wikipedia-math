# Lituus (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lituus"}

*Image: lituus.svg.* Branch for positive $r$

The **lituus** spiral () is a spiral in which the [polar angle](wikipedia://en/25120) $\theta$ is inversely proportional to the square of the radius $r$.

:::

This spiral, which has two branches depending on the sign of $r$, is [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/3107) to the $x$ axis. Its [points of inflexion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Points_of_inflexion) are at

$$(\theta, r) = \left(\tfrac12, \pm\sqrt{2k}\right).$$

The curve was named for the ancient Roman [lituus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lituus) by [Roger Cotes](wikipedia://en/236798) in a collection of papers entitled *Harmonia Mensurarum* (1722), which was published six years after his death.

## Coordinate representations {#Coordinate_representations}

### Polar coordinates {#Polar_coordinates}

The representations of the lituus spiral in [polar coordinates](wikipedia://en/25120) $(r, \theta)$ is given by the equation

$$r = \frac{a}{\sqrt{\theta}},$$

where $\theta \ge 0$ and $a \ne 0$.

### Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

The lituus spiral with the [polar coordinates](wikipedia://en/25120) $r = \tfrac{a}{0.2\operatorname{em}}$ can be converted to [Cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) like any other spiral with the relationships $x = r \cos \theta$ and $y = r \sin \theta$. With this conversion we get the parametric representations of the curve:

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \frac{a}{\sqrt{\theta}} \cos\theta, \\ y &= \frac{a}{\sqrt{\theta}} \sin\theta. \\ \end{aligned}$$

These equations can in turn be rearranged to an equation in $x$ and $y$:

$$\frac{y}{x} = \tan\left( \frac{a^2}{x^2 + y^2} \right).$$

1. Divide $y$ by $x$:$\frac{y}{x} = \frac{\frac{a}{\sqrt{\theta}} \sin\theta}{\frac{a}{\sqrt{\theta}} \cos\theta} \Rightarrow \frac{y}{x} = \tan\theta.$
1. Solve the equation of the lituus spiral in polar coordinates: $r = \frac{a}{\sqrt{\theta}} \Leftrightarrow \theta = \frac{a^2}{r^2}.$
1. Substitute $\theta = \frac{a^2}{r^2}$: $\frac{y}{x} = \tan\left( \frac{a^2}{r^2} \right).$
1. Substitute $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$: $\frac{y}{x} = \tan\left( \frac{a^2}{\left( \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \right)^2} \right) \Rightarrow \frac{y}{x} = \tan\left( \frac{a^2}{x^2 + y^2} \right).$

## Geometrical properties {#Geometrical_properties}

### Curvature {#Curvature}

The [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) of the lituus spiral can be determined using the formula

$$\kappa = \left( 8 \theta^2 - 2 \right) \left( \frac{\theta}{1 + 4 \theta^2} \right)^\frac32.$$

### Arc length {#Arc_length}

In general, the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of the lituus spiral cannot be expressed as a [closed-form expression](wikipedia://en/585143), but the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of the lituus spiral can be represented as a formula using the [Gaussian hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/2055223):

$$L = 2 \sqrt{\theta} \cdot \operatorname{_2 F_1}\left( -\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{4}; \frac{3}{4}; -\frac{1}{4 \theta^2} \right) - 2 \sqrt{\theta_0} \cdot \operatorname{_2 F_1}\left( -\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{4}; \frac{3}{4}; -\frac{1}{4 \theta_0^2} \right),$$

where the arc length is measured from $\theta = \theta_{0}$.[^o7]

### Tangential angle {#Tangential_angle}

The tangential angle (see [Pitch angle of a spiral](wikipedia://en/75407072)) of the lituus spiral can be determined using the formula[^o7]

$$\phi = \theta - \arctan 2\theta.$$
