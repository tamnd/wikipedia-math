# Samuel Arthur Saunder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Arthur Saunder"}

**Samuel Arthur Saunder** (1852 – 8 December 1912) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [selenographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selenography) who taught at [Wellington College, Berkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_College,_Berkshire). In 1894 he became a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society), and in 1908 he was made [Gresham Professor of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_Professor_of_Astronomy) giving public lectures on the subject.[^o2]

:::

Saunder was one of the first to use [photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography) of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) to measure and [triangulate](wikipedia://en/207174) its features.[^o3] He was also responsible for pointing out the confused state of lunar nomenclature at the beginning of the 20th century, and initiating the process of standardizing the names of lunar features.[^o4] The crater [Saunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunder_%28crater%29) on the Moon was named after him in 1935.[^o5][^o6]
