# Holomorph (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Holomorph"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the area of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **holomorph** of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$, denoted $\operatorname{Hol}(G)$, is a group that simultaneously contains (copies of) $G$ and its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. It provides interesting examples of groups, and allows one to treat group elements and group automorphisms in a uniform context. The holomorph can be described as a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) or as a [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634).

:::

## Hol(*G*) as a semidirect product {#Hol(G)_as_a_semidirect_product}

If $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of $G$, then

$$\operatorname{Hol}(G)=G\rtimes \operatorname{Aut}(G),$$

where the multiplication is given by

::: {#o3 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$(g,\alpha)(h,\beta)=(g\alpha(h),\alpha\beta). \tag{1}$$

:::

Typically, a semidirect product is given in the form $G\rtimes_{\phi}A$, where $G$ and $A$ are groups and $\phi:A\rightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564), and where the multiplication of elements in the semidirect product is given as

$$(g,a)(h,b)=(g\phi(a)(h),ab).$$

This is [well defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_defined) since $\phi(a)\in \operatorname{Aut}(G)$, and therefore $\phi(a)(h)\in G$.

For the holomorph, $A=\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ and $\phi$ is the [identity map](wikipedia://en/15069). As such, we suppress writing $\phi$ explicitly in the multiplication given in equation ((1)) above.

As an example, take

- $G=C_3=\langle x\rangle=\{1,x,x^2\}$ the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 3,
- $\operatorname{Aut}(G)=\langle \sigma\rangle=\{1,\sigma\}$, where $\sigma(x)=x^2$, and
- $\operatorname{Hol}(G)=\{(x^i,\sigma^j)\}$ with the multiplication given by:

$(x^{i_1},\sigma^{j_1})(x^{i_2},\sigma^{j_2}) = (x^{i_1+i_22^{^{j_1}}},\sigma^{j_1+j_2})$, where the exponents of $x$ are taken [mod](wikipedia://en/20087) 3 and those of $\sigma$ mod 2.

Observe that

$(x,\sigma)(x^2,\sigma)=(x^{1+2\cdot2},\sigma^2)=(x^2,1)$ while $(x^2,\sigma)(x,\sigma)=(x^{2+1\cdot2},\sigma^2)=(x,1)$.

Hence, this group is not [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974), and so $\operatorname{Hol}(C_3)$ is a [non-abelian group](wikipedia://en/4791400) of order 6, which, by basic group theory, must be [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_3$.

## Hol(*G*) as a permutation group {#Hol(G)_as_a_permutation_group}

A group *G* acts naturally on itself by left and right multiplication, each giving rise to a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) from *G* into the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on the underlying set of *G*. One homomorphism is defined as *λ*: *G* → Sym(*G*), *λ<sub>g</sub>*(*h*) = *g*·*h*. That is, *g* is mapped to the [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) obtained by left-multiplying each element of *G* by *g*. Similarly, a second homomorphism *ρ*: *G* → Sym(*G*) is defined by *ρ<sub>g</sub>*(*h*) = *h*·*g*<sup>−1</sup>, where the inverse ensures that *ρ<sub>gh</sub>*(*k*) = *ρ<sub>g</sub>*(*ρ<sub>h</sub>*(*k*)). These homomorphisms are called the left and right [regular representations](wikipedia://en/360011) of *G*. Each homomorphism is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective), a fact referred to as [Cayley's theorem](wikipedia://en/105967).

For example, if *G* = *C*<sub>3</sub> = {1, *x*, *x*<sup>2</sup> } is a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order three, then

- *λ<sub>x</sub>*(1) = *x*·1 = *x*,
- *λ<sub>x</sub>*(*x*) = *x*·*x* = *x*<sup>2</sup>, and
- *λ<sub>x</sub>*(*x*<sup>2</sup>) = *x*·*x*<sup>2</sup> = 1,

so *λ*(*x*) takes (1, *x*, *x*<sup>2</sup>) to (*x*, *x*<sup>2</sup>, 1).

The image of *λ* is a subgroup of Sym(*G*) isomorphic to *G*, and its [normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) in Sym(*G*) is defined to be the **holomorph** *N* of *G*. For each *n* in *N* and *g* in *G*, there is an *h* in *G* such that *n*·*λ<sub>g</sub>* = *λ<sub>h</sub>*·*n*. If an element *n* of the holomorph fixes the [identity](wikipedia://en/14962) of *G*, then for 1 in *G*, (*n*·*λ<sub>g</sub>*)(1) = (*λ<sub>h</sub>*·*n*)(1), but the left hand side is *n*(*g*), and the right side is *h*. In other words, if *n* in *N* fixes the identity of *G*, then for every *g* in *G*, *n*·*λ<sub>g</sub>* = *λ<sub>n(g)</sub>*·*n*. If *g*, *h* are elements of *G*, and *n* is an element of *N* fixing the identity of *G*, then applying this equality twice to *n*·*λ<sub>g</sub>*·*λ<sub>h</sub>* and once to the (equivalent) expression *n*·*λ<sub>gg</sub>* gives that *n*(*g*)·*n*(*h*) = *n*(*g*·*h*). That is, every element of *N* that fixes the identity of *G* is in fact an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of *G*. Such an *n* normalizes *λ<sub>G</sub>*, and the only *λ<sub>g</sub>* that fixes the identity is *λ*(1). Setting *A* to be the [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28group_theory%29) of the identity, the subgroup generated by *A* and *λ<sub>G</sub>* is [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) with [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) *λ<sub>G</sub>* and [complement](wikipedia://en/11436087) *A*. Since *λ<sub>G</sub>* is [transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_action), the subgroup generated by *λ<sub>G</sub>* and the point stabilizer *A* is all of *N*, which shows the holomorph as a permutation group is isomorphic to the holomorph as semidirect product.

It is useful, but not directly relevant, that the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of *λ<sub>G</sub>* in Sym(*G*) is *ρ<sub>G</sub>*, their intersection is $\rho_{Z(G)}=\lambda_{Z(G)}$, where Z(*G*) is the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) of *G*, and that *A* is a common complement to both of these normal subgroups of *N*.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- *ρ*(*G*) ∩ Aut(*G*) = 1
- Aut(*G*) normalizes *ρ*(*G*) so that [canonically](wikipedia://en/515096) *ρ*(*G*)Aut(*G*) ≅ *G* ⋊ Aut(*G*)
- $\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong \operatorname{Im}(g\mapsto \lambda(g)\rho(g))$ since *λ*(*g*)*ρ*(*g*)(*h*) = *ghg*<sup>−1</sup> ($\operatorname{Inn}(G)$ is the group of [inner automorphisms](wikipedia://en/61839) of *G*.)
- *K* ≤ *G* is a [characteristic subgroup](wikipedia://en/7198) if and only if *λ*(*K*) ⊴ Hol(*G*)

:::
