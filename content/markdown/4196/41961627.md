# George Springer (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Springer"}

**George Springer** (September 3, 1924 – February 18, 2019) was an American mathematician and computer scientist. He was professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of computer science at [Indiana University Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington).[^o3]

:::

Springer is perhaps best known as the coauthor with [Daniel P. Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_P._Friedman) of the widely used textbook *Scheme and the Art of Computer Programming*. [Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_%28programming_language%29) is one of the two main dialects of [LISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LISP). Three of the pioneering books for Scheme are *The Scheme Programming Language* (1982) by [R. Kent Dybvig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Kent_Dybvig), *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs* (1985) by [Harold Abelson](wikipedia://en/382535) and [Gerald Jay Sussman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Jay_Sussman) with Julie Sussman, and *Scheme and the Art of Computer Programming* (1989) by Springer and Friedman.

## Career {#Career}

Springer earned his bachelor's degree in 1945 from [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University) (then named "Case Institute of Technology") and his master's degree in 1946 from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). He earned his PhD in 1949 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) with thesis *The Coefficient Problem for Univalent Mappings of the Exterior of the Unit Circle* under [Lars Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636).

From 1949 to 1951 Springer was a [C.L.E. Moore Instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.L.E._Moore_Instructor) at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). From 1951 to 1954 he was an assistant professor at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). In the academic year 1954/1955 as a Fulbright Lecturer and visiting professor at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) he worked with [Heinrich Behnke](wikipedia://en/22922678). In the autumn of 1955 Springer became an associate professor and subsequently a professor at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas). In the academic year 1961/1962 he was a Fulbright Lecturer and visiting professor at the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg). From 1964 he was a professor of mathematics and from 1987 also a professor of computer science at [Indiana University Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington). In the academic year 1971/1972 he was a visiting professor at [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College) in London.

Springer began his career working in function theory (of one and several complex variables) and wrote a textbook on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181). In the 1980s he turned more toward computer science, working on programming languages.

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Springer was born in Cleveland, Ohio, in 1924, to a family of Jewish immigrants from [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_the_Czech_Republic).[^o7][^o8] He met his wife Annemarie (née Keiner) while at Harvard University. They were married from 1950 until her death in 2011, and had three children. Springer died on February 18, 2019, aged 94.[^o9]
