# Anita Bahn

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Anita Bahn"}

**Anita Kaplan Bahn** (1919/1920 – July 19, 1980) was an American [epidemiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiologist), [biostatistician](wikipedia://en/3878), and cancer researcher.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bahn was originally from [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City). She left high school at the age of 15, and earned a bachelor's degree in [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) four years later from [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College), together with a certification allowing her to teach high school biology. She would also go on to study "physics at [New York City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York); botany and bacteriology at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University); mathematics and statistics at [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University) and at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University)", but without completing those programs to a graduate degree.

She became head of outpatient studies at the [National Institute of Mental Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Mental_Health) from 1951 to 1966. During this time, she earned a Sc.D. from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in 1960. Her dissertation, supervised by [Jerome Cornfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Cornfield), was *A Methodological Study of the Outpatient Psychiatric Clinic Population of Maryland, 1948-59*. She then returned to academia as an associate professor of biostatistics at the [Medical College of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_College_of_Pennsylvania). While there, she worked towards an M.D., which she earned in 1972.

She became chief epidemiologist of [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland) for the following two years, and then became a professor of [community medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_medicine) and epidemiology at the [School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perelman_School_of_Medicine_at_the_University_of_Pennsylvania). At Pennsylvania, she helped found a graduate program in epidemiology, and led a research center on the epidemiology of cancer. She was also affiliated with the [Fox Chase Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Chase_Cancer_Center) and held an adjunct position at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University).

In 1980, she was recruited to head a new epidemiology program in the Graduate School of Public Health at [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University), but she died at age 60 of a cerebral hemorrhage at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) hospital before she could take up her new position.[^o14]

## Books {#Books}

Bahn authored *Basic Medical Statistics* (Grune & Stratton, 1972). and, with Judith S. Mausner, she co-wrote *Epidemiology: An Introductory Text* (Saunders, 1974).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Bahn was named a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1970. She was also a fellow of the [American Public Health Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Public_Health_Association) and the [American College of Preventive Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Preventive_Medicine).

## Personal {#Personal}

Her husband, Ralph Bahn, with whom she had two children, died in the early 1970s; she married again, in 1980, the year of her death, to nuclear physicist and science fiction fan [Milton A. Rothman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_A._Rothman).
