# Kelvin functions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kelvin functions"}

*Image: Plot of the Kelvin function ber(z) in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D.svg.* Plot of the Kelvin function ber(z) in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D

In applied mathematics, the **Kelvin functions** ber<sub>*ν*</sub>(*x*) and bei<sub>*ν*</sub>(*x*) are the [real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part) and [imaginary parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_part), respectively, of

:::

$$J_\nu \left (x e^{\frac{3 \pi i}{4}} \right ),\,$$

where *x* is real, and $J_{\nu}(z)$, is the *ν*<sup>th</sup> order [Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700) of the first kind. Similarly, the functions ker<sub>ν</sub>(*x*) and kei<sub>ν</sub>(*x*) are the real and imaginary parts, respectively, of

$$e^{-\nu\pi i/2}K_\nu \left (x e^{\frac{\pi i}{4}} \right ),\,$$

where $K_{\nu}(z)$ is the *ν*<sup>th</sup> order [modified Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700#Modified_Bessel_functions) of the second kind.

These functions are named after [William Thomson, 1st Baron Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thomson,_1st_Baron_Kelvin).

While the Kelvin functions are defined as the real and imaginary parts of Bessel functions with *x* taken to be real, the functions can be analytically continued for complex arguments $xe^{i\varphi}, 0 \le \varphi < 2\pi.$ With the exception of ber<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) and bei<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) for integral *n*, the Kelvin functions have a [branch point](wikipedia://en/483173) at *x* = 0.

Below, $\Gamma(z)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and $\psi(z)$ is the [digamma function](wikipedia://en/406935).

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Ber(x)"}

## ber(*x*) {#ber(x)}

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-ber.svg.* ber(*x*) for *x* between 0 and 20.

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-ber-norm.svg.* $\mathrm{ber}(x) / e^{x/\sqrt{2}}$ for *x* between 0 and 50.

For integers *n*, ber<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) has the series expansion

$$\mathrm{ber}_n(x) = \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^n \sum_{k \geq 0} \frac{\cos\left[\left(\frac{3n}{4} + \frac{k}{2}\right)\pi\right]}{k! \Gamma(n + k + 1)} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^k ,$$

where $\Gamma(z)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). The special case ber<sub>0</sub>(*x*), commonly denoted as just ber(*x*), has the series expansion

$$\mathrm{ber}(x) = 1 + \sum_{k \geq 1} \frac{(-1)^k}{[(2k)!]^2} \left(\frac{x}{2} \right )^{4k}$$

and [asymptotic series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_series)

$$\mathrm{ber}(x) \sim \frac{e^{\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}}}{\sqrt{2 \pi x}} \left (f_1(x) \cos \alpha + g_1(x) \sin \alpha \right ) - \frac{\mathrm{kei}(x)}{\pi},$$

where

$$\alpha = \frac{x}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{\pi}{8},$$

$$f_1(x) = 1 + \sum_{k \geq 1} \frac{\cos(k \pi / 4)}{k! (8x)^k} \prod_{l = 1}^k (2l - 1)^2$$

$$g_1(x) = \sum_{k \geq 1} \frac{\sin(k \pi / 4)}{k! (8x)^k} \prod_{l = 1}^k (2l - 1)^2 .$$

:::

## bei(*x*) {#bei(x)}

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-bei.svg.* bei(*x*) for *x* between 0 and 20.

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-bei-norm.svg.* $\mathrm{bei}(x) / e^{x/\sqrt{2}}$ for *x* between 0 and 50.

For integers *n*, bei<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) has the series expansion

$$\mathrm{bei}_n(x) = \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^n \sum_{k \geq 0} \frac{\sin\left[\left(\frac{3n}{4} + \frac{k}{2}\right)\pi\right]}{k! \Gamma(n + k + 1)} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^k .$$

The special case bei<sub>0</sub>(*x*), commonly denoted as just bei(*x*), has the series expansion

*Image: Plot of the Kelvin function ker(z) in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D.svg.* Plot of the Kelvin function ker(z) in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D

$$\mathrm{bei}(x) = \sum_{k \geq 0} \frac{(-1)^k }{[(2k+1)!]^2} \left(\frac{x}{2} \right )^{4k+2}$$

and asymptotic series

$$\mathrm{bei}(x) \sim \frac{e^{\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}}}{\sqrt{2 \pi x}} [f_1(x) \sin \alpha - g_1(x) \cos \alpha] - \frac{\mathrm{ker}(x)}{\pi},$$

where α, $f_1(x)$, and $g_1(x)$ are defined as for ber(*x*).

:::

## ker(*x*) {#ker(x)}

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-ker.svg.* ker(*x*) for *x* between 0 and 14.

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-ker-norm.svg.* $\mathrm{ker}(x) e^{x/\sqrt{2}}$ for *x* between 0 and 50.

For integers *n*, ker<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) has the (complicated) series expansion

$$\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{ker}_n(x) = - \ln\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \mathrm{ber}_n(x) + \frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{bei}_n(x) \\ &+ \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \cos\left[\left(\frac{3n}{4} + \frac{k}{2}\right)\pi\right] \frac{(n-k-1)!}{k!} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^k \\ &+ \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^n \sum_{k \geq 0} \cos\left[\left(\frac{3n}{4} + \frac{k}{2}\right)\pi\right] \frac{\psi(k+1) + \psi(n + k + 1)}{k! (n+k)!} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^k . \end{aligned}$$

*Image: Plot of the Kelvin function kei(z) in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D.svg.* Plot of the Kelvin function kei(z) in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D

The special case ker<sub>0</sub>(*x*), commonly denoted as just ker(*x*), has the series expansion

$$\mathrm{ker}(x) = -\ln\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \mathrm{ber}(x) + \frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{bei}(x) + \sum_{k \geq 0} (-1)^k \frac{\psi(2k + 1)}{[(2k)!]^2} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^{2k}$$

and the asymptotic series

$$\mathrm{ker}(x) \sim \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}} e^{-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}} [f_2(x) \cos \beta + g_2(x) \sin \beta],$$

where

$$\beta = \frac{x}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{\pi}{8},$$

$$f_2(x) = 1 + \sum_{k \geq 1} (-1)^k \frac{\cos(k \pi / 4)}{k! (8x)^k} \prod_{l = 1}^k (2l - 1)^2$$

$$g_2(x) = \sum_{k \geq 1} (-1)^k \frac{\sin(k \pi / 4)}{k! (8x)^k} \prod_{l = 1}^k (2l - 1)^2.$$

:::

## kei(*x*) {#kei(x)}

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-kei.svg.* kei(*x*) for *x* between 0 and 14.

*Image: Mplwp Kelvin-kei-norm.svg.* $\mathrm{kei}(x) e^{x/\sqrt{2}}$ for *x* between 0 and 50.

For integer *n*, kei<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) has the series expansion

$$\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{kei}_n(x) = - \ln\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \mathrm{bei}_n(x) - \frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{ber}_n(x) \\ &-\frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \sin\left[\left(\frac{3n}{4} + \frac{k}{2}\right)\pi\right] \frac{(n-k-1)!}{k!} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^k \\ &+ \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^n \sum_{k \geq 0} \sin\left[\left(\frac{3n}{4} + \frac{k}{2}\right)\pi\right] \frac{\psi(k+1) + \psi(n + k + 1)}{k! (n+k)!} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^k . \end{aligned}$$

The special case kei<sub>0</sub>(*x*), commonly denoted as just kei(*x*), has the series expansion

$$\mathrm{kei}(x) = -\ln\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \mathrm{bei}(x) - \frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{ber}(x) + \sum_{k \geq 0} (-1)^k \frac{\psi(2k + 2)}{[(2k+1)!]^2} \left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)^{2k+1}$$

and the asymptotic series

$$\mathrm{kei}(x) \sim -\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}} e^{-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}} [f_2(x) \sin \beta + g_2(x) \cos \beta],$$

where *β*, *f*<sub>2</sub>(*x*), and *g*<sub>2</sub>(*x*) are defined as for ker(*x*).

:::
