# Strategic move

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strategic move"}

A **strategic move** in [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) is an action taken by a player outside the defined actions of the game in order to gain a strategic advantage and increase one's payoff. Strategic moves can either be *unconditional moves* or *response rules*. The key characteristics of a strategic move are that it involves a *commitment* from the player, meaning the player can only restrict their own choices and that the commitment has to be *credible*, meaning that once employed it must be in the interest of the player to follow through with the move. Credible moves should also be observable to the other players.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Strategic moves are **not** *warnings* or *assurances.* Warnings and assurances are merely statements of a player's interest, rather than an actual *commitment* from the player.

The term was coined by [Thomas Schelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Schelling) in his 1960 book, *The Strategy of Conflict*, and has gained wide currency in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) and [industrial organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_organization).[^o4]
