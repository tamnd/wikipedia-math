# Spherical design

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical design"}

A **spherical design**, part of [combinatorial design](wikipedia://en/2761968) theory in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), is a finite set of *N* points on the *d*-dimensional unit [*d*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) *S<sup>d</sup>* such that the average value of any polynomial *f* of degree *t* or less on the set equals the average value of *f* on the whole sphere (that is, the integral of *f* over *S<sup>d</sup>* divided by the area or [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) of *S<sup>d</sup>*). Such a set is often called a **spherical** *t*-**design** to indicate the value of *t*, which is a fundamental parameter. The concept of a spherical design is due to Delsarte, Goethals, and Seidel,[^n1] although these objects were understood as particular examples of [cubature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubature) formulas earlier.

:::

Spherical designs can be of value in [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) for [experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_design), in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). The main problem is to find examples, given *d* and *t*, that are not too large; however, such examples may be hard to come by. Spherical t-designs have also recently been appropriated in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) in the form of [quantum t-designs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_t-designs) with various applications to [quantum information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information_theory) and [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220).

## Existence of spherical designs {#Existence_of_spherical_designs}

The existence and structure of spherical designs on the circle were studied in depth by Hong.[^n2] Shortly thereafter, Seymour and Zaslavsky[^n3] proved that such designs exist of all sufficiently large sizes; that is, given positive integers *d* and *t*, there is a number *N*(*d*,*t*) such that for every *N* ≥ *N*(*d*,*t*) there exists a spherical *t*-design of *N* points in dimension *d*. However, their proof gave no idea of how big *N*(*d*,*t*) is.

Mimura constructively found conditions in terms of the number of points and the dimension which characterize exactly when spherical 2-designs exist. Maximally sized collections of [equiangular lines](wikipedia://en/2706034) (up to identification of lines as antipodal points on the sphere) are examples of minimal sized spherical 5-designs. There are many sporadic small spherical designs; many of them are related to finite [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on the sphere.

In 2013, Bondarenko, Radchenko, and Viazovska[^n4] obtained the asymptotic upper bound $N(d,t)<C_d t^d$ for all positive integers *d* and *t*. This asymptotically matches the lower bound given originally by Delsarte, Goethals, and Seidel. The value of *C<sub>d</sub>* is currently unknown, while exact values of $N(d,t)$ are known in relatively few cases.
