# Timothy J. Healey

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Timothy J. Healey"}

**Timothy Healey** is an American [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) working in the areas of [nolinear elasticity](wikipedia://en/2210759), [nonlinear partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_partial_differential_equations), [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042) and the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882).[^o3][^o4] He is currently a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in the Department of Mathematics, [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o4]

:::

Healey is known for his mathematical contributions to nonlinear elasticity particularly the use of [group-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group-theoretic) methods in global bifurcation problems.[^o3][^o5][^o6]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Healey received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) from the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri) in 1976 and worked as a [structural engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_engineer) between 1978 and 1980.[^o8] He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in engineering from the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign) in 1985 under the guidance of Robert Muncaster in mathematics with mentoring from Donald Carlson and Arthur Robinson in [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics).[^o9] He spent a [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral) year with [Stuart Antman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Antman) and P. Michael Fitzpatrick at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland) before joining the faculty at Cornell University, where he has held full-time positions in the Department of Theoretical and Applied Mechanics, Mechanical and Aerospace engineering and Mathematics.[^o10] He has served as PhD advisor to 21 students, with 37 descendants.[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Healey's research focuses on mathematical aspects of elasticity theory. Early in his career, he made fundamental contributions to the study of global [bifurcation](wikipedia://en/2326042) problems with [symmetry](wikipedia://en/2714149) using [group-theoretic methods](wikipedia://en/41890), including work with H. Kielhöfer on the preservation of nodal properties in solutions of nonlinear [elliptic PDE](wikipedia://en/662787). Along with many co-workers, his approach based on symmetry and global bifurcation/continuation has illuminated the behavior of a wide variety of physical systems, including rod structures, [lipid-bilayer vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_bilayer), [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423), wrinkling in thin sheets, creasing in soft solids, and [brittle fracture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittle_fracture). With H. Simpson, he developed a [topological degree](wikipedia://en/3094450) similar to the [Leray-Schauder degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leray-Schauder_degree), yielding new [existence theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence) in [nonlinear elasticity](wikipedia://en/2210759). Healey's work on [transverse hemitropy and isotropy](wikipedia://en/1708801) in [Cosserat](wikipedia://en/57011943) rod theory is well known and provides a natural setting for studying the [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) of [ropes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ropes), [cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_rope) and biological filaments such as [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA). He has also established minimum-energy existence theorems for thin, nonlinearly elastic shells undergoing large membrane strains.[^o3][^o6][^o12][^o13]
