# Smith space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smith space"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Smith space** is a [complete](wikipedia://en/32339) [compactly generated](wikipedia://en/899382) [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) $X$ having a *universal compact set*, i.e. a compact set $K$ which absorbs every other compact set $T\subseteq X$ (i.e. $T\subseteq\lambda\cdot K$ for some $\lambda>0$).

:::

Smith spaces are named after [Marianne Ruth Freundlich Smith](https://www.genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=4032), who introduced them[^n1] as duals to [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) in some versions of duality theory for [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752). All Smith spaces are [stereotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_space) and are in the stereotype duality relations with [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989):[^n2][^n3]

- for any Banach space $X$ its stereotype dual space[^o5] $X^\star$ is a Smith space,
- and vice versa, for any Smith space $X$ its stereotype dual space $X^\star$ is a Banach space.

Smith spaces are special cases of [Brauner spaces](wikipedia://en/40958342).

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- As follows from the duality theorems, for any Banach space $X$ its stereotype dual space $X^\star$ is a Smith space. The [polar](wikipedia://en/1764022) $K=B^\circ$ of the unit ball $B$ in $X$ is the universal compact set in $X^\star$. If $X^*$ denotes the [normed dual space](wikipedia://en/3989#Dual_space) for $X$, and $X'$ the space $X^*$ endowed with the $X$-weak topology, then the topology of $X^\star$ lies between the topology of $X^*$ and the topology of $X'$, so there are natural (linear continuous) bijections

$$X^*\to X^\star\to X'.$$

If $X$ is infinite-dimensional, then no two of these topologies coincide. At the same time, for infinite dimensional $X$ the space $X^\star$ is not [barreled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barreled_space) (and even is not a [Mackey space](wikipedia://en/1804899) if $X$ is [reflexive as a Banach space](wikipedia://en/210091#Reflexive_Banach_spaces)[^n4]).

- If $K$ is a [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238) [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) set in a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space) $Y$, then its [linear span](wikipedia://en/56353) ${\mathbb C}K=\operatorname{span}(K)$ possesses a unique structure of a Smith space with $K$ as the universal compact set (and with the same topology on $K$).[^n5]
- If $M$ is a (Hausdorff) [compact topological space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_topological_space), and ${\mathcal C}(M)$ the [Banach space of continuous functions](wikipedia://en/3989#Classical_spaces) on $M$ (with the usual sup-norm), then the stereotype dual space ${\mathcal C}^\star(M)$ (of [Radon measures](wikipedia://en/521933) on $M$ with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets in ${\mathcal C}(M)$) is a Smith space. In the special case when $M=G$ is endowed with a structure of a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) the space ${\mathcal C}^\star(G)$ becomes a natural example of a [stereotype group algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_group_algebra).[^n6]
- A [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $X$ is a Smith space if and only if $X$ is finite-dimensional.
