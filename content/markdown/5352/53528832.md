# Bryce Reeve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bryce Reeve"}

**Bryce Byrum Reeve III** (born December 31, 1968) is an American [psychometrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychometrician), [outcomes research scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outcomes_research), professor of Health Policy and Management at the [UNC Gillings School of Global Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNC_Gillings_School_of_Global_Public_Health) (with an adjunct appointment in the Department of Psychology and Neuroscience), and faculty expert at the [UNC Lineberger Comprehensive Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNC_Lineberger_Comprehensive_Cancer_Center). He is a prominent figure in quantitative research on improving the measurement of [patient-reported health outcomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient-reported_outcome).

:::

## Career {#Career}

A [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina) native and graduate of [Chapel Hill High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill_High_School_%28Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina%29), Reeve earned a bachelor's degree with distinction from the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) in 1994 and subsequently worked for several years as a statistical consultant in the [Research Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle) area. He returned to UNC to complete a master's (1999) and PhD (2000) in the L. L. Thurstone Psychometric Laboratory under [David Thissen](wikipedia://en/53411348).[^o4] Reeve's doctoral dissertation used [item response theory](wikipedia://en/420159) to analyze data from the [Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Multiphasic_Personality_Inventory).

Upon earning his PhD, Reeve worked as a [psychometrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychometrician) and Program Director at the [National Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Institute). He joined the [UNC Gillings School of Global Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNC_Gillings_School_of_Global_Public_Health) faculty in 2010, earning tenure four years later. He is currently Full Professor in the Department of Health Policy and Management; Research Fellow at the Cecil G. Sheps Center for Health Services Research; and Adjunct Associate Professor of [Quantitative Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_Psychology) in the Department of Psychology and Neuroscience.

His record of extramural service includes advisory roles and board memberships with government agencies, nonprofit organizations, and private companies (e.g., [PatientsLikeMe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PatientsLikeMe)) dedicated to [patient-centered care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_participation) and [health-related quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life_%28healthcare%29).[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Reeve has been a [principal investigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_investigator) on numerous grants from the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health), the [Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient-Centered_Outcomes_Research_Institute), and others.[^o7] He leads the [Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient-Reported_Outcomes_Measurement_Information_System) research site at the University of North Carolina.[^o8] He has published hundreds of peer-reviewed journal articles on [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) and [patient-reported outcomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient-reported_outcome) in addition to nearly one dozen books or book chapters and nearly a hundred abstracts and presentations at scholarly conferences. His work is known for [statistical](wikipedia://en/26685) and [psychometric](wikipedia://en/24982) rigor, particularly with respect to the application of [item response theory](wikipedia://en/420159) to [questionnaire](wikipedia://en/956483) design and analysis.

He has received six Awards of Merit from the [NIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) and, in 2015, received the John Ware and Alvin Tarlov Career Achievement Prize, a major international award for research in patient-reported outcomes.[^o9]
