# Frederick F. Stephan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frederick F. Stephan"}

**Frederick Franklin Stephan** (May 17, 1903 – August 3, 1971) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [sociologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociologist), mainly known for his contributions to survey sampling procedures.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago), he received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago) in 1924 and an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in sociology from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1926. He taught at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) and at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), before he moved to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1947, where he was Professor of [Social Statistics](wikipedia://en/285943) until his retirement in 1971, shortly before his death.

Together with [W. Edwards Deming](wikipedia://en/70534), he introduced the [iterative proportional fitting](wikipedia://en/23416870) algorithm to estimate cell probabilities in contingency tables subject to constraints on the margins.

He served as president of [AAPOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_Public_Opinion_Research) in 1957–8, and as president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1966.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

He was named a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1939.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

[John Stephan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stephan) was his brother.
