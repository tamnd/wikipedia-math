# Baer ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Baer ring"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), **Baer rings**, **Baer \*-rings**, **Rickart rings**, **Rickart \*-rings**, and **[AW\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/39435930)** are various attempts to give an algebraic analogue of [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185), using axioms about [annihilators](wikipedia://en/328684) of various sets.

:::

Any von Neumann algebra is a [Baer](wikipedia://en/1612114) \*-ring, and much of the theory of [projections](wikipedia://en/294185#Projections) in von Neumann algebras can be extended to all Baer \*-rings, For example, Baer \*-rings can be divided into types I, II, and III in the same way as von Neumann algebras.

In the literature, left Rickart rings have also been termed left **PP-rings**. ("Principal implies projective": See definitions below.)

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

- An [idempotent element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent_element_%28ring_theory%29) of a ring is an element *e* which has the property that *e*<sup>2</sup> = *e*.
- The **left [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684)** of a set $X \subseteq R$ is $\{r\in R\mid rX=\{0\}\}$
- A **(left) Rickart ring** is a ring satisfying any of the following conditions:

<!-- -->

1. the left annihilator of any single element of *R* is generated (as a left ideal) by an idempotent element.
1. (For unital rings) the left annihilator of any element is a direct summand of *R*.
1. All principal left ideals (ideals of the form *Rx*) are [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) *R* modules.[^o4]

<!-- -->

- A **Baer ring** has the following definitions:

<!-- -->

1. The left annihilator of any subset of *R* is generated (as a left ideal) by an idempotent element.
1. (For unital rings) The left annihilator of any subset of *R* is a direct summand of *R*.[^o5] For unital rings, replacing all occurrences of 'left' with 'right' yields an equivalent definition, that is to say, the definition is left-right symmetric.[^o6]

In operator theory, the definitions are strengthened slightly by requiring the ring *R* to have an [involution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_with_involution) $*:R\rightarrow R$. Since this makes *R* isomorphic to its [opposite ring](wikipedia://en/3129323) *R*<sup>op</sup>, the definition of Rickart \*-ring is left-right symmetric.

- A **projection** in a [\*-ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/*-ring) is an idempotent *p* that is [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) (*p*\* = *p*).
- A **Rickart \*-ring** is a \*-ring such that left annihilator of any element is generated (as a left ideal) by a projection.
- A **Baer \*-ring** is a \*-ring such that left annihilator of any subset is generated (as a left ideal) by a projection.
- An **[AW\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/39435930)**, introduced by (Kaplansky 1951), is a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) that is also a Baer \*-ring.

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Since the principal left ideals of a left [hereditary ring](wikipedia://en/5123176) or left semihereditary ring are projective, it is clear that both types are left Rickart rings. This includes [von Neumann regular rings](wikipedia://en/907524), which are left and right semihereditary. If a von Neumann regular ring *R* is also right or left [self injective](wikipedia://en/473775#self_injective_rings), then *R* is Baer.
- Any [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) is Baer, since *all* left and right ideals are summands in *R*, including the annihilators.
- Any [domain](wikipedia://en/487627) is Baer, since all annihilators are $\{0\}$ except for the annihilator of 0, which is *R*, and both $\{0\}$ and *R* are summands of *R*.
- The ring of [bounded linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) are a Baer ring and is also a Baer \*-ring with the involution \* given by the adjoint.
- von Neumann algebras are examples of all the different sorts of ring above.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The projections in a Rickart \*-ring form a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304), which is [complete](wikipedia://en/44777) if the ring is a Baer \*-ring.
