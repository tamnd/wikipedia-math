# Linguistic demography

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Linguistic demography"}

**Linguistic demography** is the [statistical study](wikipedia://en/26685) of [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) among all populations. Estimating the number of speakers of a given language is not straightforward, and various estimates may diverge considerably. This is first of all due to the question of defining "[language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language)" vs. "[dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect)". Identification of varieties as a single language or as distinct languages is often based on ethnic, cultural, or political considerations rather than [mutual intelligibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility). The second difficulty is [multilingualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism), complicating the definition of "native language". Finally, in many countries, insufficient [census](wikipedia://en/6889) data add to the difficulties.

:::

**Demolinguistics** is a branch of [Sociology of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology_of_language) observing linguistic trends as affected by population distribution and redistribution and by the status of societies.

Also given are the estimates of [SIL Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIL_Ethnologue) (2005). Comparing estimates that do not date to the same year is problematic due to the 1.14% per year growth of [world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population) (with significant regional differences).
