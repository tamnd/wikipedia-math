# Gelfond's constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gelfond's constant"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **exponential of pi** $e^{\pi}$,[^o2] also called **Gelfond's constant**,[^o3] is the real number $e$ raised to the power [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) (i.e., the value of the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) at $\pi$).

:::

Its decimal expansion is given by

*$e^{\pi}$* = \23.14069263277926900572... .

Like both $e$ and $\pi$, this constant is both [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689) and [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325). This follows from the [Gelfond–Schneider theorem](wikipedia://en/609487), which establishes $a^{b}$ to be transcendental, given that $a$ is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1158) and not equal to [zero](wikipedia://en/34513) or [one](wikipedia://en/77591639) and $b$ is algebraic but not [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024). We have

$$e^\pi = (e^{i\pi})^{-i} = (-1)^{-i},$$

where $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358). Since $-i$ is algebraic but not rational, $e^{\pi}$ is transcendental. The numbers $\pi$, $e^{\pi}$, and the [lemniscate constant](wikipedia://en/2079065) are also known to be [algebraically independent](wikipedia://en/287808) over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), as demonstrated by [Yuri Nesterenko](wikipedia://en/13129704).[^o4] It is not known whether $e^{\pi}$ is a [Liouville number](wikipedia://en/50964).[^o5] The constant was mentioned in [Hilbert's seventh problem](wikipedia://en/392579) alongside the [Gelfond–Schneider constant](wikipedia://en/886866) $2^{\sqrt{2}}$, and the name "Gelfond's constant" stems from Soviet mathematician [Alexander Gelfond](wikipedia://en/4542389).[^o6]

## Occurrences {#Occurrences}

The constant $e^{\pi}$ appears in relation to the [volumes](wikipedia://en/32498) of [hyperspheres](wikipedia://en/39782).

*Image: hypersphere\_volume\_and\_surface\_area\_graphs.svg.* Graphs of volumes ($V_n$) and [surface areas](wikipedia://en/27725) ($S_{n-1}$) of $n$-balls of radius $1$

The volume of an *n-sphere* with [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) $R$ is given by

$$V_n(R) = \frac{\pi^\frac{n}{2}R^n}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2} + 1\right)},$$

where $\Gamma$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). Considering only unit spheres ($R = 1$) yields

$$V_n(1) = \frac{\pi^\frac{n}{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2} + 1\right)},$$

Any even-dimensional 2*n*-sphere now gives

$$V_{2n}(1) = \frac{\pi^n}{\Gamma(n + 1)} = \frac{\pi^n}{n!}.$$

Summing up all even-dimensional unit sphere volumes and utilizing the [series expansion](wikipedia://en/1575813) of the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) gives[^o8]

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty V_{2n}(1) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\pi^n}{n!} = \exp(\pi) = e^\pi.$$

Also, if one defines $k_{0} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and

$$k_{n+1} = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - k_n^2}}{1 + \sqrt{1 - k_n^2}}$$

for $n > 0$, then the sequence

$$(4/k_{n+1})^{2^{-n}}$$

converges rapidly to $e^{\pi}$.[^o9]

### In physics {#In_physics}

The [Q factor](wikipedia://en/229160) is a measure of how [underdamped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underdamped) an oscillating system is; at large values, it is approximately equal to the number of oscillation cycles it takes for the amplitude of the oscillator to decrease by a factor of $e^{\pi}$, or the energy by a factor of $e^{2\pi}$.[^o11][^o12]

In a [water wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_wave), water molecults near the surface move in a circular motion as the wave propagates, attenuating exponentially with depth. At the characteristic length scale equal to $\tfrac{1}{2}$ the wavelength, known as the [wave base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_base), the circular motion is attenuated by a factor of approximately $e^{\pi}$ relative to the surface and is negligible at lower depths. The circular motion leaves characteristic ripples in coastal seabeds shallower than the typical wave base.[^o13]

A [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) bends light coming from behind it, producing an infinite series of lensed images corresponding to the number of times light wraps around the [photon sphere](wikipedia://en/3072290). For a non-rotating black hole, the ratio of distances between successive images is asymptotically equal to $e^{-2\pi}$.[^o14][^o15]

Blumhofer and Hutter (1997) describe a toy model of a quantum field theory with a [Gribov ambiguity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gribov_ambiguity)-like mechanism that produces multiple [generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_%28particle_physics%29) of fermions, analogous to the real generations in the [Standard Model](wikipedia://en/47641). In the typical case, the mass ratio between successive generations is approximately $e^{\pi}$.[^o16]

## Similar or related constants {#Similar_or_related_constants}

### Ramanujan's constant {#Ramanujan's_constant}

The number $e^{\pi\sqrt{163}}$ is known as [Ramanujan's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan's_constant). Its decimal expansion is given by

$e^{\pi\sqrt{163}}$ = \262537412640768743.99999999999925007259... ,

which turns out to be very close to the integer $640320^{3} + 744$. This is an application of [Heegner numbers](wikipedia://en/522147), where 163 is the Heegner number in question. This number was discovered in 1859 by the mathematician [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422).[^o19] In a 1975 [April Fool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Fools'_Day) article in *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* magazine,[^o20] "Mathematical Games" columnist [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) made the hoax claim that the number was in fact an integer, and that the Indian mathematical genius [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) had predicted it—hence its name. Ramanujan's constant is also a transcendental number.

The coincidental closeness, to within one [trillionth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trillion) of the number $640320^{3} + 744$ is explained by [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) and the [*q*-expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-expansion) of the [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988), specifically:

$$j\big((1 + \sqrt{-163})/2\big) = (-640\,320)^3$$

and

$$(-640\,320)^3 = -e^{\pi \sqrt{163}} + 744 + O\left(e^{-\pi \sqrt{163}}\right),$$

where the error term

$$O\left(e^{-\pi {\sqrt {163}}}\right) = -196\,884/e^{\pi \sqrt{163}} \approx -196\,884/(640\,320^3 + 744) \approx -0.000\,000\,000\,000\,75,$$

which explains why $e^{\pi\sqrt{163}}$ is \0.00000000000075 below $640320^{3} + 744$.

For more detail on this proof, consult the article on [Heegner numbers](wikipedia://en/522147).

### The number $e^{\pi} - \pi$ {#The_number}

The number $e^{\pi} - \pi$ is also very close to an integer, its decimal expansion being given by

$e^{\pi} - \pi$ = \19.99909997918947576726... .

The explanation for this seemingly remarkable coincidence was given by A. Doman in September 2023 and is a result of a sum related to [Jacobi theta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobi_theta_functions) as follows:

$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty (8\pi k^2 - 2) e^{-\pi k^2} = 1.$$

The first term dominates, since the sum of the terms for $k \geq 2$ total $\sim 0.0003436.$ The sum can therefore be truncated to $(8\pi - 2) e^{-\pi} \approx 1,$ where solving for $e^\pi$ gives $e^\pi \approx 8\pi - 2.$ Rewriting the approximation for $e^\pi$ and using the approximation for $7\pi \approx 22$ gives

$$e^\pi \approx \pi + 7\pi - 2 \approx \pi + 22 - 2 = \pi + 20.$$

Thus, rearranging terms gives $e^\pi - \pi \approx 20.$ Ironically, the crude approximation for $7\pi$ yields an additional order of magnitude of precision.[^o22]

### The number $\pi^{e}$ {#The_number}

The decimal expansion of $\pi^{e}$ is given by

$\pi^e =$ \22.45915771836104547342... .

It is not known whether or not this number is transcendental. Note that, by [Gelfond–Schneider theorem](wikipedia://en/609487), we can only infer definitively whether or not $a^{b}$ is transcendental if $a$ and $b$ are algebraic ($a$ and $b$ are both considered [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826)).

In the case of $e^{\pi}$, we are only able to prove this number transcendental due to properties of complex exponential forms and the above equivalency given to transform it into $(-1)^{-i}$, allowing the application of Gelfond–Schneider theorem.

$\pi^{e}$ has no such equivalence, and hence, as both $\pi$ and $e$ are transcendental, we cannot use the Gelfond–Schneider theorem to draw conclusions about the transcendence of $\pi^{e}$. However the currently unproven [Schanuel's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2031353) would imply its transcendence.[^o24]

### The number $i^{i}$ {#The_number}

Using the [principal value](wikipedia://en/408225) of the [complex logarithm](wikipedia://en/4870290),

$$i^i = (e^{i\pi/2})^i = e^{-\pi/2} = (e^\pi)^{-1/2}.$$

The decimal expansion of is given by

$i^i =$ \0.20787957635076190854... .

Its transcendence follows directly from the transcendence of $e^{\pi}$ and directly from the Gelfond–Schneider theorem.
