# Symbolic dynamics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symbolic dynamics"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **symbolic dynamics** is the study of dynamical systems defined on a discrete space consisting of infinite [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) of abstract symbols. The evolution of the dynamical system is defined as a simple shift of the sequence.

:::

Because of their explicit, discrete nature, such systems are often relatively easy to characterize and understand. They form a key tool for studying topological or smooth [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), because in many important cases it is possible to reduce the dynamics of a more general dynamical system to a symbolic system. To do so, a [Markov partition](wikipedia://en/11491735) is used to provide a [finite cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_cover) for the smooth system; each set of the cover is associated with a single symbol, and the sequences of symbols result as a trajectory of the system moves from one covering set to another.

## History {#History}

The idea goes back to [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675)'s 1898 paper on the [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865) of negative [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770).[^o3] It was applied by [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523) in 1921 to the construction of a nonperiodic recurrent geodesic. Related work was done by [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) in 1924 (for the system now called [Artin billiard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_billiard)), [Pekka Myrberg](wikipedia://en/26277760), [Paul Koebe](wikipedia://en/4313643), [Jakob Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516), [G. A. Hedlund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._A._Hedlund).

The first formal treatment was developed by Morse and Hedlund in their 1938 paper.[^o4] [George Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Birkhoff), [Norman Levinson](wikipedia://en/1090122) and the pair [Mary Cartwright](wikipedia://en/1529094) and [J. E. Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Littlewood) have applied similar methods to qualitative analysis of nonautonomous second order [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309).

[Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) used symbolic sequences and [shifts of finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shift_of_finite_type) in his 1948 paper *[A mathematical theory of communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_mathematical_theory_of_communication)* that gave birth to [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773).

During the late 1960s the method of symbolic dynamics was developed to [hyperbolic toral automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_toral_automorphism) by [Roy Adler](wikipedia://en/41177893) and [Benjamin Weiss](wikipedia://en/40423267),[^o5] and to [Anosov diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/2320078) by [Yakov Sinai](wikipedia://en/1751626) who used the symbolic model to construct [Gibbs measures](wikipedia://en/3085914).[^o6] In the early 1970s the theory was extended to Anosov flows by [Marina Ratner](wikipedia://en/12687622), and to [Axiom A](wikipedia://en/5471083) diffeomorphisms and flows by [Rufus Bowen](wikipedia://en/24717250).

A spectacular application of the methods of symbolic dynamics is [Sharkovskii's theorem](wikipedia://en/87947) about [periodic orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit) of a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) of an interval into itself (1964).

## Examples {#Examples}

Consider the set of two-sided infinite sequences on two symbols, 0 and 1. A typical element in this set looks like: (..., 0, 1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 1, ... )

There will be exactly two fixed points under the shift map: the sequence of all zeroes, and the sequence of all ones. A periodic sequence will have a periodic orbit. For instance, the sequence (..., 0, 1, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0, 1, ...) will have period two.

More complex concepts such as [heteroclinic orbits](wikipedia://en/3948758) and [homoclinic orbits](wikipedia://en/3948734) also have simple descriptions in this system. For example, any sequence that has only a finite number of ones will have a homoclinic orbit, tending to the sequence of all zeros in forward and backward iterations.

## Applications {#Applications}

Symbolic dynamics originated as a method to study general dynamical systems; now its techniques and ideas have found significant applications in [data storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_storage_device) and [transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_transmission), [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), the motions of the planets and many other areas. The distinct feature in symbolic dynamics is that time is measured in *[discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_time)* intervals. So at each time interval the system is in a particular *state*. Each state is associated with a symbol and the evolution of the system is described by an infinite [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of symbols—represented effectively as [strings](wikipedia://en/27701). If the system states are not inherently discrete, then the [state vector](wikipedia://en/30876419) must be discretized, so as to get a [coarse-grained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarse-grained) description of the system.
