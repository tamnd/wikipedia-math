# Sándor Csörgő

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Sándor Csörgő"}

Csörgő Sándor **Sándor Csörgő** (16 July 1947 at [Egerfarmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egerfarmos), [Heves County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heves_County) – 14 February 2008 at [Újszeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Újszeged), [Csongrád County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csongrád_County)[^o2]) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and a professor at the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged).

:::

His main fields were [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), and [asymptotic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_theory). He did important research on the [St. Petersburg paradox](wikipedia://en/466277).

He was a member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).

His brother [Miklós Csörgő](wikipedia://en/81252297) is also a mathematician.

## Career {#Career}

Csörgő graduated from the István Dobó High School in [Eger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eger), [Heves County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heves_County). He did undergraduate studies in the mathematics department at [József Attila University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/József_Attila_University) ([University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged)), graduating in 1970, and immediately joined the [János Bolyai Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/30865370) at the University as a graduate student. In 1972, he presented his doctoral thesis to [Béla Szőkefalvi-Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béla_Szőkefalvi-Nagy) and [Károly Tandori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Károly_Tandori).

From 1972 to 1975 he completed a post-doctoral thesis at [Kiev State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_State_University) under [Anatoliy Skorokhod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Skorokhod). On its completion in 1975, he returned to Szeged, where he was appointed assistant professor, a role he held until 1978, when he was awarded a teaching professorship, leading the Department of Stochastics.

In 1984 he moved to the United States, where he was visiting researcher at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), (1984–1985), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (1985), the [University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina,_Chapel_Hill) (1989–1990), and the [University of Michigan, Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan,_Ann_Arbor) (1990–1998).

In 2000 he returned to the Bolyai Institute to teach and do research.

Csörgő's work is often cited in highly respected domestic and foreign journals, but most often in English. He is among the [ISI Highly Cited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISI_Highly_Cited) Researchers in Mathematics.

In 2001 he was elected as an Associate Member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences), full Membership following in 2007.

He died in Szeged in 2008 and is buried in [Újszeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Újszeged) cemetery.[^o2]
