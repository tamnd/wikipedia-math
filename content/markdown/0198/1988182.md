# Marcel J. E. Golay

*Image: Golay Cell Schematic.svg.* Schematic of a [Golay cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golay_cell)[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Marcel J. E. Golay"}

**Marcel Jules Edouard Golay** (; May 3, 1902 – April 27, 1989) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), and [information theorist](wikipedia://en/14773), who [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) to real-world military and industrial problems. He was born in [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuchâtel), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland).[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Golay studied [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at the Eidgenössische Technische Hochschule ([Swiss Federal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich)) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich). He joined [Bell laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_laboratories) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) in 1924, spending four years there. He received a Ph.D. in physics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1931.[^o5]

Golay then joined the [US Army Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army_Signal_Corps), eventually rising to the post of Chief Scientist. He was based mostly in [Fort Monmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Monmouth), [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey). He developed an IR "radar" based on its [Golay Detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golay_Detector), the SCR-268T specifically designed to the detection of vessels (S/S Normandie was detected at its inaugural crossing). The SCR-268 (using Barkausen vacuum tubes) and the SCR-268T were to work together. However the 268T, only used in the Pacific theater was abandoned before the end of the war.[^o5]

Between 1955 and 1963, Golay was a consultant for Philco Corporation of Philadelphia, PA, and the Perkin-Elmer Corporation of Norwalk, Connecticut. In 1963, Golay joined the [Perkin-Elmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perkin-Elmer) company full-time as senior research scientist.[^o5] Golay worked on many problems, including [gas chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chromatography) and [optical spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_spectroscopy). It was during this period when he patented an Analysis of Images, for two-dimensional parallel data processing, and worked to develop the idea, called Golay Logic for Optical Pattern Recognition along with Kendall Preston, Philip Norgren, David Dacava and [Joseph Carvalko, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Carvalko,_Jr.)[^o6] He remained with Perkin-Elmer for the rest of his life.

## Achievements {#Achievements}

- Discoverer of the famous [binary](wikipedia://en/344971) and [ternary](wikipedia://en/3070794) Golay codes, which are perfect [error-correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_correction_codes) that generalize the [Hamming code](wikipedia://en/41226). They were used in the [Voyager](wikipedia://en/344971#NASA_deep_space_missions) probes, and led to advances in the theory of [finite groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_groups).
- Co-author with [Abraham Savitzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Savitzky) of the [Savitzky–Golay filter](wikipedia://en/1988157).
- Inventor of the [Golay cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golay_cell), a type of [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) detector.
- He introduced [complementary sequences](wikipedia://en/8799355). Those are pairs of [binary sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_sequence) whose autocorrelation functions add up to zero for all non-zero time shifts. Today they are used in various WiFi and 3G standards.
- He introduced the theory of dispersion in open tubular columns (capillary columns) and demonstrated their efficacy at the Second International Symposium on Gas Chromatography at Amsterdam in 1958.[^o8]
