# Bundle gerbe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bundle gerbe"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **bundle gerbe** is a [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) model of certain 1-[gerbes](wikipedia://en/822043) with [connection](wikipedia://en/308815), or equivalently of a 2-class in [Deligne cohomology](wikipedia://en/31615715).

:::

## Topology {#Topology}

$U(1)$-[principal bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_bundles) over a space $M$ (see [circle bundle](wikipedia://en/2794417)) are geometrical realizations of 1-classes in Deligne cohomology which consist of 1-form [connections](wikipedia://en/308815) and 2-form curvatures. The topology of a $U(1)$ bundle is classified by its [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349), which is an element of $H^2(M, \mathbb{Z})$, the second integral cohomology of $M$.

[Gerbes](wikipedia://en/822043), or more precisely 1-gerbes, are abstract descriptions of Deligne 2-classes, which each define an element of $H^3(M, \mathbb{Z})$, the third integral cohomology of *M*.

### As a cohomology class in Deligne cohomology {#As_a_cohomology_class_in_Deligne_cohomology}

Recall for a smooth manifold $M$ the p-th Deligne cohomology groups are defined by the [hypercohomology](wikipedia://en/10324484) of the complex

$$\mathbb{Z}(q)_D^\infty = \underline{\mathbb{Z}}(q) \to \mathcal{A}_{M,\mathbb{C}}^0 \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{A}_{M,\mathbb{C}}^1 \xrightarrow{d} \cdots \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{A}_{M,\mathbb{C}}^{q-1}$$

called the **weight q Deligne complex**, where $\mathcal{A}^k_{M,\mathbb{C}}$ is the sheaf of germs of smooth differential k-forms tensored with $\mathbb{C}$. So, we write

$$\mathbb{H}^*_D(M,\mathbb{Z}(q)^{\infty}_D)$$

for the Deligne-cohomology groups of weight $q$. In the case $q = 3$ the Deligne complex is then

$$\underline{\mathbb{Z}}(3) \to \mathcal{A}^0_{M,\mathbb{C}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{A}^1_{M,\mathbb{C}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{A}^2_{M,\mathbb{C}}$$

We can understand the Deligne cohomology groups by looking at the Čech resolution giving a double complex. There is also an associated short exact sequence[^o4]p. 7

$$0 \to \frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C},M}^{2}(M)_{cl}}{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C},M}^{2}(M)_{cl,0}} \to \mathbb{H}^3(M, \mathbb{Z}(3)_D^\infty) \to H^3(M,\mathbb{Z}) \to 0$$

where $\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C},M}^{2}(M)_{cl}$ are the closed germs of complex valued 2-forms on $M$ and $\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C},M}^{2}(M)_{cl,0}$ is the subspace of such forms where period integrals are integral. This can be used to show $H^3(M,\mathbb{Z})$ are the isomorphism classes of $\mathbb{C}^*$ bundle-gerbes on a smooth manifold $M$, or equivalently, the isomorphism classes of $B\mathbb{C}^*$-bundles on $M$.

## History {#History}

Historically the most popular construction of a gerbe is a [category-theoretic](wikipedia://en/5869) model featured in Giraud's theory of gerbes, which are roughly [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) of [groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543) over *M*.

In 1994 [^o6] Murray introduced bundle gerbes, which are geometric realizations of 1-gerbes. For many purposes these are more suitable for calculations than Giraud's realization, because their construction is entirely within the framework of classical geometry. In fact, as their name suggests, they are [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526).

This notion was extended to higher gerbes the following year.[^o7]

## Relationship with twisted *K*-theory {#Relationship_with_twisted_K-theory}

In [Twisted K-theory and the K-theory of Bundle Gerbes](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0106194) [^o9] the authors defined modules of bundle gerbes and used this to define a [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) for bundle gerbes. They then showed that this K-theory is isomorphic to Rosenberg's [twisted K-theory](wikipedia://en/4297226), and provides an [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396)-free construction.

In addition they defined a notion of [twisted Chern character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_Chern_character) which is a [characteristic class](wikipedia://en/363530) for an element of twisted K-theory. The twisted Chern character is a [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) that represents a class in the [twisted cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_cohomology) with respect to the [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235) operator

$$d + H$$

where $d$ is the ordinary [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) and the *[twist](wikipedia://en/40392210)* $H$ is a closed 3-form. This construction was extended to [equivariant K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_topological_K-theory) and to [holomorphic K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_K-theory) by Mathai and Stevenson.[^o10]

## Relationship with field theory {#Relationship_with_field_theory}

Bundle gerbes have also appeared in the context of [conformal field theories](wikipedia://en/364774). [Gawedzki](wikipedia://en/68471920) and [Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuno_Reis_%28mathematician%29) have interpreted the Wess–Zumino term in the [Wess–Zumino–Witten model](wikipedia://en/1593924) (WZW) of [string](wikipedia://en/28305) propagation on a [group manifold](wikipedia://en/17945) as the [connection](wikipedia://en/308815) of a bundle gerbe. [Urs Schreiber](wikipedia://en/41104045), [Christoph Schweigert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Schweigert) and [Konrad Waldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Waldorf) have used this construction to extend WZW models to unoriented surfaces and, more generally, the global [Kalb–Ramond coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalb–Ramond_field) to unoriented strings.

More details can be found at the [n-Category Café](http://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/):

- *[Bundle Gerbes: General Idea and Definition](http://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2006/10/bundle_gerbes.html)*
- *[Bundle Gerbes: Connections and Surface Transport](http://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2006/10/bundle_gerbes_connections_and.html)*
