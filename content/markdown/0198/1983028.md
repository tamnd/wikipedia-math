# Władysław Ślebodziński

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Władysław Ślebodziński"}

**Władysław Zygmunt Ślebodziński** (; 6 February 1884 – 3 January 1972) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), a former [Auschwitz concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp) prisoner, a professor at the [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology) and a member of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences).

:::

In 1961–1963, he served as president of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791). He was a co-founder and a leading representative of the Wroclaw School of Mathematics. He is noted for introducing an operator in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) later named the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437).

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

*Image: Władysław in 1895.JPG.* Ślebodziński in 1895

Władysław Ślebodziński was born on 6 February 1884 in [Pysznica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pysznica),[^o5] then part of the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria), as the son of Ignacy and Anna (Gorylska).[^o6][^o7] In 1903–1908, he studied mathematics and physics at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków).[^o8]

In 1908, he bacame a teacher of mathematics and worked at [gymnasiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in [Sanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanok) and [Tarnobrzeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnobrzeg). In 1912, Ślebodziński transferred to Kraków where he held a teaching position at the Imperial-Royal Real School.[^o18] In 1913–1914, he pursued further studies at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen)[^o9] attending lectures by [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625), [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) and [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302).[^o18]

### Career beginnings {#Career_beginnings}

During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), Ślebodziński served only six months in the [Austrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army) before being transferred back to Kraków at the Imperial-Royal Real School due to his essential role in education. In this period, Ślebodziński became acquainted with such prominent mathematicians as [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735), [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), and [Włodzimierz Stożek](wikipedia://en/1821218).[^o18]

In the 1920s, Ślebodziński lectured in mathematics at the Faculty of Pharmacy of the [University of Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poznań). He simultaneously taught mathematics at the State School of Machine Construction ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Państwowa Szkoła Budowy Maszyn*).[^o11] He was also active in the Association of Teachers of Secondary and Higher Schools (TNSW) serving as chairman of the Poznań branch of the organization in 1924.[^o12]

In 1927, Ślebodziński participated in the First Polish Mathematical Congress in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów), having been appointed chairman of the Geometry Section. In 1928, Ślebodziński earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) based on his thesis *O pewnej klasie powierzchni Riemanna* (On a Certain Class of Riemann Surfaces) written under the supervision of [Kazimierz Żorawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Żorawski).[^o13] In 1934, he obtained his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) and started work as a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) at the University of Warsaw.[^o18]

*Image: 8731viki Politechnika Wrocławska ul. Wybrzeże Wyspiańskiego. Foto Barbara Maliszewska.jpg.* Ślebodziński was affiliated with the [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology) for much of his career

### World War II {#World_War_II}

During the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he was forced to relocate from [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań) to [Bochnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochnia) in southern Poland.[^o15] He gave [underground lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Poland_during_World_War_II), leading to his imprisonment by [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany). He survived three German concentration camps: [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz) (1942–1945), where he gave underground university-level lectures as prisoner no. 79053 and survived the ["Death March"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_marches_during_the_Holocaust) to [Wodzisław Śląski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodzisław_Śląski) after the camp had been evacuated before the advancement of the Soviet troops in 1945;[^o18] [Gross-Rosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross-Rosen); and [Nordhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittelbau-Dora_concentration_camp).[^o16]

### Postwar career {#Postwar_career}

In 1945, he became a joint professor at [Wrocław University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University) and at the [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology), and from 1951 he was a professor at the [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology). From 1949 until 1960, he was a Professor of the [Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/67042302) of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences).

In 1950, he was one of the co-founders of the journal *Colloquim Mathematicum* alongside [Edward Marczewski](wikipedia://en/1028149), [Bronisław Knaster](wikipedia://en/1003125) and [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735).[^o18] He is also considered one of the founders of the Wrocław School of Mathematics.[^o9] The relations between the four mathematicians are described by Roman Duda and Aleksander Weron as follows:[^o19]

> They were united by a pioneering zeal and a firm conviction that Wrocław was, and would remain, Polish, as well as by their determination to settle there permanently. As mathematicians, they possessed a substantial body of work recognized internationally, which gave them considerable and natural academic authority; they knew their own worth and held one another in high esteem. All four settled in \[the district of\] Biskupin (...), which had survived the devastation of [the siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Breslau), a circumstance that fostered close and friendly relations among them.

Between 1961–1963, he served as president of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).[^o18] His notable students included [Witold Roter](wikipedia://en/67031814), Ryszard Deszcz, Stefan Drobot, Abraham Goetz, and Tadeusz Huskowski.[^o13]

He died on 3 January 1972, in [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), and is buried in the Wrocław Cemetery located in Sępolno.[^o20]

### Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1908, he married Anna Gisella Zarnecka (1879-1970) in St. Nicholas Church in Kraków. They had two children: son Lesław, a chemistry teacher, and daughter Zofia Krystyna, a mathematics teacher and a graduate of the [University of Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poznań).[^o22][^o18]

## Awards, decorations, and honors {#Awards,_decorations,_and_honors}

- 1958: Commander's Cross of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta)[^o18]
- 1955: Polish State Award badge (team award of 2nd degree) for his contributions in differential geometry[^o24]
- 1954: [Polish Golden Cross of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Merit_%28Poland%29), for his scientific achievements[^o25][^o5]
- 1918: [1908 Jubilee Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Jubilee_Cross) ([Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary)),[^o26]

He accepted [honorary doctorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) from the [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology) (1965), [Poznań University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań_University_of_Technology) (1967),[^o27] and the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław) (1970).[^o28] He also became an honorary member of the Polish Mathematical Society.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In his early career Ślebodziński was interested in [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316);[^o18] however, he later focused primarily on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and made his most important contributions in that area. In 1931,[^o30] he introduced a new differential operator known as the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437),[^o31] in his work titled *Sur les équations canoniques de Hamilton*. According to [J.A. Schouten](wikipedia://en/2862062),[^o32] it was [David van Dantzig](wikipedia://en/528853) who named the operation "Lie derivative" (after [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010))[^o33] for the first time in a two-part paper published in 1932 acknowledging it was Ślebodziński who had introduced it.[^o34]

The Lie derivative evaluates the change of a [tensor field](wikipedia://en/256738) eg. scalar functions, [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) and [one-forms](wikipedia://en/314692), along the [flow](wikipedia://en/2619735) defined by another vector field. It played a significant role in the rapid advancement of several branches of differential geometry serving as a crucial and highly effective tool in the study of [automorphism groups](wikipedia://en/56661772) and finding broad, meaningful applications throughout [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).[^o18]
