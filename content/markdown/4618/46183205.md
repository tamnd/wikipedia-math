# Y and H transforms

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Y and H transforms"}

In mathematics, the $Y$ **transforms** and $\mathbf{H}$ **transforms** are complementary pairs of [integral transforms](wikipedia://en/430912) involving, respectively, the [Neumann function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumann_function) ([Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700) of the second kind) $Y_{\nu}$ of order $\nu$ and the [Struve function](wikipedia://en/16070185) $\mathbf{H}_{\nu}$ of the same order.

:::

For a given function $f(r)$, the $Y$-transform of order $\nu$ is given by

$$F(k) = \int_0^\infty f(r) Y_{\nu}(kr) \sqrt{kr} \, dr$$

The inverse of above is the $\mathbf{H}$-transform of the same order; for a given function $F(k)$, the $\mathbf{H}$-transform of order $\nu$ is given by

$$f(r) = \int_0^\infty F(k) \mathbf{H}_{\nu}(kr) \sqrt{kr} \, dk$$

These transforms are closely related to the [Hankel transform](wikipedia://en/1452960), as both involve Bessel functions. In problems of mathematical physics and applied mathematics, the Hankel, $Y$, $\mathbf{H}$ transforms all may appear in problems having [axial symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673). Hankel transforms are however much more commonly seen due to their connection with the 2-dimensional Fourier transform. The $Y$, $\mathbf{H}$ transforms appear in situations with singular behaviour on the axis of symmetry.[^n1]
