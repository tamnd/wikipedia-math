# Responsive predicate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Responsive predicate"}

In [formal semantics](wikipedia://en/31395652) a **responsive predicate** is an embedding predicate which can take either a [declarative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declarative_sentence) or an [interrogative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interrogative) [complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_clause). For instance, the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) [verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verb) *know* is responsive as shown by the following examples.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

1. Bill knows whether Mary left.
1. Bill knows that Mary left.

Responsives are contrasted with *rogatives* such as *wonder* which can only take an interrogative complement and *anti-rogatives* such as *believe* which can only take a declarative complement.

1. Bill wonders whether Mary left.
1. \*Bill wonders that Mary left.
1. \*Bill believes whether Mary left.
1. Bill believes that Mary left.

Some analyses have derived these distinctions from [type compatibility](wikipedia://en/40282) while others explain them in terms of particular properties of the embedding verbs and their complements.
