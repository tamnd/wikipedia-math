# Homotopical algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Homotopical algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **homotopical algebra** is a collection of concepts comprising the *nonabelian* aspects of [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), and possibly the [abelian](wikipedia://en/45063) aspects as special cases. The *homotopical* nomenclature stems from the fact that a common approach to such generalizations is via [abstract homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_homotopy_theory), as in [nonabelian algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/19515158#Nonabelian_algebraic_topology), and in particular the theory of [closed model categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_model_category).

:::

This subject has received much attention in recent years due to new foundational work of [Vladimir Voevodsky](wikipedia://en/104482), [Eric Friedlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Friedlander), [Andrei Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820), and others resulting in the [**A**<sup>1</sup> homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_homotopy_theory) for [quasiprojective varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiprojective_variety) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). Voevodsky has used this new algebraic homotopy theory to prove the [Milnor conjecture](wikipedia://en/2914126) (for which he was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859)) and later, in collaboration with [Markus Rost](wikipedia://en/6842771), the full [Bloch–Kato conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloch–Kato_conjecture).
