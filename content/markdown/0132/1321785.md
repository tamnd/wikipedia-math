# Quasi-projective variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-projective variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quasi-projective variety** in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) is a [locally closed subset](wikipedia://en/26136898) of a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469), i.e., the intersection inside some [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) of a Zariski-open and a [Zariski-closed](wikipedia://en/241744) subset. A similar definition is used in [scheme theory](wikipedia://en/364754), where a *quasi-projective scheme* is a locally closed [subscheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscheme) of some [projective space](wikipedia://en/31453337).

:::

## Relationship to affine varieties {#Relationship_to_affine_varieties}

An [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) is a Zariski-open subset of a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135), and since any closed affine subset $U$ can be expressed as an intersection of the [projective completion](wikipedia://en/1698977#Homogenization) $\bar{U}$ and the affine space embedded in the projective space, this implies that any [affine variety](wikipedia://en/320468) is quasiprojective. There are [locally closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_closed) subsets of projective space that are not affine, so that quasi-projective is more general than affine. Taking the complement of a single point in projective space of dimension at least 2 gives a non-affine quasi-projective variety. This is also an example of a quasi-projective variety that is neither affine nor projective.

## Examples {#Examples}

Since quasi-projective varieties generalize both affine and projective varieties, they are sometimes referred to simply as *varieties*. Varieties isomorphic to affine algebraic varieties as quasi-projective varieties (see [Morphism of algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/24457573)) are called [affine varieties](wikipedia://en/320468); similarly for projective varieties. For example, the complement of a point in the affine line, i.e., $X=\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$, is isomorphic to the zero set of the polynomial $xy-1$ in the affine plane. As an affine set $X$ is not closed (when one assumes that the base field be [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed) or at least infinite) since any proper closed subset of $\mathbb A^1$ is finite. More generally, the variety $\mathbb A^n\setminus\{f=0\}$, with $f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, is isomorphic to the hypersurface in $\mathbb A^{n+1}$ given by the equation $x_{n+1}f-1=0$.[^o6] For another example, the complement of any [conic](wikipedia://en/19008673) in projective space of dimension 2 is affine. Varieties isomorphic to open subsets of affine varieties are called **quasi-affine**.

Quasi-projective varieties (like their generalization, [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754)) are *locally affine* in the same sense that a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) is locally [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697): every point of a quasi-projective variety has a neighborhood which is an affine variety. This yields a basis of affine sets for the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) on a quasi-projective variety.
