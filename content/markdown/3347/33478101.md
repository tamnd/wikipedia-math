# Rep-tile

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rep-tile"}

*Image: Self-replication of sphynx hexidiamonds.svg.* The "sphinx" [polyiamond](wikipedia://en/185271) rep-tile. Four copies of the [sphinx](wikipedia://en/35188912) can be put together as shown to make a larger sphinx.

In the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671), a **rep-tile** or **reptile** is a shape that can be [dissected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) into smaller copies of the same shape. The term was coined as a [pun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pun) on animal [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptilia) by [recreational mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_mathematician) [Solomon W. Golomb](wikipedia://en/1860707) and popularized by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) in his "[Mathematical Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Games_%28column%29)" column in the May 1963 issue of *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*.[^o2] In 2012 a generalization of rep-tiles called [self-tiling tile sets](wikipedia://en/42595150) was introduced by [Lee Sallows](wikipedia://en/32308380) in *[Mathematics Magazine](wikipedia://en/4230329)*.[^n1]

:::

## Terminology {#Terminology}

*Image: L\_substitution\_tiling.svg.* The chair substitution (left) and a portion of a [chair tiling](wikipedia://en/63508559) (right)

A rep-tile is labelled rep-*n* if the dissection uses *n* copies. Such a shape necessarily forms the [prototile](wikipedia://en/39372) for a tiling of the plane, in many cases a [nonperiodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145). A rep-tile dissection using different sizes of the original shape is called an irregular rep-tile or irreptile. If the dissection uses *n* copies, the shape is said to be irrep-*n*. If all these sub-tiles are of different sizes then the tiling is additionally described as perfect. A shape that is rep-*n* or irrep-*n* is trivially also irrep-(*kn* − *k* + *n*) for any *k* > 1, by replacing the smallest tile in the rep-*n* dissection by *n* even smaller tiles. The order of a shape, whether using rep-tiles or irrep-tiles is the smallest possible number of tiles which will suffice.[^n2]

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: pinwheel 2.gif.* Defining an aperiodic tiling (the [pinwheel tiling](wikipedia://en/9402045)) by repeatedly dissecting and inflating a rep-tile.

Every [square](wikipedia://en/659939), [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048), [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496), [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884), or [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is rep-4. The [sphinx](wikipedia://en/35188912) [hexiamond](wikipedia://en/185271) (illustrated above) is rep-4 and rep-9, and is one of few known self-replicating pentagons. The [Gosper island](wikipedia://en/3918047#Properties) is rep-7. The [Koch snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959) is irrep-7: six small snowflakes of the same size, together with another snowflake with three times the area of the smaller ones, can combine to form a single larger snowflake.

A [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420) with side lengths in the ratio 1:2 is rep-5, and its rep-5 dissection forms the basis of the aperiodic [pinwheel tiling](wikipedia://en/9402045). By [Pythagoras' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagoras'_theorem), the [hypotenuse](wikipedia://en/251366), or sloping side of the rep-5 triangle, has a length of $\sqrt{5}$.

The international standard [ISO 216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_216) defines sizes of paper sheets using the $\sqrt{2}$, in which the long side of a rectangular sheet of paper is the [square root of two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_root_of_two) times the short side of the paper. Rectangles in this shape are rep-2. A rectangle (or parallelogram) is rep-*n* if its [aspect ratio](wikipedia://en/1021) is $\sqrt{n}$:1. An [isosceles](wikipedia://en/315428) right triangle is also rep-2.

## Rep-tiles and symmetry {#Rep-tiles_and_symmetry}

Some rep-tiles, like the [square](wikipedia://en/659939) and [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285), are [symmetrical](wikipedia://en/53741) and remain identical when [reflected in a mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_reflection). Others, like the [sphinx](wikipedia://en/35188912), are [asymmetrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetry) and exist in [two distinct forms](wikipedia://en/32703814) related by mirror-reflection. Dissection of the sphinx and some other asymmetric rep-tiles requires use of both the original shape and its mirror-image.

## Rep-tiles and polyforms {#Rep-tiles_and_polyforms}

Some rep-tiles are based on [polyforms](wikipedia://en/691765) like [polyiamonds](wikipedia://en/185271) and [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634), or shapes created by laying [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) edge-to-edge.

### Squares {#Squares}

If a polyomino is rectifiable, that is, able to tile a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048), then it will also be a rep-tile, because the rectangle will have an integer side length ratio and will thus tile a [square](wikipedia://en/659939). This can be seen in the [octominoes](wikipedia://en/7342033), which are created from eight squares. Two copies of some octominoes will tile a square; therefore these octominoes are also rep-16 rep-tiles.

*Image: Rep-tiles constructed from rectifiable octominoes.gif.* Rep-tiles based on rectifiable [octominoes](wikipedia://en/7342033)

Four copies of some [nonominoes](wikipedia://en/2034822) and [nonakings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyking) will tile a square, therefore these [polyforms](wikipedia://en/691765) are also rep-36 rep-tiles.

*Image: Nonominoes.gif.* Rep-tiles created from rectifiable [nonominoes](wikipedia://en/2034822) and [9-polykings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyking) (nonakings)

### Equilateral triangles {#Equilateral_triangles}

Similarly, if a [polyiamond](wikipedia://en/185271) tiles an equilateral triangle, it will also be a rep-tile.

### Right triangles {#Right_triangles}

A [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420) is a triangle containing one right angle of 90°. Two particular forms of right triangle have attracted the attention of rep-tile researchers, the 45°-90°-45° triangle and the 30°-60°-90° triangle.

#### 45°-90°-45° triangles {#45°-90°-45°_triangles}

Polyforms based on [isosceles](wikipedia://en/315428) [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420), with sides in the ratio 1 : 1 : [$\sqrt{2}$](wikipedia://en/341682), are known as [polyabolos](wikipedia://en/710415). An infinite number of them are rep-tiles. Indeed, the simplest of all rep-tiles is a single isosceles right triangle. It is rep-2 when divided by a single line bisecting the right angle to the [hypotenuse](wikipedia://en/251366). Rep-2 rep-tiles are also rep-2<sup>n</sup> and the rep-4,8,16+ triangles yield further rep-tiles. These are found by discarding half of the sub-copies and permutating the remainder until they are [mirror-symmetrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) within a right triangle. In other words, two copies will tile a right triangle. One of these new rep-tiles is reminiscent of the fish formed from three [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285).

*Image: Right triangle fish rep-tile.gif.* A fish-like rep-tile based on four isosceles right triangles

#### 30°-60°-90° triangles {#30°-60°-90°_triangles}

Polyforms based on 30°-60°-90° right triangles, with sides in the ratio 1 : [$\sqrt{3}$](wikipedia://en/9126665) : 2, are known as [polydrafters](wikipedia://en/710404). Some are identical to [polyiamonds](wikipedia://en/185271).[^o16]

## Multiple and variant rep-tilings {#Multiple_and_variant_rep-tilings}

Many of the common rep-tiles are rep-$n^{2}$ for all positive integer values of $n$. In particular this is true for three [trapezoids](wikipedia://en/201826) including the one formed from three equilateral triangles, for three axis-parallel hexagons (the L-tromino, L-tetromino, and P-pentomino), and the sphinx hexiamond.[^n3] In addition, many rep-tiles, particularly those with higher rep-*n*, can be self-tiled in different ways. For example, the rep-9 L-tetramino has at least fourteen different rep-tilings. The rep-9 sphinx hexiamond can also be tiled in different ways.

## Rep-tiles with infinite sides {#Rep-tiles_with_infinite_sides}

*Image: Horned triangle or teragonic triangle.gif.* Horned triangle or teragonic triangle

The most familiar rep-tiles are polygons with a finite number of sides, but some shapes with an infinite number of sides can also be rep-tiles. For example, the [teragonic](wikipedia://en/17677626) triangle, or horned triangle, is rep-4. It is also an example of a fractal rep-tile.

## Pentagonal rep-tiles {#Pentagonal_rep-tiles}

Triangular and quadrilateral (four-sided) rep-tiles are common, but pentagonal rep-tiles are rare. For a long time, the [sphinx](wikipedia://en/35188912) was widely believed to be the only example known, but Karl Scherer and [George Sicherman](wikipedia://en/4015574) have found more examples, including a double-pyramid and an elongated version of the sphinx.[^o21] However, the sphinx and its extended versions are the only known pentagons that can be rep-tiled with equal copies.[^o22]

## Rep-tiles and fractals {#Rep-tiles_and_fractals}

### Rep-tiles as fractals {#Rep-tiles_as_fractals}

Rep-tiles can be used to create [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913), or shapes that are [self-similar](wikipedia://en/28782) at smaller and smaller scales. A rep-tile fractal is formed by subdividing the rep-tile, removing one or more copies of the subdivided shape, and then continuing [recursively](wikipedia://en/25407). For instance, the [Sierpinski carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_carpet) is formed in this way from a rep-tiling of a square into 27 smaller squares, and the [Sierpinski triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_triangle) is formed from a rep-tiling of an equilateral triangle into four smaller triangles. When one sub-copy is discarded, a rep-4 L-[triomino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triomino) can be used to create four fractals, two of which are identical except for [orientation](wikipedia://en/2544098).

### Fractals as rep-tiles {#Fractals_as_rep-tiles}

Because [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913) are often self-similar on smaller and smaller scales, many may be decomposed into copies of themselves like a rep-tile. However, if the fractal has an empty [interior](wikipedia://en/55610), this decomposition may not lead to a tiling of the entire plane. For example, the [Sierpinski triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_triangle) is rep-3, tiled with three copies of itself, and the [Sierpinski carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_carpet) is rep-8, tiled with eight copies of itself, but repetition of these decompositions does not form a tiling. On the other hand, the [dragon curve](wikipedia://en/367064) is a [space-filling curve](wikipedia://en/352398) with a non-empty interior; it is rep-4, and does form a tiling. Similarly, the [Gosper island](wikipedia://en/3918047) is rep-7, formed from the space-filling Gosper curve, and again forms a tiling.

By construction, any fractal defined by an [iterated function system](wikipedia://en/505225) of n contracting maps of the same ratio is rep-n.

## Infinite tiling {#Infinite_tiling}

Among regular polygons, only the triangle and square can be dissected into smaller equally sized copies of themselves. However, a regular [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) can be dissected into six equilateral triangles, each of which can be dissected into a regular hexagon and three more equilateral triangles. This is the basis for an infinite [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) of the hexagon with hexagons. The hexagon is therefore an irrep-[∞](wikipedia://en/1605389) or irrep-infinity irreptile.
