# Matroid girth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matroid girth"}

In [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory), a mathematical discipline, the **girth** of a matroid is the size of its smallest circuit or dependent set. The **cogirth** of a matroid is the girth of its [dual matroid](wikipedia://en/23515837). Matroid girth generalizes the notion of the shortest cycle in a graph, the edge connectivity of a graph, Hall sets in [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431), even sets in families of sets, and general position of point sets. It is hard to compute, but [fixed-parameter tractable](wikipedia://en/603026) for linear matroids when parameterized both by the [matroid rank](wikipedia://en/37123010) and the field size of a linear representation.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The "girth" terminology generalizes the use of [girth in graph theory](wikipedia://en/12013), meaning the length of the shortest cycle in a graph: the girth of a [graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714) is the same as the girth of its underlying graph.[^o4]

The girth of other classes of matroids also corresponds to important combinatorial problems. For instance, the girth of a co-graphic matroid (or the cogirth of a graphic matroid) equals the [edge connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph) of the underlying graph, the number of edges in a [minimum cut](wikipedia://en/3562453) of the graph.[^o4] The girth of a [transversal matroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversal_matroid) gives the cardinality of a minimum Hall set in a bipartite graph: this is a set of vertices on one side of the bipartition that does not form the set of endpoints of a [matching](wikipedia://en/581797) in the graph.[^o5]

Any set of points in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) gives rise to a real [linear matroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_matroid) by interpreting the [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) of the points as the [vectors](wikipedia://en/23218956) of a [matroid representation](wikipedia://en/37165960). The girth of the resulting matroid equals one plus the dimension of the space when the underlying set of point is in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736), and is smaller otherwise. Girths of real linear matroids also arise in [compressed sensing](wikipedia://en/11403316), where the same concept is referred to as the [spark](wikipedia://en/19270266) of a matrix.[^o6]

The girth of a [binary matroid](wikipedia://en/36848165) gives the cardinality of a minimum even set, a subcollection of a family of sets that includes an even number of copies of each set element.[^o5]

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Determining the girth of a [binary matroid](wikipedia://en/36848165) is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard).[^o8]

Additionally, determining the girth of a linear matroid given by a matrix representing the matroid is [W\[1\]-hard](wikipedia://en/603026) when parameterized by the girth or by the rank of the matroid, but fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by a combination of the rank and the size of the underlying [field](wikipedia://en/10603).[^o5]

For an arbitrary matroid, given by an [independence oracle](wikipedia://en/36878315), it is impossible to find the girth using a subexponential number of matroid queries.[^o9] Similarly, for a real linear matroid of rank $r$, with $n$ elements, described by an oracle that gives the [orientation](wikipedia://en/24338041) of any $r$-tuple of elements, it requires $\Omega(n^{r-1})$ oracle queries to determine the girth.[^o10]

Computations using a girth oracle (an oracle that reports the smallest dependent subset of a given set of elements) have also been considered.[^o11]
