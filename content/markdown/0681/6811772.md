# Poncelet point

*Image: Poncelet point.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poncelet point"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Poncelet point** of four given [points](wikipedia://en/593693) is defined as follows:

:::

Let $A, B, C, D$ be four points [in the plane](wikipedia://en/1508434) that do not form an [orthocentric system](wikipedia://en/161263) such that no three of them are [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear). The [nine-point circles](wikipedia://en/161243) of triangles $\triangle ABC, \triangle BCD, \triangle CDA, \triangle DAB$ meet at one point, the Poncelet point of the points $A, B, C, D$. (If $A, B, C, D$ do form an orthocentric system, then triangles $\triangle ABC, \triangle BCD, \triangle CDA, \triangle DAB$ all share the same nine-point circle, and the Poncelet point is undefined.)

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If $A, B, C, D$ do not lie on a circle, the Poncelet point of $A, B, C, D$ lies on the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of the [pedal triangle](wikipedia://en/152983) of $D$ with respect to triangle $\triangle ABC$ and lies on the other analogous circles. (If they do lie on a circle, then those pedal triangles will be lines; namely, the [Simson line](wikipedia://en/3307757) of $D$ with respect to triangle $\triangle ABC$, and the other analogous Simson lines. In that case, those lines still concur at the Poncelet point, which will also be the [anticenter](wikipedia://en/194131#Anticenter_and_collinearities) of the cyclic quadrilateral whose vertices are $A, B, C, D$.)

The Poncelet point of $A, B, C, D$ lies on the circle through the intersection of lines $AB$ and $CD$, the intersection of lines $AC$ and $BD$, and the intersection of lines $AD$ and $BC$ (assuming all these intersections exist).

The Poncelet point of $A, B, C, D$ is the center of the unique [rectangular hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052#Rectangular_hyperbola) through $A, B, C, D$. In the case that $A, B, C, D$ form an orthocentric system and the Poncelet point is undefined, there is instead a family of rectangular hyperbolas through $A, B, C, D$, and the common nine-point circle is the locus of their centers.[^o4]

:::
