# Uniqueness theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniqueness theorem"}

In mathematics, a **uniqueness theorem**, also called a **unicity theorem**, is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) asserting the uniqueness of an object satisfying certain conditions, or the equivalence of all objects satisfying the said conditions.[^o2] Examples of uniqueness theorems include:

:::

- [Cauchy's rigidity theorem](wikipedia://en/8317687) and [Alexandrov's uniqueness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrov's_uniqueness_theorem) for three-dimensional polyhedra.
- [Black hole uniqueness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole_uniqueness_theorem)
- [Cauchy–Kowalevski theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy–Kowalevski_theorem) is the main local [existence](wikipedia://en/251177) and uniqueness theorem for [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) associated with [Cauchy initial value problems](wikipedia://en/1725027).
- [Cauchy–Kowalevski–Kashiwara theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy–Kowalevski_theorem#Cauchy–Kowalevski–Kashiwara_theorem) is a wide generalization of the Cauchy–Kowalevski theorem for systems of linear partial differential equations with analytic coefficients.
- [Division theorem](wikipedia://en/679987#Statement_of_the_theorem), the uniqueness of quotient and remainder under Euclidean division.
- [Fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556), the uniqueness of prime factorization.
- [Holmgren's uniqueness theorem](wikipedia://en/25140914) for linear partial differential equations with real analytic coefficients.
- [Picard–Lindelöf theorem](wikipedia://en/666177), the uniqueness of solutions to first-order differential equations.
- [Thompson uniqueness theorem](wikipedia://en/29764807) in finite group theory.
- [Carlson's theorem](wikipedia://en/4065365), the uniqueness of certain [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) taking given values at the [natural integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_integers).
- [Uniqueness theorem for Poisson's equation](wikipedia://en/5230919).[^o3]
- [Electromagnetism uniqueness theorem](wikipedia://en/55016824) for the solution of Maxwell's equation.
- [Uniqueness case](wikipedia://en/29931063) in finite group theory.

The word *unique* is sometimes replaced by *[essentially unique](wikipedia://en/7918341)*, whenever one wants to stress that the uniqueness is only referred to the underlying structure, whereas the form may vary in all ways that do not affect the mathematical content.[^o2]

A uniqueness theorem (or its proof) is, at least within the mathematics of differential equations, often combined with an existence theorem (or its proof) to a combined existence and uniqueness theorem (e.g., existence and uniqueness of solution to first-order differential equations with boundary condition).[^o4]
