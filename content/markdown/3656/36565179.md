# Lattice (module)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lattice"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in the field of [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a **lattice** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) which, informally, provides a general framework for taking a sparse set of points in a larger space. Lattices generalize several more specific notions, including [integer lattices](wikipedia://en/469246) in [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), [orders](wikipedia://en/1114171) in [algebraic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822), and [fractional ideals](wikipedia://en/460700) in [integral domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_domains). Formally, a lattice is a kind of [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) that is [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *R* be an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) with [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) *K*, and let *V* be a vector space over *K* (and thus also an *R*-module). An *R*-[submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) *M* of a *V* is called a **lattice** if *M* is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) over *R*. It is called **full** if *V* = *K* · *M*, i.e. if *M* contains a *K*-basis of *V*.[^o4] Some authors require lattices to be full, but we do not adopt this convention in this article.[^o5]

Any finitely-generated [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/38400572) module *M* over *R* can be considered as a full *R*-lattice by taking as the ambient space $M \otimes_R K$, the [extension of scalars](wikipedia://en/41410139#Extension_of_scalars) of *M* to *K*. To avoid this ambiguity, lattices are usually studied in the context of a fixed ambient space.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The behavior of the base ring *R* of a lattice *M* strongly influences the behavior of *M*. If *R* is a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132), *M* is completely decomposable (with respect to a suitable basis) as a direct sum of fractional ideals. Every lattice over a Dedekind domain is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488).[^o8]

Lattices are well-behaved under [localization](wikipedia://en/252216) and [completion](wikipedia://en/9477975): A lattice *M* is equal to the intersection of all the localizations $M_{(\mathfrak{p})}$ of *M* at $\mathfrak{p}$. Further, two lattices are equal if and only if their localizations are equal at all primes. Over a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132), the local-global-dictionary is even more robust: any two full *R*-lattices are equal all but finitely many localizations, and for any choice[^o9] of $R_{(\mathfrak{p})}$-lattices $N_{(\mathfrak{p})}$ there exists an *R*-lattice *M* satisfying $M_{(\mathfrak{p})} = N_{(\mathfrak{p})}$. Over Dedekind domains a similar correspondence exists between *R*-lattices and collections of lattices $N_\mathfrak{p}$ over the completions of *R* with respect at primes $\mathfrak{p}$.[^o10]

A pair of lattices *M* and *N* over *R* admit a notion of [relative index](wikipedia://en/230581) analogous to that of integer lattices in $\mathbb{R}^n$. If *M* and *N* are projective (e.g. if *R* is a Dedekind domain), then *M* and *N* have trivial relative index if and only if *M = N*.[^o11]

:::

## Pure sublattices {#Pure_sublattices}

An *R*-submodule *N* of *M* that is itself a lattice is an *R*-pure sublattice if *M*/*N* is *R*-torsion-free. There is a one-to-one correspondence between *R*-pure sublattices *N* of *M* and *K*-[subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) *W* of *V*, given by[^o13]

$$N \mapsto W = K \cdot N ; \quad W \mapsto N = W \cap M. \,$$
