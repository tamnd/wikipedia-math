# Kolmogorov automorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kolmogorov automorphism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Kolmogorov automorphism**, ***K*-automorphism**, ***K*-shift** or ***K*-system** is an invertible, [measure-preserving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure-preserving) [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) defined on a [standard probability space](wikipedia://en/10406190) that obeys [Kolmogorov's zero–one law](wikipedia://en/191797).[^o2] All [Bernoulli automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_automorphism) are *K*-automorphisms (one says they have the ***K*-property**), but not vice versa. Many [ergodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic) [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) have been shown to have the *K*-property, although more recent research has shown that many of these are in fact Bernoulli automorphisms.

:::

Although the definition of the *K*-property seems reasonably general, it stands in sharp distinction to the Bernoulli automorphism. In particular, the [Ornstein isomorphism theorem](wikipedia://en/5443884) does not apply to *K*-systems, and so the [entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_entropy) is not sufficient to classify such systems – there exist uncountably many non-isomorphic *K*-systems with the same entropy. In essence, the collection of *K*-systems is large, messy and uncategorized; whereas the *B*-automorphisms are 'completely' described by [Ornstein theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornstein_theory).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $(X, \mathcal{B}, \mu)$ be a [standard probability space](wikipedia://en/10406190), and let $T$ be an invertible, [measure-preserving transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure-preserving_transformation). Then $T$ is called a *K*-automorphism, *K*-transform or *K*-shift, if there exists a sub-[sigma algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_algebra) $\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{B}$ such that the following three properties hold:

$$\mbox{(1) }\mathcal{K}\subset T\mathcal{K}$$

$$\mbox{(2) }\bigvee_{n=0}^\infty T^n \mathcal{K}=\mathcal{B}$$

$$\mbox{(3) }\bigcap_{n=0}^\infty T^{-n} \mathcal{K} = \{X,\varnothing\}$$

Here, the symbol $\vee$ is the [join of sigma algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_%28sigma_algebra%29), while $\cap$ is [set intersection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_intersection). The equality should be understood as holding [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688), that is, differing at most on a set of [measure zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_zero).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Assuming that the sigma algebra is not trivial, that is, if $\mathcal{B}\ne\{X,\varnothing\}$, then $\mathcal{K}\ne T\mathcal{K}.$ It follows that *K*-automorphisms are [strong mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_mixing).

All [Bernoulli automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_automorphism) are *K*-automorphisms, but not *vice versa*.

Kolmogorov automorphisms are precisely the natural extensions of exact endomorphisms,[^o7] i.e. mappings $T$ for which $\bigcap_{n=0}^\infty T^{-n} \mathcal{M}$ consists of measure-zero sets or their complements, where $\mathcal{M}$ is the sigma-algebra of measureable sets.

:::
