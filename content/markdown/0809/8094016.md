# Hollow matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hollow matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **hollow matrix** may refer to one of several related classes of [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859): a sparse matrix; a matrix with a large block of zeroes; or a matrix with diagonal entries all zero.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Sparse {#Sparse}

A *hollow matrix* may be one with "few" non-zero entries: that is, a [sparse matrix](wikipedia://en/341015).[^o5]

### Block of zeroes {#Block_of_zeroes}

A *hollow matrix* may be a square $n \times n$ matrix with an $r \times s$ block of zeroes where $r + s > n$.[^o7]

### Diagonal entries all zero {#Diagonal_entries_all_zero}

A *hollow matrix* may be a [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) whose [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251#Matrices) elements are all equal to zero.[^o9] That is, an $n \times n$ matrix $A = (a_{ij})$ is hollow if $a_{ij} = 0$ whenever $i = j$ (i.e. $a_{ii} = 0$ for all $i$). The most obvious example is the [real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) [skew-symmetric](wikipedia://en/174055) matrix. Other examples are the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of a finite [simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph), and a [distance matrix](wikipedia://en/831350) or [Euclidean distance matrix](wikipedia://en/8092698).

In other words, any square matrix that takes the form

$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & \ast & & \ast & \ast \\ \ast & 0 & & \ast & \ast \\ & & \ddots \\ \ast & \ast & & 0 & \ast \\ \ast & \ast & & \ast & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

is a hollow matrix, where the symbol $\ast$ denotes an arbitrary entry.

For example,

$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & 6 & \frac{1}{3} & 4 \\ 2 & 0 & 4 & 8 & 0 \\ 9 & 4 & 0 & 2 & 933 \\ 1 & 4 & 4 & 0 & 6 \\ 7 & 9 & 23 & 8 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

is a hollow matrix.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of a hollow matrix is zero.
- If $L$ represents a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) $L:V \to V$with respect to a fixed [basis](wikipedia://en/18420), then it maps each basis vector $\mathbf{e}$ into the [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) of the [span](wikipedia://en/56353) of $\mathbf{e}$. That is, $L(\langle e \rangle) \cap \langle e \rangle = \langle 0 \rangle$ where $\langle e \rangle = \{ \lambda e : \lambda \in F\}.$
- The [Gershgorin circle theorem](wikipedia://en/2470414) shows that the moduli of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of a hollow matrix are less or equal to the sum of the moduli of the non-diagonal row entries.

:::
