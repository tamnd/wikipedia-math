# Jeff Cheeger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jeff Cheeger"}

**Jeff Cheeger** (born December 1, 1943) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Silver Professor[^o3] at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). His main interest is [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and its connections with [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Cheeger graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) with a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1964. He graduated from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with an [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in 1966 and with a PhD in 1967. He is a Silver Professor at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), where he has worked since 1993.

He worked as a teaching assistant and research assistant at Princeton University from 1966 to 1967, a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) postdoctoral fellow and instructor from 1967 to 1968, an assistant professor from 1968 to 1969 at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), and an associate professor from 1969 to 1971 at [SUNY at Stony Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University). Cheeger was a professor at SUNY, Stony Brook from 1971 to 1985, a leading professor from 1985 to 1990, and a distinguished professor from 1990 to 1992.

Cheeger has also had a number of visiting positions in Brazil (1971), at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (1972, 1977, 1978, 1995), Harvard University (1972), the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) (1984–1985), and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (1985).

He has supervised at least 13 doctoral theses and three postdoctoral fellows. He has served as a member of several [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) committees and National Science Foundation panels.

Cheeger delivered invited addresses at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1974 and 1986.

He received the [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in 1984.[^o5] In 1998 Cheeger was elected a foreign member of the [Finnish Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o6]

Cheeger was elected a member of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1997. His election citation read:

> Cheeger has discovered many of the deepest results in Riemannian geometry, such as estimates for the spectrum of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, and the identity of the analytic and geometric definitions of torsion, and has led to the solution of problems in topology, graph theory, number theory, and Markov processes.[^o7]

He received the fourteenth [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) from the American Mathematical Society in 2001.[^o8]
