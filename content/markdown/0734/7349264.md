# Weak n-category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weak n-category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) in mathematics, a **weak *n*-category** is a generalization of the notion of [strict *n*-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict_n-category) where composition and identities are not strictly associative and unital, but only associative and unital [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [coherent equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_equivalence) or coherent isomorphism.[^o2] A weak 0-category is just a set, and a weak 1-category is an ordinarily category.[^o3] This generalisation only becomes noticeable at dimensions two and above where weak 2-, 3- and 4-categories are typically referred to as [bicategories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategory), [tricategories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricategory), and [tetracategories](wikipedia://en/7301396).

:::

## History {#History}

There is much work to determine what the coherence laws for weak *n*-categories should be. Weak *n*-categories have become the main object of study in [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013). There are basically two classes of theories: those in which the higher cells and higher compositions are realized algebraically (most remarkably [Michael Batanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Batanin)'s theory of weak higher categories) and those in which more topological models are used (e.g. a higher category as a [simplicial set](wikipedia://en/1027784) satisfying some universality properties).

In a terminology due to [John Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Baez) and James Dolan, a (*n*, *k*)-category is a weak *n*-category, such that all *h*-cells for *h* > *k* are invertible. Some of the formalism for (*n*, *k*)-categories are much simpler than those for general *n*-categories. In particular, several technically accessible formalisms of [(infinity, 1)-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28infinity,_1%29-category) are now known. Now the most popular such formalism centers on a notion of [quasi-category](wikipedia://en/28662219), other approaches include a properly understood theory of simplicially enriched categories and the approach via Segal categories; a class of examples of *stable* (infinity, 1)-categories can be modeled (in the case of characteristics zero) also via pretriangulated [A-infinity categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-infinity_category) of [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509). [Quillen model categories](wikipedia://en/2895304) are viewed as a [presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_of_an_∞-category) of an (infinity, 1)-category; however not all (infinity, 1)-categories can be presented via model categories.
