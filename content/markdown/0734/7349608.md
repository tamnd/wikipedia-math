# Dennis Sullivan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dennis Sullivan"}

**Dennis Parnell Sullivan** (born February 12, 1941) is an American mathematician known for his work in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). He holds the Albert Einstein Chair at the [Graduate Center of the City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Center_of_the_City_University_of_New_York) and is a [distinguished professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor) at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).

:::

Sullivan was awarded the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2010 and the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2022.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sullivan was born in [Port Huron, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Huron,_Michigan), on February 12, 1941.[^o5][^o6] His family moved to [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston) soon afterwards.[^o5][^o6]

He entered [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) to study [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) but switched his major to mathematics in his second year after encountering a particularly motivating mathematical theorem.[^o6][^o23] The change was prompted by a special case of the [uniformization theorem](wikipedia://en/288270), according to which, in his own words: \[A\]ny surface topologically like a balloon, and no matter what shape—a banana or the [statue of David by Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_%28Michelangelo%29)—could be placed on to a perfectly round sphere so that the stretching or squeezing required at each and every point is the same in all directions at each such point.[^o7]

He received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree from [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) in 1963.[^o6] He obtained his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1966 with his thesis, *Triangulating homotopy equivalences*, under the supervision of [William Browder](wikipedia://en/6646431).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Sullivan worked at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) on a [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO) Fellowship from 1966 to 1967.[^o13] He was a [Miller Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Research_Fellows) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) from 1967 to 1969 and then a Sloan Fellow at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1969 to 1973.[^o13] He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1967–1968, 1968–1970, and again in 1975.[^o10]

Sullivan was an associate professor at [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University) from 1973 to 1974, and then became a permanent professor at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) (IHÉS) in 1974.[^o13][^o11] In 1981, he became the Albert Einstein Chair in Science (Mathematics) at the Graduate Center of the City University of New York[^o12] and reduced his duties at the IHÉS to a half-time appointment.[^o5] He joined the mathematics faculty at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) in 1996[^o13] and left the IHÉS the following year.[^o13][^o11]

Sullivan was involved in the founding of the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388) and is a member of its board of trustees.[^o14]

## Research {#Research}

### Topology {#Topology}

#### Geometric topology {#Geometric_topology}

Along with Browder and his other students, Sullivan was an early adopter of [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115), particularly for classifying high-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).[^o6][^o23][^o5] His thesis work was focused on the *[Hauptvermutung](wikipedia://en/2180532)*.[^o5]

In an influential set of notes in 1970, Sullivan put forward the radical concept that, within [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), spaces could directly "be broken into boxes"[^o18] (or [*localized*](wikipedia://en/13727822)), a procedure hitherto applied to the algebraic constructs made from them.[^o23][^o19]

The [Sullivan conjecture](wikipedia://en/13400209), proved in its original form by [Haynes Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haynes_Miller), states that the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) *BG* of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) *G* is sufficiently different from any finite [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) *X*, that it maps to such an *X* only 'with difficulty'; in a more formal statement, the space of all mappings *BG* to *X*, as [pointed spaces](wikipedia://en/1236666) and given the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335), is [weakly contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_contractible).[^o20] Sullivan's conjecture was also first presented in his 1970 notes.[^o23][^o19][^o20]

Sullivan and [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) (independently) created [rational homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/13998981) in the late 1960s and 1970s.[^o21][^o22][^o23][^o24] It examines "rationalizations" of [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) with [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) and [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041) groups [tensored](wikipedia://en/2993836) with the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), ignoring [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330) elements and simplifying certain calculations.[^o24]

#### Kleinian groups {#Kleinian_groups}

Sullivan and [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) generalized [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972)' [density conjecture](wikipedia://en/31202113) from singly degenerate Kleinian surface groups to all [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652) in the late 1970s and early 1980s.[^o26][^o27] The conjecture states that every finitely generated Kleinian group is an algebraic limit of [geometrically finite](wikipedia://en/31212031) Kleinian groups, and was independently proven by Ohshika and Namazi–Souto in 2011 and 2012 respectively.[^o26][^o27]

#### Conformal and quasiconformal mappings {#Conformal_and_quasiconformal_mappings}

The [Connes–Donaldson–Sullivan–Teleman index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connes–Donaldson–Sullivan–Teleman_index_theorem) is an extension of the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752) to [quasiconformal](wikipedia://en/2749226) manifolds due to a joint paper by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305) and Sullivan in 1989 and a joint paper by [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340), Sullivan, and Nicolae Teleman in 1994.[^o29][^o30]

In 1987, Sullivan and [Burton Rodin](wikipedia://en/19417305) proved Thurston's conjecture about the approximation of the [Riemann map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_map) by [circle packings](wikipedia://en/16648043).[^o31]

#### String topology {#String_topology}

Sullivan and Moira Chas started the field of [string topology](wikipedia://en/28725806), which examines algebraic structures on the [homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) of [free loop spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_loop_space).[^o33][^o34] They developed the Chas–Sullivan product to give a partial singular homology analogue of the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) from [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology).[^o33][^o34] String topology has been used in multiple proposals to construct [topological quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/443757) in mathematical physics.[^o35]

### Dynamical systems {#Dynamical_systems}

In 1975, Sullivan and [Bill Parry](wikipedia://en/7618438) introduced the topological [Parry–Sullivan invariant](wikipedia://en/7740082) for flows in one-dimensional dynamical systems.[^o37][^o38]

In 1985, Sullivan proved the [no-wandering-domain theorem](wikipedia://en/9795423).[^o23] This result was described by mathematician Anthony Philips as leading to a "revival of holomorphic dynamics after 60 years of stagnation."[^o5]
