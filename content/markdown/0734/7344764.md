# Alan Weinstein

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alan Weinstein"}

**Alan David Weinstein** (born 17 June 1943) is a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), working in the field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and especially in [Poisson geometry](wikipedia://en/882902).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Weinstein was born in New York City.[^o4] After attending [Roslyn High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roslyn_High_School),[^o5] Weinstein obtained a bachelor's degree at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1964. His teachers included, among others, [James Munkres](wikipedia://en/7615776), [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891), [Irving Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Segal), and, for the first senior course of differential geometry, [Sigurður Helgason](wikipedia://en/23228672).[^o5] He received a PhD at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1967 under the direction of [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905). His dissertation was entitled "*The [cut locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_locus_%28Riemannian_manifold%29) and conjugate locus of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652)*".[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Weinstein worked then at MIT on 1967 (as [Moore instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_Instructor)) and at [Bonn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 1968/69. In 1969 he returned to Berkeley as assistant professor and from 1976 he is full professor. During 1975/76 he visited [IHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in Paris[^o5] and during 1978/79 he was visiting professor at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University). Weinstein was awarded in 1971 a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship)[^o8] and in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Guggenheim_Fellowships_awarded_in_1985) a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o9] In 1978 he was [invited speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Helsinki.[^o10] In [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences_members_%281953–1993%29#1992) he was elected Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences)[^o11] and in 2012 Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o12] In 2003 he was awarded an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Doctorate) from [Universiteit Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universiteit_Utrecht).[^o13][^o14]

## Research {#Research}

Weinstein's works cover many areas in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), including [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), [Lie groupoids](wikipedia://en/310923), [geometric mechanics](wikipedia://en/41499924) and [deformation quantization](wikipedia://en/4278245).[^o5][^o16]

Among his most important contributions, in 1971 he proved a [tubular neighbourhood theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weinstein's_tubular_neighbourhood_theorem) for [Lagrangians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_submanifold) in [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356).[^o17]

In 1974 he worked with [Jerrold Marsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerrold_E._Marsden) on the theory of reduction for mechanical systems with [symmetries](wikipedia://en/53741), introducing the famous [Marsden–Weinstein quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsden-Weinstein_quotient).[^o18]

In 1978 he formulated a celebrated [conjecture](wikipedia://en/4120135) on the existence of [periodic orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit),[^o19] which has been later proved in several particular cases and has led to many new developments in [symplectic](wikipedia://en/294298) and [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398).[^o20]

In 1981 he formulated a general principle, called *symplectic creed*, stating that "everything is a Lagrangian submanifold".[^o21] Such insight has been constantly quoted as the source of inspiration for many results in symplectic geometry.[^o5][^o16]

Building on the work of [André Lichnerowicz](wikipedia://en/2389188), in a 1983 foundational paper[^o22] Weinstein proved many results which laid the ground for the development of modern [Poisson geometry](wikipedia://en/882902). A further influential idea in this field was its introduction of [symplectic groupoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_groupoid).[^o23][^o24]

He is author of more than 50 research papers in peer-reviewed journals and he has supervised 35 PhD students.[^o6]
