# Moon Duchin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moon Duchin"}

**Moon Duchin** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works as a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at [the University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Chicago). Her mathematical research concerns [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), and [Teichmüller theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_theory).[^o4] She has done significant research on the mathematics of [redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistricting) and [gerrymandering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrymandering), and founded a research group, MGGG Redistricting Lab, to advance these mathematical studies and their nonpartisan application in the real world of [US politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_United_States).[^o5] She is also interested in the cultural studies, [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), and [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science).[^o4] Duchin was one of the core faculty members and served as director of the Science, Technology, and Society program at Tufts.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Duchin was born to [Faye Duchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faye_Duchin), an economist, and Micha Brym.[^o7] Duchin was given her first name, Moon, by parents "on the science-y fringes of the [hippie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie) classification".[^o8] She was given her mother's last name, and her sister was given her father's.[^o7] She grew up knowing from a young age that she wanted to become a mathematician.[^o8] As a student at [Stamford High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_High_School_%28Stamford,_Connecticut%29) in [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut), she completed the regular high school mathematics curriculum in her sophomore year, and continued to learn mathematics through independent study.[^o8] She was active in math and science camps and competitions, and did a summer research project in the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153) with [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999).[^o8]

Duchin studied at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) as an undergraduate, where she was also active in queer organizing,[^o9] and finished a [double major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_major) in mathematics and women's studies in 1998.[^o8][^o10] At the time, she was unsure how to combine the two majors into a single thesis, so she decided to write two separate ones.[^o11]

As a graduate student in mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), she continued [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism) activism by teaching [gender studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_studies) and pushing the university to add [gender-neutral bathrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unisex_public_toilet),[^o8][^o12] and was mentioned mockingly by name on the [Rush Limbaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_Limbaugh) show.[^o8] She completed her doctorate in 2005, under the supervision of [Alex Eskin](wikipedia://en/31883666). She was a postdoctoral researcher at the [University of California at Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Davis), and the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), before joining the Tufts faculty in 2011.[^o8][^o10]

## Work {#Work}

Duchin's mathematical research has focused on [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), and [Teichmüller theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_theory).[^o4] For example, one of her results is that, for a broad class of [locally flat surfaces](wikipedia://en/2878111), the geometry of the surface is entirely determined by the shortest length in each [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) class of [simple closed curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_closed_curve).[^o15] In 2022 Duchin appeared in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) documentary *[A Trip to Infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Trip_to_Infinity)*,[^o16] discussing the mathematical implications of [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824).

Duchin's expertise in geometry has led her to conduct research on the mathematics of [gerrymandering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrymandering). A key aspect of this research is the geometric notion of the [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness_measure_of_a_shape) of a given political district, a numerical measure that attempts to quantify how extensively gerrymandered it is.[^o17] “What courts have been looking for is one definition of compactness that they can understand, that we can compute, and that they can use as a kind of go-to standard”, she said in an interview with *[The Chronicle of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chronicle_of_Higher_Education)*.[^o18]

To help tackle the challenge of finding an agreed-upon standard, Duchin has developed a long-term, wide-ranging project on the mathematics of gerrymandering.[^o18] As a part of this project, she founded a summer program to train mathematicians to become expert witnesses in related legal cases.[^o19][^o20] In 2016, she founded the [Metric Geometry and Gerrymandering Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_Geometry_and_Gerrymandering_Group) (MGGG) which is a nonpartisan research group that coordinates and publicizes research on geometry, computing, and their application to the [redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistricting) process in the US.[^o21][^o11]

In 2018-2019 she took a leave of absence from Tufts, and was a Fellow at the [Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) at Harvard University. Her research focus was "Political Geometry: The Mathematics of Redistricting".[^o22] In 2018, Governor of [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania) [Tom Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wolf) enlisted Duchin to help him evaluate newly drawn redistricting maps for fairness.[^o23] This happened as a consequence of the [Supreme Court of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Pennsylvania) decision which declared the state's 2011 US congressional districting map to be unconstitutional.[^o24] Duchin prepared a report published on February 15, 2018.[^o25][^o26]

In 2022, a panel of judges threw out Alabama's soon-to-be-used congressional maps, citing the fact that the percentage of black people in the state had risen to about a quarter of the population. To draw some new, fairer maps, they turned to Duchin, who came up with 4 nearly-similar maps that would put the Black and Democratic-leaning cities of [Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama) and [Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery,_Alabama) together, therefore complementing the one Black and blue-leaning district in the state with a second one.[^o27]

Duchin was hired by [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 2024 as part of the [Brooks School of Public Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University_Brooks_School_of_Public_Policy) and [College of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University_College_of_Arts_and_Sciences) mathematics department.[^o28] As of 2026, she is a professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o29]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2016 Duchin was named as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to geometric group theory and Teichmüller theory, and for service to the mathematical community".[^o31] She was also a [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) Distinguished Lecturer for that year, speaking on the mathematics of [voting systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_system).[^o32] In 2018 she was awarded a [Guggenheim fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_fellowship).[^o33]
