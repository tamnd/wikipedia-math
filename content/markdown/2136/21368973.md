# Hahn series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hahn series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Hahn series** (sometimes also known as **Hahn–Mal'cev–Neumann series**) are a type of [formal infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_series). They are a generalization of [Puiseux series](wikipedia://en/2604429) (themselves a generalization of [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012)) and were first introduced by [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212) in 1907[^o2] (and then further generalized by [Anatoly Maltsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Maltsev) and [Bernhard Neumann](wikipedia://en/5032588) to a non-commutative setting). They allow for arbitrary exponents of the [indeterminate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_%28variable%29) so long as the set supporting them forms a [well-ordered](wikipedia://en/33456) subset of the [value group](wikipedia://en/347049) (typically $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{R}$). Hahn series were first introduced, as groups, in the course of the [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the [Hahn embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/1075596) and then studied by him in relation to [Hilbert's second problem](wikipedia://en/152759).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

The [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of Hahn series $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$ (in the indeterminate $T$) over a field $K$ and with value group $\Gamma$ (an ordered group) is the set of formal expressions of the form

$$f = \sum_{e\in\Gamma} c_e T^e$$

with $c_e \in K$ such that the support $\operatorname{supp} f:=\{ e\in \Gamma : c_e \neq 0 \}$ of *f* is [well-ordered](wikipedia://en/33456). The sum and product of

$f = \sum_{e\in\Gamma} c_e T^e$ and $g = \sum_{e\in\Gamma} d_e T^e$

are given by

$$f+g = \sum_{e\in\Gamma} (c_e+d_e) T^e$$

and

$$fg = \sum_{e\in\Gamma} \left(\sum_{e'+e''=e} c_{e'} d_{e''}\right) T^e$$

(in the latter, the sum $\sum_{e'+e''=e}$ over values $(e',e'')$ such that $c_{e'} \neq 0$, $d_{e''} \neq 0$ and $e'+e''=e$ is finite because a well-ordered set cannot contain an infinite decreasing sequence).[^o5]

For example, $T^{-1/p} + T^{-1/p^2} + T^{-1/p^3} + \cdots$ is a Hahn series (over any field) because the set of [rationals](wikipedia://en/19727024)

$$\left\{-\frac{1}{p}, -\frac{1}{p^2}, -\frac{1}{p^3}, \ldots\right\}$$

is well-ordered; it is not a [Puiseux series](wikipedia://en/2604429) because the denominators in the exponents are unbounded. (And if the base field *K* has [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*, then this Hahn series satisfies the equation $X^p-X = T^{-1}$ so it is algebraic over $K(T)$.)

## Properties {#Properties}

### Properties of the valued field {#Properties_of_the_valued_field}

The valuation $v(f)$ of a non-zero Hahn series

$$f = \sum_{e\in\Gamma} c_e T^e$$

is defined as the smallest $e \in \Gamma$ such that $c_e \neq 0$ (in other words, the smallest element of the support of $f$): this makes $K[[T^\Gamma]]$ into a [spherically complete](wikipedia://en/33175921) [valued field](wikipedia://en/347049) with value group $\Gamma$ and residue field $K$ (justifying *a posteriori* the terminology). In fact, if $K$ has characteristic zero, then $(K[[T^{\Gamma}]],v)$ is [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) (non-unique) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) the only spherically complete valued field with residue field $K$ and value group $\Gamma$.[^o9] The valuation $v$ defines a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) on $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$. If $\Gamma \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, then $v$ corresponds to an [ultrametric](wikipedia://en/333996) absolute value $|f| = \exp(-v(f))$, with respect to which $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$ is a [complete metric space](wikipedia://en/7832). However, unlike in the case of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) or Puiseux series, the formal sums used in defining the elements of the field do *not* converge: in the case of $T^{-1/p} + T^{-1/p^2} + T^{-1/p^3} + \cdots$ for example, the absolute values of the terms tend to 1 (because their valuations tend to 0), so the series is not convergent (such series are sometimes known as "pseudo-convergent"[^o10]).

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Algebraic properties"}

### Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

If $K$ is [algebraically closed](wikipedia://en/1018) (but not necessarily of characteristic zero) and $\Gamma$ is [divisible](wikipedia://en/472147), then $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$ is algebraically closed.[^o13] Thus, the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of $K((T))$ is contained in $\overline{K}[[T^{\mathbb{Q}}]]$, where $\overline{K}$ is the algebraic closure of $K$ (when $K$ is of characteristic zero, it is exactly the field of [Puiseux series](wikipedia://en/2604429)): in fact, it is possible to give a somewhat analogous description of the algebraic closure of $K((T))$ in positive characteristic as a subset of $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$.[^o14]

If $K$ is an [ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430) then $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$ is totally ordered by making the indeterminate $T$ infinitesimal (greater than 0 but less than any positive element of $K$) or, equivalently, by using the [lexicographic order](wikipedia://en/567667) on the coefficients of the series. If $K$ is [real-closed](wikipedia://en/1066093) and $\Gamma$ is divisible then $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$ is itself real-closed.[^o15] This fact can be used to analyse (or even construct) the field of [surreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51432) (which is isomorphic, as an ordered field, to the field of Hahn series with [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) coefficients and value group the surreal numbers themselves[^o16]).

If *κ* is an infinite [regular cardinal](wikipedia://en/374128), one can consider the subset of $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$ consisting of series whose support set $\{ e\in \Gamma : c_e \neq 0 \}$ has [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) (strictly) less than *κ*: it turns out that this is also a field, with much the same algebraic closedness properties as the full $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$: e.g., it is algebraically closed or real closed when $K$ is so and $\Gamma$ is divisible.[^o17]

## Summable families {#Summable_families}

### Summable families {#Summable_families}

One can define a notion of summable families in $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$. If $I$ is a set and $(f_i)_{i \in I}$ is a family of Hahn series $f_i \in K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$, then we say that $(f_i)_{i \in I}$ is summable if the set $\bigcup \limits_{i \in I} \operatorname{supp} f_i \subset \Gamma$ is well-ordered, and each set $\{i \in I \mid e \in \operatorname{supp} f_i\}$ for $e \in \Gamma$ is finite.

We may then define the sum $\sum \limits_{i \in I} f_i$ as the Hahn series

$$\sum_{i \in I} f_i := \sum \limits_{e \in \Gamma} \left(\sum_{i \in I} f_i(e)\right) T^e.$$

If $(f_i)_{i \in I},(g_i)_{i \in I}$ are summable, then so are the families $(f_i+g_i)_{i \in I},(f_i g_j)_{(i,j) \in I \times I}$, and we have[^o20]

$$\sum_{i \in I} f_i+g_i=\sum_{i \in I} f_i+\sum_{i \in I} g_i$$

and

$$\sum_{(i,j) \in I \times I} f_i g_j = \left(\sum_{i \in I} f_i\right)\left(\sum_{i \in I} g_i\right).$$

This notion of summable family does not correspond to the notion of convergence in the valuation topology on $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$. For instance, in $\mathbb{Q}\left[\left[T^\mathbb{Q}\right]\right]$, the family $(T^{\frac{n}{n+1}}+T^{n+1})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is summable but the sequence $\big(\sum \limits_{k \leq n} T^{\frac{k}{k+1}}+T^{k+1}\big)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ does not converge.

### Evaluating analytic functions {#Evaluating_analytic_functions}

Let $a \in \mathbb{R}$ and let $\mathcal{A}_a$ denote the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of real-valued [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) which are [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) on a [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29) of $a$.

If $K$ contains $\mathbb{R}$, then we can evaluate every element $f$ of $\mathcal{A}_a$ at every element of $K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$ of the form $a+\varepsilon$, where the valuation of $\varepsilon$ is strictly positive. Indeed, the family $\bigg(\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}\varepsilon^n\bigg)_{\!n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is always summable,[^o22] so we can define $f(a+\varepsilon):=\sum \limits_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}\varepsilon^n$. This defines a ring homomorphism $\mathcal{A}_a \longrightarrow K\left[\left[T^\Gamma\right]\right]$.

## Hahn–Witt series {#Hahn–Witt_series}

The construction of Hahn series can be combined with [Witt vectors](wikipedia://en/5573710) (at least over a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088)) to form **twisted Hahn series** or **Hahn–Witt series**:[^o24] for example, over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) *K* of characteristic *p* (or their algebraic closure), the field of Hahn–Witt series with value group *Γ* (containing the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563)) would be the set of formal sums $\sum_{e\in\Gamma} c_e p^e$ where now $c_e$ are [Teichmüller representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_representative) (of the elements of *K*) which are multiplied and added in the same way as in the case of ordinary Witt vectors (which is obtained when *Γ* is the group of integers). When *Γ* is the group of rationals or reals and *K* is the algebraic closure of the finite field with *p* elements, this construction gives a (ultra)metrically complete algebraically closed field containing the [*p*-adics](wikipedia://en/51423), hence a more or less explicit description of the field $\mathbb{C}_p$ or its spherical completion.[^o25]

::: {#o27 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The field $K((T))$ of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) over $K$ can be described as $K[[T^\mathbb Z]]$.
- The field of [surreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51432) can be regarded as a field of Hahn series with real coefficients and value group the surreal numbers themselves.[^o28]
- The [Levi-Civita field](wikipedia://en/22105661) can be regarded as a [subfield](wikipedia://en/11634) of $\mathbb{R}[[T^\mathbb{Q}]]$, with the additional imposition that the coefficients be a [left-finite set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-finite_set): the set of coefficients less than a given coefficient $c_q$ is finite.
- The field of [transseries](wikipedia://en/58151408) $\mathbb{T}$ is a directed union of Hahn fields (and is an extension of the Levi-Civita field). The construction of $\mathbb{T}$ resembles (but is not literally) $T_0 = \mathbb{R}$, $T_{n+1} = \mathbb{R}\left[\left[\varepsilon^{T_n}\right]\right]$.

:::
