# Eugène Fabry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eugène Fabry"}

**Charles Eugène Fabry** (; 16 October 1856 – 6 October 1944) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Fabry is best known for studying the [singularities](wikipedia://en/54240) of [analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_functions), including proving the [Fabry gap theorem](wikipedia://en/39697327).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Eugène Fabry, born in [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille), was the second of five sons in his family. His brothers included physicist [Charles Fabry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Fabry) and astronomer [Louis Fabry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Fabry).[^o7]

He became professor of analysis at [Aix-Marseille University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University) and the [University of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montpellier), and a corresponding member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o7]

He was the 1930 recipient of the [Prix Francoeur](wikipedia://en/57185095) of the French Academy of Sciences, "for his work on the singularities of analytical functions".[^o8]
