# Rees factor semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rees factor semigroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory), a **Rees factor semigroup** (also called **Rees quotient semigroup** or just **Rees factor**), named after [David Rees](wikipedia://en/15398568), is a certain [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) constructed using a semigroup and an [ideal of the semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_of_a_semigroup).

:::

Let *S* be a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) and *I* be an ideal of *S*. Using *S* and *I* one can construct a new semigroup by collapsing *I* into a single element while the elements of *S* outside of *I* retain their identity. The new semigroup obtained in this way is called the **Rees factor semigroup of *S* modulo *I*** and is denoted by *S*/*I*.

The concept of Rees factor semigroup was introduced by [David Rees](wikipedia://en/15398568) in 1940.[^o2][^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $I$ of a semigroup $S$ is called an *ideal* of $S$ if both $SI$ and $IS$ are subsets of $I$ (where $SI = \{sx \mid s \in S \text{ and } x \in I\}$, and similarly for $IS$). Let $I$ be an ideal of a semigroup $S$. The relation $\rho$ in $S$ defined by

*x* ρ *y* ⇔ either *x* = *y* or both *x* and *y* are in *I*

is an equivalence relation in $S$. The equivalence classes under $\rho$ are the singleton sets $\{x\}$ with $x$ not in $I$ and the set $I$. Since $I$ is an ideal of $S$, the relation $\rho$ is a [congruence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_congruence) on $S$.[^o6] The [quotient semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_semigroup) $S/{\rho}$ is, by definition, the *Rees factor semigroup* of $S$ modulo $I$. For notational convenience the semigroup $S/\rho$ is also denoted as $S/I$. The Rees factor semigroup[^o7] has underlying set $(S \setminus I) \cup \{0\}$, where $0$ is a new element and the product (here denoted by $*$) is defined by

$$s * t = \begin{cases} st & \text{if } s, t, st \in S \setminus I \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$

The congruence $\rho$ on $S$ as defined above is called the *Rees congruence* on $S$ modulo $I$.

:::

## Example {#Example}

Consider the semigroup *S* = { *a*, *b*, *c*, *d*, *e* } with the binary operation defined by the following Cayley table:

::: {#o10 .table cue=table}

| · | *a* | *b* | *c* | *d* | *e* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ***a*** | *a* | *a* | *a* | *d* | *d* |
| ***b*** | *a* | *b* | *c* | *d* | *d* |
| ***c*** | *a* | *c* | *b* | *d* | *d* |
| ***d*** | *d* | *d* | *d* | *a* | *a* |
| ***e*** | *d* | *e* | *e* | *a* | *a* |

:::

Let *I* = { *a*, *d* } which is a subset of *S*. Since

*SI* = { *aa*, *ba*, *ca*, *da*, *ea*, *ad*, *bd*, *cd*, *dd*, *ed* } = { *a*, *d* } ⊆ *I*

*IS* = { *aa*, *da*, *ab*, *db*, *ac*, *dc*, *ad*, *dd*, *ae*, *de* } = { *a*, *d* } ⊆ *I*

the set *I* is an ideal of *S*. The Rees factor semigroup of *S* modulo *I* is the set *S*/*I* = { *b*, *c*, *e*, *I* } with the binary operation defined by the following Cayley table:

::: {#o11 .table cue=table}

| · | *b* | *c* | *e* | *I* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ***b*** | *b* | *c* | *I* | *I* |
| ***c*** | *c* | *b* | *I* | *I* |
| ***e*** | *e* | *e* | *I* | *I* |
| ***I*** | *I* | *I* | *I* | *I* |

:::

:::

## Ideal extension {#Ideal_extension}

A semigroup *S* is called an ideal extension of a semigroup *A* by a semigroup *B* if *A* is an ideal of *S* and the Rees factor semigroup *S*/*A* is isomorphic to *B*. [^o13]

Some of the cases that have been studied extensively include: ideal extensions of [completely simple semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_simple_semigroup), of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) by a [completely 0-simple semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_0-simple_semigroup), of a [commutative semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_semigroup) with [cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancellation_semigroup) by a group with added zero. In general, the problem of describing all ideal extensions of a semigroup is still open.
