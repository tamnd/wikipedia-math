# Reversible diffusion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reversible diffusion"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **reversible diffusion** is a specific example of a [reversible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversible_dynamics) [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895). Reversible diffusions have an elegant [characterization](wikipedia://en/780886) due to the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Andrey Nikolaevich Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Nikolaevich_Kolmogorov).

:::

## Kolmogorov's characterization of reversible diffusions {#Kolmogorov's_characterization_of_reversible_diffusions}

Let *B* denote a *d*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) standard [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436); let *b* : **R**<sup>*d*</sup> → **R**<sup>*d*</sup> be a [Lipschitz continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_continuous) [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641). Let *X* : \[0, +∞) × Ω → **R**<sup>*d*</sup> be an [Itō diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion) defined on a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) (Ω, Σ, **P**) and solving the Itō [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454)

$$\mathrm{d} X_{t} = b(X_{t}) \, \mathrm{d} t + \mathrm{d} B_{t}$$

with square-integrable initial condition, i.e. *X*<sub>0</sub> ∈ *L*<sup>2</sup>(Ω, Σ, **P**; **R**<sup>*d*</sup>). Then the following are equivalent:

- The process *X* is reversible with [stationary distribution](wikipedia://en/2524727) *μ* on **R**<sup>*d*</sup>.
- There exists a [scalar potential](wikipedia://en/461654) Φ : **R**<sup>*d*</sup> → **R** such that *b* = −∇Φ, *μ* has [Radon–Nikodym derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Nikodym_derivative) $$\frac{\mathrm{d} \mu (x)}{\mathrm{d} x} = \exp \left( - 2 \Phi (x) \right)$$ and $$\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}} \exp \left( - 2 \Phi (x) \right) \, \mathrm{d} x = 1.$$

(Of course, the condition that *b* be the negative of the [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) of Φ only determines Φ [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) an additive constant; this constant may be chosen so that exp(−2Φ(·)) is a [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) with integral 1.)
