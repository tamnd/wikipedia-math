# Eilenberg–Niven theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eilenberg–Niven theorem"}

The **Eilenberg–Niven theorem** is a theorem that generalizes the [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414) to quaternionic [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000); that is, polynomials with [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) coefficients and variables. It is due to [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501) and [Ivan M. Niven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_M._Niven).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let

$$P(x) = a_0 x a_1 x \cdots x a_n + \varphi(x),$$

where *x*, *a*<sub>*0*</sub>, *a*<sub>*1*</sub>, ... , *a*<sub>*n*</sub> are non-zero quaternions and *φ*(*x*) is a finite sum of monomials similar to the first term but with degree less than *n*. Then *P*(*x*) = 0 has at least one solution.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

If permitting multiple monomials with the highest degree, then the theorem does not hold: for a counterexample, *P*(*x*) = *x* + **i***x***i** + 1 = 0 has no solutions.

The Eilenberg–Niven theorem can also be generalized to [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436): all octonionic polynomials with a unique monomial of highest degree have at least one solution, independent of the order of the parentheses (the octonions are a [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/6533841)).[^o7][^o8] Different from quaternions, however, the monic and non-monic octonionic polynomials do not have always the same set of zeros.[^o9]

:::
