# Jarrow–Turnbull model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jarrow–Turnbull model"}

The **Jarrow–Turnbull model** is a widely used "reduced-form" [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk) model. It was published in 1995 by [Robert A. Jarrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Jarrow) and [Stuart Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Turnbull_%28economist%29).[^o2] Under the model, which returns the corporate's [probability of default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_of_default), [bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy) is modeled as a statistical process. The model extends the reduced-form model of Merton (1976) [^o3] to a random interest rates framework.

:::

Reduced-form models are an approach to credit risk modeling that contrasts sharply with "structural credit models", the best known of which is the [Merton model](wikipedia://en/58994258) of 1974. Reduced-form models focus on modeling the probability of default as a statistical process, whereas structural-models inhere a microeconomic model of the firm's [capital structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_structure), deriving the (single-period) probability of default from the random variation in the (unobservable) value of the firm's assets.[^o4] Large financial institutions [employ default models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_risk_management#Investment_management) of both the structural and reduced-form types.
