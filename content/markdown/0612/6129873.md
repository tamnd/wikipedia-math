# Schouten tensor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schouten tensor"}

In [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) the **Schouten tensor** is a second-order [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) introduced by [Jan Arnoldus Schouten](wikipedia://en/2862062) defined for *n* ≥ 3 by:

:::

$$P=\frac{1}{n - 2} \left(\mathrm{Ric} -\frac{ R}{2 (n-1)} g\right)\, \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{Ric}=(n-2) P + J g \, ,$$

where Ric is the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) (defined by contracting the first and third indices of the Riemann tensor), *R* is the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622), *g* is the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric), $J=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}R$ is the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of *P* and *n* is the dimension of the manifold.

The [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848) equals the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) minus the [Kulkarni&ndash;Nomizu product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulkarni&ndash;Nomizu_product) of the Schouten tensor with the metric. In an index notation

$$R_{ijkl}=W_{ijkl}+g_{ik} P_{jl}-g_{jk} P_{il}-g_{il} P_{jk}+g_{jl} P_{ik}\, .$$

The Schouten tensor often appears in [conformal geometry](wikipedia://en/295907) because of its relatively simple conformal transformation law

$$g_{ij}\mapsto \Omega^2 g_{ij} \Rightarrow P_{ij}\mapsto P_{ij}-\nabla_i \Upsilon_j + \Upsilon_i \Upsilon_j -\frac12 \Upsilon_k \Upsilon^k g_{ij}\, ,$$

where $\Upsilon_i := \Omega^{-1} \partial_i \Omega\, .$

Up to normalization, the curl of the Schouten tensor is the [Cotton tensor](wikipedia://en/1074140).
