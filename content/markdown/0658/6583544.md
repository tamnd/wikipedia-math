# Progressively measurable process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Progressively measurable process"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **progressive measurability** is a property in the theory of [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes). A progressively measurable process, while defined quite technically, is important because it implies the [stopped process](wikipedia://en/7308284) is [measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable). Being progressively measurable is a strictly stronger property than the notion of being an [adapted process](wikipedia://en/6583420).[^o2] Progressively measurable processes are important in the theory of [Itô integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let

- $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325);
- $(\mathbb{X}, \mathcal{A})$ be a [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333), the *state space*;
- $\{ \mathcal{F}_{t} \mid t \geq 0 \}$ be a [filtration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtration_%28abstract_algebra%29) of the [sigma algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_algebra) $\mathcal{F}$;
- $X : [0, \infty) \times \Omega \to \mathbb{X}$ be a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) (the index set could be $[0, T]$ or $\mathbb{N}_{0}$ instead of $[0, \infty)$);
- $\mathrm{Borel}([0, t])$ be the [Borel sigma algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_sigma_algebra) on $[0,t]$.

The process $X$ is said to be **progressively measurable**[^o5] (or simply **progressive**) if, for every time $t$, the map $[0, t] \times \Omega \to \mathbb{X}$ defined by $(s, \omega) \mapsto X_{s} (\omega)$ is $\mathrm{Borel}([0, t]) \otimes \mathcal{F}_{t}$-[measurable](wikipedia://en/44055). This implies that $X$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t}$-adapted.[^o2]

A subset $P \subseteq [0, \infty) \times \Omega$ is said to be **progressively measurable** if the process $X_{s} (\omega) := \chi_{P} (s, \omega)$ is progressively measurable in the sense defined above, where $\chi_{P}$ is the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of $P$. The set of all such subsets $P$ form a sigma algebra on $[0, \infty) \times \Omega$, denoted by $\mathrm{Prog}$, and a process $X$ is progressively measurable in the sense of the previous paragraph if, and only if, it is $\mathrm{Prog}$-measurable.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- It can be shown[^o2] that $L^2 (B)$, the space of stochastic processes $X : [0, T] \times \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^n$ for which the [Itô integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral)

$$\int_0^T X_t \, \mathrm{d} B_t$$

with respect to [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) $B$ is defined, is the set of [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of $\mathrm{Prog}$-measurable processes in $L^2 ([0, T] \times \Omega; \mathbb{R}^n)$.

- Every adapted process with left- or [right-continuous](wikipedia://en/6122#Directional_and_semi-continuity) paths is progressively measurable. Consequently, every adapted process with [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839) paths is progressively measurable.[^o2]
- Every measurable and adapted process has a progressively measurable [modification](wikipedia://en/47895#Modification).[^o2]

:::
