# Pompeiu problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pompeiu problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Pompeiu problem** is a conjecture in [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336), named for [Dimitrie Pompeiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Pompeiu), who posed the problem in 1929.[^o2] It is stated as follows. Suppose *f* is a nonzero [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) defined on a Euclidean space, and *K* is a [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) [Lipschitz domain](wikipedia://en/7820612), so that the [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) of *f* vanishes on every [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) copy of *K*. Then the domain is a [ball](wikipedia://en/160556).[^o3]

:::

## Schiffer's conjecture {#Schiffer's_conjecture}

A closely related and highly significant formulation is Schiffer's conjecture, named after the mathematician [Menahem Max Schiffer](wikipedia://en/48545257). While the Pompeiu problem is rooted in integral geometry, Schiffer's conjecture is framed in the language of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).

Schiffer's conjecture proposes that if a bounded, [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) domain $\Omega$ with a sufficiently smooth boundary $\partial \Omega$ admits a non-trivial solution $u$ to the following overdetermined [boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/436779):

$$\begin{cases}\Delta u + \lambda u = 0 \quad &\text{ in }\Omega \\ \frac{\partial u}{\partial n} = 0, \quad &\text{ on } \partial \Omega \\ u = 1, \quad &\text{ on } \partial \Omega \end{cases}$$

for some eigenvalue $\lambda > 0$ and some constant, then the domain $\Omega$ must be a ball. Balls always admit solutions to such overdetermined value problem. On a ball, one can pick $u$ to be a radially symmetric Neumann eigenfunction of the Laplacian, which will satisfy the first two equations above. Since moreover is radially symmetric, $u$ is constant at $\partial \Omega$, so rescaling the Neumann eigenfunction, one can ensure the third equation as well.

### Equivalence to the Pompeiu problem {#Equivalence_to_the_Pompeiu_problem}

The deep connection between the two problems was brought to light by Williams (1976). By utilizing the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) and techniques from [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), it was proven that Schiffer's conjecture is mathematically equivalent to the Pompeiu problem for the case of $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ smooth and contractible.

:::

## Counterexample {#Counterexample}

On August 3, 2026, Matthew Colbrook & George Stepaniants published a preprint establishing a counterexample in two dimensions to the Pompeiu and Schiffer problems, closing the conjecture.[^o9]

Subsequently, on August 5, 2026, Gonzalo Cao-Labora and Jaume de Dios Pont published a preprint providing an independent construction and a computer-verified proof in [Lean 4](wikipedia://en/62889984), developed with assistance from GPT-5.6 and Claude models.[^o10] The formal proof verifies the Pompeiu problem challenge specification formalized in the Google DeepMind Formal Conjectures archive.[^o11]

:::
