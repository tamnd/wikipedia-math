# Padmakar–Ivan index

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Padmakar–Ivan index"}

In [chemical graph theory](wikipedia://en/14222708), the **Padmakar–Ivan (PI) index** is a [topological index](wikipedia://en/14221614) of a [molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule), used in [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry). The Padmakar–Ivan index is a generalization introduced by [Padmakar V. Khadikar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmakar_V._Khadikar) and [Iván Gutman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iván_Gutman)[^o2] of the concept of the [Wiener index](wikipedia://en/14223173), introduced by [Harry Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Wiener). The Padmakar–Ivan index of a graph *G* is the sum over all edges *uv* of *G* of number of edges which are not equidistant from *u* and *v*. Let *G* be a graph and *e* = *uv* an edge of *G*. Here $n_{eu}(e\mid G)$ denotes the number of edges lying closer to the vertex *u* than the vertex *v*, and $n_{ev}(e\mid G)$ is the number of edges lying closer to the vertex *v* than the vertex *u*. The Padmakar–Ivan index of a graph *G* is defined as

:::

$$\operatorname{PI}(G)=\sum_{e\in E(G)}[n_{eu}(e\mid G) + n_{ev}(e\mid G)]$$

The PI index is very important in the study of [quantitative structure–activity relationship](wikipedia://en/826669) for the classification models used in the [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical), [biological sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sciences), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), and [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Dendrimer Nanostar.png.*

The PI index of [Dendrimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrimer) Nanostar of the following figure can be calculated by[^o5]

$$\operatorname{PI}(G_n) = 441\cdot4^n - 639\cdot2^n + 232, \quad n \geq 0.$$

The [double graph](wikipedia://en/81397754) of a graph $G$, denoted $\mathcal{D}[G]$, has a known index in relation to $G$ itself:[^o6]

$$\operatorname{PI}(\mathcal{D}[G]) = 8\operatorname{PI}(G)$$
