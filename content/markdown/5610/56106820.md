# Wilhelm Gross

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelm Gross"}

**Wilhelm Gross** (24 March 1886, [Molln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molln) – 22 October 1918, Vienna) was an Austrian mathematician, known for the Gross star theorem.[^o7][^o2]

:::

Wilhelm Gross graduated from the Gymnasium in [Linz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linz) and then studied from 1905 to 1910 at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), where he received his Ph.D. ([*Promotion*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_%28Germany%29)) on 20 May 1910 with [Wilhelm Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921) as thesis advisor. In October 1910 Gross passed his teaching qualification examination in mathematics and physics. After a three-semester stay in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) during the years 1910–1912, he became in 1912 an assistant and from 1913 a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at the University of Vienna. In the year 1918 he was promoted there to professor extraordinarius. In the same year he was awarded the [Richard Lieben Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieben_Prize#Richard_Lieben_Prize) for his research on the calculus of variations, but he died of influenza in the [1918-1920 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_flu_pandemic).[^o3]

Gross did research on [function theory](wikipedia://en/5759), [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762). In function theory he is known for his investigations of singularities of meromorphic functions on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181), in particular, the Gross star theorem.[^o4][^o2]

## Gross star theorem {#Gross_star_theorem}

- Hypothesis: Let f be a [meromorphic function](wikipedia://en/80857) which is the ratio of two [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372). Suppose that z is a complex number which is not a singular point of f. Define w = f(z). Consider the [germ](wikipedia://en/352714) Φ<sub>z</sub> of the inverse of f, such that Φ<sub>z</sub>(w) = z.
- Conclusion: Then the set { e<sup>iθ</sup> : 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2 π and Φ<sub>z</sub> has an analytic continuation along the ray { w + r e<sup>iθ</sup> : 0 ≤ r \< ∞ } } is equal to the unit circle, except for a set with [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) zero.[^o7]

:::
