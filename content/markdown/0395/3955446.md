# Monoid (category theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monoid"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **monoid** (or **monoid object**, or **internal monoid**, or **algebra**) $(M, \mu, \eta)$ in a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) $(\mathcal C,\otimes,I)$ is an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $M$ together with two [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891)

:::

- $\mu\colon M\otimes M\to M$ called *multiplication*,
- $\eta\colon I\to M$ called *unit*,

such that the pentagon [diagram](wikipedia://en/7651625)

*Image: Monoid multiplication.svg.*

and the unitor diagram

*Image: Monoid unit svg.svg.*

[commute](wikipedia://en/138484). In the above notation, $1$ is the [identity morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_morphism) of $M$, $I$ is the unit element and $\alpha,\lambda$ and $\rho$ are respectively the associator, the left unitor and the right unitor of the monoidal category $\mathcal C$.

Dually, a **comonoid** in a monoidal category $\mathcal C$ is a monoid in the [dual category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_category) $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}$.

Suppose that the monoidal category $\mathcal C$ has a [braiding](wikipedia://en/1470466) $\gamma$. A monoid $M$ in $\mathcal C$ is **commutative** when $\mu\circ\gamma=\mu$.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A monoid object in **[Set](wikipedia://en/26829)**, the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) (with the monoidal structure induced by the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095)), is a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) in the usual sense. In this context:
  - the unit object $I$ of the monoidal category can be taken to be any singleton.
  - the multiplication $\mu$ corresponds to the monoid operation in the usual sense.
  - the unit $\eta$ corresponds to the function that maps the single member of $I$ to the identity element in the monoid.
- A monoid object in **[Top](wikipedia://en/435051)**, the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) (with the monoidal structure induced by the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910)), is a [topological monoid](wikipedia://en/44986732).
- A monoid object in the category of monoids (with the [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) of monoids) is just a [commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid). This follows easily from the [Eckmann–Hilton argument](wikipedia://en/488809).
- A monoid object in the category of [complete join-semilattices](wikipedia://en/755647#Complete_semilattices) **[Sup](wikipedia://en/44777#Morphisms_of_complete_lattices)** (with the monoidal structure induced by the Cartesian product) is a unital [quantale](wikipedia://en/716803).
- A monoid object in (**[Ab](wikipedia://en/454748)**, ⊗<sub>**Z**</sub>, [**Z**](wikipedia://en/14563)), the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748), is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404).
- For a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*, a monoid object in
  - ([*R*-**Mod**](wikipedia://en/11231812), ⊗<sub>*R*</sub>, *R*), the [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over *R*, is a [*R*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_associative_algebra).
  - the category of [graded modules](wikipedia://en/103094#Graded_module) is a [graded *R*-algebra](wikipedia://en/103094#Graded_algebra).
  - the [category of chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404#Category_of_chain_complexes) of *R*-modules is a [differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863).
- A monoid object in *K*-**Vect**, the [category of *K*-vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) (again, with the tensor product), is a unital associative *K*-[algebra](wikipedia://en/191788), and a comonoid object is a *K*-[coalgebra](wikipedia://en/310886).
- For any category *C*, the category \[*C*, *C*\] of its [endofunctors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor) has a monoidal structure induced by the composition and the identity [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) *I*<sub>*C*</sub>. A monoid object in \[*C*, *C*\] is a [monad](wikipedia://en/355140) on *C*.
- For any category with a terminal object and [finite products](wikipedia://en/237868), every object becomes a comonoid object via the diagonal morphism Δ<sub>*X*</sub> : *X* → *X* × *X*. Dually in a category with an initial object and [finite coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889) every object becomes a monoid object via id<sub>*X*</sub> ⊔ id<sub>*X*</sub> : *X* ⊔ *X* → *X*.

:::

## Categories of monoids {#Categories_of_monoids}

Given two monoids (*M*, *μ*, *η*) and (*M*′, *μ*′, *η*′) in a monoidal category **C**, a morphism *f* : *M* → *M*′ is a **morphism of monoids** when

- *f* ∘ *μ* = *μ*′ ∘ (*f* ⊗ *f*),
- *f* ∘ *η* = *η*′.
