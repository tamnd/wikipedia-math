# Polygon-circle graph

*Image: Polygon-circle graph.svg.* On the left a set of polygons inscribed in a circle; on the right the relative **Polygon-circle graph** (intersection graph of the polygons). On the bottom the alternating sequence of polygons around the circle.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polygon-circle graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) discipline of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **polygon-circle graph** is an [intersection graph](wikipedia://en/7726759) of a set of [convex polygons](wikipedia://en/660019) all of whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) lie on a common circle. These graphs have also been called **spider graphs**. This class of graphs was first suggested by [Michael Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fellows) in 1988, motivated by the fact that it is closed under [edge contraction](wikipedia://en/3758115) and [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) operations.[^o2]

:::

A polygon-circle graph can be represented as an "alternating sequence". Such a sequence can be gained by perturbing the polygons representing the graph (if necessary) so that no two share a vertex, and then listing for each vertex (in circular order, starting at an arbitrary point) the polygon attached to that vertex.

## Closure under induced minors {#Closure_under_induced_minors}

[Contracting an edge](wikipedia://en/3758115) of a polygon-circle graph results in another polygon-circle graph. A geometric representation of the new graph may be formed by replacing the polygons corresponding to the two endpoints of the contracted edge by their [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634). Alternatively, in the alternating sequence representing the original graph, combining the subsequences representing the endpoints of the contracted edge into a single subsequence produces an alternating sequence representation of the contracted graph. Polygon circle graphs are also closed under [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) or equivalently vertex deletion operations: to delete a vertex, remove its polygon from the geometric representation, or remove its subsequence of points from the alternating sequence.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

M. Koebe announced a polynomial time recognition algorithm;[^o5] however, his preliminary version had "serious errors"[^o6] and a final version was never published.[^o2] Martin Pergel later proved that the problem of recognizing these graphs is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete).[^o7] It is also NP-complete to determine whether a given graph can be represented as a polygon-circle graph with at most $k$ vertices per polygon, for any $k \ge 3$.[^o2]

## Related graph families {#Related_graph_families}

The polygon-circle graphs are a generalization of the [circle graphs](wikipedia://en/1399873), which are intersection graphs of the chords of a circle, and the [trapezoid graphs](wikipedia://en/31675608), intersection graphs of trapezoids that all have their vertices on the same two parallel lines. They also include the [circular arc graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_arc_graph).[^o2][^o9]

Polygon-circle graphs are not, in general, [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531), but they are near-perfect, in the sense that their [chromatic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) can be bounded by an (exponential) function of their [clique numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_number).[^o10]
