# Gábor J. Székely

Székely J. Gábor

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gábor J. Székely"}

**Gábor J. Székely** (; born February 4, 1947, in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest)) is a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661)/[mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for introducing [energy statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-statistic#Energy_statistics) (E-statistics).[^o3][^o4] Examples include: the [distance correlation](wikipedia://en/28217722),[^o5][^o6][^o7] which is a bona fide dependence measure, equals zero exactly when the variables are [independent](wikipedia://en/27593); the [distance skewness](wikipedia://en/28212), which equals zero exactly when the probability distribution is diagonally symmetric;[^o8][^o9] the E-statistic for [normality test](wikipedia://en/8617378);[^o10] and the E-statistic for clustering.[^o11]

:::

Other important discoveries include the Hungarian [semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroups),[^o12][^o13][^o14] the [location testing for Gaussian scale mixture distributions](wikipedia://en/28250184),[^o15] the [uncertainty principle](wikipedia://en/31883) of game theory,[^o16] the *half-coin*[^o17] which involves [negative probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_probability), and the solution of an old open problem of [lottery mathematics](wikipedia://en/2874081): in a 5-from-90 lotto the minimum number of tickets one needs to buy to guarantee that at least one of these tickets has (at least) 2 matches is exactly 100.[^o18]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Székely attended the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) graduating in 1970. His first advisor was [Alfréd Rényi](wikipedia://en/614988). Székely received his Ph.D. in 1971 from Eötvös Loránd University, the Candidate Degree in 1976 under the direction of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), and the [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) degree from the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1986. During the years 1970-1995 he has worked as a Professor in Eötvös Loránd University at the Department of Probability Theory and Statistics.[^o20]

Between 1985 and 1995 Székely was the first program manager of the [Budapest Semesters in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8520286). Between 1990 and 1997 he was the founding chair of the Department of Stochastics of the Budapest Institute of Technology ([Technical University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Budapest)) and editor-in-chief of *Matematikai Lapok*, the official journal of the [János Bolyai Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/22777615).

In 1989 Székely was visiting professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), and in 1990-91 he was the first [Lukacs Distinguished Professor](wikipedia://en/30407970) in Ohio. Since 1995 he has been teaching at the [Bowling Green State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green_State_University) at the Department of Mathematics and Statistics.[^o20] Székely was academic advisor of [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley), NY, and [Bunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunge_Limited), [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago), helped to establish the Morgan Stanley Mathematical Modeling Centre in Budapest (2005) and the Bunge Mathematical Institute (BMI) in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) (2006) to provide [quantitative analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_%28finance%29) to support the firms' global business.

Since 2006 he is a Program Director of Statistics of the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), now retired. Székely is also Research Fellow[^o21] of the Rényi Institute of Mathematics of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).

For an informal biographical sketch see *Conversations with Gábor J. Székely*[^o22]
