# James Ax

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James Ax"}

**James Burton Ax** (10 January 1937 – 11 June 2006)[^o3] was an American mathematician who made contributions in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) using [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858). He shared, with [Simon B. Kochen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_B._Kochen), the seventh [Frank Nelson Cole Prize in Number Theory](wikipedia://en/1372177), which was awarded for a series of three joint papers[^o4][^o5][^o6] on [Diophantine problems](wikipedia://en/9109).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Ax was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) and graduated from [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) in 1954. He then joined the [Brooklyn Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Institute_of_New_York_University). He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1961 under the direction of [Gerhard Hochschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Hochschild), with a dissertation on *The Intersection of [Norm Groups](wikipedia://en/32769154)*.

After a year at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford), he joined the mathematics faculty at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). He spent the academic year 1965–1966 at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) on a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship). In 1969, he was recruited by his [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) classmate [Jim Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844) to move from Cornell to the mathematics department at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).[^o9]

In 1970 he was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Nice with the talk *Transcendence and differential algebraic geometry*.[^o10] In the 1970s, he worked on the fundamentals of physics, including an axiomatization of [space-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-time) and the [group theoretical](wikipedia://en/41890) properties of the axioms of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202).

In 1977 he retired from his academic career and joined a [hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund) run by Jim Simons. In the 1980s, he and Simons founded the quantitative finance firm Axcom Trading Advisors,[^o11][^o12] which was later acquired by [Renaissance Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies) and renamed the Medallion Fund.[^o13] The latter fund was named after the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) won by James Ax and the [Veblen Prize](wikipedia://en/7085075) won by [Jim Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844).

In the early 1990s, Ax retired from his financial career and went to [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California), where he studied further on the foundations of quantum mechanics and also attended, at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), courses on playwriting and screenwriting. (In 2005 he completed a thriller screenplay entitled *Bots*.)

The *Ax Library* in the Department of Mathematics at the University of California, San Diego houses his mathematical books.

## Personal {#Personal}

Ax is the father of American cosmologist [Brian Keating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Keating) and Kevin B. Keating (b. 1967), who is the president of the Kevin and Masha Keating Family Foundation.[^o15] After Ax and his first wife divorced, she remarried a man named Keating, and young Brian and his older brother Kevin took the stepfather's name.[^o16] Brian Keating explained (in 2020) that he and his father were not close during his childhood; his father often joked that, "I don't really care about kids until they learn algebra."[^o16]
