# Quotient of subspace theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quotient of subspace theorem"}

In mathematics, the **quotient of subspace theorem** is an important property of finite-dimensional [normed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space), discovered by [Vitali Milman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitali_Milman).[^o2]

:::

Let (*X*, ||·||) be an *N*-dimensional normed space. There exist subspaces *Z* ⊂ *Y* ⊂ *X* such that the following holds:

- The [quotient space](wikipedia://en/898784) *E* = *Y* / *Z* is of dimension dim E ≥ *c* *N*, where *c* > 0 is a universal constant.
- The induced [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) || · || on *E*, defined by

$$\| e \| =\min_{y \in e} \| y \|, \quad e \in E,$$

is uniformly [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to Euclidean. That is, there exists a positive [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) ("Euclidean structure") *Q* on *E*, such that

$\frac{\sqrt{Q(e)}}{K} \leq \| e \| \leq K \sqrt{Q(e)}$ for $e \in E,$

with *K* > 1 a universal constant.

The statement is relatively easy to prove by induction on the dimension of *Z* (even for *Y=Z*, *X*=*0*, *c=1*) with a *K* that depends only on *N*; the point of the theorem is that *K* is independent of *N*.

In fact, the constant *c* can be made arbitrarily close to 1, at the expense of the constant *K* becoming large. The original proof allowed

$c(K) \approx 1 - \text{const} / \log \log K.$[^o3]
