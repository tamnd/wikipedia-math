# K-epsilon turbulence model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K-epsilon turbulence model"}

The **k-epsilon (k-ε) turbulence model** is one of the most common [models](wikipedia://en/20590) used in [computational fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/305924) (CFD) to simulate mean flow characteristics for [turbulent](wikipedia://en/154664) flow conditions. It is a two-equation model that gives a general description of [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) by means of two [transport equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_equations) ([partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), PDEs). The original impetus for the k-epsilon model was to improve the [mixing-length model](wikipedia://en/20589543), as well as to find an alternative to algebraically prescribing turbulent length scales in moderate- to high-complexity flows.[^o2]

:::

- The first transported variable is the [turbulence kinetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbulence_kinetic_energy) (k).
- The second transported variable is the rate of dissipation of turbulence kinetic energy (ε).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="Principle"}

## Principle {#Principle}

Unlike earlier [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) models, the k-ε model focuses on the mechanisms that affect the turbulence kinetic energy. The [mixing length model](wikipedia://en/20589543) lacks this kind of generality.[^o5] The underlying assumption of this model is that the turbulent viscosity is [isotropic](wikipedia://en/14865), in other words, the ratio between [Reynolds stress](wikipedia://en/1852294) and mean [rate of deformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strain_rate) is the same in all directions.

:::

## Standard k-ε turbulence model {#Standard_k-ε_turbulence_model}

The exact k-ε equations contain many unknown and unmeasurable terms. For a much more practical approach, the standard k-ε [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) model (Launder and Spalding, 1974[^o7]) is used which is based on our best understanding of the relevant processes, thus minimizing unknowns and presenting a set of equations which can be applied to a large number of turbulent applications.

For turbulence kinetic energy *k*[^o8][^o9]

$$\frac{\partial (\rho k)}{\partial t}+ \frac {\partial (\rho k u_i)}{\partial x_i}=\frac {\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\mu + \frac {\mu_t}{\sigma_k} \right)\frac {\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]+2{\mu_t}{E_{ij}}{E_{ij}}-\rho \varepsilon$$

For dissipation $\varepsilon$[^o8][^o9]

$$\frac{\partial (\rho \varepsilon)}{\partial t}+ \frac{\partial (\rho \varepsilon u_i)}{\partial x_i} = \frac {\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\mu + \frac {\mu_t}{\sigma_\varepsilon}\right)\frac {\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j}\right] + C_{1 \varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon}{k} 2{\mu_t}{E_{ij}}{E_{ij}}- C_{2 \varepsilon } \rho \frac{\varepsilon ^2}{k}$$

where

$u_i$ represents velocity component in corresponding direction

$E_{ij}$ represents component of [rate of deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strain_rate)

$\mu$ represents the [dynamic viscosity](wikipedia://en/18963754#Dynamic_viscosity)

$\mu_t$ represents [eddy viscosity](wikipedia://en/3087410)

$$\mu _t = \rho C _{\mu} \frac{k^2}{\varepsilon}$$

The equations state that the rate of change of *k* or ε in time, plus the transport of *k* or ε by [advection](wikipedia://en/166377), equals the transport of *k* or ε by [diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion), plus the rate of production of *k* or ε, minus the rate of destruction of *k* or ε.

The equations also contain some adjustable constants: $\sigma_k$, $\sigma_{\varepsilon}$, $C_{1\varepsilon}$, and $C_{2\varepsilon}$. The values of these constants have been arrived at by numerous iterations of [data fitting](wikipedia://en/555425) for a wide range of turbulent flows. These are as follows:[^o5]

$C_{\mu} = 0.09$ 10 $\sigma_k = 1.00$ 10 $\sigma_{\varepsilon} = 1.30$ 10 $C_{1\varepsilon} = 1.44$ 10 $C_{2\varepsilon} = 1.92$

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The k-ε model has been tailored specifically for [planar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) shear layers[^o12] and recirculating flows.[^o13] This model is the most widely used and validated [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) model with applications ranging from industrial to environmental flows, which explains its popularity. It is usually useful for free-shear layer flows with relatively small pressure [gradients](wikipedia://en/12461) as well as in confined flows where the [Reynolds shear stresses](wikipedia://en/1852294) are most important.[^o14] It can also be stated as the simplest [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) model for which only [initial](wikipedia://en/498383) or [boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/436779) needs to be supplied.

However, it is more expensive in terms of memory than the [mixing length model](wikipedia://en/3087410) as it requires two extra PDEs. This model would be an inappropriate choice for problems such as inlets and [compressors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_compressor) as accuracy has been shown experimentally to be reduced for flows containing large adverse pressure [gradients](wikipedia://en/12461). The k-ε model also performs poorly in a variety of important cases such as unconfined flows,[^o15] curved boundary layers, [rotating flows](wikipedia://en/2385900), and flows in non-circular ducts.[^o16]

:::

## Other models {#Other_models}

Realizable k-ε Model: An immediate benefit of the realizable k-ɛ model is that it provides improved predictions for the spreading rate of both planar and round jets. It also exhibits superior performance for flows involving rotation, boundary layers under strong adverse pressure gradients, separation, and recirculation. In virtually every measure of comparison, the realizable k-ɛ demonstrates a superior ability to capture the mean flow of the complex structures.

[k-ω Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K–omega_turbulence_model): used when there are wall effects present within the case.

[Reynolds stress equation model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynolds_stress_equation_model): In case of complex turbulent flows, Reynolds stress models are able to provide better predictions.[^o18] Such flows include turbulent flows with high degrees of anisotropy, significant streamline curvature, flow separation, zones of recirculation, and influence of mean rotation effects.
