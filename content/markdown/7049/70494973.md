# Sina Greenwood

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sina Greenwood"}

**Sina Ruth Greenwood** is a New Zealand mathematician whose interests include [continuum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_theory), [discrete dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_dynamical_system), [inverse limits](wikipedia://en/15109), [set-valued analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-valued_analysis), and [Volterra spaces](wikipedia://en/5075391). She is an associate professor of mathematics and Associate Dean Pacific in the faculty of science at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Greenwood's parents emigrated from [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoa) to [Whanganui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whanganui) in New Zealand, shortly before Greenwood was born; they moved from there to Auckland when she was a child. She earned a bachelor's degree at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), and after some time in Australia became a secondary school teacher in Auckland.

Returning to the University of Auckland for graduate study in mathematics, she earned a master's degree and then completed her PhD in 1999, under the joint supervision of [David Gauld](wikipedia://en/52432851) and David W. Mcintyre. Her dissertation was *Nonmetrisable Manifolds*.[^o3] She and three other students who finished their doctorates at the same time became the first [topologists](wikipedia://en/29954) to earn a doctorate at Auckland.

After postdoctoral research, funded by a New Zealand Science and Technology Post-Doctoral Fellowship, she obtained a permanent position at the University of Auckland as a lecturer in 2004, later becoming an associate professor. Beyond mathematics, her work at the university has also included advocating for the interests of [Pasifika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasifika_New_Zealanders) and [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Māori_people) students.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Greenwood is a Fellow of the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society), elected in 2018.
