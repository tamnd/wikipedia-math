# Tamal Dey

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tamal Dey"}

*Image: Tamal Krishna Dey, Ohio State University.jpg.* Tamal K. Dey at the [MFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach), 2012

**Tamal Krishna Dey** (born 1964) is an Indian mathematician and computer scientist specializing in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) and [computational topology](wikipedia://en/1248833). He is a professor at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Dey graduated from [Jadavpur University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadavpur_University) in 1985, with a bachelor's degree in electronics. He earned a master's degree from the [Indian Institute of Science Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science_Bangalore) in 1987, and completed his Ph.D. at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in 1991. His dissertation, *Decompositions of Polyhedra in Three Dimensions*, was supervised by [Chandrajit Bajaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrajit_Bajaj).

After postdoctoral research with [Herbert Edelsbrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Edelsbrunner) at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign), Dey joined the Purdue faculty in 1992. He moved to the [Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Kharagpur) in 1994, and moved to the computer science and engineering department at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) in 1999. At Ohio State, he obtained a courtesy appointment in the department of mathematics in 2015. He became the interim chair of the computer science department at Ohio State in 2019, before moving to Purdue in 2020.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Dey is known for proving the tightest-known [upper bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on the [$k$-set problem](wikipedia://en/8075001) and for his work on [3D reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_reconstruction) and [computational topology](wikipedia://en/1248833).

He is the author of the book *Curve and Surface Reconstruction: Algorithms with Mathematical Analysis* (Cambridge University Press, 2006). With Siu-Wing Cheng and [Jonathan Shewchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Shewchuk), he is the co-author of *Delaunay Mesh Generation* (CRC Press, 2012).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Dey was elected as an [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) in 2018 for "contributions to computational geometry and computational topology". He is also a fellow of the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE).
