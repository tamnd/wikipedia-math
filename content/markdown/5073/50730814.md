# Ptolemaic graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ptolemaic graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **Ptolemaic graph** is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) whose [shortest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_path) distances obey [Ptolemy's inequality](wikipedia://en/7148302), which in turn was named after the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Greece) [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979). The Ptolemaic graphs are exactly the graphs that are both [chordal](wikipedia://en/744165) and [distance-hereditary](wikipedia://en/17315337); they include the [block graphs](wikipedia://en/29106883)[^o4] and are a subclass of the [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

A graph is Ptolemaic if and only if it obeys any of the following equivalent conditions:

- The [shortest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_path) distances obey [Ptolemy's inequality](wikipedia://en/7148302): for every four [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) $u$, $v$, $w$, and $x$, the inequality $d(u,v)d(w,x) + d(u,x)d(v,w) \ge d(u,w)d(v,x)$ holds.[^o4] For instance, the [gem graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gem_graph) (3-fan) in the illustration is not Ptolemaic, because in this graph $d(u,w)d(v,x) = 4$, greater than $d(u,v)d(w,x) + d(u,x)d(v,w) = 3$.
- For every two overlapping [maximal cliques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_clique), the intersection of the two cliques is a [separator](wikipedia://en/1499165) that splits the differences of the two cliques.[^o5] In the illustration of the gem graph, this is not true: cliques $uvy$ and $wxy$ are not separated by their intersection, $y$, because there is an edge $vw$ that connects the cliques but avoids the intersection.
- Every $k$-vertex [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) has at least $3(k - 3)/2$ diagonals.[^o5]
- The graph is both [chordal](wikipedia://en/744165) (every cycle of length greater than three has a diagonal) and [distance-hereditary](wikipedia://en/17315337) (every connected [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) has the same distances as the whole graph).[^o5] The gem shown is chordal but not distance-hereditary: in the subgraph induced by $uvwx$, the distance from $u$ to $x$ is 3, greater than the distance between the same vertices in the whole graph. Because both chordal and distance-hereditary graphs are [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531), so are the Ptolemaic graphs.[^o6]
- The graph is chordal and does not contain an induced gem, a graph formed by adding two non-crossing diagonals to a pentagon.[^o6]
- The graph is distance-hereditary and does not contain an [induced 4-cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_cycle).[^o7]
- The graph can be constructed from a single vertex by a sequence of operations that add a new degree-one (pendant) vertex, or duplicate (twin) an existing vertex, with the exception that a twin operation in which the new duplicate vertex is not adjacent to its twin (false twins) can only be applied when the neighbors of the twins form a clique. These three operations without the exception form all distance-hereditary graph. To form all Ptolemaic graphs, it is not enough to use pendant vertices and true twins; the exceptional case of false twins is sometimes also required.[^o8]
- The [Hasse diagram](wikipedia://en/14835049) of the subset relation on nonempty intersections of maximal cliques forms an [oriented tree](wikipedia://en/1731488).[^o9]
- The convex subsets of vertices (subsets that contain every shortest path between two vertices in the subset) form a [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/667063). That is, every convex set can be reached from the whole vertex set by repeatedly removing an extreme vertex, one that does not belong to any shortest path between the remaining vertices.[^o10] In the gem, the convex set $uxy$ cannot be reached in this way, because neither $v$ nor $w$ is extreme.

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Based on the characterization by oriented trees, Ptolemaic graphs can be recognized in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time).[^o9]

## Enumeration {#Enumeration}

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for Ptolemaic graphs can be described [symbolically](wikipedia://en/986932), allowing the fast calculation of the numbers of these graphs. Based on this method, the number of Ptolemaic graphs with $n$ labeled vertices, for $n=1,2,3,\dots$, has been found to be[^o14]

1, 1, 4, 35, 481, 9042, 216077, 6271057, 214248958, 8424002973, 374708368981, 18604033129948, 1019915376831963, ...

:::
