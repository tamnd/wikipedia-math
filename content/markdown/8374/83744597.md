# Elza Berquó

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elza Berquó"}

Elza Berquó

**Elza Berquó** (17 October 1925 – 16 July 2026) was a Brazilian mathematician and professor known as a pioneer in [demography](wikipedia://en/45642) in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil).[^o2] She was among the earliest researchers to identify Brazil's fertility decline, examined in the 1965 study *Reprodução humana no distrito de São Paulo*.

:::

A graduate of the [Pontifical Catholic University of Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Campinas) who completed her doctoral studies in [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), she founded the Center for Studies in Population Dynamics (Cedip) at the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo), which conducted research on human fertility and family planning.[^o3] In the 1980s, she directed the Núcleo de Estudos de População at the [State University of Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Campinas), studying gender and racial topics, particularly health problems faced by the [Afro-Brazilian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Brazilians).[^o4]

A member of the [Brazilian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Academy_of_Sciences), she was awarded the Grand-Cross of the [National Order of Scientific Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Order_of_Scientific_Merit) in 1998.[^o5] Berquó died on 16 July 2026, at the age of 100.[^o6]
