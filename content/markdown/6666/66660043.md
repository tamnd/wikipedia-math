# Girolamo Saladini

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Girolamo Saladini"}

**Girolamo Saladini** ([Lucca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucca), 22 July 1735 – [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna), 1 June 1813[^o3]) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Saladini was one of the brightest pupils of the Italian mathematician [Vincenzo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Riccati), with whom he had a fruitful collaboration: together they wrote the *Institutiones analyticae*, an extensive treatise on [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) published in three volumes in Bologna in 1765–1767. In 1775, Saladini published an Italian translation of the work.[^o5][^o6]

Saladini taught [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and higher mathematics at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna). He was an early member of the [Accademia nazionale delle scienze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_nazionale_delle_scienze). He published many articles, works and memoirs in various specialized Italian publications.

In a memoir written by himself and dated 1808, entitled *Sul principio delle velocità virtuali* ("On the principle of virtual speeds"),[^o7] starting from the work carried out by the mathematicians [Vittorio Fossombroni](wikipedia://en/3126271) and Vincenzo Angiulli, he tried to prove the [Principle of Virtual Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_Virtual_Work) by trying to avoid the main difficulties, including the presence of constraints.
