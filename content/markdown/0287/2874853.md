# Square lattice

*Image: Square Lattice Tiling.svg.* Upright [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809). The vertices of all squares together with their centers form an upright square lattice. For each color the centers of the squares of that color form a diagonal square lattice which is in linear scale √2 times as large as the upright square lattice.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Square lattice"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **square lattice** is a type of [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) in a two-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). It is the two-dimensional version of the [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402), and is denoted as $\mathbb{Z}^2$.[^o3] It is one of the five types of two-dimensional lattices as classified by their [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186).[^o4]

:::

Two orientations of an image of the lattice are by far the most common. They can conveniently be referred to as the upright square lattice and diagonal square lattice; the latter is also called the **centered square lattice**.[^o5] They differ by an angle of 45°. This is related to the fact that a square lattice can be partitioned into two square sub-lattices, as is evident in the colouring of a [checkerboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkerboard).

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The square lattice's [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) group is a [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214), denoted in [IUC notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUC_notation#Symmetry_notation) as $p4m$,[^o8] [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502) as $[4,4]$,[^o9] and [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678) as $*442$.[^o10] A pattern with this lattice of [translational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_symmetry) cannot have more, but may have less symmetry than the lattice itself. An upright square lattice can be viewed as a diagonal square lattice with a mesh size that is √2 times as large, with the centers of the squares added. Correspondingly, after adding the centers of the squares of an upright square lattice one obtains a diagonal square lattice with a mesh size that is √2 times as small as that of the original lattice. A pattern with 4-fold [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) has a square lattice of 4-fold rotocenters that is a factor √2 finer and diagonally oriented relative to the lattice of [translational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_symmetry).

With respect to [reflection axes](wikipedia://en/1390873) there are three possibilities:

- None. This is wallpaper group $p4$.
- In four directions. This is wallpaper group $p4m$.
- In two [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) directions. This is wallpaper group $p4g$. The points of intersection of the reflexion axes form a square grid which is as fine as, and oriented the same as, the square lattice of 4-fold rotocenters, with these rotocenters at the centers of the squares formed by the reflection axes.
