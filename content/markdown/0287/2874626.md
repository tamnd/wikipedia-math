# Interleave sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Interleave sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **interleave sequence** is obtained by merging two [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) via an [in shuffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_shuffle).

:::

Let $S$ be a [set](wikipedia://en/26691), and let $(x_i)$ and $(y_i)$, $i=0,1,2,\ldots,$ be two sequences in $S.$ The interleave sequence is defined to be the sequence $x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1, \dots$. Formally, it is the sequence $(z_i), i=0,1,2,\ldots$ given by

$$z_i := \begin{cases} x_{i/2} & \text{ if } i \text{ is even,} \\ y_{(i-1)/2} & \text{ if } i \text{ is odd.} \end{cases}$$

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The interleave sequence $(z_i)$ is [convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_sequence) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the sequences $(x_i)$ and $(y_i)$ are convergent and have the same [limit](wikipedia://en/285773).[^o4]
- Consider two [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) *a* and *b* greater than zero and smaller than 1. One can interleave the sequences of digits of *a* and *b*, which will determine a third number *c*, also greater than zero and smaller than 1. In this way one obtains an [injection](wikipedia://en/45196) from the square (0, 1) × (0, 1) to the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) (0, 1). Different [radixes](wikipedia://en/701207) give rise to different injections; the one for the [binary numbers](wikipedia://en/238686) is called the [Z-order curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z-order_curve) or Morton code.[^o5]

:::
