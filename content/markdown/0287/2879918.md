# List of probability distributions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of probability distributions"}

Many [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) that are important in theory or applications have been given specific names.

:::

## Discrete distributions {#Discrete_distributions}

*Image: Binomial distribution pmf.svg.* [Binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/3876)

*Image: Degenerate distribution PMF.png.* [Degenerate distribution](wikipedia://en/101843)

### With finite [support](wikipedia://en/381013) {#With_finite_support}

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bernoulli distribution"}

- The [Bernoulli distribution](wikipedia://en/199189), which takes value 1 with probability *p* and value 0 with probability *q* = 1 − *p*.

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rademacher distribution"}

- The [Rademacher distribution](wikipedia://en/1856890), which takes value 1 with probability 1/2 and value −1 with probability 1/2.

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Binomial distribution"}

- The [binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/3876), which describes the number of successes in a series of independent Yes/No experiments all with the same probability of success.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Beta-binomial distribution"}

- The [beta-binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/7377730), which describes the number of successes in a series of independent Yes/No experiments with heterogeneity in the success probability.

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="Degenerate distribution"}

- The [degenerate distribution](wikipedia://en/101843) at *x*<sub>0</sub>, where *X* is certain to take the value *x*<sub>0</sub>. This does not look random, but it satisfies the definition of [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685). This is useful because it puts deterministic variables and random variables in the same formalism.

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Discrete uniform distribution"}

- The [discrete uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699214), where all elements of a finite [set](wikipedia://en/27553) are equally likely. This is the theoretical distribution model for a balanced coin, an unbiased die, a casino roulette, or the first card of a well-shuffled deck.

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hypergeometric distribution"}

- The [hypergeometric distribution](wikipedia://en/180841), which describes the number of successes in the first *m* of a series of *n* consecutive Yes/No experiments, if the total number of successes is known. This distribution arises when there is no replacement.

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Negative hypergeometric distribution"}

- The [negative hypergeometric distribution](wikipedia://en/39726151), a distribution which describes the number of attempts needed to get the *n*th success in a series of Yes/No experiments without replacement.

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=list-entry name="Poisson binomial distribution"}

- The [Poisson binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/28647736), which describes the number of successes in a series of independent Yes/No experiments with different success probabilities.

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution"}

- [Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution](wikipedia://en/11962384)

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="Wallenius' noncentral hypergeometric distribution"}

- [Wallenius' noncentral hypergeometric distribution](wikipedia://en/11896192)

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Benford's law"}

- [Benford's law](wikipedia://en/36782), which describes the frequency of the first digit of many naturally occurring data.

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Soliton distribution"}

- The ideal and robust [soliton distributions](wikipedia://en/21975332).

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Zipf's law"}

- [Zipf's law](wikipedia://en/43218) or the Zipf distribution. A discrete [power-law distribution](wikipedia://en/24522#Power-law_probability_distributions), the most famous example of which is the description of the frequency of words in the English language.

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=list-entry name="Zipf–Mandelbrot law"}

- The [Zipf–Mandelbrot law](wikipedia://en/390113) is a discrete power law distribution which is a generalization of the Zipf distribution.

:::

### With infinite support {#With_infinite_support}

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Beta negative binomial distribution"}

- The [beta negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/31118503)

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Boltzmann distribution"}

- The [Boltzmann distribution](wikipedia://en/4107), a discrete distribution important in [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics) which describes the probabilities of the various discrete energy levels of a system in [thermal equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_equilibrium). It has a continuous analogue.

:::

  - The [Maxwell&ndash;Boltzmann distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell&ndash;Boltzmann_distribution) is a special case.

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Borel distribution"}

- The [Borel distribution](wikipedia://en/40323986)

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Discrete phase-type distribution"}

- The [discrete phase-type distribution](wikipedia://en/11035198), a generalization of the geometric distribution which describes the [first hit time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_hit_time) of the absorbing state of a finite terminating [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876).

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=list-entry name="Extended negative binomial distribution"}

- The [extended negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/17816534)

:::

- The generalized log-series distribution

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gauss–Kuzmin distribution"}

- The [Gauss–Kuzmin distribution](wikipedia://en/2196799)

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="Geometric distribution"}

- The [geometric distribution](wikipedia://en/45922), a discrete distribution which describes the number of attempts needed to get the first success in a series of independent Bernoulli trials, or alternatively only the number of losses before the first success (i.e. one less).

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hermite distribution"}

- The [Hermite distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermite_distribution)

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="logarithmic (series) distribution"}

- The [logarithmic (series) distribution](wikipedia://en/1119231)

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Mixed Poisson distribution"}

- The [mixed Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/70277231)

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=list-entry name="Negative binomial distribution"}

- The [negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/45177) or Pascal distribution, a generalization of the geometric distribution to the *n*th success.

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=list-entry name="Compound Poisson distribution"}

- The discrete [compound Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/949562)

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=list-entry name="Parabolic fractal distribution"}

- The [parabolic fractal distribution](wikipedia://en/1096151)

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=list-entry name="Poisson distribution"}

- The [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144), which describes a very large number of individually unlikely events that happen in a certain time interval. Related to this distribution are a number of other distributions: the [displaced Poisson](wikipedia://en/43734728), the hyper-Poisson, the general Poisson binomial and the Poisson type distributions.

:::

  - The [Conway&ndash;Maxwell&ndash;Poisson distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway&ndash;Maxwell&ndash;Poisson_distribution), a two-parameter extension of the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with an adjustable rate of decay.
  - The [zero-truncated Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/40180359), for processes in which zero counts are not observed

::: {#o34 .entry cue=list-entry name="Polya–Eggenberger distribution"}

- The [Polya–Eggenberger distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polya–Eggenberger_distribution)

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=list-entry name="Skellam distribution"}

- The [Skellam distribution](wikipedia://en/1192757), the distribution of the difference between two independent Poisson-distributed random variables.

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Skew elliptical distribution"}

- The [skew elliptical distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew_elliptical_distribution)

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=list-entry name="Yule–Simon distribution"}

- The [Yule–Simon distribution](wikipedia://en/1119623)

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=list-entry name="Zeta distribution"}

- The [zeta distribution](wikipedia://en/115742) has uses in applied statistics and statistical mechanics, and perhaps may be of interest to number theorists. It is the [Zipf distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipf_distribution) for an infinite number of elements.

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hardy distribution"}

- The [Hardy distribution](wikipedia://en/74104164), which describes the probabilities of the hole scores for a given golf player.

:::

### Supported on a bounded interval {#Supported_on_a_bounded_interval}

::: {#o42 .entry cue=list-entry name="Beta distribution"}

- The [Beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074) on \[0,1\], a family of two-parameter distributions with one mode, of which the uniform distribution is a special case, and which is useful in estimating success probabilities.

:::

  - The [four-parameter Beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074#Four_parameters), a straight-forward generalization of the Beta distribution to arbitrary bounded intervals $[a, b]$.

::: {#o43 .entry cue=list-entry name="Arcsine distribution"}

- The [arcsine distribution](wikipedia://en/26400022) on \[*a*,*b*\], which is a special case of the Beta distribution if *α* = *β* = 1/2, *a* = 0, and *b* = 1.

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=list-entry name="PERT distribution"}

- The [PERT distribution](wikipedia://en/54553868) is a special case of the four-parameter [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074).

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=list-entry name="uniform distribution"}

- The [uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29) or rectangular distribution on \[*a*,*b*\], where all points in a finite interval are equally likely, is a special case of the four-parameter Beta distribution.

:::

::: {#o46 .entry cue=list-entry name="Irwin–Hall distribution"}

- The [Irwin–Hall distribution](wikipedia://en/15329594) is the distribution of the sum of *n* independent random variables, each of which having the uniform distribution on \[0,1\].

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bates distribution"}

- The [Bates distribution](wikipedia://en/31100511) is the distribution of the mean of *n* independent random variables, each of which having the uniform distribution on \[0,1\].

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=list-entry name="Logit-normal distribution"}

- The [logit-normal distribution](wikipedia://en/28843342) on (0,1).

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dirac delta function"}

- The [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021), although not strictly a probability distribution, is a limiting form of many continuous probability functions. It represents a *discrete* probability distribution concentrated at 0 — a [degenerate distribution](wikipedia://en/101843) — it is a [Distribution (mathematics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) in the generalized function sense; but the notation treats it as if it were a continuous distribution.

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=list-entry name="Kent distribution"}

- The [Kent distribution](wikipedia://en/5624561) on the two-dimensional sphere.

:::

::: {#o51 .entry cue=list-entry name="Kumaraswamy distribution"}

- The [Kumaraswamy distribution](wikipedia://en/2049583) is as versatile as the Beta distribution but has simple closed forms for both the cdf and the pdf.

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=list-entry name="logit metalog distribution"}

- The [logit metalog distribution](wikipedia://en/66708690#/Unbounded,_semibounded,_and_bounded_metalog_distributions), which is highly shape-flexible, has simple closed forms, and can be parameterized with data using linear least squares.

:::

::: {#o53 .entry cue=list-entry name="Marchenko–Pastur distribution"}

- The [Marchenko–Pastur distribution](wikipedia://en/25115911) is important in the theory of [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices).

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Quantile-parameterized distribution"}

- The bounded [quantile-parameterized distributions](wikipedia://en/65801270), which are highly shape-flexible and can be parameterized with data using linear least squares (see [Quantile-parameterized distribution#Transformations](wikipedia://en/65801270#Transformations))

:::

::: {#o55 .entry cue=list-entry name="Raised cosine distribution"}

- The [raised cosine distribution](wikipedia://en/2655605) on \[$\mu-s,\mu+s$\]

:::

::: {#o56 .entry cue=list-entry name="Reciprocal distribution"}

- The [reciprocal distribution](wikipedia://en/39127799)

:::

::: {#o57 .entry cue=list-entry name="Triangular distribution"}

- The [triangular distribution](wikipedia://en/663496) on \[*a*, *b*\], a special case of which is the distribution of the sum of two independent uniformly distributed random variables (the *convolution* of two uniform distributions).

:::

::: {#o58 .entry cue=list-entry name="Trapezoidal distribution"}

- The [trapezoidal distribution](wikipedia://en/40750679)

:::

::: {#o59 .entry cue=list-entry name="Truncated normal distribution"}

- The [truncated normal distribution](wikipedia://en/11753597) on \[*a*, *b*\].

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=list-entry name="U-quadratic distribution"}

- The [U-quadratic distribution](wikipedia://en/14108507) on \[*a*, *b*\].

:::

::: {#o61 .entry cue=list-entry name="Von Mises–Fisher distribution"}

- The [von Mises–Fisher distribution](wikipedia://en/5624964) on the *N*-dimensional sphere has the [von Mises distribution](wikipedia://en/1840727) as a special case.

:::

::: {#o62 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bingham distribution"}

- The [Bingham distribution](wikipedia://en/18559341) on the *N*-dimensional sphere.

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=list-entry name="Wigner semicircle distribution"}

- The [Wigner semicircle distribution](wikipedia://en/1028321) is important in the theory of [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices).

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=list-entry name="Continuous Bernoulli distribution"}

- The [continuous Bernoulli distribution](wikipedia://en/64250099) is a one-parameter [exponential family](wikipedia://en/339174) obtained from exponential tilting of the [continuous uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699223). It provides a probabilistic counterpart to the binary [cross-entropy](wikipedia://en/1735250) loss.

:::

::: {#o65 .entry cue=list-entry name="continuous binomial (cobin) distribution"}

- The [continuous binomial (cobin) distribution](wikipedia://en/82512540), a two-parameter generalization of [continuous Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/64250099) with an additional dispersion parameter.

:::
