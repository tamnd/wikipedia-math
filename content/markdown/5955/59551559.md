# Evdokimov's algorithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Evdokimov's algorithm"}

In [computational number theory](wikipedia://en/511466), **Evdokimov's algorithm**, named after [Sergei Evdokimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Evdokimov), is an algorithm for [factorization of polynomials](wikipedia://en/3776351) over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615). It was the fastest algorithm known for this problem, from its publication in 1994 until 2020.[^o2] It can factorize a one-variable polynomial of degree $n$ over an explicitly given [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) of cardinality $q$. Assuming the [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567) the algorithm runs in deterministic time $(n^{\log n}\log q)^{{\mathcal O}(1)}$ [^o3] (see [Big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578)). This is an improvement of both [Berlekamp's algorithm](wikipedia://en/6057100) and Rónyai's algorithm[^o4] in the sense that the first algorithm is polynomial for small characteristic of the field, whearas the second one is polynomial for small $n$; however, both of them are exponential if no restriction is made.

:::

The factorization of a polynomial $f$ over a ground field $k$ is reduced to the case when $f$ has no multiple roots and is completely splitting over $k$ (i.e. $f$ has $n$ distinct roots in $k$). In order to find a root of $f$ in this case, the algorithm deals with polynomials not only over the ground field $k$ but also over a completely splitting [semisimple algebra](wikipedia://en/3876151) over $k$ (an example of such an algebra is given by $k[X]/(f) = k[A]$, where $A = X\bmod f$). The main problem here is to find efficiently a nonzero zero-divisor in the algebra. The GRH is used only to take roots in finite fields in polynomial time. Thus the Evdokimov algorithm, in fact, solves a polynomial equation over a finite field "by radicals" in [quasipolynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasipolynomial_time).

The analyses of Evdokimov's algorithm is closely related with some problems in the [association scheme](wikipedia://en/2238152) theory. With the help of this approach, it was proved [^o5] that if $n$ is a prime and $n-1$ has a ‘large’ $r$-smooth divisor $s$, then a modification of the Evdokimov algorithm finds a nontrivial factor of the polynomial $f$ in deterministic $\operatorname{poly}(n^r,\log q)$ time, assuming GRH and that $s=\Omega\left(\sqrt{n/2^r}\,\right)$.
