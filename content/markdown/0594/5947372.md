# Cohort effect

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cohort effect"}

The term **cohort effect** is used in [social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science) to describe shared characteristics over time among individuals who are grouped by a shared temporal experience, such as year of birth, or common life experience, such as time of exposure to radiation.[^o2][^o3] Researchers evaluate this phenomenon using a [cohort analysis](wikipedia://en/397271).[^o4]

:::

For [economists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist), the cohort effect is an important factor to consider in the context of [resource dependency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_dependence_theory). This is because cohorts, which in organizations are often defined by entry or birth date, retain some common characteristic (size, cohesiveness, competition) that can affect the organization.[^o5] For example, cohort effects are critical issues in school enrollment.

The cohort effect is relevant to [epidemiologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiologists) searching for patterns in illnesses. Cohort effects can sometimes be mistaken for the [anticipation phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticipation_%28genetics%29), which is the earlier onset of genetic illnesses in later generations.[^o6] There is evidence for both a cohort effect and an anticipation effect in the increase in [childhood-onset bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder_in_children).[^o6]
