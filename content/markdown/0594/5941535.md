# Regular number

*Image: Regular divisibility lattice.svg.* A [Hasse diagram](wikipedia://en/338199) of [divisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility) relationships among the regular numbers up to 400. The vertical scale is [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/164055).[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Regular number"}

**Regular numbers** are numbers that evenly divide powers of [60](wikipedia://en/343325) (or, equivalently, powers of [30](wikipedia://en/341668)). Equivalently, they are the numbers whose only prime divisors are [2](wikipedia://en/64516), [3](wikipedia://en/208157), and [5](wikipedia://en/6412297). As an example, 60<sup>2</sup> = 3600 = 48 × 75, so as divisors of a power of 60 both 48 and 75 are regular.

:::

These numbers arise in several areas of mathematics and its applications, and have different names coming from their different areas of study.

- In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), these numbers are called **5-smooth**, because they can be characterized as having only 2, 3, or 5 as their [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor). This is a specific case of the more general $k$-[smooth numbers](wikipedia://en/1035039), the numbers that have no prime factor greater than $k$.
- In the study of [Babylonian mathematics](wikipedia://en/3861353), the divisors of powers of 60 are called **regular numbers** or **regular sexagesimal numbers**, and are of great importance in this area because of the [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) (base 60) number system that the Babylonians used for writing their numbers, and that was central to Babylonian mathematics.
- In [music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_theory), regular numbers occur in the ratios of tones in [five-limit just intonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-limit_tuning). In connection with music theory and related theories of [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture), these numbers have been called the **harmonic whole numbers**.
- In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), regular numbers are often called **Hamming numbers**, after [Richard Hamming](wikipedia://en/99326), who proposed the problem of finding computer [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for generating these numbers in ascending order. This problem has been used as a test case for [functional programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_programming).

## Number theory {#Number_theory}

Formally, a regular number is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) of the form $2^i\cdot 3^j\cdot 5^k$, for nonnegative integers $i$, $j$, and $k$. Such a number is a divisor of $60^{\max(\lceil i\,/2\rceil,j,k)}$. The regular numbers are also called 5-[smooth](wikipedia://en/1035039), indicating that their greatest [prime factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) is at most 5.[^n1] More generally, a $k$-smooth number is a number whose greatest prime factor is at most $k$.[^n2]

The first few regular numbers are[^n1] 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 16, 18, 20, 24, 25, 27, 30, 32, 36, 40, 45, 48, 50, 54, 60, ... Several other sequences at the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004) have definitions involving 5-smooth numbers.[^o7]

Although the regular numbers appear dense within the range from 1 to 60, they are quite sparse among the larger integers. A regular number $n=2^i\cdot 3^j\cdot 5^k$ is less than or equal to some threshold $N$ if and only if the point $(i,j,k)$ belongs to the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) bounded by the coordinate planes and the plane

$$i\ln 2+j\ln 3+k\ln 5\le\ln N,$$

as can be seen by taking logarithms of both sides of the inequality $2^i\cdot 3^j\cdot 5^k\le N$. Therefore, the number of regular numbers that are at most $N$ can be estimated as the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of this tetrahedron, which is

$$\frac{\log_2 N\,\log_3 N\,\log_5 N}{6}.$$

Even more precisely, using [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578), the number of regular numbers up to $N$ is

$$\frac{\left(\ln(N\sqrt{30})\right)^3}{6\ln 2 \ln 3 \ln 5}+O(\log N),$$

and it has been conjectured that the error term of this approximation is actually $O(\log\log N)$.[^n1] A similar formula for the number of 3-smooth numbers up to $N$ is given by [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) in his first letter to [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599).[^n3]

## Babylonian mathematics {#Babylonian_mathematics}

*Image: Table de division et de conversion des fractions - Louvre - AO 6456.JPG.* AO 6456, a table of reciprocals of regular numbers from [Seleucid Uruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruk#Uruk_into_Late_Antiquity), copied from an unknown earlier source

In the Babylonian [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) notation, the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) of a regular number has a finite representation. If $n$ divides $60^k$, then the sexagesimal representation of $1/n$ is just that for $60^k/n$, shifted by some number of places. This allows for easy division by these numbers: to divide by $n$, multiply by $1/n$, then shift.[^n4]

For instance, consider division by the regular number 54 = 2<sup>1</sup>3<sup>3</sup>. 54 is a divisor of 60<sup>3</sup>, and 60<sup>3</sup>/54 = 4000, so dividing by 54 in sexagesimal can be accomplished by multiplying by 4000 and shifting three places. In sexagesimal 4000 = 1×3600 + 6×60 + 40×1, or (as listed by Joyce) 1:6:40. Thus, 1/54, in sexagesimal, is 1/60 + 6/60<sup>2</sup> + 40/60<sup>3</sup>, also denoted 1:6:40 as Babylonian notational conventions did not specify the power of the starting digit. Conversely 1/4000 = 54/60<sup>3</sup>, so division by 1:6:40 = 4000 can be accomplished by instead multiplying by 54 and shifting three sexagesimal places.

The Babylonians used tables of reciprocals of regular numbers, some of which still survive.[^n5] These tables existed relatively unchanged throughout Babylonian times.[^n4] One tablet from [Seleucid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid) times, by someone named Inaqibıt-Anu, contains the reciprocals of 136 of the 231 six-place regular numbers whose first place is 1 or 2, listed in order. It also includes reciprocals of some numbers of more than six places, such as 3${}^{23}$ (2,1,4,8,3,0,27 in sexagesimal), whose reciprocal has 17 sexagesimal digits. Noting the difficulty of both calculating these numbers and sorting them, [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) in 1972 hailed Inaqibıt-Anu as "the first man in history to solve a computational problem that takes longer than one second of time on a modern electronic computer!" (Two tables are also known giving approximations of reciprocals of non-regular numbers, one of which gives reciprocals for all the numbers from 56 to 80.)[^n6][^n7]

Although the primary reason for preferring regular numbers to other numbers involves the finiteness of their reciprocals, some Babylonian calculations other than reciprocals also involved regular numbers. For instance, tables of regular squares have been found[^n4] and the broken tablet [Plimpton 322](wikipedia://en/2983547) has been interpreted by [Neugebauer](wikipedia://en/737813) as listing [Pythagorean triples](wikipedia://en/24172) $( p^2 - q^2,\, 2pq,\, p^2 + q^2 )$ generated by $p$ and $q$ both regular and less than 60.[^o17] Fowler and Robson discuss the calculation of square roots, such as how the Babylonians found an approximation to the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682), perhaps using regular number approximations of fractions such as 17/12.[^n7]

## Music theory {#Music_theory}

In [music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_theory), the [just intonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_intonation) of the [diatonic scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatonic_scale) involves regular numbers: the [pitches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_%28music%29) in a single [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave) of this scale have frequencies proportional to the numbers in the sequence 24, 27, 30, 32, 36, 40, 45, 48 of nearly consecutive regular numbers.[^n8] Thus, for an instrument with this tuning, all pitches are regular-number [harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic) of a single [fundamental frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_frequency). This scale is called a 5-[limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_%28music%29) tuning, meaning that the [interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_%28music%29) between any two pitches can be described as a product 2<sup>i</sup>3<sup>j</sup>5<sup>k</sup> of powers of the prime numbers up to 5, or equivalently as a ratio of regular numbers.[^n9]

5-limit musical scales other than the familiar diatonic scale of Western music have also been used, both in traditional musics of other cultures and in modern experimental music: (Honingh Bod 2005) list 31 different 5-limit scales, drawn from a larger database of musical scales. Each of these 31 scales shares with diatonic just intonation the property that all intervals are ratios of regular numbers.[^n9] [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler)'s [tonnetz](wikipedia://en/5635929) provides a convenient graphical representation of the pitches in any 5-limit tuning, by factoring out the octave relationships (powers of two) so that the remaining values form a planar [grid](wikipedia://en/3771917).[^n9] Some music theorists have stated more generally that regular numbers are fundamental to tonal music itself, and that pitch ratios based on primes larger than 5 cannot be [consonant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consonance_and_dissonance).[^o25] However the [equal temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_temperament) of modern pianos is not a 5-limit tuning,[^n10] and some modern composers have experimented with tunings based on primes larger than five.[^n11]

In connection with the application of regular numbers to music theory, it is of interest to find pairs of regular numbers that differ by one. There are exactly ten such pairs $(x,x+1)$ and each such pair defines a [superparticular ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superparticular_number) $\tfrac{x+1}{x}$ that is meaningful as a musical interval. These intervals are 2/1 (the [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave)), 3/2 (the [perfect fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_fifth)), 4/3 (the [perfect fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_fourth)), 5/4 (the [just major third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_major_third)), 6/5 (the [just minor third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_minor_third)), 9/8 (the [just major tone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_major_tone)), 10/9 (the [just minor tone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_minor_tone)), 16/15 (the [just diatonic semitone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_diatonic_semitone)), 25/24 (the [just chromatic semitone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_chromatic_semitone)), and 81/80 (the [syntonic comma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntonic_comma)).[^o28]

In the Renaissance theory of [universal harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musica_universalis), musical ratios were used in other applications, including the [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture) of buildings. In connection with the analysis of these shared musical and architectural ratios, for instance in the architecture of [Palladio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladio), the regular numbers have also been called the **harmonic whole numbers**.[^n12]

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

Z15224 Algorithms for calculating the regular numbers in ascending order were popularized by [Edsger Dijkstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsger_Dijkstra). (txt) attributes to Hamming the problem of building the infinite ascending sequence of all 5-smooth numbers; this problem is now known as **Hamming's problem**, and the numbers so generated are also called the **Hamming numbers**. Dijkstra's ideas to compute these numbers are the following:

- The sequence of Hamming numbers begins with the number 1.
- The remaining values in the sequence are of the form $2h$, $3h$, and $5h$, where $h$ is any Hamming number.
- Therefore, the sequence $H$ may be generated by outputting the value 1, and then [merging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_algorithm) the sequences $2H$, $3H$, and $5H$.

This algorithm is often used to demonstrate the power of a [lazy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazy_evaluation) [functional programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_programming_language), because (implicitly) concurrent efficient implementations, using a constant number of arithmetic operations per generated value, are easily constructed as described above. Similarly efficient strict functional or [imperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperative_programming_language) sequential implementations are also possible whereas explicitly concurrent [generative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generator_%28computer_programming%29) solutions might be non-trivial.[^o33]

In the [Python programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29), lazy functional code for generating regular numbers is used as one of the built-in tests for correctness of the language's implementation.[^o34]

A related problem, discussed by (Knuth 1972), is to list all $k$-digit sexagesimal numbers in ascending order (see #Babylonian mathematics above). In algorithmic terms, this is equivalent to generating (in order) the subsequence of the infinite sequence of regular numbers, ranging from $60^k$ to $60^{k+1}$.[^n6] See (Gingerich 1965) for an early description of computer code that generates these numbers out of order and then sorts them;[^n13] Knuth describes an ad hoc algorithm, which he attributes to (Bruins 1970), for generating the six-digit numbers more quickly but that does not generalize in a straightforward way to larger values of $k$.[^n6] (Eppstein 2007) describes an algorithm for computing tables of this type in linear time for arbitrary values of $k$.[^n14]

:::

## Other applications {#Other_applications}

(Heninger Rains Sloane 2006) show that, when $n$ is a regular number and is divisible by 8, the generating function of an $n$-dimensional extremal even [unimodular lattice](wikipedia://en/1648605) is an $n$th power of a polynomial.[^n15]

As with other classes of [smooth numbers](wikipedia://en/1035039), regular numbers are important as problem sizes in computer programs for performing the [fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512), a technique for analyzing the dominant frequencies of signals in [time-varying data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28electrical_engineering%29). For instance, the method of (Temperton 1992) requires that the transform length be a regular number.[^n16]

Book VIII of [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954)'s *[Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republic_%28Plato%29)* involves an allegory of marriage centered on the highly regular number 60<sup>4</sup> = 12,960,000 and its divisors (see [Plato's number](wikipedia://en/18602826)). Later scholars have invoked both Babylonian mathematics and music theory in an attempt to explain this passage.[^o48]

Certain species of [bamboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo) release large numbers of seeds in synchrony (a process called [masting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mast_%28botany%29)) at intervals that have been estimated as regular numbers of years, with different intervals for different species, including examples with intervals of 10, 15, 16, 30, 32, 48, 60, and 120 years.[^n17] It has been hypothesized that the biological mechanism for timing and synchronizing this process lends itself to smooth numbers, and in particular in this case to 5-smooth numbers. Although the estimated masting intervals for some other species of bamboo are not regular numbers of years, this may be explainable as measurement error.[^n17]
