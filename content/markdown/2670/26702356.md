# Ingram Olkin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ingram Olkin"}

**Ingram Olkin** (July 23, 1924 – April 28, 2016) was a professor emeritus and chair of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and education at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and the [Stanford Graduate School of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Graduate_School_of_Education). He is known for developing statistical analysis for evaluating policies, particularly in education, and for his contributions to [meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329), [statistics education](wikipedia://en/24985094), [multivariate analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_analysis), and [majorization](wikipedia://en/2864495) theory.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Olkin was born in 1924 in [Waterbury, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterbury,_Connecticut).[^o4] He received a B.S. in mathematics at the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York), an M.A. from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), and his Ph.D. from the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina). Olkin also studied with [Harold Hotelling](wikipedia://en/935621). Olkin's advisor was [S. N. Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._N._Roy) and his Ph.D. thesis was "On distribution problems in multivariate analysis" submitted in 1951.

Olkin died from complications of [colorectal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorectal_cancer) at his home in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California) on April 28, 2016, aged 91.[^o5]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Olkin was awarded the fourth biennial Elizabeth Scott Award in 1998 from the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) for his achievements in supporting women in statistics. Of the 17 recipients thus far, he is the only man.

In 1962 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o7] In 1984, he was President of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics). In 1991 Olkin was awarded the [American Statistical Association's Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224).[^o8] Olkin is a Guggenheim, Fulbright, and Lady Davis Fellow, with an honorary doctorate from [De Montfort University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Montfort_University).

## Publications and editing {#Publications_and_editing}

Olkin has written many books including *Statistical methods for meta-analysis*, *Probability theory*, and *Education in a Research University*. Olkin's coauthors include [S. S. Shrikhande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._S._Shrikhande) and [Larry V. Hedges](wikipedia://en/19653610). Olkin has written two books with Albert W. Marshall, *Inequalities: Theory of [Majorization](wikipedia://en/2864495) and its Applications* (1979) and *[Life distributions](wikipedia://en/419259): Structure of nonparametric, semiparametric, and parametric families* (2007). In [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369) and [decision theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_decision_theory), Olkin wrote *Selecting and ordering populations: A new statistical methodology* with [Jean Dickinson Gibbons](wikipedia://en/55599228) and Milton Sobel (1977, 1999).

Ingram was Editor of the *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* and served as the first editor of the *[Annals of Statistics](wikipedia://en/22160768),* both published by the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics). He was a primary force in the founding of the *[Journal of Educational Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Statistics),* which is published with the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988). Olkin was also an editor with the mathematics journal, *Linear Algebra and its Applications*, and has been active in supporting a series of international conferences on [matrix theory](wikipedia://en/20556859), [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).
