# Balayage

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Balayage"}

In [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), a mathematical discipline, **balayage** (from French: *balayage* "scanning, sweeping") is a method devised by [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) for reconstructing an [harmonic function](wikipedia://en/55762) in a domain from its values on the boundary of the domain.

:::

In modern terms, the **balayage operator** maps a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $\mu$ on a closed domain $D$ to a measure $\nu$ on the boundary $\partial D$, so that the [Newtonian potentials](wikipedia://en/814816) of $\mu$ and $\nu$ coincide outside $\bar D$. The procedure is called balayage since the mass is "swept out" from $D$ onto the boundary.

For $x$ in $D$, the balayage of [$\delta_x$](wikipedia://en/37021) yields the [harmonic measure](wikipedia://en/12141074) $\nu_x$ corresponding to $x$. Then the value of a harmonic function $f$ at $x$ is equal to

$$f(x) = \int_{\partial D} f(y) \, d\nu_x(y).$$

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: VFPt\_imagecharge\_plane\_horizontal\_plusminus.svg.* The field of a positive charge above a flat conducting surface, found by the method of images.

In gravity, [Newton's shell theorem](wikipedia://en/2025989) is an example. Consider a uniform mass distribution within a solid ball $B$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The balayage of this mass distribution onto the surface of the ball (a sphere, $\partial B$) results in a uniform surface mass density. The gravitational potential outside the ball is identical for both the original solid ball and the swept-out surface mass.

In electrostatics, the [method of image charges](wikipedia://en/4404564) is an example of "reverse" balayage. Consider a point charge $q$ located at a distance $d$ from an infinite, grounded conducting plane. The effect of the charges on the conducting plane can be "reverse balayaged" to a single "image charge" of $-q$ at the mirror image position with respect to the plane.

:::
