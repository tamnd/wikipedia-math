# Metrodorus (grammarian)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Metrodorus"}

**Metrodorus** (Μητρόδωρος; fl. c. 6th century) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks) [grammarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammarian_%28Greco-Roman_world%29) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who collected mathematical [epigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigrams) which appear in the *[Greek Anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Anthology)*.

:::

Nothing is known about the life of Metrodorus. The time he lived is not certain: he may have lived as early as the 3rd century AD, but it is more likely that he lived in the time of the emperors [Anastasius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasius_I_%28emperor%29) and [Justin I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_I), in the early 6th century.[^o2]

His name occurs in connection with 45 mathematical [epigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigram) which are to be found in book 14 of the *[Greek Anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Anthology)*.[^o3] Although he may have authored some of the epigrams, it is generally accepted that he collected most of them, and some of them may predate the 5th century BC.[^o2] Many of the epigrams lead to simple equations, and they are of the same type as those found in the [Rhind Mathematical Papyrus](wikipedia://en/9210114) (17th century BC).[^o4] Among the problems Metrodorus collected are:[^o4]

- Twenty-three simple equations with one unknown, one of which is the famous epigram which reveals the age of [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110).[^o5]
- Twelve are easy [simultaneous equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_equation) with two unknowns.
- One gives a simultaneous equation with three unknowns.[^o6]
- Six are problems about filling and emptying vessels by pipes.
