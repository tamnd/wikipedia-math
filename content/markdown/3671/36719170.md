# Symmetrizable compact operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symmetrizable compact operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **symmetrizable compact operator** is a [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) that can be composed with a [positive operator](wikipedia://en/454936) with trivial kernel to produce a [self-adjoint operator](wikipedia://en/187408). Such operators arose naturally in the work on integral operators of Hilbert, Korn, Lichtenstein and Marty required to solve [elliptic boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/10468961) on bounded domains in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). Between the late 1940s and early 1960s the techniques, previously developed as part of classical [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), were abstracted within [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) by various mathematicians, including [M. G. Krein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._G._Krein), William T. Reid, [Peter Lax](wikipedia://en/486365) and [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084). [Fredholm theory](wikipedia://en/279722) already implies that any element of the [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum) is an [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue). The main results assert that the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of these operators is similar to that of compact self-adjoint operators: any [spectral value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum) is real; they form a sequence tending to zero; any [generalized eigenvector](wikipedia://en/1137612) is an [eigenvector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector); and the eigenvectors span a dense subspace of the Hilbert space.

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

Let *H* be a Hilbert space. A compact operator *K* on *H* is **symmetrizable** if there is a bounded self-adjoint operator *S* on *H* such that *S* is positive with trivial kernel, i.e. (*Sx*,*x*) > 0 for all non-zero *x*, and *SK* is self-adjoint:

$$\displaystyle{SK=K^*S.}$$

In many applications *S* is also compact. The operator *S* defines a new inner product on *H*

$$\displaystyle{(x,y)_S= (Sx,y)}.$$

Let *H*<sub>S</sub> be the Hilbert space completion of *H* with respect to this inner product.

The operator *K* defines a formally self-adjoint operator on the dense subspace *H* of *H*<sub>*S*</sub>. As [Krein (1947)](wikipedia://en/1190470) and (Reid 1951) noted, the operator has the same operator norm as *K*. In fact[^o4] the self-adjointness condition implies

$$\displaystyle{SK^n = (K^*)^nS.}$$

It follows by induction that, if (*x*,*x*)<sub>*S*</sub> = 1, then

$$\displaystyle{\|Kx\|_S|^{2^n}\le \|K^{2^{n}}x\|_S.}$$

Hence

$$\displaystyle{\|Kx\|_S\le \limsup_{n\rightarrow \infty} \|K\| (\|S\|\|x\|^2)^{1/2^n} =\|K\|}$$

If *K* is only compact, Krein gave an argument, invoking Fredholm theory, to show that *K* defines a compact operator on *H*<sub>*S*</sub>. A shorter argument is available if *K* belongs to a [Schatten class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schatten_class).

When *K* is a [Hilbert–Schmidt operator](wikipedia://en/976673), the argument proceeds as follows. Let *R* be the unique positive square root of *S* and for ε > 0 define[^o5]

$$\displaystyle{A_\varepsilon = (R+\varepsilon I)^{-1}SK(R+\varepsilon I)^{-1}.}$$

These are self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt operator on *H* which are uniformly bounded in the Hilbert–Schmidt norm:

$$\displaystyle{\|A_\varepsilon\|_2^2=(R^2(R+\varepsilon I)^{-2}K, KR^2(R+\varepsilon I)^{-2})_2\le \|K\|_2^2,}$$

Since the Hilbert–Schmidt operators form a Hilbert space, there is a subsequence converging weakly to s self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt operator *A*. Since *A*<sub>ε</sub> *R* tends to *RK* in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, it follows that

$$\displaystyle{RK=AR.}$$

Thus if *U* is the unitary induced by *R* between *H*<sub>*S*</sub> and *H*, then the operator *K*<sub>*S*</sub> induced by the restriction of *K* corresponds to *A* on *H*:

$$\displaystyle{UK_SU^*=A.}$$

The operators *K* − *λI* and *K*\* − *λI* are [Fredholm operators](wikipedia://en/279722) of index 0 for λ ≠ 0, so any spectral value of *K* or *K*\* is an eigenvalue and the corresponding eigenspaces are finite-dimensional. On the other hand, by the special theorem for compact operators, *H* is the orthogonal direct sum of the eigenspaces of *A*, all finite-dimensional except possibly for the 0 eigenspace. Since *RA* = *K*\* *R*, the image under *R* of the λ eigenspace of *A* lies in the λ eigenspace of *K*\*. Similarly *R* carries the λ eigenspace of *K* into the λ eigenspace of *A*. It follows that the eigenvalues of *K* and *K*\* are all real. Since *R* is injective and has dense range it induces isomorphisms between the λ eigenspaces of *A*, *K* and *K*\*. The same is true for generalized eigenvalues since powers of *K* − *λI* and *K*\* − *λI* are also Fredholm of index 0. Since any generalized λ eigenvector of *A* is already an eigenvector, the same is true for *K* and *K*\*. For λ = 0, this argument shows that *K*<sup>*m*</sup>*x* = 0 implies *Kx* = 0.

Finally the eigenspaces of *K*\* span a dense subspace of *H*, since it contains the image under *R* of the corresponding space for *A*. The above arguments also imply that the eigenvectors for non-zero eigenvalues of *K*<sub>*S*</sub> in *H*<sub>*S*</sub> all lie in the subspace *H*.

Hilbert–Schmidt operators *K* with non-zero real eigenvalues λ<sub>*n*</sub> satisfy the following identities proved by (Carleman 1921):

$$\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}\, K^2 = \sum \lambda_n^2,\,\,\, \det (I-zK^2) =\prod_{n=1}^\infty (1-z\lambda_n^2).}$$

Here tr is the trace on [trace-class operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace-class_operator) and det is the [Fredholm determinant](wikipedia://en/2685562). For symmetrizable Hilbert–Schmidt operators the result states that the trace or determinant for *K* or *K*\* is equal to the trace or determinant for *A*. For symmetrizable operators, the identities for *K*\* can be proved by taking *H*<sub>0</sub> to be the kernel of *K*\* and *H*<sub>m</sub> the finite dimensional eigenspaces for the non-zero eigenvalues λ<sub>*m*</sub>. Let *P*<sub>*N*</sub> be the orthogonal projection onto the direct sum of *H*<sub>*m*</sub> with 0 ≤ *m* ≤ *N*. This subspace is left invariant by *K*\*. Although the sum is not orthogonal the restriction *P*<sub>*N*</sub>*K*\**P*<sub>*N*</sub> of *K*\* is similar by a bounded operator with bounded inverse to the diagonal operator on the orthogonal direct sum with the same eigenvalues. Thus

$$\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}\,(P_NK^*P_N)^2 =\sum_{m=1}^N \lambda_m^2 \cdot \mathrm{dim}\, H_m,\,\,\, \mathrm{det}\, [P_N - z(P_NK^*P_N)^2] = \prod_{m=1}^N (1-z\lambda_m^2)^{\mathrm{dim}\, H_m}.}$$

Since *P*<sub>*N*</sub>*K*\**P*<sub>*N*</sub> tends to *K*\* in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, the identities for *K*\* follow by passing to the limit as *N* tends to infinity.
