# Anatoli Prudnikov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Anatoli Prudnikov"}

**Anatolii Platonovich Prudnikov** (Анатолий Платонович Прудников; 14 January 1927 in [Ulyanovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulyanovsk), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) – 10 January 1999) was a Russian mathematician.

:::

In 1930 the Prudnikov family moved to [Samara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara,_Russia), where Anatolii passed his *Abitur* in 1944. He then studied at the [Kuibyshev Aviation Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuibyshev_Aviation_Institute) for three years and at the [Kuibyshev Pedagogical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuibyshev_Pedagogical_Institute) for one year before completing his degree qualifying him as a teacher. In 1968 he received his doctorate under the direction of [Vitalii Arsenievich Ditkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitalii_Arsenievich_Ditkin) with the thesis *On a class of integral transforms of Volterra type and some generalizations of operational calculus*.[^o3] With Ditkin, he published several handbooks on [integral transforms](wikipedia://en/430912) and [operational calculus](wikipedia://en/8950551). Prudnikov's fame derives mainly from the five-volume work "Integrals and Series" (1981–1992), written with [Yuri Aleksandrovich Brychkov](wikipedia://en/54869248) and [Oleg Igorevich Marichev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Igorevich_Marichev).[^o4]
