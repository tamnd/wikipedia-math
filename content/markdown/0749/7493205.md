# Prime triplet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime triplet"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **prime triplet** is a set of three [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) in which the smallest and largest of the three differ by 6. In particular, the sets must have the form $(p, p + 2, p + 6)$ or $(p, p + 4, p + 6)$.[^o2] With the exceptions of (2, 3, 5) and (3, 5, 7), this is the closest possible [grouping](wikipedia://en/11421646) of three prime numbers, since one of every three sequential [odd numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_number) is a multiple of three, and hence not prime (except for 3 itself).

:::

## Subpairs of primes {#Subpairs_of_primes}

A prime triplet contains a single pair of:

- [Twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997): $(p, p + 2)$ or $(p + 4, p + 6)$;
- [Cousin primes](wikipedia://en/343085): $(p, p + 4)$ or $(p + 2, p + 6)$; and
- [Sexy primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexy_prime): $(p, p + 6)$.

## Higher-order versions {#Higher-order_versions}

A prime can be a member of up to three prime triplets - for example, 103 is a member of (97, 101, 103), (101, 103, 107) and (103, 107, 109). When this happens, the five involved primes form a [prime quintuplet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_quintuplet).

A [prime quadruplet](wikipedia://en/812561) $(p, p + 2, p + 6, p + 8)$ contains two overlapping prime triplets, $(p, p + 2, p + 6)$ and $(p + 2, p + 6, p + 8)$.

## Conjecture on prime triplets {#Conjecture_on_prime_triplets}

Similarly to the [twin prime conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_prime_conjecture), it is conjectured that there are infinitely many prime triplets. The first known [gigantic prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigantic_prime) triplet was found in 2008 by Norman Luhn and François Morain. The primes are $(p, p + 2, p + 6)$ with $p$ = 2072644824759 × 2${}^{33333}$ − 1. the largest known [proven](wikipedia://en/2549312) prime triplet contains primes with 20008 digits, namely the primes $(p, p + 2, p + 6)$ with $p$ = 4111286921397 × 2${}^{66420}$ − 1.[^o9]

The [Skewes number](wikipedia://en/250178#Equivalent_for_prime_k-tuples) for the triplet $(p, p + 2, p + 6)$ is 87613571, and for the triplet $(p, p + 4, p + 6)$ it is 337867.[^o10]
