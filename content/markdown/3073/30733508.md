# Aleksandrov–Rassias problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aleksandrov–Rassias problem"}

The theory of [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) in the framework of [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces) has its beginning in a paper by [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162) and [Stanisław M. Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam) in 1932.[^o2] They proved the [Mazur–Ulam theorem](wikipedia://en/3794727) stating that every isometry of a [normed real linear space](wikipedia://en/21538) onto a normed real linear space is a [linear mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_mapping) up to translation. In 1970, [Aleksandr Danilovich Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Danilovich_Aleksandrov) asked whether the existence of a single distance that is preserved by a mapping implies that it is an isometry, as it does for [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) by the [Beckman–Quarles theorem](wikipedia://en/7580572). [Themistocles M. Rassias](wikipedia://en/24914337) posed the following problem:

:::

> **Aleksandrov–Rassias Problem.** If $X$ and $Y$ are normed linear spaces and if $T : X \to Y$ is a continuous and/or surjective mapping such that whenever vectors $x$ and $y$ in $X$ satisfy $\lVert x-y \rVert=1$, then $\lVert T(x)-T(y) \rVert=1$ (the **distance one preserving property** or **DOPP**), is $T$ then necessarily an isometry?[^o3]

There have been several attempts in the mathematical literature by a number of researchers for the solution to this problem.
