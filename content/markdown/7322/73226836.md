# Buffered probability of exceedance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Buffered probability of exceedance"}

**Buffered probability of exceedance** (**bPOE**) is a function of a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) used in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management), including [financial risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_risk). The bPOE is the probability of a tail with known mean value $x$. The figure shows the bPOE at threshold $x$ (marked in red) as the blue shaded area. Therefore, by definition, bPOE is equal to one minus the confidence level at which the [Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR)](wikipedia://en/8307819) is equal to $x$. bPOE is similar to the [probability of exceedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_of_exceedance) of the threshold $x$, but the tail is defined by its mean rather than the lowest point $x$ of the tail.

*Image: BPOE example graph.png.* bPOE shaded in blue for the threshold, x, marked in red.

:::

bPOE has its origins in the concept of *buffered probability of failure (bPOF)*, developed by [R. Tyrrell Rockafellar](wikipedia://en/26300644) and [Johannes Royset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Royset) to measure failure risk.[^o2] It was further developed and defined as the inverse CVaR by Matthew Norton, Stan Uryasev, and Alexander Mafusalov.[^o3][^o4] Similar to CVaR, bPOE considers not only the probability that outcomes (losses) exceed the threshold $x$, but also the magnitude of these outcomes (losses).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

There are two slightly different definitions of bPOE, so called **Lower bPOE** and **Upper bPOE**.

For a random variable, $X$ the Lower bPOE,[^o3][^o4] $\bar{p}_x(X)$, at threshold $x \in [E[X], \sup X ]$ is given by:

$$\bar{p}_x (X) = \min_{a \geq 0} E[ a(X-x) +1 ]^+ = \min_{\gamma <x} \frac{ E[X - \gamma]^+ } { x - \gamma}$$

where $[\cdot]^+ = \max\{\cdot , 0\}$.

bPOE[^o4] can be expressed as the [inverse function](wikipedia://en/14907) of CVaR:

$$\bar{p}_x (X) = \{ 1 - \alpha | \bar{q}_\alpha (X) = x \},$$

where $\bar{q}_\alpha (X)$ is the CVaR of $X$ with confidence level $\alpha$.
