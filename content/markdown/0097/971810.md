# Roger Godement

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roger Godement"}

**Roger Godement** (; 1 October 1921 – 21 July 2016)[^o3] was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) as well as his expository books.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Godement started as a student at the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure) in 1940, where he became a student of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536). He started research into [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) on [locally compact abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454325), finding a number of major results; this work was in parallel but independent of similar investigations in the USSR and Japan. Work on the abstract theory of [spherical functions](wikipedia://en/17708915) published in 1952 proved very influential in subsequent work, particularly that of [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190). The isolation of the concept of [square-integrable representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-integrable_representation) is attributed to him. The [Godement compactness criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godement_compactness_criterion) in the theory of [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741) was a conjecture of his. He later worked with Jacquet on the [zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_function) of a simple algebra.

He was an active member of the [Bourbaki group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbaki_group) in the early 1950s, and subsequently gave a number of significant Bourbaki seminars. He also took part in the Cartan seminar.

His book *Topologie Algébrique et Théorie des Faisceaux* from 1958 was, as he said, a very unoriginal idea for the time (that is, to write an exposition of [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory)); as a non-specialist, he managed to write an enduring classic. It introduced the technical method of [flasque resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flasque_resolution), nowadays called [Godement resolutions](wikipedia://en/17446569). It has also been credited as the place in which a [comonad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comonad) can first be discerned.

He also wrote texts on [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).
