# Simon Donaldson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Simon Donaldson"}

**Sir Simon Kirwan Donaldson** FRS (born 20 August 1957) is an English mathematician known for his work on the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) (differentiable) four-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), [Donaldson–Thomas theory](wikipedia://en/32335603), and his contributions to [Kähler geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_geometry). He is currently a Professor in Pure Mathematics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London). After being a member of the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388) (SCGP) for a decade, he holds Emeritus Professor status at the SCGP and [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University), in New York state.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Donaldson's father was an electrical engineer in the physiology department at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and his mother earned a science degree there.[^o4] Donaldson gained a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in mathematics from [Pembroke College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_College,_Cambridge), in 1979, and in 1980 began postgraduate work at [Worcester College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_College,_Oxford), at first under [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164) and later under [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698)'s supervision. Still a postgraduate student, Donaldson proved in 1982 a result that would establish his fame and later become known as [Donaldson's theorem](wikipedia://en/2180754). He published the result in a paper "Self-dual connections and the topology of smooth 4-manifolds" which appeared in 1983. In the words of Atiyah, the paper "stunned the mathematical world."[^o5]

Whereas [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970) classified topological four-manifolds with [Freedman's classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedman_classification), Donaldson's work focused on four-manifolds admitting a [differentiable structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_structure), using [instantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instantons), a particular solution to the equations of [Yang–Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills) [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) which has its origin in [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267). One of Donaldson's first results gave severe restrictions on the [intersection form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form_%284-manifold%29) of a smooth four-manifold. As a consequence, a large class of the topological four-manifolds do not admit any [smooth structure](wikipedia://en/1542238) at all. Donaldson also derived polynomial invariants from [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155), which became known as [Donaldson invariants](wikipedia://en/11256383). These were new topological invariants sensitive to the underlying smooth structure of the four-manifold. They made it possible to deduce the existence of "exotic" smooth structures—certain topological four-manifolds could carry an infinite family of different smooth structures.

After gaining his [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree from [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) in 1983, Donaldson was appointed a Junior Research Fellow at [All Souls College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls_College,_Oxford). He spent the academic year 1983–84 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), and returned to [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) as [Wallis Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/45546398) in 1985. After spending one year visiting [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University),[^o6] he moved to [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) in 1998 as Professor of Pure Mathematics.[^o7]

In 2014, he joined the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388) at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) in New York, United States.[^o8]

## Awards {#Awards}

Donaldson was an invited speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in 1983,[^o10] and a plenary speaker at the ICM in 1986,[^o11] 1998,[^o12] and 2018.[^o13]

In 1985, Donaldson received the [Junior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Whitehead_Prize) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). In 1994, he was awarded the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in Mathematics. In February 2006, Donaldson was awarded the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize) for science for his work in pure mathematical theories linked to physics, which have helped in forming an understanding of the laws of matter at a subnuclear level. In April 2008, he was awarded the [Nemmers Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1252473), a mathematics prize awarded by [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University).

In 2009, he was awarded the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematics (jointly with [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920)) for their contributions to geometry in 3 and 4 dimensions.[^o14]

In 2014, he was awarded the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933) "for the new revolutionary invariants of 4-dimensional manifolds and for the study of the relation between stability in algebraic geometry and in global differential geometry, both for bundles and for Fano varieties."[^o15]

In January 2019, he was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) (jointly with [Xiuxiong Chen](wikipedia://en/60414667) and [Song Sun](wikipedia://en/60406143)).[^o35] In 2020 he received the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) (jointly with [Yakov Eliashberg](wikipedia://en/20652422)).[^o16]

In 1986, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) and received a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in Berkeley. He became a [Member of the Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Academia_Europaea) (MAE) in 1993.[^o17] In 2010, Donaldson was elected a foreign member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o18]

He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in the 2012 [New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year_Honours) for services to mathematics. In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o20]

In March 2014, he was awarded the degree "Docteur Honoris Causa" by [Université Joseph Fourier, Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Joseph_Fourier,_Grenoble). In January 2017, he was awarded the degree "Doctor Honoris Causa" by the Universidad Complutense de Madrid, Spain.[^o21]

## Research {#Research}

Donaldson's work is on the application of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) (especially the analysis of elliptic [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations)) to problems in geometry. The problems mainly concern [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/64324611), [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795), complex [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298). The following theorems have been mentioned:

- The diagonalizability theorem (): If the [intersection form](wikipedia://en/3148602) of a smooth, closed, simply connected [4-manifold](wikipedia://en/1362795) is positive- or negative-definite then it is diagonalizable over the integers. This result is sometimes called [Donaldson's theorem](wikipedia://en/2180754).
- A smooth [h-cobordism](wikipedia://en/681049) between simply connected 4-manifolds need not be trivial (Donaldson 1987b). This contrasts with the situation in higher dimensions.
- A stable [holomorphic vector bundle](wikipedia://en/4794668) over a non-singular projective [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) admits a [Hermitian–Einstein metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian–Einstein_metric) (Donaldson 1987c), proven using an [inductive proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_proof) and the theory of determinant bundles and [Quillen metrics](wikipedia://en/49946643).[^o26]
- A non-singular, projective algebraic surface can be diffeomorphic to the connected sum of two oriented 4-manifolds only if one of them has negative-definite intersection form (Donaldson 1990). This was an early application of the [Donaldson invariant](wikipedia://en/11256383) (or [instanton](wikipedia://en/296077) invariants).
- Any compact symplectic manifold admits a symplectic [Lefschetz pencil](wikipedia://en/2783111) (Donaldson 1999).

Donaldson's recent work centers on a problem in complex differential geometry concerning a conjectural relationship between algebro-geometric "stability" conditions for smooth projective varieties and the existence of "[extremal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_Kähler_metric)" [Kähler metrics](wikipedia://en/390538), typically those with constant [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) (see for example [cscK metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CscK_metric)). Donaldson obtained results in the toric case of the problem (see for example (Donaldson 2001)). He then solved the [Kähler–Einstein](wikipedia://en/7335270) case of the problem in 2012, in collaboration with Chen and Sun. This latest spectacular achievement involved a number of difficult and technical papers. The first of these was the paper of (Donaldson Sun 2014) on Gromov–Hausdorff limits. The summary of the existence proof for Kähler–Einstein metrics appears in (Chen Donaldson Sun 2014). Full details of the proofs appear in (txt).

### Conjecture on Fano manifolds and Veblen Prize {#Conjecture_on_Fano_manifolds_and_Veblen_Prize}

In 2019, Donaldson was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075), together with [Xiuxiong Chen](wikipedia://en/60414667) and [Song Sun](wikipedia://en/60406143), for proving a long-standing conjecture on [Fano manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_manifolds), which states "that a Fano manifold admits a [Kähler–Einstein metric](wikipedia://en/7335270) if and only if it is [K-stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-Stability)". It had been one of the most actively investigated topics in geometry since its proposal in the 1980s by [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) after he proved the [Calabi conjecture](wikipedia://en/5263012). It was later generalized by [Gang Tian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_Tian) and Donaldson. The solution by Chen, Donaldson and Sun was published in the *[Journal of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)* in 2015 as a three-article series, "Kähler–Einstein metrics on Fano manifolds, I, II and III".[^o35]

:::
