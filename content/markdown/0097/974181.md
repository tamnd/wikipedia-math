# Abelian surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abelian surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **abelian surface** is a 2-dimensional [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152).

:::

One-dimensional [complex tori](wikipedia://en/3055042) are just [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) and are all algebraic, but [Riemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann) discovered that most complex tori of dimension 2 are not algebraic via the [Riemann bilinear relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_bilinear_relations). Essentially, these are conditions on the parameter space of period matrices for complex tori which define an algebraic subvariety. This subvariety contains all of the points whose period matrices correspond to a period matrix of an abelian variety.

The algebraic ones are called abelian surfaces and are exactly the 2-dimensional [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152). Most of their theory is a special case of the theory of higher-dimensional tori or abelian varieties. Finding criteria for a complex torus of dimension 2 to be a product of two elliptic curves (up to [isogeny](wikipedia://en/1085176)) was a popular subject of study in the nineteenth century.

**Invariants:** The [plurigenera](wikipedia://en/1866873#The_plurigenera) are all 1. The surface is [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to *S*<sup>1</sup>×*S*<sup>1</sup>×*S*<sup>1</sup>×*S*<sup>1</sup> so the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) is **Z**<sup>4</sup>.

**[Hodge diamond](wikipedia://en/2164886#Hodge_diamond):** 1

**Examples:** A product of two elliptic curves. The [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of a genus 2 curve.
