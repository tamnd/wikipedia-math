# Multiplication operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplication operator"}

In [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), a **multiplication operator** is a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) $T_{f}$ defined on some [vector space of functions](wikipedia://en/340630) and whose value at a function $\varphi$ is given by multiplication by a fixed function $f$. That is,

$$T_f\varphi(x) = f(x) \varphi (x) \quad$$

for all $\varphi$ in the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of $T_{f}$, and all $x$ in the domain of $\varphi$ (which is the same as the domain of $f$).[^o2]

:::

Multiplication operators generalize the notion of operator given by a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080).[^o3] More precisely, one of the results of [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) is a [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) that states that every [self-adjoint operator](wikipedia://en/187408) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) is [unitarily equivalent](wikipedia://en/187408) to a multiplication operator on an [*L*<sup>*2*</sup> space](wikipedia://en/45194).[^o4]

These operators are often contrasted with [composition operators](wikipedia://en/2702039), which are similarly induced by any fixed function $f$. They are also closely related to [Toeplitz operators](wikipedia://en/14598730), which are [compressions](wikipedia://en/3319942) of multiplication operators on the circle to the [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A multiplication operator $T_f$ on $L^2(X)$, where $X$ is [$\sigma$-finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-finite), is [bounded](wikipedia://en/455961) if and only if $f$ is in $L^\infty(X)$. (The backward direction of the implication does not require the $\sigma$-finiteness assumption.) In this case, its [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722) is equal to $\|f\|_\infty$.[^o2]
- The [adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) of a multiplication operator $T_f$ is $T_\overline{f}$, where $\overline{f}$ is the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) of $f$. As a consequence, $T_f$ is self-adjoint if and only if $f$ is real-valued.[^o7]
- The [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of a bounded multiplication operator $T_f$ is the [essential range](wikipedia://en/18380182) of $f$; outside of this spectrum, the inverse of $(T_f - \lambda)$ is the multiplication operator $T_{\frac{1}{f - \lambda}}.$[^o2]
- Two bounded multiplication operators $T_f$ and $T_g$ on $L^2$ are equal if $f$ and $g$ are equal [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688).[^o7]

:::

## Example {#Example}

Consider the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $X = L^{2}[-1, 3]$ of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued [square integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_integrable) functions on the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) −1, 3. With $f(x) = x^{2}$, define the operator

$$T_f\varphi(x) = x^2 \varphi (x)$$

for any function $\varphi$ in $X$. This will be a [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408) [bounded linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator), with domain all of $X = L^{2}[-1, 3]$ and with [norm](wikipedia://en/246722) $9$. Its [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_of_an_operator) will be the interval 0, 9 (the [range](wikipedia://en/275991) of the function $x\mapsto x^{2}$ defined on −1, 3). Indeed, for any complex number $\lambda$, the operator $T_{f} - \lambda$ is given by

$$(T_f - \lambda)(\varphi)(x) = (x^2-\lambda) \varphi(x).$$

It is [invertible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible_function) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $\lambda$ is not in 0, 9, and then its inverse is

$$(T_f - \lambda)^{-1}(\varphi)(x) = \frac{1}{x^2-\lambda} \varphi(x),$$

which is another multiplication operator.

This example can be easily generalized to characterizing the norm and spectrum of a multiplication operator on any [*L*<sup>*p*</sup> space](wikipedia://en/45194).
