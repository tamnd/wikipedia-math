# Combinatorial group theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Combinatorial group theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **combinatorial group theory** is the theory of [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735), and the concept of a [presentation of a group](wikipedia://en/99494) by [generators](wikipedia://en/3171371) and [relations](wikipedia://en/22517558). It is much used in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) having in a natural and geometric way such a presentation. A very closely related topic is [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), which today largely subsumes combinatorial group theory, using techniques from outside combinatorics besides.

:::

It also comprises a number of [algorithmically insoluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmically_insoluble) problems, most notably the [word problem for groups](wikipedia://en/33563); and the classical [Burnside problem](wikipedia://en/151452).

## History {#History}

See the book by Chandler and Magnus for a detailed history of combinatorial group theory.[^o3]

A proto-form is found in the 1856 [icosian calculus](wikipedia://en/316081) of [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817), where he studied the [icosahedral](wikipedia://en/2877844) [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) via the edge graph of the dodecahedron.

The foundations of combinatorial group theory were laid by [Walther von Dyck](wikipedia://en/752612), student of [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), in the early 1880s, who gave the first systematic study of groups by generators and relations.[^o4]
