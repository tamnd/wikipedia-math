# Isaac Jacob Schoenberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Isaac Jacob Schoenberg"}

**Isaac Jacob Schoenberg** (April 21, 1903 – February 21, 1990) was a Romanian-American mathematician, known for his invention of [splines](wikipedia://en/457680).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Schoenberg was born in [Galați](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galați) to a Jewish family, the youngest of four children. He studied at the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iași), receiving his M.A. in 1922. From 1922 to 1925 he studied at the Universities of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), working on a topic in analytic number theory suggested by [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522). He presented his thesis to the University of Iași, obtaining his Ph.D. in 1926. In Göttingen, he met [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230), who arranged a visit for Schoenberg to the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) in 1928. During this visit, Schoenberg began his work on [total positivity](wikipedia://en/2097524) and variation-diminishing [linear transformations](wikipedia://en/18102). In 1930, he returned from Jerusalem, and married Landau's daughter Charlotte in Berlin.

In 1930, he was awarded a [Rockefeller Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Fellowship), which enabled him to go to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), visiting the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). From 1935, he taught at [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) and [Colby College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby_College). In 1941, he was appointed to the faculty at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). During 1943–1945 he was released from U. Penn. in order to perform war work as a mathematician at the [Aberdeen Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Proving_Ground). It was during this time that he initiated the work for which he is most famous, the theory of [splines](wikipedia://en/457680).

In 1966 he moved to the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) where he became a member of the Mathematics Research Center. He remained there until he retired in 1973. In 1974 he won a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award).[^o4]

## Papers {#Papers}

He wrote about 175 papers on many disparate subjects. Around 50 of these were on [Splines](wikipedia://en/457680). He also wrote on [Approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), the [Kakeya problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakeya_problem), Pólya frequency functions, and a problem of [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230). His coauthors included [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), [Hans Rademacher](wikipedia://en/1864916), [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055), [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya), [A. S. Besicovitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Besicovitch), [Gábor Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065), [Donald J. Newman](wikipedia://en/10419510), [Richard Askey](wikipedia://en/2836040), [Bernard Epstein](wikipedia://en/50917856) and [Carl de Boor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_de_Boor).
