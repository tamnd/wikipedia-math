# Victor A. Vyssotsky

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Victor A. Vyssotsky"}

**Victor Alexander Vyssotsky** (February 26, 1931 – December 24, 2012) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). He is best known for his work on [Multics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multics) and for co-creating *[Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_%28video_game%29)*, the first [programming game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_game).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Victor Alexander Vyssotsky was born on February 26, 1931, in [Charlottesville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia), to astronomers [Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Vyssotsky) and [Emma Vyssotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Vyssotsky) ( Williams).[^o8][^o9]

He was the technical head of the [Multics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multics) project at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) and later executive director of Research in the Information Systems Division of AT&T Bell Labs. Multics directly inspired [Ken Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Thompson_%28computer_programmer%29) to develop [Unix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix).[^o10] Later, Vyssotsky was the founding director of Digital's Cambridge Research Lab.

Vyssotsky co-created the BLODI Block Diagram Compiler in 1960 at Bell Labs.[^o4] In 1961, together with [Robert Morris](wikipedia://en/230338) and [Douglas McIlroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_McIlroy), he co-created *[Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_%28video_game%29)*, the first [programming game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_game),[^o5] on an [IBM 7090](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_7090) at Bell Labs.[^o6]

Vyssotsky died on December 24, 2012, in [Orleans, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orleans,_Massachusetts).
