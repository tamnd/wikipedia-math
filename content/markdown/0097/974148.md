# Automorphic function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Automorphic function"}

In mathematics, an **automorphic function** is a function on a space that is invariant under the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of some [group](wikipedia://en/19447), in other words a function on the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213). Often the space is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) and the group is a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536).

:::

## Factor of automorphy {#Factor_of_automorphy}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the notion of **factor of automorphy** arises for a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a [complex-analytic manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex-analytic_manifold). Suppose a group $G$ acts on a complex-analytic manifold $X$. Then, $G$ also acts on the space of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) from $X$ to the complex numbers. A function $f$ is termed an *[automorphic form](wikipedia://en/782146)* if the following holds:

$$f(g.x) = j_g(x)f(x)$$

where $j_g(x)$ is an everywhere nonzero holomorphic function. Equivalently, an automorphic form is a function whose divisor is invariant under the action of $G$.

The *factor of automorphy* for the automorphic form $f$ is the function $j$. An *automorphic function* is an automorphic form for which $j$ is the identity.

Some facts about factors of automorphy:

- Every factor of automorphy is a [cocycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocycle_%28algebraic_topology%29) for the action of $G$ on the multiplicative group of everywhere nonzero holomorphic functions.
- The factor of automorphy is a [coboundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coboundary) if and only if it arises from an everywhere nonzero automorphic form.
- For a given factor of automorphy, the space of automorphic forms is a vector space.
- The pointwise product of two automorphic forms is an automorphic form corresponding to the product of the corresponding factors of automorphy.

Relation between factors of automorphy and other notions:

- Let $\Gamma$ be a lattice in a Lie group $G$. Then, a factor of automorphy for $\Gamma$ corresponds to a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) on the quotient group $G/\Gamma$. Further, the automorphic forms for a given factor of automorphy correspond to sections of the corresponding line bundle.

The case of $G$ a subgroup of $SL_2(\mathbb{R})$, acting on the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826), is treated in the article on [automorphic factors](wikipedia://en/11207569). In particular, automorphic functions for the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019) $G=SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ are called [modular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_function).
