# Plane curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plane curve"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **plane curve** is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) in a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) that may be a [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/19469852), an [affine plane](wikipedia://en/30764139) or a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350). The most frequently studied cases are smooth plane curves (including [piecewise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise) smooth plane curves), and [algebraic plane curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_plane_curve). Plane curves also include the [Jordan curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_curve) (curves that enclose a region of the plane but need not be smooth) and the [graphs of continuous functions](wikipedia://en/87352).

:::

## Symbolic representation {#Symbolic_representation}

A plane curve can often be represented in [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) by an [implicit equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implicit_equation) of the form $f(x,y)=0$ for some specific function *f*. If this equation can be solved explicitly for *y* or *x* – that is, rewritten as $y=g(x)$ or $x=h(y)$ for specific function *g* or *h* – then this provides an alternative, explicit, form of the representation. A plane curve can also often be represented in Cartesian coordinates by a [parametric equation](wikipedia://en/576108) of the form $(x,y)=(x(t), y(t))$ for specific functions $x(t)$ and $y(t).$

Plane curves can sometimes also be represented in alternative [coordinate systems](wikipedia://en/81931), such as [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates) that express the location of each point in terms of an angle and a distance from the origin.

## Smooth plane curve {#Smooth_plane_curve}

A smooth plane curve is a curve in a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) Euclidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ and is a one-dimensional [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold). This means that a smooth plane curve is a plane curve which "locally looks like a [line](wikipedia://en/946975)", in the sense that near every point, it may be mapped to a line by a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function). Equivalently, a smooth plane curve can be given locally by an equation $f(x, y) = 0,$ where $f: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ is a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function), and the [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) $\partial f/\partial x$ and $\partial f/\partial y$ are never both 0 at a point of the curve.

## Algebraic plane curve {#Algebraic_plane_curve}

An [algebraic plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_plane_curve) is a curve in an [affine](wikipedia://en/30764139) or [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) given by one polynomial equation $f(x,y) = 0$ (or $F(x,y,z) = 0,$ where $F$ is a [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977), in the projective case.)

Algebraic curves have been studied extensively since the 18th century.

Every algebraic plane curve has a degree, the [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) of the defining equation, which is equal, in case of an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018), to the number of intersections of the curve with a line in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736). For example, the circle given by the equation $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ has degree 2.

The [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) plane algebraic curves of degree 2 are called [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673), and their [projective completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_completion) are all [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the projective completion of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ (that is the projective curve of equation $x^2 + y^2 - z^2 = 0$). The plane curves of degree 3 are called [cubic plane curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_plane_curve) and, if they are non-singular, [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). Those of degree 4 are called [quartic plane curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve).
