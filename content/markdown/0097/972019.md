# Zigzag

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zigzag"}

*Image: Regular zig-zag.svg.* Drawing of a zigzag

*Image: Switchback road mitsugo.JPG.* A road in [Akiruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiruno,_Tokyo), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) designed with switchbacks

*Image: Kinemetrics seismograph.jpg.* A seismograph showing zigzag lines

A **zigzag** is a [pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern) like a row of Ws joined together, consisting of a single line made up of line segments of usually constant length joined by usually constant angles in alternating directions.

:::

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), this pattern is described as a [skew apeirogon](wikipedia://en/45397992). From the point of view of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741), a regular zigzag can be generated from a simple motif like a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) by repeated application of a [glide reflection](wikipedia://en/412984).

Although the origin of the word is unclear, its first printed appearances were in French-language books and [ephemera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephemera) of the late 17th century.[^o2]

## Examples of zigzags {#Examples_of_zigzags}

- In [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering), a *switchback* is a technique used since antiquity — in places including [Machu Picchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machu_Picchu) — to build a road or path along steep terrain by zigzagging laterally across a slope.[^o4]
- The trace of a [triangle wave](wikipedia://en/52151) or a [sawtooth wave](wikipedia://en/52147) is a zigzag.
- [Pinking shears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinking_shears) are designed to cut cloth or paper with a zigzag edge, to lessen fraying.[^o5]
- In sewing, a *[zigzag stitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zigzag_stitch)* is a [machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_machine) stitch in a zigzag pattern.[^o6]
- The zigzag arch is an architectural embellishment used in [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_architecture), [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_architecture) and [Romanesque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture).[^o7][^o8]
- In [seismology](wikipedia://en/28935), earthquakes recorded in a "zigzag line" form by using [seismograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismograph).[^o9]
