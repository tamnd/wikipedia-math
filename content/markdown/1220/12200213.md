# Hajós's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hajós's theorem"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), **Hajós's theorem** states that if a [finite abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_abelian_group) is expressed as the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781), that is, sets of the form $\{e,a,a^2,\dots,a^{s-1}\}$ where $e$ is the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962), then at least one of the factors is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011). The theorem was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by the Hungarian mathematician [György Hajós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/György_Hajós) in 1941 using [group rings](wikipedia://en/349223). [Rédei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/László_Rédei) later proved the statement when the factors are only required to contain the identity element and be of prime [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174). Rédei's proof of Hajós's theorem was simplified by [Tibor Szele](wikipedia://en/22826039).

:::

*Image: Shifted square tiling.svg.* In this lattice tiling of the plane by [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) squares, the green and violet squares meet edge-to-edge, as do the blue and orange squares.

An equivalent statement on homogeneous linear forms was originally [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) by [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537). A consequence is Minkowski's conjecture on lattice [tilings](wikipedia://en/321671), which says that in any lattice tiling of [space](wikipedia://en/9697) by [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285), there are two cubes that meet face to face. [Keller's conjecture](wikipedia://en/33297462) is the same conjecture for non-lattice tilings, which turns out to be false in high dimensions.
