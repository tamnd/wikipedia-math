# William Reginald Dean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Reginald Dean"}

**William Reginald Dean** (1896–1973) was a British [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [fluid dynamicist](wikipedia://en/11034). His research interests included [Stokes flow](wikipedia://en/2047298), [solid mechanics](wikipedia://en/458866), and flow in curved channels. The [Dean number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_number) bears his name.

:::

Dean carried out pioneering work in the study of fluid flow at low [Reynolds numbers](wikipedia://en/23868856), by applying methods from [elasticity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_theory). Some of his more famous results include solutions for secondary flow in curved tubes, for the perturbation to shear flow near a wall caused by a gap in the wall, and for flow in a corner.[^o2]

Dean was an undergraduate at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). He spent five years at [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London), and was later a fellow of Trinity College. During the war he undertook mathematical work as part of the *Anti-Aircraft Experimental Section of M.I.D.*[^o3] He also held the Goldsmid Chair in Applied Mathematics at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) (from which he retired in 1964),[^o4] and a chair at the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona).[^o2]
