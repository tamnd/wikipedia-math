# Bounded expansion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bounded expansion"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a family of [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) is said to have **bounded expansion** if all of its [shallow minors](wikipedia://en/38768125) are [sparse graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_graph). Many natural families of sparse graphs have bounded expansion. A closely related but stronger property, **polynomial expansion**, is equivalent to the existence of [separator theorems](wikipedia://en/18978005) for these families. Families with these properties have efficient [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for problems including the [subgraph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/450062) and [model checking](wikipedia://en/321157) for the first order theory of graphs.

:::

## Definition and equivalent characterizations {#Definition_and_equivalent_characterizations}

A *t*-shallow minor of a graph *G* is defined to be a graph formed from *G* by contracting a collection of vertex-disjoint subgraphs of radius *t*, and deleting the remaining vertices of *G*. A family of graphs has bounded expansion if there exists a function *f* such that, in every *t*-shallow minor of a graph in the family, the ratio of edges to vertices is at most *f*(*t*).[^o3]

Equivalent definitions of classes of bounded expansions are that all shallow minors have [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) bounded by a function of *t*,[^o3] or that the given family has a bounded value of a *topological parameter*. Such a parameter is a [graph invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_invariant) that is monotone under taking subgraphs, such that the parameter value can change only in a controlled way when a graph is subdivided, and such that a bounded parameter value implies that a graph has bounded [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Polynomial expansion and separator theorems"}

## Polynomial expansion and separator theorems {#Polynomial_expansion_and_separator_theorems}

A stronger notion is **polynomial expansion**, meaning that the function *f* used to bound the edge density of shallow minors is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000). If a hereditary graph family obeys a [separator theorem](wikipedia://en/18978005), stating that any *n*-vertex graph in the family can be split into pieces with at most *n*/2 vertices by the removal of *O*(*n*<sup>*c*</sup>) vertices for some constant *c* \< 1, then that family necessarily has polynomial expansion. Conversely, graphs with polynomial expansion have sublinear separator theorems.[^o7]

:::

## Classes of graphs with bounded expansion {#Classes_of_graphs_with_bounded_expansion}

Because of the connection between separators and expansion, every [minor-closed graph family](wikipedia://en/351769), including the family of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), has polynomial expansion. The same is true for [1-planar graphs](wikipedia://en/38249632), and more generally the graphs that can be embedded onto surfaces of bounded [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) with a bounded number of crossings per edge, as well as the [biclique-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biclique-free_graph) [string graphs](wikipedia://en/16081202), since these all obey similar separator theorems to the planar graphs.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12] In higher dimensional [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697), [intersection graphs](wikipedia://en/7726759) of systems of balls with the property that any point of space is covered by a bounded number of balls also obey separator theorems[^o13] that imply polynomial expansion.

Although graphs of bounded [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) do not have sublinear separators,[^o14] they also have bounded expansion.[^o15] Other graphs of bounded expansion include graphs of bounded degree,[^o16] [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477) of bounded average degree in the [Erdős–Rényi model](wikipedia://en/10477221),[^o17] and graphs of bounded [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215).[^o4][^o18]

## Algorithmic applications {#Algorithmic_applications}

Instances of the [subgraph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/450062) in which the goal is to find a target graph of bounded size, as a subgraph of a larger graph whose size is not bounded, may be solved in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) when the larger graph belongs to a family of graphs of bounded expansion. The same is true for [finding cliques](wikipedia://en/249254) of a fixed size, finding [dominating sets](wikipedia://en/1747972) of a fixed size, or more generally testing properties that can be expressed by a formula of bounded size in the first-order [logic of graphs](wikipedia://en/47857242).[^o20][^o21]

For graphs of polynomial expansion, there exist [polynomial-time approximation schemes](wikipedia://en/666431) for the [set cover problem](wikipedia://en/870399), [maximum independent set problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_independent_set_problem), [dominating set problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominating_set_problem), and several other related graph optimization problems.[^o22]
