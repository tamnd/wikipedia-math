# Jesús A. De Loera

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jesús A. De Loera"}

**Jesús Antonio De Loera** (born January 18, 1966) is a Mexican-American mathematician at the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis), specializing in [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) and [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

De Loera did his undergraduate studies at the [National Autonomous University of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Autonomous_University_of_Mexico), earning a B.S. in mathematics in 1989. After earning a master's degree from [Western Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Michigan_University) a year later, and another master's degree in 1993 from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), he finished his doctorate from Cornell in 1995 under the supervision of [Bernd Sturmfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Sturmfels).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

De Loera joined the UC Davis faculty as an assistant professor in 1998, and was promoted to full professor in 2005.[^o4] He served on the scientific advisory board for the [Institute for Computational and Experimental Research in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31112547) (ICERM) through 2021.[^o8]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2014, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to discrete geometry and combinatorial optimization as well as for service to the profession, including mentoring and diversity".[^o10] In 2019 he was named a [SIAM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Fellow) "for contributions to discrete geometry and optimization, polynomial algebra, and mathematical software".[^o11] In 2020 he received the [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFORMS) optimization society's Farkas Prize "in recognition of his pioneering work at the intersection of discrete mathematics, optimization and algebraic geometry".[^o12] He delivered an invited plenary address at the 2021 National Math Festival.[^o13] In 2021, he was elected vice president of the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14]
