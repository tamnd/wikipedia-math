# Mara Neusel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mara Neusel"}

*Image: Mara Neusel - Alter Luisenstädtischer Friedhof (2).jpg.* Grave of Mara Neusel in Berlin

**Mara Dicle Neusel** (May 14, 1964 – September 5, 2014) was a mathematician, author, teacher and an advocate for [women in mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_mathematics). The focus of her mathematical work was on [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762), which can be briefly described as the study of [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) and their [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738).

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Mara Neusel was born in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), Germany, one of two children of Günter and Aylâ (Helvacioglu) Neusel. In 2001 she became the fourth woman to earn the advanced degree *venia legendi* ([Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation)) in mathematics from the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), following in the footsteps of the first woman mathematician to be awarded the venia legend from Göttingen in 1919, [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896).[^o4][^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Professor Neusel was the author of a research monograph, an advanced undergraduate text, and a memoir for the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822): *Invariant Theory and Finite Groups*,[^o7] *Invariant Theory*,[^o8] and *Inverse Invariant Theory and Steenrod Operations*.[^o9] The exposition in the text *Invariant Theory* "stands out by its masterly clarity, comprehensiveness, profundity, and didactical disposition."[^o10] Neusel served on the editorial boards of *Advances in Pure Mathematics* and the *International Journal of Mathematics and Applied Statistics*. A tireless advocate for girls and women in mathematics, Neusel established *Emmy Noether High School Mathematics Days* in May 2003[^o11] which continues to be celebrated with workshops and mathematical competitions.[^o12] She co-founded the Young Women in Mathematics group at Texas Tech and received a diversity grant to support the group.

Neusel began her career at [Texas Tech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Tech_University) in 2002 as an associate professor and was promoted to full professor in 2009.[^o13] She also held visiting appointments at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), and the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame).

Professor Neusel had long term memberships in the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o14] and the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o4] Neusel co-organized a special session on "[Homological Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homological_Algebra) and Its Applications" at the 2005 Meeting of the American Mathematical Society in Lubbock, Texas[^o15] as well as a special session on "Commutative Algebra and Algebraic Geometry" at the 40th Anniversary Celebration of the Association for Women in Mathematics at Brown University in 2011.[^o16]
