# Silas D. Alben

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Silas D. Alben"}

**Silas D. Alben** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of Mathematics and Director of the Applied and Interdisciplinary Mathematics Program at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). His research addresses problems from biology (especially biomechanics) and engineering that can be studied with the tools of applied mathematics and continuum mechanics.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Education {#Education}

Silas Alben attended [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) where he received in 1999 [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degrees in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude).[^o5] In 2000, he joined the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), where he received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Mathematics in 2004. His thesis *Drag Reduction by Self-Similar Bending and a Transition to Forward Flight by a Symmetry-Breaking Instability* was advised by [Michael Shelley](wikipedia://en/32860158).[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Alben's research focuses on problems in [biomechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomechanics), [material science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_science), and fluid mechanics. As a graduate student at NYU, Alben worked with Jun Zhang and Michael Shelley in investigating the [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_%28mechanics%29) of flexible structures and how such structures can become more [aerodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamic) by altering their shape. In this study, experiments visualized a short glass fiber [deforming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_%28engineering%29) in [fluid flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_flow), and analysis showed how the fiber can reduce the [drag force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_force) exerted by the fluid by changing its shape. This work was published 2002 in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* under the title *Drag Reduction Through Self-Similar Bending of a Flexible Body*,[^o8] and was the subject of various news articles in periodicals including *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*[^o9] and others.[^o10] As a Postdoctoral Fellow at Harvard, Alben collaborated with Ernst A. van Nierop and [Michael P. Brenner](wikipedia://en/58843745) in a paper titled "How Bumps on Whale Flippers Delay Stall: An Aerodynamic Model".[^o11] The paper gave a mathematical model for this [hydrodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamic) phenomenon. This result, featured in [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT)'s *[Technology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_Review)*[^o12] and *Nature*,[^o13] provides a theoretical basis for potential improvements in using bumps for more stable [airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane), more agile [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine), and more efficient [turbine blades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbine). In 2007, Alben investigated (with Michael P. Brenner) the self-assembly of 3D structures from flat, elastic sheets. This experiment, featured in *[New Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Scientist)*,[^o14] presented a new technique in [nano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nano_technology) construction; previously, the transformation of flat sheets to 3D structures was performed by [random](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random) formation, but in this study, the addition of biases into the design of the sheets gave the possibility of predicting the resulting shape.
