# Frostman lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frostman lemma"}

**Frostman's lemma** provides a convenient tool for estimating the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) of sets in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more specifically, in the theory of [fractal dimensions](wikipedia://en/285907).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Lemma"}

## Lemma {#Lemma}

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=inline-head name="Lemma"}

**Lemma:** Let *A* be a [Borel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_measurable) subset of **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, and let *s* > 0. Then the following are equivalent:

- *H*<sup>*s*</sup>(*A*) > 0, where *H*<sup>*s*</sup> denotes the *s*-dimensional [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109).
- There is an (unsigned) [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) *μ* on **R**<sup>*n*</sup> satisfying *μ*(*A*) > 0, and such that

$$\mu(B(x,r))\le r^s$$

holds for all *x* ∈ **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and *r*>0.

[Otto Frostman](wikipedia://en/34049712) proved this lemma for closed sets *A* as part of his PhD dissertation at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) in 1935. The generalization to Borel sets is more involved, and requires the theory of [Suslin sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_set).[^o6]

A useful corollary of Frostman's lemma requires the notions of the *s*-capacity of a Borel set *A* ⊂ **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, which is defined by

$$C_s(A):=\sup\Bigl\{\Bigl(\int_{A\times A} \frac{d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\Bigr)^{-1}:\mu\text{ is a Borel measure and }\mu(A)=1\Bigr\}.$$

(Here, we take inf ∅ = ∞ and $\tfrac{1}{\infty}$ = 0. As before, the measure $\mu$ is unsigned.) It follows from Frostman's lemma that for Borel *A* ⊂ **R**<sup>*n*</sup>

$$\mathrm{dim}_H(A)= \sup\{s\ge 0:C_s(A)>0\}.$$

:::
