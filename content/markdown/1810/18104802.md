# Complementarity theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complementarity theory"}

A **complementarity problem** is a type of [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033) problem. It is the problem of optimizing (minimizing or maximizing) a function of two [vector](wikipedia://en/32370) variables subject to certain requirements (constraints) which include: that the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) of the two vectors must equal zero, i.e. they are orthogonal.[^o2] In particular for finite-dimensional real vector spaces this means that, if one has vectors *X* and *Y* with all *nonnegative* components (*x*<sub>*i*</sub> ≥ 0 and *y*<sub>*i*</sub> ≥ 0 for all $i$: in the [first quadrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_quadrant) if 2-dimensional, in the first [octant](wikipedia://en/36160226) if 3-dimensional), then for each pair of components *x*<sub>*i*</sub> and *y*<sub>*i*</sub> one of the pair must be zero, hence the name *complementarity*. e.g. *X* = (1, 0) and *Y* = (0, 2) are complementary, but *X* = (1, 1) and *Y* = (2, 0) are not. A complementarity problem is a special case of a [variational inequality](wikipedia://en/1468817).

:::

## History {#History}

Complementarity problems were originally studied because the [Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions](wikipedia://en/2397362) in [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) and [quadratic programming](wikipedia://en/40324) constitute a [linear complementarity problem](wikipedia://en/1470767) (LCP) or a [mixed complementarity problem](wikipedia://en/6439136) (MCP). In 1963 [Lemke](wikipedia://en/48695353) and [Howson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.T._Howson) showed that, for two person games, computing a [Nash equilibrium](wikipedia://en/45337) point is equivalent to an LCP. In 1968 [Cottle](wikipedia://en/58472183) and [Dantzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._Dantzig) unified linear and quadratic programming and [bimatrix games](wikipedia://en/48843663). Since then the study of complementarity problems and variational inequalities has expanded enormously.

Areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) that contributed to the development of complementarity theory include: [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29), [equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_point) problems, [variational inequality theory](wikipedia://en/1468817), [fixed point theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_theory), [topological degree theory](wikipedia://en/18203720) and [nonlinear analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_analysis).
