# Multiplicative number theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplicative number theory"}

**Multiplicative number theory** is a subfield of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) that deals with [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers) and with [factorization](wikipedia://en/82341) and [divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisors). The focus is usually on developing approximate formulas for counting these objects in various contexts. The [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) is a key result in this subject. The [Mathematics Subject Classification](wikipedia://en/3008091) for multiplicative number theory is 11Nxx.

:::

## Scope {#Scope}

Multiplicative number theory deals primarily in asymptotic estimates for [arithmetic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_functions). Historically the subject has been dominated by the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692), first by attempts to prove it and then by improvements in the error term. The [Dirichlet divisor problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_divisor_problem) that estimates the average order of the [divisor function](wikipedia://en/407249) *d(n)* and [Gauss's circle problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss's_circle_problem) that estimates the average order of the number of representations of a number as a sum of two squares are also classical problems, and again the focus is on improving the error estimates.

The distribution of primes numbers among [residue classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_class) modulo an integer is an area of active research. [Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet's_theorem_on_primes_in_arithmetic_progressions) shows that there are an infinity of primes in each co-prime residue class, and the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions shows that the primes are asymptotically [equidistributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equidistributed) among the residue classes. The [Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem](wikipedia://en/4632596) gives a more precise measure of how evenly they are distributed. There is also much interest in the size of the smallest prime in an arithmetic progression; [Linnik's theorem](wikipedia://en/222405) gives an estimate.

The [twin prime conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_prime_conjecture), namely that there are an infinity of primes *p* such that *p*+2 is also prime, is the subject of active research. [Chen's theorem](wikipedia://en/1400840) shows that there are an infinity of primes *p* such that *p*+2 is either prime or the product of two primes.

## Methods {#Methods}

The methods belong primarily to [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), but elementary methods, especially [sieve methods](wikipedia://en/1035915), are also very important. The [large sieve](wikipedia://en/1967859) and [exponential sums](wikipedia://en/468937) are usually considered part of multiplicative number theory.

The [distribution of prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_prime_numbers) is closely tied to the behavior of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) and the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125), and these subjects are studied both from a [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) viewpoint and a [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) viewpoint.

:::

## Standard texts {#Standard_texts}

A large part of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) deals with multiplicative problems, and so most of its texts contain sections on multiplicative number theory. These are some well-known texts that deal specifically with multiplicative problems:

- Davenport, Harold. (2000). "Multiplicative Number Theory". Springer. isbn:978-0-387-95097-6
- Montgomery, Hugh; Robert C. Vaughan. (2005). "Multiplicative Number Theory I. Classical Theory". Cambridge University Press. isbn:978-0-521-84903-6
