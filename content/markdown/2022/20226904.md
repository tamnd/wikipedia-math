# Demographic economics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Demographic economics"}

**Demographic economics** or **population economics** is the application of [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic) analysis to [demography](wikipedia://en/45642), the study of human [populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_%28human_biology%29), including [size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_size), [growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth), [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density), [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_distribution), and [vital statistics](wikipedia://en/999909).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Aspects {#Aspects}

Aspects of the subject include:

- marriage and [fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate)[^o2][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12]
- the [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_economics)[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18][^o19]
- divorce[^o20][^o21][^o22]
- [morbidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbidity)[^o23] and [life expectancy](wikipedia://en/18669)/mortality[^o24][^o25][^o26]
- [dependency ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependency_ratio)[^o2][^o5][^o27][^o28][^o29]
- migration[^o30][^o31][^o32]
- [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth)[^o33][^o34][^o35][^o36][^o37][^o38][^o39][^o40]
- population size[^o41][^o42][^o43]
- [public policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy#Academic_discipline)[^o2][^o44][^o45][^o46][^o47][^o48][^o49][^o50]
- the [demographic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_transition) from "[population explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_explosion)" to (dynamic) stability[^o51] or [decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_decline).[^o52][^o53][^o54]

Other subfields include measuring [value of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_of_life)[^o55][^o56] and the economics of the elderly[^o57][^o58][^o59] and the handicapped[^o60][^o61][^o62] and of gender,[^o63][^o64][^o65] race, minorities, and non-labor discrimination.[^o66][^o67] In coverage and subfields, it complements [labor economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_economics)[^o68][^o69] and implicates a variety of other economics subjects.[^o70][^o71][^o72]

## Subareas {#Subareas}

The *[Journal of Economic Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Economic_Literature)* classification codes are a way of categorizing subjects in economics. There, demographic economics is paired with [labour economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_economics) as one of [19 primary classifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JEL_classification_codes) at JEL: J.[^o74] It has eight subareas:

General

Demographic Trends and Forecasts

[Marriage; Marital Dissolution; Family Structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_economics)

[Fertility; Family Planning; Child Care; Children; Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_economics)

Economics of the Elderly; Economics of the Handicapped

Economics of Minorities and Races; Non-labor Discrimination

Economics of Gender; Non-labor Discrimination

[Value of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_of_life); Foregone Income

Public Policy
