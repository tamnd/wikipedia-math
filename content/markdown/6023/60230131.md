# Emma Haruka Iwao

::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Emma Haruka Iwao"}

**Emma Haruka Iwao** (born April 21, 1984) is a Japanese [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) and cloud [developer advocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_evangelism) at [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google).[^o19][^o18] In 2019 Haruka Iwao calculated the then world record for most accurate value of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) (π); which included 31.4 trillion digits, exceeding the previous record of 22 trillion.[^o16][^o7][^o8][^o9] This record was surpassed in 2020 by Timothy Mullican who calculated 50 trillion digits,[^o10][^o11] but she reclaimed the record in 2022 with 100 trillion digits.[^o12] The record was surpassed by a computer storage company in 2024, reaching roughly 105 trillion digits.[^o13] She identifies as [queer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer).[^o14]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

As a child, Iwao became interested in pi, learning of the record-breaking computations of her Japanese compatriots [Daisuke Takahashi](wikipedia://en/62467042) and [Yasumasa Kanada](wikipedia://en/561940).[^o16][^o17] She studied [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at the [University of Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tsukuba), where she was taught by Takahashi.[^o18][^o19] She was awarded the [Dean's Award for Excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean's_Award_for_Excellence) in 2008, before starting graduate studies in computing. Her master's thesis considered high performance computer systems.[^o18] After graduating, Iwao took on several software engineering positions, working on site reliability for [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic), [GREE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gree_%28Japanese_social_network%29) and [Red Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hat).[^o19]

## Career {#Career}

Iwao joined [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) as a Cloud Developer Advocate in 2015.[^o16] She originally worked for Google in Tokyo, before moving to [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle) in 2019.[^o4] Iwao offers training in the use of the [Google Cloud Platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Cloud_Platform) (GCP), as well as supporting application developers.[^o21][^o22][^o23] She works to make [cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing) accessible for everyone, creating online demos and teaching materials.[^o24]

In March 2019 Iwao calculated the value of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) to 31,415,926,535,897 digits (equal to $\lfloor \pi \times 10^{13}\rfloor$), using 170 [terabytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terabyte) (TB) of data.[^o16][^o25][^o26][^o27] The calculation used a [multithreaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multithreading_%28computer_architecture%29) program called [y-cruncher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-cruncher) using over 25 machines for 121 days.[^o16][^o28][^o24] This was the first time the record had been set using a cloud computing service, and was achieved at a cost of roughly US\$200,000.[^o29]

In March 2022 she extended the world record to 100 trillion digits of pi.[^o12]
