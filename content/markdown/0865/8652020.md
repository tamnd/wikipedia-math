# Convex body

*Image: POV-Ray-Dodecahedron.svg.* A [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) is a convex body.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Convex body"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **convex body** in $n$-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) with non-[empty](wikipedia://en/9566) [interior](wikipedia://en/55610). Some authors do not require a non-empty interior, merely that the set is non-empty.

:::

A convex body $K$ is called **symmetric** if it is centrally symmetric with respect to the origin; that is to say, a point $x$ lies in $K$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its [antipode](wikipedia://en/367577), $- x$ also lies in $K.$ Symmetric convex bodies are in a [one-to-one correspondence](wikipedia://en/3942) with the [unit balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) of [norms](wikipedia://en/990534) on $\mathbb{R}^n.$

Some commonly known examples of convex bodies are the [Euclidean ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_ball), the [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) and the [cross-polytope](wikipedia://en/716401).

## Metric space structure {#Metric_space_structure}

Write $\mathcal K^n$ for the set of convex bodies in $\mathbb R^n$. Then $\mathcal K^n$ is a [complete metric space](wikipedia://en/7832) with metric [^o3]

$$d(K,L) := \inf\{\epsilon \geq 0 : K \subset L + B^n(\epsilon), L \subset K + B^n(\epsilon) \}.$$

Further, the [Blaschke Selection Theorem](wikipedia://en/11956019) says that every *d*-bounded sequence in $\mathcal K^n$ has a convergent subsequence.[^o3]

## Polar body {#Polar_body}

If $K$ is a bounded convex body containing the origin $O$ in its interior, the **polar body** $K^*$ is $\{u : \langle u,v \rangle \leq 1, \forall v \in K \}$. The polar body has several nice properties including $(K^*)^*=K$, $K^*$ is bounded, and if $K_1\subset K_2$ then $K_2^*\subset K_1^*$. The polar body is a type of [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) relation.
