# Stanton number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stanton number"}

The **Stanton number** ($\operatorname{St}$), is a [dimensionless number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_number) that measures the ratio of heat transferred into a fluid to the [thermal capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_capacity) of fluid. The Stanton number is named after [Thomas Stanton (engineer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Stanton_%28engineer%29) (1865–1931).[^o12][^o11] It is used to characterize [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726) in forced [convection](wikipedia://en/47526) flows.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula"}

## Formula {#Formula}

$$\mathrm{St} = \frac{h}{G c_p} = \frac{h}{\rho u c_p}$$

where

- *h* = [convection](wikipedia://en/47526) [heat transfer coefficient](wikipedia://en/1464363)
- *G* = [mass flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_flux) of the fluid
- *ρ* = [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) of the fluid
- *c<sub>p</sub>* = [specific heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat) of the fluid
- *u* = [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) of the fluid

It can also be represented in terms of the fluid's [Nusselt](wikipedia://en/58285), [Reynolds](wikipedia://en/23868856), and [Prandtl](wikipedia://en/58283) numbers:

$$\mathrm{St} = \frac{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Pr}}$$

where

- Nu is the [Nusselt number](wikipedia://en/58285);
- Re is the [Reynolds number](wikipedia://en/23868856);
- Pr is the [Prandtl number](wikipedia://en/58283).[^o10]

The Stanton number arises in the consideration of the geometric similarity of the momentum [boundary layer](wikipedia://en/224301) and the thermal boundary layer, where it can be used to express a relationship between the [shear force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_force) at the wall (due to [viscous drag](wikipedia://en/18963754)) and the total heat transfer at the wall (due to [thermal diffusivity](wikipedia://en/58282)).

:::

## Mass transfer {#Mass_transfer}

Using the heat-mass transfer analogy, a mass transfer St equivalent can be found using the [Sherwood number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_number) and [Schmidt number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmidt_number) in place of the Nusselt number and Prandtl number, respectively.

$\mathrm{St}_m = \frac{\mathrm{Sh_L}}{\mathrm{Re_L}\,\mathrm{Sc}}$[^o5]

$\mathrm{St}_m = \frac{h_m}{\rho u}$[^o5]

where

- $St_m$ is the mass Stanton number;
- $Sh_L$ is the Sherwood number based on length;
- $Re_L$ is the Reynolds number based on length;
- $Sc$ is the Schmidt number;
- $h_m$ is defined based on a concentration difference (kg s<sup>−1</sup> m<sup>−2</sup>);
- $u$ is the velocity of the fluid

## Boundary layer flow {#Boundary_layer_flow}

The Stanton number is a useful measure of the rate of change of the thermal energy deficit (or excess) in the boundary layer due to heat transfer from a planar surface. If the enthalpy thickness is defined as:[^o14]

$$\Delta_2 = \int_0^\infty \frac{\rho u}{\rho_\infty u_\infty} \frac{T - T_\infty}{T_s - T_\infty} d y$$

Then the Stanton number is equivalent to

$$\mathrm{St} = \frac{d \Delta_2}{d x}$$

for boundary layer flow over a flat plate with a constant surface temperature and properties.[^o13]

### Correlations using Reynolds-Colburn analogy {#Correlations_using_Reynolds-Colburn_analogy}

Using the Reynolds-Colburn analogy for turbulent flow with a thermal log and viscous sub layer model, the following correlation for turbulent heat transfer for is applicable[^o15]

$$\mathrm{St} = \frac{C_f / 2}{1 + 12.8 \left( \mathrm{Pr}^{0.68} - 1 \right) \sqrt{C_f / 2}}$$

where

$$C_f = \frac{0.455}{\left[ \mathrm{ln} \left( 0.06 \mathrm{Re}_x \right) \right]^2}$$
