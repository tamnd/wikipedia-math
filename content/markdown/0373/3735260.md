::: {#o10 .subject cue=lead name="Tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb"}

The **tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb**, **alternated cubic honeycomb** is a quasiregular space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [Euclidean 3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_3-space). It is composed of alternating regular [octahedra](wikipedia://en/80177635) and [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) in a ratio of 1:2.

:::

Other names include **half cubic honeycomb**, **half cubic cellulation**, or **tetragonal disphenoidal cellulation**. [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) called this honeycomb a **tetroctahedrille**, and its dual a [dodecahedrille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombic_dodecahedral_honeycomb).

[R. Buckminster Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Buckminster_Fuller) combined the two words octahedron and tetrahedron into **octet** truss, a rhombohedron consisting of one octahedron (or two square pyramids) and two opposite tetrahedra.

It is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) with 8 [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) and 6 [octahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedra) around each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417). It is [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive) with 2 tetrahedra and 2 octahedra alternating on each edge. The vertex locations of the tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb produce the [face-centered cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-centered_cubic) (FCC) lattice in [crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal).

It is part of an infinite family of [uniform honeycombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_honeycomb) called [alternated hypercubic honeycombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternated_hypercubic_honeycomb), formed as an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) of a hypercubic honeycomb and being composed of [demihypercube](wikipedia://en/6833695) and [cross-polytope](wikipedia://en/716401) facets. It is also part of another infinite family of uniform honeycombs called [simplectic honeycombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplectic_honeycomb).

In this case of 3-space, the [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) is alternated, reducing the cubic cells to tetrahedra, and the deleted vertices create octahedral voids. As such it can be represented by an extended [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) h{4,3,4} as containing *half* the vertices of the {4,3,4} cubic honeycomb.

There is a similar honeycomb called [gyrated tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_tetrahedral-octahedral_honeycomb) which has layers rotated 60 degrees so half the edges have neighboring rather than alternating tetrahedra and octahedra.

The tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb can have its symmetry doubled by placing tetrahedra on the octahedral cells, creating a nonuniform honeycomb consisting of [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) and [octahedra](wikipedia://en/80177635) (as triangular antiprisms). Its vertex figure is an [order-3 truncated triakis tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/8362228#Full_truncation). This honeycomb is the dual of the [triakis truncated tetrahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/37070120), with [triakis truncated tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/37100405) cells.

### Alternated cubic honeycomb slices {#Alternated_cubic_honeycomb_slices}

The *alternated cubic honeycomb* can be sliced into sections, where new square faces are created from inside of the octahedron. Each slice will contain up and downward facing [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) and [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) sitting on their edges. A second slice direction needs no new faces and includes alternating tetrahedral and octahedral. This slab honeycomb is a [scaliform honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaliform_honeycomb) rather than uniform because it has nonuniform cells.

## A3/D3 lattice {#A3/D3_lattice}

Its [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) represents an [A<sub>3</sub> lattice](wikipedia://en/277087#Explicit_construction_of_the_irreducible_root_systems) or *D<sub>3</sub> lattice*.[^o22][^o23] This lattice is known as the [face-centered cubic lattice](wikipedia://en/666697) in crystallography and is also referred to as the [cubic close packed lattice](wikipedia://en/901260#FCC_and_HCP_Lattices) as its vertices are the centers of a close-packing with equal spheres that achieves the highest possible average density. The tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb is the 3-dimensional case of a [simplectic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplectic_honeycomb). Its Voronoi cell is a [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), the dual of the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) vertex figure for the tet-oct honeycomb.

The D+ packing can be constructed by the union of two D<sub>3</sub> (or A<sub>3</sub>) lattices. The D+ packing is only a lattice for even dimensions. The kissing number is 2<sup>2</sup>=4, (2<sup>n−1</sup> for n\<8, 240 for n=8, and 2n(n−1) for n>8).[^o24]

The **runcic cubic honeycomb** or **runcic cubic cellulation** is a uniform space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in Euclidean 3-space. It is composed of [rhombicuboctahedra](wikipedia://en/26213), [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285), and [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) in a ratio of 1:1:2. Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is a [triangular frustum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_frustum), with a tetrahedron on one end, cube on the opposite end, and three rhombicuboctahedra around the trapezoidal sides.

[John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls this honeycomb a **3-RCO-trille**, and its dual [quarter cubille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_cubille).

#### Quarter cubille {#Quarter_cubille}

The dual of a *runcic cubic honeycomb* is called a **quarter cubille**, with [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , with faces in 2 of 4 hyperplanes of the ${\tilde{B}}_4$, \[4,3<sup>1,1</sup>\] symmetry fundamental domain.

Cells can be seen as 1/4 of [dissected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) cube, using 4 vertices and the center. Four cells exist around 6 edges, and 3 cells around 3 edges.

#### Half pyramidille {#Half_pyramidille}

The dual to the *runcitruncated cubic honeycomb* is called a **half pyramidille**, with [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) . Faces exist in 3 of 4 hyperplanes of the \[4,3<sup>1,1</sup>\], ${\tilde{B}}_3$ Coxeter group.

Cells are irregular pyramids and can be seen as 1/12 of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), or 1/24 of a [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), each defined with three corner and the cube center.

The **gyrated tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb** or **gyrated alternated cubic honeycomb** is a space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [Euclidean 3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_3-space) made up of [octahedra](wikipedia://en/80177635) and [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) in a ratio of 1:2.

It is [vertex-uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-uniform) with 8 tetrahedra and 6 octahedra around each vertex.

It is not [edge-uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-uniform). All edges have 2 tetrahedra and 2 octahedra, but some are alternating, and some are paired.

#### Construction by gyration {#Construction_by_gyration}

This is a less symmetric version of another honeycomb, tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb, in which each edge is surrounded by alternating tetrahedra and octahedra. Both can be considered as consisting of layers one cell thick, within which the two kinds of cell strictly alternate. Because the faces on the planes separating these layers form a [regular pattern of triangles](wikipedia://en/2863858), adjacent layers can be placed so that each octahedron in one layer meets a tetrahedron in the next layer, **or** so that each cell meets a cell of its own kind (the layer boundary thus becomes a [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829) plane). The latter form is called *gyrated*.

The vertex figure is called a [triangular orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1196250), compared to the tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb whose vertex figure [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) in a lower symmetry is called a *triangular gyrobicupola*, so the gyro- prefix is reversed in usage.

The **gyroelongated alternated cubic honeycomb** or **elongated triangular antiprismatic cellulation** is a space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [Euclidean 3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_3-space). It is composed of [octahedra](wikipedia://en/80177635), [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584), and [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) in a ratio of 1:2:2.

It is vertex-transitive with 3 octahedra, 4 tetrahedra, 6 triangular prisms around each vertex.

It is one of 28 [convex uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961).

The [elongated alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongated_alternated_cubic_honeycomb) has the same arrangement of cells at each vertex, but the overall arrangement differs. In the *elongated* form, each prism meets a tetrahedron at one of its triangular faces and an octahedron at the other; in the *gyroelongated* form, the prism meets the same kind of [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995) at each end.
