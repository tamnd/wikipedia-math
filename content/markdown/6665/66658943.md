# Dieter Rödding

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dieter Rödding"}

**Dieter Rödding** (24 August 1937 in [Hattingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattingen) ([Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr)) – 4 June 1984 in [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Münster)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose main research interest was [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).[^o3]

:::

Dieter Rödding was born on 24 August 1937 in Hattingen, Ruhr, [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). In 1956, Rödding began his studies at the [Westphalian Wilhelms-University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalian_Wilhelms-University) in [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Münster), Germany. In 1961, he received his doctorate with the dissertation "*Representative sentences about (in the Kalmár-Czillagian sense) elementary functions*", supervised by [Gisbert Hasenjaeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisbert_Hasenjaeger).[^o4] In 1964, he completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at Münster with the thesis "*Theory of recursivity over the domain of finite sets of finite rank*". In 1966, he succeeded [Hans Hermes](wikipedia://en/41139401) as the Chair and Director of the [Institute of Mathematical Logic and Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Logic_and_Fundamental_Research) at the Westphalian Wilhelms-University, founded by [Heinrich Scholz](wikipedia://en/41316662) in 1936.

Rödding became known through his results on the classification of [recursive functions](wikipedia://en/26469), on [recursive types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursive_type) of [classical predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_predicate_logic), on [Scholz](wikipedia://en/41316662)'s [spectrum problem](wikipedia://en/3820897), as well as on [quantifiers](wikipedia://en/43507260) in [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic), and on the [arithmetical hierarchy](wikipedia://en/186475) (also known as the [Kleene](wikipedia://en/28805)–[Mostowski](wikipedia://en/1029314) hierarchy). Rödding was one of the first to use a machine-oriented concept of [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity) for the investigation of recursive functions and logical [decision problems](wikipedia://en/8336), before the establishment of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) as an academic field.

His students included [Egon Börger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Börger), Hans Kleine Büning, Hans Georg Carstens, Elmar Cohors-Fresenborg, [Heinz-Dieter Ebbinghaus](wikipedia://en/28870090), Thomas Ottmann, Lutz Priese, and [Helmut Schwichtenberg](wikipedia://en/62211423).[^o4]

A complete list of Rödding's publications can be found in an obituary written by his student [Egon Börger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Börger).[^o3]
