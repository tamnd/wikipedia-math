# Sum of squares function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sum of squares function"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **sum of squares function** is an [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170) that gives the number of representations for a given positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *$n$* as the sum of *$k$* [squares](wikipedia://en/213919), where representations that differ only in the order of the [summands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summand) or in the signs of the numbers being squared are counted as different. It is denoted by $r_k(n)$.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The [function](wikipedia://en/185427) is defined as

$$r_k(n) = |\{(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_k) \in \mathbb{Z}^k \ : \ n = a_1^2 + a_2^2 + \cdots + a_k^2\}|$$

where $|\,\ |$ denotes the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of a [set](wikipedia://en/26691). In other words, $r_k(n)$ is the number of ways *$n$* can be written as a sum of *$k$* squares.

For example, $r_2(1) = 4$ since $1 = 0^2 + (\pm 1)^2 = (\pm 1)^2 + 0^2$ where each sum has two sign combinations, and also $r_2(2) = 4$ since $2 = (\pm 1)^2 + (\pm 1)^2$ with four sign combinations. On the other hand, $r_2(3) = 0$ because there is no way to represent 3 as a sum of two squares.

:::

## Formulae {#Formulae}

### *k* = 2 {#k_=_2}

*Image: Sum\_of\_two\_squares\_theorem.svg.* }

The number of ways to write a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) as sum of two squares is given by $r_2(n)$. It is given explicitly by

$$r_2(n) = 4(d_1(n)-d_3(n))$$

where $d_1(n)$ is the number of [divisors](wikipedia://en/407249) of *$n$* which are [congruent](wikipedia://en/20087) to 1 [modulo](wikipedia://en/1352566) 4 and $d_3(n)$ is the number of divisors of *$n$* which are congruent to 3 modulo 4. Using sums, the expression can be written as:

$$r_2(n) = 4\sum_{d \mid n \atop d\,\equiv\,1,3 \pmod 4}(-1)^{(d-1)/2}$$

The prime [factorization](wikipedia://en/82341) $n = 2^g p_1^{f_1}p_2^{f_2}\cdots q_1^{h_1}q_2^{h_2}\cdots$, where $p_i$ are the [prime factors](wikipedia://en/222390) of the form $p_i \equiv 1\pmod 4,$ and $q_i$ are the prime factors of the form $q_i \equiv 3\pmod 4$ gives another formula

$r_2(n) = 4 (f_1 +1)(f_2+1)\cdots$, if *all* exponents $h_1, h_2, \cdots$ are [even](wikipedia://en/143135). If one or more $h_i$ are [odd](wikipedia://en/143135), then $r_2(n) = 0$.

### *k* = 3 {#k_=_3}

Gauss proved that for a [squarefree number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree_number) $n>4$,

$$r_3(n) = \begin{cases} 24 h(-n), & \text{if } n\equiv 3\pmod{8}, \\ 0 & \text{if } n\equiv 7\pmod{8}, \\ 12 h(-4n) & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}$$

where $h(m)$ denotes the [class number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_%28number_theory%29) of an integer *$m$*.

There exist extensions of Gauss' formula to arbitrary integer *$n$*.[^o7][^o8]

### *k* = 4 {#k_=_4}

The number of ways to represent *$n$* as the sum of four squares was due to [Carl Gustav Jakob Jacobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustav_Jakob_Jacobi) and it is eight times the sum of all its divisors which are not divisible by 4, i.e.

$$r_4(n)=8\sum_{d\,\mid\,n,\ 4\,\nmid\,d}d.$$

Representing *$n=2^k m$*, where *$m$* is an odd integer, one can express $r_4(n)$ in terms of the [divisor function](wikipedia://en/407249) as follows:

$$r_4(n) = 8\sigma(2^{\min\{k,1\}}m).$$

### *k* = 6 {#k_=_6}

The number of ways to represent *$n$* as the sum of six squares is given by

$$r_6(n) = 4\sum_{d\mid n} d^2\big( 4\left(\tfrac{-4}{n/d}\right) - \left(\tfrac{-4}{d}\right)\big),$$

where $\left(\tfrac{\cdot}{\cdot}\right)$ is the [Kronecker symbol](wikipedia://en/706301).[^o11]

### *k* = 8 {#k_=_8}

Jacobi also found an [explicit formula](wikipedia://en/585143) for the case *$k=8$*:[^o11]

$$r_8(n) = 16\sum_{d\,\mid\,n}(-1)^{n+d}d^3.$$

## Generating function {#Generating_function}

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $r_k(n)$ for fixed $k$ can be expressed in terms of the [Jacobi theta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobi_theta_function):[^o14]

$$\vartheta(0;q)^k = \vartheta_3^k(q) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_k(n)q^n,$$

where

$$\vartheta(0;q) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^2} = 1 + 2q + 2q^4 + 2q^9 + 2q^{16} + \cdots.$$
