# Paul of Venice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul of Venice"}

**Paul of Venice** (or **Paulus Venetus**; 1369–1429) was a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theologian), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and [metaphysician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysician) of the [Order of Saint Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Paul was born, according to the chroniclers of his order, at [Udine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udine), about 1369 and died at [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) on 15 June 1429,[^o4] as **Paolo Nicoletti**.[^o5] He joined the [Augustinian Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St_Augustine) at the age of 14, at the convent of [Santo Stefano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Stefano,_Venice) in Venice. In 1390 he is said to have been sent to [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) for his studies in theology, but returned to Italy, and finished his course at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), becoming a Doctor of Arts and Theology in 1405. He lectured in the Universities of Padua, [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Siena), [Perugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Perugia), and [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) during the first quarter of the fifteenth century.[^o6] He was also a teacher to [Paolo da Pergola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_da_Pergola).[^o7]

Paul was also appointed Prior General of the Augustinian Order in 1409 by [Pope Gregory XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XII), and also served as an ambassador to the Republic of Venice. Paul was one of the theologians called to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) in 1427 by [Pope Martin V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Martin_V) to defend the orthodoxy of [St. Bernardino of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_of_Siena), occasioned by Bernardino's use of inscriptions of the name of Jesus in worship. In 1429, Paul died in Padua, while he was completing his commentary on [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s *[De Anima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Anima).*[^o6]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

*Image: Paolo - Logica, 1546 - 4629053.tif.* *Logica*, 1546

Paul's philosophy has been categorised within the [realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_realism) tradition of medieval thought.[^o9] Following on from [John Wycliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wycliffe) and the subsequent Oxonians who followed him, Paul further developed this new brand of realism, and further renewed [Walter Burley](wikipedia://en/3909460)’s opposition to [nominalism](wikipedia://en/21176). Paul's metaphysical theses are rooted fundamentally in [Scotist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotist) thought. [Duns Scotus](wikipedia://en/34293405) maintained the doctrine of the [univocity of being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univocity_of_being) and the existence of the universal forms of objects outside of the person's mind. He also maintained Scotus' notion of the real identity and the formal distinction between [essence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essence) and [being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_%28theology%29), alongside the notion of "[thisness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thisness)" as the principle of individuation.[^o10] Paul was also simultaneously influenced by other thinkers of the [Scholastic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism), including the [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_preachers) thinkers [Albert the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_the_Great) and [Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas), and his fellow Augustinian, [Giles of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giles_of_Rome). Paul also critically engaged with the works and doctrines of fourteenth-century nominalists such as [William Ockham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ockham), [John Buridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Buridan), and [Marsilius of Inghen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsilius_of_Inghen), and sometimes gauged these thinkers' theses against each other to undermine their positions.[^o11]

## Works {#Works}

His writings show a wide knowledge and interest in the [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) problems of his time.

- Commentaries on the works of Aristotle:
  - *Expositio in libros Posteriorum Aristotelis*.
  - *Expositio super VIII libros Physicorum necnon super Commento Averrois* (1409).
  - *Expositio super libros De generatione et corruptione*.
  - *Lectura super librum De Anima*.
  - *Conclusiones Ethicorum*.
  - *Conclusiones Politicorum*.
  - *Expositio super Praedicabilia et Praedicamenta* (1428).
- Logical works:
  - *Logica Parva* or *Tractatus Summularum* (1395–96).
  - *Logica Magna* (1397–98).
  - *Quadratura*.
  - *Sophismata Aurea*.
- Other works:
  - *Super Primum Sententiarum Johannis de Ripa Lecturae Abbreviatio* (1401).
  - *Summa philosophiae naturalis* (1408).
  - *De compositione mundi*.
  - *Quaestiones adversus Judaeos*.
  - *Sermones*.
- (1483). "Logica". Antonio Carcano. <https://gutenberg.beic.it/webclient/DeliveryManager?pid=14499811>
- (1546). "Logica". Bartolomeo Imperatore & Francesco Imperatore. <https://gutenberg.beic.it/webclient/DeliveryManager?pid=4629053>
