# Gyula Y. Katona

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyula Y. Katona"}

**Gyula Y. Katona** (; born 4 December 1965) is a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). He is a professor and head of the Department of Computer Science and Information Theory at the [Budapest University of Technology and Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_University_of_Technology_and_Economics) (BME), and a [Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences). His research concerns [Hamiltonian cycles](wikipedia://en/244437) in graphs and [hypergraphs](wikipedia://en/232535), [graph toughness](wikipedia://en/8839340), [factors](wikipedia://en/581797), and [graph pebbling](wikipedia://en/3042715).[^o3] In 2026 he was awarded the Officer's Cross of the [Order of Merit of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_Hungary).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Katona was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest) in 1965, the son of the mathematician [Gyula O. H. Katona](wikipedia://en/9211532).[^o6] He attended the [Fazekas Mihály Gimnázium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazekas_Mihály_Gimnázium) and studied mathematics at [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) from 1986 to 1991. His diploma thesis, *A Few Versions of the Hamiltonian Cycle Problem*, was supervised by [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938) and [András Recski](wikipedia://en/84041026).[^o3][^o6] He continued as a doctoral student at the same university from 1991 to 1994, and in 1997 received the degree of [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) from the Hungarian Academy of Sciences for the dissertation *Paths and Cycles in Graphs and Hypergraphs*, again under the supervision of Lovász and Recski.[^o3][^o7] In 2024 he was awarded the degree of Doctor of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.[^o3][^o7]

## Career {#Career}

From 1994 to 1997 Katona was a research assistant professor in the combinatorics department of the [Mathematical Institute of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/31714670), and from 1997 to 1999 he held a [JSPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Society_for_the_Promotion_of_Science) postdoctoral fellowship at [Ibaraki University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaraki_University) in Japan.[^o3] He joined the Department of Computer Science and Information Theory at the Budapest University of Technology and Economics in 1997, became an associate professor in 2002, and has headed the department since 2011.[^o3][^o7] He was appointed full professor by the [President of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Hungary) with effect from 1 September 2025.[^o10] He was a visiting professor at [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) in 2006–2007, and has taught in the [Budapest Semesters in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8520286) programme since 2003 and at the [Aquincum Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquincum_Institute_of_Technology) since 2010.[^o3]

Katona was managing editor of the Hungarian-language journal *Matematikai Lapok* from 1994 to 1997, and serves on the editorial boards of the *AKCE International Journal of Graphs and Combinatorics* and the *[International Journal of Computer Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Computer_Mathematics)*.[^o3][^o7]

## Research {#Research}

Katona's research lies at the interface of graph theory and the theory of algorithms. Much of his early work concerns [Hamiltonian cycles](wikipedia://en/244437) and their variants.[^o6] With [Henry Kierstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kierstead) he introduced a notion of Hamiltonian chains in uniform hypergraphs, the objects now commonly called tight Hamiltonian cycles, and gave a Dirac-type sufficient condition for their existence. The question of the optimal minimum-degree threshold became known as the Katona–Kierstead conjecture and was later proved in asymptotic form by [Vojtěch Rödl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojtěch_Rödl), [Andrzej Ruciński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Ruciński) and [Endre Szemerédi](wikipedia://en/663489).[^o12][^o13] His other work includes results on [graph toughness](wikipedia://en/8839340) and its edge and local variants, on [factors](wikipedia://en/581797) of graphs, on Hamiltonian-saturated graphs and hypergraphs, and on [graph pebbling](wikipedia://en/3042715) and rubbling.[^o3][^o14]

He is the coauthor of three textbooks: *Combinatorics, Graph Theory and Algorithms* (with András Recski, Technical University of Budapest, 1993), *Introduction to Finite Mathematics* (Eötvös Loránd University, 1993) and *Introduction to Computer Science* (Typotex, Budapest, 2002).[^o3]
