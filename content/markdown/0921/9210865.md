# Shape parameter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shape parameter"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **shape parameter** (also known as **form parameter**)[^o2] is a kind of [numerical parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_parameter) of a parametric family of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543)[^o3] that is neither a [location parameter](wikipedia://en/17516) nor a [scale parameter](wikipedia://en/769503) (nor a function of these, such as a [rate parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_parameter)). Such a parameter must affect the [shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_%28geometry%29) [of a distribution](wikipedia://en/9330700) rather than simply shifting it (as a location parameter does) or stretching/shrinking it (as a scale parameter does). For example, peakedness refers to how round the main peak is.[^o4]

:::

## Estimation {#Estimation}

Many [estimators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimators) measure location or scale; however, estimators for shape parameters also exist. Most simply, they can be estimated in terms of the higher [moments](wikipedia://en/368684), using the [method of moments](wikipedia://en/2175504), as in the *[skewness](wikipedia://en/28212)* (3rd moment) or *[kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848)* (4th moment), if the higher moments are defined and finite. Estimators of shape often involve [higher-order statistics](wikipedia://en/9588430) (non-linear functions of the data), as in the higher moments, but linear estimators also exist, such as the [L-moments](wikipedia://en/24350828). [Maximum likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood) estimation can also be used.

## Examples {#Examples}

The following continuous probability distributions have a shape parameter:

- [Beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074)
- [Burr distribution](wikipedia://en/12618582)
- [Dagum distribution](wikipedia://en/31102537)
- [Erlang distribution](wikipedia://en/199028)
- [ExGaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExGaussian_distribution)
- [Exponential power distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_power_distribution)
- [Fréchet distribution](wikipedia://en/13808626)
- [Gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079)
- [Generalized extreme value distribution](wikipedia://en/1494479)
- [Log-logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/15657106)
- [Log-t distribution](wikipedia://en/70445407)
- [Inverse-gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/1653565)
- [Inverse Gaussian distribution](wikipedia://en/5246161)
- [Pareto distribution](wikipedia://en/53057)
- [Pearson distribution](wikipedia://en/1804023)
- [Skew normal distribution](wikipedia://en/16266475)
- [Lognormal distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lognormal_distribution)
- [Student's t-distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_t-distribution)
- [Tukey lambda distribution](wikipedia://en/15780233)
- [Weibull distribution](wikipedia://en/206948)

By contrast, the following continuous distributions do *not* have a shape parameter, so their shape is fixed and only their location or their scale or both can change. It follows that (where they exist) the [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) and [kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848) of these distribution are constants, as skewness and kurtosis are independent of location and scale parameters.

- [Exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906)
- [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003)
- [Logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666)
- [Normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462)
- [Raised cosine distribution](wikipedia://en/2655605)
- [Uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699223)
- [Wigner semicircle distribution](wikipedia://en/1028321)
