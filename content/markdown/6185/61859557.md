# Joanne Moldenhauer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joanne Moldenhauer"}

**Joanne K. Moldenhauer** (née Gatz, March 15, 1928 – February 14, 2016) was an American high school mathematics teacher and two-time winner of the [Edyth May Sliffe Award](wikipedia://en/10237486).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Moldenhauer's father served in the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army). She was born in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska), and attended [Benson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_Benson_High_School_Magnet) in Omaha. She graduated in 1949 from the [Iowa State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University), with a degree in physics, hoping to go on to a research career in physics was but blocked from that goal because there were few places for women in physics at the time.

## Career {#Career}

After graduating from Iowa State, Moldenhauer became a high school mathematics teacher in Omaha, and two years later became a student again at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota). She completed a master's degree in mathematics at Minnesota in 1952, and started work as an electrical engineer at [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric). Her job there involved the development of [fire-control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system) for military aircraft. However, bored with her work, she soon returned to high school teaching. In 1955 she became a high school teacher in [Schenectady, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schenectady,_New_York), where she had been working for General Electric, and in 1956 she moved to [Davis Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Senior_High_School_%28California%29) in [Davis, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis,_California).

After 50 years as a teacher in Davis, Moldenhauer retired in 2006.

## Contributions and recognition {#Contributions_and_recognition}

Moldenhauer won the [Edyth May Sliffe Award](wikipedia://en/10237486) for Distinguished High School Mathematics Teaching of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) twice, in 1990 and 2001. She was also a winner of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University)'s Frederick Emmons Terman Engineering Award, given annually by the graduating engineering students at Stanford to a distinguished high school teacher. She was a two-time winner of [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College)'s Distinguished Teaching Award. Unusually for a high school mathematics teacher, Moldenhauer has an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 2, from her collaboration with mathematician [Sherman K. Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_K._Stein) and mechanical engineer Anthony S. Wexler on "Trigonometry and a Wood Bowl".
