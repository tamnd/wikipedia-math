# George Pirie (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Pirie"}

**George Pirie** (19 July 1843 – 21 August 1904) was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [mathematical scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Scientist), and Reverend in the [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland). He was an expert in the field of [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_%28mechanics%29) and the [approximation of $\pi$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximations_of_π).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Pirie was the son of Very Rev [William Robinson Pirie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Robinson_Pirie) and his wife, Margaret Chalmers Forbes, daughter of Very Rev [Lewis William Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_William_Forbes). He was born in the manse at [Dyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyce) near [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen) on 19 July 1843.[^o3]

He was educated at [Aberdeen Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Grammar_School). He then studied Mathematics and Physics (then called Natural Philosophy) at The [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen) under [Prof David Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thomson_%28physicist%29). He graduated MA in 1862. He then won a place at [Queens' College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens'_College,_Cambridge). He was Fifth Wrangler in the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) of 1866.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Pirie was a Fellow of Queens' College from 1866 to 1888.[^o4] He lectured at Cambridge from 1866 and in 1878 was offered the chair as Professor of Mathematics at [Aberdeen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_University) in replacement of Prof [Frederick Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Fuller_%28mathematician%29).[^o6]

Pirie was elected a member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in November 1881.[^o7] He also took part in the activities of the Aberdeen Branch of the [Royal Scottish Geographical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Scottish_Geographical_Society).[^o8][^o9] Since 1881 he was also a member of the [Aberdeen Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Philosophical_Society#Later_society_of_the_same_name), and served as a Vice-President of the society in 1900.[^o10]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

*Image: The grave of Rev George Pirie, St Machar's Cathedral.jpg.* The grave of Rev George Pirie, St Machar's Cathedral

He lived at 33 College Bounds close to [King's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Aberdeen) in [Old Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Aberdeen).[^o12]

He was married to Anne Elizabeth Reid daughter of Rev Reid of [Auchindoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auchindoir).

Their children included Margaret Forbes Pirie (1884–1963) and Elizabeth Mary Pirie (1888–1971).[^o13]

He died unexpectedly at [Braemar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braemar) on 21 August 1904, from heart failure.[^o14] He is buried next to his parents on the north side of [St Machar's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Machar's_Cathedral) in [Old Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Aberdeen).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

He was awarded an honorary doctorate ([LL.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL.D)) by [St. Andrews University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrews_University) in 1896.[^o4][^o16]
