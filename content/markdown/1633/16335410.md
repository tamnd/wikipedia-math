# Frequency partition of a graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frequency partition of a graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a discipline within [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **frequency partition** of a graph ([simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph)) is a [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) of its [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) grouped by their degree. For example, the [degree sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_sequence) of the left-hand graph below is (3, 3, 3, 2, 2, 1) and its frequency partition is 6 = 3 + 2 + 1. This indicates that it has 3 vertices with some degree, 2 vertices with some other degree, and 1 vertex with a third degree. The degree sequence of the [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) in the middle below is (3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1) and its frequency partition is 9 = 5 + 3 + 1. The degree sequence of the right-hand graph below is (3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2) and its frequency partition is 7 = 6 + 1.

:::

In general, there are many non-isomorphic graphs with a given frequency partition. A graph and its [complement](wikipedia://en/1175649) have the same frequency partition. For any partition *p* = *f*<sub>1</sub> + *f*<sub>2</sub> + ... + *f*<sub>*k*</sub> of an integer *p* > 1, other than *p* = 1 + 1 + 1 + ... + 1, there is at least one (connected) simple graph having this partition as its frequency partition.[^o2]

Frequency partitions of various graph families are completely identified; frequency partitions of many families of graphs are not identified.

## Frequency partitions of Eulerian graphs {#Frequency_partitions_of_Eulerian_graphs}

For a frequency [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) *p* = *f*<sub>1</sub> + *f*<sub>2</sub> + ... + *f*<sub>*k*</sub> of an integer *p* > 1, its [graphic degree sequence](wikipedia://en/1374448) is denoted as ((d<sub>1</sub>)<sup>f<sub>1</sub></sup>,(d<sub>2</sub>)<sup>f<sub>2</sub></sup>, (d<sub>3</sub>)<sup>f<sub>3</sub></sup>, ..., (d<sub>k</sub>) <sup>f<sub>k</sub></sup>) where degrees d<sub>i</sub>'s are different and *f*<sub>i</sub> ≥ *f*<sub>j</sub> for *i* \< *j*. Bhat-Nayak *et al.* (1979) showed that a partition of p with k parts, k ≤ integral part of $(p-1)/2$ is a frequency partition[^o4] of a Eulerian graph and conversely.

## Frequency partition of trees, Hamiltonian graphs, tournaments and hypegraphs {#Frequency_partition_of_trees,_Hamiltonian_graphs,_tournaments_and_hypegraphs}

The frequency partitions of families of graphs such as [trees](wikipedia://en/48560),[^o6] [Hamiltonian graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph)[^o7] [directed graphs](wikipedia://en/19721986) and [tournaments](wikipedia://en/1427502)[^o8] and to k-uniform [hypergraphs](wikipedia://en/232535).[^o9] have been characterized.

## Unsolved problems in frequency partitions {#Unsolved_problems_in_frequency_partitions}

The frequency partitions of the following families of graphs have not yet been characterized:

- [Line graphs](wikipedia://en/675231)
- [Bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431) [^o11]
