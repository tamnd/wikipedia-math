# Yuri Manin

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Yuri Manin"}

**Yuri Ivanovich Manin** (Ю́рий Ива́нович Ма́нин) (16 February 1937 – 7 January 2023) was a Russian mathematician, known for work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), and many expository works ranging from [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) to [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Manin was born on 16 February 1937 in [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol), Crimean ASSR, Soviet Union.[^o8]

He received a doctorate in 1960 at the [Steklov Mathematics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Mathematics_Institute) as a student of [Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815). He became a professor at the [Max-Planck-Institut für Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Mathematik) in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn), where he was director from 1992 to 2005 and then director emeritus.[^o5][^o8] He was also a Trustee Chair Professor at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) from 2002 to 2011.[^o6]

He had over the years more than 50 doctoral students, including [Vladimir Berkovich](wikipedia://en/34421039), [Mariusz Wodzicki](wikipedia://en/57858922), [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613), [Ivan Cherednik](wikipedia://en/33333323), [Alexei Skorobogatov](wikipedia://en/25642036), [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747), [Mikhail Kapranov](wikipedia://en/62272076), [Vyacheslav Shokurov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Shokurov), [Ralph Kaufmann](wikipedia://en/56068150), [Victor Kolyvagin](wikipedia://en/4559839), [Alexander L. Rosenberg](wikipedia://en/57780441), [Alexander A. Voronov](wikipedia://en/68168204), and [Hà Huy Khoái](wikipedia://en/36857142).

Manin died on 7 January 2023 in Bonn.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

Manin's early work included papers on the arithmetic and [formal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group) of [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties), the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture) in the [function field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_function_field) case, and [algebraic differential equations](wikipedia://en/9501827). The [Gauss–Manin connection](wikipedia://en/4550529) is a basic ingredient of the study of [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) in families of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties).[^o10]

He developed the [Manin obstruction](wikipedia://en/18960214), indicating the role of the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) in accounting for obstructions to the [Hasse principle](wikipedia://en/489707) via [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck)'s theory of global [Azumaya algebras](wikipedia://en/3125089), setting off a generation of further work.[^o12][^o13]

Manin pioneered the field of [arithmetic topology](wikipedia://en/23834912) (along with [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933), [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594), [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821), and [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679)).[^o14] He also formulated the [Manin conjecture](wikipedia://en/41912043), which predicts the asymptotic behaviour of the number of rational points of bounded height on algebraic varieties.[^o15]

In mathematical physics, Manin wrote on [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202), [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274), and [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671).[^o16][^o17] He was one of the first to propose the idea of a [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer) in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_quantum_computing) with his book *Computable and Uncomputable*.[^o18]

He wrote a book on [cubic surfaces](wikipedia://en/526126) and [cubic forms](wikipedia://en/6680683), showing how to apply both classical and contemporary methods of algebraic geometry, as well as [nonassociative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonassociative_algebra).[^o19]

## Awards {#Awards}

He was awarded the [Brouwer Medal](wikipedia://en/42578624) in 1987, the first [Nemmers Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1252473) in 1994, the [Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schock_Prize) of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1999, the [Cantor Medal](wikipedia://en/1691369) of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973) in 2002, the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize) in 2002, and the [Bolyai Prize](wikipedia://en/18706230) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2010.[^o8]

In 1990, he became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o21] He was a member of eight other academies of science and was also an honorary member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o8]
