# Sophistical Refutations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sophistical Refutations"}

***Sophistical Refutations*** (Σοφιστικοὶ Ἔλεγχοι; De Sophisticis Elenchis) is a text in [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s *[Organon](wikipedia://en/499019)* in which he identified twelve or thirteen [fallacies](wikipedia://en/53986). According to Aristotle, this is the first work to treat the subject of [deductive reasoning](wikipedia://en/61093) in ancient Greece (*Soph. Ref.*, 34, 183b34 ff.). In it, he attempts to develop a pattern of sound and genuine reasoning in opposition to the deceptive or misleading "art of [sophistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophists)".

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

*On Sophistical Refutations*[^o3][^o4] consists of 34 chapters. The book naturally falls in two parts: chapters concerned with tactics for the Questioner (3–8 and 12–15) and chapters concerned with tactics for the Answerer (16–32). Besides, there is an introduction (1–2), an interlude (9–11), and a conclusion (33–34).[^o5]

## Fallacies identified {#Fallacies_identified}

The fallacies Aristotle identifies in Chapter 4 (formal fallacies) and 5 (informal fallacies) of this book are the following:

Fallacies in the language or formal fallacies (*in dictionem*):

1. [Equivocation](wikipedia://en/17690)
1. [Amphiboly](wikipedia://en/154040)
1. [Composition](wikipedia://en/523043)
1. [Division](wikipedia://en/522936)
1. [Accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacy_of_accent)
1. [Figure of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_of_speech) or form of expression

Fallacies not in the language or informal fallacies (*extra dictionem*):

[Accident](wikipedia://en/735233); *[Secundum quid](wikipedia://en/735213)*; [Irrelevant conclusion](wikipedia://en/17689); *[Petitio principii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petitio_principii)*; [False cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_cause); [Affirming the consequent](wikipedia://en/675); [Fallacy of many questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacy_of_many_questions)
