# Magic polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Magic polygon"}

A **magic polygon** is a polygonal [magic graph](wikipedia://en/4351071) with [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) on its vertices.

:::

## Perimeter magic polygon {#Perimeter_magic_polygon}

A **magic polygon**, also called a **perimeter magic polygon**,[^o3][^o4] is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with an integers on its sides that all add up to a [magic constant](wikipedia://en/546969).[^o5][^o6] It is where positive integers (from 1 to *N*) on a *k*-sided polygon add up to a constant.[^o3] Magic polygons are a [generalization](wikipedia://en/12746) of other magic shapes[^o7] such as [magic triangles](wikipedia://en/52697333).[^o8]

*Image: Order 3 Magic Triangles.gif.* This displays order 3 [magic triangles](wikipedia://en/52697333), a type of **magic polygon.**

## Magic polygon with a center point {#Magic_polygon_with_a_center_point}

Victoria Jakicic and Rachelle Bouchat defined magic polygons as *n*-sided regular polygons with 2*n*+1 nodes such that the sum of the three nodes are equal. In their definition, a 3 × 3 magic square can be viewed as a magic 4-gon. There are no magic odd-gons with this definition.[^o10]

## Magic polygons and degenerated magic polygons {#Magic_polygons_and_degenerated_magic_polygons}

Danniel Dias Augusto and Josimar da Silva defined the magic polygon P(*n*,*k*) as a set of vertices of $k/2$ concentric *n*-gon and a center point. In this definition, magic polygons of Victoria Jakicic and Rachelle Bouchat can be viewed as P(*n*,2) magic polygons. They also defined degenerated magic polygons.[^o12]
