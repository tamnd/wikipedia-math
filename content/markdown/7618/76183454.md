# Neal Henry McCoy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neal Henry McCoy"}

**Neal Henry McCoy** (March 6, 1905 – January 5, 2001) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), university professor, and author of several textbooks for mathematics undergraduates. His 1960 textbook *Introduction to Modern Algebra* has gone through several editions and has been translated into many foreign languages.[^o2]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

McCoy was born into a homesteading family in [Waukomis, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waukomis,_Oklahoma) (which was part of the [Oklahoma Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_Territory) from 1890 to 1907). He had an older sister [Dorothy McCoy](wikipedia://en/63473332) (1903–2001), who became the first woman to receive a Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa). As an infant, he moved in 1906 with his mother and sister to [Chesapeake, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake,_Missouri) after the death of his father.[^o4] After graduating with a bachelor's degree from [Baylor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_University),[^o2] he became a graduate student at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa). There he graduated in 1929 with a Ph.D. in mathematics with adviser Edward Wilson Chittenden. McCoy's Ph.D. thesis *On commutation formulas in the algebra of quantum mechanics* was published in abbreviated form in the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)*.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

For two academic years from 1929 to 1931, McCoy studied at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as a National Research Fellow. He joined in 1931 the staff of the mathematics department of [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton,_Massachusetts)'s [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College),[^o2] There he was promoted to full professor in 1942[^o8] and was appointed to the Gates Chair in Mathematics in 1963, retiring as professor emeritus in 1970.[^o2] McCoy did research on [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), especially the theory of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) and [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) with elements in rings, and algebraic aspects of quantum mechanics.[^o8] From 1951 to 1954 he was the editor-in-chief of the *[Duke Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Mathematical_Journal)*.[^o2] He was an associate editor for the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* and the [*Pi Mu Epsilon Journal*](wikipedia://en/1565972).[^o8]

## McCoy ring {#McCoy_ring}

In 1942, McCoy proved the following theorem: Let R be a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) with multiplicative unit, and let f(x) be a [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441) in the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) R\[x\]. Then there is a nonzero element r ∈ R with f(x)r = 0.[^o10] In 1997 Mangesh B. Rege and Sima Chhawchharia introduced definitions of rings,[^o11] which in 2006, independently and motivated by McCoy's 1942 theorem, were re-introduced by Pace P. Nielsen, who gave the concepts the names "right McCoy ring", "left McCoy ring", and "McCoy ring" (a ring which is both right McCoy and left McCoy).[^o12][^o13]

## *Rings and Ideals* (1948) {#Rings_and_Ideals_(1948)}

McCoy wrote the 8th book in the series of [Carus Mathematical Monographs](wikipedia://en/2941952) published by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). The book is almost entirely self-contained.[^o15] In a 1948 review, [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) praised the book for the optimized simplicity of the mathematical proofs, an "easy and readable style", and "skillful use of examples".[^o16] In a 1949 review, [C. C. MacDuffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Colton_MacDuffee) praised the book — "as an introduction to the powerful and highly abstract method of thinking which now characterizes modern algebra, it is a gem."[^o17]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1929 in Iowa, Neal H. McCoy married Ardis Hollingsworth (1904–1988). Their only son, Paul Albert McCoy, was killed in 1957 at age 22 in an automobile accident.[^o2]
