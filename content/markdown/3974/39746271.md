# Akalanka

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Akalanka"}

**Akalaṅka** \[[IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAST): Akalaṅka\] (also known as *Akalaṅkadeva* and *Bhatta Akalaṅka*) was a Jain logician whose [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit)-language works are seen as landmarks in Indian logic.[^o4][^o3] He lived from 720 to 780 C. E. and belonged to the [Digambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digambara) sect of [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism).[^o4][^n1] His work *Aṣṭaśatī*, a commentary on *[Āptamīmaṃsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptamimamsa)* of [Ācārya Samantabhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acharya_Samantabhadra) deals mainly with *Jaina* logic. He was a contemporary of [Rāṣṭrakūta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta) king [Krishna I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_I). He is the author of Tattvārtharājavārtika, a commentary on major Jaina text *[Tattvārtha Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattvartha_Sutra)*. He greatly contributed to the development of the philosophy of *[Anekāntavāda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anekantavada)* and is therefore called the "Master of Jain logic".[^n2][^n3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Akalaṅka flourished in 750 C. E.[^n4] He was aware of the contents of the *Aṅgas*, although it cannot be said whether they represent an idea rather than a reality for him, and he also seems to have been the first [Digambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digambara) to have introduced as a valid form of scriptural classification the division into *kālika* and *utkālika* texts which was also employed by the [Śvetāmbaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śvetāmbara).[^n5] He is mentioned as a logician and a contemporary of [Subhatunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhatunga) and [Rāṣṭrakūta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta) king [Krishna I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_I).[^n4]

The samādhi of Akalaṅka is located between [Thurupammor and Karanthai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karanthai) villages, at a distance of 19 km from Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu.

## Works {#Works}

The following Sanskrit-language works are attributed to Akalaṅka. Some of these are:[^n6][^o14]

1. *Laghīyastraya:* A compendium of three small treatises - *Pramāṇapraveśa*, *Nayapraveśa*, and *Pravacanapraveśa*.[^o15]
1. *Pramānasaṅgraha*: A work on epistemology or *pramāṇa.*
1. *Nyāyaviniścaya:* A work dealing with perception, inference and pravacana.[^o15]
1. *Siddhiviniscaya-vivarana*
1. *Aṣṭaśatī*: A short but important commentary on [Samantabhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantabhadra_%28Jain_monk%29)'s [Aptamimamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptamimamsa).[^o15]
1. *Tattvārtharājavārtika:* A commentary on [Tattvartha Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattvartha_Sutra) resembling to Nyāyavārtika of [Udyotakara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udyotakara)[^o15]
