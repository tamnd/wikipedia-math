# George Piranian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Piranian"}

**George Piranian** (Գևորգ Փիրանեան; May 2, 1914 – August 31, 2009) was a Swiss-American mathematician.[^o3] Piranian was internationally known for his research in complex analysis, his association with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), and his editing of the *[Michigan Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Mathematical_Journal)*.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Piranian was born in [Thalwil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalwil) outside [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland). His father, Patvakan Piranian, was originally from Armenia. George and his brother David at home were called Gevorg and Davit, the Armenian versions of their names. His family immigrated to [Logan, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan,_Utah), in 1929. Piranian received a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.) in [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc.) in [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany) (1937) at [Utah State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_State_University). As a [Rhodes scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_scholar), Piranian first "tasted blood" in mathematics at [Hertford College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford_College,_Oxford).

After returning to the United States, Piranian earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) under [Szolem Mandelbrojt](wikipedia://en/1070245) at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) (1943). Piranian's dissertation was entitled *A Study of the Position and Nature of the Singularities of Functions Given by Their Taylor Series*.

Piranian joined the faculty at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1945.

## *Michigan Mathematical Journal* {#Michigan_Mathematical_Journal}

In 1952, Piranian, along with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), [Fritz Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Herzog) and [Arthur J. Lohwater](wikipedia://en/14380145), founded the *[Michigan Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Mathematical_Journal)*; leadership in editing was assumed by Piranian in 1954. Piranian co-authored a research paper with Erdős and Herzog;[^o9] as a consequence he has an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of one.

Piranian's editing was renowned in mathematics.[^o10][^o11]

## Teaching {#Teaching}

Piranian's teaching captivated several future research mathematicians.[^o13][^o14] Piranian also was an advisor with the Honors Program at the [College of Literature, Science and the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_College_of_Literature,_Science,_and_the_Arts) at the University of Michigan.

In the 1960s, Piranian taught and advised [Theodore Kaczynski](wikipedia://en/18621887), who was a Ph.D. student in mathematics.[^o15] In 1996, Kaczynski was arrested for the [Unabomber](wikipedia://en/18621887#Bombings) crimes and later pled guilty.
