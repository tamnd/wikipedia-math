# Judith Grabiner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Judith Grabiner"}

**Judith Victor Grabiner** (born October 12, 1938) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), who is Flora Sanborn Pitzer Professor Emerita of Mathematics at [Pitzer College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitzer_College), one of the [Claremont Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claremont_Colleges).[^o2] Her main interest is in mathematics in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Grabiner completed a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1960. She was a graduate student in the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), completing a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1962 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1966, under [I. Bernard Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._Bernard_Cohen).[^o5] Her PhD dissertation was on Italian mathematician [Joseph-Louis Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Grabiner was an instructor at Harvard for several years, before she and her husband Sandy Grabiner moved to California.[^o8] She was a professor of history at [California State University, Dominguez Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Dominguez_Hills) from 1972 to 1985.[^o5]

Grabiner joined the mathematics department at [Pitzer College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitzer_College) in 1985, and has been the [Flora Sanborn Pitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_K._Pitzer) Professor of Mathematics since 1994.[^o2] Her teaching includes courses on the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), mathematics in different cultures, and [mathematics and philosophy](wikipedia://en/46439).[^o2]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Grabiner received the [Carl B. Allendoerfer Award](wikipedia://en/40343255) for the best article in *[Mathematics Magazine](wikipedia://en/4230329)* in 1984, 1989, and 1996, and the [Lester R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford) Award in 1984, 1998, 2005, and 2010, for the best article in *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)*.[^o10][^o11]

In 2003, Grabiner received one of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)'s [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Awards for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o12] She became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012.[^o13] In 2014, she was awarded the [Beckenbach](wikipedia://en/36442901) Book Prize.[^o14]

She was the 2021 winner of the [Albert Leon Whiteman Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/26015197) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for her outstanding contributions to the history of mathematics, in particular her works on Cauchy, Lagrange, and MacLaurin; her widely-recognized gift for expository writing; and a distinguished career of teaching, lecturing, and numerous publications promoting a better understanding of mathematics and the significant roles it plays in culture generally".[^o15]
