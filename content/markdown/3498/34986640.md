# Gregory Freiman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gregory Freiman"}

**Gregory Abelevich Freiman** (Григорий Абелевич Фрейман; 3 July 1926 – 19 September 2024) was a Russian and Israeli mathematician known for his work in [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333), in particular, for proving [Freiman's theorem](wikipedia://en/2240299).[^o3][^o4] He was a professor emeritus in [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Freiman was born in [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan) on 3 July 1926. He graduated from [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University) in 1949, and obtained his [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) in [Kazan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_University) in 1956. From 1956 he worked in [Elabuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elabuga), and in 1965 he defended his [Doctor of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences) degree under the joint supervision of [Alexander Gelfond](wikipedia://en/4542389), Alexey G. Postnikov, and [Alexander Buchstab](wikipedia://en/45421432). From 1967 he worked in [Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir,_Russia),[^o8] and later in Kalinin (now [Tver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tver)).

In the 1970s and early 1980s Freiman participated in the [refusenik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusenik) movement. His [samizdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samizdat) essay *It seems I am a Jew*, described the discrimination against Jewish mathematicians in the Soviet Union.[^o9] It was published in the US in 1980.[^o10]

Later, Freiman was driven out of Russia for his different views. He chose Israel as his new home country, leaving his son, daughter and wife. In Israel he became professor in Tel Aviv University and remarried.

Freiman died on 19 September 2024, at the age of 98.
