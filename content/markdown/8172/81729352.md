# Susan D'Agostino

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susan D'Agostino"}

**Susan E. D'Agostino** is an American mathematician and science writer, an associate editor at the *[Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists)*, and the author of the book *How to Free Your Inner Mathematician: Notes on Mathematics and Life* (2020).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

D'Agostino grew up in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City). After dropping [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) in high school, she majored in [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) at [Bard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_College), while working on a dairy farm. At age 25, she set herself a task of earning a mathematics doctorate, beginning with the remedial mathematics missing from her high school education. With the encouragement of mathematician James Henle, she earned a master's degree in [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) at [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College), and then completed her doctorate at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) in 2003, with a dissertation in [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) supervised by Thomas R. Shemanske. While a student, she interviewed coding theory pioneer [Vera Pless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Pless), and won first place in an essay-writing contest of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).

Although not originally intending to go on in academia, she took a position as a faculty member at [Southern New Hampshire University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_New_Hampshire_University), the first person hired there with a mathematics Ph.D. She worked there for nearly ten years, earning tenure and receiving the university's excellence in teaching award. After the university, formerly a regional teaching school, reinvented itself as an online education provider with a massively increased enrollment, she decided to leave academia, becoming a science writer instead.

In 2018, she was named as a recipient of a Taylor/Blakeslee University Fellowship for graduate study in science writing, from the Council for the Advancement of Science Writing, and by 2020, she was studying for a master's degree in science writing from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). She completed it in 2021, and in the same year became an associate editor for the *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists*. She also has a [Master of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts) in nonfiction writing from Southern New Hampshire University.

## Writing {#Writing}

D'Agostino has written for "*[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)*, *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post)*, *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_%28magazine%29)*, *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*, *[Quanta](wikipedia://en/51804976)*, BBC *Science Focus*, *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)*, *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times)*, *[Undark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undark_Magazine)*, *[Atlas Obscura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Obscura)*, *[Discover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discover_%28magazine%29)*, *[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_%28magazine%29)*, *[Literary Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_Hub)*, and the *[Chronicle of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicle_of_Higher_Education)*, among others".

She is the author of the book *How to Free Your Inner Mathematician: Notes on Mathematics and Life* (2020). The book received the 2023 [Euler Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Book_Prize) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). She was also editor-in-chief of a book about the [EDGE program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_program) for women in mathematics, *A Celebration of the EDGE Program's Impact on the Mathematics Community and Beyond* (2019).
