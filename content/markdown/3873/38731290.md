# Alternated octagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alternated octagonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **tritetragonal tiling** or **alternated octagonal tiling** is a [uniform](wikipedia://en/18640974) [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/241291). It has [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) of {(4,3,3)} or h{8,3}.

:::

## Geometry {#Geometry}

Although a sequence of edges seem to represent straight lines (projected into curves), careful attention will show they are not straight, as can be seen by looking at it from different projective centers.

## In art {#In_art}

*Image: Circle limits III with overlay.png.* The alternated octagonal tiling, a [hyperbolic tiling](wikipedia://en/18640974) of squares and equilateral triangles, overlaid on Escher's image

[Circle Limit III](wikipedia://en/39847439) is a [woodcut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodcut) made in 1959 by Dutch artist [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127), in which "strings of fish shoot up like rockets from infinitely far away" and then "fall back again whence they came". White curves within the figure, through the middle of each line of fish, divide the plane into squares and triangles in the pattern of the tritetragonal tiling. However, in the tritetragonal tiling, the corresponding curves are chains of hyperbolic line segments, with a slight angle at each vertex, while in Escher's woodcut they appear to be smooth [hypercycles](wikipedia://en/7516582).
