# Jean-Pierre Sydler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Pierre Sydler"}

**Jean-Pierre Sydler** (1921-1988) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a [librarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Librarian), well known for his work in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), most notably on [Hilbert's third problem](wikipedia://en/152703) and the Sydler π/4 polyhedron.

*Image: Robin\_Houston\_45\_degree\_Sydler\_shape.stl.* A solution to Sydler's problem of making a polyhedron with a single non-90° angle (between faces)[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Sydler was born in 1921 in [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuchâtel), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland). He graduated from [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Institute_of_Technology_Zürich) in 1943 and received a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1947. In 1950 he became a librarian at the ETH while continuing to publish mathematical papers in his spare time. In 1960 he received a prize of the [Danish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters) for his work on [scissors congruence](wikipedia://en/152703). In 1963 he became a director of the ETH library and pioneered the use of automatisation. He continued serving as a director until the retirement in 1986. He died in 1988 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich).

- Greg N. Frederickson, *Dissections: Plane and Fancy*, Cambridge University Press, 2003.
