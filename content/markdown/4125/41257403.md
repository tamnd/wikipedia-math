# Scheffé's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scheffé's lemma"}

In mathematics, **Scheffé's lemma** is a proposition in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) concerning the [$L^1$ convergence](wikipedia://en/20018#Convergence) of sequences of [integrable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration). It states that, if $f_n$ is a sequence of integrable functions on a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ that [converges almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/340198#Almost_everywhere_convergence) to another integrable function $f$, then $\int |f_n - f| \, d\mu \to 0$ if and only if $\int | f_n | \, d\mu \to \int | f | \, d\mu$.[^o2]

:::

The proof is based fundamentally on an application of the triangle inequality and [Fatou's lemma](wikipedia://en/291614).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Applied to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), Scheffe's theorem, in the form stated here, implies that almost everywhere pointwise convergence of the [probability density functions](wikipedia://en/43487) of a sequence of $\mu$-[absolutely continuous](wikipedia://en/252670) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) implies [convergence in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) of those random variables.

:::

## History {#History}

[Henry Scheffé](wikipedia://en/25306204) published a proof of the statement on convergence of probability densities in 1947.[^o7] The result is a special case of a theorem by [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126) about convergence in [L<sup>*p*</sup> spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lp_spaces) published in 1928.[^o8]
