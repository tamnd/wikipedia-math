# Tom Snijders

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tom Snijders"}

**Thomas Augustinus Benedictus Snijders**[^o3], often simplified as **Tom Snijders** (born 26 September 1949) is [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in the [Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Sciences) at [Nuffield College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_College,_Oxford), one of the constituent colleges of the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) (since 1 October 2006). He is also professor of Methodology at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen), a position he has held for more than twenty years.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Tom Snijders was born in [Tilburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilburg), a son of Jan Snijders, professor of [Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) at Groningen University from 1949 to 1980, and Nan Snijders-Oomen, an internationally well-known [child psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_psychologist) (author of the [SON nonverbal intelligence test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SON-tests), 1943, 1978, 1991).

Snijders grew up in the province of [Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen_%28province%29), in the northern part of the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands). He was awarded a [cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_laude) Ph.D. at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specializing in mathematical statistics, for his thesis *[Asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic) optimality theory for testing problems with restricted alternatives*. His thesis advisor was Willem Schaafsma.[^o5] Since 1985 he worked as a professor of various forms of mathematics serving social science. He was part-time professor of Mathematical Sociology at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) from 1989-1992 and is an honorary senior [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne) in Australia. [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) made him an [honorary doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctor) in 2005, as did [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University) in 2011.[^o6] In 2007 Snijders became correspondent of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o7] In 2008 Snijders was awarded the [Order of Knight of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion).

Many of his former Ph.D. students and postdocs have found academic positions and are at the forefront of their terrain, including Roel Bosker, Marijtje van Duijn, Roger Leenders, Chris Snijders, Albertine J. (Tineke) Oldehinkel, Rafael Wittek, [René Veenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Veenstra), Marcel van Assen, Christian Steglich, [Miranda Lubbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miranda_Lubbers), Per Block, and Nynke Niezink.

## Social networks {#Social_networks}

Snijders is a prominent researcher in the field of statistic methods in [behavioural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioural) and social sciences. In 1998, together with others, he created Stocnet, an open software system for the advanced statistical analysis of social networks. Snijders is working especially in [social network analysis](wikipedia://en/325726): statistical methods for analysing [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network) and in network evolution, in social science, [mathematical sociology](wikipedia://en/4771336) and [mathematical response theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_response_theory), and also in [multilevel models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilevel_models) analysis, about which he wrote a textbook, titled *Multilevel Analysis. An Introduction to Basic and Advanced Multilevel Modeling* (1999). Apart from this he developed new statistical methods for social science applications, often in combination with the development of [computer software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_software) to implement these methods. Together with professor [Patrick Doreian](wikipedia://en/68495818) of the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) Tom Snijders edited the international scientific journal *[Social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Networks_%28journal%29). An international journal of structural analysis*.
