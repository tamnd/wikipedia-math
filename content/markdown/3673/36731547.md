# Rayleigh mixture distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rayleigh mixture distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) a **Rayleigh mixture distribution** is a weighted mixture of multiple [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) where the weightings are equal to the weightings of a [Rayleigh distribution](wikipedia://en/262296).[^o2] Since the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) for a (standard) Rayleigh distribution is given by[^o3]

:::

$$f(x;\sigma) = \frac{x}{\sigma^2} e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2}, \quad x \geq 0,$$

Rayleigh mixture distributions have probability density functions of the form

$$f(x;\sigma,n) = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{re^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}}{\sigma^2} \tau(x,r;n) \,\mathrm{d}r,$$

where $\tau(x,r;n)$ is a well-defined probability density function or [sampling distribution](wikipedia://en/520670).[^o2]

The Rayleigh mixture distribution is one of many types of [compound distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_distribution) in which the appearance of a value in a sample or population might be interpreted as a function of other underlying random variables. Mixture distributions are often used in [mixture models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixture_models), which are used to express probabilities of sub-populations within a larger population.
