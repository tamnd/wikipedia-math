# Great deltoidal icositetrahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great deltoidal icositetrahedron"}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great deltoidal icositetrahedron** (or **great sagittal disdodecahedron**) is the dual of the [nonconvex great rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/4157120). Its faces are darts. Part of each dart lies inside the solid, hence is invisible in solid models.

:::

One of its halves can be rotated by 45 degrees to form the [pseudo great deltoidal icositetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo_great_deltoidal_icositetrahedron), analogous to the pseudo-deltoidal icositetrahedron.

## Proportions {#Proportions}

Faces have three angles of $\arccos(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{2})\approx 31.399\,714\,809\,92^{\circ}$ and one of $360^{\circ}-\arccos(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{2})\approx 265.800\,855\,570\,24^{\circ}$. Its [dihedral angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_angles) equal $\arccos({\frac{-7+4\sqrt{2}}{17}})\approx 94.531\,580\,798\,20^{\circ}$. The ratio between the lengths of the long edges and the short ones equals $2+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}\approx 2.707\,106\,781\,19$.
