# Contraction (operator theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Contraction"}

In [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), a [bounded operator](wikipedia://en/455961) *T*: *X* → *Y* between [normed vector spaces](wikipedia://en/21538) *X* and *Y* is said to be a **contraction** if its [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722) ||*T* || ≤ 1. Every bounded operator becomes a contraction after suitable scaling. The analysis of contractions provides insight into the structure of operators, or a family of operators. The theory of contractions on [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) is largely due to [Béla Szőkefalvi-Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béla_Szőkefalvi-Nagy) and [Ciprian Foias](wikipedia://en/28465086).

:::

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Completely non-unitary contraction"}

## Contractions on a Hilbert space {#Contractions_on_a_Hilbert_space}

If *T* is a contraction acting on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal{H}$, the following basic objects associated with *T* can be defined.

The **defect operators** of *T* are the operators *D<sub>T</sub>* = (1 − *T\*T*)<sup>$\tfrac{1}{2}$</sup> and *D<sub>T\*</sub>* = (1 − *TT\**)<sup>$\tfrac{1}{2}$</sup>. The square root is the [positive semidefinite one](wikipedia://en/2929701) given by the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495). The **defect spaces** $\mathcal{D}_T$ and $\mathcal{D}_{T*}$ are the closure of the ranges Ran(*D<sub>T</sub>*) and Ran(*D<sub>T\*</sub>*) respectively. The positive operator *D<sub>T</sub>* induces an inner product on $\mathcal{H}$. The inner product space can be identified naturally with Ran(*D*<sub>*T*</sub>). A similar statement holds for $\mathcal{D}_{T*}$.

The **defect indices** of *T* are the pair

$$(\dim\mathcal{D}_T, \dim\mathcal{D}_{T^*}).$$

The defect operators and the defect indices are a measure of the non-unitarity of *T*.

A contraction *T* on a Hilbert space can be canonically decomposed into an orthogonal [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687)

$$T = \Gamma \oplus U$$

where *U* is a unitary operator and Γ is *completely non-unitary* in the sense that it has no non-zero [reducing subspaces](wikipedia://en/69116423) on which its restriction is unitary. If *U* = 0, *T* is said to be a **completely non-unitary contraction**. A special case of this decomposition is the [Wold decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_decomposition) for an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777), where Γ is a proper isometry.

Contractions on Hilbert spaces can be viewed as the operator analogs of cosθ and are called **operator angles** in some contexts. The explicit description of contractions leads to (operator-)parametrizations of positive and unitary matrices.

:::

## Dilation theorem for contractions {#Dilation_theorem_for_contractions}

[Sz.-Nagy's dilation theorem](wikipedia://en/9750065), proved in 1953, states that for any contraction *T* on a Hilbert space *H*, there is a [unitary operator](wikipedia://en/225999) *U* on a larger Hilbert space *K* ⊇ *H* such that if *P* is the [orthogonal projection](wikipedia://en/519182#Orthogonal_projections) of *K* onto *H* then $T^n = P U^n P^*$ for all *n* > 0. The operator *U* is called a [dilation](wikipedia://en/3319942) of *T* and is uniquely determined if *U* is minimal, i.e. *K* is the smallest closed subspace invariant under *U* and *U*\* containing *H*.

In fact define[^o6]

$$\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}=H\oplus H\oplus H \oplus \cdots ,}$$

the orthogonal direct sum of countably many copies of *H*.

Let *V* be the isometry on $\mathcal H$ defined by

$$\displaystyle{V(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,\dots)=(T\xi_1, \sqrt{I-T^*T}\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,\dots).}$$

Let

$$\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{H} \oplus \mathcal{H}.}$$

Define a unitary *W* on $\mathcal K$ by

$$\displaystyle{W(x,y)=(Vx+(I-VV^*)y,-V^*y).}$$

*W* is then a unitary dilation of *T* with *H* considered as the first component of $\mathcal{H}\subset \mathcal{K}$.

The minimal dilation *U* is obtained by taking the restriction of *W* to the closed subspace generated by powers of *W* applied to *H*.

:::

## Dilation theorem for contraction semigroups {#Dilation_theorem_for_contraction_semigroups}

There is an alternative proof of Sz.-Nagy's dilation theorem, which allows significant generalization.[^o9]

Let *G* be a group, *U*(*g*) a unitary representation of *G* on a Hilbert space *K* and *P* an orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace *H* = *PK* of *K*.

The operator-valued function

$$\displaystyle{\Phi(g)=PU(g)P,}$$

with values in operators on *K* satisfies the positive-definiteness condition

$$\sum \lambda_i\overline{\lambda_j} \Phi(g_j^{-1}g_i) = PT^*TP\ge 0,$$

where

$$\displaystyle{T=\sum \lambda_i U(g_i).}$$

Moreover,

$$\displaystyle{\Phi(1)=P.}$$

Conversely, every operator-valued positive-definite function arises in this way. Recall that every (continuous) scalar-valued positive-definite function on a topological group induces an inner product and group representation φ(*g*) = 〈*U<sub>g</sub> v*, *v*〉 where *U<sub>g</sub>* is a (strongly continuous) unitary representation (see [Bochner's theorem](wikipedia://en/2369850)). Replacing *v*, a rank-1 projection, by a general projection gives the operator-valued statement. In fact the construction is identical; this is sketched below.

Let $\mathcal H$ be the space of functions on *G* of finite support with values in *H* with inner product

$$\displaystyle{(f_1,f_2)=\sum_{g,h} (\Phi(h^{-1}g)f_1(g),f_2(h)).}$$

*G* acts unitarily on $\mathcal H$ by

$$\displaystyle{U(g)f(x)=f(g^{-1}x).}$$

Moreover, *H* can be identified with a closed subspace of $\mathcal H$ using the isometric embedding sending *v* in *H* to *f*<sub>*v*</sub> with

$$f_v(g)=\delta_{g,1} v. \,$$

If *P* is the projection of $\mathcal H$ onto *H*, then

$$\displaystyle{PU(g)P=\Phi(g),}$$

using the above identification.

When *G* is a [separable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_topological_space) topological group, Φ is continuous in the strong (or weak) [operator topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_topology) if and only if *U* is.

In this case functions supported on a countable dense subgroup of *G* are dense in $\mathcal H$, so that $\mathcal H$ is separable.

When *G* = **Z** any contraction operator *T* defines such a function Φ through

$$\displaystyle \Phi(0)=I, \,\,\, \Phi(n)=T^n,\,\,\, \Phi(-n)=(T^*)^n,$$

for *n* > 0. The above construction then yields a minimal unitary dilation.

The same method can be applied to prove a second dilation theorem of Sz.-Nagy for a one-parameter strongly continuous contraction semigroup *T*(*t*) (*t* ≥ 0) on a Hilbert space *H*. (Cooper 1947) had previously proved the result for one-parameter semigroups of isometries.[^o11]

The theorem states that there is a larger Hilbert space *K* containing *H* and a unitary representation *U*(*t*) of **R** such that

$$\displaystyle{T(t)=PU(t)P}$$

and the translates *U*(*t*)*H* generate *K*.

In fact *T*(*t*) defines a continuous operator-valued positive-definite function Φ on **R** through

$$\displaystyle{\Phi(0)=I, \,\,\, \Phi(t)=T(t),\,\,\, \Phi(-t)= T(t)^*,}$$

for *t* > 0. Φ is positive-definite on cyclic subgroups of **R**, by the argument for **Z**, and hence on **R** itself by continuity.

The previous construction yields a minimal unitary representation *U*(*t*) and projection *P*.

The [Hille–Yosida theorem](wikipedia://en/1578212) assigns a closed [unbounded operator](wikipedia://en/1422584) *A* to every contractive [one-parameter semigroup](wikipedia://en/1644938) *T**(*****t*) through***

$$\displaystyle{A\xi=\lim_{t\downarrow 0} {1\over t}(T(t)-I)\xi,}$$

where the domain on *A* consists of all ξ for which this limit exists.

*A* is called the **generator** of the semigroup and satisfies

$$\displaystyle{-\Re (A\xi,\xi)\ge 0}$$

on its domain. When *A* is a self-adjoint operator

$$\displaystyle{T(t)=e^{At},}$$

in the sense of the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) and this notation is used more generally in semigroup theory.

The **cogenerator** of the semigroup is the contraction defined by

$$\displaystyle{T=(A+I)(A-I)^{-1}.}$$

*A* can be recovered from *T* using the formula

$$\displaystyle{A=(T+I)(T-I)^{-1}.}$$

In particular a dilation of *T* on *K* ⊃ *H* immediately gives a dilation of the semigroup.[^o12]

## Functional calculus {#Functional_calculus}

Let *T* be totally non-unitary contraction on *H*. Then the minimal unitary dilation *U* of *T* on *K* ⊃ *H* is unitarily equivalent to a direct sum of copies the bilateral shift operator, i.e. multiplication by *z* on L<sup>2</sup>(*S*<sup>1</sup>).[^o14]

If *P* is the orthogonal projection onto *H* then for *f* in L<sup>∞</sup> = L<sup>∞</sup>(*S*<sup>1</sup>) it follows that the operator *f*(*T*) can be defined by

$$\displaystyle{f(T)\xi=Pf(U)\xi.}$$

Let H<sup>∞</sup> be the space of bounded holomorphic functions on the unit disk *D*. Any such function has boundary values in L<sup>∞</sup> and is uniquely determined by these, so that there is an embedding H<sup>∞</sup> ⊂ L<sup>∞</sup>.

For *f* in H<sup>∞</sup>, *f*(*T*) can be defined without reference to the unitary dilation.

In fact if

$$\displaystyle{f(z)=\sum_{n\ge 0} a_n z^n}$$

for |*z*| \< 1, then for *r* \< 1

$$\displaystyle{f_r(z))=\sum_{n\ge 0} r^n a_n z^n}$$

is holomorphic on |*z*| \< 1/*r*.

In that case *f*<sub>*r*</sub>(*T*) is defined by the holomorphic functional calculus and *f* (*T* ) can be defined by

$$\displaystyle{f(T)\xi=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1} f_r(T)\xi.}$$

The map sending *f* to *f*(*T*) defines an algebra homomorphism of H<sup>∞</sup> into bounded operators on *H*. Moreover, if

$$\displaystyle{f^\sim(z)=\sum_{n\ge 0} a_n \overline{z}^n,}$$

then

$$\displaystyle{f^\sim(T)=f(T^*)^*.}$$

This map has the following continuity property: if a uniformly bounded sequence *f*<sub>*n*</sub> tends almost everywhere to *f*, then *f*<sub>*n*</sub>(*T*) tends to *f*(*T*) in the strong operator topology.

For *t* ≥ 0, let *e*<sub>*t*</sub> be the inner function

$$\displaystyle{e_t(z)=\exp t{z+1\over z-1}.}$$

If *T* is the cogenerator of a one-parameter semigroup of completely non-unitary contractions *T*(*t*), then

$$\displaystyle{T(t)=e_t(T)}$$

and

$$\displaystyle{T={1\over 2}I -{1\over 2}\int_0^\infty e^{-t}T(t)\, dt.}$$

## C<sub>0</sub> contractions {#C0_contractions}

A completely non-unitary contraction *T* is said to belong to the class C<sub>0</sub> if and only if *f*(*T*) = 0 for some non-zero *f* in H<sup>∞</sup>. In this case the set of such *f* forms an ideal in H<sup>∞</sup>. It has the form φ ⋅ H<sup>∞</sup> where *g* is an [inner function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_function), i.e. such that |φ| = 1 on *S*<sup>1</sup>: φ is uniquely determined up to multiplication by a complex number of modulus 1 and is called the **minimal function** of *T*. It has properties analogous to the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667107) of a matrix.

The minimal function φ admits a canonical factorization

$$\displaystyle{\varphi(z) = c B(z) e^{-P(z)},}$$

where |*c*|=1, *B*(*z*) is a [Blaschke product](wikipedia://en/981855)

$$\displaystyle{B(z)=\prod \left[{|\lambda_i|\over \lambda_i} {\lambda_i -z \over 1-\overline{\lambda}_i }\right]^{m_i},}$$

with

$$\displaystyle{\sum m_i(1-|\lambda_i|) <\infty,}$$

and *P*(*z*) is holomorphic with non-negative real part in *D*. By the [Herglotz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/34151069#Herglotz_representation_theorem_for_holomorphic_functions),

$$\displaystyle{P(z) =\int_0^{2\pi} {1 + e^{-i\theta}z\over 1 -e^{-i\theta}z} \, d\mu(\theta)}$$

for some non-negative finite measure μ on the circle: in this case, if non-zero, μ must be [singular](wikipedia://en/4304500) with respect to Lebesgue measure. In the above decomposition of φ, either of the two factors can be absent.

The minimal function φ determines the [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum) of *T*. Within the unit disk, the spectral values are the zeros of φ. There are at most countably many such λ<sub>i</sub>, all eigenvalues of *T*, the zeros of *B*(*z*). A point of the unit circle does not lie in the spectrum of *T* if and only if φ has a holomorphic continuation to a neighborhood of that point.

φ reduces to a Blaschke product exactly when *H* equals the closure of the direct sum (not necessarily orthogonal) of the generalized eigenspaces[^o16]

$$\displaystyle{H_i=\{\xi:(T-\lambda_i I)^{m_i} \xi=0\}.}$$

## Quasi-similarity {#Quasi-similarity}

Two contractions *T*<sub>1</sub> and *T*<sub>2</sub> are said to be **quasi-similar** when there are bounded operators *A*, *B* with trivial kernel and dense range such that

$$\displaystyle{AT_1=T_2A,\,\,\, BT_2=T_1B.}$$

The following properties of a contraction *T* are preserved under quasi-similarity:

- being unitary
- being completely non-unitary
- being in the class C<sub>0</sub>
- being **multiplicity free**, i.e. having a commutative [commutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutant)

Two quasi-similar C<sub>0</sub> contractions have the same minimal function and hence the same spectrum.

The **[classification theorem](wikipedia://en/4576710)** for C<sub>0</sub> contractions states that two multiplicity free C<sub>0</sub> contractions are quasi-similar if and only if they have the same minimal function (up to a scalar multiple).[^o18]

A model for multiplicity free C<sub>0</sub> contractions with minimal function φ is given by taking

$$\displaystyle{H=H^2\ominus \varphi H^2,}$$

where H<sup>2</sup> is the [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780) of the circle and letting *T* be multiplication by *z*.[^o19]

Such operators are called **Jordan blocks** and denoted *S*(φ).

As a generalization of [Beurling's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beurling's_theorem), the commutant of such an operator consists exactly of operators ψ(*T*) with ψ in *H*<sup>≈</sup>, i.e. multiplication operators on *H*<sup>2</sup> corresponding to functions in *H*<sup>≈</sup>.

A C<sub>0</sub> contraction operator *T* is multiplicity free if and only if it is quasi-similar to a Jordan block (necessarily corresponding the one corresponding to its minimal function).

**Examples.**

- If a contraction *T* if quasi-similar to an operator *S* with

$$\displaystyle{Se_i=\lambda_i e_i}$$

with the λ<sub>i</sub>'s distinct, of modulus less than 1, such that

$$\displaystyle{\sum (1-|\lambda_i|) < 1}$$

and (*e*<sub>*i*</sub>) is an orthonormal basis, then *S*, and hence *T*, is C<sub>0</sub> and multiplicity free. Hence *H* is the closure of direct sum of the λ<sub>i</sub>-eigenspaces of *T*, each having multiplicity one. This can also be seen directly using the definition of quasi-similarity.

- The results above can be applied equally well to one-parameter semigroups, since, from the functional calculus, two semigroups are quasi-similar if and only if their cogenerators are quasi-similar.[^o20]

**Classification theorem for C<sub>0</sub> contractions:** *Every C<sub>0</sub> contraction is canonically quasi-similar to a direct sum of Jordan blocks.*

In fact every C<sub>0</sub> contraction is quasi-similar to a unique operator of the form

$$\displaystyle{S=S(\varphi_1)\oplus S(\varphi_1\varphi_2)\oplus S(\varphi_1\varphi_2\varphi_3) \oplus \cdots }$$

where the φ<sub>*n*</sub> are uniquely determined inner functions, with φ<sub>*1*</sub> the minimal function of *S* and hence *T*.[^o21]
