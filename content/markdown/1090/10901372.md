# W. Wesley Peterson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="W. Wesley Peterson"}

**William Wesley Peterson** (April 22, 1924 – May 6, 2009) was an American mathematician and computer scientist. He was best known for designing the [cyclic redundancy check](wikipedia://en/38838) (CRC),[^o3] for which research he was awarded the [Japan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Prize) in 1999.[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Peterson was born on April 22, 1924, in [Muskegon, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskegon,_Michigan) and earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1954 from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan).[^o4][^o6] Peterson was a professor of [Information and Computer Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_Computer_Science) at the [University of Hawaii at Manoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaii_at_Manoa), joining the faculty in 1964.[^o7] He started work at IBM in 1954.[^o7] He authored the publication of algebraic coding theory *Error Correcting Codes* in 1961. He co-authored a number of books on the topic of [error correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_correcting_code), including the revised 2nd edition of *Error Correcting Codes*[^o8] (co-authored with [Edward J. Weldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_J._Weldon)). In the early 1950s, he contributed significantly to the development of [signal detection theory](wikipedia://en/1156527) through participation in the [IRE Professional Group on Information Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRE_Professional_Group_on_Information_Theory).[^o9] He has also done research and published in the fields of [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015),[^o10] [systems programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_programming), and [networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_networking). As well as the [Japan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Prize) in 1999,[^o4][^o11] he was awarded the [Claude E. Shannon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award) in 1981,[^o7] and the [IEEE Centennial Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Centennial_Medal) in 1984.[^o7] In 2007, two years before Peterson's death, [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) added crc32 to the [SSE4.2](wikipedia://en/6821879#SSE4.2) instruction set of the [x86-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86#x86-64) architecture.[^o12]

Peterson finished 16th in the 2005 Honolulu Marathon for males ages 80 to 84.[^o13] He died on May 6, 2009, in [Honolulu, Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu,_Hawaii) survived by five children from two different marriages, his wife, and several grandchildren.[^o6]
