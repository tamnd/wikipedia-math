# John Heysham

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Heysham"}

**John Heysham** M.D. (1753–1834) was an English medical doctor, now remembered as a statistician.[^o2]

*Image: John Heysham.jpg.* John Heysham

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born at [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Lancashire) on 22 November 1753, he was the son of John Heysham, shipowner, by Anne Cumming, the daughter of a Westmoreland farmer. He was educated at a school kept by Quakers at [Yealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yealand,_Cumbria), near [Burton, Westmorland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton,_Westmorland), and then apprenticed for five years to a surgeon at Burton. In 1774 he joined the medical classes at Edinburgh, and graduated M.D. in 1777.[^o4]

In 1778 Heysham settled in practice at [Carlisle, Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle,_Cumberland) and resided there until his death on 23 March 1834. He was buried in St. Mary's Church, and a memorial window was placed at the east end of the south aisle of the cathedral.[^o4]

Heysham's practice was a modest financial success. With the help of the dean and chapter he established the first dispensary for the poor at Carlisle. A strong Tory and supporter of the Lonsdale family most of his life, he joined the reform movement in 1832. A biography was written by [Henry Lonsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lonsdale).[^o4]

## Works {#Works}

Heysham's thesis was *De rabie canina*. He published also *An Account of the Jail Fever at Carlisle in 1781* (London, 1782).[^o4]

In 1779 Heysham began a series of statistical observations, by which he is now best known. They comprised a record of the annual births, marriages, diseases, and deaths in Carlisle for ten years (to 1788), including a census of the inhabitants in 1780, and again in 1788. These statistics, which were published with remarks on them at Carlisle in 1797, were used in 1815 by [Joshua Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Milne), actuary of the [Sun Life Assurance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Life_Assurance_Office), as the basis of a well-known [life table](wikipedia://en/1614128), the "[Carlisle Table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_Table)".[^o4] It appeared in Milne's *Treatise on the Valuation of Annuities and Assurances on Lives and Survivorships* vol. I.[^o6] The table was still relied upon in 1851, for the [Institute of Actuaries](wikipedia://en/852871) benchmark published by [Peter Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gray_%28writer%29), William Orchard and Henry Ambrose Smith.[^o7] The Institute's revision of 1870 caused the obsolescence of the table.[^o8]

Heysham was also a naturalist, his observations on the flora and fauna of his district being recorded in [William Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hutchinson_%28topographer%29)'s *History of the County of Cumberland*. He was close to the [Carlisle Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_Cathedral) chapter, and was thought to have assisted Archdeacon [William Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Paley) on questions of [design in nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_in_nature).[^o4]
