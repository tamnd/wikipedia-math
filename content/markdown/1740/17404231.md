# Conjugate Fourier series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conjugate Fourier series"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), the **conjugate Fourier series** arises by realizing the Fourier series formally as the boundary values of the [real part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part) of a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) on the [unit disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_disc). The [imaginary part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_part) of that function then defines the conjugate series. (Zygmund 1968) studied the delicate questions of convergence of this series, and its relationship with the [Hilbert transform](wikipedia://en/574024).

:::

In detail, consider a [trigonometric series](wikipedia://en/4099125) of the form

$$f(\theta) = \tfrac12 a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_n\cos n\theta + b_n\sin n\theta\right)$$

in which the coefficients *a*<sub>*n*</sub> and *b*<sub>*n*</sub> are [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). This series is the real part of the [power series](wikipedia://en/59958)

$$F(z) = \tfrac12 a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty (a_n-ib_n)z^n$$

along the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) with $z=e^{i\theta}$. The imaginary part of *F*(*z*) is called the **conjugate series** of *f*, and is denoted

$$\tilde{f}(\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_n\sin n\theta - b_n\cos n\theta\right).$$
