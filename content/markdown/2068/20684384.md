# Infinite broom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infinite broom"}

*Image: Infinite broom.svg.* Standard infinite broom

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **infinite broom** is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) that is used as an example distinguishing various notions of [connectedness](wikipedia://en/6233). The **closed infinite broom** is the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of the infinite broom, and is also referred to as the **broom space**.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The infinite broom is the subset of the Euclidean plane that consists of all [closed line segments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_line_segment) joining the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) to the point (1, 1/*n*) as *n* varies over all [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer), together with the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) (½, 1\] on the *x*-axis.[^o5]

The closed infinite broom is then the infinite broom together with the interval (0, ½\] on the *x*-axis. In other words, it consists of all closed line segments joining the origin to the point (1, 1/*n*) or to the point (1, 0).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Both the infinite broom and its closure are [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), as every [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in the plane which contains the segment on the *x*-axis must intersect slanted segments. Neither is [locally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_connected). Despite the closed infinite broom being [arc connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_connected), the standard infinite broom is not [path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected).[^o5]

The interval \[0,1\] on the *x*-axis is a [deformation retract](wikipedia://en/2120001#Deformation_retract_and_strong_deformation_retract) of the closed infinite broom, but it is not a *strong* deformation retract.

:::
