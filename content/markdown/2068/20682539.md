# Mark Sainsbury (philosopher)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Mark Sainsbury"}

**Richard Mark Sainsbury** (; born 7 February 1943) is a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) who is Professor of Philosophy at the [University of Texas, Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas,_Austin). He is known for his work in [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), and on the philosophies of [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) and [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Sainsbury earned his [D.Phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Phil.) at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) and taught for many years at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) where he was Susan Stebbing Professor of Philosophy.[^o5] He became professor of philosophy at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) in 2002.[^o6] He was editor of the leading philosophy journal *[Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_%28journal%29)* from 1990 to 2000. He was elected a Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy) in 1998.[^o7]

## Books {#Books}

- *Bertrand Russell* (Routledge, 1979) ("Arguments of the Philosophers" series).
- *Paradoxes* (Cambridge University Press, 1988).
- *Reference Without Referents* (Oxford University Press, 2005).
- *Fiction and Fictionalism* (Routledge, 2009).
- *Seven Puzzles of Thought and How to Solve Them: An Originalist Theory of Concepts* (with [Michael Tye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Tye_%28philosopher%29)) (Oxford University Press, 2012).
- *Thinking About Things* (Oxford University Press, 2018) .
