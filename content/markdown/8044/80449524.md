# Josephine Wairimu Kagunda

Academics

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Josephine Wairimu Kagunda"}

**Josephine Wairimu Kagunda** is a Kenyan [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [epidemiologist](wikipedia://en/66997). She works on developing deterministic models of [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_diseases) to support the planning of effective public health interventions. She is a member, president and the chair of the [African Women in Mathematics Association](wikipedia://en/66387234) (AWMA) and a prominent member of the Kenya Women in Mathematical Sciences Association (KWIMSA). She is a reviewer for the international peer-reviewed journal the *Universal Journal of Applied Mathematics*.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kagunda grew up in a Kenyan village. Her father fundraised to enable her to study at university.[^o4]

Kagunda studied her bachelor's degree at the [University of Nairobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nairobi). She was awarded a PhD in [Biomathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomathematics) from the [University of Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lorraine), France.[^o5] In 2015 she was awarded an [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) (IMU) scholarship to complete a period of research at the [California State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Northridge), [Northridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northridge,_Los_Angeles), Los Angeles, United States of America.[^o6]

Kagunda teaches as a senior lecturer at the School of Mathematics, University of Nairobi[^o4][^o7] and works with the Ohio State Health and Environment Modelling team (HEALMOD) at the College of Public Health, [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio), United States of America.[^o8] She works on developing deterministic [epidemiological](wikipedia://en/66997) models of infectious diseases to support the planning of effective public health interventions. She has published research on diseases including [renal failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_failure),[^o9] [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS),[^o10] [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria)[^o11] and [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis), as well as [insecticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecticide) resistance.[^o11] Her work has been cited in further studies on the impact of temperature in malaria disease transmission dynamics[^o12] and modelling HIV/AIDS dynamics in Kenya.[^o13] Kagunda has also participated in initiatives to explore the role of data-driven methodologies in pandemic modelling and control with students at specialist mathematics Cimpa Kenya School.[^o14]

Kagunda is a member and the chair of the [African Women in Mathematics Association](wikipedia://en/66387234) (AWMA) and a member of the Kenya Women in Mathematical Sciences Association (KWIMSA).[^o4][^o15][^o16] She is a reviewer for the international peer-reviewed journal the *Universal Journal of Applied Mathematics*.[^o17]

In 2023, Kagunda presented at the SciDataCon as part of International Data Week 2023.[^o18] In January 2025, Kagunda presented at the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s Joint Mathematics Meetings AWM Special Session on modelling infectious disease dynamics.[^o19]
