# Størmer number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Størmer number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Størmer number** or **arc-cotangent irreducible number** is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ for which the greatest [prime factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) of $n^2+1$ is greater than or equal to $2n$. They are named after [Carl Størmer](wikipedia://en/1906523).

:::

## Density {#Density}

[John Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Todd_%28computer_scientist%29) proved that this sequence is neither [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) nor [cofinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite).

::: {#o4 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the natural density of the Størmer numbers?

:::

More precisely, the [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) of the Størmer numbers lies between 0.5324 and 0.905. It has been conjectured that their natural density is the [natural logarithm of 2](wikipedia://en/27153398), approximately 0.693, but this remains unproven. Because the Størmer numbers have positive density, the Størmer numbers form a [large set](wikipedia://en/3152856).

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

The Størmer numbers arise in connection with the problem of representing the [Gregory numbers](wikipedia://en/15992030) ([arctangents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctangent) of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024)) $G_{a/b}=\arctan\frac{b}{a}$ as sums of Gregory numbers for integers (arctangents of [unit fractions](wikipedia://en/491316)). The Gregory number $G_{a/b}$ may be decomposed by repeatedly multiplying the [Gaussian integer](wikipedia://en/48628) $a+bi$ by numbers of the form $n\pm i$, in order to cancel prime factors $p$ from the imaginary part; here $n$ is chosen to be a Størmer number such that $n^2+1$ is divisible by $p$.
