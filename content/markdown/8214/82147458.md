# Perfect rectangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perfect rectangle"}

*Image: Schramm-Biermann Perfektes Rechteck 47x65.jpg.* Perfect rectangle made of 10 squares in [concrete art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_art) (Image by the painter Irene Schramm-Biermann)

A **perfect rectangle** is a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) that can be divided into [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) of different sizes. If a perfect rectangle is specifically a square, it is analogously called a [perfect square](wikipedia://en/67903).

:::

A rectangle that is not perfect is also called an **imperfect rectangle**.[^o2]

## Discoverers of Perfect Rectangles (Selection) {#Discoverers_of_Perfect_Rectangles_(Selection)}

Many [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) have been involved in the discovery of perfect rectangles and perfect squares.

Below is a selection of important discoveries in this field.

- 1925: Zbigniew Moroń decomposed a perfect smallest possible 33x32 rectangle into nine squares.
- 1939: The [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) mathematician [Roland Sprague](wikipedia://en/42359462) published a large perfect square with 55 squares.
- 1978: A. J. W. Duijvestijn dissected a perfect square into 21 squares with a total side length of 112, where 21 is the lowest possible number of subsquares of perfect squares.[^o4]
