# Federico Cafiero

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Federico Cafiero"}

**Federico Cafiero** (24 May 1914 – 7 May 1980) was an Italian mathematician known for his contributions in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), [measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and [integration theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_%28mathematics%29), and in the theory of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753). In particular, generalizing the [Vitali convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/13197969), the [Fichera convergence theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fichera_convergence_theorem) and previous results of [Vladimir Mikhailovich Dubrovskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Mikhailovich_Dubrovskii), he proved a necessary and sufficient condition for the passage to the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602) under the sign of [integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_%28mathematics%29):[^o5] this result is, in some sense, definitive.[^o6] In the field of ordinary differential equations, he studied existence and uniqueness problems under very general hypotheses for the left member of the given first-order equation, developing an important approximation method and proving a fundamental uniqueness theorem.[^o7]

:::

## Life and academic career {#Life_and_academic_career}

Cafiero was born in [Riposto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riposto), [Province of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Catania), on May 24, 1914.[^o9] He obtained his [Laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) in mathematics, [cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_laude), from the [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II) in 1939.[^o10] During the 1939–1940 [academic year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_year), he won an "[Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Alta_Matematica)" scholarship and went in Rome to the institute:[^o11] there he followed the courses held by [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716), [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009), [Luigi Fantappiè](wikipedia://en/7255073), [Giulio Krall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Krall) and [Leonida Tonelli](wikipedia://en/10018991).[^o12]

### World War II years: 1941–1943 {#World_War_II_years:_1941–1943}

He was appointed instructor of the course of "Elementi di matematica"[^o14] by the Faculty of Statistical Sciences of the University of Rome, for the 1940–1941 academic year:[^o15] however, he was able to hold the course only for a few months, since he was called to arms in January 1941[^o16] and stationed from May 1942 to September 1943 on the Northern African coasts as an [officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer) of the [San Marco Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marco_Regiment).[^o17] It was there that, after having successfully completed a dangerous [sabotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabotage) operation, the [Armistice between Italy and Allied armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_between_Italy_and_Allied_armed_forces) surprised him and the other members of his unit, leaving them without any support.[^o16] Nonetheless, in desperate conditions, he was able to lead his men to the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italia) coasts with a [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber) [dinghy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinghy), and was awarded a [Silver Medal of Military Valor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Medal_of_Military_Valor) for this act.[^o16]

### Rebuilding and researching: the years 1944–1953 {#Rebuilding_and_researching:_the_years_1944–1953}

After being discharged from [Military Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Service) in February 1944,[^o10] he remained in Naples.[^o16]

The Institute of Mathematics of the University of Naples was in the process of reconstituting[^o19] as the eight former mathematics institutes of the university was literally "torn to pieces" by the [Allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) [Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police).[^o20]

Reconstitution consisted of collecting and reordering all the volumes of the previously existing library, then piled on the floor of a single room, catalogue them [ex novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_novo) and create new records and provide the library administration for the new library as there was no administrative personnel available nor financial resources.[^o21]

work was done to organize courses and exams for the numerous war veterans coming back from the front and for new students, with more than a half of the teaching personnel blocked beyond the [Gothic Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Line):[^o20] and in performing all those task Cafiero, jointly with few others and working as an adjunct professor of "Esercitazioni di Matematiche", was a collaborator of [Renato Caccioppoli](wikipedia://en/4262313) and [Carlo Miranda](wikipedia://en/49974918).[^o22]

Also in 1944 he married Jole Giorgini, his lifelong companion, and soon after they had a daughter, Anna.[^o10]

Due to the scarce possibilities of being hired permanently by the Faculty of Sciences at that time, he accepted a position as adjunct [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) to the chair of [Financial Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Mathematics),[^o23] working with Luigi Lordi first at the [Istituto Universitario Navale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Universitario_Navale) and then to the Faculty of Economics and Business, where he was appointed full [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) in June 1949.[^o24] Nonetheless, his ties with the Faculty of Sciences remained strong, being employed there as an adjunct professor of "Esercitazioni di Matematiche" several times, during those years:[^o25] he was likewise assigned to several other courses related to Financial Mathematics by the Istituto Universitario Navale and by the Faculty of Business and Economics.[^o25][^o26]

The scientific aspect of the collaboration with the Faculty of Sciences was nonetheless very intense,[^o25] leading him to the "libera docenza" in March 1951, and to a full professorship chair in 1953:[^o27][^o28] during this period, his scientific activity was done side by side at first with Carlo Miranda and later with Renato Caccioppoli, who found in him a dear pupil and friend.[^o29]

Ranked first of the three winners of the competition for the chair of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) of the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania),[^o30] in December 1953 he was appointed as [extraordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_professor) to that chair, and left Naples for Catania.[^o31][^o32]

### First in Catania and then in Pisa: the years 1954–1959 {#First_in_Catania_and_then_in_Pisa:_the_years_1954–1959}

Cafiero started his service at the University of Catania in January 1954.[^o34] His arrival at the university brought several innovations, both in teaching and in the research activity on [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).[^o32][^o35] In particular, he established a [seminar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminar) on abstract measure theory open to assistant professors and to graduate students as well, and this was felt as true scientific revolution:[^o35] he held the chair of mathematical analysis for three years.[^o36] After becoming [ordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_professor) in 1956,[^o10] he went to the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa) on request by [Sandro Faedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Faedo):[^o37] during his stay, he held courses also at the [Scuola Normale Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore),[^o38] becoming director of the "*[Leonida Tonelli](wikipedia://en/10018991)*" Institute and member of [Board of directors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_directors) of the [Centro Studi Calcolatrici Elettroniche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_Studi_Calcolatrici_Elettroniche).[^o39]

### Honors {#Honors}

Apart from the silver medal awarded him for his valor acts during the military service,[^o16] the importance of his scientific achievements was acknowledged several times. In 1952 he received the Tenore Prize of the Accademia Pontaniana for a memoir on the theory of integration,[^o3] later published as the paper (Cafiero 1953) and the monograph (Cafiero 1953a). On May 25, 1954 he was elected *resident corresponding member* of the [Accademia Gioenia di Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Gioenia_di_Catania) during his stay at the University of Catania, and became *non resident corresponding member* from November 16, 1956 on, after his moving to Pisa and then to Naples.[^o43]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

The papers of Federico Cafiero listed in this section are also included in his "*Opere scelte*" (Cafiero 1996), which collects all his published notes and one of his books.

- Cafiero, Federico. (1953). "Sul passaggio al limite sotto il segno d'integrale per successioni d'integrali di Stieltjes-Lebesgue negli spazi astratti, con masse variabili con gli integrandi". *Rendiconti del Seminario Matematico della Università di Padova*. 22. p. 223–245. mr:57951. zbl:0052.05003. <http://www.numdam.org/item?id=RSMUP_1953__22__223_0>, is the first paper where Federico Cafiero states and proves his convergence theorem.
- Cafiero, Federico. (1953a). "Funzioni additive d'insieme e integrazione negli spazi astratti". Libreria Editrice Liguori. p. 178. mr:0056671. zbl:0050.27801, is the prize-winning first monograph where Federico Cafiero states and proves his convergence theorem.
- Cafiero, Federico. (1959). "Misura e integrazione". 5. Edizioni Cremonese. p. VII+451. mr:0215954. zbl:0171.01503. <https://archive.org/embed/consiglionaziona0005fede>, is a definitive monograph on integration and measure theory: the treatment of the limiting behaviour of the integral of various kinds of [sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequences) of measure-related structures (measurable functions, [measurable sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set), measures and their combinations) is somewhat conclusive.
- Cafiero, Federico. (1996). "Opere scelte". Giannini Editore. p. 701. Federico Cafiero's "*Selected works*", including all his published papers, three postcards from his master Renato Caccioppoli concerning his research and his book "*Lezioni sulla teoria delle funzioni di variabili reali*" ("Lectures on the theory of functions of real variables").
