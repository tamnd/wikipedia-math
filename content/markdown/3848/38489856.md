# Ladyzhenskaya's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ladyzhenskaya's inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Ladyzhenskaya's inequality** is any of a number of related functional inequalities named after the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_people) [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Olga Aleksandrovna Ladyzhenskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Aleksandrovna_Ladyzhenskaya). The original such inequality, for functions of two real variables, was introduced by Ladyzhenskaya in 1958 to prove the existence and uniqueness of long-time solutions to the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395) in two spatial dimensions (for smooth enough initial data). There is an analogous inequality for functions of three real variables, but the exponents are slightly different; much of the difficulty in establishing existence and uniqueness of solutions to the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations stems from these different exponents. Ladyzhenskaya's inequality is one member of a broad class of inequalities known as [interpolation inequalities](wikipedia://en/38508663).

:::

Let $\Omega$ be a [Lipschitz domain](wikipedia://en/7820612) in $\mathbb R^{n}$ for $n = 2 \text{ or } 3$ and let $u: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb R$ be a [weakly differentiable](wikipedia://en/1411087) function that vanishes on the boundary of $\Omega$ in the sense of [trace](wikipedia://en/11199199) (that is, $u$ is a limit in the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $H^1(\Omega)$ of a sequence of [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) that are [compactly supported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_supported) in $\Omega$). Then there exists a constant $C$ depending only on $\Omega$ such that, in the case $n = 2$:

$$\| u \|_{L^{4}} \leq C \| u \|_{L^{2}}^{1/2} \| \nabla u \|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}$$

and in the case $n = 3$:

$$\| u \|_{L^4} \leq C \| u \|_{L^2}^{1/4} \| \nabla u \|_{L^2}^{3/4}$$

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

- Both the two- and three-dimensional versions of Ladyzhenskaya's inequality are special cases of the [Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality](wikipedia://en/38500906)

$$\| u \|_{L^p} \leq C \| u \|_{L^q}^\alpha \| u \|_{H_0^s}^{1-\alpha},$$

which holds whenever

$$p > q \geq 1, s > n ( \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{p} ), \text{ and } \tfrac{1}{p} = \tfrac{\alpha}{q} + (1 - \alpha) ( \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{s}{n} ).$$

Ladyzhenskaya's inequalities are the special cases $p = 4, q = 2, s = 1$ $\alpha = \tfrac{1}{2}$ when $n = 2$ and $\alpha = \tfrac{1}{4}$ when $n = 3$.

- A simple modification of the argument used by Ladyzhenskaya in her 1958 paper (see e.g. Constantin & Seregin 2010) yields the following inequality for $u: \mathbb R^{2} \rightarrow \mathbb R$, valid for all $r \ge 2$:

$$\| u \|_{L^{2r}} \leq C r \| u \|_{L^r}^{1/2} \| \nabla u \|_{L^2}^{1/2}.$$

- The usual Ladyzhenskaya inequality on $\mathbb R^{n}, n = 2 \text{ or } 3$, can be generalized (see McCormick & al. 2013) to use the [weak $L^{2}$ "norm"](wikipedia://en/45194#Weak_Lp) of $u$ in place of the usual $L^{2}$ norm:

$$\| u \|_{L^{4}} \leq \begin{cases} C \| u \|_{L^{2,\infty}}^{1/2} \| \nabla u \|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}, & n = 2, \\ C \| u \|_{L^{2,\infty}}^{1/4} \| \nabla u \|_{L^{2}}^{3/4}, & n = 3. \end{cases}$$

:::
