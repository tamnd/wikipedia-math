# Spectrahedron

*Image: S asdf rot.png.* A spectrahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spectrahedron"}

In [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/4165882), a **spectrahedron** is a shape that can be represented as a [linear matrix inequality](wikipedia://en/5410577). Alternatively, the set of $n \times n$ [positive semidefinite matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_semidefinite_matrix) forms a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) in $\mathbf{R}^{n \times n}$, and a spectrahedron is a shape that can be formed by intersecting this cone with an [affine subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_subspace).

:::

Spectrahedra are the [feasible regions](wikipedia://en/9157119) of [semidefinite programs](wikipedia://en/4993539).[^o2] The images of spectrahedra under [linear or affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449) are called *projected spectrahedra* or *spectrahedral shadows*. Every spectrahedral shadow is a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) that is also [semialgebraic](wikipedia://en/13329722), but the converse (conjectured to be true until 2017) is false.[^o3]

An example of a spectrahedron is the **spectraplex**, defined as

$$\mathrm{Spect}_n = \{ X \in \mathbf{S}^n_+ \mid \operatorname{Tr}(X) = 1\},$$

where $\mathbf{S}^n_+$ is the set of $n \times n$ positive semidefinite matrices and $\operatorname{Tr}(X)$ is the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of the matrix $X$.[^o4] The spectraplex is a compact set, and can be thought of as the "semidefinite" analog of the [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781).
