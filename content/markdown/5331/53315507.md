# Henry Clarke (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Clarke"}

**Henry Clarke** (1743–1818) was an English mathematician, a significant teacher in north-west England, and mathematical writer and translator. He worked also as a surveyor and science lecturer.[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was the son of Thomas Clarke, born at [Salford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Salford) and baptised 17 April 1743; William Augustus Clarke the [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist) minister and [Protestant Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Association) member was his brother.[^o2][^o4][^o5] He was educated at [Manchester Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Grammar_School), and at age of 13 became assistant in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds) academy of the Quaker Aaron Grimshaw, a [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker). Here he made the acquaintance of [Joseph Priestley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Priestley).[^o6]

After a brief partnership with Robert Pulman, a schoolmaster at [Sedbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedbergh), he travelled in Europe, and returned to settle as a [land surveyor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_surveyor) in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester). He again became a schoolmaster, and the rest of his career was spent in educational posts. He first had a "commercial and mathematical" school in Salford, giving lectures on astronomy and other scientific subjects.[^o6] While the school was not a success in financial terms, Clarke trained future mathematicians, such as Hugh Byrom and [Thomas Molineux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Molineux_%28mathematician%29), and contributors to mathematical periodicals, including the *[Ladies' Diary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies'_Diary)*.[^o2] Clarke in 1788 was an unsuccessful candidate for the salaried mastership of a school in [Stretford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretford).[^o6]

A member of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society), in 1783 Clarke became lecturer in mathematics and experimental philosophy in its "College of Arts and Sciences", which only lasted a few years.[^o2] In 1792 he moved to [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool), and, after returning to Manchester, was at Bristol from 1799 till 1802. He was in that year appointed professor of history, geography, and experimental philosophy at the [Royal Military College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_College,_Sandhurst) at [Great Marlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Marlow), which moved in 1812 to [Sandhurst, Berkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhurst,_Berkshire). In 1802 he was also made LL.D. by the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).[^o6]

Clarke retired on a pension in 1817, and died at [Islington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islington), 29 April 1818. He had been a candidate for the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1783, but was opposed by the influence of [Sir Joseph Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Joseph_Banks), then its President. [Samuel Horsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Horsley), a critic of Banks, singled out this case.[^o6]

## Works {#Works}

Clarke's works were:[^o6]

- *Practical Perspective*, 1776 (for the use of schools).
- *The Rationale of Circulating Numbers*, 1777.
- *Dissertation on the Summation of Infinite Converging Series with Algebraic Divisors*, translated from [Antonio Maria Lorgna](wikipedia://en/46666197), 1779, with appendix. [John Landen](wikipedia://en/1400598) attacked it in a pamphlet, stating that the method was contained in [Thomas Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Simpson)'s *Mathematical Dissertations*. Clarke replied in a *Supplement* (1782), and to a further attack in *Additional Remarks*, 1783. The controversy was noted in [Charles Hutton](wikipedia://en/938017)'s *Mathematical Dictionary* (under "Landen"), the work having been dedicated to Hutton. Clarke was attacked in the *[Monthly Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monthly_Review_%28London%29)* for 1783, and defended by Horsley.[^o2][^o6]
- *The School Candidates*, 1788, a [squib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squib_%28writing%29) on the election to Stretford school. Clarke appears to have published two further pieces, *The Pedagogue* and *The College*, of similar character, about the same time.
- *Tabula Linguarum*, 1793, tables of declension and conjugation in forty languages, a book of outmoded philology.
- *Tachygraphy, or Shorthand improved*, before 1800, based on [John Byrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Byrom)'s system.
- *The Seaman's Desiderata*, 1800, tables for calculating longitude, etc.
- *Animadversions on Dr. Dickson's translation of Carnot's reflections on the Theory of the Infinitissimal Calculus*, 1802. Against William Dickson's translation of [Lazare Carnot](wikipedia://en/26532764).
- *Abstract of Geography*, 1807, school book for Marlow College.
- *Virgil revindicated*, 1809, answer to a tract by Horsley on the "Two Seasons of Honey" in the *[Georgics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgics)* of [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil).

Clarke was a contributor to mathematical journals, especially to the *Ladies' Diary*, edited by Charles Hutton, from 1772 to 1782. He drew some plates for [John Whitaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Whitaker_%28historian%29)'s *History of Manchester*.[^o6]

## Family {#Family}

On 2 April 1766 Clarke married Martha Randle of Manchester. Of 17 children, two sons and four daughters survived him.[^o6]
