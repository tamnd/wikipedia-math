# Yangian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yangian"}

In [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), a **Yangian** is an infinite-dimensional [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714), a type of a [quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678). Yangians first appeared in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) in the work of [Ludvig Faddeev](wikipedia://en/4854639) and his school in the late 1970s and early 1980s concerning the [quantum inverse scattering method](wikipedia://en/25672752). The name *Yangian* was introduced by [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747) in 1985 in honor of [C.N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.N._Yang).

:::

Initially, they were considered a convenient tool to generate the solutions of the quantum [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810).

The center of the Yangian can be described by the quantum determinant.

The Yangian is a degeneration of the quantum [loop algebra](wikipedia://en/1336457) (i.e. the [quantum affine algebra](wikipedia://en/25666636) at vanishing [central charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_charge)).[^o2]

## Description {#Description}

For any finite-dimensional [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) *a*, Drinfeld defined an infinite-dimensional [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) *Y*(*a*), called the **Yangian** of *a*. This Hopf algebra is a deformation of the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) *U*(*a*\[*z*\]) of the Lie algebra of polynomial loops of *a* given by explicit generators and relations. The relations can be encoded by identities involving a rational [*R*-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996). Replacing it with a trigonometric *R*-matrix, one arrives at [affine quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_quantum_group), defined in the same paper of Drinfeld.

In the case of the [general linear Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/113564) *gl*<sub>*N*</sub>, the Yangian admits a simpler description in terms of a single *ternary* (or *RTT*) *relation* on the matrix generators due to Faddeev and coauthors. The Yangian Y(*gl*<sub>*N*</sub>) is defined to be the algebra generated by elements $t_{ij}^{(p)}$ with 1 ≤ *i*, *j* ≤ *N* and *p* ≥ 0, subject to the relations

$$[t_{ij}^{(p+1)}, t_{kl}^{(q)}] - [t_{ij}^{(p)}, t_{kl}^{(q+1)}]= -(t_{kj}^{(p)}t_{il}^{(q)} - t_{kj}^{(q)} t_{il}^{(p)}).$$

Defining $t_{ij}^{(-1)}=\delta_{ij}$, setting

$$T(z) = \sum_{p\ge -1} t_{ij}^{(p)} z^{-p+1}$$

and introducing the [R-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996) *R*(*z*) = I + *z*<sup>−1</sup> *P* on **C**<sup>*N*</sup>$\otimes$**C**<sup>*N*</sup>, where *P* is the operator permuting the tensor factors, the above relations can be written more simply as the [ternary relation](wikipedia://en/3784565):

$$\displaystyle{ R_{12}(z-w) T_{1}(z)T_{2}(w) = T_{2}(w) T_{1}(z) R_{12}(z-w).}$$

The Yangian becomes a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) with comultiplication Δ, counit ε and antipode *s* given by

$$(\Delta \otimes \mathrm{id})T(z)=T_{12}(z)T_{13}(z), \,\, (\varepsilon\otimes \mathrm{id})T(z)= I, \,\, (s\otimes \mathrm{id})T(z)=T(z)^{-1}.$$

At special values of the spectral parameter $(z-w)$, the *R*-matrix degenerates to a rank one projection. This can be used to define the **quantum determinant** of $T(z)$, which generates the center of the Yangian.

The **twisted Yangian** Y<sup>−</sup>(*gl*<sub>*2N*</sub>), introduced by G. I. Olshansky, is the co-ideal generated by the coefficients of

$$\displaystyle{ S(z)=T(z)\sigma T(-z),}$$

where σ is the involution of *gl*<sub>*2N*</sub> given by

$$\displaystyle{\sigma(E_{ij}) = (-1)^{i+j}E_{2N-j+1,2N-i+1}.}$$

## Applications {#Applications}

### Classical representation theory {#Classical_representation_theory}

Grigori I. Olshansky and [Ivan Cherednik](wikipedia://en/33333323) discovered that the Yangian of *gl*<sub>*N*</sub> is closely related with the branching properties of irreducible finite-dimensional representations of general linear algebras. In particular, the classical Gelfand–Tsetlin construction of a basis in the space of such a representation has a natural interpretation in the language of Yangians, studied by Maxim Nazarov and Vitaly Tarasov. Later Olshansky, Nazarov and [Alexander Molev](wikipedia://en/19228635) discovered a generalization of this theory to other [classical Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/10172878), based on the twisted Yangian.

### Physics {#Physics}

The Yangian appears as a symmetry group in different models in physics.

Yangian appears as a symmetry group of one-dimensional exactly solvable models such as [spin chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_model_%28quantum%29), [Hubbard model](wikipedia://en/1702398) and in models of one-dimensional [relativistic quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativistic_quantum_field_theory).

The most famous occurrence is in planar [supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N=4_super_Yang-Mills) in four dimensions, where Yangian structures appear on the level of symmetries of operators,[^o8][^o9] and [scattering amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude) as was discovered by James M. Drummond, Johannes Henn and [Jan Plefka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Christoph_Plefka).

:::

## Representation theory {#Representation_theory}

Irreducible finite-dimensional representations of Yangians were parametrized by Drinfeld in a way similar to the highest weight theory in the representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras. The role of the [highest weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight) is played by a finite set of *Drinfeld polynomials*. Drinfeld also discovered a generalization of the classical [Schur–Weyl duality](wikipedia://en/15291970) between representations of general linear and [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) that involves the Yangian of *sl*<sub>*N*</sub> and the degenerate [affine Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/10361630) (graded Hecke algebra of type A, in [George Lusztig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lusztig)'s terminology).

Representations of Yangians have been extensively studied, but the theory is still under active development.
