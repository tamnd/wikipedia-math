# HP-19C/-29C

HP-19C

*Image: HP-19C.jpg.* HP-19C calculator

*Image: HP29C.jpg.* HP-29C with AC-powered battery charger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="HP-19C/-29C"}

The **HP-19C** and **HP-29C** were scientific/engineering [pocket calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_calculator) made by [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) between 1977 and 1979. They were the most advanced and last models of the "20" family (compare [HP-25](wikipedia://en/4402245)) and included *[Continuous Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_Continuous_memory)* (battery-backed [CMOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMOS) memory) as a standard feature.

:::

The HP-19C included a small [thermal printer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_printer), one of the very few hand-held scientific calculators to offer such a feature (HP-91, HP-92 and [HP-97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP-97) were desktop units and later models like the [HP-41C](wikipedia://en/630715) only supported external printers). Due to the printer's power requirements, the 19C used a battery pack of four [AA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AA_battery)-sized [NiCd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel-cadmium_battery) cells, adding to the weight of the calculator and printer mechanism.

All other capabilities were the same in both models – [RPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_Polish_Notation) expression logic, 98 program memory locations, statistical functions, and 30 registers.

Users could develop software for the HP-29C/19C, such as a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) generator.[^o2] The calculators expanded the HP-25's program capabilities by adding subroutines, increment/decrement looping, relative branching and indirect addressing (via register 0 as index).

HP's internal code name for the 29C was *[Bonnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_and_Clyde)*, the 19C was correspondingly named *[Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_and_Clyde)*.

The HP-19C and HP-29C were introduced at [MSRPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suggested_retail_price) of \$345[^o3] and \$195,[^o4] respectively.

A version adapted to support an additional backward-facing display manufactured by Educational Calculator Devices named EduCALC 29C GD existed as well.[^o5]
