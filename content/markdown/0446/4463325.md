# Cuntz algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cuntz algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cuntz algebra** $\mathcal{O}_n$, named after [Joachim Cuntz](wikipedia://en/38543091), is the [universal C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/668934) generated by $n$ [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) of an infinite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal{H}$ satisfying certain relations.[^o2] These algebras were introduced as the first concrete examples of a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) infinite simple C\*-algebra, meaning that as a Hilbert space, $\mathcal{O}_n$ is isometric to the [sequence space](wikipedia://en/1701075) $l^2(\mathbb{N})$, and it has no non-trivial closed ideals.

:::

These algebras are fundamental to the study of simple infinite C\*-algebras since any such algebra contains, for any given $n$, a subalgebra that has $\mathcal{O}_n$ as quotient.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $n\geq 2$ and $\mathcal{H}$ be a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). Consider the [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) $\mathcal{A}$ generated by a set $\{s_i\}_{i=1}^n$ of [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) (i.e., $s_i^*s_i = 1$) acting on $\mathcal{H}$ satisfying

$$\sum_{i=1}^n s_i s_i^* = 1.$$

This universal C\*-algebra is called the **Cuntz algebra**, denoted by $\mathcal{O}_n$.

A [simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_algebra) C\*-algebra is said to be **purely infinite** if every [hereditary C\*-subalgebra](wikipedia://en/13437009) of it is infinite. $\mathcal{O}_n$ is a separable, simple, purely infinite C\*-algebra. Any simple infinite C\*-algebra contains a subalgebra that has $\mathcal{O}_n$ as a quotient.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

### Classification {#Classification}

The Cuntz algebras are pairwise non-[isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic), i.e., $\mathcal{O}_n$ and $\mathcal{O}_m$ are non-isomorphic for $n\neq m$. The [*K*<sub>0</sub>](wikipedia://en/13314265) group of $\mathcal{O}_n$ is $\mathbb{Z}/(n-1)\mathbb{Z}$, the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) $n-1$. Since $K_0$ is an isomorphism invariant, $\mathcal{O}_n$ and $\mathcal{O}_m$ are non-isomorphic.

### Relation between concrete C\*-algebras and the universal C\*-algebra {#Relation_between_concrete_C*-algebras_and_the_universal_C*-algebra}

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.** The concrete C\*-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ is isomorphic to the universal C\*-algebra $\mathcal{L}$ generated by $n$ generators $s_1,\dots,s_n$ subject to relations $s_i^*s_i=1$ for all $i$ and $\textstyle\sum s_is_i^*=1$.

The proof of the theorem hinges on the following fact: any C\*-algebra generated by $n$ isometries $s_1,\dots,s_n$ with orthogonal ranges contains a copy of the [UHF algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UHF_algebra) $\mathcal{F}$ type $n^\infty$. Namely, $\mathcal{F}$ is spanned by words of the form

$$s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}s_{j_1}^* \cdots s_{j_k}^*,\quad k \geq 0.$$

The \*-subalgebra $\mathcal{F}$, being [approximately finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/17936142), has a unique C\*-norm. The subalgebra $\mathcal{F}$ plays role of the space of *[Fourier coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_coefficient)* for elements of the algebra. A key technical lemma, due to Cuntz, is that an element in the algebra is zero [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) all its Fourier coefficients vanish. Using this, one can show that the quotient map from $\mathcal{L}$ to $\mathcal{A}$ is [injective](wikipedia://en/45196), which proves the theorem.

The UHF algebra $\mathcal{F}$ has a non-unital subalgebra $\mathcal{F}'$ that is canonically isomorphic to $\mathcal{F}$ itself: in the $M_n$ stage of the direct system defining $\mathcal{F}$, consider the rank-1 projection *e*<sub>11</sub>, the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) that is 1 in the upper left corner and zero elsewhere. Propagate this projection through the direct system. At the $M_{n^k}$ stage of the direct system, one has a rank $n^{k-1}$ projection. In the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566), this gives a projection $P$ in $\mathcal{F}$. The corner

$$P \mathcal{F} P = \mathcal{F'}$$

is isomorphic to $\mathcal{F}$. The \*-endomorphism $\phi$ that maps $\mathcal{F}$ onto $\mathcal{F}'$ is implemented by the isometry $s_1$, i.e., $\phi(\cdot)=s_1(\cdot)s_1^*$. $\;\mathcal{O}_n$ is in fact the [crossed product](wikipedia://en/2749495) of $\mathcal{F}$ with the [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) $\phi$.

:::

### Cuntz algebras to represent direct sums {#Cuntz_algebras_to_represent_direct_sums}

The relations defining the Cuntz algebras align with the definition of the [biproduct](wikipedia://en/59574) for [preadditive categories](wikipedia://en/59521). This similarity is made precise in the C\*-category of unital \*-endomorphisms over C\*-algebras. The objects of this category are unital \*-endomorphisms, and morphisms are the elements $a\in A$, where $a:\rho\to\sigma$ if $a\rho(b)=\sigma(b)a$ for every $b\in A$. A unital \*-endomorphism $\rho:A\to A$ is the direct sum of endomorphisms $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, ..., \sigma_n$ if there are isometries $\{S_k\}_{k=1}^n$ satisfying the $\mathcal{O}_n$ relations and

$$\rho(x) = \sum_{k=1}^n S_k\sigma_k(x)S_k^*, \forall x\in A.$$

In this direct sum, the inclusion morphisms are $S_k:\sigma_k\to \rho$, and the projection morphisms are $S_k^*:\rho\to\sigma_k$.

### Generalisations {#Generalisations}

Cuntz algebras have been generalised in many ways. Notable amongst which are the [Cuntz–Krieger algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuntz-Krieger_algebra), [graph C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/46697691) and [k-graph C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/36004020).

:::

## Applied mathematics {#Applied_mathematics}

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), a [subband filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subband_coding) with exact reconstruction give rise to representations of a Cuntz algebra. The same filter also comes from the [multiresolution analysis](wikipedia://en/1963076) construction in [wavelet](wikipedia://en/50903) theory.[^o13]
