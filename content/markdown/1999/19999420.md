# Container (type theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Container"}

In [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282), a discipline within [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **containers** are abstractions which permit various "collection types", such as [lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_%28computing%29) and [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_%28data_structure%29), to be represented in a uniform way. A ([unary](wikipedia://en/161006)) container is defined by a type of *shapes* S and a type family of *positions* P, indexed by S. The *extension* of a container is a family of dependent pairs consisting of a shape (of type S) and a function from positions of that shape to the element type. Containers can be seen as [canonical forms](wikipedia://en/515096) (standard form) for collection types.[^o2]

:::

For lists, the shape type is the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) (including zero). The corresponding position types are the types of natural numbers less than the shape, for each shape.

For trees, the shape type is the type of trees of units (that is, trees with no information in them, just structure). The corresponding position types are isomorphic to the types of valid paths from the root to particular nodes on the shape, for each shape.

Note that the natural numbers are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to lists of units. In general the shape type will always be isomorphic to the original non-generic container type family (list, tree etc.) applied to unit.

One of the main motivations for introducing the notion of containers is to support [generic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_programming) in a [dependently typed](wikipedia://en/1949487) setting.[^o2]

## Categorical aspects {#Categorical_aspects}

The extension of a container is an [endofunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor). It takes a function `g` to

$$\lambda(s,f).(s,g\circ f)$$

This is equivalent to the familiar `map g` in the case of lists, and does something similar for other containers.

Containers form a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) in which a morphism from $S \triangleleft P$ to $T \triangleleft Q$ is a function $u \colon S \to T$ on shapes together with functions $h_s \colon Q\,(u\,s) \to P\,s$ mapping positions *backwards*; it acts on extensions by $\lambda(s,f).(u\,s,\, f \circ h_s)$. Extension is a [full and faithful](wikipedia://en/876534) functor into endofunctors: [natural transformations](wikipedia://en/45022) — in particular [polymorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_polymorphism) functions such as `reverse` — correspond exactly to container morphisms, and are thereby determined by a first-order action on shapes and positions.[^o4][^o2]

Containers are closed under sums, products, composition, and least and greatest [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738). The [initial algebra](wikipedia://en/4116488) of the extension of $S \triangleleft P$ is the W-type (type of wellfounded trees) $\mathsf{W}_{s:S}\,P\,s$ of [Martin-Löf type theory](wikipedia://en/345023), so every strictly positive type — including nested inductive types — is the extension of a container.[^o2][^o5] Over the category of sets, extensions of containers coincide with the one-variable [polynomial functors](wikipedia://en/56343400), and the theory can be developed in any [locally cartesian closed category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_cartesian_closed_category) with W-types in the categorical sense of Moerdijk and Palmgren.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

Containers also admit a formal derivative, whose shapes are shapes with one distinguished position (a "one-hole context"); it obeys laws analogous to those of [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416) and underlies [zipper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipper_%28data_structure%29) structures.[^o9] The containers whose extension carries a [comonad](wikipedia://en/355140) structure are exactly the *directed containers*, such as nonempty lists, where the comonadic structure is given by taking suffixes.[^o10]

## Indexed containers {#Indexed_containers}

**Indexed containers** (also known as **dependent polynomial functors**) are a generalisation of containers, which can represent a wider class of types, such as vectors (sized lists).[^o13]

The element type (called the *input type*) is indexed by shape and position, so it can vary by shape and position, and the extension (called the *output type*) is also indexed by shape.

:::
