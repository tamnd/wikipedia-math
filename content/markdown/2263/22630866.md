# Kostka polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kostka polynomial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Kostka polynomials**, named after the mathematician [Carl Kostka](wikipedia://en/22619593), are families of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) that generalize the [Kostka numbers](wikipedia://en/22606041). They are studied primarily in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

:::

The two-variable Kostka polynomials *K*<sub>λμ</sub>(*q*, *t*) are known by several names including **Kostka–Foulkes polynomials**, **Macdonald–Kostka polynomials** or ***q*,*t*-Kostka polynomials**. Here the indices λ and μ are [integer partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) and *K*<sub>λμ</sub>(*q*, *t*) is polynomial in the variables *q* and *t*. Sometimes one considers single-variable versions of these polynomials that arise by setting *q* = 0, i.e., by considering the polynomial *K*<sub>λμ</sub>(*t*) = *K*<sub>λμ</sub>(0, *t*).

There are two slightly different versions of them, one called **transformed Kostka polynomials**.

The one-variable specializations of the Kostka polynomials can be used to relate [Hall-Littlewood polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall-Littlewood_polynomials) *P*<sub>μ</sub> to [Schur polynomials](wikipedia://en/3147062) *s*<sub>λ</sub>:

$$s_\lambda(x_1,\ldots,x_n) =\sum_\mu K_{\lambda\mu}(t)P_\mu(x_1,\ldots,x_n;t).\$$

These polynomials were [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) to have non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) by Foulkes, and this was later [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) in 1978 by [Alain Lascoux](wikipedia://en/32555229) and [Marcel-Paul Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337). [^o2] In fact, they show that

$$K_{\lambda\mu}(t) = \sum_{T \in SSYT(\lambda,\mu)} t^{charge(T)}$$

where the sum is taken over all semi-standard [Young tableaux](wikipedia://en/683368) with shape λ and weight μ. Here, *charge* is a certain combinatorial statistic on semi-standard Young tableaux.

The Macdonald–Kostka polynomials can be used to relate [Macdonald polynomials](wikipedia://en/10354285) (also denoted by *P*<sub>μ</sub>) to [Schur polynomials](wikipedia://en/3147062) *s*<sub>λ</sub>:

$$s_\lambda(x_1,\ldots,x_n) =\sum_\mu K_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)J_\mu(x_1,\ldots,x_n;q,t)\$$

where

$$J_\mu(x_1,\ldots,x_n;q,t) = P_\mu(x_1,\ldots,x_n;q,t)\prod_{s\in\mu}(1-q^{arm(s)}t^{leg(s)+1}).\$$

[Kostka numbers](wikipedia://en/22606041) are special values of the one- or two-variable Kostka polynomials:

$$K_{\lambda\mu}= K_{\lambda\mu}(1)=K_{\lambda\mu}(0,1).\$$
