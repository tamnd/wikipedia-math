# Moser's trick

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moser's trick"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Moser's trick** (or Moser's argument) is a method to relate two [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) $\alpha_0$ and $\alpha_1$ on a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) by a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) $\psi \in \mathrm{Diff}(M)$ such that $\psi^* \alpha_1 = \alpha_0$, provided that one can find a family of [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) satisfying a certain [ODE](wikipedia://en/32742753).

:::

More generally, the argument holds for a family $\{ \alpha_t \}_{t \in [0,1]}$ and produce an entire [isotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotopy_%28topology%29) $\psi_t$ such that $\psi_t^* \alpha_t = \alpha_0$.

It was originally given by [Jürgen Moser](wikipedia://en/1752316) in 1965 to check when two [volume forms](wikipedia://en/1855811) are equivalent,[^o2] but its main applications are in [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298). It is the standard argument for the modern proof of [Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358), as well as for the proof of [Darboux-Weinstein theorem](wikipedia://en/73688310#Darboux-Moser-Weinstein_theorem)[^o3] and other [normal form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_form_%28mathematics%29) results.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

## General statement {#General_statement}

> Let $\{ \omega_t \}_{t \in [0,1]} \subset \Omega^k (M)$ be a family of differential forms on a compact manifold $M$. If the ODE $\frac{d}{dt} \omega_t + \mathcal{L}_{X_t} \omega_t = 0$ admits a solution $\{ X_t \}_{t \in [0,1]} \subset \mathfrak{X}(M)$, then there exists a family $\{ \psi_t \}_{t \in [0,1]}$ of diffeomorphisms of $M$ such that $\psi_t^*\omega_t = \omega_0$ and $\psi_0 = \mathrm{id}_M$.
>
> In particular, there is a diffeomorphism $\psi := \psi_1$ such that $\psi^*\omega_1 = \omega_0$.

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The trick consists in viewing $\{ \psi_t \}_{t \in [0,1]}$ as the flows of a [time-dependent vector field](wikipedia://en/3761865), i.e. of a smooth family $\{ X_t \}_{t \in [0,1]}$ of vector fields on $M$. Using the definition of flow, i.e. $\frac{d}{dt} \psi_t = X_t \circ \psi_t$ for every $t \in [0,1]$, one obtains from the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113) that $\frac{d}{dt} (\psi_t^* \omega_t) = \psi_t^* \Big( \frac{d}{dt} \omega_t + \mathcal{L}_{X_t}\omega_t \Big).$ By hypothesis, one can always find $X_t$ such that $\frac{d}{dt} \omega_t + \mathcal{L}_{X_t} \omega_t = 0$, hence their flows $\psi_t$ satisfies $\psi_t^* \omega_t = \mathrm{const} = \psi_0^* \omega_0 = \omega_0$. In particular, as $M$ is compact, this flows exists at $t = 1$.

:::

## Application to volume forms {#Application_to_volume_forms}

> Let $\alpha_0, \alpha_1$ be two [volume forms](wikipedia://en/1855811) on a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) $n$-dimensional manifold $M$. Then there exists a diffeomorphism $\psi$ of $M$ such that $\psi^*\alpha_1 = \alpha_0$ if and only if $\int_M \alpha_0 = \int_M \alpha_1$.[^o2]

::: {#o11 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

One implication holds by the invariance of the integral by diffeomorphisms: $\int_M \alpha_0 = \int_M \psi^*\alpha_1 = \int_{\psi(M)} \alpha_1 = \int_M \alpha_1$.

For the converse, we apply Moser's trick to the family of volume forms $\alpha_t := (1-t) \alpha_0 + t \alpha_1$. Since $\int_M (\alpha_1 - \alpha_0) = 0$, the [de Rham cohomology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_cohomology_class) $[\alpha_0 - \alpha_1] \in H^n_{dR}(M)$ vanishes, as a consequence of [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) and the [de Rham theorem](wikipedia://en/77429163). Then $\alpha_1 - \alpha_0 = d\beta$ for some $\beta \in \Omega^{n-1} (M)$, hence $\alpha_t = \alpha_0 + t d\beta$. By Moser's trick, it is enough to solve the following ODE, where we used the [Cartan's magic formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_magic_formula), and the fact that $\alpha_t$ is a top-degree form:

$$0 = \frac{d}{dt} \alpha_t + \mathcal{L}_{X_t} \alpha_t = d\beta + d (\iota_{X_t} \alpha_t) + \iota_{X_t} (\cancel{d \alpha_t}) = d (\beta + \iota_{X_t} \alpha_t).$$

However, since $\alpha_t$ is a volume form, i.e. $TM \xrightarrow{\cong} \wedge^{n-1} T^*M, \quad X_t \mapsto \iota_{X_t} \alpha_t$, given $\beta$ one can always find $X_t$ such that $\beta + \iota_{X_t} \alpha_t = 0$.

:::

## Application to symplectic structures {#Application_to_symplectic_structures}

In the context of [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), the Moser's trick is often presented in the following form.[^o4][^o5]

> Let $\{ \omega_t \}_{t \in [0,1]} \subset \Omega^2 (M)$ be a family of symplectic forms on $M$ such that $\frac{d}{dt} \omega_t = d \sigma_t$, for $\{ \sigma_t \}_{t \in [0,1]} \subset \Omega^1 (M)$. Then there exists a family $\{ \psi_t \}_{t \in [0,1]}$ of diffeomorphisms of $M$ such that $\psi_t^*\omega_t = \omega_0$ and $\psi_0 = \mathrm{id}_M$.

::: {#o14 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

In order to apply Moser's trick, we need to solve the following ODE

$$0 = \frac{d}{dt} \omega_t + \mathcal{L}_{X_t}\omega_t = d \sigma_t + \iota_{X_t} (\cancel{d\omega_t}) + d (\iota_{X_t} \omega_t) = d (\sigma_t + \iota_{X_t} \omega_t),$$

where we used the hypothesis, the [Cartan's magic formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_magic_formula), and the fact that $\omega_t$ is closed. However, since $\omega_t$ is non-degenerate, i.e. $TM \xrightarrow{\cong} T^*M, \quad X_t \mapsto \iota_{X_t} \omega_t$, given $\sigma_t$ one can always find $X_t$ such that $\sigma_t + \iota_{X_t} \omega_t = 0$.

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="Corollary"}

### Corollary {#Corollary}

> Given two symplectic structures $\omega_0$ and $\omega_1$ on $M$ such that $(\omega_0)_x = (\omega_1)_x$ for some point $x \in M$, there are two neighbourhoods $U_0$ and $U_1$ of $x$ and a diffeomorphism $\phi: U_0 \to U_1$ such that $\phi(x) = x$ and $\phi^*\omega_1 = \omega_0$.[^o4][^o5]

This follows by noticing that, by [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030), the difference $\omega_1 - \omega_0$ is locally $d\sigma$ for some $\sigma \in \Omega^1 (M)$; then, shrinking further the neighbourhoods, the result above applied to the family $\omega_t := (1-t) \omega_0 + t \omega_1$ of symplectic structures yields the diffeomorphism $\phi := \psi_1$.

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Darboux theorem for symplectic structures"}

### Darboux theorem for symplectic structures {#Darboux_theorem_for_symplectic_structures}

The **[Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358)** for symplectic structures states that any point $x$ in a given [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) $(M,\omega)$ admits a local [coordinate chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_chart) $(U, x^1,\ldots,x^n,y^1,\ldots,y^n)$ such that

$$\omega|_U = \sum_{i=1}^n dx^i \wedge dy^i.$$

While the original proof by [Darboux](wikipedia://en/1142530) required a more general statement for 1-forms,[^o19] Moser's trick provides a straightforward proof. Indeed, choosing any [symplectic basis](wikipedia://en/42486404) of the [symplectic vector space](wikipedia://en/292852) $(T_x M,\omega_x)$, one can always find local coordinates $(\tilde{U}, \tilde{x}^1,\ldots,\tilde{x}^n,\tilde{y}^1,\ldots,\tilde{y}^n)$ such that $\omega_x = \sum_{i=i}^n (d\tilde{x}^i \wedge d\tilde{y}^i) |_x$. Then it is enough to apply the corollary of Moser's trick discussed above to $\omega_0 = \omega |_{\tilde{U}}$ and $\omega_1 = \sum_{i=i}^n d\tilde{x}^i \wedge d\tilde{y}^i$, and consider the new coordinates $x^i = \tilde{x}^i \circ \phi, y^i = \tilde{y}^i \circ \phi$.[^o4][^o5]

:::

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Application: Moser stability theorem"}

## Application: Moser stability theorem {#Application:_Moser_stability_theorem}

Moser himself provided an application of his argument for the stability of symplectic structures,[^o2] which is known now as **Moser stability theorem**.[^o4][^o5]

> Let $\{ \omega_t \}_{t \in [0,1]} \subset \Omega^2 (M)$ a family of symplectic form on $M$ which are [cohomologous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomologous), i.e. the [deRham cohomology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_cohomology_class) $[\omega_t] \in H^2_{dR}(M)$ does not depend on $t$. Then there exists a family $\psi_t$ of diffeomorphisms of $M$ such that $\psi^*\omega_t = \omega_0$ and $\psi_0 = \mathrm{id}_M$.

::: {#o23 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

It is enough to check that $\frac{d}{dt} \omega_t = d \sigma_t$; then the proof follows from the previous [application of Moser's trick to symplectic structures](wikipedia://en/73989969#Application_to_symplectic_structures). By the cohomologous hypothesis, $\omega_t - \omega_0$ is an exact form, so that also its derivative $\frac{d}{dt} (\omega_t - \omega_0) = \frac{d}{dt} \omega_t$ is exact for every $t$. The actual proof that this can be done in a smooth way, i.e. that $\frac{d}{dt} \omega_t = d \sigma_t$ for a *smooth* family of functions $\sigma_t$, requires some [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). One option is to prove it by [induction](wikipedia://en/18881), using [Mayer-Vietoris sequences](wikipedia://en/378677);[^o4] another is to choose a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) and employ [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590).[^o2]

:::
