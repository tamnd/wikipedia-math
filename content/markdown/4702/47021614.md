# Dualizing sheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dualizing sheaf"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **dualizing sheaf** on a [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_scheme) [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* of dimension *n* over a field *k* is a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) $\omega_X$ together with a linear functional

:::

$$t_X: \operatorname{H}^n(X, \omega_X) \to k$$

that induces a natural isomorphism of vector spaces

$$\operatorname{Hom}_X(F, \omega_X) \simeq \operatorname{H}^n(X, F)^*, \, \varphi \mapsto t_X \circ \varphi$$

for each coherent sheaf *F* on *X* (the superscript \* refers to a [dual vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space)).[^o2] The linear functional $t_X$ is called a **trace morphism**.

A pair $(\omega_X, t_X)$, if it is exists, is unique up to a natural isomorphism. In fact, in the language of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), $\omega_X$ is an [object representing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representing_object) the contravariant functor $F \mapsto \operatorname{H}^n(X, F)^*$ from the category of coherent sheaves on *X* to the category of *k*-vector spaces.

For a [normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_variety) projective variety *X*, the dualizing sheaf exists and it is in fact the [canonical sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_sheaf): $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}_X(K_X)$ where $K_X$ is a [canonical divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_divisor). More generally, the dualizing sheaf exists for any projective scheme.

There is the following variant of [Serre's duality theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_duality_theorem): for a projective scheme *X* of pure dimension *n* and a [Cohen–Macaulay sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen–Macaulay_sheaf) *F* on *X* such that $\operatorname{Supp}(F)$ is of pure dimension *n*, there is a natural isomorphism[^o3]

$$\operatorname{H}^i(X, F) \simeq \operatorname{H}^{n-i}(X, \operatorname{\mathcal{H}om}(F, \omega_X))^*.$$

In particular, if *X* itself is a [Cohen–Macaulay scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen–Macaulay_scheme), then the above duality holds for any locally free sheaf.

## Relative dualizing sheaf {#Relative_dualizing_sheaf}

Given a proper finitely presented morphism of schemes $f: X \to Y$, (Kleiman 1980) defines the **relative dualizing sheaf** $\omega_f$ or $\omega_{X/Y}$ as[^o6] the sheaf such that for each open subset $U \subset Y$ and a quasi-coherent sheaf $F$ on $U$, there is a canonical isomorphism

$$(f|_U)^! F = \omega_f \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y} F,$$

which is functorial in $F$ and commutes with open restrictions.

::: {#o7 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example**:[^o8] If $f$ is a [local complete intersection morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_complete_intersection_morphism) between schemes of finite type over a field, then (by definition) each point of $X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ and a factorization $f|_U: U \overset{i}\to Z \overset{\pi}\to Y$, a [regular embedding](wikipedia://en/47089144) of codimension $k$ followed by a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041) of relative dimension $r$. Then

$$\omega_f |_U \simeq \wedge^r i^* \Omega^1_{\pi} \otimes \wedge^k N_{U/Z}$$

where $\Omega^1_{\pi}$ is the [sheaf of relative Kähler differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_of_relative_Kähler_differentials) and $N_{U/Z}$ is the [normal bundle](wikipedia://en/2370200) to $i$.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### Dualizing sheaf of a nodal curve {#Dualizing_sheaf_of_a_nodal_curve}

For a smooth curve *C*, its dualizing sheaf $\omega_C$ can be given by the [canonical sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_sheaf) $\Omega^1_C$.

For a nodal curve *C* with a node *p*, we may consider the normalization $\pi:\tilde C\to C$ with two points *x*, *y* identified. Let $\Omega_{\tilde C}(x+y)$ be the sheaf of rational 1-forms on $\tilde C$ with possible simple poles at *x* and *y*, and let $\Omega_{\tilde C}(x+y)_0$ be the subsheaf consisting of rational 1-forms with the sum of residues at *x* and *y* equal to zero. Then the direct image $\pi_*\Omega_{\tilde C}(x+y)_0$ defines a dualizing sheaf for the nodal curve *C*. The construction can be easily generalized to nodal curves with multiple nodes.

This is used in the construction of the [Hodge bundle](wikipedia://en/26155744) on the compactified [moduli space of curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_space_of_curves): it allows us to extend the relative canonical sheaf over the boundary which parametrizes nodal curves. The Hodge bundle is then defined as the direct image of a relative dualizing sheaf.

### Dualizing sheaf of projective schemes {#Dualizing_sheaf_of_projective_schemes}

As mentioned above, the dualizing sheaf exists for all projective schemes. For *X* a closed subscheme of **P**<sup>*n*</sup> of codimension *r*, its dualizing sheaf can be given as $\mathcal{Ext}^r_{\mathbf{P}^n}(\mathcal{O}_X,\omega_{\mathbf{P}^n})$. In other words, one uses the dualizing sheaf on the ambient **P**<sup>*n*</sup> to construct the dualizing sheaf on *X*.
