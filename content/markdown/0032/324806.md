# Sporadic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sporadic group"}

In the mathematical [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), there are a number of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) which do not fit into any infinite family. These are called the **sporadic simple groups**, or the **sporadic finite groups**, or just the **sporadic groups**.

:::

A [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) is a group *G* that does not have any [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918) except for the trivial group and *G* itself. The mentioned classification theorem states that the [list of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/1749665) consists of 18 [countably](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably) infinite families plus 26 exceptions that do not follow such a systematic pattern. These 26 exceptions are the sporadic groups. The [Tits group](wikipedia://en/606719) is sometimes regarded as a sporadic group because it is not strictly a [group of Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490),[^o2] in which case there would be 27 sporadic groups.

The [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422), or *friendly giant*, is the largest of the sporadic groups, and all but six of the other sporadic groups are [subquotients](wikipedia://en/1639294) of it.[^o3]

## Names {#Names}

Several of these groups were predicted to exist before they were constructed. Most of the groups are named after the mathematician(s) who first predicted their existence. Various constructions for these groups were first compiled in (Conway Curtis Norton Parker Wilson), including [character tables](wikipedia://en/911520), individual [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176) and lists of [maximal subgroup](wikipedia://en/1472649), as well as [Schur multipliers](wikipedia://en/1121587) and orders of their [outer automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_automorphism). These are also listed online at (Wilson Parker Nickerson Bray 1999), updated with their group [presentations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_presentation) and semi-presentations. The degrees of minimal faithful representation or [*Brauer characters*](wikipedia://en/1110685#Brauer_characteristic) over fields of characteristic *p* ≥ 0 for all sporadic groups have also been calculated, and for some of their covering groups. These are detailed in (Jansen 2005). A further [exception](wikipedia://en/5280515) in the classification of [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) is the [Tits group](wikipedia://en/606719) *T*, which is sometimes considered of [Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490)[^o14] or sporadic — it is almost but not strictly a group of Lie type[^o15] — which is why in some sources the number of sporadic groups is given as 27, instead of 26.[^o16][^o17]

The diagram at right is based on (Ronan 2006, 247). It does not show the numerous non-sporadic simple subquotients of the sporadic groups. The earliest use of the term *sporadic group* may be (Burnside 1911, 504) where he comments about the Mathieu groups: "These apparently sporadic simple groups would probably repay a closer examination than they have yet received." (At the time, the other sporadic groups had not been discovered.)

## Organization {#Organization}

::: {#o22 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Happy Family (group theory)"}

### Happy Family {#Happy_Family}

Of the 26 (or 27) sporadic groups, 20 can be seen inside the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422) as [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) or [quotients](wikipedia://en/11526) of subgroups ([sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_%28group_theory%29)). These twenty have been called the *happy family* by [Robert Griess](wikipedia://en/5034215), and can be organized into three generations.[^o23]

#### First generation (5 groups): the Mathieu groups {#First_generation_(5_groups):_the_Mathieu_groups}

M<sub>*n*</sub> for *n* = 11, 12, 22, 23 and 24 are multiply transitive [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634) on *n* points. They are all subgroups of M<sub>24</sub>, which is a permutation group on [24](wikipedia://en/362201) points.[^o25]

#### Second generation (7 groups): the Leech lattice {#Second_generation_(7_groups):_the_Leech_lattice}

All the [subquotients](wikipedia://en/1639294) of the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of a lattice in [24](wikipedia://en/362201) dimensions called the [Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919):[^o27]

- *Co*<sub>1</sub> is the quotient of the automorphism group by its center {±1}
- *Co*<sub>2</sub> is the stabilizer of a type 2 (i.e., length 2) vector
- *Co*<sub>3</sub> is the stabilizer of a type 3 (i.e., length $\sqrt{6}$) vector
- *Suz* is the group of automorphisms preserving a complex structure (modulo its center)
- *McL* is the stabilizer of a type 2-2-3 triangle
- *HS* is the stabilizer of a type 2-3-3 triangle
- *J*<sub>2</sub> is the group of automorphisms preserving a quaternionic structure (modulo its center).

#### Third generation (8 groups): other subgroups of the Monster {#Third_generation_(8_groups):_other_subgroups_of_the_Monster}

Consists of subgroups which are closely related to the Monster group *M*:[^o29]

- *B* or *F*<sub>2</sub> has a double cover which is the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of an element of order 2 in *M*
- *Fi*<sub>24</sub>′ has a triple cover which is the centralizer of an element of order 3 in *M* (in [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) "3A")
- *Fi*<sub>23</sub> is a subgroup of *Fi*<sub>24</sub>′
- *Fi*<sub>22</sub> has a double cover which is a subgroup of *Fi*<sub>23</sub>
- The product of *Th* = *F*<sub>3</sub> and a group of order 3 is the centralizer of an element of order 3 in *M* (in conjugacy class "3C")
- The product of *HN* = *F*<sub>5</sub> and a group of order 5 is the centralizer of an element of order 5 in *M*
- The product of *He* = *F*<sub>7</sub> and a group of order 7 is the centralizer of an element of order 7 in *M*.
- Finally, the Monster group itself is considered to be in this generation.

(This series continues further: the product of *M*<sub>12</sub> and a group of order 11 is the centralizer of an element of order 11 in *M*.)

:::

### Pariahs {#Pariahs}

The six sporadic groups which are not subquotients of the monster group *M* are *J*<sub>1</sub>, *J*<sub>3</sub>, *J*<sub>4</sub>, *O'N*, *Ru*, and *Ly*, sometimes known as the [pariahs](wikipedia://en/16569533).[^o31][^o32]

### Tits group {#Tits_group}

If regarded as a sporadic group, the Tits group <sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>(2)′ (***T*** for short) would be a third generation group. There is a subgroup S<sub>4</sub>×<sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>(2)′ normalizing a 2C<sup>2</sup> subgroup of the [Baby monster](wikipedia://en/324799) *B*, giving rise to a subgroup 2·S<sub>4</sub>×<sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>(2)′ normalizing a certain Q<sub>8</sub> subgroup of the Monster *M*. <sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>(2)′ is also a subquotient of the Fischer group *Fi*<sub>22</sub>, and thus also of *Fi*<sub>23</sub> and *Fi*<sub>24</sub>′, and of the Baby Monster *B*; involvement of *T* outside of third-generation sporadic groups, is only seen as being a subquotient of the (pariah) Rudvalis group *Ru*. *T* has no part in sporadic simple groups except the ones already mentioned. In terms of [classification as a finite simple group](wikipedia://en/7445), *T* is the (*n*=0)–member <sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>(2)′ of the infinite family of [commutator groups](wikipedia://en/8847) <sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>(2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>)′, thus in a strict sense not sporadic, nor of Lie type[^n1] (sources have historically regarded the Tits group as belonging in either grouping, or as neither sporadic nor of Lie type, or both altogether).[^o35] For *n* > 0 these finite simple groups coincide with the groups of Lie type <sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>(2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>), also known as [Ree groups of type <sup>2</sup>*F*<sub>4</sub>](wikipedia://en/1739637#Ree_groups_of_type_2F4).
