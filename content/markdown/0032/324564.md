# Isadore Singer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Isadore Singer"}

**Isadore Manuel Singer** (May 3, 1924 – February 11, 2021) was an American mathematician. He was an [institute professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_professor) in the [Department of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mathematics_Department) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and a professor of Mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

Singer is noted for his work with [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), proving the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752) in 1962, which enabled new interactions between [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).[^o6] In early 1980s, while a professor at Berkeley, Singer co-founded the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI) with [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905) and [Calvin Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_C._Moore).[^o7][^o8]

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Singer was born on May 3, 1924, in [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit), [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan), to Polish Jewish immigrants. His father Simon was employed as a printer and only spoke [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish), and his mother, Freda (Rosemaity), worked as a seamstress. Singer learned English swiftly and subsequently taught it to the rest of his family.[^o11][^o12]

Singer studied [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), graduating in 1944 after just two-and-a-half years so that he could join the military.[^o11][^o13] He was stationed in the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army) in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines), where he was a radar officer. During the daytime, he operated a communications school for the [Philippine Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Army). He undertook [correspondence courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_education) in mathematics at night in order to satisfy the prerequisites for [relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202).[^o11] Upon his return from military service, Singer studied mathematics for one year at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o11] Although he initially intended to go back to physics, his interest in math was piqued, and he continued with the subject,[^o11] earning an M.S. in Mathematics in 1948 and a Ph.D. in Mathematics in 1950 under the supervision of [Irving Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Segal).[^o4][^o5][^o3]

### Career {#Career}

Singer held a postdoctoral fellowship as a CLE Moore instructor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1950.[^o3] After appointments at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), he returned to MIT as a professor in 1956 and was appointed as the Norbert Wiener Professor from 1970 to 1979.[^o3] In 1979, he moved to the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), as Miller Professor.[^o3] He returned to MIT in 1983 as the first John D. MacArthur Professor, before being appointed as an Institute Professor in 1987.[^o3]

Singer was chair of the Committee of Science & Public Policy of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), a member of the White House Science Council (1982–88), and on the Governing Board of the [United States National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council) (1995–99).[^o3] He was one of the founders of the independent non-profit [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute), based in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).[^o11]

Singer died on February 11, 2021, at his home in [Boxborough, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxborough,_Massachusetts). He was 96.[^o11]

## Research {#Research}

Partnering with British-Lebanese mathematician [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), Singer created a linkage between the fields of analysis, especially [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), and topology. In particular, they resolved a conjecture of [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643)'s on how topological constructs could count the number of solutions of differential equations. The [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752), as it is now known, opened a new field of mathematics called [index theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_theory).[^o11] The development of their work made use of the [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448), the general geometric construction of which was a notable new discovery. It is sometimes called the Atiyah–Singer operator in their honor.[^o16] In discussions between mathematician [Jim Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844) and physicist [Yang Chen-Ning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning) in the 1970s, it was found that the Atiyah–Singer theorem has a number of applications to physics.[^o6][^o11]

With [Richard V. Kadison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_V._Kadison), he proposed the [Kadison–Singer problem](wikipedia://en/48769660) in 1959.[^o17] Inspired by quantum mechanics, it turned out to have reformulations in engineering and computer science. It was finally proved in 2013.[^o11]

Singer also developed [analytic torsion](wikipedia://en/14388042) with [D.B. Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Burrill_Ray) and with [Henry McKean](wikipedia://en/35051081) introduced heat equation formulae to the Atiyah–Singer index theorem.[^o11] Singer's other notable contributions in mathematics include the [Ambrose–Singer holonomy theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose–Singer_holonomy_theorem) and the [McKean–Singer theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKean–Singer_theorem).[^o18]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Singer was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o20] the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society),[^o21] and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o22] He was a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o23] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o24]

Among the awards he has received are the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) (1969) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) (1983), the [Eugene Wigner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wigner_Medal) (1988), the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) for Lifetime Achievement (2000) from the American Mathematical Society, the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) (2004) shared with [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698),[^o25] the 2004 [Gauss Lecture](wikipedia://en/48440954) and the [James Rhyne Killian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rhyne_Killian) Faculty Achievement Award from MIT (2005).[^o26]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Singer's first marriage was to Sheila Ruff, a play therapist for disabled children; they later divorced. His second marriage was to Rosemarie Singer, and they remained married until his death. He had five children: Stephen (born visually impaired), Eliot, and Natasha (with Sheila); Emily, and Annabelle (with Rosemarie).[^o11] Singer's brother Sidney was a particle physicist with [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory) in New Mexico and predeceased him in 2016.[^o11]
