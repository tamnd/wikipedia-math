# Newman–Shanks–Williams prime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Newman–Shanks–Williams prime"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Newman–Shanks–Williams prime** (**NSW prime**) is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) which can be written in the form

:::

$$S_{2m+1}=\frac{\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right)^{2m+1} + \left(1 - \sqrt{2}\right)^{2m+1}}{2}.$$

NSW primes were first described by [Morris Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Newman), [Daniel Shanks](wikipedia://en/18171362) and [Hugh C. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_C._Williams) in 1981 during their study of [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600) with [square](wikipedia://en/213919) [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group).[^n1]

The first few NSW primes are [7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_%28number%29), [41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41_%28number%29), [239](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/239_%28number%29), 9369319, 63018038201, . . . , corresponding to the indices 3, 5, 7, 19, 29, . . . .

The sequence $S$ alluded to in the formula can be described by the following [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806):

$$S_0=1 \,$$

$$S_1=1 \,$$

$$S_n=2S_{n-1}+S_{n-2}\qquad\text{for all }n\geq 2.$$

The first few terms of the sequence are 1, 1, 3, 7, 17, 41, 99, . . . . Each term in this sequence is half the corresponding term in the sequence of [companion Pell numbers](wikipedia://en/1355482). These numbers also appear in the [continued fraction](wikipedia://en/739199) convergents to [$\sqrt{2}$](wikipedia://en/341682).
