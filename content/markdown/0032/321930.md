# Weird number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weird number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **weird number** is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) that is [abundant](wikipedia://en/321831) but not [semiperfect](wikipedia://en/321918).[^o2][^o3] In other words, the sum of the [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisor) ([divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) including 1 but not itself) of the number is greater than the number, but no [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of those divisors sums to the number itself.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The smallest weird number is 70. Its proper divisors are 1, 2, 5, 7, 10, 14, and 35; these sum to 74, but no subset of these sums to 70. The number 12, for example, is abundant but *not* weird, because the proper divisors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6, which sum to 16; but 2 + 4 + 6 = 12.[^o6]

The first several weird numbers are

[70](wikipedia://en/343334), [836](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/836_%28number%29), 4030, 5830, 7192, 7912, 9272, 10430, 10570, 10792, 10990, 11410, 11690, 12110, 12530, 12670, 13370, 13510, 13790, 13930, 14770, ... .

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

::: {#o9 .problem cue=unsolved}

Are there any odd weird numbers?

:::

Infinitely many weird numbers exist.[^o10] For example, 70*p* is weird for all [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* ≥ 149. In fact, the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of weird numbers has positive [asymptotic density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_density).[^o11]

It is not known if any [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) weird numbers exist. If so, they must be greater than 10<sup>21</sup>.[^o12]

Sidney Kravitz has shown that for *k* a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), *Q* a prime exceeding 2<sup>*k*</sup>, and

$$R = \frac{2^kQ-(Q+1)}{(Q+1)-2^k}$$

also prime and greater than 2<sup>*k*</sup>, then

$$n = 2^{k-1}QR$$

is a weird number.[^o13] With this formula, he found the large weird number

$$n=2^{56}\cdot(2^{61}-1)\cdot153722867280912929\ \approx\ 2\cdot10^{52}.$$

::: {#o15 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Primitive weird number"}

### Primitive weird numbers {#Primitive_weird_numbers}

A property of weird numbers is that if *n* is weird, and *p* is a prime greater than the [sum of divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_of_divisors) σ(*n*), then *pn* is also weird.[^o11] This leads to the definition of *primitive weird numbers*: weird numbers that are not a [multiple](wikipedia://en/3912709) of other weird numbers . Among the 1765 weird numbers less than one million, there are 24 primitive weird numbers. The construction of Kravitz yields primitive weird numbers, since all weird numbers of the form $2^k p q$ are primitive, but the existence of infinitely many *k* and *Q* which yield a prime *R* is not guaranteed. It is [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that there exist infinitely many primitive weird numbers, and [Melfi](wikipedia://en/2271697) has shown that the infinitude of primitive weird numbers is a consequence of [Cramér's conjecture](wikipedia://en/290441).[^o16] Primitive weird numbers with as many as 16 prime factors and 14712 digits have been found.[^o17]
