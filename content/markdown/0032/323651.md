# Wall–Sun–Sun prime

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wall–Sun–Sun prime"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **Wall–Sun–Sun prime** or **Fibonacci–Wieferich prime** is a certain kind of [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) which is conjectured to exist, although none are known.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $p$ be a prime number. When each term in the sequence of [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_numbers) $F_n$ is reduced [modulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulo_operation) $p$ ([i.e.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.e.) subtracted by the largest multiple of $p$ less than it), the result is a [periodic sequence](wikipedia://en/810250). The (minimal) period length of this sequence is called the [Pisano period](wikipedia://en/1127427) and denoted $\pi(p)$. Since $F_0 = 0$, it follows that *p* [divides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divides) $F_{\pi(p)}$. A prime *p* such that *p*<sup>2</sup> divides $F_{\pi(p)}$ is called a **Wall–Sun–Sun prime**.

A prime $p$ is a Wall–Sun–Sun prime if and only if the [quotient](wikipedia://en/332640) of $F_{\pi(p)}$ divided by *p*<sup>2</sup> is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474).

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Equivalent definitions"}

### Equivalent definitions {#Equivalent_definitions}

If $\alpha(m)$ denotes the rank of apparition modulo $m$ (i.e., $\alpha(m)$ is the smallest positive index such that $m$ divides $F_{\alpha(m)}$), then a Wall–Sun–Sun prime can be equivalently defined as a prime $p$ such that $p^2$ divides $F_{\alpha(p)}$.

For a prime *p* ≠ 2, 5, the rank of apparition $\alpha(p)$ is known to divide $p - \left(\tfrac{p}{5}\right)$, where the [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567) $\textstyle\left(\frac{p}{5}\right)$ has the values

$$\left(\frac{p}{5}\right) = \begin{cases} 1 &\text{if }p \equiv \pm1 \pmod 5;\\ -1 &\text{if }p \equiv \pm2 \pmod 5.\end{cases}$$

This observation gives rise to an equivalent characterization of Wall–Sun–Sun primes as primes $p$ such that $p^2$ divides the Fibonacci number $F_{p - \left(\frac{p}{5}\right)}$.[^o8]

A prime $p$ is a Wall–Sun–Sun prime if and only if $\pi(p^2) = \pi(p)$.

A prime $p$ is a Wall–Sun–Sun prime if and only if $L_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}$, where $L_p$ is the $p$-th [Lucas number](wikipedia://en/398404).[^o9]

McIntosh and Roettger establish several equivalent characterizations of [Lucas–Wieferich primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas–Wieferich_prime).[^o13] In particular, let $\epsilon = \left(\tfrac{p}{5}\right)$; then the following are equivalent:

- $$F_{p - \epsilon} \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2}$$
- $$L_{p - \epsilon} \equiv 2\epsilon \pmod{p^4}$$
- $$L_{p - \epsilon} \equiv 2\epsilon \pmod{p^3}$$
- $$F_p \equiv \epsilon \pmod{p^2}$$
- $$L_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}$$

:::

## Existence {#Existence}

::: {#o11 .problem cue=unsolved}

Are there any Wall–Sun–Sun primes? If yes, are there an infinite number of them?

:::

In a study of the Pisano period $k(p)$, [Donald Dines Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Dines_Wall) determined that there are no Wall–Sun–Sun primes less than $10000$. In 1960, he wrote:[^o20]

It has since been conjectured that there are infinitely many Wall–Sun–Sun primes.[^o12]

In 2007, Richard J. McIntosh and Eric L. Roettger showed that if any exist, they must be > 2.[^o13] Dorais and Klyve extended this range to 9.7 without finding such a prime.[^o14]

In December 2011, another search was started by the [PrimeGrid](wikipedia://en/3255603) project;[^o15] however, it was suspended in May 2017.[^o16] In November 2020, PrimeGrid started another project that searches for [Wieferich](wikipedia://en/323631) and Wall–Sun–Sun primes simultaneously.[^o17] The project ended in December 2022, proving that any Wall–Sun–Sun prime must exceed $2^{64}$ (about $1.8\cdot 10^{19}$).[^o18]

## History {#History}

Wall–Sun–Sun primes are named after [Donald Dines Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Dines_Wall),[^o20][^o21] [Zhi Hong Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhi_Hong_Sun) and [Zhi Wei Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhi_Wei_Sun); Z. H. Sun and Z. W. Sun showed in 1992 that if the first case of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) was false for a certain prime *p*, then *p* would have to be a Wall–Sun–Sun prime.[^o22] As a result, prior to [Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/21950759), the search for Wall–Sun–Sun primes was also the search for a potential [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to this centuries-old [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138).

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

A **tribonacci–Wieferich prime** is a prime *p* satisfying *h*(*p*) = *h*(*p*<sup>2</sup>), where *h*(*m*) is the least positive integer *k* satisfying \[*T*<sub>*k*</sub>,*T*<sub>*k*+1</sub>,*T*<sub>*k*+2</sub>\] ≡ \[*T*<sub>0</sub>, *T*<sub>1</sub>, *T*<sub>2</sub>\] (mod *m*) and *T*<sub>*n*</sub> denotes the *n*-th [tribonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_number). No tribonacci–Wieferich prime exists below 10<sup>11</sup>.[^o25]

A **Pell–Wieferich prime** is a prime *p* satisfying *p*<sup>2</sup> divides *P*<sub>*p*−1</sub>, when *p* congruent to 1 or 7 (mod 8), or *p*<sup>2</sup> divides *P*<sub>*p*+1</sub>, when *p* congruent to 3 or 5 (mod 8), where *P*<sub>*n*</sub> denotes the *n*-th [Pell number](wikipedia://en/1355482). For example, 13, 31, and 1546463 are Pell–Wieferich primes, and no others below 10<sup>9</sup> . In fact, Pell–Wieferich primes are 2-Wall–Sun–Sun primes.

### Near-Wall–Sun–Sun primes {#Near-Wall–Sun–Sun_primes}

A prime *p* such that $F_{p - \left(\frac{p}{5}\right)} \equiv Ap \pmod{p^2}$ with small |*A*| is called **near-Wall–Sun–Sun prime**.[^o13] Near-Wall–Sun–Sun primes with *A* = 0 would be Wall–Sun–Sun primes. PrimeGrid recorded cases with |*A*| ≤ 1000.[^o27] A dozen cases are known where *A* = ±1 .

### Wall–Sun–Sun primes with discriminant *D* {#Wall–Sun–Sun_primes_with_discriminant_D}

Wall–Sun–Sun primes can be considered for the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $Q_{\sqrt{D}}$ with [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) *D*. For the conventional Wall–Sun–Sun primes, *D* = 5. In the general case, a [Lucas–Wieferich prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas–Wieferich_prime) *p* associated with (*P*, *Q*) is a Wieferich prime to base *Q* and a Wall–Sun–Sun prime with discriminant *D* = *P*<sup>2</sup> − 4*Q*.[^o8] In this definition, the prime *p* should be odd and not divide *D*.

It is conjectured that for every [fundamental discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607#Fundamental_discriminants) *D* not equal to 1, there are infinitely many Wall–Sun–Sun primes with discriminant *D*.

The case of $(P,Q) = (k,-1)$ corresponds to the ***k*-Wall–Sun–Sun primes**, for which Wall–Sun–Sun primes represent the special case *k* = 1. The *k*-Wall–Sun–Sun primes can be explicitly defined as primes *p* such that *p*<sup>2</sup> divides the *k*-Fibonacci number $F_k(\pi_k(p))$, where *F<sub>k</sub>*(*n*) = *U<sub>n</sub>*(*k*, −1) is a [Lucas sequence](wikipedia://en/734635) [of the first kind](wikipedia://en/734635#Recurrence_relations) with discriminant *D* = *k*<sup>2</sup> + 4 and $\pi_k(p)$ is the Pisano period of *k*-Fibonacci numbers modulo *p*.[^o29] For a prime *p* ≠ 2 and not dividing *D*, this condition is equivalent to either of the following.

- *p*<sup>2</sup> divides $F_k\left(p - \left(\tfrac{D}{p}\right)\right)$, where $\left(\tfrac{D}{p}\right)$ is the [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567);
- *V<sub>p</sub>*(*k*, −1) ≡ *k* (mod *p*<sup>2</sup>), where *V<sub>n</sub>*(*k*, −1) is a Lucas sequence of the second kind.

The smallest *k*-Wall–Sun–Sun primes for *k* = 2, 3, ... are

13, 241, 2, 3, 191, 5, 2, 3, 2683, ...

:::
