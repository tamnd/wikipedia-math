## History {#History}

Theoretical computer science is closely related to mathematics and logic. In the 20th century, it emancipated itself and became an independent discipline. Pioneers of the discipline were [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), [Alonzo Church](wikipedia://en/49731), [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), [Stephen Cole Kleene](wikipedia://en/28805), [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693), [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) and [Noam Chomsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky). While logical inference and mathematical proof had existed previously, in 1931 Kurt Gödel proved with his [incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incompleteness_theorem) that there are fundamental limitations on what statements could be proved or disproved.

[Information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) was added to the field with [a 1948 mathematical theory of communication](wikipedia://en/828061) by Claude Shannon. In the same decade, [Donald Hebb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Hebb) introduced a mathematical model of [learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebbian_learning) in the brain. With mounting biological data supporting this hypothesis with some modification, the fields of [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network) and [parallel distributed processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_distributed_processing) were established. In 1971, [Stephen Cook](wikipedia://en/39432) and, working [independently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_discovery), [Leonid Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Levin), proved that there exist practically relevant problems that are [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) – a landmark result in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543).[^o4]

Modern theoretical computer science research is based on these basic developments, but includes many other mathematical and interdisciplinary problems that have been posed, as shown below:

### Algorithms {#Algorithms}

An [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) is a step-by-step procedure for calculations. Algorithms are used for [calculation](wikipedia://en/239121), [data processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing), and [automated reasoning](wikipedia://en/2884728).

An algorithm is an [effective method](wikipedia://en/12979890) expressed as a finite list[^o8] of well-defined instructions[^o9] for calculating a [function](wikipedia://en/185427).[^o10] Starting from an initial state and initial input (perhaps [empty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_string)),[^o11] the instructions describe a [computation](wikipedia://en/5926) that, when [executed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_%28computing%29), proceeds through a finite[^o12] number of well-defined successive states, eventually producing "output"[^o13] and terminating at a final ending state. The transition from one state to the next is not necessarily [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic); some algorithms, known as [randomized algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_algorithms), incorporate random input.[^o14]

### Automata theory {#Automata_theory}

[Automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356) is the study of *[abstract machines](wikipedia://en/60492)* and *[automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automaton)*, as well as the computational problems that can be solved using them. It is a theory in theoretical computer science, under [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) (a section of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and also of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science)). *Automata* comes from the Greek word αὐτόματα, meaning "self-acting".

Automata Theory is the study of self-operating virtual machines to help in the logical understanding of input and output process, without or with intermediate stage(s) of [computation](wikipedia://en/5926) (or any [function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_%28engineering%29)/process).

### Coding theory {#Coding_theory}

[Coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) is the study of the properties of codes and their fitness for a specific application. Codes are used for [data compression](wikipedia://en/8013), [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), [error correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_correction) and more recently also for [network coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_coding). Codes are studied by various scientific disciplines – such as [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) – for the purpose of designing efficient and reliable [data transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_transmission) methods. This typically involves the removal of redundancy and the correction (or detection) of errors in the transmitted data.

### Computational complexity theory {#Computational_complexity_theory}

[Computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) is a branch of the [theory of computation](wikipedia://en/30402) that focuses on classifying [computational problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_problems) according to their inherent difficulty, and relating those [classes](wikipedia://en/502426) to each other. A computational problem is understood to be a task that is in principle amenable to being solved by a computer, which is equivalent to stating that the problem may be solved by mechanical application of mathematical steps, such as an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775).

A problem is regarded as inherently difficult if its solution requires significant resources, whatever the [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) used. The theory formalizes this intuition, by introducing mathematical [models of computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Models_of_computation) to study these problems and quantifying the amount of resources needed to solve them, such as time and storage. Other [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity) measures are also used, such as the amount of communication (used in [communication complexity](wikipedia://en/50329)), the number of [gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_gate) in a circuit (used in [circuit complexity](wikipedia://en/7641969)) and the number of processors (used in [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing)). One of the roles of computational complexity theory is to determine the practical limits on what [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) can and cannot do.

### Computational geometry {#Computational_geometry}

[Computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) is a branch of computer science devoted to the study of algorithms that can be stated in terms of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). Some purely geometrical problems arise out of the study of computational geometric algorithms, and such problems are also considered to be part of computational geometry.

The main impetus for the development of computational geometry as a discipline was progress in [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) and computer-aided design and manufacturing ([CAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_design)/[CAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_manufacturing)), but many problems in computational geometry are classical in nature, and may come from [mathematical visualization](wikipedia://en/25948250).

Other important applications of computational geometry include [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics) (motion planning and visibility problems), [geographic information systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_system) (GIS) (geometrical location and search, route planning), [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) design (IC geometry design and verification), [computer-aided engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_engineering) (CAE) (mesh generation), [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) (3D reconstruction).

### Computational learning theory {#Computational_learning_theory}

Theoretical results in machine learning mainly deal with a type of inductive learning called supervised learning. In supervised learning, an algorithm is given samples that are labeled in some useful way. For example, the samples might be descriptions of mushrooms, and the labels could be whether or not the mushrooms are edible. The algorithm takes these previously labeled samples and uses them to induce a classifier. This classifier is a function that assigns labels to samples including the samples that have never been previously seen by the algorithm. The goal of the supervised learning algorithm is to optimize some measure of performance such as minimizing the number of mistakes made on new samples.

### Computational number theory {#Computational_number_theory}

[Computational number theory](wikipedia://en/511466), also known as **algorithmic number theory**, is the study of [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for performing [number theoretic](wikipedia://en/21527) [computations](wikipedia://en/5926). The best known problem in the field is [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491).

### Cryptography {#Cryptography}

[Cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) is the practice and study of techniques for [secure communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_communication) in the presence of third parties (called [adversaries](wikipedia://en/789126)).[^o22] More generally, it is about constructing and analyzing [protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_protocol) that overcome the influence of adversaries[^o23] and that are related to various aspects in [information security](wikipedia://en/15036) such as data [confidentiality](wikipedia://en/353859), [data integrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_integrity), [authentication](wikipedia://en/47967), and [non-repudiation](wikipedia://en/52491).[^o24] Modern cryptography intersects the disciplines of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), and [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering). Applications of cryptography include [ATM cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_teller_machine), [computer passwords](wikipedia://en/24304), and [electronic commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_commerce).

Modern cryptography is heavily based on mathematical theory and computer science practice; cryptographic algorithms are designed around [computational hardness assumptions](wikipedia://en/6158383), making such algorithms hard to break in practice by any adversary. It is theoretically possible to break such a system, but it is infeasible to do so by any known practical means. These schemes are therefore termed computationally secure; theoretical advances, e.g., improvements in [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491) algorithms, and faster computing technology require these solutions to be continually adapted. There exist [information-theoretically secure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theoretic_security) schemes that provably cannot be broken even with unlimited computing power—an example is the [one-time pad](wikipedia://en/22210)—but these schemes are more difficult to implement than the best theoretically breakable but computationally secure mechanisms.

### Data structures {#Data_structures}

A [data structure](wikipedia://en/8519) is a particular way of organizing [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_%28computing%29) in a computer so that it can be used [efficiently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_efficiency).[^o26][^o27]

Different kinds of data structures are suited to different kinds of applications, and some are highly specialized to specific tasks. For example, databases use [B-tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-tree) indexes for small percentages of data retrieval and [compilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler) and databases use dynamic [hash tables](wikipedia://en/13833) as look up tables.

Data structures provide a means to manage large amounts of data efficiently for uses such as large [databases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database) and [internet indexing services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_indexing). Usually, efficient data structures are key to designing efficient [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775). Some formal design methods and [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015) emphasize data structures, rather than algorithms, as the key organizing factor in software design. Storing and retrieving can be carried out on data stored in both [main memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_memory) and in [secondary memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_memory).

### Distributed computation {#Distributed_computation}

[Distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing) studies distributed systems. A distributed system is a software system in which components located on [networked computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network) communicate and coordinate their actions by [passing messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Message_passing).[^o29] The components interact with each other in order to achieve a common goal. Three significant characteristics of distributed systems are: concurrency of components, lack of a global clock, and independent failure of components.[^o29] Examples of distributed systems vary from [SOA-based systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service-oriented_architecture) to [massively multiplayer online games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massively_multiplayer_online_game) to [peer-to-peer applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer), and blockchain networks like [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265).

A [computer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program) that runs in a distributed system is called a **distributed program**, and distributed programming is the process of writing such programs.[^o30] There are many alternatives for the message passing mechanism, including [RPC-like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_procedure_call) connectors and [message queues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Message-oriented_middleware). An important goal and challenge of distributed systems is [location transparency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_transparency).

### Information-based complexity {#Information-based_complexity}

[Information-based complexity](wikipedia://en/8221717) (IBC) studies optimal algorithms and computational complexity for continuous problems. IBC has studied continuous problems as path integration, partial differential equations, systems of ordinary differential equations, nonlinear equations, integral equations, fixed points, and very-high-dimensional integration.

### Formal methods {#Formal_methods}

[Formal methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_methods) are a particular kind of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) based techniques for the [specification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_specification), development and [verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_verification) of [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) and [hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware) systems.[^o33] The use of formal methods for software and hardware design is motivated by the expectation that, as in other engineering disciplines, performing appropriate mathematical analysis can contribute to the reliability and robustness of a design.[^o34]

Formal methods are best described as the application of a fairly broad variety of theoretical computer science fundamentals, in particular [logic](wikipedia://en/1151991) calculi, [formal languages](wikipedia://en/10939), [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356), and [program semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_semantics), but also [type systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_systems) and [algebraic data types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_data_types) to problems in software and hardware specification and verification.[^o35]

### Information theory {#Information_theory}

[Information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) is a branch of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) involving the [quantification](wikipedia://en/43507282) of [information](wikipedia://en/18985062). Information theory was developed by [Claude E. Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon) to find fundamental limits on [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) operations such as [compressing data](wikipedia://en/8013) and on reliably [storing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_data_storage) and [communicating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications) data. Since its inception it has broadened to find applications in many other areas, including [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577), [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing), [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), [neurobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurobiology),[^o37] the evolution[^o38] and function[^o39] of molecular codes, [model selection](wikipedia://en/3664073) in statistics,[^o40] thermal physics,[^o41] [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), plagiarism detection,[^o42] [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706), [anomaly detection](wikipedia://en/8190902) and other forms of [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954).[^o43]

Applications of fundamental topics of information theory include [lossless data compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lossless_data_compression) (e.g. [ZIP files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZIP_%28file_format%29)), [lossy data compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lossy_data_compression) (e.g. [MP3s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MP3) and [JPEGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG)), and [channel coding](wikipedia://en/231204) (e.g. for [Digital Subscriber Line (DSL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSL)). The field is at the intersection of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [neurobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurobiology), and [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering). Its impact has been crucial to the success of the [Voyager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_program) missions to deep space, the invention of the compact disc, the feasibility of mobile phones, the development of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet), the study of [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) and of human perception, the understanding of [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650), and numerous other fields. Important sub-fields of information theory are [source coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_coding), [channel coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_coding), [algorithmic complexity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_complexity_theory), [algorithmic information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_information_theory), [information-theoretic security](wikipedia://en/677968), and measures of information.

### Machine learning {#Machine_learning}

[Machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) is a [scientific discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_disciplines) that deals with the construction and study of [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) that can [learn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning) from data.[^o45] Such algorithms operate by building a [model](wikipedia://en/27576) based on inputs[^o46] and using that to make predictions or decisions, rather than following only explicitly programmed instructions.

Machine learning can be considered a subfield of computer science and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). It has strong ties to [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) and [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), which deliver methods, theory and application domains to the field. Machine learning is employed in a range of computing tasks where designing and programming explicit, rule-based [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) is infeasible. Example applications include [spam filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spam_filter), [optical character recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition) (OCR),[^o47] [search engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_to_rank) and [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision). Machine learning is sometimes conflated with [data mining](wikipedia://en/42253),[^o48] although that focuses more on exploratory data analysis.[^o49] Machine learning and [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706) "can be viewed as two facets of the same field."[^o46]

### Parallel computation {#Parallel_computation}

[Parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing) is a form of [computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) in which many calculations are carried out simultaneously,[^o52] operating on the principle that large problems can often be divided into smaller ones, which are then solved ["in parallel"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallelism_%28computing%29). There are several different forms of parallel computing: [bit-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit-level_parallelism), [instruction level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instruction_level_parallelism), [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_parallelism), and [task parallelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_parallelism). Parallelism has been employed for many years, mainly in [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_performance_computing), but interest in it has grown lately due to the physical constraints preventing [frequency scaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_scaling).[^o53] As power consumption (and consequently heat generation) by computers has become a concern in recent years,[^o54] parallel computing has become the dominant paradigm in [computer architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_architecture), mainly in the form of [multi-core processors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-core_processor).[^o55]

[Parallel computer programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_algorithm) are more difficult to write than sequential ones,[^o56] because concurrency introduces several new classes of potential [software bugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_bug), of which [race conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_condition) are the most common. [Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_networking) and [synchronization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronization_%28computer_science%29) between the different subtasks are typically some of the greatest obstacles to getting good parallel program performance.

The maximum possible [speed-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedup) of a single program as a result of parallelization is known as [Amdahl's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdahl's_law).

### Programming language theory and program semantics {#Programming_language_theory_and_program_semantics}

[Programming language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_language_theory) is a branch of computer science that deals with the design, implementation, analysis, characterization, and classification of [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015) and their individual [features](wikipedia://en/23015#Elements). It falls within the discipline of theoretical computer science, both depending on and affecting [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), software engineering, and [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics). It is an active research area, with numerous dedicated academic journals.

In [programming language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_language_theory), **semantics** is the field concerned with the rigorous mathematical study of the meaning of [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015). It does so by evaluating the meaning of [syntactically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_language_syntax) legal [strings](wikipedia://en/27701) defined by a specific programming language, showing the computation involved. In such a case that the evaluation would be of syntactically illegal strings, the result would be non-computation. Semantics describes the processes a computer follows when executing a program in that specific language. This can be shown by describing the relationship between the input and output of a program, or an explanation of how the program will execute on a certain [platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_platform), hence creating a [model of computation](wikipedia://en/1773278).

### Quantum computation {#Quantum_computation}

A [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer) is a [computation](wikipedia://en/5926) system that makes direct use of [quantum-mechanical](wikipedia://en/25202) [phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomena), such as [superposition](wikipedia://en/82728) and [entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336), to perform [operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instruction_%28computer_science%29) on [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data).[^o59] Quantum computers are different from digital computers based on [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor). Whereas digital computers require data to be encoded into binary digits ([bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit)), each of which is always in one of two definite states (0 or 1), quantum computation uses [qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubits) (quantum bits), which can be in [superpositions](wikipedia://en/82728) of states. A theoretical model is the [quantum Turing machine](wikipedia://en/4786555), also known as the universal quantum computer. Quantum computers share theoretical similarities with [non-deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-deterministic_Turing_machine) and [probabilistic computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_automaton); one example is the ability to be in more than one state simultaneously. The field of quantum computing was first introduced by [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086) in 1980[^o60] and [Richard Feynman](wikipedia://en/25523) in 1982.[^o61][^o62] A quantum computer with spins as quantum bits was also formulated for use as a quantum [space–time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space–time) in 1968.[^o63]

Experiments have been carried out in which quantum computational operations were executed on a very small number of qubits.[^o64] Both practical and theoretical research continues, and many national governments and military funding agencies support quantum computing research to develop quantum [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) for both civilian and national security purposes, such as [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715).[^o65]

### Symbolic computation {#Symbolic_computation}

[Computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805), also called symbolic computation or algebraic computation is a scientific area that refers to the study and development of [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) and [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) for manipulating [mathematical expressions](wikipedia://en/609125) and other [mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961). Although, properly speaking, computer algebra should be a subfield of [scientific computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computing), they are generally considered as distinct fields because scientific computing is usually based on [numerical computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_computation) with approximate [floating point numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_point_number), while symbolic computation emphasizes *exact* computation with expressions containing [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) that have not any given value and are thus manipulated as symbols (therefore the name of *symbolic computation*).

[Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) applications that perform symbolic calculations are called *[computer algebra systems](wikipedia://en/54427)*, with the term *system* alluding to the complexity of the main applications that include, at least, a method to represent mathematical data in a computer, a user programming language (usually different from the language used for the implementation), a dedicated memory manager, a [user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_interface) for the input/output of mathematical expressions, a large set of [routines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_%28computer_science%29) to perform usual operations, like simplification of expressions, [differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiation_%28mathematics%29) using [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113), [polynomial factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_factorization), [indefinite integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_integration), etc.

### Very-large-scale integration {#Very-large-scale_integration}

[Very-large-scale integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very-large-scale_integration) (**VLSI**) is the process of creating an [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) (IC) by combining thousands of [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistors) into a single chip. VLSI began in the 1970s when complex [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) and [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication) technologies were being developed. The [microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor) is a VLSI device. Before the introduction of VLSI technology, most ICs had a limited set of functions they could perform. An [electronic circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_circuit) might consist of a [CPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit), [ROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read-only_memory), [RAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_Access_Memory) and other [glue logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glue_logic). VLSI allows IC makers to add all of these circuits into one chip.
