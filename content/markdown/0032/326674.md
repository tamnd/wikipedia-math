# Alan Sokal

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alan Sokal"}

**Alan David Sokal** ( ; born January 24, 1955) is an American professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) and professor emeritus of [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). He works with [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

Sokal is a critic of [postmodernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodernism), and caused the [Sokal affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokal_affair) in 1996 when his deliberately nonsensical paper was published by [Duke University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University_Press)'s *[Social Text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Text)*. He also co-authored a paper criticizing the [critical positivity ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_positivity_ratio) concept in [positive psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_psychology).

## Early life and academic career {#Early_life_and_academic_career}

Sokal was born to [Nathan O. Sokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_O._Sokal) (1929–2016), an electrical engineer of Polish descent, and Zelda Kaufman Sokal.[^o4] He received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) in 1976 and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1981.[^o5] He was advised by the physicist [Arthur Wightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Wightman). During the summers of 1986, 1987, and 1988, Sokal taught [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [National Autonomous University of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Autonomous_University_of_Nicaragua), when the [Sandinistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinistas) controlled the elected government.

## Research interests {#Research_interests}

Sokal's research involves [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and combinatorics. In particular, he studies the interplay between these topics based on questions concerning statistical mechanics and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267). This includes work on the [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126) and the [Tutte polynomial](wikipedia://en/3892303), which appear both in [algebraic graph theory](wikipedia://en/1620216) and in the study of [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423) in statistical mechanics. His interests include [computational physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_physics) and [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms), such as [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) algorithms for problems in statistical physics. He also co-authored a book on [quantum triviality](wikipedia://en/22003136).[^o7]

In 2013, Sokal co-authored a paper with Nicholas Brown and Harris Friedman, rejecting the [Losada Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losada_Line), a concept popular in [positive psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_psychology). Named after its proposer, [Marcial Losada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcial_Losada), it refers to a critical range for an individual's ratio of positive to [negative emotions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_emotion), outside of which the individual will tend to have poorer life and occupational outcomes.[^o8] This concept of a critical positivity ratio was much cited and popularised by psychologists such as [Barbara Fredrickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Fredrickson). The trio's paper, published in *American Psychologist*, contended that the ratio was based on faulty mathematical reasoning and therefore invalid.[^o9]

During his high school years and undergraduate studies at Harvard in the 1970s, Sokal collaborated with his father, Nathan, on his work on [RF power amplifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RF_power_amplifier):[^o4] their work was motivated by their mutual interest in [amateur radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio).[^o10] This has resulted in the introduction of [class-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_amplifier_classes#Class_E) high-efficiency power amplifier,[^o11] and Sokals were awarded the U.S. Patents 3,900,823 and 3,919,656. In 2007, Alan and Nathan Sokal were awarded Microwave Pioneer Award by [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) Microwave Theory and Technology Society for "development of the class E power amplifier."[^o5][^o12]

## Critiques of postmodernism {#Critiques_of_postmodernism}

### Sokal affair {#Sokal_affair}

In 1996, Sokal was curious whether the then-non-peer-reviewed [postmodern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern_philosophy) [cultural studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_studies) journal *Social Text* (published by [Duke University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University_Press)) would publish a submission which "flattered the editors' ideological preconceptions". Sokal submitted a grand-sounding but completely nonsensical paper titled "Transgressing the Boundaries: Toward a Transformative [Hermeneutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermeneutics) of Quantum Gravity".[^o15][^o16]

After holding the article back from earlier issues because of Sokal's refusal to consider revisions, the staff published it in the "Science Wars" issue as a relevant contribution.[^o17] Soon thereafter, Sokal then revealed that the article was a [hoax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoax) in the journal *[Lingua Franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Franca_%28magazine%29)*,[^o18] arguing that [leftists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_politics) and [social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science) would be better served by intellectual underpinnings based on [reason](wikipedia://en/42446). The affair was front-page news in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* on May 18, 1996.[^o19] Sokal responded to leftist and postmodernist criticism of the deception by asserting that he was himself a leftist, and that his motivation was to "defend the Left from a trendy segment of itself".[^o20]

Sokal followed up in 1997 by co-authoring the book *[Impostures Intellectuelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashionable_Nonsense)* with physicist and philosopher of science [Jean Bricmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Bricmont) (published in English, a year later, as *Fashionable Nonsense*). The book accuses some social sciences academics of using scientific and mathematical terms incorrectly and criticizes proponents of the "[strong program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_program)" of the [sociology of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology_of_scientific_knowledge) for denying the value of truth. The book had contrasted reviews, with some lauding the effort,[^o21] and some more reserved.[^o22][^o23]

In 2008, Sokal reviewed the Sokal affair and its implications in the book *[Beyond the Hoax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_the_Hoax)*.

### Other critiques {#Other_critiques}

In 2024, Sokal co-authored an opinion-editorial article in the newspaper *[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe)* with evolutionary biologist [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins) criticizing the use of the terminology "sex assigned at birth" instead of "sex" by the [American Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Medical_Association), the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association), the [American Academy of Pediatrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Pediatrics), and the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention). Sokal and Dawkins argued that [sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sex) is an "objective biological reality" that "is determined at conception and is then *observed* at birth", rather than [assigned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_assignment) by a medical professional. Terming this "[social constructionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism) gone amok", Sokal and Dawkins argued further that "distort\[ing\] the scientific facts in the service of a social cause" risks undermining trust in medical institutions.[^o25] Sokal repeated these criticisms in an editorial for the magazine [*The Critic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Critic_%28modern_magazine%29) discussing the more general [politicization of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politicization_of_science), especially biology and medicine.[^o26]
