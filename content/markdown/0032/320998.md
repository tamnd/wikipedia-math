# Lemoine point

*Image: Lemoine punkt.svg.* A triangle with [medians](wikipedia://en/1072006) (black), [angle bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisectors) (dotted) and [symmedians](wikipedia://en/320997) (red). The symmedians intersect in the symmedian point L, the angle bisectors in the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) I and the medians in the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) G.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lemoine point"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Lemoine point**, **Grebe point** or **symmedian point** is the intersection of the three [symmedians](wikipedia://en/320997) ([medians](wikipedia://en/1072006) reflected at the associated [angle bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisectors)) of a triangle. In other words, it is the [isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) of the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) of a triangle.

:::

[Ross Honsberger](wikipedia://en/37256856) called its existence "one of the crown jewels of modern geometry".[^o11]

In the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593) the symmedian point appears as the sixth point, X(6).[^o2] For a non-equilateral triangle, it lies in the open [orthocentroidal disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthocentroidal_disk) punctured at its own center, and could be any point therein.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The symmedian point of a triangle with side lengths $a$, $b$ and $c$ has homogeneous [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) $[a : b : c]$.[^o2]

An algebraic way to find the symmedian point is to express the triangle by three linear equations in two unknowns given by the [hesse normal forms](wikipedia://en/20665580) of the corresponding lines. The solution of this [overdetermined system](wikipedia://en/10160606) found by the [least squares method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_squares_method) gives the coordinates of the point. It also solves the optimization problem to find the point with a minimal sum of squared distances from the sides.

The symmedian point of a triangle $ABC$ can be constructed in the following way: let the [tangent lines](wikipedia://en/31482) of the circumcircle of $ABC$ through $B$ and $C$ meet at $A'$, and analogously define $B'$ and $C'$; then $A'B'C'$ is the [tangential triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_triangle) of $ABC$, and the lines $AA'$, $BB'$ and $CC'$ intersect at the symmedian point of $ABC$. It can be shown that these three lines meet at a point using [Brianchon's theorem](wikipedia://en/1554065). Line $AA'$ is a symmedian, as can be seen by drawing the circle with center $A'$ through $B$ and $C$.

:::

## Relation to other triangle centers {#Relation_to_other_triangle_centers}

The [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point) of a triangle is the same as the symmedian point of the [contact triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_triangle).[^o7] The [mittenpunkt](wikipedia://en/14941140) of a triangle is the same as the symmedian point of the [excentral triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excentral_triangle).

The centroid of the [pedal triangle](wikipedia://en/152983) of the symmedian point is the symmedian point.[^o11] The centroid of the antipedal triangle of the symmedian point is the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter).[^o8]

## Tetrahedra {#Tetrahedra}

For the extension to an irregular tetrahedron see [symmedian](wikipedia://en/320997).

## History {#History}

The French mathematician [Émile Lemoine](wikipedia://en/15956333) proved the existence of the symmedian point in 1873, and [Ernst Wilhelm Grebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Wilhelm_Grebe) published a paper on it in 1847. [Simon Antoine Jean L'Huilier](wikipedia://en/27357356) had also noted the point in 1809.[^o11]
