# Semiperfect number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Semiperfect number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **semiperfect number** or **pseudoperfect number** is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n* equal to the sum of all or some of its [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisor). A semiperfect number equal to the sum of all its proper divisors is a [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670).

:::

The first few semiperfect numbers are: [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29), [12](wikipedia://en/208155), [18](wikipedia://en/208294), [20](wikipedia://en/208259), [24](wikipedia://en/362201), [28](wikipedia://en/362213), [30](wikipedia://en/341668), [36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36_%28number%29), [40](wikipedia://en/341671), ...

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every [multiple](wikipedia://en/3912709) of a semiperfect number is semiperfect.[^n1] A semiperfect number not [divisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisible) by any smaller semiperfect number is called *primitive*.
- Every number of the form 2<sup>*m*</sup>*p* for a natural number *m* and an [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* such that *p* \< 2<sup>*m*+1</sup> is also semiperfect.
  - In particular, every number of the form 2<sup>*m*</sup>(2<sup>*m*+1</sup> − 1) is semiperfect, and is indeed perfect if 2<sup>*m*+1</sup> − 1 is a [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908).
- The smallest odd semiperfect number is [945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/945_%28number%29).
- A semiperfect number is necessarily either perfect or [abundant](wikipedia://en/321831). An abundant number that is not semiperfect is called a [weird number](wikipedia://en/321930).
- Except for 2, all [primary pseudoperfect numbers](wikipedia://en/7184831) are semiperfect.
- Every [practical number](wikipedia://en/1205310) that is not a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948) is semiperfect.
- The [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) of the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of semiperfect numbers exists.[^n2]

:::

## Primitive semiperfect numbers {#Primitive_semiperfect_numbers}

A **primitive semiperfect number** (also called a *primitive pseudoperfect number*, *irreducible semiperfect number* or *irreducible pseudoperfect number*) is a semiperfect number that has no semiperfect proper divisor.[^n2]

The first few primitive semiperfect numbers are [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29), [20](wikipedia://en/208259), [28](wikipedia://en/362213), [88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/88_%28number%29), [104](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/104_%28number%29), [272](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/272_%28number%29), [304](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/304_%28number%29), [350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/350_%28number%29), ...

There are infinitely many such numbers. All numbers of the form 2<sup>*m*</sup>*p*, with *p* a prime between 2<sup>*m*</sup> and 2<sup>*m*+1</sup>, are primitive semiperfect, but not all primitive semiperfect numbers follow this form; for example, [770](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/770_%28number%29).[^n1][^n2] There are infinitely many odd primitive semiperfect numbers, the smallest being [945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/945_%28number%29). There are infinitely many primitive semiperfect numbers that are not [harmonic divisor numbers](wikipedia://en/462698).[^n1]

Every semiperfect number is a multiple of a primitive semiperfect number.
