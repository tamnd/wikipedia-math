# Quasiperfect number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasiperfect number"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

Do quasiperfect numbers exist?

:::

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quasiperfect number** is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$ for which the sum of all its [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) (the [sum-of-divisors function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum-of-divisors_function) $\sigma(n)$) is equal to $2n + 1$. Equivalently, $n$ is the sum of its non-trivial divisors (that is, its divisors excluding 1 and $n$). No quasiperfect numbers have been found so far.

:::

The quasiperfect numbers are the [abundant numbers](wikipedia://en/321831) of minimal abundance (which is 1).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Theorems"}

## Theorems {#Theorems}

If a quasiperfect number exists, it must be an [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) [square number](wikipedia://en/213919) greater than 10<sup>35</sup> and have at least seven distinct [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor).[^o5]

:::

## Related {#Related}

For a [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670) $n$ the sum of all its divisors is equal to $2n$. For an [almost perfect number](wikipedia://en/322008) $n$ the sum of all its divisors is equal to $2n - 1$.

Numbers $n$ whose sum of factors equals $2n + 2$ are known to exist. They are of form $2^{n - 1} \times (2^n - 3)$ where $2^n - 3$ is a prime. The only exception known so far is $650 = 2 \times 5^2 \times 13$. They are 20, 104, 464, 650, 1952, 130304, 522752, ... . Numbers $n$ whose sum of factors equals $2n - 2$ are also known to exist. They are of form $2^{n - 1} \times (2^n + 1)$ where $2^n + 1$ is prime. No exceptions are found so far. Because of the five known [Fermat primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime), there are five such numbers known: 3, 10, 136, 32896 and 2147516416

[Betrothed numbers](wikipedia://en/37417233) relate to quasiperfect numbers like [amicable numbers](wikipedia://en/3259) relate to perfect numbers.
