# Cullen number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cullen number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Cullen number** is a member of the [integer sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) $C_n = n \cdot 2^n + 1$ (where $n$ is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474)). Cullen numbers were first studied by Irish mathematician [James Cullen](wikipedia://en/741786) in 1905. The numbers are special cases of [Proth numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proth_number).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

In 1976 [Christopher Hooley](wikipedia://en/5380265) showed that the [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) of positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $n \leq x$ for which *C*<sub>*n*</sub> is a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) is of the [order](wikipedia://en/44578#Little-o_notation) *o*(*x*) for $x \to \infty$. In that sense, [almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) Cullen numbers are [composite](wikipedia://en/82289).[^o4] Hooley's proof was reworked by Hiromi Suyama to show that it works for any sequence of numbers *n*·2<sup>*n* + *a*</sup> + *b* where *a* and *b* are integers, and in particular also for [Woodall numbers](wikipedia://en/321962). The only known **Cullen primes**, sixteen in all, are those for *n* equal to:

1, 141, 4713, 5795, 6611, 18496, 32292, 32469, 59656, 90825, 262419, 361275, 481899, 1354828, 6328548, 6679881 .

Still, it is [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that there are infinitely many Cullen primes.

A Cullen number *C*<sub>*n*</sub> is [divisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility) by *p* = 2*n* − 1 if *p* is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) of the form 8*k* − 3; furthermore, it follows from [Fermat's little theorem](wikipedia://en/53453) that if *p* is an [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) prime, then *p* divides *C*<sub>*m*(*k*)</sub> for each *m*(*k*) = (2<sup>*k*</sup> − *k*) (*p* − 1) − *k* (for *k* > 0). It has also been shown that the prime number *p* divides *C*<sub>(*p* + 1)/2</sub> when the [Jacobi symbol](wikipedia://en/97313) (2 | *p*) is −1, and that *p* divides *C*<sub>(3*p* − 1)/2</sub> when the Jacobi symbol (2 | *p*) is + 1.

It is unknown whether there exists a prime number *p* such that *C*<sub>*p*</sub> is also prime.

*C<sub>n</sub>* follows the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$C_n=4(C_{n-1}-C_{n-2})+1.$$

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Sometimes, a **generalized Cullen number base *b*** is defined to be a number of the form *n*·*b*<sup>*n*</sup> + 1, where *n* + 2 > *b*; if a prime can be written in this form, it is then called a **generalized Cullen prime**. [Woodall numbers](wikipedia://en/321962) are sometimes called **Cullen numbers of the second kind**.[^o7]

As of April 2025, the largest known generalized Cullen prime is 4052186·69<sup>4052186</sup> + 1. It has 7,451,366 digits and was discovered by a [PrimeGrid](wikipedia://en/3255603) participant.[^o8][^o9]

According to [Fermat's little theorem](wikipedia://en/53453), if there is a prime *p* such that *n* is divisible by *p* − 1 and *n* + 1 is divisible by *p* (especially, when *n* = *p* − 1) and *p* does not divide *b*, then *b*<sup>*n*</sup> must be [congruent](wikipedia://en/20087) to 1 mod *p* (since *b*<sup>*n*</sup> is a power of *b*<sup>*p* − 1</sup> and *b*<sup>*p* − 1</sup> is congruent to 1 mod *p*). Thus, *n*·*b*<sup>*n*</sup> + 1 is divisible by *p*, so it is not prime. For example, if some *n* congruent to 2 mod 6 (i.e. 2, 8, 14, 20, 26, 32, ...), *n*·*b*<sup>*n*</sup> + 1 is prime, then *b* must be divisible by 3 (except *b* = 1).
