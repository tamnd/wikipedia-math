# Power center (geometry)

*Image: Radical center.svg.* **Diagram of the radical center of three circles.** solid #222222 2px solid blue 2px \#9c5f5a solid orange 2px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Power center"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **power center** of three [circles](wikipedia://en/6220), also called the **radical center**, is the [intersection point](wikipedia://en/41604289) of the three [radical axes](wikipedia://en/2093844) of the pairs of circles. If the radical center lies outside of all three circles, then it is the center of the unique circle (the **radical circle**) that intersects the three given circles [orthogonally](wikipedia://en/74072119); the construction of this orthogonal circle corresponds to **Monge's problem**. This is a special case of the [three conics theorem](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/ThreeConicsTheorem.html).

:::

The three radical axes meet in a single point, the radical center, for the following reason. The radical axis of a pair of circles is defined as the set of points that have equal [power](wikipedia://en/2094044) $h$ with respect to both circles. For example, for every point $P$ on the radical axis of circles 1 and 2, the powers to each circle are equal: $h_{1} = h_{2}$. Similarly, for every point on the radical axis of circles 2 and 3, the powers must be equal, $h_{2} = h_{3}$. Therefore, at the [intersection point of these two lines](wikipedia://en/8240558), all three powers must be equal, $h_{1} = h_{2} = h_{3}$. Since [this implies](wikipedia://en/90446#Basic_properties) that $h_{1} = h_{3}$, this point must also lie on the radical axis of circles 1 and 3. Hence, all three radical axes pass through the same point, the radical center.

The radical center has several applications in geometry. It has an important role in a solution to [Apollonius' problem](wikipedia://en/2642185) published by [Joseph Diaz Gergonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Diaz_Gergonne) in 1814. In the [power diagram](wikipedia://en/35793286) of a system of circles, all of the vertices of the diagram are located at radical centers of triples of circles. The [Spieker center](wikipedia://en/35728942) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is the radical center of its [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircles).[^o2] Several types of radical circles have been defined as well, such as the radical circle of the [Lucas circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_circle).
