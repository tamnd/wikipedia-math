# Almost perfect number

*Image: Deficient number Cuisenaire rods 8.png.* Demonstration, with [Cuisenaire rods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisenaire_rods), that the number 8 is almost perfect, and [deficient](wikipedia://en/321843).

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

Do any non-power of 2 almost perfect numbers exist?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Almost perfect number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **almost perfect number** (sometimes also called **slightly defective** or **least deficient** **number**) is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n* such that the sum of all [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) of *n* (the [sum-of-divisors function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum-of-divisors_function) *σ*(*n*)) is equal to 2*n* − 1, the sum of all [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisor) of *n*, *s*(*n*) = *σ*(*n*) − *n*, then being equal to *n* − 1. The only known almost perfect numbers are [powers of 2](wikipedia://en/376948) with non-negative exponents . Therefore the only known [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) almost perfect number is 2<sup>0</sup> = 1, and the only known even almost perfect numbers are those of the form 2<sup>*k*</sup> for some positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *k*; however, it has not been shown that all almost perfect numbers are of this form. It is known that an odd almost perfect number greater than 1 would have at least six [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor).[^o3][^o4]

:::

If *m* is an odd almost perfect number then *m*(2*m* − 1) is a [Descartes number](wikipedia://en/40093498).[^o5] Moreover if *a* and *b* are positive odd integers such that $b+3<a<\sqrt{m/2}$ and such that 4*m* − *a* and 4*m* + *b* are both [primes](wikipedia://en/23666), then *m*(4*m* − *a*)(4*m* + *b*) would be an odd [weird number](wikipedia://en/321930).[^o6]
