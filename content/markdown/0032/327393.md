# Decagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **decagon** (from the Greek δέκα *déka* and γωνία *gonía,* "ten angles") is a ten-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) or **10-gon**.[^o2] The total sum of the [interior angles](wikipedia://en/524003) of a [simple](wikipedia://en/1059530) decagon is 1440°.

:::

## Regular decagon {#Regular_decagon}

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) decagon* has all sides of equal length and each internal angle will always be equal to 144°.[^o2] Its [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) is {10} [^o4] and can also be constructed as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014), t{5}, a quasiregular decagon alternating two types of edges.

### Side length {#Side_length}

*Image: 01-Zehneck-Seitenlänge.svg.*

The picture shows a regular decagon with side length $a$ and radius $R$ of the [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262).

- The triangle $E_{10}E_1M$ has two equally long legs with length $R$ and a base with length $a$
- The circle around $E_1$ with radius $a$ intersects $]M\,E_{10}[$ in a point $P$ (not designated in the picture).
- Now the triangle ${E_{10}E_1P}\;$ is an [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428) with vertex $E_1$ and with base angles $m\angle E_1 E_{10} P = m\angle E_{10} P E_1 = 72^\circ \;$.
- Therefore $m\angle P E_1 E_{10} = 180^\circ -2\cdot 72^\circ = 36^\circ \;$. So $\; m\angle M E_1 P = 72^\circ- 36^\circ = 36^\circ\;$ and hence $\; E_1 M P\;$ is also an isosceles triangle with vertex $P$. The length of its legs is $a$, so the length of $[P\,E_{10}]$ is $R-a$.
- The isosceles triangles $E_{10} E_1 M\;$ and $P E_{10} E_1\;$ have equal angles of 36° at the vertex, and so they are [similar](wikipedia://en/39377), hence: $\;\frac{a}{R}=\frac{R-a}{a}$
- Multiplication with the denominators $R,a >0$ leads to the quadratic equation: $\;a^2=R^2-aR\;$
- This equation for the side length $a\,$ has one positive solution: $\;a=\frac{R}{2}(-1+\sqrt{5})$

So the regular decagon can be constructed with *[ruler and compass](wikipedia://en/61229)*.

**Further conclusions**

$\;R=\frac{2a}{\sqrt{5}-1}=\frac{a}{2}(\sqrt{5}+1)\;$ and the base height of $\Delta\,E_{10} E_1 M\,$ (i.e. the length of $[M\,D]$) is $h = \sqrt{R^2-(a/2)^2}=\frac{a}{2}\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}}\;$ and the triangle has the area: $A_\Delta=\frac{a}{2}\cdot h = \frac{a^2}{4}\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}}$.

### Area {#Area}

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a regular decagon of side length *a* is given by:[^o7]

$$A = \frac{5}{2} a^2\cot\left(\frac{\pi}{10} \right) = \frac{5}{2} a^2\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}} \simeq 7.694208843\,a^2$$

In terms of the [apothem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothem) *r* (see also [inscribed figure](wikipedia://en/4470203)), the area is:

$$A = 10 \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{10}\right) r^2 = 2r^2\sqrt{5\left(5-2\sqrt5\right)} \simeq 3.249196962\,r^2$$

In terms of the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) *R*, the area is:

$$A = 5 \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{5}\right) R^2 = \frac{5}{2}R^2\sqrt{\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}} \simeq 2.938926261\,R^2$$

An alternative formula is $A=2.5da$ where *d* is the distance between parallel sides, or the height when the decagon stands on one side as base, or the [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of the decagon's [inscribed circle](wikipedia://en/4470203). By simple [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261),

$$d=2a\left(\cos\tfrac{3\pi}{10}+\cos\tfrac{\pi}{10}\right),$$

and it can be written [algebraically](wikipedia://en/4283745) as

$$d=a\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}}.$$

### Construction {#Construction}

As 10 = 2 × 5, a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948) times a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime), it follows that a regular decagon is [constructible](wikipedia://en/523430) using [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge), or by an edge-[bisection](wikipedia://en/152547) of a regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014).[^o10]

An alternative (but similar) method is as follows:

1. Construct a pentagon in a circle by one of the methods shown in [constructing a pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014#Construction_of_a_regular_pentagon).
1. Extend a line from each vertex of the pentagon through the center of the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) to the opposite side of that same circle. Where each line cuts the circle is a vertex of the decagon. In other words, [the image](wikipedia://en/579311#Image_of_a_subset) of a regular pentagon under a [point reflection](wikipedia://en/12487536) with respect of [its center](wikipedia://en/333306#Symmetry) is a [concentric](wikipedia://en/19049087) *[congruent](wikipedia://en/39330)* pentagon, and the two pentagons have in total the vertices of a concentric *regular decagon*.
1. The five corners of the pentagon constitute alternate corners of the decagon. Join these points to the adjacent new points to form the decagon.

:::

## The golden ratio in decagon {#The_golden_ratio_in_decagon}

Both in the construction with given circumcircle[^o12] as well as with given side length is the [golden ratio dividing a line segment by exterior division](wikipedia://en/12386#Geometry) the determining construction element.

- In the construction with given circumcircle the circular arc around G with radius $\overline{\operatorname{GE}_{3}}$ produces the segment $\overline{\operatorname{AH}}$, whose division corresponds to the golden ratio.

$$\frac{\overline{AM}}{\overline{MH}} = \frac{\overline{AH}}{\overline{AM}} = \frac{1+ \sqrt{5}}{2} = \Phi \approx 1.618 \text{.}$$

- In the construction with given side length[^o13] the circular arc around D with radius $\overline{\operatorname{DA}}$ produces the segment $\overline{E_{10}F}$, whose division corresponds to the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386#Calculation).

$$\frac{\overline{E_1 E_{10}}}{\overline{E_1 F}} = \frac{\overline{E_{10} F}}{\overline{E_1 E_{10}}} = \frac{R}{a} = \frac{1+ \sqrt{5}}{2} =\Phi \approx 1.618 \text{.}$$

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Symmetries\_of\_decagon.png.* Symmetries of a regular decagon. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Blue mirrors are drawn through vertices, and purple mirrors are drawn through edges. Gyration orders are given in the center.

The *regular decagon* has [Dih<sub>10</sub> symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 20. There are 3 subgroup dihedral symmetries: Dih<sub>5</sub>, Dih<sub>2</sub>, and Dih<sub>1</sub>, and 4 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: Z<sub>10</sub>, Z<sub>5</sub>, Z<sub>2</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>.

These 8 symmetries can be seen in 10 distinct symmetries on the decagon, a larger number because the lines of reflections can either pass through vertices or edges. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o16] Full symmetry of the regular form is **r20** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars), and **i** when reflection lines path through both edges and vertices. Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g10** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

The highest symmetry irregular decagons are **d10**, an [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) decagon constructed by five mirrors which can alternate long and short edges, and **p10**, an [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) decagon, constructed with equal edge lengths, but vertices alternating two different internal angles. These two forms are [duals](wikipedia://en/19865549) of each other and have half the symmetry order of the regular decagon.

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) states that every [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103) (a 2*m*-gon whose opposite sides are parallel and of equal length) can be dissected into *m*(*m*-1)/2 parallelograms.[^o19] In particular this is true for regular polygons with evenly many sides, in which case the parallelograms are all rhombi. For the *regular decagon*, *m*=5, and it can be divided into 10 rhombs, with examples shown below. This decomposition can be seen as 10 of 80 faces in a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) projection plane of the [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215). A dissection is based on 10 of 30 faces of the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025). The list defines the number of solutions as 62, with 2 orientations for the first symmetric form, and 10 orientations for the other 6.

A *skew decagon* is a [skew polygon](wikipedia://en/6545461) with 10 vertices and edges but not existing on the same plane. The interior of such a decagon is not generally defined. A *skew zig-zag decagon* has vertices alternating between two parallel planes.

A *[regular skew decagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_skew_decagon)* is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) with equal edge lengths. In 3-dimensions it will be a zig-zag skew decagon and can be seen in the vertices and side edges of a [pentagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860091), [pentagrammic antiprism](wikipedia://en/4157315), and [pentagrammic crossed-antiprism](wikipedia://en/4157338) with the same D<sub>5d</sub>, \[2<sup>\+</sup>,10\] symmetry, order 20.

These can also be seen in these four convex polyhedra with [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844). The polygons on the perimeter of these projections are regular skew decagons.
