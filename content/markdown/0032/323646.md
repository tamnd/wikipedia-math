# Wilson prime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilson prime"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **Wilson prime** is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ such that $p^2$ [divides](wikipedia://en/49492) $(p-1)!+1$, where "$!$" denotes the [factorial function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factorial_function); compare this with [Wilson's theorem](wikipedia://en/227323), which states that every prime $p$ divides $(p-1)!+1$. Both are named for 18th-century [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people) mathematician [John Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilson_%28mathematician_and_judge%29); in 1770, [Edward Waring](wikipedia://en/9723) credited the theorem to Wilson,[^o3] although it had been stated centuries earlier by [Ibn al-Haytham](wikipedia://en/1645).

:::

The only known Wilson primes are [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_%28number%29), [13](wikipedia://en/37231), and [563](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/563_%28number%29) . Costa et al. write that "the case $p=5$ is trivial", and credit the observation that 13 is a Wilson prime to (Mathews 1892).[^o11][^o6] Early work on these numbers included searches by [N. G. W. H. Beeger](wikipedia://en/1550971) and [Emma Lehmer](wikipedia://en/7682439),[^o7][^o11][^o8] but 563 was not discovered until the early 1950s, when computer searches could be applied to the problem.[^o11][^o9][^o10] If any others exist, they must be greater than 2 × 10<sup>13</sup>.[^o11] It has been [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that infinitely many Wilson primes exist, and that the number of Wilson primes in an interval $[x,y]$ is about $\log\log_x y$.[^o12]

Several computer searches have been done in the hope of finding new Wilson primes.[^o13][^o14][^o15] The [Ibercivis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibercivis) [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing) project includes a search for Wilson primes.[^o16] Another search was coordinated at the [Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search](wikipedia://en/12917) forum.[^o17]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

### Wilson primes of order $n$ {#Wilson_primes_of_order}

Wilson's theorem can be expressed in general as $(n-1)!(p-n)!\equiv(-1)^n\ \bmod p$ for every [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n \ge 1$ and prime $p \ge n$. Generalized Wilson primes of order $n$ are the primes $p$ such that $p^2$ divides $(n-1)!(p-n)! - (-1)^n$.

It was conjectured that for every [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$, there are infinitely many Wilson primes of order $n$.

Near-Wilson primes with $B=0$ are bona fide Wilson primes. The table on the right lists all such primes with $|B|\le 100$ from 6 up to 4.[^o11]

### Wilson numbers {#Wilson_numbers}

A **Wilson number** is a natural number $n$ such that $W(n)\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}{n^2})$, where

$$W(n) = \pm1+\prod_\stackrel{1 \le k \le n}{\gcd(k,n)=1}{k},$$

and where the $\pm1$ term is positive [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $n$ has a [primitive root](wikipedia://en/186864) and negative otherwise.[^o25] For every natural number $n$, $W(n)$ is divisible by $n$, and the quotients (called generalized [Wilson quotients](wikipedia://en/1974015)) are listed in .
