# Affine-regular polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine-regular polygon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **affine-regular polygon** or **affinely regular polygon** is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) that is related to a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) by an [affine transformation](wikipedia://en/38449). Affine transformations include [translations](wikipedia://en/204682), uniform and non-uniform [scaling](wikipedia://en/651752), [reflections](wikipedia://en/295829), [rotations](wikipedia://en/39789), [shears](wikipedia://en/799405), and other [similarities](wikipedia://en/39377) and some, but not all [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

All [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) are affine-regular. In other words, all triangles can be generated by applying affine transformations to an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285). A [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) is affine-regular if and only if it is a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496), which includes [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048) and [rhombuses](wikipedia://en/183884) as well as [squares](wikipedia://en/659939). In fact, affine-regular polygons may be considered a natural generalization of parallelograms.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Many properties of regular polygons are invariant under affine transformations, and affine-regular polygons share the same properties. For instance, an affine-regular quadrilateral can be [equidissected](wikipedia://en/36643448) into $m$ equal-area triangles if and only if $m$ is even, by affine invariance of equidissection and [Monsky's theorem](wikipedia://en/35098575) on equidissections of squares.[^o7] More generally an $n$-gon with $n > 4$ may be [equidissected](wikipedia://en/36643448) into $m$ equal-area triangles if and only if $m$ is a multiple of $n$.[^o8]
