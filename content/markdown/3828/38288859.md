# John William Theodore Youngs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John William Theodore Youngs"}

**John William Theodore Youngs** (usually cited as J. W. T. Youngs, known as Ted Youngs; 21 August 1910 [Bilaspur, Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilaspur,_Chhattisgarh), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) – 20 July 1970 [Santa Cruz, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz,_California)) was an American mathematician.

:::

Youngs was the son of a missionary. He completed his undergraduate study at [Wheaton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheaton_College_%28Illinois%29) and received his PhD from [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) in 1934 under [Tibor Radó](wikipedia://en/1490393). He then taught for 18 years at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University), where for eight years he was chair of the mathematics department. From 1964, he was a professor at the [University of California, Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Cruz), where he developed the mathematics faculty and was chair of the academic senate of the university.

Youngs worked in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), for example, questions on the Frechét-equivalence of topological maps.[^o2] He is famous for the [Ringel–Youngs theorem](wikipedia://en/1585274) (*i.e.* Ringel and Youngs's 1968 proof of the Heawood conjecture),[^o3] which is closely related to the analogue of the [four-color theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-color_theorem) for surfaces of higher genus.

John Youngs was a consultant for [Sandia National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories), the [Rand Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Corporation) and the [Institute for Defense Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses) as well as a trustee for *Carver Research Foundation Institute* in [Tuskegee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuskegee). In 1946–1947, he was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow). At the University of Santa Cruz, a mathematics prize for undergraduates is named after him.[^o4]
