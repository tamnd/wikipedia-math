# Friendship graph

*Image: Friendship graphs.svg.* The friendship graphs $F_{2}$, $F_{3}$ and $F_{4}$.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Friendship graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **friendship graph** (or **Dutch windmill graph** or **$n$-fan**) $F_{n}$ is a [planar](wikipedia://en/24314), [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with $2n + 1$ [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and $3n$ edges.

:::

The friendship graph $F_{n}$ can be constructed by joining $n$ copies of the [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) $C_{3}$ with a common vertex, which becomes a [universal vertex](wikipedia://en/51352780) for the graph.[^o4]

By construction, the friendship graph $F_{n}$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to the [windmill graph](wikipedia://en/24373456) $\operatorname{Wd}(3, n)$. It is [unit distance](wikipedia://en/7158665) with [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 3, diameter 2 and radius 1. The graph $F_{2}$ is isomorphic to the [butterfly graph](wikipedia://en/4372111). Friendship graphs are generalized by the [triangular cactus graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_cactus_graph).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Friendship theorem"}

## Friendship theorem {#Friendship_theorem}

The **friendship theorem** of (txt)[^o8] states that the finite graphs with the property that every two vertices have exactly one neighbor in common are exactly the friendship graphs. Informally, if a group of people has the property that every pair of people has exactly one friend in common, then there must be one person who is a friend to all the others. However, for infinite graphs, there can be many different graphs with the same [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) that have this property.[^o9]

A [combinatorial proof](wikipedia://en/741875) of the friendship theorem was given by Mertzios and Unger.[^o10] Another proof was given by [Craig Huneke](wikipedia://en/41178067).[^o11] A formalised proof in [Metamath](wikipedia://en/6023946) was reported by Alexander van der Vekens in October 2018 on the Metamath mailing list.[^o12]

:::

## Labeling and colouring {#Labeling_and_colouring}

The friendship graph has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 3 and [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) $2n$. Its [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126) can be deduced from the chromatic polynomial of the cycle graph $C_{3}$ and is equal to

$$(x-2)^n (x-1)^n x.$$

The friendship graph $F_{n}$ is [edge-graceful](wikipedia://en/1821467) if and only if $n$ is odd. It is [graceful](wikipedia://en/8978968) if and only if $n \equiv 0\pmod{4}$ or $n \equiv 1\pmod{4}$.[^o14][^o15]

Every friendship graph is [factor-critical](wikipedia://en/7684634).

## Extremal graph theory {#Extremal_graph_theory}

According to [extremal graph theory](wikipedia://en/529568), every graph with sufficiently many edges (relative to its number of vertices) must contain a $k$-fan as a subgraph. More specifically, this is true for an $n$-vertex graph (for $n$ sufficiently large in terms of $k$) if the number of edges is

$$\left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor + f(k),$$

where $f(k)$ is $k^2-k$ if $k$ is odd, and $f(k)$ is $k^2-3k/2$ if $k$ is even. These bounds generalize [Turán's theorem](wikipedia://en/360601) on the number of edges in a [triangle-free graph](wikipedia://en/7667996), and they are the best possible bounds for this problem (when $n\ge 50k^2$), in that for any smaller number of edges there exist graphs that do not contain a $k$-fan.[^o17]

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Any two vertices having exactly one neighbor in common is equivalent to any two vertices being connected by exactly one path of length two. This has been generalized to $P_k$-graphs, in which any two vertices are connected by a unique path of length $k$. For $k\ge 3$ no such graphs are known, and the claim of their non-existence is [Kotzig's conjecture](wikipedia://en/77390020).

:::
