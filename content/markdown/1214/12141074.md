# Harmonic measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harmonic measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), **harmonic measure** is a concept related to the theory of [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762) that arises from the solution of the classical [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109).

*Image: BrownianMotionHarmonicMeasure.svg.* Harmonic measure is the exit distribution of Brownian motion

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the harmonic measure of a subset of the boundary of a bounded domain in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $R^n$, $n\geq 2$ is the probability that a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) started inside a domain hits that subset of the boundary. More generally, harmonic measure of an [Itō diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion) *X* describes the distribution of *X* as it hits the boundary of *D*. In the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), harmonic measure can be used to estimate the [modulus](wikipedia://en/991) of an [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) inside a domain *D* given bounds on the modulus on the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of the domain; a special case of this principle is [Hadamard's three-circle theorem](wikipedia://en/1499388). On simply connected planar domains, there is a close connection between harmonic measure and the theory of [conformal maps](wikipedia://en/50627).

:::

The term *harmonic measure* was introduced by [Rolf Nevanlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Nevanlinna) in 1928 for planar domains,[^o2][^o3] although Nevanlinna notes the idea appeared implicitly in earlier work by Johansson, [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126), [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz), [Torsten Carleman](wikipedia://en/6063370), [Alexander Ostrowski](wikipedia://en/5827091) and [Gaston Julia](wikipedia://en/249107). The connection between harmonic measure and Brownian motion was first identified by Kakutani in 1944.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *D* be a [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258), [open domain](wikipedia://en/39358) in *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, *n* ≥ 2, and let ∂*D* denote the boundary of *D*. Any [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) *f* : ∂*D* → **R** determines a unique [harmonic function](wikipedia://en/55762) *H*<sub>*f*</sub> that solves the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109)

$$\begin{cases} - \Delta H_{f} (x) = 0, & x \in D; \\ H_{f} (x) = f(x), & x \in \partial D. \end{cases}$$

If a point *x* ∈ *D* is fixed, by the [Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem](wikipedia://en/38948504) and the [maximum principle](wikipedia://en/1436104) *H*<sub>*f*</sub>(*x*) determines a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) *ω*(*x*, *D*) on ∂*D* by

$$H_{f} (x) = \int_{\partial D} f(y) \, \mathrm{d} \omega(x, D) (y).$$

The measure *ω*(*x*, *D*) is called the **harmonic measure** (of the domain *D* with pole at *x*).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- For any Borel subset *E* of ∂*D*, the harmonic measure *ω*(*x*, *D*)(*E*) is equal to the value at *x* of the solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data equal to the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of *E*.
- For fixed *D* and *E* ⊆ ∂*D*, *ω*(*x*, *D*)(*E*) is a harmonic function of *x* ∈ *D* and

$$0 \leq \omega(x, D)(E) \leq 1;$$

$$1 - \omega(x, D)(E) = \omega(x, D)(\partial D \setminus E);$$

Hence, for each *x* and *D*, *ω*(*x*, *D*) is a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on ∂*D*.

- If *ω*(*x*, *D*)(*E*) = 0 at even a single point *x* of *D*, then $y \mapsto\omega(y,D)(E)$ is identically zero, in which case *E* is said to be a set of **harmonic measure zero**. This is a consequence of [Harnack's inequality](wikipedia://en/2976061).

Since explicit formulas for harmonic measure are not typically available, we are interested in determining conditions which guarantee a set has harmonic measure zero.

- **F. and M. Riesz Theorem**:[^o9] If $D\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ is a simply connected planar domain bounded by a [rectifiable curve](wikipedia://en/1898401) (i.e. if $H^1(\partial D)<\infty$), then harmonic measure is mutually absolutely continuous with respect to arc length: for all $E\subset\partial D$, $\omega(X,D)(E)=0$ if and only if $H^1(E)=0$.
- **Makarov's theorem**:[^o10] Let $D\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ be a simply connected planar domain. If $E\subset\partial D$ and $H^s(E)=0$ for some $s<1$, then $\omega(x,D)(E)=0$. Moreover, harmonic measure on *D* is [mutually singular](wikipedia://en/4304500) with respect to *t*-dimensional Hausdorff measure for all *t* > 1.
- **Dahlberg's theorem**:[^o11] If $D\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is a bounded [Lipschitz domain](wikipedia://en/7820612), then harmonic measure and (*n* − 1)-dimensional Hausdorff measure are mutually absolutely continuous: for all $E\subset\partial D$, $\omega(X,D)(E)=0$ if and only if $H^{n-1}(E)=0$.

:::

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If $\mathbb{D}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^2:|X|<1\}$ is the unit disk, then harmonic measure of $\mathbb{D}$ with pole at the origin is length measure on the unit circle normalized to be a probability, i.e. $\omega(0,\mathbb{D})(E)=|E|/2\pi$ for all $E\subset S^1$ where $|E|$ denotes the length of $E$.
- If $\mathbb{D}$ is the unit disk and $X\in \mathbb{D}$, then $\omega(X,\mathbb{D})(E)=\int_E \frac{1-|X|^2}{|X-Q|^2}\frac{dH^1(Q)}{2\pi}$ for all $E\subset S^1$ where $H^1$ denotes length measure on the unit circle. The [Radon–Nikodym derivative](wikipedia://en/338746) $d\omega(X,\mathbb{D})/dH^1$ is called the [Poisson kernel](wikipedia://en/2214583).
- More generally, if $n\geq 2$ and $\mathbb{B}^n=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^n:|X|<1\}$ is the *n*-dimensional unit ball, then harmonic measure with pole at $X\in \mathbb{B}^n$ is $\omega(X,\mathbb{B}^n)(E)=\int_E \frac{1-|X|^2}{|X-Q|^n}\frac{dH^{n-1}(Q)}{\sigma_{n-1}}$ for all $E\subset S^{n-1}$ where $H^{n-1}$ denotes surface measure ((*n* − 1)-dimensional [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109)) on the unit sphere $S^{n-1}$ and $H^{n-1}(S^{n-1})=\sigma_{n-1}$.
- *Image: RiemannMapHarmonicMeasure.svg.* Harmonic Measure on Simply Connected Planar Domains If $D\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ is a simply connected planar domain bounded by a [Jordan curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_curve) and *X*$\in$*D*, then $\omega(X,D)(E)=|f^{-1}(E)|/2\pi$ for all $E\subset\partial D$ where $f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow D$ is the unique [Riemann map](wikipedia://en/26215) which sends the origin to *X*, i.e. $f(0)=X$. See [Carathéodory's theorem](wikipedia://en/2256844).
- If $D\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ is the domain bounded by the [Koch snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959), then there exists a subset $E\subset\partial D$ of the Koch snowflake such that $E$ has zero length ($H^1(E)=0$) and full harmonic measure $\omega(X,D)(E)=1$.

:::

## The harmonic measure of a diffusion {#The_harmonic_measure_of_a_diffusion}

Consider an **R**<sup>*n*</sup>-valued Itō diffusion *X* starting at some point *x* in the interior of a domain *D*, with law **P**<sup>*x*</sup>. Suppose that one wishes to know the distribution of the points at which *X* exits *D*. For example, canonical Brownian motion *B* on the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) starting at 0 exits the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) (−1, +1) at −1 with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and at +1 with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, so *B*<sub>*τ*<sub>(−1, +1)</sub></sub> is [uniformly distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28discrete%29) on the set {−1, +1}.

In general, if *G* is [compactly embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_embedded) within **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, then the **harmonic measure** (or **hitting distribution**) of *X* on the boundary ∂*G* of *G* is the measure *μ*<sub>*G*</sub><sup>*x*</sup> defined by

$$\mu_{G}^{x} (F) = \mathbf{P}^{x} \big[ X_{\tau_{G}} \in F \big]$$

for *x* ∈ *G* and *F* ⊆ ∂*G*.

Returning to the earlier example of Brownian motion, one can show that if *B* is a Brownian motion in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> starting at *x* ∈ **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and *D* ⊂ **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is an [open ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_ball) centred on *x*, then the harmonic measure of *B* on ∂*D* is [invariant](wikipedia://en/7635201) under all [rotations](wikipedia://en/39789) of *D* about *x* and coincides with the normalized [surface measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_measure) on ∂*D*
