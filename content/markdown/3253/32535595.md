# Michel Demazure

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Demazure"}

**Michel Demazure** (; born 2 March 1937)[^o3] is a French mathematician. He made contributions in the fields of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision), and participated in the [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) collective. He has also been president of the [French Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France) and directed two French science museums.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

In the 1960s, Demazure was a student of [Alexandre Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Grothendieck), and, together with Grothendieck, he ran and edited the [Séminaire de Géométrie Algébrique du Bois Marie](wikipedia://en/394508) on [group schemes](wikipedia://en/510523) at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) near Paris from 1962 to 1964. Demazure obtained his doctorate from the [Université de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Paris) in 1965 under Grothendieck's supervision, with a dissertation entitled *Schémas en groupes réductifs*.[^o5] He was [maître de conférence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_rank_in_France) at [Strasbourg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg_University) (1964–1966),[^o6] and then university professor at [Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_11_University) in [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay) (1966–1976)[^o7] and the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in [Palaiseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaiseau) (1976–1999).[^o7] From approximately 1965 to 1985, he was also one of the core members of the Bourbaki group, a group of French mathematicians writing under the collective pseudonym [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394).[^o8]

In 1988 Demazure was the president of the [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France).[^o9] From 1991 to 1998, he was the director of the [Palais de la Découverte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_de_la_Découverte) in Paris and, from 1998 to 2002, the chairman of the [Cité des Sciences et de l'Industrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cité_des_Sciences_et_de_l'Industrie) in [La Villette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_de_la_Villette), two major science museums in France;[^o7] in taking these positions, he changed places with [Jean Audouze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Audouze), who was at La Villette from 1993 to 1996, and became director of the Palais de la Découverte on Demazure's departure.[^o10] Demazure also chairs the regional advisory committee of research for [Languedoc-Roussillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedoc-Roussillon).[^o11]

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

In [SGA3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGA3), Demazure introduced the definition of a [root datum](wikipedia://en/6825924), a generalization of [root systems](wikipedia://en/277087) for [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386) that is central to the notion of [Langlands duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual).[^o13] A 1970 paper of Demazure on [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of the [Cremona group](wikipedia://en/7252026)[^o14] has been later recognized as the beginning of the study of [toric varieties](wikipedia://en/737087).[^o15]

The [Demazure character formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demazure_character_formula) and [Demazure modules](wikipedia://en/32561183) and [Demazure conjecture](wikipedia://en/37429514) are named after Demazure, who wrote about them in 1974.[^o16] Demazure modules are submodules of a finite-dimensional representation of a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587), and the Demazure character formula is an extension of the [Weyl character formula](wikipedia://en/5011916) to these modules. Demazure's work in this area was marred by a dependence on a false lemma in an earlier paper (also by Demazure); the flaw was pointed out by [Victor Kac](wikipedia://en/4697697), and subsequent research clarified the conditions under which the formula remains valid.[^o17]

Later in his career, Demazure's research emphasis shifted from pure mathematics to more computational problems, involving the application of algebraic geometry to [image reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_reconstruction) problems in [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision).[^o18] The [Kruppa–Demazure theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruppa–Demazure_theorem), stemming from this work, shows that if a scene consisting of five points is viewed from two cameras with unknown positions but known [focal lengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focal_length) then, in general, there will be exactly ten different scenes that could have generated the same two images. Austrian mathematician [Erwin Kruppa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Kruppa) had many years earlier narrowed the number of possible scenes to eleven, and Demazure provided the first complete solution to the problem.[^o19]
