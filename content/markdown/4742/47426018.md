# Joaquín Bustoz Jr.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joaquín Bustoz Jr."}

**Joaquín Bustoz Jr.** (1939–2003) was an American mathematician who worked as a professor of mathematics at [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University). His mathematical research concerned [classical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_analysis), including [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), [summability methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summability_methods), [univalent function](wikipedia://en/1534382), [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) and [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110). He made contributions to all of these topics, most notably in collaboration with [Mourad Ismail](wikipedia://en/32809815), Sergei Suslov and with his PhD students Luís Daniel Abreu, José Luis Cardoso and Luis Gordillo. But he was primarily known as a mentor to underrepresented minorities in mathematics.[^o2]

:::

Bustoz was born in [Tempe, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempe,_Arizona); his parents worked on the local farms and also for the [Tempe Elementary School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempe_Elementary_School_District), which eventually named the Joaquin and Ramona Bustoz Elementary School after them. He graduated from Arizona State University in 1962 with a degree in mathematics, and after two years in California working for [Univac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univac) returned to ASU,[^o2] where he completed a doctorate in 1967 under the supervision of Walter Tandy Scott. After teaching at the [University of Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cincinnati) from 1969 to 1976, during which he also spent a year at the [National University of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Colombia) as a [Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program), he returned to ASU again as an associate professor in 1976, and was promoted to full professor in 1978.[^o2][^o5] He chaired the ASU mathematics department from 1982 to 1985.[^o5]

In 1985, Bustoz founded the Summer Math–Science Honors program for high school students,[^o2][^o4][^o5] which continues at ASU as the Joaquin Bustoz Math–Science Honors Program.[^o5][^o6] Bustoz also worked on mathematics education on the [Navajo Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_Nation) and the [Pima reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pima_people).[^o2] For his efforts, president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton) honored him in 1996 with the Presidential Award for Excellence in Science, Mathematics and Engineering Mentoring.[^o7]

He was killed by a car accident on August 13, 2003.[^o4][^o5] As well as the Math–Science Honors program, the Joaquin Bustoz Jr. Professorship at ASU, held by [Carlos Castillo-Chavez](wikipedia://en/15989869), is named after Bustoz.[^o8]
