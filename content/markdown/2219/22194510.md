# Signorini problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Signorini problem"}

The **Signorini problem** is an [elastostatics](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics) problem in [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429): it consists in finding the [elastic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics) [configuration](wikipedia://en/5918#Formulation_of_models) of an [anisotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropy#Material_science_and_engineering) [non-homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_media) [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body), resting on a [rigid](wikipedia://en/455769) [frictionless](wikipedia://en/11062) [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) and subject only to its [mass forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight). The name was coined by [Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) to honour his teacher, [Antonio Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29): the original name coined by him is **problem with ambiguous [boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/436779)**.

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Classical Signorini problem.svg.* The classical Signorini problem: what will be the [equilibrium](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics) [configuration](wikipedia://en/5918#Formulation_of_models) of the orange spherically shaped [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) resting on the blue [rigid](wikipedia://en/455769) [frictionless plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frictionless_plane)?

The problem was posed by [Antonio Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29) during a course taught at the *[Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Alta_Matematica)* in 1959, later published as the article (Signorini 1959), expanding a previous short exposition he gave in a note published in 1933. (Signorini 1959, 128) himself called it *problem with ambiguous [boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/436779)*,[^o7] since there are two alternative sets of [boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/436779) the solution *must satisfy* on any given [contact point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29). The statement of the problem involves not only [equalities](wikipedia://en/90446) *but also [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489)*, and *it is not [a priori](wikipedia://en/6679056) known what of the two sets of boundary conditions is satisfied at each point*. Signorini asked to determine if the problem is [well-posed](wikipedia://en/176673) or not in a physical sense, i.e. if its solution exists and is unique or not: he explicitly invited young [analysts](wikipedia://en/48396) to study the problem.[^o8]

[Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) and [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009) attended the course, and Fichera started to investigate the problem: since he found no references to similar problems in the theory of [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779),[^o9] he decided to approach it by starting from [first principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_principle), specifically from the [virtual work principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_work_principle).

During Fichera's researches on the problem, Signorini began to suffer serious health problems: nevertheless, he desired to know the answer to his question before his death. Picone, being tied by a strong friendship with Signorini, began to chase Fichera to find a solution: Fichera himself, being tied as well to Signorini by similar feelings, perceived the last months of 1962 as worrying days.[^o10] Finally, on the first days of January 1963, Fichera was able to give a complete proof of the existence of a unique solution for the problem with ambiguous boundary condition, which he called the "Signorini problem" to honour his teacher. A preliminary research announcement, later published as (Fichera 1963), was written up and submitted to Signorini exactly a week before his death. Signorini expressed great satisfaction to see a solution to his question. A few days later, Signorini had with his [family Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_doctor), Damiano Aprile, the conversation quoted above.[^o12]

The solution of the Signorini problem coincides with the birth of the field of [variational inequalities](wikipedia://en/1468817).[^o13]

## Formal statement of the problem {#Formal_statement_of_the_problem}

The content of this section and the following subsections follows closely the treatment of [Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) in (Fichera 1963), (Fichera 1964b) and also (Fichera 1995): his derivation of the problem is different from [Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29)'s one in that he does not consider only [incompressible bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incopressible_body) and a plane rest [surface](wikipedia://en/27865), as Signorini does.[^o18] The problem consists in finding the [displacement vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_vector) from the [natural configuration](wikipedia://en/5918#Formulation_of_models) $\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(u_1(\boldsymbol{x}),u_2(\boldsymbol{x}),u_3(\boldsymbol{x})\right)$ of an [anisotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropy#Material_science_and_engineering) [non-homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_media) [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) that lies in a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $A$ of the three-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) whose [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) is $\scriptstyle\partial A$ and whose [interior normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_normal) is the [vector](wikipedia://en/32533) ***$n$***, resting on a [rigid](wikipedia://en/455769) [frictionless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frictionless_plane) [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) whose [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29) [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) (or more generally contact [set](wikipedia://en/26691)) is $\Sigma$ and subject only to its [body forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_force) $\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(f_1(\boldsymbol{x}),f_2(\boldsymbol{x}),f_3(\boldsymbol{x})\right)$, and [surface forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_force) $\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(g_1(\boldsymbol{x}),g_2(\boldsymbol{x}),g_3(\boldsymbol{x})\right)$ applied on the free (i.e. not in contact with the rest surface) surface $\scriptstyle\partial A\setminus\Sigma$: the set $A$ and the contact surface $\Sigma$ characterize the natural configuration of the body and are known a priori. Therefore, the body has to satisfy the general [equilibrium equations](wikipedia://en/228107#Equilibrium_equations_and_symmetry_of_the_stress_tensor)

((1))$\qquad\frac{\partial\sigma_{ik}}{\partial x_k}- f_i= 0\qquad\text{for } i=1,2,3$

written using the [Einstein notation](wikipedia://en/195407) as all in the following development, the ordinary [boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/436779) on $\scriptstyle\partial A\setminus\Sigma$

((2))$\qquad\sigma_{ik}n_k-g_i=0\qquad\text{for } i=1,2,3$

and the following two sets of [boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/436779) on $\Sigma$, where **$\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\sigma} = \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u})$** is the [Cauchy stress tensor](wikipedia://en/3323565). Obviously, the body forces and surface forces cannot be given in arbitrary way but they must satisfy a condition in order for the body to reach an equilibrium configuration: this condition will be deduced and analyzed in the following development.

### The ambiguous boundary conditions {#The_ambiguous_boundary_conditions}

If ***$\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3)$*** is any [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) to the [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29) [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $\Sigma$, then the ambiguous boundary condition in each [point](wikipedia://en/593693) of this set are expressed by the following two systems of [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489)

((3))$\quad \begin{cases} u_i n_i & = 0 \\ \sigma_{ik} n_i n_k & \geq 0\\ \sigma_{ik} n_i \tau_k & = 0 \end{cases}$5or5((4))$\begin{cases} u_i n_i & > 0 \\ \sigma_{ik} n_i n_k & = 0 \\ \sigma_{ik} n_i \tau_k & = 0 \end{cases}$

Let's analyze their meaning:

- Each [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of conditions consists of three [relations](wikipedia://en/3931), [equalities](wikipedia://en/90446) or [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489), and all the second members are the [zero function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_function#Other_uses_of_zero_in_mathematics).
- The [quantities](wikipedia://en/691277) at first member of each first relation are [proportional](wikipedia://en/81863) to the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) of the [component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_component) of the [displacement vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_vector) directed along the [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) **$n$**.
- The quantities at first member of each second relation are proportional to the norm of the component of the [tension vector](wikipedia://en/228107#Relationship_stress_vector_-_stress_tensor) directed along the [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) **$n$**,
- The quantities at the first member of each third relation are proportional to the norm of the component of the tension vector along any [vector](wikipedia://en/32533) **$\tau$** [tangent](wikipedia://en/191999) in the given [point](wikipedia://en/593693) to the [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29) [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $\Sigma$.
- The quantities at the first member of each of the three relations are [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) if they have the same [sense](wikipedia://en/32533) of the [vector](wikipedia://en/32533) they are [proportional](wikipedia://en/81863) to, while they are [negative](wikipedia://en/154616) if not, therefore the [constants of proportionality](wikipedia://en/81863) are respectively $\scriptstyle +1$ and $\scriptstyle -1$.

Knowing these facts, the set of conditions ((3)) applies to [points](wikipedia://en/593693) of the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of the body which *do not* leave the [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29) set $\Sigma$ in the [equilibrium configuration](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics), since, according to the first [relation](wikipedia://en/3931), the [displacement vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_vector) **$u$** *has no [components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_component)* directed as the [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) **$n$**, while, according to the second relation, the [tension vector](wikipedia://en/228107#Relationship_stress_vector_-_stress_tensor) *may have a component* directed as the normal vector **$n$** and having the same [sense](wikipedia://en/32533). In an analogous way, the set of conditions ((4)) applies to points of the boundary of the body which *leave* that set in the equilibrium configuration, since displacement vector **$u$** *has a component* directed as the normal vector **$n$**, while the [tension vector](wikipedia://en/228107#Relationship_stress_vector_-_stress_tensor) *has no components* directed as the normal vector **$n$**. For both sets of conditions, the tension vector has no tangent component to the [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29) set, according to the [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) that the body rests on a rigid *frictionless* surface.

Each system expresses a **unilateral constraint**, in the sense that they express the physical impossibility of the [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) to penetrate into the surface where it rests: the ambiguity is not only in the unknown values non-[zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero) quantities must satisfy on the [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29) set but also in the fact that it is not a priori known if a point belonging to that set satisfies the system of boundary conditions ((3)) or ((4)). The set of points where ((3)) is satisfied is called the **area of support** of the elastic body on $\Sigma$, while its [complement respect to $\Sigma$](wikipedia://en/54347#Relative_complement) is called the **area of separation**.

The above formulation is *general* since the [Cauchy stress tensor](wikipedia://en/3323565) i.e. the [constitutive equation](wikipedia://en/1034699) of the [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) has not been made explicit: it is equally valid assuming the [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) of [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429) or the ones of [nonlinear elasticity](wikipedia://en/2210759). However, as it would be clear from the following developments, the problem is inherently [nonlinear](wikipedia://en/146103), therefore *assuming a [linear stress tensor](wikipedia://en/301429#Mathematical_formulation) does not simplify the problem*.

### The form of the stress tensor in the formulation of Signorini and Fichera {#The_form_of_the_stress_tensor_in_the_formulation_of_Signorini_and_Fichera}

The form assumed by [Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29) and [Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) for the [elastic potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_potential_energy) is the following one (as in the previous developments, the [Einstein notation](wikipedia://en/195407) is adopted)

$$W(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})=a_{ik,jh}(\boldsymbol{x})\varepsilon_{ik}\varepsilon_{jh}$$

where

- $\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{a}(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(a_{ik,jh}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)$ is the [elasticity tensor](wikipedia://en/7739252)
- $\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})=\left(\varepsilon_{ik}(\boldsymbol{u})\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_k} + \frac{\partial u_k}{\partial x_i} \right)\right)$ is the [infinitesimal strain tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_strain#Infinitesimal_strain_tensor)

The [Cauchy stress tensor](wikipedia://en/3323565) has therefore the following form

((5))$\sigma_{ik}= - \frac{\partial W}{\partial \varepsilon_{ik}} \qquad\text{for } i,k=1,2,3$

and it is *[linear](wikipedia://en/18102)* with respect to the components of the infinitesimal strain tensor; however, it is not [homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneity_%28physics%29) nor [isotropic](wikipedia://en/14865).

## Solution of the problem {#Solution_of_the_problem}

As for the section on the formal statement of the Signorini problem, the contents of this section and the included subsections follow closely the treatment of [Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) in (Fichera 1963), (Fichera 1964b), (Fichera 1972) and also (Fichera 1995): obviously, the exposition focuses on the basics steps of the proof of the existence and uniqueness for the solution of problem ((1)), ((2)), ((3)), ((4)) and ((5)), rather than the technical details.

### The potential energy {#The_potential_energy}

The first step of the analysis of Fichera as well as the first step of the analysis of [Antonio Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29) in (Signorini 1959) is the analysis of the **potential energy**, i.e. the following [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948)

((6))$I(\boldsymbol{u})=\int_A W(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})\mathrm{d}x - \int_A u_i f_i\mathrm{d}x - \int_{\partial A\setminus\Sigma}u_i g_i \mathrm{d}\sigma$

where **$u$** belongs to the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of **admissible displacements** $\scriptstyle\mathcal{U}_\Sigma$ i.e. the set of [displacement vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_vector) satisfying the system of [boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/436779) ((3)) or ((4)). The meaning of each of the three terms is the following

- the first one is the total [elastic potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_potential_energy) of the [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body)
- the second one is the total [potential energy](wikipedia://en/23703) due to the [body forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_force), for example the [gravitational force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_force)
- the third one is the potential energy due to [surface forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_force), for example the [forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_%28physics%29) exerted by the [atmospheric pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_pressure)

(Signorini 1959, 129–133) was able to prove that the admissible displacement **$u$** which [minimize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum) the integral **$I(u)$** is a solution of the problem with ambiguous boundary conditions ((1)), ((2)), ((3)), ((4)) and ((5)), provided it is a [$C^1$ function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [supported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29#Support_of_a_distribution) on the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) $\scriptstyle \bar A$ of the set $A$: however [Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) gave a class of [counterexamples](wikipedia://en/143151) in (Fichera 1964b, 619–620) showing that in general, admissible displacements are not [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) of these class. Therefore, Fichera tries to minimize the [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) ((6)) in a wider [function space](wikipedia://en/340630): in doing so, he first calculates the [first variation](wikipedia://en/3655074) (or [functional derivative](wikipedia://en/301504)) of the given functional in the [neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_%28topology%29) of the sought minimizing admissible displacement $\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{u} \in \mathcal{U}_\Sigma$, and then requires it to be greater than or equal to [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero)

$$\left. \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} I( \boldsymbol{u} + t \boldsymbol{v}) \right\vert_{t=0} = -\int_A \sigma_{ik}(\boldsymbol{u})\varepsilon_{ik}(\boldsymbol{v})\mathrm{d}x - \int_A v_i f_i\mathrm{d}x - \int_{\partial A\setminus\Sigma}\!\!\!\!\! v_i g_i \mathrm{d}\sigma \geq 0 \qquad \forall \boldsymbol{v} \in \mathcal{U}_\Sigma$$

Defining the following functionals

$$B(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}) = -\int_A \sigma_{ik}(\boldsymbol{u})\varepsilon_{ik}(\boldsymbol{v})\mathrm{d}x \qquad \boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v} \in \mathcal{U}_\Sigma$$

and

$$F(\boldsymbol{v}) = \int_A v_i f_i\mathrm{d}x + \int_{\partial A\setminus\Sigma}\!\!\!\!\! v_i g_i \mathrm{d}\sigma\qquad \boldsymbol{v} \in \mathcal{U}_\Sigma$$

the preceding [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) is can be written as

((7))$B(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}) - F(\boldsymbol{v}) \geq 0 \qquad \forall \boldsymbol{v} \in \mathcal{U}_\Sigma$

This inequality is the **[variational inequality](wikipedia://en/1468817) for the Signorini problem**.
