# János Pintz

Pintz János

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="János Pintz"}

**János Pintz** (; born 20 December 1950 in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest))[^o2] is a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513). He is a fellow of the [Rényi Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/31714670) and is also a member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences). In 2014, he received the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).

:::

## Mathematical results {#Mathematical_results}

Pintz is best known for proving in 2005 (with [Daniel Goldston](wikipedia://en/2393553) and [Cem Yıldırım](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cem_Yıldırım))[^o4] that

$$\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{\log p_n}=0$$

where $p_n$ denotes the *n*<sup>th</sup> [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). In other words, for every ε > 0, there exist infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes *p*<sub>*n*</sub> and *p*<sub>*n*+1</sub> that are closer to each other than the average distance between consecutive primes by a factor of ε, i.e., *p*<sub>*n*+1</sub> − *p*<sub>*n*</sub> \< ε log *p*<sub>*n*</sub>. This result was originally reported in 2003 by [Daniel Goldston](wikipedia://en/2393553) and [Cem Yıldırım](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cem_Yıldırım) but was later retracted.[^o5][^o6] Pintz joined the team and completed the proof in 2005 and developed the so-called [GPY sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPY_sieve). Later, they improved this to showing that *p*<sub>*n*+1</sub> − *p*<sub>*n*</sub> \< ε$\sqrt{\log n}$(log log *n*)<sup>2</sup> occurs infinitely often. Further, if one assumes the [Elliott–Halberstam conjecture](wikipedia://en/1898427), then one can also show that primes within 16 of each other occur infinitely often, which is nearly the [twin prime conjecture](wikipedia://en/41997).

Additionally,

- With [János Komlós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Komlós_%28mathematician%29) and [Endre Szemerédi](wikipedia://en/663489), he disproved the [Heilbronn conjecture](wikipedia://en/1013950).[^o7]
- With [Iwaniec](wikipedia://en/9338319), he proved that for sufficiently large *n* there is a prime between *n* and *n* + *n*<sup>23/42</sup>.[^o8]
- Pintz gave an effective upper bound for the first number for which the [Mertens conjecture](wikipedia://en/379847) fails.[^o9]
- He gave an O(*x*<sup>2/3</sup>) upper bound for the number of those numbers that are less than *x* and not the sum of two primes.
- With [Imre Z. Ruzsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Z._Ruzsa), he improved a result of [Linnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Linnik) by showing that every sufficiently large even number is the sum of two primes and at most 8 powers of 2.
- Goldston, S. W. Graham, Pintz, and Yıldırım proved that the difference between numbers which are products of exactly 2 primes is infinitely often at most 6.[^o10]
