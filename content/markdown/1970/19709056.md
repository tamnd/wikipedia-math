# Gil Kalai

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gil Kalai"}

**Gil Kalai** (גיל קלעי; born 1955) is an Israeli mathematician and computer scientist. He is the Henry and Manya Noskwith Professor Emeritus of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), professor of computer science at the [Interdisciplinary Center, Herzliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Interdisciplinary_Center), and adjunct professor of mathematics and of computer science at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), United States.[^o28]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kalai was born in 1955 in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv).[^o4] He is the son of [Hanoch Kalai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoch_Kalai), who was a leader of [Irgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun) and co-founder of [Lehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehi_%28militant_group%29). He received his PhD from Hebrew University in 1983 advised by [Micha Perles](wikipedia://en/35294296),[^o5] and held a postdoctoral position at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o6] He joined the Hebrew University faculty in 1985, where he has remained since.

From 1995 to 2001, he was the editor-in-chief of the *[Israel Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Journal_of_Mathematics)*. In 2016, he was elected honorary member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o7] In 2018 he was a plenary speaker with talk *Noise Stability, Noise Sensitivity and the Quantum Computer Puzzle* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Rio de Janeiro.

## Research {#Research}

Kalai is known for finding variants of the [simplex algorithm](wikipedia://en/349458) in [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) that can be proven to run in subexponential time,[^o9] for showing that every [monotone property of graphs](wikipedia://en/13552465) has a sharp [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423),[^o10] for solving [Borsuk's problem](wikipedia://en/7765817) on the number of pieces needed to partition convex sets into subsets of smaller diameter,[^o11] and for his work on the [Hirsch conjecture](wikipedia://en/10412371) on the diameter of [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885) and in [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519) more generally.[^o12]

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Quantum computing skepticism and conjectures"}

### Quantum computing skepticism and conjectures {#Quantum_computing_skepticism_and_conjectures}

Kalai is a noted skeptic of [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220) and argues that true quantum computing, which would give exponential speedups over classical computing, cannot be achieved because of inherent limitations when trying to implement [quantum error correction](wikipedia://en/892803) experimentally. He has engaged in a series of public debates with quantum computing researcher [Aram Harrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aram_Harrow) on his blog,[^o15][^o16][^o17] and has formalized his argument by stating a series of conjectures.[^o18] Harrow and Steven Flammia published a preprint on [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) in 2012 claiming to refute Kalai's Conjecture C,[^o19] although Kalai later argued in 2022 that there were flaws in their argument.[^o20] In 2025, Kalai publicly debated quantum computing researcher Matthias Christandl at the [Learned Society of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_the_Czech_Republic) on whether true quantum computing had already been achieved.[^o21]

Kalai's conjectures are stated as follows:

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=inline-head name="Conjecture 1 (No quantum error correction)"}

**Conjecture 1 (No quantum error correction)**. The process for creating a quantum error-correcting code will necessarily lead to a mixture of the desired codewords with undesired codewords. The probability of the undesired codewords is uniformly bounded away from zero. (In every implementation of quantum error-correcting codes with one encoded qubit, the probability of not getting the intended qubit is at least some δ > 0, independently of the number of qubits used for encoding.)

:::

::: {#o23 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=inline-head name="Conjecture 2"}

**Conjecture 2**. A noisy quantum computer is subject to noise in which information leaks for two substantially entangled qubits have a substantial positive correlation.

:::

::: {#o24 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=inline-head name="Conjecture 3"}

**Conjecture 3**. In any quantum computer at a highly entangled state there will be a strong effect of error-synchronization.

:::

::: {#o25 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=inline-head name="Conjecture 4"}

**Conjecture 4**. Noisy quantum processes are subject to detrimental noise.[^o26]

:::

:::

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Kalai was the recipient of the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28SIAM%29) in 1992, the [Erdős Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_Prize) of the Israel Mathematical Society in 1993, and the [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) in 1994.[^o28] He was also the winner of the 2012 [Rothschild Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Prize) in mathematics.[^o29] He was named to the 2023 class of Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), "for contributions to combinatorics, convexity, and their applications, as well as to the exposition and communication of mathematics".[^o30]
