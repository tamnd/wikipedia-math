# Moyal bracket

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moyal bracket"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **Moyal bracket** is the suitably normalized antisymmetrization of the phase-space [star product](wikipedia://en/1946702).

:::

The Moyal bracket was developed in about 1940 by [José Enrique Moyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Enrique_Moyal), but Moyal only succeeded in publishing his work in 1949 after a lengthy dispute with [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742).[^o2][^o3] In the meantime this idea was independently introduced in 1946 by [Hip Groenewold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbrand_J._Groenewold).[^o4]

## Overview {#Overview}

The Moyal bracket is a way of describing the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) of observables in the [phase space formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_space_formulation) of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) when these observables are described as functions on [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101). It relies on schemes for identifying functions on phase space with quantum observables, the most famous of these schemes being the [Wigner–Weyl transform](wikipedia://en/4277086). It underlies [Moyal's dynamical equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_space_formulation#Time_evolution), an equivalent formulation of [Heisenberg's quantum equation of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_equation), thereby providing the quantum generalization of [Hamilton's equations](wikipedia://en/198319).

Mathematically, it is a [deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) of the phase-space [Poisson bracket](wikipedia://en/294340) (essentially an [extension](wikipedia://en/45603435) of it), the deformation parameter being the reduced [Planck constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_constant) $\text{ħ}$. Thus, its [group contraction](wikipedia://en/34976899) $\text{ħ}\to0$ yields the [Poisson bracket](wikipedia://en/294340) [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944).

Up to formal equivalence, the Moyal Bracket is the *unique one-parameter Lie-algebraic deformation* of the Poisson bracket. Its algebraic isomorphism to the algebra of commutators bypasses the negative result of the Groenewold–van Hove theorem, which precludes such an isomorphism for the Poisson bracket, a question implicitly raised by Dirac in his 1926 doctoral thesis,[^o6] the "method of classical analogy" for quantization.[^o7]

For instance, in a two-dimensional flat [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101), and for the [Weyl-map correspondence](wikipedia://en/4277086), the Moyal bracket reads,

$$\begin{aligned} \{\{f,g\}\} & \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \frac{1}{i\hbar}(f\star g-g\star f) \\ & = \{f,g\} + O(\hbar^2), \\ \end{aligned}$$

where ★ is the star-product operator in phase space (cf. [Moyal product](wikipedia://en/1946702)), while $f$ and $g$ are differentiable phase-space functions, and $\{f, g\}$ is their Poisson bracket.[^o8]

A popular (Fourier) integral representation for it, introduced by George Baker[^o10] is

$$\{ \{ f,g \} \}(x,p) = {2 \over \hbar^3 \pi^2 } \int dp' \, dp'' \, dx' \, dx'' f(x+x',p+p') g(x+x'',p+p'')\sin \left( \tfrac{2}{\hbar} (x'p''-x''p')\right)~.$$

Each correspondence map from phase space to Hilbert space induces a characteristic "Moyal" bracket (such as the one illustrated here for the Weyl map). All such Moyal brackets are *formally equivalent* among themselves, in accordance with a systematic theory.[^o11]

The Moyal bracket specifies the eponymous infinite-dimensional [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944)—it is antisymmetric in its arguments $f$ and $g$, and satisfies the [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370). The corresponding abstract [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is realized by $T_{f} \equiv f$★, so that

$$[ T_f ~, T_g ] = T_{i\hbar \{ \{ f,g \} \} }.$$

On a 2-torus phase space, $T^{2}$, with periodic coordinates $x$ and $p$, each in $[0,2\pi]$, and integer mode indices $m_{i}$ , for basis functions $\exp(i (m_{1}x+m_{2}p))$, this Lie algebra reads,[^o12]

$$[ T_{m_1,m_2} ~ , T_{n_1,n_2} ] = 2i \sin \left (\tfrac{\hbar}{2}(n_1 m_2 - n_2 m_1 )\right ) ~ T_{m_1+n_1,m_2+ n_2}, ~$$

which reduces to *SU*(*N*) for integer $N \equiv 4\pi/\text{ħ}$. *SU*(*N*) then emerges as a deformation of *SU*(∞), with deformation parameter 1/*N*.

Generalization of the Moyal bracket for quantum systems with [second-class constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_class_constraints) involves an operation on equivalence classes of functions in phase space,[^o13] which can be considered as a [quantum deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) of the [Dirac bracket](wikipedia://en/13657747).

## Sine bracket and cosine bracket {#Sine_bracket_and_cosine_bracket}

Next to the sine bracket discussed, Groenewold further introduced[^o4] the cosine bracket, elaborated by Baker,[^o10][^o15]

$$\begin{aligned} \{ \{ \{f ,g\} \} \} & \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \tfrac{1}{2}(f\star g+g\star f) = f g + O(\hbar^2). \\ \end{aligned}$$

Here, again, ★ is the star-product operator in phase space, $f$ and $g$ are differentiable phase-space functions, and $f g$ is the ordinary product.

The sine and cosine brackets are, respectively, the results of antisymmetrizing and symmetrizing the star product. Thus, as the sine bracket is the [Wigner map](wikipedia://en/4277086) of the commutator, the cosine bracket is the Wigner image of the [anticommutator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutator) in standard quantum mechanics. Similarly, as the Moyal bracket equals the Poisson bracket up to higher orders of $\text{ħ}$, the cosine bracket equals the ordinary product up to higher orders of $\text{ħ}$. In the [classical limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_limit), the Moyal bracket helps reduction to the [Liouville equation (formulated in terms of the Poisson bracket)](wikipedia://en/312301#Poisson_bracket), as the cosine bracket leads to the classical [Hamilton–Jacobi equation](wikipedia://en/897539).[^o16]

The sine and cosine bracket also stand in relation to equations of [a purely algebraic description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_Hiley#Projections_into_shadow_manifolds) of quantum mechanics.[^o16][^o17]
