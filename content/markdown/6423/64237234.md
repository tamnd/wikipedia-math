# Weyl expansion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weyl expansion"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **Weyl expansion**, also known as the **Weyl identity** or **angular spectrum expansion**, expresses an outgoing [spherical wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_wave) as a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of [plane waves](wikipedia://en/41559). In a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706), it can be denoted as[^n1][^n2]

:::

$$\frac{e^{-i k_0 r}}{r}=\frac{1}{ 2\pi i} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dk_x dk_y e^{-i(k_x x + k_y y)} \frac{e^{-ik_z |z|}}{k_z},$$

where $k_x$, $k_y$ and $k_z$ are the [wavenumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavenumber) in their respective coordinate axes:

$$k_0=\sqrt{k_x^2+k_y^2+k_z^2}.$$

The expansion is named after [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), who published it in 1919.[^o14] The Weyl identity is largely used to characterize the reflection and transmission of spherical waves at planar interfaces; it is often used to derive the [Green's functions](wikipedia://en/311001) for [Helmholtz equation](wikipedia://en/1156215) in layered media. The expansion also covers [evanescent wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanescent_wave) components. It is often preferred to the [Sommerfeld identity](wikipedia://en/3644058) when the field representation is needed to be in Cartesian coordinates.[^n1]

The resulting Weyl integral is commonly encountered in [microwave integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolithic_microwave_integrated_circuit) analysis and electromagnetic radiation over a stratified medium; as in the case for Sommerfeld integral, it is [numerically evaluated](wikipedia://en/170089).[^o12] As a result, it is used in calculation of Green's functions for [method of moments](wikipedia://en/68572267) for such geometries.[^n3] Other uses include the descriptions of [dipolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_dipole) emissions near surfaces in [nanophotonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanophotonics),[^n4][^o13][^o10] [holographic](wikipedia://en/66338) [inverse scattering problems](wikipedia://en/385661),[^o15] Green's functions in [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268)[^o11] and [acoustic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_wave) or [seismic waves](wikipedia://en/89830).[^n5]
