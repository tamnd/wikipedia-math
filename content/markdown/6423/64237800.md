# Fundamental theorem of Hilbert spaces

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fundamental theorem of Hilbert spaces"}

In mathematics, specifically in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) theory, the **fundamental theorem of Hilbert spaces** gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [pre-Hilbert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Hilbert_space) to be a Hilbert space in terms of the canonical isometry of a pre-Hilbert space into its [anti-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-dual).

:::

## Preliminaries {#Preliminaries}

### Antilinear functionals and the anti-dual {#Antilinear_functionals_and_the_anti-dual}

Suppose that $H$ is a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS). A function $f:H\to\mathbb{C}$ is called [semilinear](wikipedia://en/24918060) or [antilinear](wikipedia://en/293432)[^n1] if for all $x,y\in H$ and all scalars $c$,

- $f$ is [additive](wikipedia://en/22473529): $f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)$;
- $f$ is [conjugate homogeneous](wikipedia://en/293432): $f(c\,x)=\bar{c}\,f(x)$.

The vector space of all continuous antilinear functions on $H$ is called the [anti-dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-dual_space) or [complex conjugate dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugate_vector_space) of $H$ and is denoted by $\overline{H}^{\prime}$. (In contrast, the continuous dual space of $H$ is denoted by $H^{\prime}$), which we make into a [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) by endowing it with the canonical norm (defined in the same way as the [canonical norm](wikipedia://en/246722) on the [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) of $H$.)[^n1]

### Pre-Hilbert spaces and sesquilinear forms {#Pre-Hilbert_spaces_and_sesquilinear_forms}

A [sesquilinear form](wikipedia://en/860138) is a map $B:H\times H\to\mathbb{C}$ such that for all $y\in H$, the map defined by $x\mapsto B(x,y)$ is [linear](wikipedia://en/18102), and for all $x\in H$, the map defined by $y\mapsto B(x,y)$ is [antilinear](wikipedia://en/24918060).[^n1] Note that in [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the convention is that a sesquilinear form is linear in its *second* coordinate and antilinear in its first coordinate.

A sesquilinear form $B$ on $H$ is called [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_definite) if $B(x,x)>0$ for all non-0 $x\in H$; it is called non-negative if $B(x,x)\ge 0$ for all $x\in H$.[^n1] A sesquilinear form $B$ on $H$ is called a [Hermitian form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form) if in addition it has the property that $B(x, y) = \overline{B(y, x)}$ for all $x,y\in H$.[^n1]

### Pre-Hilbert and Hilbert spaces {#Pre-Hilbert_and_Hilbert_spaces}

A [pre-Hilbert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Hilbert_space) is a pair consisting of a vector space $H$ and a non-negative sesquilinear form $B$ on $H$; if in addition this sesquilinear form $B$ is positive definite then $(H,B)$ is called a Hausdorff pre-Hilbert space.[^n1] If $B$ is non-negative then it induces a canonical [seminorm](wikipedia://en/292067) on $H$, denoted by $\| \cdot \|$, defined by $x\mapsto B(x,x)^{1/2}$, where if $B$ is also positive definite then this map is a [norm](wikipedia://en/990534).[^n1] This canonical semi-norm makes every pre-Hilbert space into a [seminormed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminormed_space) and every Hausdorff pre-Hilbert space into a [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space). The sesquilinear form $B:H\times H\to\mathbb{C}$ is separately uniformly continuous in each of its two arguments and hence can be extended to a separately continuous sesquilinear form on the [completion](wikipedia://en/53695856) of $H$; if $H$ is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) then this completion is a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).[^n1] A Hausdorff pre-Hilbert space that is [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) is called a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

## Fundamental theorem {#Fundamental_theorem}

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Fundamental theorem of Hilbert spaces"}

**Fundamental theorem of Hilbert spaces[^n1].**

Suppose that $(H,B)$ is a [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [pre-Hilbert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Hilbert_space) where $B:H\times H\to\mathbb{C}$ is a [sesquilinear form](wikipedia://en/860138) that is [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear) in its first coordinate and antilinear in its second coordinate. Then the canonical linear mapping from $H$ into the [anti-dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-dual_space) of $H$ is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) if and only if $(H,B)$ is a Hilbert space, in which case the canonical map is a surjective [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) of $H$ onto its anti-dual.
