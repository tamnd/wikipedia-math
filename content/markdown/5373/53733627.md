# Louise Nixon Sutton

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Louise Nixon Sutton"}

**Louise Nixon Sutton** (November 4, 1925 – May 14, 2006) was a mathematician. She was the first African-American woman to be awarded a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics education by [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University),[^o3][^o4] and the first chair of the Department of Physical Sciences and Mathematics at [Elizabeth City State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_City_State_University).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sutton was born in [Hertford, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford,_North_Carolina).[^o8] Her parents were Annie McNair Nixon and John Calhoun Nixon, and she had three brothers: John Stuart Nixon, Norris Lee Nixon, and Thomas Rufus Nixon.[^o8] She gained a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) from the [North Carolina Agricultural and Technical State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Agricultural_and_Technical_State_University) in 1946, and an MA from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in 1951.[^o3] She was awarded the first PhD in mathematics education to an African American at New York University in 1962, for a dissertation titled *Concept learning in trigonometry and analytic geometry at the college level: a comparative study of two methods of teaching trigonometry and analytic geometry at the college level*.[^o9]

## Career {#Career}

After gaining her degrees, Sutton taught mathematics at [James B. Dudley High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_B._Dudley_High_School) in [Greensboro, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro,_North_Carolina), from 1946 to 1947. She was very interested in math at school.[^o3] From 1947 to 1954 she was assistant professor at North Carolina A&T State University, before moving to hold the same position at Delaware State College from 1957 to 1962.[^o3]

In 1962 she became Professor and Chair of the Physical Sciences and Mathematics Department at [Elizabeth City State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_City_State_University) in [Elizabeth City, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_City,_North_Carolina).[^o3]

Sutton was a member of the North Carolina A&T State University Alumni Association, [Delta Sigma Theta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Sigma_Theta) sorority, National Association of University Women, and the National Council of Teachers of Mathematics.[^o8] She was Chairperson of the National Association of University Women from 1977 to 1978, and a member of [Alpha Kappa Mu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Kappa_Mu), and [Beta Kappa Chi](wikipedia://en/44516824) Scientific Society.[^o3]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Sutton died at home in Hertford, North Carolina, on May 14, 2006.[^o8] She had been an active member of the St. Paul [A.M.E. Zion Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.M.E._Zion_Church), [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Colored_People), George Washington Carver Floral Club, Arabia Court No. 35 of [Daughters of Isis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughters_of_Isis), and the North Carolina State Retired Employees Association.[^o8]
