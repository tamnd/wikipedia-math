# Minerva Cordero

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Minerva Cordero"}

**Minerva Cordero Braña** [^o3][^o4] is a Puerto Rican mathematician and a professor of mathematics at the [University of Texas at Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Arlington). She is also the university's Senior Associate Dean for the College of Science, where she is responsible for the advancement of the research mission of the college.[^o5] President Biden awarded her the [Presidential Award for Excellence in Science, Mathematics, and Engineering Mentoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Award_for_Excellence_in_Science,_Mathematics,_and_Engineering_Mentoring) (PAESMEM) on February 8, 2022.[^o6]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Cordero was born in [Bayamón, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayamón,_Puerto_Rico). Her mother, whose schooling stopped after the fifth grade,[^o20] made education a top priority in the family home. She told her children "the best thing I can give you is an education." Cordero and her siblings would do their homework together and discussed what they learned in school each day.[^o20] Cordero said, "We learned each other's subjects." Wanting to go to college, Cordero bought herself a college exam preparation book in high school and studied for the college-entrance exam.[^o20][^o8] She states that her exam scores were the highest scores for her high school, Miguel Melendez Munoz High School.[^o8]

Cordero attended the Universidad de Puerto Rico in Rio Piedras and received her B.S. in Mathematics in 1981. She was granted a National Science Foundation Minority Graduate Fellowship which she used to attend the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) to obtain her masters in mathematics in 1983. She continued her studies at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa), and obtained her Ph.D. in mathematics in 1989 under Norman Johnson.[^o9][^o8]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Cordero's research is in the area of finite semifields ([non-associative algebras](wikipedia://en/6533841)) and their associated planes (viewed affinely or projectively) in the general area of [finite geometry](wikipedia://en/267061).[^o11]

After earning her Ph.D., Cordero worked as an associate and an assistant professor at [Texas Tech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Tech_University) until 2001, when she joined the faculty at the [University of Texas at Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Arlington).[^o5] Cordero served as the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)'s Governor-at-Large for Minority Interests from 2008 to 2011.[^o12][^o13]

Cordero's most-cited work is *A survey of finite [semifields](wikipedia://en/10417428)*.[^o14] She was the Principal Investigator for a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) grant of \$2.85 million awarded to the [University of Texas at Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Arlington) in 2009 for a project that placed mathematics graduate students in [Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas) public schools to enhance teaching and learning in the classrooms and to inspire students to pursue careers in [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663) (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics).[^o15]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- New Faculty Award, Texas Tech University, 1994[^o8]
- Professor of the Year, student chapter of the Mathematical Association of America at Texas Tech University, 1995[^o8]
- President's Excellence in Teaching Award, Texas Tech University, 1999
- Outstanding Hispanic Women of the South Plains, South Plains Hispanic Chamber of Commerce, 2000
- Featured Biography, Mathematical Association of America Strengthening Underrepresented Minority Mathematics Achievement (SUMMA) Program, 2001[^o17]
- University of Texas Board of Regents' Outstanding Teaching Award, 2009[^o18]
- Featured Biography, SACNAS Biography Project, 2011[^o19][^o20]
- Certificate of Meritorious Service, [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), 2012
- Ford Legendary Women (Mujeres Legendarias de Ford), 2016[^o21][^o22]
- Great Minds in STEM 2016 HENAAC Award Education Distinction, 2016[^o22]
- [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) IF/THEN Ambassador, 2019[^o23]
- Presidential Award for Excellence in Science, Mathematics and Engineering Mentoring (PAESMEM), 2022[^o6][^o24]
- Fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the Class of 2023.[^o25]
