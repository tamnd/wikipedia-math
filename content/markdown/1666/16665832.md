# Richard Ernest Kronauer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Ernest Kronauer"}

**Richard Ernest Kronauer** (1925 – October 18, 2019) was the [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) [Gordon McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_McKay) professor of mechanical engineering at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o2] He conducted research in both [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).

:::

He is primarily known for his pioneering work in [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology), especially his research on human [circadian rhythms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circadian_rhythms). Kronauer's 1982 paper "Mathematical model of the human circadian system with two interacting oscillators"[^o3] outlined a new method for understanding the biological circuits that underlie daily body cycles in variables such as blood pressure or body temperature. Professor Kronauer's research also has direct implications for the causes and possible cures for many types of sleep disorders, and for he received the [Farrell Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrell_Prize) in Sleep Medicine in June 2008.[^o4][^o5]

## Biography {#Biography}

- Born in 1925
- BS in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) from [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Institute_of_Technology) in 1947
- MS in mechanical engineering from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1948
- PhD in mechanical engineering from Harvard University in 1951
  - [Dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation): *Secondary Flows in Fluid Dynamics*, with advisor [Howard Wilson Emmons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Wilson_Emmons)
- Stayed at Harvard after PhD, and eventually named Gordon McKay professor (sometime before 1976)[^o7]
- Wife Joanne Edwards Kronauer
- As of March 2008, Professor Kronauer is listed as co-inventor on 6 patents for devices relating to circadian rhythm: US Patent Numbers 5163426, 5176133, 5167228, 5304212, 5503637, 5545192
- Co-inventor for pending patent application 11/113,356, filed Apr 25, 2005
- Co-inventor for a reciprocating actuator (patent #3312842, 1964) and a fluid operating apparatus (patent #3274795, 1964)
- Died October 18, 2019.
