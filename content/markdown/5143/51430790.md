::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="Möbius–Kantor polygon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Möbius–Kantor polygon** is a [regular complex polygon](wikipedia://en/50885422) <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>, , in $\mathbb{C}^2$. <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub> has 8 vertices, and 8 edges. It is self-dual. Every vertex is shared by 3 triangular edges.[^o9] Coxeter named it a *Möbius–Kantor polygon* for sharing the [complex configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_configuration) structure as the [Möbius–Kantor configuration](wikipedia://en/13396856), (8<sub>3</sub>).[^o10]

:::

Discovered by [G.C. Shephard](wikipedia://en/33354532) in 1952, he represented it as 3(24)3, with its symmetry, Coxeter called as <sub>3</sub>\[3\]<sub>3</sub>, isomorphic to the [binary tetrahedral group](wikipedia://en/10997586), order 24.

## Real representation {#Real_representation}

It has a real representation as the [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422), , in 4-dimensional space, sharing the same 8 vertices. The 24 edges in the 16-cell are seen in the Möbius–Kantor polygon when the 8 triangular edges are drawn as 3-separate edges. The triangles are represented 2 sets of 4 red or blue outlines. The B<sub>4</sub> projections are given in two different symmetry orientations between the two color sets.
