# Kinoshita–Terasaka knot

*Image: Knudemutation.svg.* The [prime](wikipedia://en/315030) **Kinoshita–Terasaka knot** (11n42) (left) and the prime [Conway knot](wikipedia://en/3727038) (11n34) (right) showing how they are related by [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_%28knot_theory%29)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kinoshita–Terasaka knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Kinoshita–Terasaka knot** is a particular [prime knot](wikipedia://en/315030) with 11 crossings.[^o3] It is named after Japanese mathematicians Shinichi Kinoshita and Hidetaka Terasaka, who wrote about it in 1957.[^o4] The Kinoshita–Terasaka knot has a variety of interesting mathematical properties.[^o5] It is related by [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_%28knot_theory%29) to the [Conway knot](wikipedia://en/3727038),[^o6] with which it shares a [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642). It has the same [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) as the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075).[^o7]

:::
