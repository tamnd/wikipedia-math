# Desuspension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Desuspension"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a field within mathematics, **desuspension** is an operation [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) to [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In general, given an *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) space $X$, the suspension $\Sigma{X}$ has dimension *n* + 1. Thus, the operation of suspension creates a way of moving up in dimension. In the 1950s, to define a way of moving down, mathematicians introduced an inverse operation $\Sigma^{-1}$, called desuspension.[^o5] Therefore, given an *n*-dimensional space $X$, the desuspension $\Sigma^{-1}{X}$ has dimension *n* – 1.

In general, $\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma{X}\ne X$.

:::

## Reasons {#Reasons}

The reasons to introduce desuspension:

1. Desuspension makes the category of spaces a [triangulated category](wikipedia://en/1679700).
1. If arbitrary [coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889) were allowed, desuspension would result in all [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) being representable.
