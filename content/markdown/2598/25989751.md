# R-algebroid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="R-algebroid"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **R-algebroids** are constructed starting from [groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543). These are more abstract concepts than the [Lie algebroids](wikipedia://en/310921) that play a similar role in the theory of [Lie groupoids](wikipedia://en/310923) to that of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) in the theory of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945). (Thus, a Lie algebroid can be thought of as 'a *[Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) with [many objects](wikipedia://en/1596969)* ').

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

An **R-algebroid**, $R\mathsf{G}$, is constructed from a groupoid $\mathsf{G}$ as follows. The object set of $R\mathsf{G}$ is the same as that of $\mathsf{G}$ and $R\mathsf{G}(b,c)$ is the [free](wikipedia://en/326454) [R-module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-module) on the set $\mathsf{G}(b,c)$, with composition given by the usual bilinear rule, extending the composition of $\mathsf{G}$.[^o4]

:::

## R-category {#R-category}

A groupoid $\mathsf{G}$ can be regarded as a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) with invertible morphisms. Then an **R-category** is defined as an extension of the **R**-algebroid concept by replacing the groupoid $\mathsf{G}$ in this construction with a general category **C** that does not have all morphisms invertible.

## R-algebroids *via* convolution products {#R-algebroids_via_convolution_products}

One can also define the **R-algebroid**, ${\bar R}\mathsf{G}:=R\mathsf{G}(b,c)$, to be the **set of functions** $\mathsf{G}(b,c){\longrightarrow}R$ **with [finite support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_support)**, and with the **[convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) product** defined as follows: $\displaystyle (f*g)(z)= \sum \{(fx)(gy)\mid z=x\circ y \}$ .[^o7]

Only this second construction is natural for the topological case, when one needs to replace '[function](wikipedia://en/185427)' by '[continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) with [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support)', and in this case $R\cong \mathbb{C}$.

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is a Lie algebroid over the one point [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470).
- The Lie algebroid associated to a [Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923).

:::
