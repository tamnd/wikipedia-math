# Double groupoid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double groupoid"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in [higher-dimensional algebra](wikipedia://en/19515158) and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), a **double groupoid** generalises the notion of [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) and of [category](wikipedia://en/53759) to a higher dimension.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **double groupoid** $D$ is a higher-dimensional groupoid involving a relationship for both 'horizontal' and 'vertical' groupoid structures.[^o4] (A double groupoid can also be considered as a generalization of certain higher-dimensional groups.[^o5]) The geometry of squares and their [compositions](wikipedia://en/195947) leads to a common representation of a *double groupoid* in the following [diagram](wikipedia://en/7651625):

*Image: Doublegroupoid.svg.* Double groupoid diagram

where $M$ is a set of 'points', $H$ and $V$ are, respectively, 'horizontal' and 'vertical' groupoids, and $S$ is a set of 'squares' with two compositions. The [composition laws](wikipedia://en/195947) for a double groupoid $D$ make it also describable as a groupoid internal to the [category of groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543).

Given two groupoids $H$ and $V$ over a set $M$, there is a double groupoid $\Box(H,V)$ with $H$,$V$ as horizontal and vertical edge groupoids, and squares given by quadruples

$$\begin{pmatrix} & h& \\[-0.9ex] v & & v'\\[-0.9ex]& h'& \end{pmatrix}$$

where $h$, $h'$ are in $H$ and $v$, $v'$ are in $V$, and the initial and final points of these edges match in $M$ as suggested by the notation; that is for example $sh = sv$, $th = sv\boldsymbol{}$, ..., etc. The compositions are to be inherited from those of $H$,$V$; that is:

$$\begin{pmatrix} & h& \\[-0.9ex] v & & v'\\[-0.9ex]& h'& \end{pmatrix} \circ_1 \begin{pmatrix} & h'& \\[-0.9ex] w & & w'\\[-0.9ex]& h''& \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} & h& \\[-0.9ex] vw & & v'w'\\[-0.9ex]& h''& \end{pmatrix}$$

and

$$\begin{pmatrix} & h& \\[-0.9ex] v & & v'\\[-0.9ex]& h'& \end{pmatrix} \circ_2 \begin{pmatrix} & k& \\[-0.9ex] v' & & v''\\[-0.9ex]& k'& \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} & hk& \\[-0.9ex] v & & v''\\[-0.9ex]& h'k'& \end{pmatrix}$$

This construction is the right adjoint to the [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) which takes the double groupoid as above, to the pair of groupoids $H$,$V$ over $M$.

Other related constructions are that of a double groupoid with connection[^o6] and homotopy double groupoids.[^o7] The homotopy double groupoid of a pair of pointed spaces is a key element of the proof of a two-dimensional Seifert-van Kampen Theorem, first proved by Brown and Higgins in 1978,[^o8] and given an extensive treatment in the book.[^o9]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

An easy class of examples can be cooked up by considering [crossed modules](wikipedia://en/1977281), or equivalently the data of a morphism of groups

> $$[G_1 \xrightarrow{\phi} G_0]$$

which has an equivalent description as the groupoid internal to the category of groups

> $$s,t:G_0\times G_1 \to G_0$$

where

> $$\begin{matrix} s(g_0,g_1) = g_0 & t(g_0,g_1) = \phi(g_1)g_0 \end{matrix}$$

are the structure morphisms for this groupoid. Since groups embed in the category of groupoids sending a group $G$ to the category $\textbf{B}G$ with a single object and morphisms giving the group $G$, the structure above gives a double groupoid. Let's give an explicit example: from the [group extension](wikipedia://en/345807)

> $$1 \to \mathbb{Z}_4 \to Q_8 \to \mathbb{Z}_2 \to 1$$

and the embedding of $\mathbb{Z}_2 \to \mathbb{Z}_4$, there is an associated double groupoid from the two term complex of groups

> $$Q_8 \to \mathbb{Z}_4$$

with kernel is $\mathbb{Z}_4$ and [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) is given by $\mathbb{Z}_2$. This gives an associated [homotopy type](wikipedia://en/21368075) $X$[^o12] with

> $\pi_1(X) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ and $\pi_2(X) = \mathbb{Z}_4$

Its [postnikov invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postnikov_tower) can be determined by the class of $Q_8 \to \mathbb{Z}_4$ in the [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) group $H^3(\mathbb{Z}_2,\mathbb{Z}_4) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. Because this is not a trivial crossed-module, it's postnikov invariant is $1$, giving a homotopy type which is not equivalent to the [geometric realization](wikipedia://en/39431504) of a [simplicial abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_abelian_group).

:::

## Homotopy double groupoid {#Homotopy_double_groupoid}

A generalisation to dimension 2 of the fundamental groupoid on a set of base points was given by Brown and Higgins in 1978 as follows. Let $(X,A,C)$ be a triple of spaces, i.e. $C \subseteq A \subseteq X$. Define $\rho(X,A,C)$ to be the set of homotopy classes rel vertices of maps of a square into **X** which take the edges into **A** and the vertices into **C**. It is not entirely trivial to prove that the natural compositions of such squares in two directions are inherited by these homotopy classes to give a double groupoid, which also has an extra structure of so-called connections necessary to discuss the idea of commutative cube in a double groupoid. This double groupoid is used in an essential way to prove a two-dimensional Seifert-van Kampen theorem, which gives new information and computations on second relative homotopy groups as part of a crossed module. For more information, see Part I of the [book](http://pages.bangor.ac.uk/~mas010/nonab-a-t.html) by Brown, Higgins, Sivera listed below.

## Double groupoid category {#Double_groupoid_category}

The category whose objects are double groupoids and whose morphisms are double groupoid [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) that are double groupoid diagram (**D**) [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) is called the **double groupoid category**, or the **category of double groupoids**.
