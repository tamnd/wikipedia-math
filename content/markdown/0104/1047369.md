# Jean Frédéric Frenet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Frédéric Frenet"}

**Jean Frédéric Frenet** (; 7 February 1816 – 12 June 1900) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), and [meteorologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorologist). He was born and died in [Périgueux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Périgueux), France.

:::

He is best known for being an independent co-discoverer of the [Frenet–Serret formulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frenet&ndash;Serret_equations). He wrote six out of the nine formulas, which at that time were not expressed in [vector notation](wikipedia://en/5959843). These formulas are important in the theory of [space curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve) ([differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625)), and they were presented in his doctoral thesis at [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) in 1847. That year he became a professor at Toulouse, and one year later, 1848, he became professor of mathematics at [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon). He also was director of an astronomical observatory at Lyon. Four years later, in 1852, he published the Frenet formulas in the *[Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_de_Mathématiques_Pures_et_Appliquées)*.

In 1856 his [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) primer was first published, which ran through seven editions, the last one published posthumously in 1917.
