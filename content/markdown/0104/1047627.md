# Complete variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complete variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **complete algebraic variety** is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) $X$, such that for any variety $Y$ the [projection](wikipedia://en/237868) [morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_map_%28algebraic_geometry%29)

:::

$$X \times Y \to Y$$

is a [closed map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_map) (i.e. maps [closed sets](wikipedia://en/47279) onto closed sets). This can be seen as an analogue of [compactness](wikipedia://en/6042) in algebraic geometry: a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is compact if and only if the above projection map is closed with respect to topological products.

The image of a complete variety is closed and is a complete variety. A closed [subvariety](wikipedia://en/248808#Subvariety) of a complete variety is complete.

A complex variety is complete if and only if it is compact as a [complex-analytic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex-analytic_variety).[^n1]

The most common example of a complete variety is a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469), but there do exist [complete non-projective varieties](wikipedia://en/39648291) in [dimensions](wikipedia://en/253251) 2 and higher. While any complete nonsingular surface is projective,[^o3] there exist nonsingular complete varieties in dimension 3 and higher which are not projective.[^o4] The first examples of non-projective complete varieties were given by [Masayoshi Nagata](wikipedia://en/3086117)[^o4] and [Heisuke Hironaka](wikipedia://en/13822).[^o5] An [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) of positive dimension is not complete.

The morphism taking a complete variety to a point is a [proper morphism](wikipedia://en/603782), in the sense of [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory). An intuitive justification of "complete", in the sense of "no missing points", can be given on the basis of the [valuative criterion of properness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuative_criterion_of_properness), which goes back to [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040).
