# 1,000,000

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="1,000,000"}

**1,000,000** (**one million**), or one [thousand](wikipedia://en/442472) thousand, is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [999,999](wikipedia://en/1462080#900,000_to_999,999) and preceding 1,000,001. The word is derived from the early Italian *millione* (*milione* in modern Italian), from *mille*, "thousand", plus the [augmentative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentative) suffix *\-one*.[^o3]

:::

It is commonly abbreviated:

- in British English as **m**[^o4][^o5][^o6] (not to be confused with the [metric prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_prefix) "m" *[milli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milli-)*, for × 10<sup>\-3</sup>, or with [metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre)),
- **M**,[^o7][^o8]
- **MM** ("thousand thousands", from Latin "Mille"; not to be confused with the [Roman numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral) MM = 2,000),
- **mm** (not to be confused with [millimetre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millimetre)), or
- **mn**, **mln**, or **mio** can be found in financial contexts.[^o9][^o10]

In [scientific notation](wikipedia://en/59715), it is written as 1 × 10<sup>6</sup> or 10<sup>6</sup>.[^o11] [Physical quantities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_quantity) can also be expressed using the [SI prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_prefix) [mega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega-) (M), when dealing with [SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) units; for example, 1 [megawatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatt) (1 MW) equals 1,000,000 [watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt).

The meaning of the word "million" is common to the [short scale and long scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_and_short_scales) numbering systems, unlike the larger numbers, which have different names in the two systems.

The million is sometimes used in the English language as a [metaphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphor) for a very large number, as in "Not in a million years" and "You're one in a million", or a [hyperbole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbole), as in "I've walked a million miles" and "You've asked a million-dollar question".

1,000,000 is also the [square](wikipedia://en/659942) of [1000](wikipedia://en/442472) and the [cube](wikipedia://en/451445) of [100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_%28number%29).

## Visualising one million {#Visualising_one_million}

*Image: Visualisation 1 million.svg.* Visualisation of powers of ten from 1 to 1 million

Even though it is often stressed that counting to precisely a million would be an exceedingly tedious task due to the time and concentration required, there are many ways to bring the number "down to size" in approximate quantities, ignoring irregularities or packing effects.

- Information: Not counting spaces, the text printed on 136 pages of an [Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica), or 600 pages of [pulp paperback fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_magazine) contains approximately one million characters.
- Length: There are one million [millimetres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millimetre) in a [kilometre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometre), and roughly a million sixteenths of an [inch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch) in a [mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile) (1 sixteenth = 0.0625). A typical [car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car) [tire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tire) might rotate a million times in a 1200 mi trip, while the engine would do several times that number of [revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_per_minute).
- Fingers: If the [width of a human finger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_%28unit%29) is 22 mm, then a million fingers lined up would cover a distance of 22 km. If a person walks at a speed of 4 km/h, it would take them approximately five and a half hours to reach the end of the fingers.
- Area: A square a thousand objects or units on a side contains a million such objects or square units, so a million holes might be found in less than three square yards of window screen, or similarly, in about one half square foot (400–500 cm<sup>2</sup>) of bed sheet cloth. A city lot 70 by 100 feet is about a million square inches.
- Volume: The cube root of one million is one hundred, so a million objects or cubic units is contained in a cube a hundred objects or linear units on a side. A million grains of [table salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_salt) or granulated [sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar) occupies about 64 mL, the volume of a cube one hundred grains on a side. One million cubic inches would be the volume of a small room $8\tfrac{1}{3}$ feet long by $8\tfrac{1}{3}$ feet wide by $8\tfrac{1}{3}$ feet high.
- Mass: A million cubic millimetres (small droplets) of water would have a volume of one [litre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litre) and a mass of one [kilogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogram). A million millilitres or [cubic centimetres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_centimetre) (one [cubic metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_metre)) of water has a mass of a million [grams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram) or one [tonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne).
- Weight: A million 80 mg [honey bees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey_bee) would weigh the same as an 80 kg person.
- Landscape: A [pyramidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid) hill 600 ft wide at the base and 100 ft high would weigh about a million short tons.
- Computer: A [display resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_resolution) of 1,280 by 800 [pixels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel) contains 1,024,000 pixels.
- Money: A [U.S. dollar bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Note) of any denomination has a mass of 1 g. One million dollar bills have a mass of 1 Mg or 1 tonne (just over 1 [short ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_ton)).
- Time: A million [seconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second), 1 megasecond, is 11.57 [days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day).

In [Indian English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_English) and [Pakistani English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_English), it is also expressed as 10 [lakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakh). Lakh is derived from sa for 100,000 in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit).

### Prime numbers {#Prime_numbers}

There are 78,498 primes less than 10<sup>6</sup>, where 999,983 is the largest prime number smaller than 1,000,000.

Increments of 10<sup>6</sup> from 1 million through a 10 million have the following prime counts:

- **70,435** primes between 1,000,000 and 2,000,000.
- **67,883** primes between 2,000,000 and 3,000,000.
- **66,330** primes between 3,000,000 and 4,000,000.
- **65,367** primes between 4,000,000 and 5,000,000.
- **64,336** primes between 5,000,000 and 6,000,000.
- **63,799** primes between 6,000,000 and 7,000,000.
- **63,129** primes between 7,000,000 and 8,000,000.
- **62,712** primes between 8,000,000 and 9,000,000.
