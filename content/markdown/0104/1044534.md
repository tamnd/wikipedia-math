# Michel Loève

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Loève"}

**Michel Loève** (מישל לוב; January 22, 1907 – February 17, 1979) was a French-American [probabilist](wikipedia://en/23542) and [mathematical statistician](wikipedia://en/888711), of Jewish origin.[^o3] He is known in mathematical statistics and probability theory for the [Karhunen–Loève theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karhunen–Loève_theorem) and [Karhunen–Loève transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karhunen–Loève_transform).

:::

Michel Loève was born in [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa) (then part of the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire)) in 1907, to a Jewish family. He passed most of his childhood years in Egypt and received his primary and secondary education there in French schools. Later, after achieving the grades of B.L. in 1931 and A.B. in 1936, he studied mathematics at the [Université de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Paris) under [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462), and received his *[Doctorat ès Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) (Mathématiques)* in 1941. In 1936 was employed as actuaire of the [University of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lyon).

Because of his Jewish origin, he was arrested during the [German occupation of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_France_during_World_War_II) and sent to [Drancy internment camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drancy_internment_camp). One of his books[^o4] is dedicated "To Line and To the students and teachers of the School in the Camp de Drancy". Having survived the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust), after the liberation he became between 1944 and 1946 chief of research at the [Institut Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/10513595) at Paris University, then until 1948 worked at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London).

After one term as a visiting professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) he accepted the position of professor of mathematics at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), in 1955 adding the title professor of statistics.

He is the author of one of the earliest books on measure-theoretic probability theory and one of the best known textbooks.[^o5] He is memorialized via the [Loève Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loève_Prize) created by his widow Line.[^o6]
