# Jonathan Keating

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jonathan Keating"}

**Jonathan Peter Keating** (born 20 September 1963) is a British mathematician. As of September 2019, he is the [Sedleian Professor of Natural Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedleian_Professor_of_Natural_Philosophy) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and from 2012 to 2019 was the Henry Overton Wills Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), where he served as Dean of the Faculty of Science (2009–2013). He has made contributions to [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), in particular to [quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779), [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Education {#Education}

He read for an MA in physics at [New College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford), before obtaining his PhD in 1989 at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) supervised by [Michael Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Berry_%28physicist%29).[^o8][^o4]

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

He lectured in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) from 1991 to 1995 before moving to the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), as a reader in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) (1995–1997) and then as a professor in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) (1997–2012). He served as head of the Mathematics department (2001–2004) and was appointed to the Henry Overton Wills Chair in Mathematics at Bristol in 2012. His research has focused on [quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779), [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory) and its connection to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), especially the theory of the [Riemann zeta-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_zeta-function) and other [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283). He is known for his work on the resummation of semiclassical periodic orbit formulae, the statistics of quantum energy levels, quantum maps, [quantum graphs](wikipedia://en/11264285), the statistics of the [zeros](wikipedia://en/264210) of the [Riemann zeta-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_zeta-function) and other [L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-functions), and the moments of the Riemann zeta-function and other L-functions. He has served on the editorial boards of several [peer reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review) [scientific journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_journal) including:

- Member (1996–2004) of the editorial board of *Journal of Physics A*.
- Member (1997–2004) of the editorial board of *[Nonlinearity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinearity_%28journal%29)*.
- Joint Editor-in-Chief (2004–2012) of *Nonlinearity*.
- Member (2003–2017) of the editorial board of *Applied Mathematics Research Express*.
- Member (2007–present) of the editorial board of *[Journal of Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Mathematical_Physics)*.

He was the Chair of the [Heilbronn Institute for Mathematical Research](wikipedia://en/61922775) until July 2020.

In September 2019, he succeeded [John M. Ball](wikipedia://en/3578468) as [Sedleian Professor of Natural Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedleian_Professor_of_Natural_Philosophy) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o6]

In November 2019 he succeeded [Caroline Series](wikipedia://en/43764434) as president of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o7]

His doctoral students include [Nina Snaith](wikipedia://en/9277265).[^o9]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Between 2004 and 2009 Keating was supported by an EPSRC Senior Research Fellowship.

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 2009.[^o11]

In 2010, he was awarded the London Mathematical Society's [Fröhlich Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fröhlich_Prize).[^o12]

In 2014, he was awarded a [Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Wolfson_Research_Merit_Award).[^o13]

He holds an ERC Advanced Grant.[^o14]
