# John Bryce McLeod

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Bryce McLeod"}

**John Bryce McLeod**, [^o3] (23 December 1929 – 20 August 2014[^o4]) was a British mathematician, who worked on linear and nonlinear partial and ordinary differential equations.

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

McLeod was born in Aberdeen, Scotland, on 23 December 1929.[^o4] He was educated at [Aberdeen Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Grammar_School); the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen), where he took a first in Mathematics and Natural Philosophy in 1950; and [Christ Church, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford), where he took a first in Mathematics in 1952. He was a Harmsworth Senior Scholar at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford), from 1955 to 1956.[^o6] He obtained his PhD in 1959 under the supervision of [Edward Charles Titchmarsh](wikipedia://en/1709459) at the University of Oxford.

He was a junior lecturer in Mathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) from 1956 to 1958, and a lecturer in mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) from 1958 to 1960. He then returned to Oxford to take up a Fellowship in Pure Mathematics at [Wadham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford).[^o6] He remained in Oxford until 1988, becoming a university lecturer in 1970, and a senior research fellow of the [Science and Engineering Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_Engineering_Research_Council) from 1986 to 1991.[^o8] In 1988 McLeod took up a professorship at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), where he remained until his retirement in 2007.[^o9]

McLeod married Eunice Third in 1956; they had three sons and a daughter.[^o8] He died in England on 20 August 2014, aged 84.[^o9]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1965, he was awarded the [Sir Edmund Whittaker Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/4579429). he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1974, and received the Society's [Keith Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Medal) in 1987.[^o8] He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1992).[^o3]

In 2011 he was awarded the [Naylor Prize and Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naylor_Prize_and_Lectureship).[^o11]
