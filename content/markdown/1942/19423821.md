# Hadjicostas's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hadjicostas's formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Hadjicostas's formula** is a formula relating a certain [double integral](wikipedia://en/2757224) to values of the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). It is named after Petros Hadjicostas.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *s* be a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) with *s* ≠ -1 and Re(*s*) > −2. Then

$$\int_0^1\int_0^1 \frac{1-x}{1-xy}(-\log(xy))^s\,dx\,dy=\Gamma(s+2)\left(\zeta(s+2)-\frac{1}{s+1}\right).$$

Here Γ is the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and ζ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

:::

## Background {#Background}

The first instance of the formula was proved and used by [Frits Beukers](wikipedia://en/36707078) in his 1978 paper giving an alternative proof of [Apéry's theorem](wikipedia://en/1711336).[^o5] He proved the formula when *s* = 0, and proved an equivalent formulation for the case *s* = 1. This led Petros Hadjicostas to conjecture the above formula in 2004,[^o6] and within a week it had been proven by Robin Chapman.[^o7] He proved the formula holds when Re(*s*) > −1, and then extended the result by [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) to get the full result.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

As well as the two cases used by Beukers to get alternate expressions for ζ(2) and ζ(3), the formula can be used to express the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant) as a double integral by letting *s* tend to −1:

$$\gamma=\int_0^1\int_0^1\frac{1-x}{(1-xy)(-\log(xy))}\,dx\,dy.$$

The latter formula was first discovered by Jonathan Sondow[^o10] and is the one referred to in the title of Hadjicostas's paper.

:::
