# Pinch point (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pinch point"}

*Image: Whitney\_unbrella.png.* Section of the [Whitney umbrella](wikipedia://en/1964554), an example of pinch point singularity.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **pinch point** or **cuspidal point** is a type of [singular point](wikipedia://en/981915) on an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311).

:::

The equation for the surface near a pinch point may be put in the form

$$f(u,v,w) = u^2 - vw^2 + [4] \,$$

where \[4\] denotes [terms](wikipedia://en/24885593) of [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_a_monomial) 4 or more and $v$ is not a square in the ring of functions.

For example the surface $1-2x+x^2-yz^2=0$ near the point $(1,0,0)$, meaning in coordinates vanishing at that point, has the form above. In fact, if $u=1-x, v=y$ and $w=z$ then {$u, v, w$} is a system of coordinates vanishing at $(1,0,0)$ then $1-2x+x^2-yz^2=(1-x)^2-yz^2=u^2-vw^2$ is written in the canonical form.

The simplest example of a pinch point is the hypersurface defined by the equation $u^2-vw^2=0$ called [Whitney umbrella](wikipedia://en/1964554).

The pinch point (in this case the origin) is a limit of [normal crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_crossing_divisor) singular points (the $v$-axis in this case). These singular points are intimately related in the sense that in order to [resolve](wikipedia://en/9914115) the pinch point singularity one must [blow-up](wikipedia://en/1817790) the whole $v$-axis and not only the pinch point.
