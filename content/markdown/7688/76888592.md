# Sergei Treil

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sergei Treil"}

**Sergei Raimondovich Treil** (or **Treil'**; Сергей Раймондович Треиль) is a Russian mathematician, specializing in analysis.

:::

At [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) (now named Saint Petersburg State University), Sergei R. Treil graduated in mathematics in 1982 with an M.Sc. and in 1985 with a Ph.D.[^o2] His Ph.D. thesis, dealing with geometric aspects of [Hankel operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankel_operator) and [Toeplitz operators](wikipedia://en/14598730), was supervised by [Nikolai Kapitonovich Nikolski](wikipedia://en/56802364). From 1986 to 1989 Treil was an assistant professor at the [Baikonur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur) branch of the [Moscow Aviation Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Aviation_Institute) (MAI). From 1989 to 1991 he was a researcher at the laboratory of theoretical cybernetics at Leningrad University. In [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University)'s department of mathematics, he was from 1991 to 1992 a visiting assistant professor, from 1992 to 1994 an assistant professor, from 1994 to 1998 he was an associate professor, and from 1998 to 1999 a full professor. In [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University)'s department of mathematics, he was from 2000 to 2001 an associate professor and was appointed in 2001 a full professor.[^o2] He was a [visiting scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) in 1990 at the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452), where he worked with Alexandre Megretski of the [KTH Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTH_Royal_Institute_of_Technology).[^o4] Treil was in autumn 1998 a visiting professor at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)'s department of electrical engineering and computer science.[^o2]

Treil has done important research in the intersection of [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). Much of his research is on problems related to [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), such as [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), [stationary random processes](wikipedia://en/329898), [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), and [wavelets](wikipedia://en/50903). His research interest include Hankel operators, Toeplitz operators, [functional models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_model) of operators, [spectral decompositions of operators](wikipedia://en/123495), spectral theory of matrix- and operator-valued functions, the [Corona problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_problem), and the interplay between operator theory and [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101).[^o5]

In 1993 Treil was awarded the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize).[^o2] He wrote several papers with [Alexander Volberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Volberg) and [Fedor Nazarov](wikipedia://en/21079027), who were his colleagues at Michigan State University. Volberg shared the 1988 Salem Prize with [Jean-Christophe Yoccoz](wikipedia://en/589307). Nazarov won the 1999 Salem Prize.
