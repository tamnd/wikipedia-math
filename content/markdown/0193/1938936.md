# Sum-free sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sum-free sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **sum-free sequence** is an increasing [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563),

:::

$$a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots,$$

such that no term $a_n$ can be represented as a sum of any [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the preceding elements of the sequence.

This differs from a [sum-free set](wikipedia://en/11120026), where only pairs of sums must be avoided, but where those sums may come from the whole set rather than just the preceding terms.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The [powers of two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_two),

1, 2, 4, 8, 16, ...

form a sum-free sequence: each term in the sequence is one more than the sum of all preceding terms, and so cannot be represented as a sum of preceding terms.

:::

## Sums of reciprocals {#Sums_of_reciprocals}

A set of integers is said to be [small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_set_%28combinatorics%29) if the sum of its [reciprocals](wikipedia://en/229940) [converges](wikipedia://en/1743431) to a finite value. For instance, by the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692), the [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) are not small. (txt) [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that every sum-free sequence is small, and asked how large the sum of reciprocals could be. For instance, the sum of the reciprocals of the powers of two (a [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)) is two.[^n1]

If $R$ denotes the [supremum](wikipedia://en/39382) of all sums of reciprocals of a sum-free sequence, then through subsequent research it is known that $2.0654 < R < 2.8570$.[^o7]

## Density {#Density}

It follows from the fact that sum-free sequences are small that they have zero [Schnirelmann density](wikipedia://en/343156); that is, if $A(x)$ is defined to be the number of sequence elements that are less than or equal to $x$, then $A(x)=o(x)$. (Erdős 1962) showed that for every sum-free sequence there exists an unbounded sequence of numbers $x_i$ for which $A(x_i)=O(x^{\varphi-1})$ where $\varphi$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386), and he exhibited a sum-free sequence for which, for all values of $x$, $A(x)=\Omega(x^{2/7})$,[^n1] subsequently improved to $A(x)=\Omega(x^{1/3})$ by Deshouillers, Erdős and Melfi in 1999[^n2] and to $A(x)=\Omega(x^{1/2-\varepsilon})$ by Luczak and Schoen in 2000, who also proved that the exponent 1/2 cannot be further improved.[^n3]
