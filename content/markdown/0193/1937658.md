# Time reversibility

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Time reversibility"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), **time-reversibility** is the [property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_%28mathematics%29) of a process whose governing rules remain unchanged when the direction of its sequence of actions is reversed.

:::

A [deterministic process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_process) is time-reversible if the time-reversed process satisfies the same [dynamic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_equation_%28disambiguation%29) as the original process; in other words, the equations are [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) or [symmetrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetrical) under a change in the [sign](wikipedia://en/7951270) of time. A [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) is reversible if the statistical properties of the process are the same as the statistical properties for time-reversed data from the same process.

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) is time-reversible if the forward evolution is [one-to-one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_function), so that for every state there exists a transformation (an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450)) π which gives a one-to-one mapping between the time-reversed evolution of any one state and the forward-time evolution of another corresponding state, given by the operator equation:

$$U_{-t} = \pi \, U_{t}\, \pi$$

Any time-independent structures (e.g. [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) or [attractors](wikipedia://en/191094)) which the dynamics give rise to must therefore either be self-symmetrical or have symmetrical images under the involution π.

## Physics {#Physics}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the [laws of motion](wikipedia://en/55212) of [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) exhibit time reversibility, as long as the operator π reverses the [conjugate momenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_momentum) of all the particles of the system, i.e. $\mathbf{p} \rightarrow \mathbf{-p}$ ([T-symmetry](wikipedia://en/151013)).

In [quantum mechanical](wikipedia://en/25202) systems, however, the [weak nuclear force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_nuclear_force) is not invariant under T-symmetry alone; if weak interactions are present, reversible dynamics are still possible, but only if the operator π also reverses the signs of all the [charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_%28physics%29) and the [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_%28physics%29) of the spatial co-ordinates ([C-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-symmetry) and [P-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-symmetry)). This reversibility of several linked properties is known as [CPT symmetry](wikipedia://en/151040).

[Thermodynamic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_process) can be [reversible](wikipedia://en/416681) or [irreversible](wikipedia://en/636094), depending on the change in [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) during the process. Note, however, that the fundamental laws that underlie the thermodynamic processes are all time-reversible (classical laws of motion and laws of electrodynamics),[^o4] which means that on the microscopic level, if one were to keep track of all the particles and all the degrees of freedom, the many-body system processes are all reversible; However, such analysis is beyond the capability of any human being (or [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence)), and the [macroscopic properties](wikipedia://en/2747182) (like entropy and temperature) of many-body system are only *[defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_variable)* from the [statistics of the ensembles](wikipedia://en/265823). When we talk about such macroscopic properties in thermodynamics, in certain cases, we can see irreversibility in the [time evolution](wikipedia://en/937739) of these quantities on a statistical level. Indeed, the [second law of thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/133017) predicates that the entropy of the entire universe must not decrease, not because the probability of that is zero, but because it is so unlikely that it is a *[statistical impossibility](wikipedia://en/3772909)* for all practical considerations (see [Crooks fluctuation theorem](wikipedia://en/5920062)).

## Stochastic processes {#Stochastic_processes}

A [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) is time-reversible if the joint probabilities of the forward and reverse state sequences are the same for all sets of time increments { *τ*<sub>*s*</sub> }, for *s* = 1, ..., *k* for any *k*:[^o6]

$$p(x_t, x_{t+\tau_1}, x_{t+\tau_2}, \ldots , x_{t+\tau_k}) = p(x_{t'}, x_{t'-\tau_1}, x_{t'-\tau_2} , \ldots , x_{t'-\tau_k}).$$

A [univariate](wikipedia://en/2398982) stationary [Gaussian process](wikipedia://en/302944) is time-reversible. [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process) are reversible if and only if their stationary distributions have the property of [detailed balance](wikipedia://en/2212867):

$$p(x_t=i,x_{t+1}=j) = \,p(x_t=j,x_{t+1}=i).$$

[Kolmogorov's criterion](wikipedia://en/18971898) defines the condition for a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) or [continuous-time Markov chain](wikipedia://en/1125342) to be time-reversible.

Time reversal of numerous classes of stochastic processes has been studied, including [Lévy processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lévy_processes),[^o7] [stochastic networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_network) ([Kelly's lemma](wikipedia://en/38477955)),[^o8] [birth and death processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_and_death_processes),[^o9] [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876),[^o10] and [piecewise deterministic Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise_deterministic_Markov_processes).[^o11]

## Waves and optics {#Waves_and_optics}

Time reversal method works based on the linear reciprocity of the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691), which states that the time reversed solution of a [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) is also a solution to the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) since standard wave equations only contain even derivatives of the unknown variables.[^o13] Thus, the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) is symmetrical under time reversal, so the time reversal of any valid solution is also a solution. This means that a wave's path through space is valid when travelled in either direction.

[Time reversal signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_reversal_signal_processing)[^o14] is a process in which this property is used to reverse a received signal; this signal is then re-emitted and a temporal compression occurs, resulting in a reversal of the initial excitation waveform being played at the initial source.
