# Simons' formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simons' formula"}

In the mathematical field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Simons formula** (also known as the **Simons identity**, and in some variants as the **Simons inequality**) is a fundamental equation in the study of [minimal submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_submanifold). It was discovered by [James Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844) in 1968. It can be viewed as a formula for the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) of the [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078) of a [Riemannian submanifold](wikipedia://en/1086619). It is often quoted and used in the less precise form of a formula or inequality for the Laplacian of the length of the second fundamental form.

:::

In the case of a hypersurface $M$ of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), the formula asserts that

$$\Delta h=\operatorname{Hess}H+Hh^2-|h|^2h,$$

where, relative to a local choice of unit normal [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641), $h$ is the [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078), $H$ is the [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210), and $h^{2}$ is the symmetric 2-tensor on $M$ given by $h^{2}_{ij} = g^{pq}h_{ip}h_{qj}$. This has the consequence that

$$\frac{1}{2}\Delta|h|^2=|\nabla h|^2-|h|^4+\langle h,\operatorname{Hess}H\rangle+H\operatorname{tr}(A^3)$$

where $A$ is the [shape operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_operator). In this setting, the derivation is particularly simple:

$$\begin{aligned} \Delta h_{ij}&=\nabla^p\nabla_p h_{ij}\\ &=\nabla^p\nabla_ih_{jp}\\ &=\nabla_i\nabla^p h_{jp}-{{R^p}_{ij}}^qh_{qp}-{{R^p}_{ip}}^qh_{jq}\\ &=\nabla_i\nabla_jH-(h^{pq}h_{ij}-h_j^ph_i^q)h_{qp}-(h^{pq}h_{ip}-Hh_i^q)h_{jq}\\ &=\nabla_i\nabla_jH-|h|^2h+Hh^2; \end{aligned}$$

the only tools involved are the [Codazzi equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Codazzi_equations) (equalities #2 and 4), the [Gauss equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Codazzi_equations) (equality #4), and the commutation identity for covariant differentiation (equality #3). The more general case of a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) in a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) requires additional terms to do with the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526). In the even more general setting of arbitrary codimension, the formula involves a complicated polynomial in the second fundamental form.
