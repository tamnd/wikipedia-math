# Function and Concept

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Function and Concept"}

"**Function and Concept**" (German: "Funktion und Begriff") is a [lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecture) delivered by [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416) in [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena) at a meeting of the Jenaische Gesellschaft für Medicin und Naturwissenschaft on 9 January 1891, and published the same year as an article.[^o2] The lecture involves a clarification of his earlier distinction between concepts and objects. It

:::

The original article was reprinted in 1962.[^o3]

## Overview {#Overview}

In general, a concept is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) whose value is always a [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) (139). A relation is a two place function whose value is always a truth value (146).

Frege draws an important distinction between concepts on the basis of their level. Frege tells us that a first-level concept is a one-place function that correlates objects with [truth-values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth-value) (147). First level concepts have the value of true or false depending on whether the object falls under the concept. So, the concept $F$ has the value the True with the argument the object named by 'Jamie' if and only if Jamie falls under the concept $F$ (or is in the extension of F).

Second order concepts correlate concepts and relations with truth values. So, if we take the relation of identity to be the argument $f$, the concept expressed by the sentence:

$$\forall x \forall y f(x, y) \rightarrow \forall z (f (x, z) \rightarrow y=z)$$

correlates the relation of identity with the True.

The [conceptual range](wikipedia://en/275991) (*Begriffsumfang* in Frege 1891, p. 16) follows the truth value of the function:

$x^2 = 1$ and $(x + 1)^2 = 2(x + 1)$ have the same conceptual range.
