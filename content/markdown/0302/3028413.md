# Hasse invariant of a quadratic form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hasse invariant of a quadratic form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hasse invariant** (or **Hasse–Witt invariant**) of a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) *Q* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* takes values in the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) Br(*K*). The name "Hasse–Witt" comes from [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) and [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911).

:::

The quadratic form *Q* may be taken as a [diagonal form](wikipedia://en/3028504)

Σ *a*<sub>*i*</sub>*x*<sub>*i*</sub><sup>2</sup>.

Its invariant is then defined as the product of the classes in the Brauer group of all the [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890)

(*a*<sub>*i*</sub>, *a*<sub>*j*</sub>) for *i* \< *j*.

This is independent of the diagonal form chosen to compute it.[^o2]

It may also be viewed as the second [Stiefel–Whitney class](wikipedia://en/877761) of *Q*.

## Symbols {#Symbols}

The invariant may be computed for a specific [symbol](wikipedia://en/35903941) φ taking values in the group C<sub>2</sub> = {±1}.[^o4]

In the context of quadratic forms over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375), the Hasse invariant may be defined using the [Hilbert symbol](wikipedia://en/5924217), the unique symbol taking values in C<sub>2</sub>.[^o5] The invariants of a quadratic forms over a local field are precisely the dimension, [discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discriminant_of_a_quadratic_form) and Hasse invariant.[^o6]

For quadratic forms over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), there is a Hasse invariant ±1 for every [finite place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_place). The invariants of a form over a number field are precisely the dimension, discriminant, all local Hasse invariants and the [signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_of_a_quadratic_form) coming from real embeddings.[^o7]
