# Hasse–Witt matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hasse–Witt matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hasse–Witt matrix** *H* of a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) *C* over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) *F* is the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) of the [Frobenius mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_mapping) (*p*-th power mapping where *F* has *q* elements, *q* a power of the [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*) with respect to a basis for the [differentials of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentials_of_the_first_kind). It is a *g* × *g* matrix where *C* has [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g*. The rank of the Hasse–Witt matrix is the **Hasse** or **Hasse–Witt invariant**.

:::

## Approach to the definition {#Approach_to_the_definition}

This definition, as given in the introduction, is natural in classical terms, and is due to [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) and [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911) (1936). It provides a solution to the question of the *p*-rank of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) *J* of *C*; the *p*-rank is bounded by the [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_of_a_matrix) of *H*, specifically it is the rank of the Frobenius mapping composed with itself *g* times. It is also a definition that is in principle algorithmic. There has been substantial recent interest in this as of practical application to [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), in the case of *C* a [hyperelliptic curve](wikipedia://en/499361). The curve *C* is **superspecial** if *H* = 0.

That definition needs a couple of caveats, at least. Firstly, there is a convention about Frobenius mappings, and under the modern understanding what is required for *H* is the *transpose* of Frobenius (see [arithmetic and geometric Frobenius](wikipedia://en/6988121) for more discussion). Secondly, the Frobenius mapping is not *F*-linear; it is linear over the [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field) **Z**/*p***Z** in *F*. Therefore the matrix can be written down but does not represent a linear mapping in the straightforward sense.

## Cohomology {#Cohomology}

The interpretation for [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) is this: the *p*-power map acts on

*H*<sup>1</sup>(*C*,*O*<sub>*C*</sub>),

or in other words the first cohomology of *C* with coefficients in its [structure sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_sheaf). This is now called the **Cartier–Manin operator** (sometimes just **Cartier operator**), for [Pierre Cartier](wikipedia://en/1470549) and [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086). The connection with the Hasse–Witt definition is by means of [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175), which for a curve relates that group to

*H*<sup>0</sup>(*C*, Ω<sub>*C*</sub>)

where Ω<sub>*C*</sub> = Ω<sup>1</sup><sub>*C*</sub> is the sheaf of [Kähler differentials](wikipedia://en/603916) on *C*.

## Abelian varieties and their *p*-rank {#Abelian_varieties_and_their_p-rank}

The *p*-rank of an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) *A* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* of [characteristic p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_p) is the integer *k* for which the kernel *A*\[*p*\] of multiplication by *p* has *p*<sup>*k*</sup> points. It may take any value from 0 to *d*, the dimension of *A*; by contrast for any other prime number *l* there are *l*<sup>2*d*</sup> points in *A*\[*l*\]. The reason that the *p*-rank is lower is that multiplication by *p* on *A* is an [inseparable isogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inseparable_isogeny): the differential is *p* which is 0 in *K*. By looking at the kernel as a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) one can get the more complete structure (reference [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594) *Abelian Varieties* pp. 146–7); but if for example one looks at [reduction mod p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_mod_p) of a [division equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_equation), the number of solutions must drop.

The rank of the Cartier–Manin operator, or Hasse–Witt matrix, therefore gives an upper bound for the *p*-rank. The *p*-rank is the rank of the Frobenius operator composed with itself *g* times. In the original paper of Hasse and Witt the problem is phrased in terms intrinsic to *C*, not relying on *J*. It is there a question of classifying the possible [Artin–Schreier extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin–Schreier_extension) of the [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) *F*(*C*) (the analogue in this case of [Kummer theory](wikipedia://en/581610)).

## Case of genus 1 {#Case_of_genus_1}

The case of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) was worked out by Hasse in 1934. Since the genus is 1, the only possibilities for the matrix *H* are: *H* is zero, Hasse invariant 0, *p*-rank 0, the *[supersingular](wikipedia://en/3028216)* case; or *H* non-zero, Hasse invariant 1, *p*-rank 1, the *[ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_elliptic_curve)* case.[^o6] Here there is a congruence formula saying that *H* is congruent modulo *p* to the number *N* of points on *C* over *F*, at least when *q* = *p*. Because of [Hasse's theorem on elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/1108758), knowing *N* modulo *p* determines *N* for *p* ≥ 5. This connection with [local zeta-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_zeta-function) has been investigated in depth.

For a plane curve defined by a cubic *f*(*X*,*Y*,*Z*) = 0, the Hasse invariant is zero if and only if the coefficient of (*XYZ*)<sup>*p*−1</sup> in *f*<sup>*p*−1</sup> is zero.[^o6]
