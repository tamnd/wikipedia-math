# Direct image functor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Direct image functor"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **direct image functor** describes how structured data assigned to one space can be systematically transferred to another space using a continuous map between them. More precisely, if we have a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466)—an object that encodes data like functions or sections over open regions—defined on a space *X*, and a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) from *X* to another space *Y*, then the direct image functor produces a corresponding sheaf on *Y*. This construction is a central tool in sheaf theory and is widely used in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) to relate local data across spaces.

:::

More formally, given a sheaf *F* defined on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* and a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) *f*: *X* → *Y*, we can define a new sheaf *f*<sub>∗</sub>*F* on *Y*, called the **direct image sheaf** or the **pushforward sheaf** of *F* along *f*, such that the global sections of *f*<sub>∗</sub>*F* is given by the global sections of *F*. This assignment gives rise to a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) *f*<sub>∗</sub> from the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of sheaves on *X* to the category of sheaves on *Y*, which is known as the direct image functor. Similar constructions exist in many other algebraic and geometric contexts, including that of [quasi-coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaves) and [étale sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_sheaf) on a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *f*: *X* → *Y* be a continuous map of topological spaces, and let Sh(–) denote the category of sheaves of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) on a topological space. The direct image functor

$$f_*: \operatorname{Sh}(X) \to \operatorname{Sh}(Y)$$

sends a sheaf *F* on *X* to its direct image presheaf *f*<sub>∗</sub>*F* on *Y*, defined on open subsets *U* of *Y* by

$$f_*F(U) := F(f^{-1}(U)).$$

This turns out to be a sheaf on *Y*, and is called the direct image sheaf or pushforward sheaf of *F* along *f*.

Since a [morphism of sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_of_sheaves) φ: *F* → *G* on *X* gives rise to a morphism of sheaves *f*<sub>∗</sub>(φ): *f*<sub>∗</sub>(*F*) → *f*<sub>∗</sub>(*G*) on *Y* in an obvious way, we indeed have that *f*<sub>∗</sub> is a functor.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

If *Y* is a point, and *f*: *X* → *Y* the unique continuous map, then Sh(*Y*) is the category **Ab** of abelian groups, and the direct image functor *f*<sub>∗</sub>: Sh(*X*) → **Ab** equals the [global sections functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_sections_functor).

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variants"}

### Variants {#Variants}

If dealing with sheaves of sets instead of sheaves of abelian groups, the same definition applies. Similarly, if *f*: (*X*, *O<sub>X</sub>*) → (*Y*, *O<sub>Y</sub>*) is a morphism of [ringed spaces](wikipedia://en/248014), we obtain a direct image functor *f*<sub>∗</sub>: Sh(*X*,*O<sub>X</sub>*) → Sh(*Y*,*O<sub>Y</sub>*) from the category of sheaves of *O<sub>X</sub>*-modules to the category of sheaves of *O<sub>Y</sub>*-modules. Moreover, if *f* is now a morphism of [quasi-compact](wikipedia://en/12410777) and [quasi-separated](wikipedia://en/28281191) schemes, then *f*<sub>∗</sub> preserves the property of being quasi-coherent, so we obtain the direct image functor between categories of quasi-coherent sheaves.[^o8]

A similar definition applies to sheaves on [topoi](wikipedia://en/21391464), such as [étale sheaves](wikipedia://en/5707590). There, instead of the above preimage *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*U*), one uses the [fiber product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_product) of *U* and *X* over *Y*.

:::

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- Forming sheaf categories and direct image functors itself defines a functor from the category of topological spaces to the category of categories: given continuous maps *f*: *X* → *Y* and *g*: *Y* → *Z*, we have (*gf*)<sub>∗</sub>=*g*<sub>∗</sub>*f*<sub>∗</sub>.
- The direct image functor is [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) to the [inverse image functor](wikipedia://en/7086661), which means that for any continuous $f: X \to Y$ and sheaves $\mathcal F, \mathcal G$ respectively on *X*, *Y*, there is a natural isomorphism:

$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf {Sh}(X)}(f^{-1} \mathcal G, \mathcal F ) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf {Sh}(Y)}(\mathcal G, f_*\mathcal F).$$

- If *f* is the inclusion of a closed subspace *X* ⊆ *Y* then *f*<sub>∗</sub> is [exact](wikipedia://en/453945). Actually, in this case *f*<sub>∗</sub> is an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) between the category of sheaves on *X* and the category of sheaves on *Y* supported on *X*. This follows from the fact that the stalk of $(f_* \mathcal F)_y$ is $\mathcal F_y$ if $y \in X$ and zero otherwise (here the closedness of *X* in *Y* is used).
- If *f* is the morphism of affine schemes $\mathrm{Spec} \, S \to \mathrm{Spec} \, R$ determined by a ring homomorphism $\phi: R \to S$, then the direct image functor *f*<sub>∗</sub> on quasi-coherent sheaves identifies with the [restriction of scalars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restriction_of_scalars) functor along φ.

:::

## Higher direct images {#Higher_direct_images}

The direct image functor is [left exact](wikipedia://en/453945), but usually not right exact. Hence one can consider the right [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875) of the direct image. They are called **higher direct images** and denoted *R<sup>q</sup> f*<sub>∗</sub>.

One can show that there is a similar expression as above for higher direct images: for a sheaf *F* on *X*, the sheaf *R<sup>q</sup> f*<sub>∗</sub>(*F*) is the sheaf associated to the presheaf

$$U \mapsto H^q(f^{-1}(U), F),$$

where *H<sup>q</sup>* denotes [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357).

In the context of algebraic geometry and a morphism $f: X \to Y$ of quasi-compact and quasi-separated schemes, one likewise has the right derived functor

$$Rf_*: D_{qcoh}(X) \to D_{qcoh}(Y)$$

as a functor between the (unbounded) [derived categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_categories) of quasi-coherent sheaves. In this situation, $Rf_*$ always admits a right adjoint $f^{\times}$.[^o12] This is closely related, but not generally equivalent to, the [exceptional inverse image functor](wikipedia://en/15606646) $f^!$, unless $f$ is also [proper](wikipedia://en/603782).
