# Great stellated dodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great stellated dodecahedron"}

Reg star polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great stellated dodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great stellated dodecahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great stellated dodecahedron** is a [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedron](wikipedia://en/16814), with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{\tfrac{5}{2}, 3\}$. It is one of four [nonconvex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex) [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/505253#Non-convex_2).

:::

It is composed of 12 intersecting [pentagrammic](wikipedia://en/197544) faces, with three pentagrams meeting at each vertex.

It shares its [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644), although not its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) or [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745), with the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), as well as being a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of a (smaller) dodecahedron. It is the only dodecahedral stellation with this property, apart from the dodecahedron itself. Its dual, the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898), is related in a similar fashion to the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084).

It can be constructed by augmenting each face of a regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) with a right [triangular pyramid](wikipedia://en/30606) of height $\sqrt{\frac{7+3\sqrt5}{6}}$ times the icosahedron's edge length.[^o2]

If the pentagrammic faces are broken into triangles, it is topologically related to the [triakis icosahedron](wikipedia://en/722873), with the same face connectivity, but much taller [isosceles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosceles) triangle faces. If the triangles are instead made to invert themselves and excavate the central icosahedron, the result is a [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441).

The great stellated dodecahedron can be constructed analogously to the pentagram, its two-dimensional analogue, by attempting to stellate the $n$-dimensional [pentagonal polytope](wikipedia://en/33020517) (which has pentagonal polytope faces and simplex vertex figures) until it can no longer be stellated; that is, it is its final stellation.

## Formulas {#Formulas}

For a great stellated dodecahedron with edge length $E$ (where $E$ represents the length of any edge of the internal icosahedron),

| [Inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}$ |
|---|---|
| [Midradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{4}$ |
| [Circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{\sqrt{3}(3+\sqrt{5})}{4}$ |
| [Surface area](wikipedia://en/27725): | $\quad E^2 \times 15\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}}$ |
| [Volume](wikipedia://en/32498): | $\quad E^3 \times \frac{5(3+\sqrt{5})}{4}$ |

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

*Image: Great stellated dodecahedron truncations.gif.* Animated truncation sequence from $\{\tfrac{5}{2}, 3\}$ to $\{3, \tfrac{5}{2}\}$

A truncation process applied to the great stellated dodecahedron produces a series of uniform polyhedra. Truncating edges down to points produces the [great icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157391) as a rectified great stellated dodecahedron. The process completes as a birectification, reducing the original faces down to points, and producing the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898).

The [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) *great stellated dodecahedron* is a degenerate polyhedron, with 20 triangular faces from the truncated vertices, and 12 (hidden) pentagonal faces as truncations of the original pentagram faces, the latter forming a [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441) inscribed within and sharing the edges of the icosahedron.
