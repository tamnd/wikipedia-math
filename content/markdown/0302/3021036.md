# Hilbert class field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert class field"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), the **Hilbert class field** $E$ of a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $K$ is the [maximal abelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_abelian_extension) [unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unramified) extension of $K$. Its degree over $K$ equals the class number of $K$ and the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of $E$ over $K$ is canonically isomorphic to the [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) of $K$ using [Frobenius elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element) for [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) in $K$.

:::

In this context, the Hilbert class field of $K$ is not just unramified at the [finite places](wikipedia://en/23666#Primes_in_valuation_theory) (the classical ideal theoretic interpretation) but also at the infinite places of $K$. That is, every [real embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_embedding) of $K$ extends to a real embedding of $E$, rather than to a complex embedding of $E$.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If the ring of integers of $K$ is a [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546), in particular if $K = \mathbb{Q}$, then $K$ is its own Hilbert class field.
- Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$, of [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) $-15$. The field $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}, \sqrt{5})$ has discriminant $225=(-15)^2$ and so is an everywhere unramified extension of $K$, and it is abelian. Using the [Minkowski bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_bound), one can show that $K$ has class number 2. Hence, its Hilbert class field is $L$. A non-principal ideal of $K$ is $(2,(1+\sqrt{-15})/2)$, and in $L$ this becomes the principal ideal $((1+\sqrt{5})/2)$.
- The field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$ has class number 3. Its Hilbert class field can be formed by adjoining a root of $x^3 - x - 1$, which has discriminant $-23$.
- To see why ramification at the archimedean primes must be taken into account, consider the [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$ obtained by adjoining the square root of 3 to $\mathbb{Q}$. This field has class number 1 and discriminant 12. Its extension $K(i)$ has discriminant $144=12^2$,[^o4] and so is unramified at all prime ideals in $K$, which means that $K$ admits finite abelian extensions of degree greater than 1 in which all finite primes of $K$ are unramified. This doesn't contradict the Hilbert class field of $K$ being $K$ itself: every proper finite abelian extension of $K$ must ramify at some place, and in the extension $K(i)/K$ there is ramification at the archimedean places: the real embeddings of $K$ extend to complex (rather than real) embeddings of $K(i)$.
- Likewise, for $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{6})$ (class number 1, discriminant 24), the extension $K(\sqrt{-2},\sqrt{-3})$ has discriminant $576=24^2$,[^o5] so is unramified at all prime ideals in $K$, but ramified at both real places.
- By the theory of [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183), the Hilbert class field of an [imaginary quadratic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field) is generated by the value of the [elliptic modular function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_modular_function) at a generator for the ring of integers (as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module).

:::

## History {#History}

The existence of a (narrow) Hilbert class field for a given number field $K$ was conjectured by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)[^n1] and proved by [Philipp Furtwängler](wikipedia://en/895349).[^n2] The existence of the Hilbert class field is a valuable tool in studying the structure of the [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) of a given field.

## Additional properties {#Additional_properties}

The Hilbert class field $E$ also satisfies the following:

- $E$ is a finite Galois [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of $K$ and $[E:K]=h_k$, where $h_K$ is the [class number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_class) of $K$.
- The [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) of $K$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/1160) to the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of $E$ over $K$.
- Every [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of $\mathcal{O}_K$ extends to a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) of the ring extension $\mathcal{O}_E$ ([principal ideal theorem](wikipedia://en/3136164)).
- Every [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) $P$ of $\mathcal{O}_K$ decomposes into the product of $h_K/f$ prime ideals in $\mathcal{O}_E$, where $f$ is the [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group_element) of $[P]$ in the ideal class group of $\mathcal{O}_K$.

In fact, $E$ is the unique [field](wikipedia://en/10603) satisfying the first, second, and fourth properties.

## Explicit constructions {#Explicit_constructions}

If $K$ is imaginary quadratic and $A$ is an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) with [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) by the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of $K$, then adjoining the [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988) of $A$ to $K$ gives the Hilbert class field.[^n3]

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695), one studies the [ray class field](wikipedia://en/45018204) with respect to a given [modulus](wikipedia://en/18510677), which is a formal product of prime ideals (including, possibly, archimedean ones). The ray class field is the maximal abelian extension unramified outside the primes dividing the modulus and satisfying a particular ramification condition at the primes dividing the modulus. The Hilbert class field is then the ray class field with respect to the trivial modulus *1*.

The *narrow class field* is the ray class field with respect to the modulus consisting of all infinite primes. For example, the argument above shows that $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}, i)$ is the narrow class field of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$.

:::
