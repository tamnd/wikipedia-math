# Étale morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Étale morphism"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), an **étale morphism** () is a morphism of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) that is [formally étale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_étale) and locally of finite presentation; the étale morphism is connected to the concept of **étale covering**. This is an algebraic analogue of the notion of a local isomorphism in the complex analytic topology.

:::

They satisfy the hypotheses of the [implicit function theorem](wikipedia://en/581005), but because open sets in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) are so large, they are not necessarily local isomorphisms. Despite this, étale maps retain many of the properties of local analytic isomorphisms, and are useful in defining the [algebraic fundamental group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_fundamental_group) and the [étale topology](wikipedia://en/5707590).

The word *étale* is a French [adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective), which means "slack", as in "slack tide", or, figuratively, calm, immobile, something left to settle.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\phi : R \to S$ be a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411). This makes $S$ an $R$-algebra. Choose a [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) $f$ in $R[x]$ and a polynomial $g$ in $R[x]$ such that the [derivative](wikipedia://en/3832917) $f'$ of $f$ is a unit in $(R[x]/fR[x])_g$. We say that $\phi$ is *standard étale* if $f$ and $g$ can be chosen so that $S$ is isomorphic as an $R$-algebra to $(R[x]/fR[x])_g$ and $\phi$ is the canonical map.

Let $f : X \to Y$ be a [morphism of schemes](wikipedia://en/248014#Morphisms). We say that $f$ is *étale* if it has any of the following equivalent properties:

1. $f$ is [flat](wikipedia://en/542520) and [unramified](wikipedia://en/58653736).[^o5]
1. $f$ is a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041) and unramified.[^o5]
1. $f$ is flat, [locally of finite presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#Locally_of_finite_presentation), and for every $y$ in $Y$, the fiber $f^{-1}(y)$ is the disjoint union of points, each of which is the spectrum of a finite separable [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of the residue field $\kappa(y)$.[^o5]
1. $f$ is flat, locally of finite presentation, and for every $y$ in $Y$ and every algebraic closure $k'$ of the residue field $\kappa(y)$, the geometric fiber $f^{-1}(y) \otimes_{\kappa(y)} k'$ is the disjoint union of points, each of which is isomorphic to $\mbox{Spec } k'$.[^o5]
1. $f$ is a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041) of relative dimension zero.[^o6]
1. $f$ is a smooth morphism and a locally [quasi-finite morphism](wikipedia://en/2143056).[^o7]
1. $f$ is locally of finite presentation and is locally a standard étale morphism, that is,

For every $x$ in $X$, let $y = f(x)$. Then there is an open affine neighborhood $\operatorname{Spec} R$ of $y$ and an open affine neighborhood $\operatorname{Spec} S$ of $x$ such that $f(\operatorname{Spec} S)$ is contained in $\operatorname{Spec} R$ and such that the ring homomorphism $R \rightarrow S$ induced by $f$ is standard étale.[^o8]

1. $f$ is locally of finite presentation and is [formally étale](wikipedia://en/30285123).[^o5]
1. $f$ is locally of finite presentation and is formally étale for maps from local rings, that is:

Let $A$ be a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) and $J$ be an ideal of $A$ such that $J^2=0$. Set $Z=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $Z_0 = \operatorname{Spec} A/J$, and let $i \colon Z_0 \rightarrow Z$ be the canonical [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986). Let $z$ denote the [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408) of $Z_0$. Let $h \colon Z \rightarrow Y$ and $g_0 \colon Z_0 \rightarrow X$ be morphisms such that $f(g_0(z)) = h(i(z))$. Then there exists a unique $Y$-morphism $g \colon Z \rightarrow X$ such that $gi = g_0$.[^o9]

Assume that $Y$ is locally noetherian and *f* is locally of finite type. For $x$ in $X$, let $y = f(x)$ and let $\hat{\mathcal O}_{Y,y} \to \hat{\mathcal O}_{X,x}$ be the induced map on [completed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28ring_theory%29) local rings. Then the following are equivalent:

1. $f$ is étale.
1. For every $x$ in $X$, the induced map on completed local rings is formally étale for the adic topology.[^o10]
1. For every $x$ in $X$, $\hat{\mathcal O}_{X,x}$ is a free $\hat{\mathcal O}_{Y,y}$-module and the fiber $\hat{\mathcal O}_{X,x}/m_y\hat{\mathcal O}_{X,x}$ is a field which is a finite separable field extension of the residue field $\kappa(y)$.[^o10] (Here $m_y$ is the maximal ideal of $\hat{\mathcal O}_{Y,y}$.)
1. $f$ is formally étale for maps of local rings with the following additional properties. The local ring $A$ may be assumed Artinian. If $m$ is the maximal ideal of $A$, then $J$ may be assumed to satisfy $mJ = 0$. Finally, the morphism on residue fields $\kappa(y) \rightarrow A/m$ may be assumed to be an isomorphism.[^o11]

If in addition all the maps on residue fields $\kappa(y) \to \kappa(x)$ are isomorphisms, or if $\kappa(y)$ is separably closed, then $f$ is étale [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) for every $x$ in $X$, the induced map on completed local rings is an isomorphism.[^o10]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Any [open immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#Open_immersion) is étale because it is locally an isomorphism.

Covering spaces form examples of étale morphisms. For example, if $d \geq 1$ is an integer invertible in the ring $R$ then

$$\text{Spec}(R[t,t^{-1},y]/(y^d - t)) \to \text{Spec}(R[t,t^{-1}])$$

is a degree $d$ étale morphism.

Any [ramified covering](wikipedia://en/3002253#Scheme_Theoretic_Examples) $\pi:X \to Y$ has an unramified locus

$$\pi: X_{un} \to Y_{un}$$

which is étale.

Morphisms

$$\text{Spec}(L) \to \text{Spec}(K)$$

induced by finite separable field extensions are étale — they form *arithmetic covering spaces* with group of deck transformations given by $\text{Gal}(L/K)$.

Any ring homomorphism of the form $R \to S=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]_g/(f_1,\ldots, f_n)$, where all the $f_i$ are polynomials, and where the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) determinant $\det(\partial f_i/\partial x_j)$ is a unit in $S$, is étale. For example the morphism $\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}] \to \mathbb{C}[x,t,t^{-1}]/(x^n - t)$ is etale and corresponds to a degree $n$ covering space of $\mathbb{G}_m \in Sch/\mathbb{C}$ with the group $\mathbb{Z}/n$ of deck transformations.

Expanding upon the previous example, suppose that we have a morphism $f$ of smooth complex algebraic varieties. Since $f$ is given by equations, we can interpret it as a map of complex manifolds. Whenever the Jacobian of $f$ is nonzero, $f$ is a local isomorphism of complex manifolds by the [implicit function theorem](wikipedia://en/581005). By the previous example, having non-zero Jacobian is the same as being étale.

Let $f : X\to Y$ be a dominant morphism of finite type with *X*, *Y* locally noetherian, irreducible and *Y* normal. If *f* is [unramified](wikipedia://en/58653736), then it is étale.[^o14]

For a field *K*, any *K*-algebra *A* is necessarily flat. Therefore, *A* is an etale algebra if and only if it is unramified, which is also equivalent to

$$A \otimes_{K}\bar{K}\cong\bar{K}\oplus ...\oplus\bar{K},$$

where $\bar K$ is the [separable closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_closure) of the field *K* and the right hand side is a finite direct sum, all of whose summands are $\bar K$. This characterization of etale *K*-algebras is a stepping stone in reinterpreting classical [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) (see [Grothendieck's Galois theory](wikipedia://en/372470)).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- Étale morphisms are preserved under composition and base change.
- Étale morphisms are local on the source and on the base. In other words, $f: X\to Y$ is étale if and only if for each covering of $X$ by open subschemes the restriction of $f$ to each of the open subschemes of the covering is étale, and also if and only if for each cover of $Y$ by open subschemes the induced morphisms $f_{(U)} : X \times_Y U \to U$ is étale for each subscheme $U$ of the covering. In particular, it is possible to test the property of being étale on open affines $V=\operatorname{Spec}(B)\to U=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$.
- The product of a finite family of étale morphisms is étale.
- Given a finite family of morphisms $\{f_\alpha : X_\alpha \to Y\}$, the disjoint union $\coprod f_\alpha : \coprod X_\alpha \to Y$ is étale if and only if each $f_\alpha$ is étale.
- Let $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to Z$, and assume that $g$ is unramified and $gf$ is étale. Then $f$ is étale. In particular, if $X$ and $X'$ are étale over $Y$, then any $Y$-morphism between $X$ and $X'$ is étale.
- [Quasi-compact](wikipedia://en/12410777) étale morphisms are [quasi-finite](wikipedia://en/2143056).
- A morphism $f : X \to Y$ is an open immersion if and only if it is étale and [radicial](wikipedia://en/14483490).[^o17]
- If $f : X \to Y$ is étale and surjective, then $\dim X = \dim Y$ (finite or otherwise).

:::

## Inverse function theorem {#Inverse_function_theorem}

Étale morphisms

*f*: *X* → *Y*

are the algebraic counterpart of local [diffeomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphisms). More precisely, a morphism between smooth varieties is étale at a point iff the differential between the corresponding [tangent spaces](wikipedia://en/30807) is an isomorphism. This is in turn precisely the condition needed to ensure that a map between [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) is a local diffeomorphism, i.e. for any point *y* ∈ *Y*, there is an [open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) neighborhood *U* of *x* such that the restriction of *f* to *U* is a diffeomorphism. This conclusion does not hold in algebraic geometry, because the topology is too coarse. For example, consider the projection *f* of the [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231)

*y* = *x*<sup>2</sup>

to the *y*-axis. This morphism is étale at every point except the origin (0, 0), because the differential is given by 2*x*, which does not vanish at these points.

However, there is no ([Zariski-](wikipedia://en/241744))local inverse of *f*, just because the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) is not an [algebraic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_map_%28algebraic_geometry%29), not being given by polynomials. However, there is a remedy for this situation, using the étale topology. The precise statement is as follows: if $f : X\to Y$ is étale and finite, then for any point *y* lying in *Y*, there is an étale morphism *V* → *Y* containing *y* in its image (*V* can be thought of as an étale open neighborhood of *y*), such that when we base change *f* to *V*, then $X\times_Y V\to V$ (the first member would be the pre-image of *V* by *f* if *V* were a Zariski open neighborhood) is a finite disjoint union of open subsets isomorphic to *V*. In other words, *étale-locally* in *Y*, the morphism *f* is a topological finite cover.

For a smooth morphism $f : X\to Y$ of relative dimension *n*, *étale-locally* in *X* and in *Y*, *f* is an open immersion into an [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) $\mathbb A^n_Y$. This is the étale analogue version of the structure theorem on [submersions](wikipedia://en/356413#Local_normal_form).

:::
