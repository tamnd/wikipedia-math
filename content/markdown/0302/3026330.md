# Generalized Jacobian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized Jacobian"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) a **generalized Jacobian** is a commutative [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) associated to a curve with a divisor, generalizing the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of a complete curve. They were introduced by [Maxwell Rosenlicht](wikipedia://en/26817022) in 1954, and can be used to study [ramified coverings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramified_covering) of a curve, with abelian [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673). Generalized Jacobians of a curve are extensions of the Jacobian of the curve by a [commutative](wikipedia://en/2974) [affine algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885), giving nontrivial examples of [Chevalley's structure theorem](wikipedia://en/37657294).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose *C* is a complete [nonsingular](wikipedia://en/981915) curve, *m* an effective divisor on *C*, *S* is the support of *m*, and *P* is a fixed base point on *C* not in *S*. The generalized Jacobian *J*<sub>*m*</sub> is a commutative algebraic group with a rational map *f* from *C* to *J*<sub>*m*</sub> such that:

- *f* takes *P* to the identity of *J*<sub>*m*</sub>.
- *f* is regular outside *S*.
- *f*(*D*) = 0 whenever *D* is the divisor of a rational function *g* on *C* such that *g*≡1 mod *m*.

Moreover *J*<sub>*m*</sub> is the universal group with these properties, in the sense that any rational map from *C* to a group with the properties above factors uniquely through *J*<sub>*m*</sub>. The group *J*<sub>*m*</sub> does not depend on the choice of base point *P*, though changing *P* changes that map *f* by a translation.

:::

## Structure of the generalized Jacobian {#Structure_of_the_generalized_Jacobian}

For *m* = 0 the generalized Jacobian *J*<sub>*m*</sub> is just the usual Jacobian *J*, an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) of [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) *g*, the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891#Algebraic_geometry) of *C*.

For *m* a nonzero effective divisor the generalized Jacobian is an extension of *J* by a connected commutative affine algebraic group *L*<sub>*m*</sub> of dimension deg(*m*)−1. So we have an exact sequence

0 → *L*<sub>*m*</sub> → *J*<sub>*m*</sub> → *J* → 0

The group *L*<sub>*m*</sub> is a quotient

0 → *G*<sub>*m*</sub> → Π*U*<sub>*P*<sub>*i*</sub></sub><sup>(*n*<sub>*i*</sub>)</sup> → *L*<sub>*m*</sub> → 0

of a product of groups *R*<sub>*i*</sub> by the multiplicative group *G*<sub>*m*</sub> of the underlying field. The product runs over the points *P*<sub>*i*</sub> in the support of *m*, and the group *U*<sub>*P*<sub>*i*</sub></sub><sup>(*n*<sub>*i*</sub>)</sup> is the group of invertible elements of the [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) modulo those that are 1 mod *P*<sub>*i*</sub><sup>*n*<sub>*i*</sub></sup>. The group *U*<sub>*P*<sub>*i*</sub></sub><sup>(*n*<sub>*i*</sub>)</sup> has dimension *n*<sub>i</sub>, the number of times *P*<sub>*i*</sub> occurs in *m*. It is the product of the multiplicative group *G*<sub>*m*</sub> by a unipotent group of dimension *n*<sub>*i*</sub>−1, which in characteristic 0 is isomorphic to a product of *n*<sub>*i*</sub>−1 additive groups.

## Complex generalized Jacobians {#Complex_generalized_Jacobians}

Over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), the algebraic structure of the generalized Jacobian determines an analytic structure of the generalized Jacobian making it a [complex Lie group](wikipedia://en/2187847).

The analytic subgroup underlying the generalized Jacobian can be described as follows. (This does not always determine the algebraic structure as two non-isomorphic commutative algebraic groups may be isomorphic as analytic groups.) Suppose that *C* is a curve with an effective divisor *m* with support *S*. There is a natural map from the [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) *H*<sub>1</sub>(*C* − *S*) to the dual Ω(−*m*)\* of the complex [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) Ω(−*m*) (1-forms with poles on *m*) induced by the integral of a 1-form over a 1-cycle. The analytic generalized Jacobian is then the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) Ω(−*m*)\*/*H*<sub>1</sub>(*C* − *S*).
