# Great icosahedron

Reg star polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great icosahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great icosahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great icosahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great icosahedron** is one of four [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler–Poinsot_polyhedra) ([nonconvex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex) [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/505253#Non-convex_2)), with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{3,\tfrac{5}{2}\}$ and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) of . It is composed of 20 intersecting triangular faces, having five triangles meeting at each vertex in a [pentagrammic](wikipedia://en/197544) sequence.

:::

The great icosahedron can be constructed analogously to the pentagram, its two-dimensional analogue, via the extension of the $(n-1)$-dimensional [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) faces of the core $n$-polytope (equilateral triangles for the great icosahedron, and [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) for the pentagram) until the figure regains regular faces. The [grand 600-cell](wikipedia://en/8604538) can be seen as its four-dimensional analogue using the same process.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The edge length of a great icosahedron is $\frac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2} = \left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}2\right)^4$ times that of the original icosahedron.

:::

## Formulas {#Formulas}

For a great icosahedron with edge length $E$ (the edge of its dodecahedron core),

| [Inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{3\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{15}}{4}$ |
|---|---|
| [Midradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{4}$ |
| [Circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{\sqrt{2(5-\sqrt{5})}}{4}$ |
| [Surface area](wikipedia://en/27725): | $\quad E^2 \times 3\sqrt{3}\Bigl(5+4\sqrt{5}\Bigr)$ |
| [Volume](wikipedia://en/32498): | $\quad E^3 \times \frac{25+9\sqrt{5}}{4}$ |

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

*Image: Great stellated dodecahedron truncations.gif.* Animated truncation sequence from $\{5/2, 3\}$ to $\{3, 5/2\}$

It shares the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) as the regular convex [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084). It also shares the same [edge arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_arrangement) as the [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843).

A truncation operation, repeatedly applied to the great icosahedron, produces a sequence of uniform polyhedra. Truncating edges down to points produces the [great icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157391) as a rectified great icosahedron. The process completes as a birectification, reducing the original faces down to points, and producing the [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878).

The [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) *great stellated dodecahedron* is a degenerate polyhedron, with 20 triangular faces from the truncated vertices, and 12 (hidden) doubled up pentagonal faces ($\{10/2\}$) as truncations of the original pentagram faces, the latter forming two [great dodecahedra](wikipedia://en/2860441) inscribed within and sharing the edges of the icosahedron.
