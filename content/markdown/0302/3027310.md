# Kretschmann scalar

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kretschmann scalar"}

In the theory of [Lorentzian manifolds](wikipedia://en/287555), particularly in the context of applications to [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), the **Kretschmann scalar** is a quadratic [scalar invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvature_invariant_%28general_relativity%29). It was introduced by [Erich Kretschmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Kretschmann).[^o4]

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Kretschmann invariant is[^o4][^o5]

$$K = R_{abcd} \, R^{abcd}$$

where $R^{a}{}_{bcd} = \partial_{c}\Gamma^{a}{}_{db} - \partial_{d}\Gamma^{a}{}_{cb} + \Gamma^{a}{}_{ce}\Gamma^{e}{}_{db} - \Gamma^{a}{}_{de}\Gamma^{e}{}_{cb}$ is the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) and $\Gamma$ is the [Christoffel symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffel_symbol). Because it is a sum of squares of tensor components, this is a *quadratic* invariant.

[Einstein summation convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_summation_convention) with [raised and lowered indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raising_and_lowering_indices) is used above and throughout the article. An explicit summation expression is

$$K = R_{abcd} \, R^{abcd} =\sum_{a =0}^{3}\sum_{b =0}^3 \sum_{c=0}^3\sum_{d=0}^3 R_{abcd} \, R^{abcd} \text{ with } R^{abcd} =\sum_{i=0}^3 g^{ai}\,\sum_{j=0}^3 g^{bj}\,\sum_{k=0}^3 g^{ck}\,\sum_{\ell=0}^3 g^{d\ell}\, R_{ijk\ell}. \,$$

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

For a [Schwarzschild black hole](wikipedia://en/264606) of mass $M$, the Kretschmann scalar is[^o4]

$$K = \frac{48 G^2 M^2}{c^4 r^6} \,.$$

where $G$ is the gravitational constant.

For a general [FRW spacetime](wikipedia://en/343031) with metric

$$ds^2 = - \mathrm{d}t^2 + {a(t)}^2 \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}r^2}{1-k r^2} + r^2 \, \mathrm{d}\theta^2 + r^2 \sin^2 \theta \, \mathrm{d}\varphi^2 \right),$$

the Kretschmann scalar is

$$K=\frac{12 \left[a(t)^2 a''(t)^2+\left(k+a'(t)^2 \right)^2\right]}{a(t)^4}.$$

:::

:::

## Relation to other invariants {#Relation_to_other_invariants}

Another possible invariant (which has been employed for example in writing the gravitational term of the Lagrangian for some *higher-order gravity* theories) is

$$C_{abcd} \, C^{abcd}$$

where $C_{abcd}$ is the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848), the conformal curvature tensor which is also the completely traceless part of the Riemann tensor. In $d$ dimensions this is related to the Kretschmann invariant by[^o9]

$$R_{abcd} \, R^{abcd} = C_{abcd} \, C^{abcd} +\frac{4}{d-2} R_{ab}\, R^{ab} - \frac{2}{(d-1)(d-2)}R^2$$

where $R^{ab}$ is the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) tensor and $R$ is the Ricci [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) (obtained by taking successive traces of the Riemann tensor). The Ricci tensor vanishes in vacuum spacetimes (such as the Schwarzschild solution mentioned above), and hence there the Riemann tensor and the Weyl tensor coincide, as do their invariants.

### Gauge theory invariants {#Gauge_theory_invariants}

The Kretschmann scalar and the *Chern-Pontryagin scalar*

$$R_{abcd} \, {{}^\star \! R}^{abcd}$$

where ${{}^\star R}^{abcd}$ is the *left dual* of the Riemann tensor, are mathematically analogous (to some extent, physically analogous) to the familiar invariants of the [electromagnetic field tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field_tensor)

$$F_{ab} \, F^{ab}, \; \; F_{ab} \, {{}^\star \! F}^{ab}.$$

Generalising from the $U(1)$ gauge theory of electromagnetism to general non-abelian gauge theory, the first of these invariants is

$$\text{Tr}(F_{ab} F^{ab}),$$

an expression proportional to the [Yang–Mills Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/672202). Here $F_{ab}$ is the curvature of a [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848), and $\text{Tr}$ is a [trace form](wikipedia://en/982386). The Kretschmann scalar arises from taking the connection to be on the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550).
