# Genus of a multiplicative sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Genus of a multiplicative sequence"}

*Image: Cobordism.svg.* A cobordism (*W*; *M*, *N*).

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **genus of a multiplicative sequence** is a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) from the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of smooth [compact manifolds](wikipedia://en/669475) up to the equivalence of bounding a smooth manifold with boundary (i.e., up to suitable [cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832)) to another ring, usually the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), having the property that they are constructed from a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of polynomials in [characteristic classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_classes) that arise as coefficients in formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) with good multiplicative properties.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **genus** $\varphi$ assigns a number $\Phi(X)$ to each manifold *X* such that

1. $\Phi(X \sqcup Y) = \Phi(X) + \Phi(Y)$ (where $\sqcup$ is the disjoint union);
1. $$\Phi(X \times Y) = \Phi(X)\Phi(Y);$$
1. $\Phi(X) = 0$ if *X* is the boundary of a manifold with boundary.

The manifolds and manifolds with boundary may be required to have additional structure; for example, they might be oriented, spin, stably complex, and so on (see [list of cobordism theories](wikipedia://en/3314988#Bordism_and_cobordism_theories) for many more examples). The value $\Phi(X)$ is in some ring, often the ring of rational numbers, though it can be other rings such as $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ or the [ring of modular forms](wikipedia://en/62004450).

The conditions on $\Phi$ can be rephrased as saying that $\Phi$ is a ring homomorphism from the cobordism ring of manifolds (with additional structure) to another ring.

Example: If $\Phi(X)$ is the [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) of the oriented manifold *X*, then $\Phi$ is a genus from oriented manifolds to the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613).

:::

## The genus associated to a formal power series {#The_genus_associated_to_a_formal_power_series}

A sequence of polynomials $K_1, K_2,\ldots$ in variables $p_1, p_2,\ldots$ is called **multiplicative** if

$$1 + p_1z + p_2z^2 + \cdots = (1 + q_1z + q_2z^2 + \cdots) (1 + r_1z + r_2z^2 + \cdots)$$

implies that

$$\sum_j K_j(p_1, p_2, \ldots)z^j = \sum_j K_j (q_1, q_2, \ldots) z^j\sum_k K_k (r_1, r_2, \ldots)z^k$$

If $Q(z)$ is a [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) in *z* with [constant term](wikipedia://en/79861) 1, we can define a multiplicative sequence

$$K = 1+ K_1 + K_2 + \cdots$$

by

$$K(p_1, p_2, p_3, \ldots) = Q(z_1)Q(z_2)Q(z_3)\cdots,$$

where $p_k$ is the *k*th [elementary symmetric function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_symmetric_function) of the indeterminates $z_i$. (The variables $p_k$ will often in practice be [Pontryagin classes](wikipedia://en/385944).)

The genus $\Phi$ of [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [smooth](wikipedia://en/2119219), [oriented](wikipedia://en/187446) manifolds corresponding to *Q* is given by

$$\Phi(X) = K(p_1, p_2, p_3, \ldots)$$

where the $p_k$ are the [Pontryagin classes](wikipedia://en/385944) of *X*. The power series *Q* is called the **characteristic power series** of the genus $\Phi$. A theorem of [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633), which states that the rationals tensored with the cobordism ring is a polynomial algebra in generators of degree 4*k* for positive integers *k*, implies that this gives a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between formal power series *Q* with rational coefficients and leading coefficient 1, and genera from oriented manifolds to the rational numbers.

## L genus {#L_genus}

The **L genus** is the genus of the formal power series

$${\sqrt{z}\over \tanh(\sqrt z)} = \sum_{k\ge 0} \frac{2^{2k}B_{2k}z^k}{(2k)!} = 1 + {z \over 3} - {z^2 \over 45} + \cdots$$

where the numbers $B_{2k}$ are the [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964). The first few values are:

$$\begin{aligned} L_0 &= 1 \\ L_1 &= \tfrac13 p_1 \\ L_2 &= \tfrac1{45}\left(7p_2 - p_1^2\right) \\ L_3 &= \tfrac1{945}\left(62 p_3 - 13 p_1 p_2 + 2 p_1^3\right) \\ L_4 &= \tfrac1{14175}\left(381 p_4 - 71 p_1 p_3 - 19 p_2^2 + 22 p_1^2 p_2 - 3 p_1^4\right) \end{aligned}$$

(for further *L*-polynomials see [^o7] or ). Now let *M* be a closed smooth oriented manifold of dimension 4*n* with [Pontrjagin classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontrjagin_class) $p_i = p_i(M)$. [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) showed that the *L* genus of *M* in dimension 4*n* evaluated on the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of $M$, denoted $[M]$, is equal to $\sigma(M)$, the [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) of *M* (i.e., the signature of the [intersection form](wikipedia://en/3148602#Topological_intersection_form) on the 2*n*th cohomology group of *M*):

$$\sigma(M) = \langle L_n(p_1(M), \ldots, p_n(M)), [M]\rangle.$$

This is now known as the **[Hirzebruch signature theorem](wikipedia://en/7192444)** (or sometimes the **Hirzebruch index theorem**).

The fact that $L_2$ is always integral for a smooth manifold was used by [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) to give an example of an 8-dimensional [piecewise linear (PL) manifold](wikipedia://en/2174184) with no [smooth structure](wikipedia://en/1542238). Pontryagin numbers can also be defined for PL manifolds, and Milnor showed that his PL manifold had a non-integral value of $p_2$, and so was not smoothable.

### Application on K3 surfaces {#Application_on_K3_surfaces}

Since projective [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/385944#Pontryagin_classes_on_a_Quartic_K3_Surface) are smooth [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) of dimension two, their only non-trivial [Pontryagin class](wikipedia://en/385944) is $p_1$ in $H^4(X)$. It can be computed as -48 using the tangent sequence and comparisons with complex [Chern classes](wikipedia://en/294349). Since $L_1 = -16$, we have its signature. This can be used to compute its intersection form as a [unimodular lattice](wikipedia://en/1648605) since it has $\operatorname{dim}\left(H^2(X)\right) = 22$, and using the classification of unimodular lattices.[^o9]

:::

## Todd genus {#Todd_genus}

The **Todd genus** is the genus of the formal power series

$$\frac{z}{1 - \exp(-z)} = \sum_{i=0}^\infty\frac{B_i}{i!}z^i$$

with $B_i$ as before, Bernoulli numbers. The first few values are

$$\begin{aligned} Td_0 &= 1 \\ Td_1 &= \frac1{2} c_1 \\ Td_2 &= \frac1{12} \left (c_2 + c_1^2 \right ) \\ Td_3 &= \frac1{24} c_1 c_2 \\ Td_4 &= \frac1{720} \left(-c_1^4 + 4 c_2 c_1^2 + 3c_2^2 + c_3 c_1 - c_4\right) \end{aligned}$$

The Todd genus has the particular property that it assigns the value 1 to all complex projective spaces (i.e. $\mathrm{Td}_n(\mathbb{CP}^n) = 1$), and this suffices to show that the Todd genus agrees with the arithmetic genus for [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) as the [arithmetic genus](wikipedia://en/1950390) is also 1 for complex projective spaces. This observation is a consequence of the [Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/2892412), and in fact is one of the key developments that led to the formulation of that theorem.

## Â genus {#Â_genus}

The **Â genus** is the genus associated to the characteristic power series

$$Q(z) = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{z}}{\sinh\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{z}\right)} = 1 - \frac{z}{24} + \frac{7z^2}{5760} - \cdots$$

(There is also an A genus which is less commonly used, associated to the characteristic series $Q(16z)$.) The first few values are

$$\begin{aligned} \hat{A}_0 &= 1 \\ \hat{A}_1 &= -\tfrac1{24}p_1 \\ \hat{A}_2 &= \tfrac1{5760}\left(-4p_2 + 7 p_1^2\right) \\ \hat{A}_3 &= \tfrac1{967680}\left(-16p_3 + 44p_2 p_1 - 31 p_1^3\right) \\ \hat{A}_4 &= \tfrac1{464486400}\left(-192p_4 + 512 p_3 p_1 + 208p_2^2 - 904p_2 p_1^2 + 381p_1^4\right) \end{aligned}$$

The Â genus of a [spin manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold) is an integer, and an even integer if the dimension is 4 mod 8 (which in dimension 4 implies [Rochlin's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochlin's_theorem)) – for general manifolds, the Â genus is not always an integer. This was proven by [Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) and [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672); this result both motivated and was later explained by the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752), which showed that the Â genus of a spin manifold is equal to the index of its [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448).

By combining this index result with a [Weitzenbock formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weitzenbock_formula) for the Dirac Laplacian, [André Lichnerowicz](wikipedia://en/2389188) deduced that if a compact spin manifold admits a metric with positive [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622), its Â genus must vanish. This only gives an obstruction to positive scalar curvature when the dimension is a multiple of 4, but [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164) later discovered an analogous $\mathbb{Z}_2$-valued obstruction in dimensions 1 or 2 mod 8. These results are essentially sharp. Indeed, [Mikhail Gromov](wikipedia://en/771562), [H. Blaine Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson), and Stephan Stolz later proved that the Â genus and Hitchin's $\mathbb{Z}_2$-valued analog are the only obstructions to the existence of positive-scalar-curvature metrics on simply-connected spin manifolds of dimension greater than or equal to 5.

:::

## Elliptic genus {#Elliptic_genus}

A genus is called an **elliptic genus** if the power series $Q(z) = z/f(z)$ satisfies the condition

$${f'}^2 = 1 - 2\delta f^2 + \epsilon f^4$$

for constants $\delta$ and $\epsilon$. (As usual, *Q* is the characteristic power series of the genus.)

One explicit expression for *f*(*z*) is

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{a}\operatorname{sn}\left( az, \frac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{a^2} \right)$$

where

$$a = \sqrt{\delta + \sqrt{\delta^2 - \epsilon}}$$

and *sn* is the [Jacobi elliptic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobi_elliptic_function).

Examples:

- $\delta = \epsilon = 1, f(z) = \tanh(z)$. This is the L-genus.
- $\delta = -\frac{1}{8}, \epsilon = 0, f(z) = 2\sinh\left(\frac{1}{2}z\right)$. This is the Â genus.
- $\epsilon = \delta^2 , f(z) = \frac{\tanh(\sqrt{\delta}z)}{\sqrt{\delta}}$. This is a generalization of the L-genus.

The first few values of such genera are:

$$\frac{1}{3}\delta p_1$$

$$\frac{1}{90} \left [ \left (-4\delta^2 +18\epsilon \right )p_2+ \left (7\delta^2-9\epsilon \right )p_1^2\right ]$$

$$\frac{1}{1890} \left [ \left (16\delta^3 + 108\delta \epsilon \right )p_3 + \left (-44\delta^3 +18\delta \epsilon \right )p_2p_1 + \left (31\delta^3 -27\delta \epsilon \right )p_1^3\right ]$$

Example (elliptic genus for quaternionic [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350)) :

$$\begin{aligned} \Phi_{ell}(HP^2) &= \int_{HP^2}\tfrac1{90}\big[(-4\delta^2 +18\epsilon )p_2+(7\delta^2-9\epsilon )p_1^2\big] \\ &= \int_{HP^2}\tfrac1{90}\big[(-4\delta^2 +18\epsilon )(7u^2)+(7\delta^2-9\epsilon )(2u)^2\big] \\ &= \int_{HP^2} [u^2 \epsilon ] \\ &= \epsilon \int_{HP^2} [u^2] \\ &= \epsilon * 1 = \epsilon \end{aligned}$$

Example (elliptic genus for octonionic projective plane, or [Cayley plane](wikipedia://en/9410951)):

$$\begin{aligned} \Phi_{ell}(OP^2) &= \int_{OP^2}\tfrac1{113400} \left[(-192\delta^4 + 1728\delta^2\epsilon + 1512\epsilon^2)p_4 + (208\delta^4 - 1872\delta^2\epsilon + 1512\epsilon^2)p_2^2\right] \\ &= \int_{OP^2}\tfrac1{113400}\big[(-192\delta^4 + 1728\delta^2\epsilon + 1512\epsilon^2)(39u^2) + (208\delta^4 - 1872\delta^2\epsilon + 1512\epsilon^2)(6u)^2\big] \\ &= \int_{OP^2}\big[ \epsilon^2 u^2 \big] \\ &= \epsilon^2\int_{OP^2} \big[ u^2 \big] \\ &= \epsilon^2* 1 = \epsilon^2 \\ &= \Phi_{ell}(HP^2) ^2 \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Witten genus {#Witten_genus}

The **Witten genus** is the genus associated to the characteristic power series

$$Q(z) = \frac{z}{\sigma_L(z)} = \exp\left(\sum_{k\ge 2} {2G_{2k}(\tau)z^{2k}\over(2k)!}\right)$$

where σ<sub>L</sub> is the [Weierstrass sigma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_sigma_function) for the lattice *L*, and *G* is a multiple of an [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458).

The Witten genus of a 4*k* dimensional compact oriented smooth spin manifold with vanishing first Pontryagin class is a [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000) of weight 2*k*, with integral Fourier coefficients.
