# S. Barry Cooper

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="S. Barry Cooper"}

**S. Barry Cooper** (9 October 1943 – 26 October 2015) was an English mathematician and [computability theorist](wikipedia://en/155414). He was a professor of [pure mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Mathematics) at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Cooper grew up in [Bognor Regis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bognor_Regis) and attended [Chichester High School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester_High_School_for_Boys), during which time he played scrum-half for the under-15s England rugby team.[^o5]

Cooper graduated from [Jesus College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford), in 1966 and in 1970 received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester) under the supervision of [Reuben Goodstein](wikipedia://en/1586970) and C.E.M. Yates, with a thesis entitled *Degrees of Unsolvability*.

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Cooper was appointed [Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) in the School of Mathematics at the University of Leeds in 1969, where he remained for the rest of his career. He was promoted to [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) in Mathematical Logic in 1991 and to [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of Pure Mathematics in 1996. In 2011, he was awarded an honorary doctorate at the [University of Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sofia) "St. Kliment Ohridski".

His book *Computability Theory* made the technical research area accessible to a new generation of students. He was a leading mover of the return to basic questions of the kind considered by [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), and of interdisciplinary developments related to computability. He was President of the Association [Computability in Europe](wikipedia://en/22521356), and Chair of the Turing Centenary Advisory Committee (TCAC), which co-ordinated the [Alan Turing Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing_Year). The book *Alan Turing: His Work and Impact*, edited by Cooper and [Jan van Leeuwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Leeuwen), won the [Association of American Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Publishers)' R. R. Hawkins Award.

Cooper was a member of the [editorial board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_board) for *[The Rutherford Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rutherford_Journal)*.[^o7]

## Interests {#Interests}

Cooper was a keen long-distance runner, and was also interested in jazz and improvised music, founding Leeds Jazz and being involved in the Termite Club.

In the 1970s, he was also a leading figure in the Chile Solidarity Campaign, welcoming Chilean refugees to Leeds.

## Death {#Death}

Cooper died on 26 October 2015 after a short illness.[^o10]
