# Circular arc

*Image: Circle arc.svg.* A [circular sector](wikipedia://en/1967249) is shaded in green. Its curved boundary of length L is a circular arc.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Circular arc"}

A **circular arc** is the [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) between a pair of distinct [points](wikipedia://en/593693). If the two points are not directly opposite each other, one of these arcs, the **minor arc**, [subtends](wikipedia://en/1999527) a [central angle](wikipedia://en/462705) that is less than [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) [radians](wikipedia://en/26003) (180 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294)); and the other arc, the **major arc**, subtends an [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) greater than $\pi$ radians. An arc of a circle is defined as a part of the [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of the circle. A straight line that connects the two ends of an arc is known as a *[chord](wikipedia://en/297947)* of the circle. If the [length](wikipedia://en/18542) of an arc is exactly half of the circle, it is known as a *[semicircular arc](wikipedia://en/617831)*.

:::

## Length {#Length}

The length (more precisely, [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401)) of an arc of a circle with radius *r* and subtending an angle *θ* (measured in radians) with the circle center — i.e., the **[central angle](wikipedia://en/462705)** — is

$$L = \theta r.$$

This is because

$$\frac{L}{\mathrm{circumference}}=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}.$$

Substituting in the circumference

$$\frac{L}{2\pi r}=\frac{\theta}{2\pi},$$

and, with *α* being the same angle measured in degrees, since *θ* = $\tfrac{\alpha}{180}$$\pi$, the arc length equals

$$L=\frac{\alpha\pi r}{180}.$$

A practical way to determine the length of an arc in a circle is to plot two lines from the arc's endpoints to the center of the circle, measure the angle where the two lines meet the center, then solve for L by cross-multiplying the statement:

measure of [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) in degrees/360° = *L*/circumference.

For example, if the measure of the angle is 60 degrees and the circumference is 24 inches, then

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{60}{360} &= \frac{L}{24} \\[6pt] 360L &= 1440 \\[6pt] L &= 4. \end{aligned}$$

This is so because the circumference of a circle and the degrees of a circle, of which there are always 360, are directly proportional.

The upper half of a circle can be parameterized as

$$y=\sqrt{r^2-x^2}.$$

Then the arc length from $x=a$ to $x=b$ is

$$L=r\Big[\arcsin \left(\frac{x}{r}\right)\Big]^b_a.$$

## Sector area {#Sector_area}

The area of the sector formed by an arc and the center of a circle (bounded by the arc and the two radii drawn to its endpoints) is

$$A=\frac{r^2 \theta}{2}.$$

The area *A* has the same proportion to the [circle area](wikipedia://en/6220#Area_enclosed) as the angle *θ* to a full circle:

$$\frac{A}{\pi r^2}=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}.$$

We can cancel $\pi$ on both sides:

$$\frac{A}{r^2}=\frac{\theta}{2}.$$

By multiplying both sides by *r*2, we get the final result:

$$A=\frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta.$$

Using the conversion described above, we find that the area of the sector for a central angle measured in degrees is

$$A=\frac{\alpha}{360} \pi r^2.$$

## Segment area {#Segment_area}

The area of the shape bounded by the arc and the straight line between its two end points is

$$\frac{1}{2} r^2 (\theta - \sin\theta).$$

To get the area of the [arc segment](wikipedia://en/6220#Terminology), we need to subtract the area of the triangle, determined by the circle's center and the two end points of the arc, from the area $A$. See [Circular segment](wikipedia://en/404049) for details.

## Radius {#Radius}

*Image: Circle with some chords.jpg.* [The product](wikipedia://en/24977) of the [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) AP and PB equals the product of the line segments CP and PD. If the arc has a width AB and height CP, then the circle's diameter $CD=\frac{AP \cdot PB}{CP}+CP$

Using the [intersecting chords theorem](wikipedia://en/2094044#Intersecting_secants_theorem,_intersecting_chords_theorem) (also known as [power of a point](wikipedia://en/2094044) or secant tangent theorem) it is possible to calculate the radius *r* of a circle given the height *H* and the width *W* of an arc:

Consider the [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) with the same endpoints as the arc. Its perpendicular bisector is another chord, which is a diameter of the circle. The length of the first chord is *W*, and it is divided by the bisector into two equal halves, each with length $\tfrac{W}{2}$. The total length of the diameter is 2*r*, and it is divided into two parts by the first chord. The length of one part is the [sagitta](wikipedia://en/24947530) of the arc, *H*, and the other part is the remainder of the diameter, with length 2*r* − *H*. Applying the intersecting chords theorem to these two chords produces

$$H(2r-H)=\left(\frac{W}{2}\right)^2,$$

whence

$$2r-H=\frac{W^2}{4H},$$

so

$$r=\frac{W^2}{8H}+\frac{H}{2}.$$

The arc, chord, and sagitta derive their names respectively from the Latin words for [bow, bowstring, and arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_%28weapon%29).
