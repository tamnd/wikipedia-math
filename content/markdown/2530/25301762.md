# Gerald L. Thompson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerald L. Thompson"}

**Gerald L. Thompson** (born November 25, 1923, [Rolfe, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolfe,_Iowa); died November 9, 2009 [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh), Pennsylvania) was the [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) Professor of Systems and Operations Research (Emeritus) in the [Tepper School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepper_School_of_Business) of [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University).

:::

From 1943 to 1946, Thompson served in the [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy) as an ensign on the Harwood, which was stationed in the Pacific. By correspondence he obtained a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree from [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) in 1944. After the war he attended [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), graduating with Master of Science in 1948. He then took up further graduate study at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), obtaining the Ph.D. in 1953 under the supervision of [Robert M. Thrall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Thrall).[^o2]

In addition to being a mathematician, Thompson also was interested in painting. The illustration shown here is a study for "Chromatic Hamiltonian Knight's Tour". The painting combines Thompson's aesthetic and mathematical skills (the indicated moves of a knight on a chessboard cycle through the color wheel).

Thompson's students include [Fred W. Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_W._Glover).

## Dartmouth College {#Dartmouth_College}

From 1953 to 1958, Thompson taught at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) in [Hanover, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover,_New_Hampshire), where he wrote *Introduction to Finite Mathematics* with [John G. Kemeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Kemeny) and [J. Laurie Snell](wikipedia://en/33962324). *Introduction to Finite Mathematics* was "the first book which introduced mathematics into the study of management and business problems", according to Thompson's Carnegie-Mellon colleague, Professor [Egon Balas](wikipedia://en/35872667).[^o4] "Kemeny-Snell-Thompson" became a standard textbook in [management science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_science).

From 1958 to 1959, Thompson taught at [Ohio Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Wesleyan_University) in [Delaware, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware,_Ohio).

## Carnegie Mellon University {#Carnegie_Mellon_University}

In 1959, Thompson joined the faculty of the [Graduate School of Industrial Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_School_of_Industrial_Administration) at the [Carnegie Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institute_of_Technology) in [Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania). At Carnegie Mellon University, Thompson became the [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) Professor of Systems and Operations Research and a Senior Researcher at the Innovation, Creativity, and Capital Institute. He developed new methods for mathematical and computational modeling and expanded the use of mathematics in management science and economics. His research encompassed mathematical programming, combinatorial optimization, [production planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_planning), large scale linear and network programming, computational economics, market games, optimal control theory, scheduling theory and practice, and management.

In 2001, Thompson retired from Carnegie Mellon University at the age of seventy-eight. In 2003, a conference was held to honor Thompson's 80th birthday. At this conference, [William W. Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_W._Cooper) gave an address on Thompson's work and impact on [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476); Cooper's address was published in the festschrift for Thompson.[^o6]
