# Henry Scheffé

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Scheffé"}

**Henry Scheffé** (April 11, 1907 – July 5, 1977) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661).[^o4] He is known for the [Lehmann–Scheffé theorem](wikipedia://en/342602) and [Scheffé's method](wikipedia://en/14913760).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Scheffé was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on April 11, 1907, the child of German immigrants. The family moved to [Islip, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islip,_New_York), where Scheffé went to high school. He graduated in 1924, took night classes at [Cooper Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union), and a year later entered the [Polytechnic Institute of Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Institute_of_New_York_University). He transferred to the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) in 1928, and earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics there in 1931.[^o4] Staying at Wisconsin, he married his wife Miriam in 1934 and finished his PhD in 1935, on the subject of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), under the supervision of [Rudolf Ernest Langer](wikipedia://en/38442527).[^o3][^o4]

After teaching mathematics at Wisconsin, [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University), and [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College), Scheffé moved to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1941. At Princeton, he began working in statistics instead of in pure mathematics, and assisted the U.S. war effort as a consultant with the [Office of Scientific Research and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Scientific_Research_and_Development). Scheffé moved several more times, to [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University) in 1944, the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1946, and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1948, where he chaired the statistics department. He settled at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) from 1953 until he retired in 1974; he took a turn as department chair there as well, from 1965 to 1968. After retiring from Berkeley, he spent more years on the faculty of [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University).[^o3][^o4]

In 1951 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o7] Scheffé was president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1954, and also served as vice president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) from 1954 to 1956.[^o4]

Scheffé died in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).
