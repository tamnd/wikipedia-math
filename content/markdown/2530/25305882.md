# Special values of L-functions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Special values of L-functions"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the study of **special values of $L$-functions** is a subfield of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) devoted to generalising formulae such as the [Leibniz formula for $\pi$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_formula_for_pi), namely

$$1 \,-\, \frac{1}{3} \,+\, \frac{1}{5} \,-\, \frac{1}{7} \,+\, \frac{1}{9} \,-\, \cdots \;=\; \frac{\pi}{4},\!$$

:::

by the recognition that expression on the left-hand side is equal to $L(1)$ where $L(s)$ is the [Dirichlet $L$-function](wikipedia://en/400092) for the field of [Gaussian rational](wikipedia://en/318052) numbers. The right-hand side then follows from the [analytic class number formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_class_number_formula), and the factor $\tfrac14$ appears because the Gaussian field has [class number 1](wikipedia://en/596282) and contains four [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="Conjectures"}

## Conjectures {#Conjectures}

There are two families of conjectures, formulated for general classes of [$L$-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) (the very general setting being for $L$-functions associated to [Chow motives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_motive) over [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field)), the division into two reflecting the questions of:

how to replace $\pi$ in the [Leibniz formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_formula_for_pi) by some other "transcendental" number (regardless of whether it is currently possible for [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698) to provide a proof of the transcendence); and how to generalise the rational factor in the formula (class number divided by number of roots of unity) by some algebraic construction of a rational number that will represent the ratio of the $L$-function value to the "transcendental" factor.

Subsidiary explanations are given for the integer values of $n$ for which formulae of this sort involving $L(n)$ can be expected to hold.

The conjectures for (a) are called *Beilinson's conjectures*, for [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613).[^o4][^o5] The idea is to abstract from the [regulator of a number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulator_of_a_number_field) to some "higher regulator" (the [Beilinson regulator](wikipedia://en/39654854)), a determinant constructed on a real vector space that comes from [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500).

The conjectures for (b) are called the *Bloch–Kato conjectures for special values*(for [Spencer Bloch](wikipedia://en/25794633) and [Kazuya Kato](wikipedia://en/4616818); this circle of ideas is distinct from the [Bloch–Kato conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloch–Kato_conjecture) of K-theory, extending the [Milnor conjecture](wikipedia://en/2914126), a proof of which was announced in 2009). They are also called the *Tamagawa number conjecture*, a name arising via the [Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer_conjecture) and its formulation as an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) analogue of the [Tamagawa number](wikipedia://en/1168653) problem for [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885).[^o6] In a further extension, the equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture (ETNC) has been formulated, to consolidate the connection of these ideas with [Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/396150), and its so-called [Main Conjecture](wikipedia://en/22091332).

### Current status {#Current_status}

All of these conjectures are known to be true only in special cases.
