# Horatio Scott Carslaw

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Horatio Scott Carslaw"}

*Image: Horatio Scott Carslaw.gif.* Horatio Scott Carslaw

Dr **Horatio Scott Carslaw** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) LLD (12 February 1870, [Helensburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensburgh), [Dunbartonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbartonshire), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) – 11 November 1954, [Burradoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burradoo), [New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia)) was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland)-[Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia) mathematician.[^o2] The book he wrote with his colleague [John Conrad Jaeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Conrad_Jaeger), *Conduction of Heat in Solids*, remains a classic in the field.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Carslaw was born in [Helensburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensburgh), Scotland, the son of the Rev Dr William Henderson Carslaw[^o5] (a Free Church minister) and his wife, Elizabeth Lockhead.[^o2] He was educated at [The Glasgow Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Glasgow_Academy). He went on to study at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) and then obtained a postgraduate doctorate at [Glasgow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_University).

## Career {#Career}

Carslaw was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1901.[^o7] He was a Fellow of [Emmanuel College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge) and worked as a lecturer in Mathematics at Glasgow University when, in late 1902, he moved to Australia.[^o8]

In 1903, upon the retirement of Theodore Thomas Gurney, Carslaw was appointed Professor and the Chair of Pure and Applied Mathematics in the now [School of Mathematics and Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_School_of_Mathematics_and_Statistics) at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney). He retired in 1935[^o10] to his house in Burradoo where he produced most of his best work.[^o2] The Carslaw Building at the University, completed in the 1960s and containing the school, is named after him.[^o11]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Carslaw married Ethel Maude Clarke (daughter of [Sir William Clarke, 1st Baronet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Clarke,_1st_Baronet)[^o2]) in 1907 but she died later in the same year.[^o7]

Carslaw died at home in [Burradoo, New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burradoo,_New_South_Wales) and was buried in the Anglican section of [Bowral Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowral_Cemetery) in [Bowral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowral), New South Wales.[^o2]

- with John Conrad Jaeger: *Operational methods in applied mathematics*, 1941,[^o16] 1948
- with Jaeger: *Conduction of Heat in Solids*, Oxford 1947, 1959
