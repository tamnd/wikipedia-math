# Serre's criterion for normality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Serre's criterion for normality"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), **Serre's criterion for normality**, introduced by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a commutative [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) *A* to be a [normal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_ring). The criterion involves the following two conditions for *A*:

:::

- $R_k: A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a [regular local ring](wikipedia://en/506959) for any prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of height ≤ *k*.
- $S_k: \operatorname{depth} A_{\mathfrak{p}} \ge \inf \{k, \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \}$ for any prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$.[^o2]

The statement is:

- *A* is a [reduced ring](wikipedia://en/539749) $\Leftrightarrow R_0, S_1$ hold.
- *A* is a normal ring $\Leftrightarrow R_1, S_2$ hold.
- *A* is a [Cohen–Macaulay ring](wikipedia://en/497407) $\Leftrightarrow S_k$ hold for all *k*.

Items 1, 3 trivially follow from the definitions. Item 2 is much deeper.

For an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), the criterion is due to [Krull](wikipedia://en/964328). The general case is due to Serre.

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

### Sufficiency {#Sufficiency}

(After EGA IV<sub>2</sub>. Theorem 5.8.6.)

Suppose *A* satisfies *S*<sub>2</sub> and *R*<sub>1</sub>. Then *A* in particular satisfies *S*<sub>1</sub> and *R*<sub>0</sub>; hence, it is reduced. If $\mathfrak{p}_i, \, 1 \le i \le r$ are the minimal prime ideals of *A*, then the [total ring of fractions](wikipedia://en/4276418) *K* of *A* is the direct product of the residue fields $\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_i) = Q(A/\mathfrak{p}_i)$: see [total ring of fractions of a reduced ring](wikipedia://en/4276418#Total_ring_of_fractions_of_a_reduced_ring). That means we can write $1 = e_1 + \dots + e_r$ where $e_i$ are idempotents in $\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_i)$ and such that $e_i e_j = 0, \, i \ne j$. Now, if *A* is integrally closed in *K*, then each $e_i$ is integral over *A* and so is in *A*; consequently, *A* is a direct product of integrally closed domains *Ae*<sub>*i*</sub>'s and we are done. Thus, it is enough to show that *A* is integrally closed in *K*.

For this end, suppose

$$(f/g)^n + a_1 (f/g)^{n-1} + \dots + a_n = 0$$

where all *f*, *g*, *a*<sub>*i*</sub>'s are in *A* and *g* is moreover a non-zerodivisor. We want to show:

$$f \in gA.$$

Now, the condition *S*<sub>2</sub> says that $gA$ is [unmixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmixed_theorem) of height one; i.e., each [associated primes](wikipedia://en/17286442) $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A/gA$ has height one. This is because if $\mathfrak{p}$ has height greater than one, then $\mathfrak{p}$ would contain a non [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441) in $A/gA$. However, $\mathfrak{p}$ is associated to the zero ideal in $A/gA$ so it can only contain zero divisors, see [here](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/3630663/zero-divisors-and-associated-primes-of-the-zero-ideal-in-a-noetherian-ring). By the condition *R*<sub>1</sub>, the localization $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is integrally closed and so $\phi(f) \in \phi(g)A_{\mathfrak{p}}$, where $\phi: A \to A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the localization map, since the [integral equation](wikipedia://en/9478630) persists after localization. If $gA = \cap_i \mathfrak{q}_i$ is the [primary decomposition](wikipedia://en/1208391), then, for any *i*, the radical of $\mathfrak{q}_i$ is an associated prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A/gA$ and so $f \in \phi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}_i A_{\mathfrak{p}}) = \mathfrak{q}_i$; the equality here is because $\mathfrak{q}_i$ is a $\mathfrak{p}$-[primary ideal](wikipedia://en/3021435). Hence, the assertion holds.

### Necessity {#Necessity}

Suppose *A* is a [normal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_ring). For *S*<sub>2</sub>, let $\mathfrak{p}$ be an associated prime of $A/fA$ for a non-zerodivisor *f*; we need to show it has height one. Replacing *A* by a localization, we can assume *A* is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$. By definition, there is an element *g* in *A* such that $\mathfrak{p} = \{ x \in A | xg \equiv 0 \text{ mod }fA \}$ and $g \not\in fA$. Put *y* = *g*/*f* in the total ring of fractions. If $y \mathfrak{p} \subset \mathfrak{p}$, then $\mathfrak{p}$ is a faithful $A[y]$-module and is a finitely generated *A*-module; consequently, $y$ is integral over *A* and thus in *A*, a contradiction. Hence, $y \mathfrak{p} = A$ or $\mathfrak{p} = f/g A$, which implies $\mathfrak{p}$ has height one ([Krull's principal ideal theorem](wikipedia://en/542421)).

For *R*<sub>1</sub>, we argue in the same way: let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) of height one. Localizing at $\mathfrak{p}$ we assume $\mathfrak{p}$ is a [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) and the similar argument as above shows that $\mathfrak{p}$ is in fact principal. Thus, *A* is a regular local ring. $\square$

:::
