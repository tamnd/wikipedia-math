# Namioka's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Namioka's theorem"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), **Namioka's theorem** is a result concerning the relationship between [separate continuity and joint continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separate_and_joint_continuity) of functions defined on [product spaces](wikipedia://en/14856). Named after [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Isaac Namioka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Namioka), who proved it in his 1974 paper *Separate Continuity and Joint Continuity* published in the [Pacific Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Journal_of_Mathematics), the theorem establishes conditions under which a separately continuous function must be jointly continuous on a topologically large subset of its [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

> **Namioka's theorem**. Let $X$ be a [Čech-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Čech-complete) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) (such as a [complete metric space](wikipedia://en/7832)), $Y$ be a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637), and $Z$ be a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). If $f: X \times Y \rightarrow Z$ is *separately continuous*, meaning that
>
> for each fixed $x_0 \in X$, the function $y \mapsto f(x_0, y)$ is continuous on $Y$, and
>
> for each fixed $y_0 \in Y$, the function $x \mapsto f(x, y_0)$ is continuous on $X$, then there exists a [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) [$G_\delta$-subset](wikipedia://en/252076) $G$ of $X$ such that $f$ is jointly continuous at each point of $G \times Y$.[^o2][^o5]

Namioka's theorem can be equivalently stated in terms of the set $C(f)$ of points where $f$ is continuous, stating that the [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) of $C(f)$ onto $X$ contains a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $X$.[^o2][^o5]

:::

## History {#History}

French mathematician [René Baire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Baire) was among the first to systematically study the relationship between separate and joint continuity in 1899, for [real-valued functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-valued_functions) of real variables.[^o7] Austrian mathematician [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212) later extended these investigations in 1932, proving similar results for functions defined on complete metric spaces.[^o8] Namioka generalized these results to [non-metrizable spaces](wikipedia://en/19738), particularly to Čech-complete spaces, which include all complete metric spaces as a [special case](wikipedia://en/31308010). There exists a proof using tools from [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) such as the Arkhangel'skii–Frolík covering theorem and the [Kuratowski and Ryll-Nardzewski measurable selection theorem](wikipedia://en/46310740).[^o5]
