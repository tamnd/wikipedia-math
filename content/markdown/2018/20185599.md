# Frank Morgan (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Morgan"}

**Frank Morgan** is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and the Webster Atwell '21 Professor of Mathematics, emeritus, at [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College). He is known for contributions to [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694), [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734), and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), including the resolution of the [double bubble conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bubble_conjecture). He was vice-president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o3] and the Mathematical Association of America.

:::

Morgan studied at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), and received his Ph.D. from Princeton in 1977, under the supervision of [Frederick J. Almgren Jr.](wikipedia://en/12998276) He taught at MIT for ten years before joining the Williams faculty.[^o4][^o5]

In 1993 Morgan received the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o6]

Morgan is the founder of SMALL, one of the largest and best known summer undergraduate Mathematics research programs. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7]

Frank Morgan is also an avid dancer. He gained fame for his work "Dancing the Parkway".[^o8]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

*Image: Double bubble.png.* Double bubble

He is known for proving, in collaboration with [Michael Hutchings](wikipedia://en/38294757), Manuel Ritoré, and Antonio Ros, the [Double Bubble conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Bubble_conjecture), which states that the minimum-surface-area enclosure of two given volumes is formed by three spherical patches meeting at 120-degree angles at a common circle.

He has also made contributions to the study of manifolds with density, which are [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) together with a measure of volume which is deformed from the standard Riemannian volume form. Such deformed volume measures suggest modifications of the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) of the Riemannian manifold, as introduced by [Dominique Bakry](wikipedia://en/44572547) and Michel Émery.[^o10] Morgan showed how to modify the classical Heintze-Karcher inequality, which controls the volume of certain cylindrical regions in the space by the Ricci curvature in the region and the [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) of the region's cross-section, to hold in the setting of manifolds with density. As a corollary, he was also able to put the Levy-Gromov [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182) into this setting. Much of his current work deals with various aspects of isoperimetric inequalities and manifolds with density.
