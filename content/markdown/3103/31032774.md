# Pythagorean field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pythagorean field"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **Pythagorean field** is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) in which every sum of two squares is a square: equivalently it has a [Pythagoras number](wikipedia://en/36818246) equal to 1. A **Pythagorean extension** of a field $F$ is an extension obtained by adjoining an element $\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}$ for some $\lambda$ in $F$. So a Pythagorean field is one [closed under](wikipedia://en/293802) taking Pythagorean extensions. For any field $F$ there is a minimal Pythagorean field $F^{\mathrm{py}}$ containing it, unique [up to isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_to_isomorphism), called its **Pythagorean closure**.[^o2] The *Hilbert field* is the minimal ordered Pythagorean field.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every [Euclidean field](wikipedia://en/12756005) (an [ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430) in which all non-negative elements are squares) is an ordered Pythagorean field, but the converse does not hold.[^o6] A [quadratically closed field](wikipedia://en/36823185) is Pythagorean field but not conversely ($\mathbf{R}$ is Pythagorean); however, a non [formally real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_real) Pythagorean field is quadratically closed.[^o7]

The [Witt ring](wikipedia://en/5578523) of a Pythagorean field is of order 2 if the field is not [formally real](wikipedia://en/1067223), and torsion-free otherwise.[^o2] For a field $F$ there is an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) involving the [Witt rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_ring_%28forms%29)

$$0 \rightarrow \operatorname{Tor} I W(F) \rightarrow W(F) \rightarrow W(F^{\mathrm{py}})$$

where $IW(F)$ is the fundamental ideal of the Witt ring of $F$[^o8] and $\operatorname{Tor} IW(F)$ denotes its [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052) (which is just the [nilradical](wikipedia://en/289636) of $W(F)$).[^o9]

### Equivalent conditions {#Equivalent_conditions}

Finding mistakes The following conditions on a field *F* are equivalent to *F* being Pythagorean:

- The [general *u*-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_u-invariant) *u*(*F*) is 0 or 1.[^o11]
- If *ab* is not a square in *F* then there is an order on *F* for which *a*, *b* have different signs.[^o12]
- *F* is the intersection of its [Euclidean closures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_closure).[^o13]

:::

## Models of geometry {#Models_of_geometry}

Pythagorean fields can be used to construct models for some of [Hilbert's axioms](wikipedia://en/838151) for geometry (Iyanaga Kawada 1980, 163 C). The coordinate geometry given by $F^n$ for $F$ a Pythagorean field satisfies many of Hilbert's axioms, such as the incidence axioms, the congruence axioms and the axioms of parallels. However, in general this geometry need not satisfy all Hilbert's axioms unless the field *F* has extra properties: for example, if the field is also ordered then the geometry will satisfy Hilbert's ordering axioms, and if the field is also complete the geometry will satisfy Hilbert's completeness axiom.

The Pythagorean closure of a [non-archimedean ordered field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-archimedean_ordered_field), such as the Pythagorean closure of the field of [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) $\mathbf{Q}(x)$ in one variable over the rational numbers $\mathbf{Q},$ can be used to construct non-archimedean geometries that satisfy many of Hilbert's axioms but not his axiom of completeness.[^o16] Dehn used such a field to construct two [Dehn planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehn_planes), examples of [non-Legendrian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Legendrian_geometry) and [semi-Euclidean geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-Euclidean_geometry) respectively, in which there are many lines though a point not intersecting a given line but where the sum of the angles of a triangle is at least π.[^o17]

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Diller–Dress theorem"}

## Diller–Dress theorem {#Diller–Dress_theorem}

This theorem states that if *E*/*F* is a finite [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634), and *E* is Pythagorean, then so is *F*.[^o20] As a consequence, no [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) is Pythagorean, since all such fields are finite over **Q**, which is not Pythagorean.[^o21]

:::

::: {#o23 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Superpythagorean"}

## Superpythagorean fields {#Superpythagorean_fields}

A **superpythagorean field** *F* is a formally real field with the property that if *S* is a subgroup of index 2 in *F*<sup>∗</sup> and does not contain −1, then *S* defines an ordering on *F*. An equivalent definition is that *F* is a formally real field in which the set of squares forms a [fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_%28order%29). A superpythagorean field is necessarily Pythagorean.[^o20]

The analogue of the Diller–Dress theorem holds: if *E*/*F* is a finite extension and *E* is superpythagorean then so is *F*.[^o24] In the opposite direction, if *F* is superpythagorean and *E* is a formally real field containing *F* and contained in the quadratic closure of *F* then *E* is superpythagorean.[^o25]

:::
