# André plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="André plane"}

In mathematics, **André planes** are a class of finite [translation planes](wikipedia://en/2628762) found by André.[^o2] The [Desarguesian plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desarguesian_plane) and the [Hall planes](wikipedia://en/35791745) are examples of André planes; the two-dimensional regular [nearfield](wikipedia://en/4033255) planes are also André planes.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Let $F = GF(q)$ be a finite [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and let $K = GF(q^n)$ be a degree $n$ [extension field](wikipedia://en/11634) of $F$. Let $\Gamma$ be the group of [field automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_automorphism) of $K$ over $F$, and let $\beta$ be an arbitrary mapping from $F$ to $\Gamma$ such that $\beta(1)=1$. Finally, let $N$ be the [norm](wikipedia://en/450555) function from $K$ to $F$.

Define a quasifield $Q$ with the same elements and addition as K, but with multiplication defined via $a \circ b = a^{\beta(N(b))} \cdot b$, where $\cdot$ denotes the normal field multiplication in $K$. Using this [quasifield](wikipedia://en/4029839) to [construct a plane](wikipedia://en/2628762#Algebraic_Construction_with_Coordinates) yields an André plane.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

1. André planes exist for all proper prime powers $p^n$ with $p$ prime and $n$ a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) greater than one.
1. Non-Desarguesian André planes exist for all proper prime powers except for $2^n$ where $n$ is prime.

:::

## Small examples {#Small_examples}

For planes of order 25 and below, classification of Andrè planes is a consequence of either theoretical calculations or computer searches which have determined all translation planes of a given order:

- The smallest non-Desarguesian André plane has order 9, and it is isomorphic to the [Hall plane](wikipedia://en/35791745#The_Hall_plane_of_order_9) of that order.
- The translation planes of order 16 have all been classified, and again the only non-Desarguesian André plane is the [Hall plane](wikipedia://en/35791745).[^o9]
- There are three non-Desarguesian André planes of order 25.[^o10] These are the [Hall plane](wikipedia://en/35791745), the regular [nearfield](wikipedia://en/4033255) plane, and a third plane not constructible by other techniques.[^o11]
- There is a single non-Desarguesian André plane of order 27.[^o12]

Enumeration of Andrè planes specifically has been performed for other small orders:[^o13]
