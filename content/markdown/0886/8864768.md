::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amoeba"}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **amoeba** is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) associated with a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) in one or more [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). Amoebas have applications in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), especially [tropical geometry](wikipedia://en/867041).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider the function

$$\operatorname{Log}: \big({\mathbb C} \setminus \{0\}\big)^n \to \mathbb R^n$$

defined on the set of all *n*-[tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) $z = (z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n)$ of non-zero [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) with values in the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb R^n,$ given by the formula

$$\operatorname{Log}(z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n) = \big(\log|z_1|, \log|z_2|, \dots, \log|z_n|\big).$$

Here, log denotes the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476). If *p*(*z*) is a polynomial in $n$ complex variables, its **amoeba** $\mathcal A_p$ is defined as the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of the set of [zeros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) of *p* under Log, so

$$\mathcal A_p = \left\{\operatorname{Log}(z) : z \in \big(\mathbb C \setminus \{0\}\big)^n, p(z) = 0\right\}.$$

Amoebas were introduced in 1994 in a book by [Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643), Kapranov, and [Zelevinsky](wikipedia://en/32415867).[^o4]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $V \subset (\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}$ be the zero locus of a polynomial

$$f(z) = \sum_{j \in A}a_{j}z^{j}$$

where $A \subset \mathbb{Z}^{n}$ is finite, $a_{j} \in \mathbb{C}$ and $z^{j} = z_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{j_{n}}$ if $z = (z_{1},\dots,z_{n})$ and $j = (j_{1},\dots,j_{n})$. Let $\Delta_{f}$ be the [Newton polyhedron](wikipedia://en/742477) of $f$, i.e.,

$$\Delta_{f} = \text{Convex Hull}\{j \in A \mid a_{j} \ne 0\}.$$

Then

- Any amoeba is a [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279).
- Any [connected component](wikipedia://en/6233) of the [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) $\mathbb R^n \setminus \mathcal A_p$ is [convex](wikipedia://en/6292).[^o7]
- The area of an amoeba of a not identically zero polynomial in two complex variables is finite.
- A two-dimensional amoeba has a number of "tentacles", which are infinitely long and exponentially narrow towards infinity.
- The number of connected components of the complement $\mathbb{R}^{n} \setminus \mathcal{A}_{p}$ is not greater than $\#(\Delta_{f} \cap \mathbb{Z}^{n})$ and not less than the number of vertices of $\Delta_{f}$.[^o7]
- There is an injection from the set of connected components of complement $\mathbb{R}^{n} \setminus \mathcal{A}_{p}$ to $\Delta_{f} \cap \mathbb{Z}^{n}$. The vertices of $\Delta_{f}$ are in the image under this injection. A connected component of complement $\mathbb{R}^{n} \setminus \mathcal{A}_{p}$ is bounded if and only if its image is in the interior of $\Delta_{f}$.[^o7]
- If $V \subset (\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}$, then the area of $\mathcal{A}_{p}(V)$ is not greater than $\pi^{2}\text{Area}(\Delta_{f})$.[^o7]

:::

## Ronkin function {#Ronkin_function}

A useful tool in studying amoebas is the **Ronkin function**. For *p*(*z*), a polynomial in *n* complex variables, one defines the Ronkin function

$$N_p : \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R$$

by the formula

$$N_p(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^n} \int_{\operatorname{Log}^{-1}(x)} \log|p(z)| \,\frac{dz_1}{z_1} \wedge \frac{dz_2}{z_2} \wedge \cdots \wedge \frac{dz_n}{z_n},$$

where $x$ denotes $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n).$ Equivalently, $N_p$ is given by the integral

$$N_p(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^n} \int_{[0, 2\pi]^n} \log|p(z)| \,d\theta_1 \,d\theta_2 \cdots d\theta_n,$$

where

$$z = \left(e^{x_1+i\theta_1}, e^{x_2+i\theta_2}, \dots, e^{x_n+i\theta_n}\right).$$

The Ronkin function is convex and [affine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_function) on each connected component of the complement of the amoeba of $p(z)$.[^o9]

As an example, the Ronkin function of a [monomial](wikipedia://en/357416)

$$p(z) = a z_1^{k_1} z_2^{k_2} \dots z_n^{k_n}$$

with $a \ne 0$ is

$$N_p(x) = \log|a| + k_1 x_1 + k_2 x_2 + \cdots + k_n x_n.$$
