# Emilio Artom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Emilio Artom"}

**Emilio Artom** (9 November 1888 – 11 December 1952) was a Jewish [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who was born and died in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin). For two years he was assistant to [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661) in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna) and subsequently became a high-school teacher.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in a Jewish family of modest economic conditions. Immediately after graduation he stayed two years at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) as an assistant of [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661), but he renounced in 1911 convinced that, as he had produced little, it was better to start teaching.

In 1911 he won the Chair at school and became a professor at the Teaching Institute of Aosta. In 1914 he married Amalia Artom (also a mathematician) and they had two children: historian and [partisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement) Emanuele (1915–1944) and Ennio (1920–1940), who died doing mountain trekking. In 1920 he taught at a technical institute in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) and subsequently at the Scientific High School "Galileo Ferraris", also in Turin.

Immediately after [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), Artom produced the scientific publications which would gain him the 1930 [Mathesis award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathesis_award) for a history of [conic sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic_sections) in elementary mathematics.

Almost resolved to a more sustained effort in scientific research, he could not however obtain a [professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship) due to political reasons, as he was not willing to join the [National Fascist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Fascist_Party). Artom hence committed himself to linguistic studies.

The tragic events of the family and the entire Italian Jewish community after the 1938 [Italian Racial Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Racial_Laws) kept him permanently away from the study of mathematics. He died in Turin in 1952.
