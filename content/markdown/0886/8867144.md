# Willem Abraham Wythoff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Willem Abraham Wythoff"}

**Willem Abraham Wythoff** (; born **Wijthoff**; 6 October 1865 – 21 May 1939) was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Wythoff was born in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) to Anna C. F. Kerkhoven and Abraham Willem Wijthoff,[^o4] who worked in a sugar refinery.[^o5] He studied at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), and earned his Ph.D. in 1898 under the supervision of [Diederik Korteweg](wikipedia://en/1978798).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Wythoff is known in [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) for his study of [Wythoff's game](wikipedia://en/10222175), whose solution involves the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number).[^o5] The [Wythoff array](wikipedia://en/36264444), a two-dimensional array of numbers related to this game and to the Fibonacci sequence, is also named after him.[^o8][^o9]

In geometry, Wythoff is known for the [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) of [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635) and [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra) and for the [Wythoff symbol](wikipedia://en/3691548) used as a notation for these geometric objects.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Willem Abraham Wythoff was the youngest of four children born into the wealthy family of Abraham Willem Wijthoff and Anna Catharina Frederika Kerkhoven. His father was a Lutheran and son of the Amsterdam sugar refinery family Wijthoff & Son. His older sisters were: mathematician [Geertruida "Truida" Wijthoff](wikipedia://en/77197950), writer Henriëtte Wijthoff and illustrator Anna Catharina Frederika Wijthoff.[^o11]
