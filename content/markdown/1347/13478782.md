# The Mathematical Experience

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Mathematical Experience"}

***The Mathematical Experience*** (1981) is a book by [Philip J. Davis](wikipedia://en/3753768) and [Reuben Hersh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuben_Hersh) that discusses the practice of modern [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from a [historical](wikipedia://en/14220) and [philosophical](wikipedia://en/46439) perspective. The book discusses the psychology of mathematicians, and gives examples of famous proofs and outstanding problems. It goes on to speculate about what a proof really means, in relationship to actual truth. Other topics include mathematics in education and some of the math that occurs in computer science.

:::

The first paperback edition won a U.S. [National Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award) in Science.[^o3][^o4] It is cited by some mathematicians as influential in their decision to continue their studies in [graduate school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_school); and has been hailed as a classic of mathematical literature.[^o5] On the other hand, [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) disagreed with some of the authors' philosophical opinions.[^o6]

A new edition, published in 1995, includes exercises and problems, making the book more suitable for classrooms. There is also *The Companion Guide to The Mathematical Experience, Study Edition*. Both were co-authored with [Elena Marchisotto](wikipedia://en/59028633).[^o7] Davis and Hersh wrote a follow-up book, *Descartes' Dream: The World According to Mathematics* ([Harcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harcourt_%28publisher%29), 1986), and each has written other books with related themes, such as *Mathematics And Common Sense: A Case of Creative Tension* by Davis and *What is Mathematics, Really?* by Hersh.
