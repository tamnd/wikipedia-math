# Shlomo Sternberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shlomo Sternberg"}

**Shlomo Zvi Sternberg** (January 20, 1936 – August 23, 2024) was an American mathematician known for his work in geometry, particularly [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) and [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681). He also wrote some well-known textbooks.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Sternberg earned his PhD in 1955 from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), with a thesis entitled *Some Problems in Discrete Nonlinear Transformations in One and Two Dimensions*, supervised by [Aurel Wintner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurel_Wintner).[^o4]

After postdoctoral work at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) (1956–1957) and an instructorship at [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) (1957–1959), Sternberg joined the Mathematics Department at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1959, where he was George Putnam Professor of Pure and Applied Mathematics until 2017. Since 2017, he was Emeritus Professor at the Harvard Mathematics Department.[^o5]

Sternberg was awarded a [Guggenheim fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Guggenheim_Fellowships_awarded_in_1974) in 1974[^o6] and an honorary doctorate by the [University of Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mannheim) in 1991.[^o7][^o8] He delivered the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) [Colloquium Lecture](wikipedia://en/72818332) in 1990[^o9] and the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University)'s Albert Einstein Memorial Lecture in 2006.[^o10]

Sternberg was elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1969,[^o11] of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1986,[^o12] of the [Spanish Royal Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Royal_Academy_of_Sciences) In 1999,[^o13] and of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 2010.[^o14]

## Research {#Research}

Sternberg's first well-known published result, based on his PhD thesis, is known as the "Sternberg linearization theorem" which asserts that a [smooth map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_map) near a [hyperbolic fixed point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_fixed_point) can be made linear by a smooth change of coordinates provided that certain non-resonance conditions are satisfied. He also proved generalizations of the Birkhoff canonical form theorems for volume preserving mappings in n-dimensions and symplectic mappings, all in the smooth case.[^o16][^o17][^o18]

In the 1960s, Sternberg became involved with [Isadore Singer](wikipedia://en/324564) in the project of revisiting [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274)'s papers from the early 1900s on the classification of the simple transitive infinite Lie [pseudogroups](wikipedia://en/1255458), and of relating Cartan's results to recent results in the theory of [G-structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure) and supplying rigorous (by present-day standards) proofs of his main theorems.[^o19] Together with [Victor Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636) and [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519), he extended this classification to a larger class of pseudogroups: the primitive infinite pseudogroups. As a by-product, they also obtained the "integrability of characteristics" theorem for [over-determined systems](wikipedia://en/10160606) of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).[^o20]

Sternberg provided contributions also to the topic of [Lie group actions](wikipedia://en/39904936) on [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356), in particular involving various aspects of the theory of symplectic reduction. For instance, together with [Bertram Kostant](wikipedia://en/3520590) he showed how to use reduction techniques to give a rigorous mathematical treatment of what is known in the physics literature as the [BRST quantization](wikipedia://en/6769980) procedure.[^o21] Together with [David Kazhdan](wikipedia://en/3052833) and [Bertram Kostant](wikipedia://en/3520590), he showed how one can simplify the analysis of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) of Calogero type by describing them as [symplectic reductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_reduction) of much simpler systems.[^o22] Together with [Victor Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636) he gave the first rigorous formulation and proof of a hitherto vague assertion about Lie group actions on symplectic manifolds, namely the [Quantization commutes with reduction](wikipedia://en/44977826) conjecture.[^o23] This last work was also the inspiration for a result in equivariant symplectic geometry that disclosed for the first time a surprising and unexpected connection between the theory of Hamiltonian [torus actions](wikipedia://en/41405104) on [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) symplectic manifolds and the theory of [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885). This theorem, the "AGS convexity theorem," was simultaneously proved by Guillemin-Sternberg[^o24] and [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698)[^o25] in the early 1980s.

Sternberg's contributions to symplectic geometry and Lie theory have also included a number of basic textbooks on these subjects, among them the three graduate level texts with [Victor Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636): "Geometric Asymptotics,"[^o26] "Symplectic Techniques in Physics",[^o27] and "Semi-Classical Analysis".[^o28] His "Lectures on Differential Geometry"[^o29] is a popular standard textbook for upper-level undergraduate courses on [differential manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_manifold), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681) and the geometry of [G-structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure). He also published the more recent "[Curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) in mathematics and physics".[^o30]

Sternberg worked with [Yuval Ne'eman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuval_Ne'eman) on [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636) in [elementary particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle_physics), exploring from this perspective the [Higgs mechanism](wikipedia://en/644550), the method of spontaneous symmetry breaking and a unified approach to the theory of [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarks) and [leptons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptons).[^o31]

## Religion {#Religion}

Sternberg was Jewish and an orthodox [rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi).[^o11]

## Death {#Death}

Sternberg died in the old city of Jerusalem, on August 23, 2024. His funeral took place at Eretz Hachayim Cemetery in Beit Shemesh Israel on August 25, 2024.[^o34]
