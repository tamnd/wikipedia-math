# Artin conductor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artin conductor"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Artin conductor** is a number or [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) associated to a [character](wikipedia://en/691741) of a [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of a [local](wikipedia://en/145375) or [global](wikipedia://en/288358) [field](wikipedia://en/10603), introduced by [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351)[^o2][^o3] as an expression appearing in the [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377) of an [Artin L-function](wikipedia://en/2774171).

:::

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Local Artin conductor"}

## Local Artin conductors {#Local_Artin_conductors}

Suppose that $L$ is a finite [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of the local field $K$, with Galois group $G$. If $\chi$ is a character of $G$, then the Artin conductor of $\chi$ is the number

$$f(\chi)=\sum_{i\ge 0}\frac{g_i}{g_0}(\chi(1)-\chi(G_i)),$$

where $G_i$ is the $i$-th [ramification group](wikipedia://en/501614) (in [lower numbering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_numbering)), of order $g_i$, and $\chi(G_i)$ is the average value of $\chi$ on $G_i$.[^n1] The local conductor is an integer.[^n2][^n3] Heuristically, the Artin conductor measures how far the action of the higher ramification groups is from being trivial. In particular, if $\chi$ is unramified, then its Artin conductor is zero. Thus, if $L$ is unramified over $K$, then the Artin conductors of all $\chi$ are zero.

The *wild invariant*[^n3] or *Swan conductor*[^n4] of the character is

$$f(\chi) - (\chi(1)-\chi(G_0)),$$

in other words, the sum of the higher order terms with $i>0$.

:::

::: {#o12 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Global Artin conductor"}

## Global Artin conductors {#Global_Artin_conductors}

The **global Artin conductor** of a representation $\chi$ of the Galois group $G$ of a finite extension $L/K$ of global fields is an ideal of $K$, defined to be

$$\mathfrak{f}(\chi) = \prod_p p^{f(\chi,p)}$$

where the product is over the primes $p$ of $K$, and $f(\chi,p)$ is the local Artin conductor of the restriction of $\chi$ to the decomposition group of some prime of $L$ lying over $p$.[^n2] Since the local Artin conductor is zero at unramified primes, the above product only need be taken over primes that ramify in $L/K$.

:::

::: {#o15 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Artin character"}

## Artin representation and Artin character {#Artin_representation_and_Artin_character}

Suppose that $L$ is a finite Galois extension of the local field $K$, with Galois group $G$. The **Artin character** $a_G$ of $G$ is the character

$$a_G=\sum_\chi f(\chi)\chi$$

and the **Artin representation** $A_G$ is the complex linear representation of *G* with this character. (Weil 1946) asked for a direct construction of the Artin representation. (txt) showed that the Artin representation can be realized over the local field $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, for any prime $\ell$ not equal to the residue characteristic $p$. (Fontaine 1971) showed that it can be realized over the corresponding ring of Witt vectors. It cannot in general be realized over the rationals or over the local field $\mathbb{Q}_p$, suggesting that there is no easy way to construct the Artin representation explicitly.[^n5]

:::

::: {#o21 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Swan character"}

## Swan representation {#Swan_representation}

The **Swan character** *sw*<sub>*G*</sub> is given by

$$sw_G= a_G -r_G+1$$

where *r*<sub>*g*</sub> is the character of the regular representation and 1 is the character of the trivial representation.[^n6] The Swan character is the character of a representation of *G*. (txt) showed that there is a unique [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) representation of *G* over the [*l*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer) with character the Swan character.

:::

::: {#o25 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Artin conductor appears in the [conductor-discriminant formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductor-discriminant_formula) for the discriminant of a global field.[^n5]

The optimal level in the [Serre modularity conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_modularity_conjecture) is expressed in terms of the Artin conductor.

The Artin conductor appears in the functional equation of the [Artin L-function](wikipedia://en/2774171).

The Artin and Swan representations are used to define the [conductor of an elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/43604164) or abelian variety.

:::
