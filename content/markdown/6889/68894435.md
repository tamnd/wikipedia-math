# Maximilian Curtze

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maximilian Curtze"}

**Ernst Ludwig Wilhelm Maximilian Curtze** (4 August 1837 – 3 January 1903) was a German mathematician and [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_mathematics). He translated many classical mathematical texts.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Curtze was born in [Ballenstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballenstedt), in the Principality of [Anhalt-Bernburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhalt-Bernburg), the fourth son of physician Eduard Curtze and Johanna Nicolai. After attending the Carolinum grammar school at [Bernburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernburg), he graduated from the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald) in 1857 after training under [Johann August Grunert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_August_Grunert). He then passed the examination for teachers in 1861 and taught at the [Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń) (now Torn, Poland) Gymnasium until his retirement in 1896. A colleague was the Copernicus scholar [Leopold Prove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Prowe). His knowledge of languages and mathematics led him to examine works published in the Middle Ages. He translated the works of [Nicole Oresme](wikipedia://en/593659) - *Algorismus proportionum* (1868), Anartius' commentary on Euclid, Peter of Dacia's commentary on Sacrobosco's Algorisms (1897) and other materials which he collected into his 1902 publication *Urkunden zur Geschichte der Mathematik im Mittelalter und der Renaissance*.[^o4]
