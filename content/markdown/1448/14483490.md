# Radicial morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radicial morphism"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a [morphism](wikipedia://en/7482797) of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754)

:::

*f*: *X* → *Y*

is called **radicial** or **universally injective**, if, for every field *K*, the induced map *X*(*K*) → *Y*(*K*) is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) (EGA I (1960), (3.5.4)), (EGA I (1971), (3.7.2)). This is a generalization of the notion of a [purely inseparable extension](wikipedia://en/4770099) of fields (sometimes called a [radicial extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicial_extension), which should not be confused with a [radical extension](wikipedia://en/33085116)).

It suffices to check this for *K* [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed).

This is equivalent to the following condition: *f* is injective on the topological spaces and for every point *x* in *X*, the extension of the [residue fields](wikipedia://en/10789680)

*k*(*f*(*x*)) ⊂ *k*(*x*)

is radicial, i.e. purely inseparable.

It is also equivalent to every [base change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_change_%28scheme_theory%29) of *f* being injective on the underlying topological spaces. (Thus the term *universally injective*.)

Radicial morphisms are stable under composition, products and base change. If *gf* is radicial, so is *f*.
