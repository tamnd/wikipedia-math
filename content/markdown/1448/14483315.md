# Bonnor–Ebert mass

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bonnor–Ebert mass"}

In [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics), the **Bonnor–Ebert mass** is the largest mass that an [isothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isothermal) gas sphere embedded in a pressurized medium can have while still remaining in [hydrostatic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/51776). Clouds of gas with masses greater than the Bonnor–Ebert mass must inevitably undergo [gravitational collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_collapse) to form much smaller and denser objects.[^o2][^o3] As the gravitational collapse of an interstellar gas cloud is the first stage in the formation of a [protostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protostar), the Bonnor–Ebert mass is an important quantity in the study of [star formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_formation).[^o4]

:::

For a gas cloud embedded in a medium with a [gas pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_pressure) $p_{0}$, the Bonnor–Ebert mass is given by[^o5]

$$M_{BE} (p_0)={225\over {32 \sqrt{5 \pi}}}{c_s^4\over {(aG)}^{3 / 2}} {1\over \sqrt{p_0}}$$

where G is the [gravitational constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_constant) and $c_s \equiv \sqrt{kT/{\mu m_H}}$ is the [isothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isothermal)[sound speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_speed) ($\gamma = 1$) with $\mu$ as the molecular mass. $a$ is a [dimensionless constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity) which varies based on the density distribution of the cloud. For a uniform mass density $a=1$ and for a centrally peaked density $a\approx 1.67$.[^o5]
