# Two-sample hypothesis testing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Two-sample hypothesis testing"}

In [statistical hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing), a **two-sample test** is a test performed on the data of two [random samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_sample), each [independently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paired_data) obtained from a different given [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_%28statistics%29). The purpose of the test is to determine whether the difference between these two populations is [statistically significant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistically_significant). When the only knowledge of the two populations is given through the samples, two-sample hypothesis testing becomes an instance of [universal hypothesis testing](wikipedia://en/82255159).

:::

There are a large number of statistical tests that can be used in a two-sample test. Which one(s) are appropriate depend on a variety of factors, such as:

- Which assumptions (if any) may be made *a priori* about the [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28statistics%29) from which the data have been sampled? For example, in many situations it may be assumed that the underlying distributions are [normal distributions](wikipedia://en/21462). In other cases the data are [categorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_data), coming from a [discrete distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_distribution) over a [nominal scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_scale), such as which entry was selected from a menu.
- Does the hypothesis being tested apply to the distributions as a whole, or just some [population parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_parameter), for example the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean#Mean_of_a_probability_distribution) or the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344)?
- Is the [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_hypothesis) being tested merely that there is a difference in the relevant population characteristics (in which case a [two-sided test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-sided_test) may be indicated), or does it involve a specific bias ("A is better than B"), so that a [one-sided test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-sided_test) can be used?

Two-sample testing does not mean that only two random variables can be used for inference (as is sometimes incorrectly assumed). Instead, it gets its name because the problem involves data from two populations, distinct from one-sample hypothesis testing in which samples from a single population are used to make a decision.[^o2]

## Relevant tests {#Relevant_tests}

Statistical tests that may apply for two-sample testing include:

- [Hotelling's T-squared distribution#Two-sample statistic](wikipedia://en/934711#Two-sample_statistic)
- [Kernel embedding of distributions#Kernel two-sample test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_embedding_of_distributions#Kernel_two-sample_test)
- [Kolmogorov–Smirnov\_test#Two-sample\_Kolmogorov–Smirnov\_test](wikipedia://en/16714#Two-sample_Kolmogorov–Smirnov_test)
- [Kuiper's test](wikipedia://en/16720)
- [Median test](wikipedia://en/236641)
- [Pearson's chi-squared test](wikipedia://en/226676)
- [Student's t-test#Two-sample\_t-tests](wikipedia://en/536080#Two-sample_t-tests)
- [Welch's t-test](wikipedia://en/7849883)
- [Tukey–Duckworth test](wikipedia://en/47816198)
- [Mann–Whitney U test](wikipedia://en/433164)
- [Two-proportion Z-test](wikipedia://en/78552463)
- [Classifier Two-sample test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classifier_Two-sample_test) (C2ST) [^o4]
- Plug in statistics using [empirical measures](wikipedia://en/5647670)[^o5] (usually for discrete distributions)

## Mathematical Definition {#Mathematical_Definition}

Given samples $X^n = (X_1, \dots, X_n)$ taken [independently and identically distributed](wikipedia://en/30876902) (iid) from a population $P$, and $Y^m = (Y_1, \dots, Y_m)$ taken iid from a population $Q$, the goal of a two sample test is to determine which of the two hypothesis are correct:

$$\begin{aligned} H_0: P = Q\\ H_1: P \neq Q \end{aligned}$$

In many instances of two-sample hypothesis testing, the only information that we have about the populations comes from the samples, meaning that any test needs to be [non-parametric](wikipedia://en/223369).

## Optimal Error Exponent {#Optimal_Error_Exponent}

When considering tests with fixed (or vanishing) [false positive rate](wikipedia://en/5657877), the best achievable exponent for the false negative rate is the log of the [Bhattacharya distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhattacharya_distance)[^o8] or equivalently with the [Renyi divergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renyi_divergence)[^o5]. The first test known to achieve this optimal error exponent was based on maximum mean discrepancy.[^o9]
