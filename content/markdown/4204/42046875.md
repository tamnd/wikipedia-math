# Nilpotent algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nilpotent algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a **nilpotent algebra over a commutative ring** is an [algebra over a commutative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29), in which for some positive integer *n* every product containing at least *n* elements of the algebra is zero. The concept of a [nilpotent Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/3358867) has a different definition, which depends upon the [Lie bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_bracket). (There is no Lie bracket for many algebras over commutative rings; a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) involves its Lie bracket, whereas, there is no Lie bracket defined in the general case of an algebra over a commutative ring.) Another possible source of confusion in terminology is the *quantum nilpotent algebra*,[^o2] a concept related to [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) and [Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/325714).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

An [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) $A$ over a commutative ring $R$ is defined to be a **nilpotent algebra** if and only if there exists some positive integer $n$ such that $0=y_1\ y_2\ \cdots\ y_n$ for all $y_1, \ y_2, \ \ldots,\ y_n$ in the algebra $A$. The smallest such $n$ is called the **index** of the algebra $A$.[^o5] In the case of a [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/6533841), the definition is that every different multiplicative [association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity) of the $n$ elements is zero.

:::

::: {#o7 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Nil algebra"}

## Nil algebra {#Nil_algebra}

A [power associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_associative) algebra in which every element of the algebra is [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235) is called a *nil algebra*.[^o8]

Nilpotent algebras are trivially nil, whereas nil algebras may not be nilpotent, as each element being nilpotent does not force products of distinct elements to vanish.
