# Castelnuovo's contraction theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Castelnuovo's contraction theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Castelnuovo's contraction theorem** is used in the [classification theory](wikipedia://en/3029260) of [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311) to construct the [minimal model](wikipedia://en/15148924) of a given smooth algebraic surface.

:::

More precisely, let $X$ be a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) [projective surface](wikipedia://en/648311) over $\mathbb{C}$ and $C$ a (−1)-[curve](wikipedia://en/253260) on $X$ (which means a smooth [rational curve](wikipedia://en/253260#rational_curves) of [self-intersection](wikipedia://en/3148602#self-intersection) number −1), then there exists a [morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_of_varieties) from $X$ to another smooth projective surface $Y$ such that the curve $C$ has been contracted to one point $P$, and moreover this morphism is an isomorphism outside $C$ (i.e., $X\setminus C$ is isomorphic with $Y\setminus P$).

This [contraction morphism](wikipedia://en/52622473) is sometimes called a blowdown, which is the inverse operation of [blowup](wikipedia://en/1817790). The curve $C$ is also called an [exceptional curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exceptional_curve) of the first kind.
