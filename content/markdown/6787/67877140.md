# Wayne Nelson (statistician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wayne Nelson"}

*For the American musician, see [Wayne Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Nelson).*

:::

**Wayne Nelson** is an American statistician. His main contributions to the reliability theory are the [Nelson-Aalen Estimator](wikipedia://en/22788211) for lifetime data, various statistical procedures for [accelerated life testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerated_life_testing) and both: nonparametric and parametric procedures for [recurrent data analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurrent_data_analysis).

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Nelson was born in Chicago in 1936. He studied Physics at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) and graduated with a Bachelor of Science in 1958. Nelson obtained a Master of Science in Physics from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) in 1959, then a Ph.D. in statistics from the same university in 1965.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

Nelson was employed from 1965 to 1989 at General Electric R&D.[^o5] He was also an adjunct professor teaching graduate courses on applications of statistics at [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College) and [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute).[^o3] Currently, Nelson works as a private consultant and legal expert witness in statistical analysis and modeling of data in many industries; including automotive, aviation, electric power, electronics, materials, medical devices, microelectronics, military hardware, nuclear power, railroad, software, and transportation.[^o5]

## Work {#Work}

His research work focuses on collecting and analyzing reliability data, laboratory tests, accelerated tests, quality control, measurement error analysis, planned experiments, sampling, and data analysis. Nelson worked with [Odd Aalen](wikipedia://en/7755735) on constructing the [Nelson Aalen estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson-Aalen_estimator).,[^o7][^o8][^o9] a non-parametric approximation of the cumulative hazard function that can account for both failure and censored data. He also developed a method to estimate [Weibull distribution](wikipedia://en/206948) (with few or no failures) for products with [evolutionary design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_design) (same [shape parameter](wikipedia://en/9210865) β).[^o10] In the late 1960s, Wayne developed a cumulative hazard analysis method for nonparametric estimation of a population's cumulative life distribution. The resulting estimate is most conveniently displayed and interpreted on a probability plot. Until Wayne developed his method practitioners relied on crude approximations for such analyses. Wayne’s paper "Hazard Plotting for Incomplete Failure Data"[^o7] in the inaugural issue of the J. of Quality technology received the Brumbaugh Award for the ASQ as the 1969 [^o11] paper that made a great contribution to the development of industrial applications of quality control. Moreover, his paper "Theory and Applications of Hazard Plotting for Censored Failure Data" was reprinted in the 40th Anniversary issue of Technometrics (2000)[^o12] as one of the "Two Classics in Reliability Theory." Dr. Wayne also developed software that is widely used in reliability analysis; STATPAC is the first complete package for analysis of reliability and accelerated test data, including censored and interval data. It was the first to provide probability plots, confidence limits, maximum likelihood fitting of many models including accelerated life test models, proper analysis of step-stress data, residuals and their analyses, and a simple user interface. Its versatile reliability features stimulated imitations in SPLUS, [SAS](wikipedia://en/990677), [JMP](wikipedia://en/1979375), ReliaSoft, WinSmith, and others.[^o13] Also, POWNOR, a software that fits the power-(log)normal distribution to censored life data on specimens of differing sizes. This was developed on his NSF-NIST-ASA senior research fellowship at NIST to develop better statistical models for electromigration failures of microcircuits.[^o14]

## Awards {#Awards}

- Brumbaugh Award of the American Society for Quality (1969)[^o11]
- Fulbright Award for research and lecturing (in Spanish) on reliability data analysis (2001)[^o22]
- 2003 [Shewhart Medal](wikipedia://en/831964) of American Society for Quality for his technical leadership[^o23]
- Lifetime Achievement Award of the IEEE Reliability Society for outstanding developments of reliability methodology and contributions to reliability education. Nelson was the second person to receive this annual award. (2005)[^o24]
- 2010 Shainin Medals of the American Society for Quality[^o25]
- 2018 Hahn Award of the American Statistical Association[^o26]
