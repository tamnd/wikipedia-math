# Log-Laplace distribution

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Log-Laplace distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **log-Laplace distribution** is the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) whose [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) has a [Laplace distribution](wikipedia://en/1106213). If *X* has a [Laplace distribution](wikipedia://en/1106213) with parameters *μ* and *b*, then *Y* = *e*<sup>*X*</sup> has a log-Laplace distribution. The distributional properties can be derived from the Laplace distribution.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

A [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) has a log-Laplace(*μ*, *b*) distribution if its [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is:[^o5]

$$f(x|\mu,b) = \frac{1}{2bx} e^{-\frac{|\ln x-\mu|}{b}}$$

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) for *Y* when *y* > 0, is

$$F(y) = 0.5 \left[ 1 + \operatorname{sgn}(\ln(y)-\mu) \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{|\ln(y)-\mu|}{b}} \right) \right]$$

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

Versions of the log-Laplace distribution based on an [asymmetric Laplace distribution](wikipedia://en/48938996) also exist.[^o8] Depending on the parameters, including asymmetry, the log-Laplace may or may not have a finite [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) and a finite [variance](wikipedia://en/32344).[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The mean or expected value of a log-Laplace distributed random variable X with a location parameter *μ* and a scale parameter *b* is given by

$$E(X)=\frac{e^{\mu}}{1-b^2}$$

The variance of X is given by

$$Var(X)=\frac{e^{2\mu}b^4+2e^{2\mu}b^2}{1-4b^6+9b^4-6b^2}$$

:::
