# Inclusion order

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inclusion order"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924), an **inclusion order** is the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) that arises as the [subset](wikipedia://en/27631)-inclusion relation on some collection of objects. In a simple way, every [poset](wikipedia://en/23572) *P* = (*X*,≤) is ([isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to) an inclusion order (just as every group is isomorphic to a [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634) – see [Cayley's theorem](wikipedia://en/105967)). To see this, associate to each element *x* of *X* the set

:::

$$X_{\leq(x)} = \{ y \in X \mid y \leq x\} ;$$

then the transitivity of ≤ ensures that for all *a* and *b* in *X*, we have

$$X_{\leq(a)} \subseteq X_{\leq(b)} \text{ precisely when } a \leq b .$$

There can be sets $S$ of [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6173) less than $|X|$ such that *P* is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to the inclusion order on *S*. The size of the smallest possible *S* is called the [2-dimension](wikipedia://en/3121852#k-dimension_and_2-dimension) of *P*.

Several important classes of poset arise as inclusion orders for some natural collections, like the [Boolean lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_lattice) *Q*<sup>*n*</sup>, which is the collection of all 2<sup>*n*</sup> subsets of an *n*-element set, the **interval-containment orders**, which are precisely the orders of [order dimension](wikipedia://en/3121852) at most two, and the dimension-*n* orders, which are the containment orders on collections of *n*-boxes anchored at the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432). Other containment orders that are interesting in their own right include the **circle orders**, which arise from disks in the plane, and the **angle orders**.
