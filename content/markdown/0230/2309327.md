# Calabi flow

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Calabi flow"}

In the mathematical fields of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474), the **Calabi flow** is a [geometric flow](wikipedia://en/14617515) which deforms a [Kähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric) on a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742). Precisely, given a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) $M$, the Calabi flow is given by:

:::

$$\frac{\partial g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial^2 R^g}{\partial z^\alpha\partial\overline{z}^\beta},$$

where $g$ is a mapping from an open interval into the collection of all Kähler metrics on $M$, $R^{g}$ is the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) of the individual Kähler metrics, and the indices $\alpha, \beta$ correspond to arbitrary holomorphic coordinates $z^{\alpha}$. This is a fourth-order geometric flow, as the right-hand side of the equation involves fourth [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) of $g$.

The Calabi flow was introduced by [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000) in 1982 as a suggestion for the construction of [extremal Kähler metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_Kähler_metric), which were also introduced in the same paper. It is the gradient flow of the *Calabi functional*; extremal Kähler metrics are the [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) of the Calabi functional.

A convergence theorem for the Calabi flow was found by Piotr Chruściel in the case that $M$ has complex dimension equal to one. [Xiuxiong Chen](wikipedia://en/60414667) and others have made a number of further studies of the flow, although as of 2020 the flow is still not well understood.
