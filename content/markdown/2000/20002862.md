# X-parameters

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="X-parameters"}

*Image: Agilent-pna-x-advanced-design-system.jpg.* Agilent engineer demonstrates X-parameter functionality at [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) [MTT-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Microwave_Theory_and_Techniques_Society) [International Microwave Symposium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_MTT-S_International_Microwave_Symposium), Boston MA June 10, 2009.)

**X-parameters** are a generalization of [S-parameters](wikipedia://en/1972332) and are used for characterizing the amplitudes and relative phase of harmonics generated by nonlinear components under large input power levels. X-parameters are also referred to as the parameters of the Poly-Harmonic Distortion (PHD) nonlinear behavioral model.

:::

## Description {#Description}

**X-parameters** represent a new category of [nonlinear](wikipedia://en/146103) [network parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_analysis_%28electrical_circuits%29) for high-frequency design (Nonlinear vector network analyzers are sometimes called large signal network analyzers.[^o3])

X-parameters are applicable to both [large-signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large-signal_model) and [small-signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small-signal_model) conditions, for linear and nonlinear components. They are an extension of [S-parameters](wikipedia://en/1972332)[^o4] meaning that, in the limit of a small signal, X-parameters reduce to S-parameters.

They help overcome a key challenge in [RF engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RF_engineering), namely that nonlinear [impedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_impedance) differences, [harmonic mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_mixer), and nonlinear reflection effects occur when components are cascaded under large signal operating conditions. This means that there is a nonlinear and as such non-trivial relationship between the properties of the individual cascaded components and the composite properties of the resulting cascade. This situation is unlike that at [DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current), where one can simply add the values of [resistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistor) connected in series. X-parameters help solve this cascading problem: if the X-parameters of a set of components are measured individually, the X-parameters (and hence the non-linear transfer function) can be calculated of any cascade made from them. Calculations based on X-parameters are usually performed within a harmonic balance simulator environment.[^o5]

## Development {#Development}

X-parameters were developed and introduced by [Keysight Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keysight_Technologies) as functionality included in [N5242A Nonlinear Vector Network Analyzer](https://www.keysight.com/us/en/product/N5242A/pnax-microwave-network-analyzer-265-ghz.html),[^o7][^o8] and the W2200 [Advanced Design System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Design_System) in 2008.

X-parameters are the parameters of the polyharmonic distortion modeling work of Dr. Jan Verspecht[^o9][^o10] and Dr. David E. Root.[^o10]
