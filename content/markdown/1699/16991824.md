# BL (logic)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="BL"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **basic fuzzy logic** (or shortly **BL**), the logic of the [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [t-norms](wikipedia://en/2099179), is one of the [t-norm fuzzy logics](wikipedia://en/13727501). It belongs to the broader class of [substructural logics](wikipedia://en/412909), or logics of [residuated lattices](wikipedia://en/2483542);[^o2] it extends the logic [MTL](wikipedia://en/10464507) of all left-continuous t-norms.

:::

## Syntax {#Syntax}

### Language {#Language}

The language of the propositional logic BL consists of [countably](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) many [propositional variables](wikipedia://en/1557562) and the following primitive [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153):

- **Implication** $\rightarrow$ ([binary](wikipedia://en/42301))
- **Strong conjunction** $\otimes$ (binary). The sign & is a more traditional notation for strong conjunction in the literature on fuzzy logic, while the notation $\otimes$ follows the tradition of substructural logics.
- **Bottom** $\bot$ ([nullary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullary) — a [propositional constant](wikipedia://en/4089175)); $0$ or $\overline{0}$ are common alternative signs and **zero** a common alternative name for the propositional constant (as the constants bottom and zero of substructural logics coincide in MTL).

The following are the most common defined logical connectives:

- **Weak conjunction** $\wedge$ (binary), also called **lattice conjunction** (as it is always realized by the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) operation of [meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_%28mathematics%29) in algebraic semantics). Unlike [MTL](wikipedia://en/10464507) and weaker substructural logics, weak conjunction is definable in BL as

$$A \wedge B \equiv A \otimes (A \rightarrow B)$$

- **Negation** $\neg$ ([unary](wikipedia://en/161006)), defined as

$$\neg A \equiv A \rightarrow \bot$$

- **Equivalence** $\leftrightarrow$ (binary), defined as

$$A \leftrightarrow B \equiv (A \rightarrow B) \wedge (B \rightarrow A)$$

As in MTL, the definition is equivalent to $(A \rightarrow B) \otimes (B \rightarrow A).$

- **(Weak) disjunction** $\vee$ (binary), also called **lattice disjunction** (as it is always realized by the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) operation of [join](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_%28mathematics%29) in algebraic semantics), defined as

$$A \vee B \equiv ((A \rightarrow B) \rightarrow B) \wedge ((B \rightarrow A) \rightarrow A)$$

- **Top** $\top$ (nullary), also called **one** and denoted by $1$ or $\overline{1}$ (as the constants top and zero of substructural logics coincide in MTL), defined as

$$\top \equiv \bot \rightarrow \bot$$

[Well-formed formulae](wikipedia://en/404582) of BL are defined as usual in [propositional logics](wikipedia://en/18154). In order to save parentheses, it is common to use the following order of precedence:

- Unary connectives (bind most closely)
- Binary connectives other than implication and equivalence
- Implication and equivalence (bind most loosely)

### Axioms {#Axioms}

A [Hilbert-style deduction system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert-style_deduction_system) for BL has been introduced by [Petr Hájek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Hájek) (1998). Its single derivation rule is [modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900):

from $A$ and $A \rightarrow B$ derive $B.$

The following are its [axiom schemata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_scheme):

$$\begin{array}{ll} {\rm (BL1)}\colon & (A \rightarrow B) \rightarrow ((B \rightarrow C) \rightarrow (A \rightarrow C)) \\ {\rm (BL2)}\colon & A \otimes B \rightarrow A\\ {\rm (BL3)}\colon & A \otimes B \rightarrow B \otimes A\\ {\rm (BL4)}\colon & A \otimes (A \rightarrow B) \rightarrow B \otimes (B \rightarrow A)\\ {\rm (BL5a)}\colon & (A \rightarrow (B \rightarrow C)) \rightarrow (A \otimes B \rightarrow C)\\ {\rm (BL5b)}\colon & (A \otimes B \rightarrow C) \rightarrow (A \rightarrow (B \rightarrow C))\\ {\rm (BL6)}\colon & ((A \rightarrow B) \rightarrow C) \rightarrow (((B \rightarrow A) \rightarrow C) \rightarrow C)\\ {\rm (BL7)}\colon & \bot \rightarrow A \end{array}$$

The axioms (BL2) and (BL3) of the original axiomatic system were shown to be redundant (Chvalovský, 2012) and (Cintula, 2005). All the other axioms were shown to be independent (Chvalovský, 2012).

:::

## Semantics {#Semantics}

Like in other propositional [t-norm fuzzy logics](wikipedia://en/13727501), [algebraic semantics](wikipedia://en/2848519) is predominantly used for BL, with three main classes of [algebras](wikipedia://en/106364) with respect to which the logic is [complete](wikipedia://en/31271451):

- **General semantics**, formed of all *BL-algebras* — that is, all algebras for which the logic is [sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundness_theorem)
- **Linear semantics**, formed of all *linear* BL-algebras — that is, all BL-algebras whose [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) order is [linear](wikipedia://en/30330)
- **Standard semantics**, formed of all *standard* BL-algebras — that is, all BL-algebras whose lattice reduct is the real unit interval \[0, 1\] with the usual order; they are uniquely determined by the function that interprets strong conjunction, which can be any continuous [t-norm](wikipedia://en/2099179).
