# Richard Pendlebury

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Pendlebury"}

**Richard Pendlebury** (1847–1902) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musician), [bibliophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliophile) and [mountaineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaineer).

:::

Pendlebury was born in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) on 28 March 1847 and educated at [Liverpool College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_College). He went up to [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) in 1866 and graduated [senior wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_wrangler) in 1870: he was then elected to a college fellowship. He was appointed University Lecturer in Mathematics in 1888. He collected early mathematical books and printed music, donating his collections to his college and university. His presentation of a collection of music books and manuscripts to the [Fitzwilliam Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitzwilliam_Museum) stimulated the formation of the Music Faculty at Cambridge University and the university's [Pendlebury Library of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendlebury_Library_of_Music) is named after him.[^o3]

Most of his more difficult alpine expeditions were made with his brother, W. M. Pendlebury[^o7] and it was with his brother, along with Rev. C Taylor and the guide Ferdinand Imseng, that he made the [first ascent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_ascent) of the east face of the alpine peak [Monte Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Rosa) from Macugnaga in 1872.[^o2][^o4] His first ascent of the [Pic Olan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olan_%28mountain%29), on 8 July 1875, was made with Arthur Cust.[^o5]

The Pendlebury Traverse on [Pillar Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillar_%28Lake_District%29#Pillar_Rock) in Cumberland bears his name, he took part in the first ascent of this climb in September 1872, along with his brother William and [Frederick Gardiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Gardiner_%28mountaineer%29), but it was actually Gardiner who initiated and led the climb.[^o6]

In his later years Pendlebury moved to [Keswick, Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keswick,_Cumbria) where he died on 13 March 1902.[^o7]
