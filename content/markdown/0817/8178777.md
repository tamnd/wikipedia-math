# Lucio Russo

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lucio Russo"}

**Lucio Russo** (22 November 1944 – 12 July 2025) was an Italian physicist, mathematician and historian of science. Born in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), he taught at the Mathematics Department of the [University of Rome Tor Vergata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_Tor_Vergata). He died in Bologna on 12 July 2025, at the age of 80.[^o3]

:::

Among his main areas of interest were [Gibbs measure](wikipedia://en/3085914) of the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744), [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805),[^o4] and finite [Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/199189) schemes, within which he proved an approximate version of the classical [Kolmogorov's zero–one law](wikipedia://en/191797).[^o5]

In the history of science, he reconstructed some contributions of the [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) astronomer [Hipparchus](wikipedia://en/13600), through the analysis of his surviving works, and the proof of [heliocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliocentrism) attributed by [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch) to [Seleucus of Seleucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucus_of_Seleucia) and studied the history of theories of [tides](wikipedia://en/30718),[^o6] from the Hellenistic to modern age.

## Books {#Books}

### *The Forgotten Revolution* {#The_Forgotten_Revolution}

In *The Forgotten Revolution: How Science Was Born in 300 BC and Why It Had to Be Reborn* (Italian: *La rivoluzione dimenticata*), Russo promotes the belief that Hellenistic science in the period 320–144 BC reached heights not achieved by [Classical age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_age) science, and proposes that it went further than ordinarily thought, in multiple fields not normally associated with ancient science.

According to Russo, Hellenistic scientists were not simply forerunners, but actually achieved scientific results of high importance, in the fields of "mathematics, solid and fluid mechanics, optics, astronomy, anatomy, physiology, scientific medicine",[^o9] even psychoanalysis. They may have even discovered the [inverse square law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_square_law) of [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation) (Russo's argument on this point hinges on well established, but seldom discussed, evidence). Hellenistic scientists, among them [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331), [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) and [Eratosthenes](wikipedia://en/46117), developed an axiomatic and deductive way of argumentation. When this way of argumentation was dropped, the ability to understand the results were lost as well. Thus, Russo conjectures that the definitions of elementary geometric objects were introduced in Euclid's *[Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)* by [Heron of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heron_of_Alexandria), 400 years after the work was completed.[^o9] More concretely, Russo shows how the theory of tides must have been well developed in Antiquity, because several pre-Newtonian sources relay various complementary parts of the theory without grasping their import or justification (getting the empirical facts wrong but the theory right).

Hellenistic science was focused on the city of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria). The emerging scientific revolution in Alexandria was ended when [Ptolemy VIII Physcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_VIII_Physcon) came to power. He engaged in mass purges and expulsions of all intellectuals. Other centers of Hellenistic science mentioned in Russo's book were [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch), [Pergamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon), [Cyzicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyzicus), [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes), [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) and [Massilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massilia).

He also concludes that the 17th-century scientific revolution in Europe was due in large part to the recovery of Hellenistic science.[^o10] *The Forgotten Revolution* has received mixed reviews, praising Russo's enthusiasm but noting that his conclusions outreach his sources.[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14]

### *L'America dimenticata* {#L'America_dimenticata}

In *L'America dimenticata*, Russo suggests that the Americas [were known to some European civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_trans-oceanic_contact_theories) in ancient times, probably discovered by the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_Phoenician_discovery_of_the_Americas) or the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginians), but that the knowledge was lost under Roman expansion in the second century BC.[^o16]

Russo notes paintings dating to the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) period that, he claims, represent American fruits (*[Ananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ananas)*), and small [Mesoamerican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican) toys representing wheeled trucks, when the wheel had not been invented nor used in pre-Columbian times.

With the collapse of the Hellenistic world under the attacks of the Romans around the middle of the 2nd century BC (specifically, the destruction of [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth) and [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) in 146 BC and the expulsion of the scientific elite from [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) in 145 BC), he claims, these geographic notions were lost. According to his theories, Ptolemy's later identification of the Blessed Islands with the [Canaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands) was incorrect. Instead, he claims that the Blessed Islands were known to be antipodal to the eastern part of China, and that Ptolemy made ends meet by erroneously enlarging the longitude of all known places, and shrinking the width of a degree of longitude (500 instead of 700 stadia).

Based on this correction, Lucio Russo claims to pinpoint the position of the mythical [Thule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule), reached in the 4th century BC by explorer [Pytheas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pytheas), on the coast of [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland). He interprets in light of this claim an obscure sentence of [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_elder) according to which Hipparchus would have enlarged the [ecumene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumene) (the known world) by 26,000 [stadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_%28unit%29).[^o17]
