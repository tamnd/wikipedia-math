# Pfister form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pfister form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Pfister form** is a particular kind of [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478), introduced by [Albrecht Pfister](wikipedia://en/35551178) in 1965.[^o2] In what follows, quadratic forms are considered over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) not 2. For a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n*, an ***n*-fold Pfister form** over *F* is a quadratic form of dimension 2<sup>*n*</sup> that can be written as a [tensor product of quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/10137896)

:::

$$\langle\!\langle a_1, a_2, \ldots , a_n \rangle\!\rangle \cong \langle 1, -a_1 \rangle \otimes \langle 1, -a_2 \rangle \otimes \cdots \otimes \langle 1, -a_n \rangle,$$

for some nonzero elements *a*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>*n*</sub> of *F*.[^o3] (Some authors omit the signs in this definition; the notation here simplifies the relation to [Milnor K-theory](wikipedia://en/2914128), discussed below.) An *n*-fold Pfister form can also be constructed inductively from an (*n*−1)-fold Pfister form *q* and a nonzero element *a* of *F*, as $q \oplus (-a)q$.

So the 1-fold and 2-fold Pfister forms look like:

$$\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\cong \langle 1, -a \rangle = x^2 - ay^2.$$

$$\langle\!\langle a,b\rangle\!\rangle\cong \langle 1, -a, -b, ab \rangle = x^2 - ay^2 - bz^2 + abw^2.$$

For *n* ≤ 3, the *n*-fold Pfister forms are norm forms of [composition algebras](wikipedia://en/3861635).[^o4] In that case, two *n*-fold Pfister forms are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the corresponding composition algebras are isomorphic. In particular, this gives the classification of [octonion algebras](wikipedia://en/10277277).

The *n*-fold Pfister forms additively generate the *n*-th power *I*<sup>*n*</sup> of the fundamental ideal of the [Witt ring](wikipedia://en/5578523) of *F*.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterizations"}

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

A quadratic form *q* over a field *F* is **multiplicative** if, for vectors of indeterminates **x** and **y**, we can write *q*(**x**).*q*(**y**) = *q*(**z**) for some vector **z** of [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) in the **x** and **y** over *F*. [Isotropic quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/10917170) are multiplicative.[^o8] For [anisotropic quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropic_quadratic_form), Pfister forms are multiplicative, and conversely.[^o9]

For *n*-fold Pfister forms with *n* ≤ 3, this had been known since the 19th century; in that case *z* can be taken to be bilinear in *x* and *y*, by the properties of composition algebras. It was a remarkable discovery by Pfister that *n*-fold Pfister forms for all *n* are multiplicative in the more general sense here, involving rational functions. For example, he deduced that for any field *F* and any natural number *n*, the set of sums of 2<sup>*n*</sup> squares in *F* is closed under multiplication, using that the quadratic form $x_1^2 +\cdots + x_{2^n}^2$ is an *n*-fold Pfister form (namely, $\langle\!\langle -1, \ldots , -1 \rangle\!\rangle$).[^o10]

Another striking feature of Pfister forms is that every isotropic Pfister form is in fact hyperbolic, that is, isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/10917170#Hyperbolic_plane) $\langle 1, -1 \rangle$. This property also characterizes Pfister forms, as follows: If *q* is an anisotropic quadratic form over a field *F*, and if *q* becomes hyperbolic over every extension field *E* such that *q* becomes isotropic over *E*, then *q* is isomorphic to *a*φ for some nonzero *a* in *F* and some Pfister form φ over *F*.[^o11]

:::

## Connection with *K*-theory {#Connection_with_K-theory}

Let *k*<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) be the *n*-th [Milnor *K*-group](wikipedia://en/2914128) modulo 2. There is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) from *k*<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) to the quotient *I*<sup>*n*</sup>/*I*<sup>*n*+1</sup> in the Witt ring of *F*, given by

$$\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\} \mapsto \langle\!\langle a_1, a_2, \ldots , a_n \rangle\!\rangle ,$$

where the image is an *n*-fold Pfister form.[^o13] The homomorphism is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective), since the Pfister forms additively generate *I*<sup>*n*</sup>. One part of the [Milnor conjecture](wikipedia://en/2914126), [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by Orlov, Vishik and [Voevodsky](wikipedia://en/104482), states that this homomorphism is in fact an isomorphism *k*<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) ≅ *I*<sup>*n*</sup>/*I*<sup>*n*+1</sup>.[^o14] That gives an explicit description of the [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *I*<sup>*n*</sup>/*I*<sup>*n*+1</sup> by [generators and relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generators_and_relations). The other part of the Milnor conjecture, proved by Voevodsky, says that *k*<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) (and hence *I*<sup>*n*</sup>/*I*<sup>*n*+1</sup>) maps isomorphically to the [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) group *H*<sup>*n*</sup>(*F*, **F**<sub>2</sub>).

## Pfister neighbors {#Pfister_neighbors}

A **Pfister neighbor** is an anisotropic form σ which is isomorphic to a subform of *a*φ for some nonzero *a* in *F* and some Pfister form φ with dim φ \< 2 dim σ.[^o17] The associated Pfister form φ is determined up to isomorphism by σ. Every anisotropic form of dimension 3 is a Pfister neighbor; an anisotropic form of dimension 4 is a Pfister neighbor if and only if its [discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discriminant_of_a_quadratic_form) in *F*<sup>\*</sup>/(*F*<sup>\*</sup>)<sup>2</sup> is trivial.[^o18] A field *F* has the property that every 5-dimensional anisotropic form over *F* is a Pfister neighbor if and only if it is a [linked field](wikipedia://en/39227440).[^o19]

:::
