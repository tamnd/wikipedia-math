# Norm variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Norm variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **norm variety** is a particular type of [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F*, introduced for the purposes of [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) by [Voevodsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voevodsky). The idea is to relate [Milnor K-theory](wikipedia://en/2914128) of *F* to geometric objects *V*, having [function fields](wikipedia://en/2783843) *F*(*V*) that 'split' given 'symbols' (elements of Milnor K-groups).[^o2]

:::

The formulation is that *p* is a given prime number, different from the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of *F*, and a symbol is the class mod *p* of an element

$$\{a_1, \dots, a_n\}\$$

of the *n*-th Milnor K-group. A [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) is said to *split* the symbol, if its image in the K-group for that field is 0.

The conditions on a norm variety *V* are that *V* is [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_variety) and a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) [complete variety](wikipedia://en/1047627). Further it should have [dimension](wikipedia://en/253251) *d* equal to

$$p^{n - 1} - 1.\$$

The key condition is in terms of the *d*-th [Newton polynomial](wikipedia://en/216333) *s*<sub>*d*</sub>, evaluated on the (algebraic) total [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) of the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of *V*. This number

$$s_d(V)\$$

should not be divisible by *p*<sup>2</sup>, it being known it is divisible by *p*.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

These include (*n* = 2) cases of the [Severi–Brauer variety](wikipedia://en/1669368) and (*p* = 2) [Pfister forms](wikipedia://en/7204602). There is an existence theorem in the general case (paper of [Markus Rost](wikipedia://en/6842771) cited).

:::
