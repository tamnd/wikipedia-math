# Norm form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Norm form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **norm form** is a [homogeneous form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_form) in *n* variables constructed from the [field norm](wikipedia://en/450555) of a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *L*/*K* of degree *n*.[^o2] That is, writing *N* for the norm mapping to *K*, and selecting a basis *e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*n*</sub> for *L* as a vector space over *K*, the form is given by

:::

*N*(*x*<sub>1</sub>*e*<sub>1</sub> + ... + *x*<sub>*n*</sub>*e*<sub>*n*</sub>)

in variables *x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>.

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) norm forms are studied as [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109), where they generalize, for example, the [Pell equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pell_equation).[^o3] For this application the field *K* is usually the rational number field, the field *L* is an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822), and the basis is taken of some [order](wikipedia://en/1114171) in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) *O*<sub>*L*</sub> of *L*.
