# D'Alembert's equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="D'Alembert's equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **d'Alembert's equation**, sometimes also known as **Lagrange's equation**,[^o2] is a first order nonlinear [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753), named after the French mathematician [Jean le Rond d'Alembert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_le_Rond_d'Alembert). The equation reads as[^o3]

:::

$$y = x f\left( \frac{dy}{dx} \right) + g\left( \frac{dy}{dx}\right).$$

After differentiating once, and rearranging with $p=dy/dx$, we have

$$\frac{dx}{dp} + \frac{x f'(p) + g'(p)}{f(p)-p}=0$$

The above equation is a first order linear differential equation:

$$\frac{dx}{dp} + \frac{f'(p)}{f(p)-p}x=\frac{- g'(p)}{f(p)-p}$$

as the general form:

$$\frac{dx}{dp} + R(p) x=Q(p)$$

When $f(p)=p$, d'Alembert's equation is reduced to [Clairaut's equation](wikipedia://en/453519).
