# Noneism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Noneism"}

**Noneism**, also known as **modal Meinongianism**[^o6][^o2] (named after [Alexius Meinong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius_Meinong)), is a theory in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics). It holds that some things do [not exist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonexistence). It was coined by [Richard Routley](wikipedia://en/433347) in 1980 and appropriated again in 2005 by [Graham Priest](wikipedia://en/3659382).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Noneism holds that some things do not exist. That is, we can quantify over [non-existent objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-existent_object) ("items") using the so-called particular quantifier (also known—misleadingly in the view of noneists—as the [existential quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_quantifier)). They also hold that "there is" is like "exist", rather than like the particular quantifier. Thus, they deny that *there are* things that do not exist. In this theory, there are no [empty names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_names), wherefore the "problem of empty names" that afflicts many theories about names (in particular, [Millianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millianism)), is not a problem at all.[^o6]

While Priest also espouses [dialetheism](wikipedia://en/591359), he maintains that his dialetheism is mostly capable of being separated from his noneism. The connection is that impossible objects may exist in impossible worlds, much as [nonexistent objects](wikipedia://en/7201028) may exist in possible (but not actual) worlds.

[Richard Routley](wikipedia://en/433347)'s book, *Exploring Meinong's Jungle and Beyond: An Investigation of Noneism and the Theory of Items*, was published in 1980, while the first edition of Priest's book entitled *Towards Non-Being: The Logic and Metaphysics of Intentionality* was published in 2005 (second revised edition in 2016).
