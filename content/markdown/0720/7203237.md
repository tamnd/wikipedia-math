# Henry F. Baker

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Henry F. Baker"}

**Henry Frederick Baker** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)[^o4] [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) (3 July 1866 – 17 March 1956) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working mainly in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), but also remembered for contributions to [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) (related to what would become known as [solitons](wikipedia://en/51654)), and [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) the son of Henry Baker, a butler, and Sarah Ann Britham.[^o7]

## Education {#Education}

He was educated at [The Perse School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perse_School) before winning a scholarship to [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) in October 1884. Baker graduated as [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) in 1887, bracketed with 3 others. His doctoral advisor was [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034).

## Career {#Career}

Baker was elected Fellow of St John's in 1888 where he remained for 68 years.

In June 1898, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).[^o4] In 1911, he gave the presidential address to the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).

Baker was one of the mathematicians (along with E. W. Hobson) to whom [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) wrote before [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) but his papers were returned without comment.

In January 1914, he was appointed [Lowndean Professor of Astronomy](wikipedia://en/648990).

[Gordon Welchman](wikipedia://en/2385693) recalled that in the 1930s before the war [Dennis Babbage](wikipedia://en/39167044) and he were members of a group of geometers known as Professor Baker's "Tea Party", who met once a week to discuss the areas of research in which we were all interested.[^o11]

He married twice. Firstly in 1893, to Lilly Isabella Hamfield Klopp, who died in 1903, then he remarried in 1913, to Muriel Irene Woodyard.

He died in Cambridge and is buried at the [Ascension Parish Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground), with his second wife Muriel (1885–1956).
