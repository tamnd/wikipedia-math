# Hypoelliptic operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypoelliptic operator"}

In the theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), a partial [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) $P$ defined on an [open subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset)

:::

$$U \subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$$

is called **hypoelliptic** if for every [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) $u$ defined on an open subset $V \subset U$ such that $Pu$ is $C^\infty$ ([smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function)), $u$ must also be $C^\infty$.

If this assertion holds with $C^\infty$ replaced by real-analytic, then $P$ is said to be *analytically hypoelliptic*.

Every [elliptic operator](wikipedia://en/550137) with $C^\infty$ coefficients is hypoelliptic. In particular, the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) is an example of a hypoelliptic operator (the Laplacian is also analytically hypoelliptic). In addition, the operator for the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) ($P(u)=u_t - k\,\Delta u\,$)

$$P= \partial_t - k\,\Delta_x\,$$

(where $k>0$) is hypoelliptic but not elliptic. However, the operator for the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) ($P(u)=u_{tt} - c^2\,\Delta u\,$)

$$P= \partial^2_t - c^2\,\Delta_x\,$$

(where $c\ne 0$) is not hypoelliptic.
