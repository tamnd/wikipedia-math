# Nonlinear expectation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nonlinear expectation"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **nonlinear expectation** is a nonlinear generalization of the [expectation](wikipedia://en/9653). Nonlinear expectations are useful in [utility theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_theory) as they more closely match human behavior than traditional expectations.[^o2] The common use of nonlinear expectations is in assessing risks under uncertainty. Generally, nonlinear expectations are categorized into sub-linear and super-linear expectations dependent on the additive properties of the given sets. Much of the study of nonlinear expectation is attributed to work of mathematicians within the past two decades.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) $\mathbb{E}: \mathcal{H} \to \mathbb{R}$ (where $\mathcal{H}$ is a [vector lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_lattice) on a given set $\Omega$) is a nonlinear expectation if it satisfies:[^o5][^o6][^o7]

1. Monotonicity: if $X,Y \in \mathcal{H}$ such that $X \geq Y$ then $\mathbb{E}[X] \geq \mathbb{E}[Y]$
1. Preserving of constants: if $c \in \mathbb{R}$ then $\mathbb{E}[c] = c$

The complete consideration of the given set, the linear space for the functions given that set, and the nonlinear expectation value is called the nonlinear expectation space.

Often other properties are also desirable, for instance [convexity](wikipedia://en/245568), [subadditivity](wikipedia://en/745789), [positive homogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_homogeneity), and translative of constants.[^o5] For a nonlinear expectation to be further classified as a sublinear expectation, the following two conditions must also be met:

1. Subadditivity: for $X,Y \in \mathcal{H}$ then $\mathbb{E}[X] + \mathbb{E}[Y] \geq \mathbb{E}[X+Y]$
1. Positive homogeneity: for $\lambda\geq0$ then $\mathbb{E}[\lambda X] = \lambda \mathbb{E}[X]$

For a nonlinear expectation to instead be classified as a superlinear expectation, the subadditivity condition above is instead replaced by the condition:[^o8]

1. [Superadditivity](wikipedia://en/1464384): for $X,Y \in \mathcal{H}$ then $\mathbb{E}[X] + \mathbb{E}[Y] \leq \mathbb{E}[X+Y]$

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Choquet expectation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choquet_expectation): a subadditive or superadditive integral that is used in image processing and behavioral decision theory.
- [g-expectation](wikipedia://en/36678177) via nonlinear BSDE's: frequently used to model financial drift uncertainty.[^o11]
- If $\rho$ is a [risk measure](wikipedia://en/12498127) then $\mathbb{E}[X] := \rho(-X)$ defines a nonlinear expectation.
- [Markov Chains](wikipedia://en/60876): for the prediction of events undergoing model uncertainties.[^o12]

:::
