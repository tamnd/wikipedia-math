# Bochner measurable function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bochner measurable function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) – specifically, in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) – a **Bochner-measurable function** taking values in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) that equals almost everywhere the limit of a sequence of measurable countably-valued functions, i.e.,

:::

$$f(t) = \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_n(t)\text{ for almost every }t, \,$$

where the functions $f_n$ each have a countable range and for which the pre-image $f_n^{-1}(\{x\})$ is measurable for each element *x*. The concept is named after [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847).

Bochner-measurable functions are sometimes called **[strongly measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_measurable)**, **$\mu$-measurable** or just **measurable** (or **[uniformly measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_measurable)** in case that the Banach space is the space of continuous [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) between Banach spaces).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The relationship between measurability and weak measurability is given by the following result, known as **[Pettis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._J._Pettis)' theorem** or **Pettis measurability theorem**.

> Function *f* is **[almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) separably valued** (or **essentially separably valued**) if there exists a subset *N* ⊆ *X* with *μ*(*N*) = 0 such that *f*(*X* \\ *N*) ⊆ *B* is separable.

> A function f : *X* → *B* defined on a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) (*X*, Σ, *μ*) and taking values in a Banach space *B* is (strongly) measurable (with respect to Σ and the [Borel algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_algebra) on *B*) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is both [weakly measurable](wikipedia://en/14502271) and almost surely separably valued.

In the case that *B* is separable, since any subset of a separable Banach space is itself separable, one can take *N* above to be empty, and it follows that the notions of weak and strong measurability agree when *B* is separable.
