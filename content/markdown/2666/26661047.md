# Counting rods

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Counting rods"}

*Image: Yanghui triangle.gif.* [Yang Hui](wikipedia://en/1716898) (Pascal's) [triangle](wikipedia://en/49497), as depicted by [Zhu Shijie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Shijie) in 1303, using rod numerals

**Counting rods** (筭) are small bars, typically 3–14 cm (1" to 6") long, that were used by mathematicians for calculation in ancient [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia). They are placed either horizontally or vertically to represent any [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) or [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024).

:::

The written forms based on them are called **rod numerals**. They are a true [positional numeral system](wikipedia://en/437052) with [digits](wikipedia://en/280582) for 1–9 and a blank for 0, from the [Warring states period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period) (circa 475 BCE)[^o3] to the 16th century.

## History {#History}

Chinese arithmeticians used counting rods well over two thousand years ago.

In 1954, forty-odd counting rods of the Warring States period (5th century BCE to 221 BCE) were found in [Zuǒjiāgōngshān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuǒjiāgōngshān) (左家公山) [Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_%28state%29) Grave No.15 in [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha), [Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan).[^o5][^o6]

In 1973, archeologists unearthed a number of wood scripts from a tomb in Hubei dating from the period of the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) (206 BCE to 220 CE). On one of the wooden scripts was written: "当利二月定算𝍥". This is one of the earliest examples of using counting-rod numerals in writing.

A square lacquer box, dating from c. 168 BCE, containing a square chess board with the TLV patterns, chessmen, counting rods, and other items, was excavated in 1972, from [Mawangdui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawangdui) M3, Changsha, Hunan Province.[^o7][^o8]

In 1976, a bundle of [Western Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Han)-era (202 BCE to 9 CE) counting rods made of bones was unearthed from [Qianyang County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianyang_County) in [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi).[^o9][^o10] The use of counting rods must predate it; [Sunzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Tzu) (c. 544 to c. 496 BCE), a military strategist at the end of [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period) of 771 BCE to 5th century BCE, mentions their use to make calculations to win wars before going into the battle;[^o11] [Laozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laozi) (died 531 BCE), writing in the Warring States period, said "a good calculator doesn't use counting rods".[^o12] The *[Book of Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Han)* (finished 111 CE) recorded: "they calculate with bamboo, diameter one fen, length six cun, arranged into a hexagonal bundle of two hundred seventy one pieces".[^o13]

At first, calculating rods were round in cross-section, but by the time of the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty) (581 to 618 CE) mathematicians used triangular rods to represent positive numbers and rectangular rods for [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616).

*Image: Toán trù 算籌.jpg.* Toán trù 算籌 (counting rods) in a Vietnamese mathematics textbook, Cửu chương lập thành toán pháp 九章立成算法 is shown at the bottom of the page.

After the [abacus](wikipedia://en/655) flourished, counting rods were abandoned except in Japan, where rod numerals developed into a symbolic notation for [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923).

## Usage {#Usage}

*Image: Counting board.jpg.* Japanese counting board with grids

*Image: Checker counting board.jpg.* A checker counting board diagram in an 18th-century Japanese mathematics textbook

*Image: Seki Kowa Katsuyo Sampo Bernoulli numbers.png.* Counting rod numerals in grids in a Japanese mathematic book

Counting rods represent digits by the number of rods, and the [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) rod represents five. To avoid confusion, vertical and horizontal forms are alternately used. Generally, vertical rod numbers are used for the position for the units, hundreds, ten thousands, etc., while horizontal rod numbers are used for the tens, thousands, hundred thousands etc. It is written in *[Sunzi Suanjing](wikipedia://en/30291690)* that "one is vertical, ten is horizontal".[^o15]

Red rods represent [positive numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) and black rods represent [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616).[^o16] Ancient Chinese clearly understood negative numbers and zero (leaving a blank space for it), though they had no symbol for the latter. [The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207), which was mainly composed in the first century CE, stated "(when using subtraction) subtract same signed numbers, add different signed numbers, subtract a positive number from zero to make a negative number, and subtract a negative number from zero to make a positive number".[^o17][^o18] Later, a [go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_%28game%29) stone was sometimes used to represent zero.

### Place value {#Place_value}

The value of a number depends on its physical position on the counting board. A 9 at the rightmost position on the board stands for 9. Moving the batch of rods representing 9 to the left one position (i.e., to the tens place) gives 9\[\] or 90. Shifting left again to the third position (to the hundreds place) gives 9\[\]\[\] or 900. Each time one shifts a number one position to the left, it is multiplied by 10. Each time one shifts a number one position to the right, it is divided by 10. This applies to single-digit numbers or multiple-digit numbers.

Song dynasty mathematician [Jia Xian](wikipedia://en/15141098) used hand-written Chinese decimal orders 步十百千萬 as rod numeral place values, as evident from a facsimile from a page of [Yongle Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Encyclopedia). He arranged 七萬一千八百二十四 as

七一八二四

萬千百十步

He treated the Chinese order numbers as place value markers, and 七一八二四 became place value decimal numbers. He then wrote the rod numerals according to their place value:

| 七 | 一 | 八 | 二 | 四 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 萬 | 千 | 百 | 十 | 步 |

## Rod numerals {#Rod_numerals}

Rod numerals are a positional numeral system made from shapes of counting rods. Positive numbers are written as they are and the negative numbers are written with a slant bar at the last digit. The vertical bar in the horizontal forms 6–9 are drawn shorter to have the same character height.

A circle (〇) is used for 0. Many historians think it was imported from [Indian numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_numerals) by [Gautama Siddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Siddha) in 718,[^o17] but some think it was created from the Chinese text space filler "□", and others think that the Indians acquired it from China, because it resembles a Confucian philosophical symbol for "nothing".[^o25]

In the 13th century, [Southern Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) mathematicians changed digits for 4, 5, and 9 to reduce strokes.[^o25] The new horizontal forms eventually transformed into [Suzhou numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou_numerals). Japanese continued to use the traditional forms.

## Fractions {#Fractions}

*Image: Rod fraction.jpg.* Fraction $\tfrac{1}{7}$

A fraction was expressed with rod numerals as two rod numerals one on top of another (without any other symbol, like the modern horizontal bar).

## Rod calculus {#Rod_calculus}

The method for using counting rods for mathematical calculation was called *rod calculation* or [rod calculus](wikipedia://en/11919629) (筹算). Rod calculus can be used for a wide range of calculations, including finding the value of [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601), finding [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208), [cube roots](wikipedia://en/292506), or [higher order roots](wikipedia://en/235029), and solving a [system of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087).

Before the introduction of a written zero, a space was used to indicate no units, and the rotation of the character in the subsequent unit column, by 90°, adopted, to help reduce the ambiguity in record values calculated on the rods.[^o33] For example 107 (𝍠 𝍧) and 17 (𝍩𝍧) would be distinguished by rotation, though multiple zero units could lead to ambiguity, eg. 1007 (𝍩 𝍧), and 10007 (𝍠 𝍧). Once written zero came into play, the rod numerals had become independent, and their use indeed outlives the counting rods, after its replacement by [abacus](wikipedia://en/655). One variation of horizontal rod numerals, the [Suzhou numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou_numerals) is still in use for book-keeping and in herbal medicine prescription in [Chinatowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) in some parts of the world.

## Unicode {#Unicode}

[Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) 5.0 includes counting rod numerals in their own block in the [Supplementary Multilingual Plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28Unicode%29#Supplementary_Multilingual_Plane) (SMP) from U+1D360 to U+1D37F. The [code points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) for the horizontal digits 1–9 are U+1D360 to U+1D368 and those for the vertical digits 1–9 are U+1D369 to U+1D371. The former are called *unit digits* and the latter are called *tens digits*,[^o35][^o36] which is opposite of the convention described above. The Unicode Standard states that the orientation of the Unicode characters follows Song dynasty convention, which differs from Han dynasty practice which represented digits as vertical lines, and tens as horizontal lines.[^o37] Zero should be represented by U+3007 (〇, ideographic number zero) and the negative sign should be represented by U+20E5 (combining reverse solidus overlay).[^o38] As these were recently added to the character set and since they are included in the SMP, font support may still be limited.
