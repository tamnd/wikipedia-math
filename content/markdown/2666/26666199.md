# Hypercomplex analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypercomplex analysis"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **hypercomplex analysis** is the extension of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) to the [hypercomplex numbers](wikipedia://en/51438). The first instance is functions of a [quaternion variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternion_variable), where the argument is a [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) (in this case, the sub-field of hypercomplex analysis is called [quaternionic analysis](wikipedia://en/23779912)). A second instance involves functions of a [motor variable](wikipedia://en/2580834) where arguments are [split-complex numbers](wikipedia://en/893559).

:::

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), there are hypercomplex systems called [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305). The study of functions with arguments from a Clifford algebra is called [Clifford analysis](wikipedia://en/22424754).

A [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) may be considered a hypercomplex number. For example, the study of functions of 2 × 2 [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) matrices shows that the [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) of the [space](wikipedia://en/5308894) of hypercomplex numbers determines the function theory. Functions such as [square root of a matrix](wikipedia://en/2929701), [matrix exponential](wikipedia://en/672731), and [logarithm of a matrix](wikipedia://en/2931687) are basic examples of hypercomplex analysis.[^o2] The function theory of [diagonalizable matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonalizable_matrices) is particularly transparent since they have [eigendecompositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigendecomposition).[^o3] Suppose $\textstyle T = \sum _{i=1}^N \lambda_i E_i$ where the *E*<sub>*i*</sub> are [projections](wikipedia://en/519182). Then for any [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) $f$, $f(T) = \sum_{i=1}^N f(\lambda_i ) E_i.$

The modern terminology for a "system of hypercomplex numbers" is an *[algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) over the real numbers*, and the algebras used in applications are often [Banach algebras](wikipedia://en/4665) since [Cauchy sequences](wikipedia://en/6085) can be taken to be [convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_sequence). Then the function theory is enriched by [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) and [series](wikipedia://en/15287). In this context the extension of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) of a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) variable is developed as the [holomorphic functional calculus](wikipedia://en/3131706). Hypercomplex analysis on Banach algebras is called [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).
