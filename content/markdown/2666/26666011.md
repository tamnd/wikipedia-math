# Square knot (mathematics)

*Image: Square ribbon knot.svg.* The square knot, drawn as a [ribbon knot](wikipedia://en/9809292)

*Image: Square knot sum of trefoils stick number.png.* Square knot \= trefoil + trefoil reflection. [Sticks depicted](wikipedia://en/12527733).

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Square knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **square knot** is a [composite knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_knot) obtained by taking the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134#Connected_sum_of_knots) of a [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773) with its [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829). It is closely related to the [granny knot](wikipedia://en/26666182), which is also a connected sum of two trefoils. Because the trefoil knot is the simplest nontrivial knot, the square knot and the granny knot are the simplest of all composite knots.

:::

The square knot is the mathematical version of the common [reef knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_knot).

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The square knot can be constructed from two trefoil knots, one of which must be left-handed and the other right-handed. Each of the two knots is cut, and then the loose ends are joined together pairwise. The resulting connected sum is the square knot.

It is important that the original trefoil knots be mirror images of one another. If two identical trefoil knots are used instead, the result is a granny knot.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The square knot is [amphichiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphichiral_knot), meaning that it is indistinguishable from its own mirror image. The [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598) of a square knot is six, which is the smallest possible crossing number for a composite knot.

The [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) of the square knot is

$$\Delta(t) = (t - 1 + t^{-1})^2, \,$$

which is simply the [square](wikipedia://en/659942) of the Alexander polynomial of a trefoil knot. Similarly, the [Alexander–Conway polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander–Conway_polynomial) of a square knot is

$$\nabla(z) = (z^2+1)^2.$$

These two polynomials are the same as those for the granny knot. However, the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) for the square knot is

$$V(q) = (q^{-1} + q^{-3} - q^{-4})(q + q^3 - q^4) = -q^3 + q^2 - q + 3 - q^{-1} + q^{-2} - q^{-3}. \,$$

This is the product of the Jones polynomials for the right-handed and left-handed trefoil knots, and is different from the Jones polynomial for a granny knot.

The [knot group](wikipedia://en/1164126) of the square knot is given by the presentation

$$\langle x, y, z \mid x y x = y x y, x z x = z x z \rangle. \,$$

This is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the knot group of the granny knot, and is the simplest example of two different knots with isomorphic knot groups.

Unlike the granny knot, the square knot is a [ribbon knot](wikipedia://en/9809292), and it is therefore also a [slice knot](wikipedia://en/4158126).
