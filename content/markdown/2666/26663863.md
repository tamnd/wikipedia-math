# Jacob Lurie

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jacob Lurie"}

**Jacob Alexander Lurie** (born December 7, 1977) is an American mathematician who is a professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o3] In 2014, Lurie received a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program). Lurie's research interests are [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831).

:::

## Life {#Life}

When he was a student in the Science, Mathematics, and Computer Science Magnet Program at [Montgomery Blair High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_Blair_High_School), Lurie took part in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), where he won a gold medal with a perfect score in 1994.[^o5] In 1996 he took first place in the [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search) and was featured in a front-page story in the *[Washington Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Times)*.[^o6]

Lurie earned his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in mathematics from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) in 2000 and was awarded in the same year the [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157) for his undergraduate thesis on [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).[^o7] He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) under supervision of [Michael J. Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569), in 2004 with a thesis on [derived algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/40778281). In 2007, he became associate professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), and in 2009 he became professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o8][^o9] In 2019, he joined the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) as a permanent faculty member in mathematics.[^o10]

Lurie is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_people).[^o11]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Lurie's research interests started with [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and the theory of [surreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51432) when he was in high school.[^o13] He is best known for his work, starting with his thesis, on [infinity categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_category) and [derived algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/40778281). Derived algebraic geometry is a way of infusing [homotopical](wikipedia://en/61994831) methods into [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), with two purposes: deeper insight into algebraic geometry (e.g. into [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602)) and the use of methods of algebraic geometry in [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655). The latter area is the topic of Lurie's work on [elliptic cohomology](wikipedia://en/10295395). Infinity categories (in the form of [André Joyal](wikipedia://en/9157736)'s quasi-categories) are a convenient framework to do homotopy theory in abstract settings. They are the main topic of his book *[Higher Topos Theory](wikipedia://en/44533369)*.

Another part of Lurie's work is his article on [topological field theories](wikipedia://en/443757), where he sketches a classification of extended field theories using the language of infinity categories ([cobordism hypothesis](wikipedia://en/41419738)). In joint work with [Dennis Gaitsgory](wikipedia://en/6571030), he used his non-abelian [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) in an algebraic-geometric setting, to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) the [Siegel mass formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel_mass_formula) for [function fields](wikipedia://en/2783843).

Lurie was one of the inaugural winners of the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933) in 2014, "for his work on the foundations of [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013) and derived algebraic geometry; for the classification of fully extended topological quantum field theories; and for providing a moduli-theoretic interpretation of elliptic cohomology."[^o14] Lurie was also awarded a MacArthur "genius grant" Fellowship in 2014.[^o15][^o16]
