# Alpha shape

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alpha shape"}

*Image: ScagnosticsBase.svg.* Convex hull, alpha shape and [minimal spanning tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_spanning_tree) of a bivariate data set

In [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), an **alpha shape**, or ***α*-shape**, is a family of piecewise linear simple curves in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) associated with the shape of a finite set of points. They were first defined by (Edelsbrunner Kirkpatrick Seidel 1983). The alpha-shape associated with a set of points is a generalization of the concept of the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634), i.e., every convex hull is an alpha-shape but not every alpha shape is a convex hull.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

For each real number $\alpha$, define the concept of a *generalized disk of radius* $1/\alpha$ as follows:

- If $\alpha=0$, it is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [half-plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-plane);
- If $\alpha>0$, it is a closed disk of radius $1/\alpha$;
- If $\alpha<0$, it is the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of the complement of a disk of radius $-1/\alpha$.

Then, an edge of the alpha-shape is drawn between two members of the finite point set whenever there exists a generalized disk of radius $1/\alpha$ that has the two points on its [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) and that contains none of the point set in its [interior](wikipedia://en/55610).

If $\alpha=0$, then the alpha-shape associated with the finite point set is its ordinary convex hull.

:::

## Alpha complex {#Alpha_complex}

Alpha shapes are closely related to alpha complexes, which are subcomplexes of the [Delaunay triangulation](wikipedia://en/8864) of the point set. Each edge or triangle of the Delaunay triangulation may be associated with a characteristic radius: the radius of the smallest empty circle containing the edge or triangle. For each [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\alpha$, the $\alpha$-complex of the given set of points is the [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) formed by the set of edges and triangles whose radii are at most $1/\alpha$.

The $\alpha$-complex is also a subcomplex of the [Čech complex](wikipedia://en/53325526), but computationally more efficient if the [ambient space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_space) has dimension 2 or 3.[^o6][^o7]

The union of the edges and triangles in the $\alpha$-complex forms a shape closely resembling the $\alpha$-shape; however it differs in that it has polygonal edges rather than edges formed from arcs of circles. More specifically, (Edelsbrunner 1995) showed that the two shapes are [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent). (In this later work, Edelsbrunner used the name "$\alpha$-shape" to refer to the union of the cells in the $\alpha$-complex, and instead called the related curvilinear shape an $\alpha$-body.)

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Bulk Ag, Fermi surface.png.* Fermi surface of bulk silver: alpha-shape reconstruction from [KKR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korringa–Kohn–Rostoker_method) Bloch spectral function reconstruction

This technique can be employed to reconstruct a [Fermi surface](wikipedia://en/986096) from the electronic Bloch [spectral function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_function) evaluated at the [Fermi level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_level), as obtained from the [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001) in a generalised ab-initio study of the problem. The Fermi surface is then defined as the set of reciprocal space points within the first [Brillouin zone](wikipedia://en/562067), where the signal is highest. The definition has the advantage of covering also cases of various forms of disorder.

:::
