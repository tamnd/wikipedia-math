# Great Elephant Census

*Image: Great Elephant Census logo.png.*

*Image: African Elephants in Sweetwater National Parks Kenya.jpg.* African Elephants in Sweetwater National Parks, Kenya

*Image: African Savannah Elephant (Loxodonta africana) (6861464825).jpg.* An [African savannah elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_bush_elephant)

*Image: Makuleke6.JPG.* A group of elephants crossing the [Luvuvhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levubu_River) river

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great Elephant Census"}

The **Great Elephant Census** was an Africa-wide census designed to provide accurate data about the number and distribution of [African elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_elephant) by using standardized aerial surveys of terrain in Africa.[^o2] The census was published on the online journal *[PeerJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeerJ)* on 31 August 2016[^o3] at a cost of US\$7 million.[^o4] It's considered the largest wildlife census in history.

:::

## History {#History}

The scientific community believes that there were as many as 20 million African elephants in the 1800s and at the time of the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa). By 1979, an estimated 600,000 elephants remained on the continent, around a 97% decrease from pre-colonization populations.[^o2]

At the time, a pan-African elephant census had not been conducted since around 1979. The idea of a modern census was devised by the group Elephants Without Borders and supported, both financially and logistically, by [Paul Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Allen). It was also supported by other organizations and individuals, including African Parks, the [Frankfurt Zoological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Zoological_Society), [Wildlife Conservation Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Conservation_Society), [The Nature Conservancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nature_Conservancy), [IUCN African Elephant Specialist Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN), Howard Frederick, Mike Norton-Griffith, Kevin Dunham, Chris Touless, and Curtice Griffin (as credited by the final report).[^o6]

Mike Chase, the founder of Elephants Without Borders, was the lead scientist of the census. Chase led a group of 90 scientists and 286 crew members in 18 African countries for over two years to collect the data. During this time the team flew a distance of over 500000 km, equivalent to flying to the moon and a quarter of the way back, in over 10,000 hours of collecting data.[^o2] The area covered represents 93% of the elephants known range.[^o2]

[Forest Elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Elephant) which live in central and western Africa were excluded from the survey.[^o2]

## Report {#Report}

*Image: Elephant576.JPG.* An elephant skull with removed tusks, indicating that it was killed by poachers, [Voi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voi) area, Kenya

The final report was released on 31 August 2016 in Honolulu at the IUCN World Conservation Congress. Data collected showed a 30 percent decline in the population of African savanna elephant in 15 of the 18 countries surveyed.[^o8] The reduction occurred between 2007 and 2014, representing a loss of approximately 144,000 elephants.[^o2]

The total population of Africa's savannah elephants were enumerated as 352,271, far lower than what the surveyors had previously estimated.[^o2]

Three countries with significant elephant populations were not surveyed; [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia) didn't release any figures and both [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan) and the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic) were in the middle of an armed conflict.[^o9]

The rate of decline in the population is also accelerating and reached 8% in 2014.[^o10] The loss of population is primarily a result of [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching) with the elephants being killed for their [tusks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusk). The [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory) is then illegally sold around the world, such as [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) and [the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). As a result of the survey, it was estimated that approximately 100 elephants were killed every day for ivory. Losses of the elephant's [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat) are another reason for the drastic reduction in population.[^o10]

84% of the population were sighted in legally protected areas, such as reserves and national parks. Not only that, a high number of carcasses were found in the same protected areas, indicating poaching.[^o8]
