# Schur algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schur algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Schur algebras**, named after [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522), are certain [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) closely associated with [Schur&ndash;Weyl duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur&ndash;Weyl_duality) between [general linear](wikipedia://en/113564) and [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901). They are used to relate the [representation theories](wikipedia://en/19378200) of those two [groups](wikipedia://en/19447). Their use was promoted by the influential monograph of [J. A. Green](wikipedia://en/10670093) first published in 1980.[^o2] The name "Schur algebra" is due to Green. In the modular case (over infinite [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) of positive [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621)) Schur algebras were used by Gordon James and [Karin Erdmann](wikipedia://en/24659626) to show that the (still [open](wikipedia://en/1333335)) problems of computing decomposition numbers for general linear groups and symmetric groups are actually equivalent.[^o3] Schur algebras were used by [Friedlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Friedlander) and [Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820) to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) finite generation of [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of finite [group schemes](wikipedia://en/510523).[^o4]

:::

## Construction {#Construction}

The Schur algebra $S_k(n, r)$ can be defined for any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $k$ and [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $n, r \geq 0$. Consider the [algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) $k[x_{ij}]$ of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) (with [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) in $k$) in $n^2$ commuting variables $x_{ij}$, 1 ≤ *i*, *j* ≤ $n$. Denote by $A_k(n, r)$ the homogeneous polynomials of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) $r$. Elements of $A_k(n, r)$ are *k*-linear combinations of [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416) formed by multiplying together $r$ of the generators $x_{ij}$ (allowing repetition). Thus

$$k[x_{ij}] = \bigoplus_{r\ge 0} A_k(n, r).$$

Now, $k[x_{ij}]$ has a natural [coalgebra](wikipedia://en/310886) structure with comultiplication $\Delta$ and counit $\varepsilon$ the algebra homomorphisms given on generators by

$\Delta(x_{ij}) = \textstyle\sum_l x_{il} \otimes x_{lj}, \quad \varepsilon(x_{ij}) = \delta_{ij}\quad$ ([Kronecker's delta](wikipedia://en/182890)).

Since comultiplication is an algebra homomorphism, $k[x_{ij}]$ is a [bialgebra](wikipedia://en/310887). One easily checks that $A_k(n, r)$ is a subcoalgebra of the bialgebra $k[x_{ij}]$, for every *r* ≥ 0.

::: {#o7 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition.** The Schur algebra (in degree $r$) is the algebra $S_k (n, r) = \mathrm{Hom}_k( A_k (n, r), k)$. That is, $S_k(n,r)$ is the linear dual of $A_k(n,r)$.

It is a general fact that the linear [dual](wikipedia://en/7988) of a coalgebra $A$ is an algebra in a natural way, where the multiplication in the algebra is induced by dualizing the comultiplication in the coalgebra. To see this, let

$$\Delta(a) = \textstyle \sum a_i \otimes b_i$$

and, given linear functionals $f$, $g$ on $A$, define their product to be the linear functional given by

$$\textstyle a \mapsto \sum f(a_i) g(b_i).$$

The identity element for this multiplication of functionals is the counit in $A$.

:::

## Main properties {#Main_properties}

- One of the most basic properties expresses $S_k(n,r)$ as a centralizer algebra. Let $V = k^n$ be the space of rank $n$ column vectors over $k$, and form the [tensor](wikipedia://en/30844) power

$$V^{\otimes r} = V \otimes \cdots \otimes V \quad (r\text{ factors}).$$

Then the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $\mathfrak{S}_r$ on $r$ letters acts naturally on the tensor space by place permutation, and one has an isomorphism

$$S_k(n,r) \cong \mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{S}_r} (V^{\otimes r}).$$

In other words, $S_k(n,r)$ may be viewed as the algebra of [endomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endomorphisms) of tensor space commuting with the action of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901).

- $S_k(n,r)$ is free over $k$ of rank given by the [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) $\tbinom{n^2+r-1}{r}$.
- Various bases of $S_k(n,r)$ are known, many of which are indexed by pairs of semistandard [Young tableaux](wikipedia://en/683368) of shape $\lambda$, as $\lambda$ varies over the set of [partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) of $r$ into no more than $n$ parts.
- In case *k* is an infinite field, $S_k(n,r)$ may also be identified with the enveloping algebra (in the sense of H. Weyl) for the action of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ acting on $V^{\otimes r}$ (via the diagonal action on tensors, induced from the natural action of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ on $V = k^n$ given by matrix multiplication).
- Schur algebras are "defined over the integers". This means that they satisfy the following change of scalars property:

$$S_k(n,r) \cong S_{\mathbb{Z}}(n,r) \otimes _{\mathbb{Z}} k$$

for any commutative ring $k$.

- Schur algebras provide natural examples of quasihereditary algebras[^o9] (as defined by Cline, Parshall, and Scott), and thus have nice [homological](wikipedia://en/139410) properties. In particular, Schur algebras have finite [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739).

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

- **Generalized Schur algebras** (associated to any reductive [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924)) were introduced by Donkin in the 1980s.[^o12] These are also quasihereditary.
- Around the same time, Dipper and James[^o13] introduced the **quantized Schur algebras** (or **q-Schur algebras** for short), which are a type of q-deformation of the classical Schur algebras described above, in which the symmetric group is replaced by the corresponding [Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/521808) and the general linear group by an appropriate [quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678).
- There are also **generalized q-Schur algebras**, which are obtained by generalizing the work of Dipper and James in the same way that Donkin generalized the classical Schur algebras.[^o14]
- There are further generalizations, such as the **affine q-Schur algebras**[^o15] related to affine [Kac–Moody](wikipedia://en/1146292) [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) and other generalizations, such as the **cyclotomic q-Schur algebras**[^o16] related to Ariki-Koike algebras (which are q-deformations of certain [complex reflection groups](wikipedia://en/5381096)).

The study of these various classes of generalizations forms an active area of contemporary research.
