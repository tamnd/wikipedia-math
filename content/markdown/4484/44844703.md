# Orientation of a vector bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orientation of a vector bundle"}

In mathematics, an **orientation** of a real [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) is a generalization of an [orientation of a vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_of_a_vector_space); thus, given a real vector bundle π: *E* →*B*, an orientation of *E* means: for each fiber *E*<sub>*x*</sub>, there is an orientation of the vector space *E*<sub>*x*</sub> and one demands that each trivialization map (which is a bundle map)

:::

$$\phi_U : \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times \mathbf{R}^n$$

is fiberwise orientation-preserving, where **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is given the [standard orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_orientation). In more concise terms, this says that the structure group of the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) of *E*, which is the real [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) *GL*<sub>n</sub>(**R**), can be reduced to the subgroup consisting of those with positive determinant.

If *E* is a real vector bundle of rank *n*, then a choice of metric on *E* amounts to a reduction of the structure group to the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) *O*(*n*). In that situation, an orientation of *E* amounts to a reduction from *O*(*n*) to the [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) *SO*(*n*).

A vector bundle together with an orientation is called an **oriented bundle**. A vector bundle that can be given an orientation is called an **orientable vector bundle**.

The basic invariant of an oriented bundle is the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393). The multiplication (that is, cup product) by the Euler class of an oriented bundle gives rise to a [Gysin sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gysin_sequence).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A [complex vector bundle](wikipedia://en/1538109) is oriented in a canonical way.

The notion of an orientation of a vector bundle generalizes an [orientation of a *differentiable* manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_of_a_manifold): an orientation of a differentiable manifold is an orientation of its tangent bundle. In particular, a differentiable manifold is orientable if and only if its tangent bundle is orientable as a vector bundle. (note: as a manifold, a tangent bundle is always orientable.)

:::

## Operations {#Operations}

To give an orientation to a real vector bundle *E* of rank *n* is to give an orientation to the (real) [determinant bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinant_bundle) $\operatorname{det} E = \wedge^n E$ of *E*. Similarly, to give an orientation to *E* is to give an orientation to the [unit sphere bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_sphere_bundle) of *E*.

Just as a real vector bundle is classified by the real infinite [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810), oriented bundles are classified by the infinite Grassmannian of oriented real vector spaces.

## Thom space {#Thom_space}

From the cohomological point of view, for any ring Λ, a Λ-orientation of a real vector bundle *E* of rank *n* means a choice (and existence) of a class

$$u \in H^n(T(E); \Lambda)$$

in the cohomology ring of the [Thom space](wikipedia://en/1243451) *T*(*E*) such that *u* generates $\tilde{H}^*(T(E); \Lambda)$ as a free $H^*(E; \Lambda)$-module globally and locally: i.e.,

$$H^*(E; \Lambda) \to \tilde{H}^*(T(E); \Lambda), x \mapsto x \smile u$$

is an isomorphism (called the [Thom isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_isomorphism)), where "tilde" means [reduced cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_cohomology), that restricts to each isomorphism

$$H^*(\pi^{-1}(U); \Lambda) \to \tilde{H}^*(T(E|_U); \Lambda)$$

induced by the trivialization $\pi^{-1}(U) \simeq U \times \mathbf{R}^n$. One can show, with some work, that the usual notion of an orientation coincides with a **Z**-orientation.
