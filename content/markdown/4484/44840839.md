# Judy L. Walker

Judy Walker

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Judy L. Walker"}

**Judy Leavitt Walker** is an American mathematician. She is the Aaron Douglas Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln), where she chaired the mathematics department from 2012 through 2016 and currently serves as the Associate Vice Chancellor for Faculty Affairs. Her research is in the area of [algebraic coding theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_coding_theory).[^o3]

:::

In 2012, Walker became one of the inaugural [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4]

Throughout her career, Walker has worked on issues related to women in mathematics. She is co-founder of the University of Nebraska's All Girls/All Math program for high school girls; she represented her department at the White House when they were recognized with a 1998 Presidential Award for Excellence in Science, Mathematics, and Engineering mentoring; and she is co-founder of the Nebraska Conference for Undergraduate Women in Mathematics.

## Education {#Education}

Walker graduated from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1990,[^o3] and went on to graduate studies at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign), earning her Ph.D. in 1996 under the supervision of [Nigel Boston](wikipedia://en/36983565).[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

She joined the University of Nebraska–Lincoln faculty in 1996, was promoted to full professor in 2006 and took up the Aaron Douglas Professorship in 2012.[^o3] She held a visiting professorship at [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://www.epfl.ch) (EPFL) during fall 2011.

## Academic interests {#Academic_interests}

Walker's research is in [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869), i.e., the study of error-correcting codes. Her primary contributions have been in algebraic geometry codes and [low-density parity check codes](wikipedia://en/516393).
