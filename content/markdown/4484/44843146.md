# Susan Loepp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susan Loepp"}

**Susan Renee Loepp** (born 1967)[^o2] is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works as a professor of mathematics at [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College).[^o3] Her research concerns [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990).[^o13]

:::

## Professional career {#Professional_career}

Loepp graduated from [Bethel College (Kansas)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethel_College_%28Kansas%29) in 1989,[^o13][^o5] and earned her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1994 from the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin), under the supervision of [Raymond Heitmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Heitmann). After postdoctoral studies at the [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln) she took her present faculty position at Williams.[^o13][^o5] She has publications in [Journal of Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Algebra) and [Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Pure_and_Applied_Algebra).[^o13]

## Book {#Book}

With [William Wootters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wootters), she is the co-author of the book *Protecting Information: From Classical Error Correction to Quantum Cryptography* (Cambridge University Press, 2006). The book covers topics in [quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/28676005) and [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220) and the potential impacts of [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Physics). These potential impacts include quantum computers which, if built, could crack our currently used [public-key cryptosystems](wikipedia://en/24222), and quantum cryptography which promises to provide an alternative to these cryptosystems.[^o5][^o8][^o9]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2007, Loepp won the Young Alumnus Award from Bethel College.[^o11] In 2010, she won the Northeastern Section of the [Mathematical Association of America’s](wikipedia://en/198772) Teaching Award.[^o12] In 2012, she won a [Deborah and Franklin Tepper Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), which honors “college or university teachers who have been widely recognized as extraordinarily successful and whose teaching effectiveness has been shown to have had influence beyond their own institutions.”[^o13][^o14] In 2013, she was elected as one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o15]

Loepp was an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) Council member at large from 2019-2021.[^o16]
