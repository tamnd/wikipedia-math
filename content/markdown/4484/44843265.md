# Jane Cronin Scanlon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jane Cronin Scanlon"}

**Jane Smiley Cronin Scanlon** (July 17, 1922 – June 19, 2018) was an American mathematician and an [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor) of mathematics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University). Her research concerned [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Scanlon earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics from Wayne University (now [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University)).[^o10] She completed her Ph.D. in mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1949, under the supervision of [Erich Rothe](wikipedia://en/67679736). Her dissertation was *Branch Points of Solutions of Equations in Banach Space*.[^o3][^o4]

After working for the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) and the American Optical Company, she returned to academia as a lecturer at [Wheaton College (Massachusetts)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheaton_College_%28Massachusetts%29) and then [Stonehill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehill_College). She moved to the [Polytechnic Institute of Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Institute_of_Brooklyn) in 1957, and to Rutgers in 1965. In 1974 Scanlon was elected as an [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) Member at Large and held the position until 1976.[^o7] She retired in 1991.[^o3][^o4][^o8] During her twenty-six years at Rutgers, she supervised seven doctoral students.[^o9]

She died in June 2018 at the age of 95.[^o10]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Scanlon was a [Noether Lecturer](wikipedia://en/11566873) in 1985,[^o3] and [Pi Mu Epsilon](wikipedia://en/1565972) J. Sutherland Frame Lecturer in 1989.[^o12] Her talks concerned "[entrainment of frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrainment_%28physics%29)" and the application of this principle to mathematical models of the [Purkinje fibers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purkinje_fibers) in the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart).[^o3][^o12] In 2012, she became one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

She married the physicist Joseph Scanlon in 1953. The two divorced in 1979.[^o9] Upon her death, she was survived by four children and seven grandchildren.[^o10]
