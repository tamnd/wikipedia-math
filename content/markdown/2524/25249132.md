# Fibonorial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fibonorial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Fibonorial** $n!_{F}$, also called the **Fibonacci factorial**, where $n$ is a [nonnegative integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonnegative_integer), is defined as the product of the first $n$ positive [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_numbers), i.e.

:::

$${n!}_F := \prod_{i=1}^n F_i,\quad n \ge 0,$$

where $F_{i}$ is the $i$<sup>th</sup> Fibonacci number, and $0!_{F}$ gives the [empty product](wikipedia://en/181289) (defined as the [multiplicative identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_identity), i.e. 1).

The Fibonorial $n!_{F}$ is defined analogously to the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) $n!$. The Fibonorial numbers are used in the definition of [Fibonomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/22272370) (or Fibonacci-binomial coefficients) similarly as the factorial numbers are used in the definition of [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficients).

## Asymptotic behaviour {#Asymptotic_behaviour}

The [series](wikipedia://en/15287) of fibonorials is [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/3107) to a function of the golden ratio $\varphi$: $n!_F \sim C \frac {\varphi^{n (n+1)/2}} {5^{n/2}}$.

Here the *fibonorial constant* (also called the *fibonacci factorial constant*[^o3]) $C$ is defined by $C = \prod_{k=1}^\infty (1-a^k)$, where $a=-\frac{1}{\varphi^2}$ and $\varphi$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

An approximate truncated value of $C$ is 1.226742010720 (see for more digits).

## Almost-Fibonorial numbers {#Almost-Fibonorial_numbers}

Almost-Fibonorial numbers: $n!_{F} - 1$.

Almost-Fibonorial primes: [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers) among the almost-Fibonorial numbers.

## Quasi-Fibonorial numbers {#Quasi-Fibonorial_numbers}

Quasi-Fibonorial numbers: $n!_{F} + 1$.

Quasi-Fibonorial primes: [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers) among the quasi-Fibonorial numbers.

## Connection with the q-Factorial {#Connection_with_the_q-Factorial}

The fibonorial can be expressed in terms of the [q-factorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-factorial) and the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) $\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}2$:

$$n!_F = \varphi^{\binom n 2} \, [n]_{-\varphi^{-2}}!.$$

## Sequences {#Sequences}

Product of first $n$ nonzero Fibonacci numbers $F(1), ..., F(n)$.

and for $n$ such that $n!_{F} - 1$ and $n!_{F} + 1$ are primes, respectively.
