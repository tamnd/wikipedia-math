# Seven-dimensional space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Seven-dimensional space"}

**Seven-dimensional** (**7D**) **space** is a sequence of $n$ [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) (when $n$ = 7) that can be understood as a [location](wikipedia://en/593693) in [$n$-dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) space. Often such a space is studied as a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), without any notion of distance. Seven-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is seven-dimensional space equipped with a [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric), which is defined by the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093).

:::

More generally, the term may refer to a seven-dimensional vector space over any [field](wikipedia://en/10603), such as a seven-dimensional [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) vector space, which has 14 real dimensions. It may also refer to a seven-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) such as a [7-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-sphere), or a variety of other geometric constructions.

Seven-dimensional spaces have a number of special properties, many of them related to the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436). An especially distinctive property is that a [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) can be defined only in three or seven dimensions. This is related to [Hurwitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/25167317), which prohibits the existence of algebraic structures like the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and octonions in dimensions other than 2, 4, and 8. The first [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931) ever discovered were seven-dimensional.

## Geometry {#Geometry}

### 7-polytope {#7-polytope}

A [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) in seven dimensions is called a 7-polytope. The most studied are the [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693), of which there are only [three in seven dimensions](wikipedia://en/505253#Convex_4): the [7-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-simplex), [7-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-cube), and [7-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-orthoplex). A wider family are the [uniform 7-polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821719), constructed from fundamental symmetry domains of reflection, each domain defined by a [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004). Each uniform polytope is defined by a ringed [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram). The [7-demicube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-demicube) is a unique polytope from the D<sub>7</sub> family, and [3<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236353), [2<sub>31</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070163), and [1<sub>32</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070862) polytopes from the E<sub>7</sub> family.

### 6-sphere {#6-sphere}

The [6-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) or hypersphere in seven-dimensional Euclidean space is the six-dimensional surface equidistant from a point, e.g. the origin. It has symbol $S^{6}$, with formal definition for the 6-sphere with radius $r$ of

$$S^6 = \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R}^7 : \|x\| = r\right\}.$$

The volume of the space bounded by this 6-sphere is

$$V_7\,=\frac{16 \pi^3}{105}\,r^7$$

which is $4.72477 \times r^{7}$, or 0.0369 of the [7-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-cube) that contains the 6-sphere.

## Applications {#Applications}

### Cross product {#Cross_product}

A cross product, that is a vector-valued, [bilinear](wikipedia://en/4365), [anticommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutativity) and [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221) product of two vectors, is defined in seven dimensions. Along with the more usual [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) in three dimensions it is the only such product, except for trivial products.

### Exotic spheres {#Exotic_spheres}

In 1956, [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) constructed an [exotic sphere](wikipedia://en/1114931) in 7 dimensions and showed that there are at least 7 differentiable structures on the 7-sphere. In 1963 he showed that the exact number of such structures is 28.
