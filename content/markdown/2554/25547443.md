# Nash blowing-up

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nash blowing-up"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Nash blowing-up** is a process in which, roughly speaking, each [singular point](wikipedia://en/981915) is replaced by all limiting positions of the [tangent spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_spaces) at the non-singular points. More formally, let $X$ be an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) of pure [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) *r* [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in a [smooth variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) $Y$ of dimension *n*, and let $X_\text{reg}$ be the complement of the singular locus of $X$. Define a map $\tau:X_\text{reg}\rightarrow X\times G_{r}(TY)$, where $G_{r}(TY)$ is the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of *r*-planes in the tangent bundle of $Y$, by $\tau(a):=(a,T_{X,a})$, where $T_{X,a}$ is the tangent space of $X$ at $a$. The closure of the image of this map together with the projection to $X$ is called the Nash blow-up of $X$.

:::

Although the above construction uses an embedding, the Nash blow-up itself is unique up to unique isomorphism.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Nash blowing-up is locally a [monoidal transformation](wikipedia://en/1817790).
- If *X* is a [complete intersection](wikipedia://en/3116835) defined by the vanishing of $f_1,f_2,\ldots,f_{n-r}$ then the Nash blow-up is the blow-up with center given by the ideal generated by the (*n* − *r*)-minors of the matrix with entries $\partial f_i/\partial x_j$.
- For a variety over a field of characteristic zero, the Nash blow-up is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) if and only if *X* is non-singular.
- For an algebraic curve over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) of characteristic zero, repeated Nash blowing-up leads to [desingularization](wikipedia://en/9914115) after a finite number of steps.
- Both of the prior properties may fail in positive characteristic. For example, in characteristic *q* > 0, the curve $y^2-x^q=0$ has a Nash blow-up which is the monoidal transformation with center given by the ideal $(x^{q})$, for *q* = 2, or $(y^2)$, for $q>2$. Since the center is a hypersurface the blow-up is an isomorphism.

:::
