# Tacnode

*Image: Tacnode.gif.* A tacnode at the origin of the curve defined by $(x^2+y^2-3x)^2 - 4x^2(2-x) = 0.$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tacnode"}

In classical [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **tacnode** (also called a **point of osculation** or **double cusp**)[^o2] is a kind of [singular point](wikipedia://en/4230456) of a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246). It is defined as a point where two (or more) [osculating circles](wikipedia://en/1896705) to the curve at that point are [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482). This means that two branches of the curve have ordinary tangency at the double point.[^o2]

:::

The canonical example is

$$y^2-x^4= 0.$$

A tacnode of an arbitrary curve may then be defined from this example, as a point of self-tangency [locally](wikipedia://en/1462457) [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to the point at the origin of this curve. Another example of a tacnode is given by the [links curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Links_curve) shown in the figure, with equation

$$(x^2+y^2-3x)^2 - 4x^2(2-x) = 0.$$

## More general background {#More_general_background}

Consider a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [real-valued function](wikipedia://en/22284647) of two [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109), say $f (x, y)$ where $x$ and $y$ are [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). So $f$ is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) from the plane to the line. The space of all such smooth functions is [acted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) upon by the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) of the plane and the diffeomorphisms of the line, i.e. diffeomorphic changes of [coordinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate) in both the [source](wikipedia://en/50263) and the [target](wikipedia://en/275991). This action splits the whole [function space](wikipedia://en/340630) up into [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260), i.e. [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_orbit#Orbits_and_stabilizers) of the group action.

One such family of equivalence classes is [denoted by](wikipedia://en/23802570) $A_k^\pm,$ where $k$ is a non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). This notation was introduced by [V. I. Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._I._Arnold). A function $f$ is said to be of type $A_k^\pm$ if it lies in the orbit of $x^2 \pm y^{k+1},$ i.e. there exists a diffeomorphic change of coordinate in source and target which takes $f$ into one of these forms. These simple forms $x^2 \pm y^{k+1}$ are said to give [normal forms](wikipedia://en/515096) for the type $A_k^\pm$-singularities.

A curve with equation $f = 0$ will have a tacnode, say at the origin, if and only if $f$ has a type $A_3^-$-singularity at the origin.

Notice that a [node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node_%28algebraic_geometry%29) $(x^2-y^2=0)$ corresponds to a type $A_1^-$-singularity. A tacnode corresponds to a type $A_3^-$-singularity. In fact each type $A_{2n+1}^-$-singularity, where $n \ge 0$ is an integer, corresponds to a curve with self-intersection. As $n$ increases, the order of self-intersection increases: transverse crossing, ordinary tangency, etc.

The type $A_{2n+1}^+$-singularities are of no interest over the real numbers: they all give an isolated point. Over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), type $A_{2n+1}^+$-singularities and type $A_{2n+1}^-$-singularities are equivalent: $(x, y) \to (x, iy)$ gives the required diffeomorphism of the normal forms.
