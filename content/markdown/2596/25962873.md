# Credit valuation adjustment

::: {#o1 .table cue=table}

| CVA related concepts: |
|---|
| The mathematical concept as defined below; A part of the regulatory Capital and RWA ([risk-weighted asset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk-weighted_asset)) calculation [^o3] introduced under [Basel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III); The CVA internal department of an investment bank, whose purpose is to: hedge for possible losses due to counterparty default; hedge to reduce the amount of capital required under the CVA calculation of [Basel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III); The "CVA charge". The hedging of the CVA desk has a cost associated to it, i.e. the bank has to buy the hedging instrument. This cost is then allocated to each business line of an investment bank (usually as a contra revenue). This allocated cost is called the "CVA Charge". |

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Credit valuation adjustment"}

A **Credit valuation adjustment** (**CVA**),

in [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics), is an "adjustment" to a [derivative's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_%28finance%29) price, as charged by a bank to a [counterparty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterparty#Financial_services_sector) to compensate it for taking on the [credit risk of that counterparty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterparty_credit_risk) during the life of the transaction. "CVA" can refer more generally to several related concepts, as delineated aside. The most common transactions attracting CVA involve [interest rate derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_derivative), [foreign exchange derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_derivative), and combinations thereof. CVA has a specific [capital charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_requirement) under [Basel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III), and may also result in earnings volatility under [IFRS 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFRS_13), and is therefore managed by a specialized desk. CVA is one of a family of related valuation adjustments, collectively [xVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVA); for further context here see Financial economics.

:::

## Calculation {#Calculation}

In [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics) one defines CVA as the difference between the risk-free portfolio value and the true [portfolio value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_%28finance%29) that takes into account the possibility of a [counterparty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterparty)'s default. In other words, CVA is the [market value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_value) of [counterparty credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterparty_risk). This price adjustment will depend on counterparty [credit spreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_spread_%28bond%29) as well as on the market risk factors that drive derivatives' values and, therefore, exposure. It is typically calculated under [a simulation framework](wikipedia://en/1358940).[^o6] [^o13] [^o7] (Which can become computationally intensive; see .)

### Risk-neutral expectation {#Risk-neutral_expectation}

Unilateral CVA is given by the [risk-neutral](wikipedia://en/441911) expectation of the discounted loss. The risk-neutral expectation can be written [^o9] [^o10] as

$$\mathrm{CVA(T)} = E^Q[L^*] = \int_0^T E^Q\left[\left.LGD\frac{B_0}{B_t} E(t)\;\right|\;t=\tau\right] d\mathrm{PD}(0,t)$$

where $T$ is the [maturity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maturity_%28finance%29) of the longest transaction in the portfolio, $B_t$ is the future value of one unit of the [base currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_pair) invested today at the prevailing interest rate for maturity $t$, $LGD$ is the [loss given default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_given_default), $\tau$ is the time of default, $E(t)$ is the exposure at time $t$, and $\mathrm{PD}(s,t)$ is the risk neutral probability of counterparty default between times $s$ and $t$. These probabilities can be obtained from the term structure of [credit default swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_default_swap) (CDS) spreads.

### Exposure, independent of counterparty default {#Exposure,_independent_of_counterparty_default}

Assuming independence between exposure and counterparty's [credit quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_quality) greatly simplifies the analysis. Under this assumption this simplifies to

$$\mathrm{CVA} = LGD \int_0^T \mathrm{EE}^*(t)~d\mathrm{PD}(0,t)$$

where $\mathrm{EE}^*$ is the risk-neutral discounted expected exposure (EE):

$$\mathrm{EE}^*(t) = \mathbb{E}\left\lbrack{\frac{B_0}{B_t}~E(t)}\right\rbrack$$

### Approximation {#Approximation}

The full calculation of CVA, as above, is via a [Monte-Carlo simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte-Carlo_simulation) on all risk factors; this is computationally demanding. There exists a simple [approximation](wikipedia://en/336271) for CVA, sometimes referred to as the "net current exposure method".[^o13] This consists in: buying default protection, typically a [credit default swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Default_Swap), netted for each counterparty; and the CDS price may then be used to [back out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solver) the CVA charge.[^o13][^o14]

### Accounting treatment {#Accounting_treatment}

The CVA charge may be seen as an [accounting adjustment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjusting_entries) made to [reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_%28accounting%29) a portion of profits on uncollateralized financial derivatives. These reserved profits can be viewed as the [net present value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_present_value) of the [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk) embedded in the transaction. Thus, as outlined, under [IFRS 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFRS_13) changes in counterparty risk will result in earnings volatility; see XVA#Accounting impact and next section.

## Function of the CVA desk {#Function_of_the_CVA_desk}

In the course of trading and investing, Tier 1 [investment banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banking) generate counterparty *EPE* and *ENE* (expected positive/negative [exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_future_exposure)). Whereas historically, this exposure was a concern of both the [front office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_office) trading desk and [middle office finance teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banking#Middle_office), increasingly CVA pricing and hedging is under the "ownership" of a centralized CVA lending and treasury desk.[^o17][^o18]

In particular, this desk addresses volatility in earnings due to the abovementioned [IFRS 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFRS_13) [accounting standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounting_standard) requiring that CVA be considered in [mark-to-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark-to-market) accounting. The [hedging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_%28finance%29) here focuses on addressing changes to the counterparty's [credit worthiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_spread_%28bond%29), offsetting [potential future exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_future_exposure) at a given quantile. Further, since under [Basel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III), banks are required to hold specific [regulatory capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_capital) on the net CVA-risk,[^o13] the CVA desk is responsible also for managing (minimizing) the capital requirements under Basel.
