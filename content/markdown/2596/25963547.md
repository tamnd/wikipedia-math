::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Rectified 5-orthoplexes"}

In five-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **rectified 5-orthoplex** is a convex [uniform 5-polytope](wikipedia://en/14415338), being a [rectification](wikipedia://en/2192366) of the regular [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615).

:::

There are 5 degrees of rectifications for any 5-polytope, the zeroth here being the [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615) itself, and the 4th and last being the [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215). Vertices of the rectified 5-orthoplex are located at the edge-centers of the 5-orthoplex. Vertices of the birectified 5-orthoplex are located in the triangular face centers of the 5-orthoplex.

Its 40 vertices represent the root vectors of the [simple Lie group](wikipedia://en/292831) D<sub>5</sub>. The vertices can be seen in 3 [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862), with the 10 vertices [rectified 5-cells](wikipedia://en/2720430) cells on opposite sides, and 20 vertices of a [runcinated 5-cell](wikipedia://en/2386577) passing through the center. When combined with the 10 vertices of the [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615), these vertices represent the 50 root vectors of the B<sub>5</sub> and C<sub>5</sub> simple [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).

[E. L. Elte](wikipedia://en/27806942) identified it in 1912 as a semiregular polytope, identifying it as Cr<sub>5</sub><sup>1</sup> as a first rectification of a 5-dimensional [cross polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_polytope).

### Construction {#Construction}

There are two [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) associated with the *rectified pentacross*, one with the C<sub>5</sub> or \[4,3,3,3\] Coxeter group, and a lower symmetry with two copies of *16-cell* facets, alternating, with the D<sub>5</sub> or \[3<sup>2,1,1</sup>\] Coxeter group.

:::
