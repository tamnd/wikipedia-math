# Ogawa integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ogawa integral"}

In [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001), the **Ogawa integral**, also called the **non-causal stochastic integral**, is a [stochastic integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_integral) for [non-adapted processes](wikipedia://en/6583420) as [integrands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrand). The corresponding calculus is called **non-causal calculus** which distinguishes it from the anticipating calculus of the [Skorokhod integral](wikipedia://en/18420736). The term causality refers to the adaptation to the [natural filtration](wikipedia://en/5864648) of the integrator.

:::

The integral was introduced by the Japanese mathematician [Shigeyoshi Ogawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeyoshi_Ogawa) in 1979.[^o2]

## Ogawa integral {#Ogawa_integral}

Let

- $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ be a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325),
- $W=(W_t)_{t\in[0,T]}$ be a one-dimensional standard [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) with $T\in\mathbb{R}_+$,
- $\mathcal{F}_t^W=\sigma(W_s;0\leq s \leq t)\subset \mathcal{F}$ and $\mathbf{F}^W=\{\mathcal{F}_t^W, t\geq 0\}$ be the natural filtration of the Wiener process,
- $\mathcal{B}([0,T])$ the [Borel σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_σ-algebra),
- $\int f\; dW_t$ be the Wiener integral,
- $dt$ be the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198).

Further let $\mathbf{H}$ be the set of real-valued processes $X\colon [0,T]\times \Omega \to\mathbb{R}$ that are $\mathcal{B}([0,T])\times \mathcal{F}$-measurable and [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) in $L^2([0,T],dt)$, i.e.

$$P\left(\int_0^T |X(t,\omega)|^2 \, dt< \infty\right)=1.$$

### Ogawa integral {#Ogawa_integral}

Let $\{\varphi_n\}_{n\in \mathbb{N}}$ be a [complete](wikipedia://en/195063#Incomplete_orthogonal_sets) [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) of the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $L^2([0,T],dt)$.

A process $X\in\mathbf{H}$ is called $\varphi$-integrable if the random series

$$\int_0^T X_t \, d_\varphi W_t:=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(\int_0^T X_t \varphi_n(t) \, dt\right) \int_0^T\varphi_n(t) \, dW_t$$

[converges in probability](wikipedia://en/50723#Convergence_in_probability) and the corresponding sum is called the **Ogawa integral** with respect to the basis $\{\varphi_n\}$.

If $X$ is $\varphi$-integrable for any complete orthonormal basis of $L^2([0,T],dt)$ and the corresponding integrals share the same value then $X$ is called **universal Ogawa integrable** (or **u-integrable**).[^o5]

More generally, the Ogawa integral can be defined for any $L^2(\Omega,P)$-process $Z_t$ (such as the [fractional Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/1607785)) as integrators

$$\int_0^T X_t \, d_\varphi Z_t:=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(\int_0^T X_t \varphi_n(t) \, dt\right) \int_0^T\varphi_n(t) \, dZ_t$$

as long as the integrals

$$\int_0^T\varphi_n(t) \, dZ_t$$

are well-defined.[^o5]

#### Remarks {#Remarks}

- The convergence of the series depends not only on the orthonormal basis but also on the ordering of that basis.
- There exist various equivalent definitions for the Ogawa integral which can be found in ([^o5]). One way makes use of the [Itô–Nisio theorem](wikipedia://en/74090169).

#### Regularity of the orthonormal basis {#Regularity_of_the_orthonormal_basis}

An important concept for the Ogawa integral is the *regularity* of an orthonormal basis. An orthonormal basis $\{\varphi_n\}_{n\in \mathbb{N}}$ is called *regular* if

$$\sup_n \int_0^T \left( \sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_i(t)\int_0^t \varphi_i(s) \, ds\right)^2 \, dt<\infty$$

holds.

The following results on regularity are known:

- Every [semimartingale](wikipedia://en/9653775) (causal or not) is $\varphi$-integrable if and only if $\{\varphi_n\}$ is regular.[^o5]
- It was proven that there exist a non-regular basis for $L^2([0,1], dt)$.[^o8]

## Further topics {#Further_topics}

- There exist a non-causal [Itô formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_formula),[^o5] a non-causal integration by parts formula and a non-causal [Girsanov theorem](wikipedia://en/336940).[^o10]

### Relationship to other integrals {#Relationship_to_other_integrals}

- [Stratonovich integral](wikipedia://en/2314574): let $X$ be a continuous $\mathbf{F}^W$-adapted semimartingale that is universal Ogawa integrable with respect to the Wiener process, then the Stratonovich integral exist and coincides with the Ogawa integral.[^o12]
- [Skorokhod integral](wikipedia://en/18420736): the relationship between the Ogawa integral and the Skorokhod integral was studied in ([^o13]).
