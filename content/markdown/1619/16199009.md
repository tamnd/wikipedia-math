# Differential fault analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential fault analysis"}

**Differential fault analysis** (DFA) is a type of active [side-channel attack](wikipedia://en/667678) in the field of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), specifically [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715). The principle is to induce *faults*—unexpected environmental conditions—into cryptographic operations to reveal their internal states.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Principles"}

## Principles {#Principles}

Taking a [smartcard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartcard) containing an embedded [processor](wikipedia://en/59865) as an example, some unexpected environmental conditions it could experience include being subjected to high temperature, receiving [unsupported supply voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glitching) or current, being excessively [overclocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overclocking), experiencing strong [electric](wikipedia://en/41092) or [magnetic fields](wikipedia://en/36563), or even receiving [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation) to influence the operation of the processor. When stressed like this, the processor may begin to output incorrect results due to physical [data corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_corruption), which may help a [cryptanalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalyst) deduce the instructions that the processor is running, or what the internal state of its data is.[^o4][^o5]

For [DES](wikipedia://en/7978) and [Triple DES](wikipedia://en/48375), about 200 single-flipped bits are necessary to obtain a secret [key](wikipedia://en/53039).[^o6] DFA has also been applied successfully to the [AES](wikipedia://en/1260) cipher.[^o7]

Many countermeasures have been proposed to defend from these kinds of attacks. Most of them are based on error detection schemes.[^o8][^o9]

:::

## Fault injection {#Fault_injection}

A fault injection attack involves stressing the [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor) responsible for [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294) tasks to generate faults that will then be used as input for analysis. The stress can be an electromagnetic pulse (EM pulse or [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser) pulse).

Practical fault injection consists of using an electromagnetic probe connected to a pulser or a laser generating a disturbance of a similar length to the processor's [cycle time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_rate) (of the order of a nanosecond). The energy transferred to the chip may be sufficient to burn out certain components of the chip, so the voltage of the pulser (a few hundred volts) and the positioning of the probe must be finely calibrated. For greater precision, the chips are often decapsulated (chemically eroded to expose the bare silicon).[^o11]
