# Whitehead's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitehead's lemma"}

**Whitehead's lemma** is a technical result in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) used in [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500). It states that a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) of the form

:::

$$\begin{bmatrix} u & 0 \\ 0 & u^{-1} \end{bmatrix}$$

is equivalent to the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) by [elementary transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_matrices) (that is, transvections):

$$\begin{bmatrix} u & 0 \\ 0 & u^{-1} \end{bmatrix} = e_{21}(u^{-1}) e_{12}(1-u) e_{21}(-1) e_{12}(1-u^{-1}).$$

Here, $e_{ij}(s)$ indicates a matrix whose diagonal block is $1$ and $ij$-th entry is $s$.

The name "Whitehead's lemma" also refers to the closely related result that the [derived group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_group) of the [stable general linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_general_linear_group) is the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) generated by [elementary matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_matrices).[^o2][^o3] In symbols,

$$\operatorname{E}(A) = [\operatorname{GL}(A),\operatorname{GL}(A)].$$

This holds for the stable group (the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/15063922) of matrices of finite size) over any [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), but not in general for the unstable groups, even over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). For instance for

$$\operatorname{GL}(2, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$$

one has:

$$\operatorname{Alt}(3) \cong [\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})] < \operatorname{E}_2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) = \operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) = \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(3),$$

where Alt(3) and Sym(3) denote the [alternating](wikipedia://en/49726) resp. [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on 3 letters.
