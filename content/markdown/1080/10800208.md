# Zuckerman functor

This article is about the Zuckerman induction functor, which is not the same as the (Zuckerman) [translation functor](wikipedia://en/32176298).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zuckerman functor"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Zuckerman functor** is used to construct representations of real [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386) [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) from representations of [Levi subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_subgroup). They were introduced by [Gregg Zuckerman](wikipedia://en/10822168) (1978). The **Bernstein functor** is closely related.

:::

## Notation and terminology {#Notation_and_terminology}

- *G* is a connected reductive real affine [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) (for simplicity; the theory works for more general groups), and *g* is the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of *G*.
- *K* is a [maximal compact subgroup](wikipedia://en/1496726) of *G*.
- A **(g,K)-module** is a vector space with compatible actions of *g* and *K*, on which the action of *K* is *K*-finite. A representation of *K* is called **[K-finite](wikipedia://en/11054062)** if every vector is contained in a finite-dimensional representation of *K*.
- *W*<sub>*K*</sub> is the subspace of *K*-finite vectors of a representation *W* of *K*.
- R(*g*,*K*) is the [Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/32197567) of *G* of all distributions on *G* with support in *K* that are left and right *K* finite. This is a ring which does not have an identity but has an [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628), and the approximately unital R(*g*,*K*)- modules are the same as (*g*,*K*) modules.
- *L* is a [Levi subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_subgroup) of *G*, the centralizer of a compact connected abelian subgroup, and *l* is the Lie algebra of *L*.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Zuckerman functor Γ is defined by

$$\Gamma^{g,K}_{g,L\cap K}(W) = \hom_{R(g,L\cap K)}(R(g,K),W)_K$$

and the Bernstein functor Π is defined by

$$\Pi^{g,K}_{g,L\cap K}(W) = R(g,K)\otimes_{R(g,L\cap K)}W.$$

:::
