# Charlotte Wedell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charlotte Wedell"}

**Charlotte Bolette Sophie, Baroness Wedell-Wedellsborg** (27 January 1862 – 22 July 1953) was one of four women mathematicians to attend the inaugural [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), held in Zurich in 1897.

:::

## Early life and background {#Early_life_and_background}

Wedell was originally from [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark), the daughter of Vilhelm Ferdinand, Baron Wedell-Wedellsborg (of the [Wedel noble family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedel_%28family%29)) and Louise Marie Sophie, Countess Schulin, and the granddaughter of Johan Sigismund Schulin (1808–1880).

## Career {#Career}

At the time of the Congress, in 1897, she had just completed a doctorate at the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne) in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), with [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690) as an unofficial mentor. The subject of her dissertation was the application of [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) to the construction of the [Malfatti circles](wikipedia://en/1475894).

At the congress, Wedell was listed as being affiliated with the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). The other three women at the congress were Iginia Massarini, Vera von Schiff, and [Charlotte Scott](wikipedia://en/8610998). None were speakers; the first Congress with a woman as a speaker was in 1912.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Wedell married engineer Eugène Tomasini in Copenhagen in 1898; they divorced in 1909.
