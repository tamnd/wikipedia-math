# David Drasin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Drasin"}

**David Drasin** (born 3 November 1940, [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia)) is an American mathematician, specializing in function theory.

:::

Drasin received in 1962 his bachelor's degree from [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) and in 1966 his doctorate from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) supervised by [Wolfgang Fuchs](wikipedia://en/5397705) and [Clifford John Earle, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_John_Earle_Jr.) with thesis *An integral Tauberian theorem and other topics*. After that he was an assistant professor, from 1969 an associate professor, and from 1974 a full professor at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University). He was visiting professor in 2005 at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel) and in 2005/2006 at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki).

In 1976, Drasin gave a complete solution to the inverse problem of [Nevanlinna theory](wikipedia://en/393135) (value distribution theory),[^o3] which was posed by [Rolf Nevanlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Nevanlinna) in 1929.[^o4] In the 1930s, the problem was investigated by Nevanlinna and by, among others, [Egon Ullrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Ullrich)<sup>(de)</sup> (1902–1957) with later investigations by [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller) (1913–1943), Hans Wittich, [Le Van Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Van_Thiem) (1918–1991) and other mathematicians. [Anatolii Goldberg](wikipedia://en/34278093) (1930–2008) was the first to completely solve the inverse problem in the special case where the number of exceptional values is finite.[^o5] For entire functions the problem was solved in 1962 by Wolfgang Fuchs and [Walter Hayman](wikipedia://en/34191584).[^o6] The general problem concerns the question of the existence of a meromorphic function at given values of the exceptional values and associated deficiency values and branching values (with constraints from the Nevanlinna theory). Drasin proved that there is a positive answer to Nevanlinna's problem.[^o7]

In 1994 Drasin was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Zurich.[^o8] Since 1996 he is a co-editor of the *Annals of the Finnish Academy of Sciences* and a co-editor of *Computational Methods in Function Theory*. He was a co-editor of the [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) from 1968 to 1971. From 2002 to 2004 he was a program director/analyst for the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation).

He is married and has three children.
