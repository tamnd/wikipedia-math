# Perfect magic cube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perfect magic cube"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **perfect magic cube** is a [magic cube](wikipedia://en/372356) in which not only the columns, rows, pillars, and main [space diagonals](wikipedia://en/1337505), but also the [cross section](wikipedia://en/1337370) diagonals sum up to the cube's [magic constant](wikipedia://en/546969).[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

Perfect magic cubes of order one are trivial; cubes of orders two to four can be [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) not to exist,[^o5] and cubes of orders five and six were first discovered by [Walter Trump](wikipedia://en/2885379) and [Christian Boyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Boyer) on November 13 and September 1, 2003, respectively.[^o6] A perfect magic cube of order seven was given by [A. H. Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._H._Frost) in 1866, and on March 11, 1875, an article was published in the [Cincinnati Commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Commercial) newspaper on the discovery of a perfect magic cube of order 8 by [Gustavus Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus_Frankenstein). Perfect magic cubes of orders nine and eleven have also been constructed. The first perfect cube of order 10 was constructed in 1988 (Li Wen, China).[^o10]

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="An alternative definition"}

## An alternative definition {#An_alternative_definition}

In recent years, an alternative definition for the perfect magic cube was proposed by [John R. Hendricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Hendricks). By this definition, a perfect magic cube is one in which all possible lines through each cell sum to the magic constant. The name [Nasik magic hypercube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasik_magic_hypercube) is another, unambiguous, name for such a cube. This definition is based on the fact that a [pandiagonal magic square](wikipedia://en/491524) has traditionally been called 'perfect', because all possible lines sum correctly.[^o9]

Gabriel Arnoux constructed an order 17 perfect magic cube in 1887. F.A.P.Barnard published order 8 and order 11 perfect cubes in 1888.[^o10]

By the modern (given by [J.R. Hendricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Hendricks)) definition, there are actually six [classes of magic cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_cube_class); [simple magic cubes](wikipedia://en/13268241), [pantriagonal magic cubes](wikipedia://en/1403538), [diagonal magic cubes](wikipedia://en/1403664), pantriagonal diagonal magic cubes, [pandiagonal magic cubes](wikipedia://en/1403847), and perfect magic cubes.[^o9]

:::
