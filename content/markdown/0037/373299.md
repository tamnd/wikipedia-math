# Language of mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Language of mathematics"}

The **language of mathematics** or **mathematical language** is an extension of the [natural language](wikipedia://en/21173) (for example [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)) that is used in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and in [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) for expressing results ([scientific laws](wikipedia://en/244629), [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977), [proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_%28mathematics%29), [logical deductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_deduction), etc.) with concision, precision and unambiguity.

:::

## Features {#Features}

The main features of the mathematical language are the following.

- Use of common words with a derived meaning, generally more specific and more precise. For example, "[or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Or_%28logic%29)" means "one, the other or both", while, in common language, "both" is sometimes included and sometimes not. Also, a "[line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29)" is straight and has zero width.
- Use of common words with a meaning that is completely different from their common meaning. For example, a mathematical [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) is not related to any other meaning of "ring". [Real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) and [imaginary numbers](wikipedia://en/14554) are two sorts of numbers, none being more real or more imaginary than the others.
- Use of [neologisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism). For example [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000), [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564).
- Use of [symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_%28mathematics%29) as words or phrases. For example, $A=B$ and $\forall x$ are respectively read as "$A$ equals $B$" and "for all $x$".
- Use of [formulas](wikipedia://en/164040) as part of sentences. For example: "$E=mc^2$*represents quantitatively the [mass–energy equivalence](wikipedia://en/422481).*" A formula that is not included in a sentence is generally meaningless, since the meaning of the symbols may depend on the context: in "$E=mc^2\,$", this is the context that specifies that $E$ is the [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) of a [physical body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body), $m$ is its [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass), and $c$ is the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light).
- Use of phrases that cannot be decomposed into their components. In particular, adjectives do not always restrict the meaning of the corresponding noun, and may change the meaning completely. For example, most [algebraic integers](wikipedia://en/98981) are not [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) and integers are specific algebraic integers. So, an *algebraic integer* is not an *integer* that is *algebraic*.
- Use of [mathematical jargon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_jargon) that consists of phrases that are used for informal explanations or shorthands. For example, "killing" is often used in place of "replacing with zero", and this led to the use of *[assassinator](wikipedia://en/17286442)* and *[annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684)* as technical words.

## Understanding mathematical text {#Understanding_mathematical_text}

The consequence of these features is that a mathematical text is generally not understandable without some prerequisite knowledge. For example, the sentence "*a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) that has a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420)*" is perfectly correct, although it may appear as grammatically correct nonsense to someone who does not know the definitions of *basis*, *module*, and *free module*.

[H. B. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Burt_Williams), an [electrophysiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrophysiologist), wrote in 1927: Now mathematics is both a body of truth and a special language, a language more carefully defined and more highly abstracted than our ordinary medium of thought and expression. Also it differs from ordinary languages in this important particular: it is subject to rules of manipulation. Once a statement is cast into mathematical form it may be manipulated in accordance with these rules and every configuration of the symbols will represent facts in harmony with and dependent on those contained in the original statement. Now this comes very close to what we conceive the action of the brain structures to be in performing intellectual acts with the symbols of ordinary language. In a sense, therefore, the mathematician has been able to perfect a device through which a part of the labor of logical thought is carried on outside the [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system) with only that supervision which is requisite to manipulate the symbols in accordance with the rules.[^o4]
