# Real tree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real tree"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **real trees** (also called **$\mathbb R$-trees**) are a class of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) generalising simplicial [trees](wikipedia://en/48560). They arise naturally in many mathematical contexts, in particular [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). They are also the simplest examples of [Gromov hyperbolic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov_hyperbolic_space).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and examples"}

## Definition and examples {#Definition_and_examples}

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

### Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

*Image: Triangle in a tree.svg.* A triangle in a real tree

A metric space $X$ is a real tree if it is a [geodesic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space) where every triangle is a tripod. That is, for every three points $x, y, \rho \in X$ there exists a point $c = x \wedge y$ such that the geodesic segments $[\rho,x], [\rho,y]$ intersect in the segment $[\rho,c]$ and also $c \in [x,y]$. This definition is equivalent to $X$ being a "zero-hyperbolic space" in the sense of Gromov (all triangles are "zero-thin").

Real trees can also be characterised by a [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) property. A metric space $X$ is a real tree if for any pair of points $x, y \in X$ all [topological embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_embedding) $\sigma$ of the segment $[0,1]$ into $X$ such that $\sigma(0) = x, \, \sigma(1) = y$ have the same image (which is then a geodesic segment from $x$ to $y$).

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Simple examples"}

### Simple examples {#Simple_examples}

- If $X$ is a connected graph with the combinatorial metric then it is a real tree if and only if it is a tree (i.e. it has no [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609)). Such a tree is often called a simplicial tree. They are characterised by the following topological property: a real tree $T$ is simplicial if and only if the set of singular points of $X$ (points whose complement in $X$ has three or more connected components) is closed and discrete in $X$.
- The $\mathbb R$-tree obtained in the following way is nonsimplicial. Start with the interval \[0, 2\] and glue, for each positive integer *n*, an interval of length 1/*n* to the point 1 − 1/*n* in the original interval. The set of singular points is discrete, but fails to be closed since 1 is an ordinary point in this $\mathbb R$-tree. Gluing an interval to 1 would result in a [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) of singular points at the expense of discreteness.
- The [Paris metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_metric) makes the plane into a real tree. It is defined as follows: one fixes an origin $P$, and if two points are on the same ray from $P$, their distance is defined as the Euclidean distance. Otherwise, their distance is defined to be the sum of the Euclidean distances of these two points to the origin $P$.
- The plane under the Paris metric is an example of a [hedgehog space](wikipedia://en/8016170), a collection of line segments joined at a common endpoint. Any such space is a real tree.

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

*Image: Four\_point\_condition.png.* Visualisation of the four points condition and the 0-hyperbolicity. In green: $(x,y)_t=(y,z)_t$ ; in blue: $(x,z)_t$.

Here are equivalent characterizations of real trees which can be used as definitions:

\1) *(similar to [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_%28data_structure%29) as graphs)* A real tree is a [geodesic](wikipedia://en/475335) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) which contains no subset [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to a circle.[^o10]

\2) A real tree is a connected metric space $(X,d)$ which has the **four points condition**[^o11] (see figure):

For all $x,y,z,t\in X,$ $d(x,y)+d(z,t)\leq \max[d(x,z)+d(y,t)\,;\, d(x,t)+d(y,z)]$.

\3) A real tree is a connected [0-hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space) metric space[^o12] (see figure). Formally,

For all $x,y,z,t\in X,$ $(x,y)_t\geq \min [ (x,z)_t\, ; \, (y,z)_t ],$

where $(x,y)_t$ denotes the [Gromov product](wikipedia://en/12942590) of $x$ and $y$ with respect to $t$, that is, $\textstyle\frac 1 2 \left( d(x, t) + d(y, t) - d(x, y) \right).$

\4) *(similar to the characterization of [plane trees](wikipedia://en/48560#Plane_tree) by their [contour process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton-Watson_tree)).* Consider a positive excursion of a function. In other words, let $e$ be a continuous real-valued function and $[a,b]$ an interval such that $e(a)=e(b)=0$ and $e(t)>0$ for $t\in ]a,b[$.

For $x, y\in [a,b]$, $x\leq y$, define a [pseudometric](wikipedia://en/20018) and an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) with:

$$d_e( x, y) := e(x)+e(y)-2\min(e(z)\, ;z\in[x,y]),$$

$$x\sim_e y \Leftrightarrow d_e(x,y)=0.$$

Then, the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) $([a,b]/\sim_e\, ,\, d_e)$ is a real tree.[^o12] Intuitively, the [local minima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_minima) of the excursion *e* are the parents of the [local maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_maxima). Another visual way to construct the real tree from an excursion is to "put glue" under the curve of *e*, and "bend" this curve, identifying the glued points (see animation).

## Examples {#Examples}

Real trees often appear, in various situations, as limits of more classical metric spaces.

### Brownian trees {#Brownian_trees}

A [Brownian tree](wikipedia://en/298408)[^o16] is a random metric space whose value is a (non-simplicial) real tree almost surely. Brownian trees arise as limits of various random processes on finite trees.[^o17]

### Ultralimits of metric spaces {#Ultralimits_of_metric_spaces}

Any [ultralimit](wikipedia://en/18609360) of a sequence $(X_i)$ of $\delta_i$-[hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1285375) spaces with $\delta_i \to 0$ is a real tree. In particular, the [asymptotic cone](wikipedia://en/18609360#Asymptotic_cones) of any hyperbolic space is a real tree.

### Limit of group actions {#Limit_of_group_actions}

Let $G$ be a [group](wikipedia://en/19447). For a sequence of based $G$-spaces $(X_i, *_i, \rho_i)$ there is a notion of convergence to a based $G$-space $(X_\infty, x_\infty, \rho_\infty)$ due to M. Bestvina and F. Paulin. When the spaces are hyperbolic and the actions are unbounded the limit (if it exists) is a real tree.[^o20]

A simple example is obtained by taking $G = \pi_1(S)$ where $S$ is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) surface, and $X_i$ the universal cover of $S$ with the metric $i\rho$ (where $\rho$ is a fixed hyperbolic metric on $S$).

This is useful to produce actions of hyperbolic groups on real trees. Such actions are analyzed using the so-called [Rips machine](wikipedia://en/31275945). A case of particular interest is the study of degeneration of groups acting [properly discontinuously](wikipedia://en/12781#Remarkable_properties_of_actions) on a [real hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) (this predates Rips', Bestvina's and Paulin's work and is due to J. Morgan and [P. Shalen](wikipedia://en/3520910)[^o21]).

### Algebraic groups {#Algebraic_groups}

If $F$ is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) with an [ultrametric](wikipedia://en/333996) [valuation](wikipedia://en/347049) then the [Bruhat–Tits building](wikipedia://en/275015) of $\mathrm{SL}_2(F)$ is a real tree. It is simplicial if and only if the valuations is discrete.

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

### Λ-trees {#Λ-trees}

If $\Lambda$ is a [totally ordered abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_abelian_group) there is a natural notion of a distance with values in $\Lambda$ (classical metric spaces correspond to $\Lambda = \mathbb R$). There is a notion of $\Lambda$-tree[^o25] which recovers simplicial trees when $\Lambda = \mathbb Z$ and real trees when $\Lambda = \mathbb R$. The structure of [finitely presented groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) acting [freely](wikipedia://en/12781#Remarkable_properties_of_actions) on $\Lambda$-trees was described.[^o26] In particular, such a group acts freely on some $\mathbb R^n$-tree.

### Real buildings {#Real_buildings}

The axioms for a [building](wikipedia://en/275015) can be generalized to give a definition of a real building. These arise for example as asymptotic cones of higher-rank [symmetric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_spaces) or as Bruhat-Tits buildings of higher-rank groups over valued fields.
