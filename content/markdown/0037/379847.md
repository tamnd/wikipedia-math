# Mertens conjecture

*Image: Congettura Mertens.png.* The graph shows the [Mertens function](wikipedia://en/435639) $M(n)$ and the square roots $\pm \sqrt{n}$ for $n \le 10,000$. After computing these values, Mertens conjectured that the absolute value of $M(n)$ is always bounded by $\sqrt{n}$. This hypothesis, known as the Mertens conjecture, was disproved in 1985 by [Andrew Odlyzko](wikipedia://en/1986042) and [Herman te Riele](wikipedia://en/1587570).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mertens conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Mertens conjecture** is the statement that the [Mertens function](wikipedia://en/435639) $M(n)$ is bounded by $\pm\sqrt{n}$. Although now disproven, it had been shown to imply the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125). It was conjectured by [Thomas Joannes Stieltjes](wikipedia://en/40589), in an 1885 letter to [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) (reprinted in (txt)), and again in print by (txt), and disproved by (txt). It is a striking example of a mathematical conjecture proven false despite a large amount of computational evidence in its favor.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the [Mertens function](wikipedia://en/435639) is defined as

$$M(n) = \sum_{1 \le k \le n} \mu(k),$$

where μ(k) is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961); the **Mertens conjecture** is that for all *n* > 1,

$$|M(n)| < \sqrt{n}.$$

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Disproof of the conjecture"}

## Disproof of the conjecture {#Disproof_of_the_conjecture}

[Stieltjes](wikipedia://en/40589) claimed in 1885 to have proven a weaker result, namely that $m(n) := M(n)/\sqrt{n}$ was [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509), but did not publish a proof.[^o9] (In terms of $m(n)$, the Mertens conjecture is that $-1 < m(n) < 1$.)

In 1985, [Andrew Odlyzko](wikipedia://en/1986042) and [Herman te Riele](wikipedia://en/1587570) proved the Mertens conjecture false using the [Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász lattice basis reduction algorithm](wikipedia://en/1735228):[^o10][^o11]

$\liminf m(n) < -1.009$ 4 and 4 $\limsup m(n) > 1.06.$

It was later shown that the first [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) appears below $e^{3.21\times10^{64}} \approx 10^{1.39\times10^{64}}$[^o12] but above 10<sup>16</sup>.[^o13] The upper bound has since been lowered to $e^{1.59\times10^{40}}$[^o14] or approximately $10^{6.91\times10^{39}},$ and then again to $e^{1.017\times10^{29}} \approx 10^{4.416\times10^{28}}$.[^o15] In 2024, Seungki Kim and Phong Nguyen lowered the bound to $e^{1.96\times10^{19}} \approx 10^{8.512\times10^{18}}$,[^o16] but no *explicit* counterexample is known.

The [law of the iterated logarithm](wikipedia://en/496558) states that if $\mu$ is replaced by a [random sequence](wikipedia://en/48256) of +1s and −1s then the order of growth of the [partial sum](wikipedia://en/15287) of the first $n$ terms is (with probability 1) about $\sqrt{n \log \log n}$, which suggests that the order of growth of $m(n)$ might be somewhere around $\sqrt{\log \log n}$. The actual order of growth may be somewhat smaller; in the early 1990s Steve Gonek conjectured[^o17] that the order of growth of $m(n)$ was $(\log\log\log n)^{5/4},$ which was affirmed by Ng (2004), based on a heuristic argument, that assumed the Riemann hypothesis and certain conjectures about the averaged behavior of zeros of the Riemann zeta function.[^o17]

In 1979, Cohen and Dress[^o18] found the largest known value of $m(n) \approx 0.570591$ for $M(7766842813) = 50286,$ and in 2011, Kuznetsov found the largest known negative value (largest in the sense of [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991)) $m(n) \approx -0.585768$ for $M(11609864264058592345) = -1995900927.$[^o19] In 2016, Hurst computed $M(n)$ for every $n \le 10^{16}$ but did not find larger values of $m(n)$.[^o13]

In 2006, Kotnik and te Riele improved the upper bound and showed that there are infinitely many values of $n$ for which $m(n) > 1.2184$, but without giving any specific value for such an $n$.[^o20] In 2016, Hurst made further improvements by showing

$\liminf m(n) < -1.837625$ 4 and 4 $\limsup m(n) > 1.826054.$

:::

## Connection to the Riemann hypothesis {#Connection_to_the_Riemann_hypothesis}

The connection to the Riemann hypothesis is based on the [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) for the reciprocal of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809),

$$\frac{1}{\zeta(s)} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\mu(n)}{n^s},$$

valid in the region $\mathcal{Re}(s) > 1$. We can rewrite this as a [Stieltjes integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stieltjes_integral)

$$\frac{1}{\zeta(s)} = \int_0^\infty x^{-s} dM(x)$$

and after [integrating by parts](wikipedia://en/147252), obtain the reciprocal of the zeta function as a [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297)

$$\frac{1}{s \zeta(s)} = \left\{ \mathcal{M} M \right\}(-s) = \int_0^\infty x^{-s} M(x)\, \frac{dx}{x}.$$

Using the [Mellin inversion theorem](wikipedia://en/1498625) we now can express $M$ in terms of $\tfrac{1}{\zeta}$ as

$$M(x) = \frac{1}{2 \pi i} \int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty} \frac{x^s}{s \zeta(s)}\,ds$$

which is valid for $1 < \sigma < 2$, and valid for $\tfrac{1}{2} < \sigma < 2$ on the Riemann hypothesis. From this, the Mellin transform integral must be convergent, and hence $M(x)$ must be $O(x^{e})$ for every exponent *e* greater than $\tfrac{1}{2}$. From this it follows that

$$M(x) = O\Big(x^{\tfrac{1}{2} + \epsilon}\Big)$$

for all positive $\varepsilon$ is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis, which therefore would have followed from the stronger Mertens hypothesis, and follows from the hypothesis of Stieltjes that

$$M(x) = O\Big(x^\tfrac{1}{2}\Big).$$

:::
