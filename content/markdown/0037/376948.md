# Power of two

*Image: powers\_of\_two\_cuboids.svg.* Visualization of powers of two from 1 to 1024 (2<sup>0</sup> to 2<sup>10</sup>) as base-2 [Dienes blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dienes_blocks)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Power of two"}

A **power of two** is a number of the form $2^{n}$ where $n$ is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), that is, the result of [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491) with the number [two](wikipedia://en/64516) as the [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_%28exponentiation%29) and integer $n$ as the [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent). In the [fast-growing hierarchy](wikipedia://en/24947285), $2^{n}$ is exactly equal to $f_1^n(1)$. In the [Hardy hierarchy](wikipedia://en/25264191), $2^{n}$ is exactly equal to $H_{\omega{n}}(1)$.

:::

Powers of two with [non-negative](wikipedia://en/7951270#Terminology_for_signs) exponents are integers: $2^{0} = 1$, $2^{1} = 2$, and $2^{n}$ is *n* 2s [multiplied](wikipedia://en/20845) together.[^o2][^o3] The first ten powers of 2 for non-negative values of $n$ are:

[1](wikipedia://en/77591639), [2](wikipedia://en/64516), [4](wikipedia://en/208161), [8](wikipedia://en/208174), [16](wikipedia://en/208254), [32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32_%28number%29), [64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64_%28number%29), [128](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/128_%28number%29), [256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/256_%28number%29), [512](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/512_%28number%29), ...

By comparison, powers of two with [negative exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_exponents) are [fractions](wikipedia://en/1704824): for positive integer $n$, $2^{-n}$ is [one half](wikipedia://en/464963) multiplied by itself $n$ times. Thus the first few negative powers of 2 are $\tfrac{1}{2}$, $\tfrac{1}{4}$, $\tfrac{1}{8}$, $\tfrac{1}{16}$, etc. Sometimes these are called *inverse powers of two* because each is the [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) of a positive power of two.

## Computer science {#Computer_science}

Most [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) use a [binary](wikipedia://en/238686) (base two) representation of numbers, a number system in which each place represents a power of 2, and the only possible binary digits ("[bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit)") are 0 and 1. Powers of two are thus ubiquitous in computing and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). Written in binary, a power of two always has the form 100...000 or 0.00...001, like a [power of 10](wikipedia://en/1161104) in the [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) (base ten) system. Just as a decimal number can be multiplied by a power of ten by adding zeros or moving the [decimal point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_point), a binary number can be multiplied by a power of two using [bit shifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_shift) (moving all of the bits a certain number of places to the left or right), one of the fastest available arithmetical operations.

Two to the $n$th power is the number of values in which the bits in a [binary word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_%28computer_architecture%29) of length $n$ can be set. A word, interpreted as representing an [integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_%28computer_science%29) in a range starting at zero, referred to as an "unsigned integer", can represent values from 0 ($000...000_{2}$) to $2^{n} - 1$ ($111...111_{2}$) inclusively. An alternative representation, referred to as a [signed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signedness) integer, allows values that can be positive, negative and zero; see *[Signed number representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_number_representations)*.

One less than a power of two is often the upper bound of an integer in binary computers. As a consequence, numbers of this form show up frequently in computer software. As an example, a [video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game) running on an 8-bit system might limit the score or the number of items the player can hold to 255—the result of using a [byte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte), which is [8 bits long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octet_%28computing%29), to store the number, allowing the representation of 256 distinct values from $0$ to $2^{8} - 1 = 255$. For example, in the original *[Legend of Zelda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda_%28video_game%29)* the main character was limited to carrying 255 rupees (the currency of the game) at any given time, and the video game *[Pac-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-Man)* famously has a [kill screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_video_game_terms#Kill_screen) at level 256.

Powers of two are often used to define units in which to quantify computer memory sizes. A "byte" now typically refers to eight bits (an [octet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octet_%28computing%29)), resulting in the possibility of 256 values (2<sup>8</sup>). (The term *byte* once meant (and in some cases, still means) a [collection of bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit#Multiple_bits) that was defined by the hardware context, typically of 5 to 32 bits, rather than only an 8-bit unit.) The prefix *kilo*, in conjunction with *byte*, has been used by computer scientists to mean 1,024 (2<sup>10</sup>). However, in general, the term *kilo* has been used in the [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) to mean 1,000 (10<sup>3</sup>). A series of [binary prefixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_prefixes) has been standardized, including *kibi* (Ki) meaning 1,024. Nearly all [processor registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processor_register) have sizes that are a power of two bits, 8, 16, 32 or 64 bits being very common, with the last two being most common except for very small processors.

Powers of two occur in a range of other places as well. For many [disk drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disk_drive), at least one of the sector size, number of sectors per track, and number of tracks per surface is a power of two. The logical block size is almost always a power of two.

Numbers that are closely related to powers of two occur in a number of computer hardware designs, such as with the number of pixels in the width and height of video screens, where the number of pixels in each direction is often the product of a power of two and a small number. For example, $640 = 128 \times 5$, and $480 = 32 \times 15$.

## Mersenne and Fermat primes {#Mersenne_and_Fermat_primes}

A [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) that is one less than a power of two is called a [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908). For example, the prime number [31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_%28number%29) is a Mersenne prime because it is 1 less than 32 (2<sup>5</sup>). Similarly, a prime number (like [257](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/257_%28number%29)) that is one more than a positive power of two is called a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime)—the exponent itself is a power of two. A [fraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_%28mathematics%29) that has a power of two as its [denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominator) is called a [dyadic rational](wikipedia://en/56263). The numbers that can be represented as sums of consecutive positive integers are called [polite numbers](wikipedia://en/4047259); they are exactly the numbers that are not powers of two.

## Euclid's *Elements*, Book IX {#Euclid's_Elements,_Book_IX}

The [geometric progression](wikipedia://en/23474670) 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, ... (or, in the [binary numeral system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system), 1, 10, 100, 1000, 10000, 100000, ... ) is important in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Book IX, Proposition 36 of [*Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107) proves that if the sum of the first $n$ terms of this progression is a prime number (and thus is a Mersenne prime as mentioned above), then this sum times the $n$th term is a [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670). For example, the sum of the first 5 terms of the series $1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 = 31$, which is a prime number. The sum 31 multiplied by 16 (the 5th term in the series) equals 496, which is a perfect number.

Book IX, Proposition 35, proves that in a geometric series if the first term is subtracted from the second and last term in the sequence, then as the excess of the second is to the first—so is the excess of the last to all those before it. (This is a restatement of our formula for geometric series from above.) Applying this to the geometric progression 31, 62, 124, 248, 496 (which results from 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 by multiplying all terms by 31), we see that 62 minus 31 is to 31 as 496 minus 31 is to the sum of 31, 62, 124, 248. Therefore, the numbers 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 31, 62, 124 and 248 add up to 496 and further these are all the numbers that [divide](wikipedia://en/49492) 496. For suppose that $p$ divides 496 and it is not amongst these numbers. Assume $pq$ is equal to $16\,\times\,31$, or 31 is to $q$ as $p$ is to 16. Now $p$ cannot divide 16 or it would be amongst the numbers 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16. Therefore, 31 cannot divide $q$. And since 31 does not divide $q$ and $q$ measures 496, the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556) implies that $q$ must divide 16 and be among the numbers 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16. Let $q$ be 4, then $p$ must be 124, which is impossible since by hypothesis $p$ is not amongst the numbers 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 31, 62, 124 or 248.

### Last digits {#Last_digits}

Starting with 2 the last digit is periodic with period 4, with the cycle 2–4–8–6–, and starting with 4 the last two digits are periodic with period 20. These patterns are generally true of any power, with respect to any [base](wikipedia://en/701207). The pattern continues where each pattern has starting point $2^{k}$, and the period is the [multiplicative order](wikipedia://en/344542) of 2 modulo $5^{k}$, which is $\varphi(5^{k}) = 4 \times 5^{k-1}$ (see *[Multiplicative group of integers modulo n](wikipedia://en/1195234)*).

### Last digits for powers of two whose exponents are powers of two {#Last_digits_for_powers_of_two_whose_exponents_are_powers_of_two}

All of these numbers over 4 end with the digit 6. Starting with 16 the last two digits are periodic with period 4, with the cycle 16–56–36–96–, and starting with 16 the last three digits are periodic with period 20. These patterns generally occur with any power, with respect to any [base](wikipedia://en/701207). The pattern continues where each pattern has starting point $2^{k}$, and the period is the [multiplicative order](wikipedia://en/344542) of 2 modulo $5^{k}$, which is $\varphi(5^{k}) = 4 \times 5^{k-1}$ (see *[Multiplicative group of integers modulo n](wikipedia://en/1195234)*).

### Facts about powers of two whose exponents are powers of two {#Facts_about_powers_of_two_whose_exponents_are_powers_of_two}

In a connection with [nimbers](wikipedia://en/164501), these numbers are often called *[Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) 2-powers*.

The numbers $2^{2^n}$ form an [irrationality sequence](wikipedia://en/37187938): for every sequence $x_i$ of [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer), the [series](wikipedia://en/15287)

$$\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{2^i} x_i} = \frac{1}{2x_0}+\frac{1}{4x_1}+\frac{1}{16x_2}+\cdots$$

converges to an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689). Despite the rapid growth of this sequence, it is the slowest-growing irrationality sequence known.[^o16]

### Powers of two whose exponents are powers of two in computer science {#Powers_of_two_whose_exponents_are_powers_of_two_in_computer_science}

Since it is common for computer [data types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_type) to have a *size* which is a power of two, these numbers count the number of representable *values* of that type. For example, a 32-bit word consisting of 4 bytes can represent $2^{32}$ distinct values, which can either be regarded as mere bit-patterns, or are more commonly interpreted as the unsigned numbers from 0 to $2^{32} - 1$, or as the range of signed numbers between $-2^{31}$ and $2^{31} - 1$. For more about representing signed numbers see *[Two's complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two's_complement)*.

## Selected powers of two {#Selected_powers_of_two}

**2<sup>2</sup> = 4**

The number that is the [square](wikipedia://en/213919) of two. Also the first power of two [tetration](wikipedia://en/482379) of two.

**2<sup>8</sup> = 256**

The number of values represented by the 8 [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit) in a [byte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte), more specifically termed as an [octet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octet_%28computing%29). (The term [byte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte) is often defined as a [collection of bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit#Multiple_bits) rather than the strict definition of an 8-bit quantity, as demonstrated by the term [kilobyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilobyte).)

**2<sup>10</sup> = 1,024**

The binary approximation of the [kilo-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilo-), or 1,000 multiplier, which causes a change of prefix. For example: 1,024 [bytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte) = 1 [kilobyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilobyte#Binary_%281024_bytes%29)[^o20] (or [kibibyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibibyte)).

**2<sup>12</sup> = 4,096**

The hardware [page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_%28computing%29) size of an [Intel x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_x86)-compatible processor.

**2<sup>15</sup> = 32,768**

The number of non-negative values for a *signed* 16-bit integer.

**2<sup>16</sup> = 65,536**

The number of distinct values representable in a single [word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_%28data_type%29) on a [16-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16-bit_computing) processor, such as the original [x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86) processors.[^o21]

The maximum range of a [short integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_integer) variable in the [C#](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_Sharp_%28programming_language%29), [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29), and [SQL](wikipedia://en/29004) programming languages. The maximum range of a **Word** or **Smallint** variable in the [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_%28programming_language%29) programming language.

The number of [binary relations](wikipedia://en/3931) on a 4-element set.

**2<sup>20</sup> = 1,048,576**

The binary approximation of the [mega-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega-), or 1,000,000 multiplier, which causes a change of prefix. For example: 1,048,576 [bytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte) = 1 [megabyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabyte#Base_2) (or [mebibyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mebibyte)).

**2<sup>24</sup> = 16,777,216**

The number of unique [colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color) that can be displayed in [truecolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-bit_color), which is used by common [computer monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_monitor).

This number is the result of using the three-channel [RGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RGB) system, where colors are defined by three values (red, green and blue) independently ranging from 0 (`00`) to 255 (`FF`) inclusive. This gives 8 bits for each channel, or 24 bits in total; for example, pure black is `#000000`, pure white is `#FFFFFF`. The space of all possible colors, 16,777,216, can be determined by 16<sup>6</sup> (6 digits with 16 possible values for each), 256<sup>3</sup> (3 channels with 256 possible values for each), or 2<sup>24</sup> (24 bits with 2 possible values for each).

The size of the largest unsigned integer or address in computers with [24-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-bit_computing) registers or data buses.

**2<sup>30</sup> = 1,073,741,824**

The binary approximation of the [giga-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giga-), or 1,000,000,000 multiplier, which causes a change of prefix. For example, 1,073,741,824 [bytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte) = 1 [gigabyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigabyte#Base_2_%28binary%29) (or [gibibyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibibyte)).

**2<sup>31</sup> = 2,147,483,648**

The number of non-negative values for a *signed* 32-bit integer. Since [Unix time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix_time) is measured in seconds since January 1, 1970, it will run out at 2,147,483,647 seconds or 03:14:07 UTC on Tuesday, 19 January 2038 on 32-bit computers running Unix, a problem known as the [year 2038 problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_2038_problem).

**2<sup>32</sup> = 4,294,967,296**

## Powers of two in music theory {#Powers_of_two_in_music_theory}

In [musical notation](wikipedia://en/20201), all unmodified [note values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Note_value) have a duration equal to a [whole note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_note) divided by a power of two; for example a [half note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_note) (1/2), a [quarter note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_note) (1/4), an [eighth note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_note) (1/8) and a [sixteenth note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteenth_note) (1/16). [Dotted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotted_note) or otherwise modified notes have other durations. In [time signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_signature) the lower numeral, the *beat unit*, which can be seen as the [denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominator) of a fraction, is almost always a power of two.

If the ratio of [frequencies](wikipedia://en/10779) of two pitches is a power of two, then the [interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_%28music%29) between those pitches is full [octaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave). In this case, the corresponding notes have the same name.

The [mathematical coincidence](wikipedia://en/740817) $2^{7} \approx (\tfrac{3}{2})^{12}$, from $\frac{\log3}{\log2} = 1.5849\ldots \approx \frac{19}{12}$, closely relates the interval of 7 [semitones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitone) in [equal temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_temperament) to a [perfect fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_fifth) of [just intonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_intonation): $2^{7/12}\approx 3/2$, correct to about 0.1%. The just fifth is the basis of [Pythagorean tuning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_tuning); the difference between [twelve just fifths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_fifths) and seven octaves is the [Pythagorean comma](wikipedia://en/177721).[^o29]

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

*Image: Binomial\_expansion\_visualisation.svg.* As each increase in dimension doubles the number of shapes, the sum of coefficients on each row of [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) is a power of two

*Image: sum\_of\_powers\_of\_two\_including\_fractions.svg.* The sum of powers of two from zero to a given power, inclusive, is 1 less than the next power of two, whereas the sum of powers of two from negative infinity to a given power, inclusive, equals the next power of two.

The sum of all $n$-choose [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) is equal to $2^{n}$. Consider the set of all $n$-digit binary integers. Its [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) is $2^{n}$. It is also the sums of the cardinalities of certain subsets: the subset of integers with no 1s (consisting of a single number, written as $n$ 0s), the subset with a single 1, the subset with two 1s, and so on up to the subset with $n$ 1s (consisting of the number written as $n$ 1s). Each of these is in turn equal to the binomial coefficient indexed by $n$ and the number of 1s being considered (for example, there are 10-choose-3 binary numbers with ten digits that include exactly three 1s).

Currently, powers of two are the only known [almost perfect numbers](wikipedia://en/322008).

The [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of a set $a$ is always $2^{\lvert a\rvert}$, where $\lvert a\rvert$ is the cardinality of $a$.

The number of [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of an $n$-dimensional [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) is $2^{n}$. Similarly, the number of $(n - 1)$-faces of an $n$-dimensional [cross-polytope](wikipedia://en/716401) is also $2^{n}$ and the formula for the number of $x$-faces an $n$-dimensional cross-polytope has is $2^x \tbinom{n}{x}.$

The sum of the first $n$ powers of two (starting from $1 = 2^{0}$) is given by

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} 2^k = 2^0 + 2^1 + 2^2 + \cdots + 2^{n-1} = 2^{n}-1$$

for $n$ being any positive integer.

Thus, the sum of the powers

$$1 + 2^1 + 2^2 + \cdots + 2^{63}$$

can be computed simply by evaluating: $2^{64}-1$ (which is the "chess number").

The [sum of the reciprocals of the powers of two](wikipedia://en/10073693) is [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29). The [sum of the reciprocals of the squared powers of two](wikipedia://en/10073845) (powers of four) is 1/3.

The smallest natural power of two whose [decimal representation](wikipedia://en/8214) begins with 7 is[^o31]

$$2^{46} = 70\ 368\ 744\ 177\ 664.$$

Every power of 2 (excluding 1) can be written as the [sum of four square numbers in 24 ways](wikipedia://en/18808748). The powers of 2 are the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) greater than 1 that can be written as the sum of four square numbers in the fewest ways.

As a real [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000), *a*<sup>*n*</sup> + *b*<sup>*n*</sup> is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725), if and only if *n* is a power of two. (If *n* is odd, then *a*<sup>*n*</sup> + *b*<sup>*n*</sup> is divisible by *a*+*b*, and if *n* is even but not a power of 2, then *n* can be written as *n* = *mp*, where *m* is odd, and thus $a^n + b^n = (a^p)^m + (b^p)^m$, which is divisible by *a*<sup>*p*</sup> + *b*<sup>*p*</sup>.) But in the domain of [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers), the polynomial $a^{2n} + b^{2n}$ (where *n* ≥ 1) can always be factorized as $a^{2n} + b^{2n} = (a^n + b^ni) \cdot (a^n - b^ni)$, even if *n* is a power of two.

The only known powers of 2 with all digits even are 2<sup>1</sup> = 2, 2<sup>2</sup> = 4, 2<sup>3</sup> = 8, 2<sup>6</sup> = 64 and 2<sup>11</sup> = 2048. The first 3 powers of 2 with all but last digit odd is 2<sup>4</sup> = 16, 2<sup>5</sup> = 32 and 2<sup>9</sup> = 512. The next such power of 2 of form 2<sup>*n*</sup> should have *n* of at least 6 digits. The only powers of 2 with all digits distinct are 2<sup>0</sup> = 1 to 2<sup>15</sup> = 32768, 2<sup>20</sup> = 1048576 and 2<sup>29</sup> = 536870912.

## Negative powers of two {#Negative_powers_of_two}
