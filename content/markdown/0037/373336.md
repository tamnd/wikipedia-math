# Mikio Sato

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Mikio Sato"}

**Mikio Sato** was a Japanese mathematician known for founding the fields of [algebraic analysis](wikipedia://en/20846707), [hyperfunctions](wikipedia://en/1678626), and holonomic quantum fields. He was a professor at the [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1978962) in Kyoto.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in Tokyo on 18 April 1928,[^o5] Sato studied at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), receiving his BSc in 1952 and PhD under [Shokichi Iyanaga](wikipedia://en/18357287) in 1963.[^o11] He was a professor at [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University) and the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) before moving to the [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1978962) (RIMS) attached to [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) in 1970.[^o11] He was director of RIMS from 1987 to 1991.[^o11]

His disciples include [Masaki Kashiwara](wikipedia://en/4376209), [Takahiro Kawai](wikipedia://en/57758222), [Tetsuji Miwa](wikipedia://en/62132537), as well as [Michio Jimbo](wikipedia://en/22363355), who have been called the "Sato School".[^o7]

Sato died at home in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) on 9 January 2023, aged 94.[^o8][^o2]

## Research {#Research}

Sato was known for his innovative work in a number of fields, such as [prehomogeneous vector spaces](wikipedia://en/13760785) and [Bernstein–Sato polynomials](wikipedia://en/1636721); and particularly for his hyperfunction theory.[^o11] This theory initially appeared as an extension of the ideas of [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) theory; it was soon connected to the [local cohomology](wikipedia://en/3212091) theory of [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck), for which it was an independent realisation in terms of [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) theory. Further, it led to the theory of [microfunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfunction) and [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841) in linear [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [Fourier theory](wikipedia://en/14147), such as for wave fronts, and ultimately to the current developments in [*D*-module](wikipedia://en/3440755) theory.[^o5][^o10] Part of Sato's hyperfunction theory is the modern theory of [holonomic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomic_system): PDEs overdetermined to the point of having finite-dimensional spaces of solutions ([algebraic analysis](wikipedia://en/20846707)).[^o11]

In theoretical physics, Sato wrote a series of papers in the 1970s with [Michio Jimbo](wikipedia://en/22363355) and [Tetsuji Miwa](wikipedia://en/62132537) that developed the theory of holonomic quantum fields.[^o5] When Sato was awarded the 2002–2003 [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278), this work was described as "a far-reaching extension of the mathematical formalism underlying the two-dimensional Ising model, and introduced along the way the famous tau functions."[^o5][^o11] Sato also contributed basic work to non-linear [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) theory, with the use of [Grassmannians](wikipedia://en/373810) of infinite dimension.[^o11]

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), he and [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) independently posed the [Sato–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/597837) on [*L*-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) around 1960.[^o12]

[Pierre Schapira](wikipedia://en/41082949) remarked, "Looking back, 40 years later, we realize that Sato's approach to mathematics is not so different from that of Grothendieck, that Sato did have the incredible temerity to treat [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) as [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and was also able to build the algebraic and geometric tools adapted to his problems."[^o13]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Sato received the 1969 [Asahi Prize of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Prize_of_Science), the 1976 [Japan Academy Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy_Prize_%28academics%29), the 1984 [Person of Cultural Merits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person_of_Cultural_Merits) award of the [Japanese Education Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Education_Ministry), the 1997 [Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schock_Prize), and the 2002–2003 [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278).[^o11]

Sato was a plenary speaker at the 1983 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw).[^o11] He was elected a foreign member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1993.[^o11]
