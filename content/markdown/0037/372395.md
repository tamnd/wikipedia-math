# Tensor product of algebras

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tensor product of algebras"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of two [algebras](wikipedia://en/191788#Generalization:_algebra_over_a_ring) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* is also an *R*-algebra. This gives the **tensor product of algebras**. When the ring is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), the most common application of such products is to describe the [product of algebra representations](wikipedia://en/43079145).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *R* be a commutative ring and let *A* and *B* be *R*-algebras. Since *A* and *B* may both be regarded as *R*-[modules](wikipedia://en/276410), their [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836)

$$A \otimes_R B$$

is also an *R*-module. The tensor product can be given the structure of a ring by defining the product on elements of the form *a* ⊗ *b* by[^o4][^n1]

$$(a_1\otimes b_1)(a_2\otimes b_2) = a_1 a_2\otimes b_1b_2$$

and then extending by linearity to all of *A* ⊗<sub>*R*</sub> *B*. This ring is an *R*-algebra, [associative](wikipedia://en/2112) and unital with the identity element given by 1<sub>*A*</sub> ⊗ 1<sub>*B*</sub>,[^o6] where 1<sub>*A*</sub> and 1<sub>*B*</sub> are the identity elements of *A* and *B*. If *A* and *B* are [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29), then the tensor product is commutative as well.

The tensor product turns the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of *R*-algebras into a [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/3865249).

:::

## Further properties {#Further_properties}

There are natural [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) from *A* and *B* to *A* ⊗<sub>*R*</sub> *B* given by[^o8]

$$a\mapsto a\otimes 1_B$$

$$b\mapsto 1_A\otimes b$$

These maps make the tensor product the [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) in the [category of commutative *R*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_commutative_algebras). The tensor product is *not* the coproduct in the category of all *R*-algebras; there the coproduct is given by a more general [free product of algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_product_of_algebras). Nevertheless, the tensor product of non-commutative algebras can be described by a [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) similar to that of the coproduct:

$$\text{Hom}(A\otimes B,X) \cong \lbrace (f,g)\in \text{Hom}(A,X)\times \text{Hom}(B,X) \mid \forall a \in A, b \in B: [f(a), g(b)] = 0\rbrace,$$

where \[-, -\] denotes the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193). The [natural isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) is given by identifying a morphism $\phi:A\otimes B\to X$ on the left hand side with the pair of morphisms $(f,g)$ on the right hand side where $f(a):=\phi(a\otimes 1)$ and similarly $g(b):=\phi(1\otimes b)$.

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The tensor product of commutative algebras is of frequent use in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). For [affine schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) *X*, *Y*, *Z* with morphisms from *X* and *Z* to *Y*, so *X* = Spec(*A*), *Y* = Spec(*R*), and *Z* = Spec(*B*) for some commutative rings *A*, *R*, *B*, the [fiber product scheme](wikipedia://en/56444110) is the affine scheme corresponding to the tensor product of algebras:

$$X\times_Y Z = \operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_R B).$$

More generally, the fiber product of schemes is defined by gluing together affine fiber products of this form.

:::

::: {#o12 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The tensor product can be used as a means of taking [intersections](wikipedia://en/52580781) of two subschemes in a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754): consider the $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$-algebras $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/f$, $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/g$, then their tensor product is $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f) \otimes_{\mathbb{C}[x,y]} \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(g) \cong \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f,g)$, which describes the intersection of the [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) *f* = 0 and *g* = 0 in the affine plane over **C**.
- More generally, if $A$ is a commutative ring and $I,J\subseteq A$ are [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977), then $\frac{A}{I}\otimes_A\frac{A}{J}\cong \frac{A}{I+J}$, with a unique [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) sending $(a+I) \otimes (b+J)$ to $ab + I+J$.
- Tensor products can be used as a means of [changing coefficients](wikipedia://en/41410139). For example, $\mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(x^3 + 5x^2 + x - 1)\otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/5 \cong \mathbb{Z}/5[x,y]/(x^3 + x - 1)$ and $\mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(f) \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f)$.
- Tensor products also can be used for taking [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_product) of affine schemes over a field. For example, $\mathbb{C}[x_1,x_2]/(f(x)) \otimes_\mathbb{C} \mathbb{C}[y_1,y_2]/(g(y))$ is isomorphic to the algebra $\mathbb{C}[x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2]/(f(x),g(y))$ which corresponds to an affine surface in $\mathbb{A}^4_\mathbb{C}$ if *f* and *g* are not zero.
- Given $R$-algebras $A$ and $B$ whose underlying rings are [graded-commutative rings](wikipedia://en/52405825), the tensor product $A\otimes_RB$ becomes a graded commutative ring by defining $(a\otimes b)(a'\otimes b')=(-1)^{|b||a'|}aa'\otimes bb'$ for homogeneous $a$, $a'$, $b$, and $b'$.
- The tensor product of two [matrix algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_algebra) is $M_m(A)\otimes_AM_n(A)\cong M_{mn}(A)$, the isomorphism given by extending the [Kronecker product](wikipedia://en/712430) of two matrices via the universal property.
