# Emanuel Sperner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Emanuel Sperner"}

**Emanuel Sperner** (9 December 1905 – 31 January 1980) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), best known for two [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977). He was born in [Waltdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prusinowice,_Opole_Voivodeship) (near Neiße, [Upper Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Silesia), now [Nysa, Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nysa,_Poland)), and died in [Sulzburg-Laufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulzburg), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany). He was a student at [Carolinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolegium_Carolinum_Neisse) in Nysa and then [Hamburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_University) where his advisor was [Wilhelm Blaschke](wikipedia://en/728998). He was appointed Professor in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) in 1934, and subsequently held posts in a number of universities until 1974.

:::

[Sperner's theorem](wikipedia://en/748844), from 1928, says that the size of an [antichain](wikipedia://en/348973) in the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of an *n*-set (a [Sperner family](wikipedia://en/748730)) is at most the middle [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668)(s).[^o3] It has several proofs and numerous generalizations, including the [Sperner property of a partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/26534180).

[Sperner's lemma](wikipedia://en/465067), from 1928, states that every [Sperner coloring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperner_coloring) of a [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440970) of an *n*-dimensional [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) contains a cell colored with a complete set of colors.[^o4] It was proven by Sperner to provide an alternate proof of a theorem of [Lebesgue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue) characterizing [dimensionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionality) of [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697). It was later noticed that this lemma provides a direct proof of the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/4101) without explicit use of [homology](wikipedia://en/142432).[^o5]

Sperner's students included [Kurt Leichtweiss](wikipedia://en/31821456) and [Gerhard Ringel](wikipedia://en/1571968).
