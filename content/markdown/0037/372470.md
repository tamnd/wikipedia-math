# Grothendieck's Galois theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grothendieck's Galois theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Grothendieck's Galois theory** is an abstract approach to the [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) of fields, developed around 1960 to provide a way to study the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) in the setting of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). It provides, in the classical setting of [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29), an alternative perspective to that of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) based on [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), which became standard from about the 1930s.

:::

The approach of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) is concerned with the [category-theoretic](wikipedia://en/5869) properties that characterise the categories of finite *G*-sets for a fixed [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266) *G*. For example, *G* might be the group denoted $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ (see [profinite integer](wikipedia://en/46531203)), which is the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of the cyclic additive groups $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ — or equivalently the completion of the [infinite cyclic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic_group) $\mathbb{Z}$ for the topology of subgroups of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581). A finite *G*-set is then a finite set *X* on which *G* acts through a quotient finite cyclic group, so that it is specified by giving some permutation of *X*.

In the above example, a connection with classical [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) can be seen by regarding $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ as the profinite Galois group Gal(*F*/*F*) of the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) *F* of any [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) *F*, over *F*. That is, the automorphisms of *F* fixing *F* are described by the inverse limit, as we take larger and larger finite [splitting fields](wikipedia://en/174440) over *F*. The connection with geometry can be seen when we look at [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323) of the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) with the origin removed: the finite covering realised by the *z*<sup>*n*</sup> map of the disk, thought of by means of a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) variable *z*, corresponds to the subgroup $n \mathbb{Z}$ of the fundamental group of the punctured disk.

The theory of Grothendieck, published in [SGA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGA1), shows how to reconstruct the category of *G*-sets from a *fibre functor* $\Phi$, which in the geometric setting takes the fibre of a covering above a fixed base point (as a set). In fact there is an isomorphism proved of the type

$$G \cong \operatorname{Aut}(\Phi),$$

the latter being the group of automorphisms (self-[natural equivalences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_equivalence)) of $\Phi$. An abstract classification of categories with a functor to the category of sets is given, by means of which one can recognise categories of *G*-sets for *G* profinite.

To see how this applies to the case of fields, one has to study the [tensor product of fields](wikipedia://en/372240). In [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464) theory this is a part of the study of *[atomic toposes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_topos)*.
