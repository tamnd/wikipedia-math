# Nathaniel Bliss

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Nathaniel Bliss"}

**Nathaniel Bliss** (28 November 1700 – 2 September 1764) was an English astronomer who served as the [Astronomer Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer_Royal) from 1762 to 1764. Bliss studied at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) and later became the [Savilian Professor of Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Geometry). He made important meridian observations of a [comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet) and a [solar eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse) visible from [Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich), and many of his observations proved useful in solving the [longitude problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_longitude), and were bought by the [Board of Longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Longitude) after his death.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Nathaniel Bliss was born in the [Cotswolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotswolds) village of [Bisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisley,_Gloucestershire) in [Gloucestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire). His father, also named Nathaniel Bliss, was a [clothier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloth_merchant). Bliss studied at [Pembroke College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_College,_Oxford). He graduated [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1720 and [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1723, and married shortly afterwards.[^n1]

In 1736, Bliss became rector of [St Ebbe's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Ebbe's_Church,_Oxford) in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford). Supported by, among others; the [2nd Earl of Macclesfield (George Parker)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Parker,_2nd_Earl_of_Macclesfield), [Savilian Professor of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Astronomy) [James Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bradley) and by [William Jones](wikipedia://en/239126), Bliss succeeded [Edmond Halley](wikipedia://en/36858805) as [Savilian Professor of Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Geometry) at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) in February 1742 – being elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in May the same year. As Savilian Professor he lectured courses in arithmetic, algebra, plane and spherical geometry, the use of logarithms and surveying instruments.

In 1762 he succeeded Bradley to become the fourth Astronomer Royal, but held the post for only two years before his unexpected death. He died in Oxford, but was buried close to Edmond Halley in [St Margaret's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Margaret's,_Lee) churchyard in [Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee,_London) in south-east [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London). As men of independent means, the first four Astronomers Royal including Bliss, were paid only a minimal salary. This situation changed when [Nevil Maskelyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevil_Maskelyne) succeeded Bliss as Astronomer Royal upon his death – Maskelyne commanded a salary of £350 per annum, permitting him to make the post his main occupation. In 2000 the [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union) named a [crater on the Moon after Bliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bliss_%28crater%29), in commemoration of his position as Astronomer Royal.

## Observations {#Observations}

George Parker, the Earl of Macclesfield, had established an observatory at [Shirburn Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirburn_Castle) in [Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfordshire). Working for and with the Earl of Macclesfield, Bliss made [meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_%28astronomy%29) observations of a comet approaching the Sun in 1744 at Shirburn Castle and at [Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Observatory,_Greenwich).[^o7] Bliss worked alongside James Bradley at the [Royal Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Observatory,_Greenwich) in Greenwich and in June 1761, owing to the poor health of Bradley, he undertook the observations for the [transit of Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_of_Venus).

His assistant at the Royal Observatory was [Charles Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Green_%28astronomer%29), and upon his death, Green continued Bliss's work until the appointment of the next Astronomer Royal. In April 1764 Bliss made observations of an [annular (solar) eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse) visible from Greenwich. Many of the observations made by Bliss were considered potentially useful for solving the [longitude problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_longitude), important for cartography and ocean navigation, and were therefore bought by the [Board of Longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Longitude) from his widow Elizabeth Bliss (née Hillman). His Greenwich observations were not published until 1805, when they were included as a supplement in an edition by [Thomas Hornsby](wikipedia://en/4231354) on the observations of Bradley.[^o8][^o9]
