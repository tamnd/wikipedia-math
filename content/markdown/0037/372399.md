# Opposite category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Opposite category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **opposite category** or **dual category** $C^{\text{op}}$ of a given [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $C$ is formed by reversing the [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891), i.e. interchanging the source and target of each morphism. Doing the reversal twice yields the original category, so the opposite of an opposite category is the original category itself. In symbols, $(C^{\text{op}})^{\text{op}} = C$. The construction can be generalized to [∞-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category) using the [opposite simplicial set](wikipedia://en/79855588).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- An example comes from reversing the direction of inequalities in a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order). So if *X* is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) and ≤ a partial order relation, we can define a new partial order relation ≤<sup>op</sup> by

*x* ≤<sup>op</sup> *y* if and only if *y* ≤ *x*.

The new order is commonly called dual order of ≤, and is mostly denoted by ≥. Therefore, [duality](wikipedia://en/361924#Duality) plays an important role in order theory and every purely order theoretic concept has a dual. For example, there are opposite pairs child/parent, descendant/ancestor, [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum)/[supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum), [down-set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down-set)/[up-set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_set), [ideal](wikipedia://en/530559)/[filter](wikipedia://en/19719) etc. This order theoretic duality is in turn a special case of the construction of opposite categories as every ordered set can be [understood](wikipedia://en/361924#Category_theory) as a category.

- Given a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) (*S*, ·), one usually defines the opposite semigroup as (*S*, ·)<sup>op</sup> = (*S*, \*) where *x*\**y* ≔ *y*·*x* for all *x*,*y* in *S*. So also for semigroups there is a strong duality principle. Clearly, the same construction works for groups, as well, and is known in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), too, where it is applied to the multiplicative semigroup of the ring to give the opposite ring. Again this process can be described by completing a semigroup to a monoid, taking the [corresponding](wikipedia://en/19652#Relation_to_category_theory) opposite category, and then possibly removing the unit from that monoid.
- The category of [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959) and Boolean [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the opposite of the category of [Stone spaces](wikipedia://en/380219) and [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122#Continuous_functions_between_topological_spaces).
- The category of [affine schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) is [equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_%28category_theory%29) to the opposite of the category of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346).
- The [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) restricts to an equivalence between the category of [compact](wikipedia://en/6042#Definition) [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315) and the opposite of the category of (discrete) abelian groups.
- By the Gelfand–Naimark theorem, the category of localizable [measurable spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra) (with [measurable maps](wikipedia://en/44055)) is equivalent to the category of commutative [Von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) (with [normal](wikipedia://en/190167) [unital](wikipedia://en/29268538) homomorphisms of [\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/293426)).[^o4]

## Properties {#Properties}

Opposite preserves products:

$(C\times D)^{\text{op}} \cong C^{\text{op}}\times D^{\text{op}}$ (see [product category](wikipedia://en/4587226))

Opposite preserves [functors](wikipedia://en/10987):

$(\mathrm{Funct}(C,D))^{\text{op}} \cong \mathrm{Funct}(C^{\text{op}},D^{\text{op}})$[^o7][^o8] (see [functor category](wikipedia://en/407039), [opposite functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_functor))

Opposite preserves slices:

$(F\downarrow G)^{\text{op}} \cong (G^{\text{op}}\downarrow F^{\text{op}})$ (see [comma category](wikipedia://en/840950))
