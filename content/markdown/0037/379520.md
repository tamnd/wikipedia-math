# Hexomino

*Image: All 35 free hexominoes.svg.* The 35 free hexominoes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hexomino"}

A **hexomino** (or **6-omino**) is a [polyomino](wikipedia://en/72634) of order 6; that is, a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) in the [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) made of 6 equal-sized [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) connected edge to edge.[^o2] The name of this type of figure is formed with the prefix hex(a)-. When [rotations](wikipedia://en/39789) and [reflections](wikipedia://en/295829) are not considered to be distinct shapes, there are [35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_%28number%29) different [*free*](wikipedia://en/72634#Free,_one-sided,_and_fixed_polyominoes) hexominoes. When reflections are considered distinct, there are [60](wikipedia://en/343325) *one-sided* hexominoes. When rotations are also considered distinct, there are [216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/216_%28number%29) *fixed* hexominoes.[^o3][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The figure above shows all 35 possible free hexominoes, coloured according to their [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186):

- The twenty grey hexominoes have no [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741). Their symmetry group consists only of the [identity mapping](wikipedia://en/15069).
- The six red hexominoes have an axis of [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873) parallel to the gridlines. Their symmetry group has two elements, the identity and a reflection in a line parallel to the sides of the squares.
- The two green hexominoes have an axis of mirror symmetry at 45° to the gridlines. Their symmetry group has two elements, the identity and a diagonal reflection.
- The five blue hexominoes have point symmetry, also known as [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) of order 2. Their symmetry group has two elements, the identity and the 180° rotation.
- The two purple hexominoes have two axes of mirror symmetry, both parallel to the gridlines (thus one horizontal axis and one vertical axis). Their symmetry group has four elements. It is the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of order 2, also known as the [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928).

If reflections of a hexomino are considered distinct, as they are with one-sided hexominoes, then the first and fourth categories above would each double in size, resulting in an extra 25 hexominoes for a total of 60. If rotations are also considered distinct, then the hexominoes from the first category count eightfold, the ones from the next three categories count fourfold, and the ones from the last category count twice. This results in 20 × 8 + (6 + 2 + 5) × 4 + 2 × 2 = 216 fixed hexominoes.

:::

## Packing and tiling {#Packing_and_tiling}

Each of the 35 hexominoes satisfies the [Conway criterion](wikipedia://en/42806211); hence, every hexomino is capable of tiling the plane.[^o8]

Although a complete set of 35 hexominoes has a total of 210 squares, it is not possible to pack them into a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048). (Such an arrangement is possible with the 12 [pentominoes](wikipedia://en/23712), which can be packed into any of the rectangles 3 × 20, 4 × 15, 5 × 12 and 6 × 10.) A simple way to demonstrate that such a packing of hexominoes is not possible is via a [parity](wikipedia://en/143135) argument. If the hexominoes are placed on a [checkerboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkerboard) pattern, then 11 of the hexominoes will cover an even number of black squares (either 2 white and 4 black or vice versa) and the other 24 hexominoes will cover an [odd number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_number) of black squares (3 white and 3 black). Overall, an even number of black squares will be covered in any arrangement. However, any rectangle of 210 squares will have 105 black squares and 105 white squares, and therefore cannot be covered by the 35 hexominoes.

However, there are other simple figures of 210 squares that can be packed with the hexominoes. For example, a 15 × 15 square with a 3 × 5 rectangle removed from the centre has 210 squares. With checkerboard colouring, it has 106 white and 104 black squares (or vice versa), so parity does not prevent a packing, and a packing is indeed possible.[^o9] It is also possible for two sets of pieces to fit a rectangle of size 420, or for the set of 60 one-sided hexominoes (18 of which cover an even number of black squares) to fit a rectangle of size 360.[^o10]

## Polyhedral nets for the cube {#Polyhedral_nets_for_the_cube}

*Image: The 11 cubic nets.svg.* All 11 unfoldings of the cube

A [polyhedral net](wikipedia://en/1148129) for the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is necessarily a hexomino, with 11 hexominoes (shown at right) actually being nets. They appear on the right, again coloured according to their symmetry groups.

A polyhedral net for the cube cannot contain the O-tetromino, nor the I-pentomino, the U-pentomino, or the V-pentomino.
