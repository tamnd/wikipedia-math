# Lie group decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lie group decomposition"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Lie group decompositions** are used to analyse the structure of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and associated objects, by showing how they are built up out of [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011). They are essential technical tools in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of Lie groups and [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944); they can also be used to study the [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) of such groups and associated [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325). Since the use of Lie group methods became one of the standard techniques in twentieth century mathematics, many phenomena can now be referred back to decompositions.

:::

The same ideas are often applied to Lie groups, Lie algebras, [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) and [p-adic number](wikipedia://en/51423) analogues, making it harder to summarise the facts into a unified theory.

## List of decompositions {#List_of_decompositions}

- The [Jordan–Chevalley decomposition](wikipedia://en/11578785) of an element in algebraic group as a product of semisimple and unipotent elements
- The [Bruhat decomposition](wikipedia://en/1139142) $G=BWB$ of a [semisimple algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group) into double [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) of a [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) can be regarded as a generalization of the principle of [Gauss–Jordan elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss–Jordan_elimination), which generically writes a matrix as the product of an upper triangular matrix with a lower triangular matrix—but with exceptional cases. It is related to the Schubert cell decomposition of [Grassmannians](wikipedia://en/373810): see [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) for more details.
- The [Cartan decomposition](wikipedia://en/1139145) writes a semisimple real Lie algebra as the sum of eigenspaces of a [Cartan involution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_involution).[^o3]
- The [Iwasawa decomposition](wikipedia://en/1139148) $G=KAN$ of a semisimple group $G$ as the product of [compact](wikipedia://en/750326), abelian, and [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569) subgroups generalises the way a square real matrix can be written as a product of an [orthogonal matrix](wikipedia://en/105620) and an [upper triangular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_triangular_matrix) (a consequence of [Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram–Schmidt_orthogonalization)).
- The [Langlands decomposition](wikipedia://en/11085278) $P=MAN$ writes a parabolic subgroup $P$ of a Lie group as the product of semisimple, abelian, and nilpotent subgroups.
- The [Levi decomposition](wikipedia://en/2774198) writes a finite dimensional Lie algebra as a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of a [solvable](wikipedia://en/2966308) ideal and a [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) subalgebra.
- The [LU decomposition](wikipedia://en/6243993) of a dense subset in the general linear group. It can be considered as a special case of the [Bruhat decomposition](wikipedia://en/1139142).
- The [Birkhoff decomposition](wikipedia://en/32023896), a special case of the [Bruhat decomposition](wikipedia://en/1139142) for affine groups.
