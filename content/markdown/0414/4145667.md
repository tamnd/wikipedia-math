# Unit of observation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unit of observation"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **unit of observation** (or **individual**) is the [unit](wikipedia://en/27580) described by the [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data) that one [analyzes](wikipedia://en/2720954). A study may treat groups as a unit of observation within a broader [unit of analysis](wikipedia://en/4145703), for example, drawing conclusions on group characteristics from data [collected](wikipedia://en/11501746) over a more general level.

:::

For example, in a study of the [demand for money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand_for_money), the unit of observation might be chosen as the [individual person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_person), with different observations for a given instant in time differing as to which particular individual they refer to; or the unit of observation might be the country, with different observations differing only in regard to the country they refer to.

## Unit of observation vs. unit of analysis {#Unit_of_observation_vs._unit_of_analysis}

The unit of observation should not be confused with the [unit of analysis](wikipedia://en/4145703). A study may have a differing unit of observation and unit of analysis: for example, in [community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community) research, the [research design](wikipedia://en/3345681) may collect data at the individual level of observation but the [level of analysis](wikipedia://en/4145703) might be at the neighborhood level, drawing conclusions on neighborhood characteristics from data collected from individuals. Together, the unit of observation and the [level of analysis](wikipedia://en/4145703) define the [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_population) of a research enterprise.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Data point"}

## Data point {#Data_point}

A **data point** is a set of one or more [observed values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observed_value) on a single instance of the unit of observation. For example, in a study of the determinants of [money demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_demand) with the unit of observation being the individual, a data point might be the set of values of income, wealth, age of individual, and number of dependents of a specific person. [Statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) about the population would be conducted using a [statistical sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_sample) consisting of various such data points.

In addition, in [statistical graphics](wikipedia://en/15934463), a "data point" may be an individual item with a statistical display; such points may relate to either a single member of a population or to a [summary statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summary_statistic) calculated for a given subpopulation.

:::

## Types of data {#Types_of_data}

The measurements contained in a unit of observation are formally *typed*, where here *type* is used in a way compatible with [datatype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datatype) in [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing); so that the type of measurement can specify whether the measurement results in a [Boolean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_logic) value from {yes, no}, an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) or [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438), the identity of some [category](wikipedia://en/53759), or some [vector](wikipedia://en/32370) or [array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Array_data_structure).

The implication of *point* is often that the data may be plotted in a graphic display, but in many cases the data are [processed numerically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing) before that is done. In the context of [statistical graphics](wikipedia://en/15934463), measured values for individuals or summary statistics for different subpopulations are displayed as separate symbols within a display; since such symbols can differ by shape, size and colour, a single *data point* within a display can convey multiple aspects of the set of measurements for an individual or subpopulation.
