# Great disnub dirhombidodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great disnub dirhombidodecahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great disnub dirhombidodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great disnub dirhombidodecahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great disnub dirhombidodecahedron**, also called *Skilling's figure*, is a degenerate [uniform star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8464397). Its full realization has 204 faces (120 [triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), 60 [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), and 24 [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544)), 360 [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), and 60 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417). 6 triangles, 4 squares, and 2 pentagrams meet at each vertex. Due to its geometric realization having some double edges where 4 faces meet, it is not strictly a uniform polyhedron, but rather considered a [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) uniform polyhedron.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

It was proven in 1970 that there are only 75 [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223) other than the infinite families of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851). John Skilling discovered another degenerate example by relaxing the condition that edges must be single, the great disnub dirhombidodecahedron.[^n1] More precisely, he allowed any even number of faces to meet at each edge, as long as the set of faces couldn't be separated into two connected sets.

The number of edges can be considered ambiguous, because the underlying abstract polyhedron has 360 edges, but 120 pairs of these have the same image in the geometric realization, so that the geometric realization has 120 single edges and 120 double edges where 4 faces meet, for a total of 240 edges. The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) has 4 [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) faces passing through the center of the model.

The Euler characteristic of the abstract polyhedron is −96. If the pairs of coinciding edges in the geometric realization are considered to be single edges, then it has only 240 edges and Euler characteristic 24.

### Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

It shares the same [edge arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_arrangement) as the [great dirhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2910030), but has a different set of triangular faces. The vertices and edges are also shared with the uniform compounds of [twenty octahedra](wikipedia://en/14737027) or [twenty tetrahemihexahedra](wikipedia://en/14736821). 180 of the edges are shared with the [great snub dodecicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157674).

### Dual polyhedron {#Dual_polyhedron}

*Image: Great\_dirhombicosidodecacron.png.* The great disnub dirhombidodecacron

The [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of the **great disnub dirhombidodecahedron** is called the *great disnub dirhombidodecacron*. It is a nonconvex infinite [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470).

Like the visually identical [great dirhombicosidodecacron](wikipedia://en/24368309) in [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685)'s *Dual Models*, it is represented with intersecting infinite [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) passing through the model center, cut off at a certain point that is convenient for the maker. Wenninger suggested these figures are members of a new class of [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) polyhedra, called [stellation to infinity](wikipedia://en/180253#Stellation_to_infinity). However, he also acknowledged that strictly speaking they are not polyhedra because their construction does not conform to the usual definitions.
