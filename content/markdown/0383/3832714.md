# Modal operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modal operator"}

A **modal connective** (or **modal operator**) is a [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) for [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365). It is an [operator](wikipedia://en/3943) which forms [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094) from propositions. In general, a modal operator has the "formal" property of being non-[truth-functional](wikipedia://en/604707) in the following sense: The truth-value of composite formulae sometimes depend on factors other than the actual truth-value of their components. In the case of alethic modal logic, a modal operator can be said to be truth-functional in another sense, namely, that of being sensitive only to the distribution of truth-values across possible worlds, actual or not. Finally, a modal operator is "intuitively" characterized by expressing a modal attitude (such as [necessity](wikipedia://en/3986852), [possibility](wikipedia://en/394008), [belief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief), or [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge)) about the proposition to which the operator is applied.[^o2]

:::

## Syntax for modal operators {#Syntax_for_modal_operators}

The syntax rules for modal operators $\Box$ and $\Diamond$ are very similar to those for universal and existential [quantifiers](wikipedia://en/43507260); In fact, any formula with modal operators $\Box$ and $\Diamond$, and the usual [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) in [propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus) ($\land,\lor,\neg,\rightarrow,\leftrightarrow$) can be [rewritten](wikipedia://en/415847#Logic) to a [*de dicto*](wikipedia://en/530256) normal form, similar to [prenex normal form](wikipedia://en/421068). One major caveat: Whereas the universal and existential quantifiers only binds to the [propositional variables](wikipedia://en/1557562) or the [predicate variables](wikipedia://en/1011332) following the quantifiers, since the modal operators $\Box$ and $\Diamond$ quantifies over [accessible](wikipedia://en/3708585) [possible worlds](wikipedia://en/780566), they will bind to any formula in their [scope](wikipedia://en/48347082). For example, $(\exists x (x^2 = 1)) \land (0 = y)$ is logically equivalent to $\exists x (x^2 = 1\land 0 = y)$, but $(\Diamond (x^2 = 1)) \land (0 = y)$ is not logically equivalent to $\Diamond (x^2 = 1\land 0 = y)$; Instead, $\Diamond (x^2 = 1\land 0 = y)$ logically entails $(\Diamond (x^2 = 1)) \land \Diamond(0 = y)$.

When there are both modal operators and quantifiers in a formula, different order of an adjacent pair of modal operator and quantifier can lead to [different semantic meanings](wikipedia://en/530256#Representing_de_dicto_and_de_re_in_modal_logic). Also, when [multimodal logic](wikipedia://en/11233252) is involved, different order of an adjacent pair of modal operators can also lead to different semantic meanings.

## Modality interpreted {#Modality_interpreted}

There are several ways to [interpret](wikipedia://en/14511671) modal operators in modal logic, including at least: [alethic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alethic_modality), [deontic](wikipedia://en/1343980), [axiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiology), [epistemic](wikipedia://en/3827197), and [doxastic](wikipedia://en/12065590).

### Alethic {#Alethic}

[Alethic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alethic_modality) modal operators (M-operators) determine the fundamental conditions of [possible worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possible_worlds), especially [causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality), time-space parameters, and the action capacity of persons. They indicate the [possibility](wikipedia://en/394008), [impossibility](wikipedia://en/1547308) and [necessity](wikipedia://en/3986852) of actions, states of affairs, events, people, and qualities in the possible worlds.

### Deontic {#Deontic}

[Deontic](wikipedia://en/1343980) modal operators (P-operators) influence the construction of possible worlds as proscriptive or prescriptive norms, i.e. they indicate what is prohibited, obligatory, or permitted.

### Axiological {#Axiological}

[Axiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiology) modal operators (G-operators) transform the world's entities into values and disvalues as seen by a social group, a culture, or a historical period. Axiological modalities are highly subjective categories: what is good for one person may be considered as bad by another one.

### Epistemic {#Epistemic}

[Epistemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_logic) modal operators (K-operators) reflect the level of knowledge, ignorance and belief in the possible world.

### Doxastic {#Doxastic}

[Doxastic](wikipedia://en/12065590) modal operators express belief in statements.

### Boulomaic {#Boulomaic}

Boulomaic modal operators express desire.
