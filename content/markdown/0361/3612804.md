# Thomas Allen (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Allen"}

*Image: Thomas Allen.jpg.* Thomas Allen, 18th-century engraving by [James Bretherton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bretherton)

**Thomas Allen (or Alleyn)** (21 December 1542 – 30 September 1632) was an English mathematician and [astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrologer). Highly reputed in his lifetime, he published little, but was an active private teacher of mathematics. He was also well connected in the English intellectual networks of the period.[^o2]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Uttoxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttoxeter), Staffordshire. He was admitted scholar of [Trinity College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford), in 1561; and graduated as M.A. in 1567. In 1571 he left his college and fellowship, and moved to [Gloucester Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucester_Hall).[^o2] He became known for his knowledge of antiquity, philosophy, and mathematics.

## At Gloucester Hall {#At_Gloucester_Hall}

Gloucester Hall suited Allen, a sympathiser at least with [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism), because there was no stringent religious observance required there; indeed there was no chapel in the Hall.[^o6] Allen's beliefs have been classified as "church papist", but also his posture as "crypto-Catholic": a Catholic faith combined with outward conformity to the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England).[^o7][^o8] He joined there his friends Edmund Reynolds, Miles Windsor, and [George Napper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Napper), who had also left their colleges at a time of increasing religious tensions on Oxford; Napper was to be a Catholic martyr. Trinity shed six more of its Fellows within a few years.[^o9]

Allen encouraged other scholars to migrate there, such as [John Budden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Budden)[^o11] and [William Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Burton_%28antiquary,_died_1657%29).[^o12] He had a wide range of pupils and followers: [Kenelm Digby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenelm_Digby)[^o6] and [Brian Twyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Twyne)[^o13] in [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy), with [Theodore Haak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Haak) coming later.[^o14] The mathematical school of Allen included [Thomas Harriot](wikipedia://en/780445) and [Walter Warner](wikipedia://en/21433743),[^o15] and [Sir John Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Davies_%28poet,_born_1569%29) (to whom Allen taught Catholic doctrine).[^o16]

Mathematical geography was an important topical subject in which Allen was reputed, pursued by several groups in England, including another around [Henry Briggs](wikipedia://en/210399): Allen may have taught the geographer [Richard Hakluyt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hakluyt). He did teach [Robert Fludd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fludd) and [Sir Thomas Aylesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Aylesbury).[^o17] In the humanities there were [Robert Hegge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hegge),[^o18] and [William Fulbecke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Fulbecke).[^o19] When the [Camden Chair of Ancient History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden_Chair_of_Ancient_History) was being set up in the early 1620s, Allen successfully supported the candidacy of [Degory Wheare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degory_Wheare) with [William Camden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Camden); and a few years later, in 1626, Wheare came to Gloucester Hall as Principal.[^o20]

Allen died at Gloucester Hall.

## Other associations {#Other_associations}

Allen corresponded with [Henry Percy, 9th Earl of Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Percy,_9th_Earl_of_Northumberland).[^o17] Northumberland invited Allen to visit, and he spent some time with the [Syon House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syon_House) group around the Earl; he became acquainted with [Thomas Harriot](wikipedia://en/780445), [John Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dee_%28mathematician%29), and other mathematicians. He also knew well [Sir Robert Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bruce_Cotton), [William Camden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Camden), and their antiquarian associates.[^o4] He pointed out the historian [Æthelweard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Æthelweard_%28historian%29) (Fabius Quaestor) to Camden.[^o22]

## Astrologer {#Astrologer}

Allen was noted as astrologer to [Robert Dudley, 1st Earl of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Dudley,_1st_Earl_of_Leicester), as Dee was for [Queen Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England).[^o24] Foster believes Allen probably met Leicester through Lady Paulet, wife of [Hugh Paulet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Paulet), née Elizabeth Blount, who was the widow of [Sir Thomas Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Pope), the founder of Trinity College, Oxford.[^o25]

There is a surviving 62-page [horoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horoscope) cast for the teenage [Philip Sidney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sidney) in the Ashmole manuscripts in the [Bodleian Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodleian_Library), in the period 1570–2 when he was studying at Oxford, where Leicester was Chancellor, and it has been attributed to Allen; the case has also been made that it was by Dee. A link between the two is that [Edward Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kelley) is said to have worked briefly for Allen.[^o26][^o27] Allen definitely cast a natal horoscope for [Robert Pierrepont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pierrepont,_1st_Earl_of_Kingston-upon-Hull) (1584), and cast also for [William Herbert, 3rd Earl of Pembroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Herbert,_3rd_Earl_of_Pembroke), a later Chancellor of Oxford, in 1626.[^o28]

## Reputation {#Reputation}

Allen's skill in mathematics and astrology earned him the credit of being a [magician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magician_%28paranormal%29). In an incident related in [John Aubrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Aubrey)'s *[Brief Lives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brief_Lives)*, it was in a visit to [Holme Lacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holme_Lacy) as the guest of [Sir John Scudamore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Scudamore_%28courtier%29) that the servants threw his ticking watch into the [moat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moat), thinking it the Devil.[^o30] The author of *[Leicester's Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester's_Commonwealth)* accuses him of employing the art of "figuring" to further the earl of Leicester's unlawful designs, and of endeavouring by the "black art" to bring about a match between his patron and the Queen.[^o4] There Allen's name is coupled with Dee's as [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist), in a series of claims that Leicester found physicians and other lackeys for his evil-doing at Oxford and elsewhere.[^o31]

After his death, funeral orations praising Allen were given by [William Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Burton_%28antiquary,_died_1657%29) and George Bathurst (1610–1644).[^o33] Burton's retailed the story of how Leicester had offered a bishopric to Allen, who declined the offer. Allen in fact was, by choice, not in holy orders.[^o34]

## Works {#Works}

He wrote a Latin commentary on the second and third books of [Claudius Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy) of [Pelusium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelusium), *Concerning the Judgment of the Stars*, or, as it is commonly called, *Of the Quadripartite Construction, with an Exposition*. He also wrote notes on [John Bale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bale)'s *De Scriptoribus M. Britanniae*.[^o4]

## Library and legacy {#Library_and_legacy}

Allen collected manuscripts relating to history, [antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology), philosophy, and mathematics. At least 250 items from his library can still be traced.[^o2] He also acquired manuscripts from dissolved monasteries, such as [Reading Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_Abbey), for which his sources may have been [Gerbrand Harkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerbrand_Harkes), the Protestant dealer, and Clement Burdett.[^o37] While in Allen's possession, most of his manuscripts were unbound or had simple covers.[^o38]

A considerable part of Allen's collection was presented to the [Bodleian Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodleian_Library) by Sir Kenelm Digby, to whom it had been left:[^o4] over 200 manuscripts, which were rebound in calf. This bequest was strong in works by early English scientists, including [Roger Bacon](wikipedia://en/25879), [Simon Bredon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Bredon), [John Eschenden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Eschenden), [Robert Grosseteste](wikipedia://en/94721), John Sharp, and [Richard Wallingford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wallingford). But Allen's library was in flux during his lifetime, as he lent or gave items, and was consulted by others. He was a significant supporter of [Sir Thomas Bodley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Bodley)'s effort to found the Library; and gave it a number of works. Some went to the [Cottonian Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottonian_Library), presumably via [Richard James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_James_%28scholar%29). Sir Thomas Aylesbury, another former pupil,[^o17] was another one of Allen's major legatees.[^o39] The [Cuthbert Gospel of St John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuthbert_Gospel_of_St_John), seen in his library by [James Ussher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ussher), appears to have left his possession by 1622, as it is not in a catalogue of that date. Ussher wrote to Camden in 1606 of the help he had had from Allen's collection, consulting [William of Malmesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury), and a papal bull from [Giraldus Cambrensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraldus_Cambrensis) via Johannes Rossus.[^o40]
