# William Kruskal

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Kruskal"}

**William Henry Kruskal** (; October 10, 1919 – April 21, 2005) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). He is best known for having formulated the [Kruskal–Wallis one-way analysis of variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruskal–Wallis_one-way_analysis_of_variance) (together with [W. Allen Wallis](wikipedia://en/8710912)), a widely used [nonparametric statistical method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kruskal was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jews) family[^o4] in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to a successful fur wholesaler.[^o5][^o6] His mother, [Lillian Rose Vorhaus Kruskal Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillian_Rose_Vorhaus_Kruskal_Oppenheimer), became a noted promoter of [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami) during the early era of television.[^o5] He was the oldest of five children, three of whom, including himself, became researchers in mathematics and physics; see [Joseph Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kruskal) and [Martin Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kruskal). Kruskal left [Antioch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_College) to attend [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), receiving bachelor's and master's degrees in mathematics in 1940 and 1941. He pursued a Ph.D. in [mathematical sciences](wikipedia://en/25750436) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), graduating in 1955 under the supervision of [Henry Scheffé](wikipedia://en/25306204) and [Howard Levene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Levene).[^o7] During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Kruskal served at the [U.S. Naval Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Surface_Warfare_Center_Dahlgren_Division) in [Dahlgren, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlgren,_Virginia). After brief stints working for his father and lecturing at Columbia, he joined the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) faculty as an instructor in statistics in 1950.

In 1958 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o8] He edited the [Annals of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/23772805) from 1958 to 1961, served as president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1971, and of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1982. Kruskal retired as professor emeritus in 1990.[^o5] In 1992 Kruskal was awarded the [American Statistical Association Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224).[^o9]

He died in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) in 2005.[^o5]
