# Completeness of atomic initial sequents

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Completeness of atomic initial sequents"}

In [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329), the **completeness of atomic initial sequents** states that initial sequents $A \vdash A$ (where $A$ is an arbitrary [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28logic%29)) can be derived from only atomic initial sequents $p \vdash p$ (where $p$ is an [atomic formula](wikipedia://en/4472066)). This theorem plays a role analogous to [eta expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eta_expansion) in [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203), and dual to [cut elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_elimination) and [beta reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_reduction). Typically it can be established by [induction on the structure](wikipedia://en/211399) of $A$, much more easily than cut elimination.

:::
