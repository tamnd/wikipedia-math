# Shannon wavelet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shannon wavelet"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Shannon wavelet** (or **sinc wavelets**) is a decomposition that is defined by signal analysis by ideal [bandpass filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandpass_filter). Shannon wavelet may be either of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) type.

:::

Shannon wavelet is not well-localized (noncompact) in the [time domain](wikipedia://en/173419), but its Fourier transform is band-limited (compact support). Hence Shannon wavelet has poor time localization but has good frequency localization. These characteristics are in stark contrast to those of the [Haar wavelet](wikipedia://en/50905). The Haar and sinc systems are Fourier duals of each other.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Sinc function is the starting point for the definition of the Shannon wavelet.

### Scaling function {#Scaling_function}

First, we define the scaling function to be the sinc function.

$$\phi^{\text{(Sha)}}(t) := \frac {\sin \pi t} {\pi t} = \operatorname{sinc}(t).$$

And define the dilated and translated instances to be

$$\phi^n_k(t) := 2^{n/2}\phi^{\text{(Sha)}}(2^n t-k)$$

where the parameter $n,k$ means the dilation and the translation for the wavelet respectively.

Then we can derive the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the scaling function:

$\Phi^{\text{(Sha)}}(\omega) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\Pi(\frac{\omega}{2\pi}) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{2\pi}, & \mbox{if } {|\omega| \le \pi}, \\ 0 & \mbox{if } \mbox{otherwise}. \\ \end{cases}$ where the (normalised) [gate function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_function) is defined by

$\Pi ( x):= \begin{cases} 1, & \mbox{if } {|x| \le 1/2}, \\ 0 & \mbox{if } \mbox{otherwise}. \\ \end{cases}$ Also for the dilated and translated instances of scaling function: $\Phi^n_k(\omega) = \frac{2^{-n/2}}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega(k+1)/2^n}\Pi(\frac{\omega}{2^{n+1}\pi})$

### Mother wavelet {#Mother_wavelet}

Use $\Phi^{\text{(Sha)}}$ and multiresolution approximation we can derive the Fourier transform of the Mother wavelet:

$$\Psi^{\text{(Sha)}}(\omega) = \frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega} \bigg(\Pi(\frac{\omega}{\pi}-\frac{3}{2})+\Pi(\frac{\omega}{\pi}+\frac{3}{2})\bigg)$$

And the dilated and translated instances:

$$\Psi^n_k(\omega) = \frac{2^{-n/2}}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega(k+1)/2^n} \bigg(\Pi(\frac{\omega}{2^n\pi}-\frac{3}{2})+\Pi(\frac{\omega}{2^n\pi}+\frac{3}{2})\bigg)$$

Then the shannon mother wavelet function and the family of dilated and translated instances can be obtained by the inverse Fourier transform:

$$\psi^{\text{(Sha)}}(t) = \frac{\sin\pi(t-(1/2))-\sin2\pi(t-(1/2))}{\pi(t-1/2)} =\operatorname{sinc}\bigg(t-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)-2\operatorname{sinc}\bigg(2(t-\frac{1}{2})\bigg)$$

$$\psi^n_k(t) = 2^{n/2}\psi^{\text{(Sha)}}(2^nt-k)$$

### Property of mother wavelet and scaling function {#Property_of_mother_wavelet_and_scaling_function}

- Mother wavelets are orthonormal, namely,

$$<\psi^n_k(t), \psi^m_h(t)>=\delta^{nm}\delta_{hk}= \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if }h=k \text{ and } n=m\\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

- The translated instances of scaling function at level $n=0$ are orthogonal

$$<\phi^0_k(t), \phi^0_h(t)>=\delta^{kh}$$

- The translated instances of scaling function at level $n=0$ are orthogonal to the mother wavelets

$$<\phi^0_k(t), \psi^m_h(t)>=0$$

- Shannon wavelets has an infinite number of vanishing moments.

:::

## Reconstruction of a Function by Shannon Wavelets {#Reconstruction_of_a_Function_by_Shannon_Wavelets}

Suppose $f(x)\in L_2(\mathbb{R})$ such that $\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{FT}\{f\}\subset[-\pi,\pi]$ and for any dilation and the translation parameter $n,k$,

$\Bigg|\int^\infty_{-\infty}f(t)\phi^0_k(t)dt\Bigg|<\infty$, $\Bigg|\int^\infty_{-\infty}f(t)\psi^n_k(t)dt\Bigg|<\infty$

Then

$f(t)=\sum^\infty_{k=\infty}\alpha_k\phi^0_k(t)$ is uniformly convergent, where $\alpha_k=f(k)$

## Real Shannon wavelet {#Real_Shannon_wavelet}

*Image: Wavelet Shan.svg.* Real Shannon wavelet

The [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the Shannon mother wavelet is given by:

$$\Psi^{(\operatorname{Sha}) }(w) = \prod \left( \frac {w- 3 \pi /2} {\pi}\right)+\prod \left( \frac {w+ 3 \pi /2} {\pi}\right).$$

where the (normalised) [gate function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_function) is defined by

$$\prod ( x):= \begin{cases} 1, & \mbox{if } {|x| \le 1/2}, \\ 0 & \mbox{if } \mbox{otherwise}. \\ \end{cases}$$

The analytical expression of the real Shannon wavelet can be found by taking the inverse [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247):

$$\psi^{(\operatorname{Sha}) }(t) = \operatorname{sinc} \left( \frac {t} {2}\right)\cdot \cos \left( \frac {3 \pi t} {2}\right)$$

or alternatively as

$$\psi^{(\operatorname{Sha})}(t)=2 \cdot \operatorname{sinc}(2t)-\operatorname{sinc}(t),$$

where

$$\operatorname{sinc}(t):= \frac {\sin {\pi t}} {\pi t}$$

is the usual [sinc function](wikipedia://en/610583) that appears in [Shannon sampling theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_sampling_theorem).

This wavelet belongs to the $C^\infty$-class of [differentiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function#Differentiability_classes), but it decreases slowly at infinity and has no [bounded support](wikipedia://en/381013#Compact_support), since [band-limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band-limited) signals cannot be time-limited.

The [scaling function](wikipedia://en/50903#Scaling_function) for the Shannon MRA (or *Sinc*-MRA) is given by the sample function:

$$\phi^{(Sha)}(t)= \frac {\sin \pi t} {\pi t} = \operatorname{sinc}(t).$$

## Complex Shannon wavelet {#Complex_Shannon_wavelet}

In the case of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [continuous wavelet](wikipedia://en/862322), the Shannon wavelet is defined by

$$\psi^{(CSha) }(t)=\operatorname{sinc}(t) \cdot e^{-2\pi i t},$$
