# Bernhard Pattenhausen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bernhard Pattenhausen"}

**Bernhard Nikolaus Philipp Pattenhausen** (July 7, 1855 – August 25, 1926) was a German [geodesist](wikipedia://en/12608).[^o3] He co-founded the [Lohrmann Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohrmann_Observatory) in [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden) in 1913.[^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Pattenhausen was born in [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt), Germany in 1855, the twelfth child of a family of merchants. He studied engineering at the [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology) from 1872 to 1876.

## Career {#Career}

After graduating from KIT, Pattenhausen worked for two years as an assistant to [Wilhelm Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Jordan_%28geodesist%29). He received a post-doctoral habilitation in 1889 which enabled him to teach independently at a university. He also worked at the Ducal Forestry Institute in [Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig) for several years before being appointed director in 1893. That year, he was made professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and geodesy at the [Dresden University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dresden), becoming [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) in 1907 or 1908.[^o7]

Pattenhausen was director of the Mathematical and Physical Salon in\[Dresden after 1894. He was appointed chairman of the Society for Geography in Dresden from 1904 to 1919, and became a [Privy Councillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_council) in 1906. He co-founded the [Lohrmann Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohrmann_Observatory) at TU Dresden in 1913.[^o8]

He was awarded an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from the [Braunschweig University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Braunschweig) in 1926. He died of a stroke in 1926 and was burined in the Tolkweitz cemetery of Dresden.
