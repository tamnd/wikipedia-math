# Radó's theorem (Riemann surfaces)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radó's theorem"}

In mathematical complex analysis, **Radó's theorem**, proved by (txt), states that every [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) is [second-countable](wikipedia://en/901459) (has a countable base for its topology).

:::

The [Prüfer surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prüfer_surface) is an example of a surface with no countable base for the topology, so cannot have the structure of a Riemann surface.

The obvious analogue of Radó's theorem in higher dimensions is false: there are 2-dimensional connected complex manifolds that are not second-countable.
