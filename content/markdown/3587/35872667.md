# Egon Balas

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Egon Balas"}

**Egon Balas** (June 7, 1922 in [Cluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj,_Romania), Romania – March 18, 2019)[^o3] was an [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969) and a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of industrial administration and applied mathematics at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University). He was the Thomas Lord Professor of Operations Research at Carnegie Mellon's [Tepper School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepper_School_of_Business) and did fundamental work in developing [integer and disjunctive programming](wikipedia://en/411215).[^o4]

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Balas was born in [Cluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca) (Romania) in a [Hungarian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Jewish) family. His original name was Blatt, which was first changed to the Hungarian Balázs and then later to the Romanian Balaş. He was married to art historian [Edith Balas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Balas), a survivor of Auschwitz, with whom he had two daughters.[^o6][^o7] He was imprisoned by the Communist authorities for several years after the war.[^o8]

He left Romania in 1966 and accepted an appointment with [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) in 1967. Balas obtained a "Diploma Licentiate" in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) ([Bolyai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University), 1949) and [Ph.D.s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in economics ([University of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Brussels_%281834–1969%29), 1967) and mathematics ([University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), 1968).[^o9] His mathematics PhD thesis was titled *Minimax et dualité en programmation discrète* and was written under the direction of [Robert Fortet](wikipedia://en/35256220).
