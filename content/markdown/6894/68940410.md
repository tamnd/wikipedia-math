# Eleanor P. Cushing

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eleanor P. Cushing"}

**Eleanor Philbrook Cushing** (December 27, 1856 – April 21, 1925) was an American mathematics professor, on the faculty of [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) from 1881 to 1922.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Cushing was born in [Bath, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Maine), the daughter of Samuel Woodward Cushing and Mary Ann Mereen Cushing. Her father was a merchant. Her brother William Lee Cushing taught Latin at Yale and ran the [Westminster School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_School_%28Connecticut%29) in Connecticut. Playwright [Tom Cushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cushing) was her nephew.[^o4]

Cushing was a member of Smith College's first graduating class in 1879.[^o5] She earned a master's degree in 1882.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

After graduating from Smith College, she taught school in Bath and [New Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut), then became a member of the Smith College faculty from 1881[^o8] to 1922, as professor of mathematics and chair of the department.[^o9][^o10] She was also president of the college's Alumnae Association and the undergraduate Mathematics Club.[^o11]

In 1890, she was named to the Commission Of Colleges On Admission Examinations In New England.[^o12] "It displeased her to be called professor and she could not be persuaded to wear an academic cap and gown—never appearing where they were required," [Laurenus Clark Seelye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurenus_Clark_Seelye) recalled of Cushing's disdain for "ostentatious display". She resigned as professor emerita, and retired to Maine.[^o5]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Cushing inherited a [Queen Anne-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Anne_style_architecture) house in [Northampton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton,_Massachusetts) from her friend, Elizabeth Jarvis Hopkins. She lived there from 1897 to 1922, then donated the building to Smith College.[^o14] She died in 1925, in Bath, aged 69 years.[^o15][^o16] Cushing House residence which was built in 1922 on the campus of Smith College is named for her.[^o17]
