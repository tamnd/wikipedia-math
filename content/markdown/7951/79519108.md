# Susan Assmann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susan Assmann"}

**Susan Fera Assmann** (June 26, 1956 – May 30, 2020) was an American mathematician and statistician. Her research in mathematics included work on the [bin covering problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bin_covering_problem) (a [discrete optimization](wikipedia://en/420567) problem), while her later work in statistics and biostatistics included work on [subgroup analysis](wikipedia://en/5434504) and on the use of [spironolactone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spironolactone) for treating heart failure.

:::

## Early life, education, and career {#Early_life,_education,_and_career}

Assmann is originally from [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), where she was born on June 26, 1956. Her father, Frederick Fera Assmann (1915–2004) was a chemical engineer for the US Army and Thiokol Chemical Corporation; her mother, Mary Assmann (died 2010), was a science teacher at [The Pennington School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pennington_School). In her doctoral dissertation, Susan Assmann writes that her interest in mathematics "was sparked by the 'interesting test' which constituted part of the application for entrance to the [Hampshire College Summer Studies in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/242549) program", a summer program for high school mathematics students.

She was the 1974 valedictorian at [Hopewell Valley Central High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_Valley_Central_High_School) in [Pennington, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennington,_New_Jersey), and a 1978 [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude) graduate of [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College). She completed a Ph.D. in mathematics in 1983 at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), with the dissertation *Problems in Discrete and Applied Mathematics* supervised by [Daniel Kleitman](wikipedia://en/5299894). Through her joint publications with Kleitman on problems including the [bin covering problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bin_covering_problem), she has [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) 2.

After continuing in academia as a mathematics professor at the [University of Massachusetts Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Lowell) and [Regis College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regis_College_%28Massachusetts%29), Assmann came to work for the New England Research Institute (later known as HealthCore), where she continued as a principal statistician for nearly 26 years. Supporting the corresponding shift in her research interests, she received a master's degree in [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) from the School of Public Health & Health Sciences at the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst) in 1994.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Assmann married Jeffrey Del Papa, a private equity manager. Her interests included [change ringing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_ringing) and early [harpsichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpsichord) music.

Assmann died of cancer on May 30, 2020.
