# Subfunctor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subfunctor"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **subfunctor** is a special type of [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) that is an analogue of a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a [category](wikipedia://en/53759), and let $F$ be a contravariant functor from $\mathcal{C}$ to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) **Set**. A contravariant functor $G$ from $\mathcal{C}$ to **Set** is a **subfunctor** of *F* if

1. For all objects *c* of $\mathcal{C}$, $G(c) \subseteq F(c)$, and
1. For all arrows $f: c' \rightarrow c$ of $\mathcal{C}$, $G(f)$ is the [restriction](wikipedia://en/1589032) of $F(f)$ to $G(c')$.

This relation is often written as $G \subseteq F$.

For example, let **1** be the category with a single object and a single arrow. A functor *F*: **1** → **Set** maps the unique object of **1** to some set *S* and the unique identity arrow of **1** to the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) 1<sub>*S*</sub> on *S*. A subfunctor *G* of *F* maps the unique object of **1** to a subset *T* of *S* and maps the unique identity arrow to the identity function 1<sub>*T*</sub> on *T*. Notice that 1<sub>*T*</sub> is the restriction of 1<sub>*S*</sub> to *T*. Consequently, subfunctors of *F* correspond to subsets of *S*.

:::

## Remarks {#Remarks}

Subfunctors in general are like global versions of subsets. For example, if one imagines the objects of some category **C** to be analogous to the open sets of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), then a contravariant functor from **C** to the category of sets gives a set-valued [presheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf) on **C**, that is, it associates sets to the objects of **C** in a way that is compatible with the arrows of **C**. A subfunctor then associates a subset to each set, again in a compatible way.

The most important examples of subfunctors are subfunctors of the [Hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037). Let *c* be an object of the category **C**, and consider the functor Hom(−, *c*). This functor takes an object *c* of **C** and gives back all of the morphisms *c* → *c*. A subfunctor of Hom(−, *c*) gives back only some of the morphisms. Such a subfunctor is called a [sieve](wikipedia://en/2163544),[^o5] and it is usually used when defining [Grothendieck topologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_topologies).

## Open subfunctors {#Open_subfunctors}

Subfunctors are also used in the construction of [representable functors](wikipedia://en/844461) on the category of [ringed spaces](wikipedia://en/248014). Let *F* be a contravariant functor from the category of ringed spaces to the category of sets, and let *G* ⊆ *F*. Suppose that this inclusion morphism *G* → *F* is representable by open immersions, i.e., for any representable functor Hom(−, *X*) and any morphism Hom(−, *X*) → *F*, the fibered product *G*×<sub>*F*</sub>Hom(−, *X*) is a representable functor Hom(−, *Y*) and the morphism *Y* → *X* defined by the [Yoneda lemma](wikipedia://en/34359) is an open immersion. Then *G* is called an **open subfunctor** of *F*. If *F* is covered by representable open subfunctors, then, under certain conditions, it can be shown that *F* is representable. This is a useful technique for the construction of ringed spaces. It was discovered and exploited heavily by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), who applied it especially to the case of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754). For a formal statement and proof, see Grothendieck, *[Éléments de géométrie algébrique](wikipedia://en/471387)*, vol. 1, 2nd ed., chapter 0, section 4.5.
