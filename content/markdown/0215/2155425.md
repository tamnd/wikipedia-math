# NOEKEON

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="NOEKEON"}

**NOEKEON** is a family of two [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594) designed by [Joan Daemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Daemen), [Michaël Peeters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michaël_Peeters), [Gilles Van Assche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_Van_Assche) and [Vincent Rijmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Rijmen) and submitted to the [NESSIE](wikipedia://en/497430) project in September 2000. The two ciphers are "direct mode" NOEKEON, to be used for maximum efficiency where [related-key attacks](wikipedia://en/1516144) are not possible, and "indirect mode" NOEKEON where they are.

:::

NOEKEON has a 128-bit block and key size. Each round of NOEKEON employs a sequence of self-inverse transformations which can be implemented easily in hardware or software, even where [differential power analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_power_analysis) is a concern. It is designed according to a variant of the [wide-trail strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-trail_strategy).

Cryptanalysis by [Lars Knudsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Ramkilde_Knudsen) and [Håvard Raddum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Håvard_Raddum) in April 2001 showed that "indirect mode" NOEKEON was still vulnerable to certain peculiar kinds of related-key cryptanalysis, and showed weaknesses in NOEKEON-variant ciphers which cast doubt on the design strategy behind NOEKEON and thus on its security. As a result, it was not a NESSIE selected algorithm.

The authors of NOEKEON contend ("On NOEKEON, no!") that the related-key attacks required to break "indirect mode" NOEKEON are not a practical concern, and that it is as a result of deliberate design that NOEKEON is not vulnerable to the attacks that break the variant ciphers; they assert that NOEKEON is still a good and useful cipher.
