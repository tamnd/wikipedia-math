# PGPfone

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="PGPfone"}

**PGPfone** was a [secure voice](wikipedia://en/1560035) telephony system developed by [Philip Zimmermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Zimmermann) in 1995. The PGPfone protocol had little in common with Zimmermann's popular [PGP](wikipedia://en/23080) email encryption package, except for the use of the name. It used ephemeral [Diffie-Hellman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffie-Hellman) protocol to establish a session key, which was then used to encrypt the stream of voice packets. The two parties compared a short authentication string to detect a [Man-in-the-middle attack](wikipedia://en/144676), which is the most common method of wiretapping secure phones of this type. PGPfone could be used point-to-point (with two [modems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modem)) over the public switched telephone network, or over the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) as an early [Voice over IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_over_IP) system.

:::

In 1996, there were no protocol standards for Voice over IP. Ten years later, Zimmermann released the successor to PGPfone, [Zfone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zfone) and [ZRTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZRTP), a newer and secure VoIP protocol based on modern VoIP standards. Zfone builds on the ideas of PGPfone.

According to the [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) PGPfone web page,[^o8] "MIT is no longer distributing PGPfone. Given that the software has not been maintained since 1997, we doubt it would run on most modern systems."
