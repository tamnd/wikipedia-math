# George Adomian

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="George Adomian"}

**George Adomian** (1922 – June 17, 1996)[^o3][^o4] was an American [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mathematicians), aerospace engineer, and academic of Armenian descent.[^o3] He developed the [Adomian decomposition method](wikipedia://en/2155356) (ADM) for solving [nonlinear differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_differential_equation), both ordinary and partial.[^o4] The method is explained, among other places, in his book *Solving Frontier Problems in Physics: The Decomposition Method* (Kluwer, Dordrecht, 2004).[^o5]

:::

He was educated at [Cass Technical High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cass_Technical_High_School) in Detroit. He earned a bachelor's and master's of science in electrical engineering at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and a PhD in physics from [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles)[^o5] in 1961, advised by [David Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Saxon).[^o6] His dissertation was titled *Linear Stochastic Operators*.[^o6]

He was a professor at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) from 1964-66, and then was Chair of Applied Mathematics at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) (UGA) from 1966 through 1989. While at UGA, he started the [Center for Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Applied_Mathematics).[^o5]

He was a [Fellow of the American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o4] He was also a member of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376), [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society), [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi), [Tau Beta Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau_Beta_Pi), [Eta Kappa Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eta_Kappa_Nu), and [Sigma Pi Sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Pi_Sigma).[^o4]
