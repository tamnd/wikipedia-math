# Darboux's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Darboux's formula"}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), **Darboux's formula** is a formula introduced by (txt) for [summing](wikipedia://en/246160) [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) by using [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532) or evaluating integrals using infinite series. It is a generalization to the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) of the [Euler–Maclaurin summation formula](wikipedia://en/9637), which is used for similar purposes and derived in a similar manner (by repeated [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) of a particular choice of [integrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrand)). Darboux's formula can also be used to derive the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) from [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

If *φ*(*t*) is a polynomial of degree *n* and *f* an analytic function then

$$\begin{aligned} & \sum_{m=0}^n (-1)^m (z - a)^m \left[\varphi^{(n - m)}(1)f^{(m)}(z) - \varphi^{(n - m)}(0)f^{(m)}(a)\right] \\ = {} & (-1)^n(z - a)^{n + 1}\int_0^1\varphi(t)f^{(n+1)}\left[a + t(z - a)\right]\, dt. \end{aligned}$$

The formula can be proved by repeated [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

Taking *φ* to be a [Bernoulli polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_polynomial) in Darboux's formula gives the [Euler–Maclaurin summation formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Maclaurin_summation_formula). Taking *φ* to be (*t* − 1)<sup>*n*</sup> gives the formula for a [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448).

:::
