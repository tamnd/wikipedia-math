# Robinson arithmetic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robinson arithmetic"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Robinson arithmetic** is a [finitely axiomatized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_axiomatized) fragment of first-order [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) (PA), first set out by [Raphael M. Robinson](wikipedia://en/693495) in 1950.[^n1] It is usually denoted **Q**.

:::

**Q** is PA without the [axiom schema](wikipedia://en/764613) of [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881). **Q** is weaker than PA but it has the same language, and both theories are [incomplete](wikipedia://en/10086335). **Q** is important and interesting because it is a finitely axiomatized fragment of PA that is recursively incompletable and [essentially undecidable](wikipedia://en/913118#Decidability_of_a_theory).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Axioms"}

## Axioms {#Axioms}

The [background logic](wikipedia://en/396102#Logical_system) of **Q** is [first-order logic with identity](wikipedia://en/10983#Equality_and_its_axioms), denoted by infix '='. The individuals, called [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474), are members of a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) called **N** with a distinguished member **0**, called [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero). There are three [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245) over **N**:

- A [unary operation](wikipedia://en/161006) called [successor](wikipedia://en/325637) and denoted by [prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefix_notation) *S*;
- Two [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948), [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) and [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845), denoted by infix **\+** and **·**, respectively.

The following [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) for **Q** are Q1–Q7 in (Burgess 2005, 42) (cf. also the axioms of [first-order arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_arithmetic)). [Variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) not bound by an [existential quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_quantifier) are bound by an implicit [universal quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_quantifier).

1. *Sx* ≠ **0**
   - **0** is not the successor of any number.
1. (*Sx* = *Sy*) → *x* = *y*
   - If the successor of *x* is identical to the successor of *y*, then *x* and *y* are identical. (1) and (2) yield the minimum of facts about **N** (it is an [infinite set](wikipedia://en/142679) bounded by **0**) and *S* (it is an [injective function](wikipedia://en/45196) whose [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) is **N**) needed for non-triviality. The [converse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_%28logic%29) of (2) follows from the properties of identity.
1. *y*=**0** ∨ ∃*x* (*Sx* = *y*)
   - Every number is either **0** or the successor of some number. The [axiom schema](wikipedia://en/764613) of [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) present in arithmetics stronger than **Q** turns this axiom into a theorem.
1. *x* + **0** = *x*
1. *x* + *Sy* = *S*(*x* + *y*)
   - (4) and (5) are the [recursive definition](wikipedia://en/1047605) of [addition](wikipedia://en/61338).
1. *x*·**0** = **0**
1. *x·Sy* = (*x·y*) + *x*
   - (6) and (7) are the [recursive definition](wikipedia://en/1047605) of [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845).

### Variant axiomatizations {#Variant_axiomatizations}

The axioms in (Robinson 1950) are (1)–(13) in (Mendelson 2015, 202-203). The first 6 of Robinson's 13 axioms are required only when, unlike here, the background logic does not include identity.

The usual strict [total order](wikipedia://en/30330) on **N**, "less than" (denoted by "\<"), can be defined in terms of addition via the rule *x* \< *y* ↔ ∃*z* (*Sz* + *x* = *y*). Equivalently, we get a definitional conservative extension of **Q** by taking "\<" as primitive and adding this rule as an eighth axiom; this system is termed "*Robinson arithmetic* **R**" in (Boolos Burgess Jeffrey 2002, Sec 16.4).

A different extension of **Q**, which we temporarily call **Q+**, is obtained if we take "\<" as primitive and add (instead of the last definitional axiom) the following three axioms to axioms (1)–(7) of **Q**:[^n2]

- ¬(*x* \< 0)
- *x* \< *Sy* ↔ (*x* \< *y* ∨ *x* = *y*)
- *x* \< *y* ∨ *x* = *y* ∨ *y* \< *x*

**Q+** is still a conservative extension of **Q**, in the sense that any formula provable in **Q+** not containing the symbol "\<" is already provable in **Q**. (Adding only the first two of the above three axioms to **Q** gives a conservative extension of **Q** that is equivalent to what (Burgess 2005, 56) calls **Q\***. See also (Burgess 2005, 230), but note that the second of the above three axioms cannot be deduced from "the pure definitional extension" of **Q** obtained by adding only the axiom *x* \< *y* ↔ ∃*z* (*Sz* + *x* = *y*).)

Among the axioms (1)–(7) of **Q**, axiom (3) needs an inner existential quantifier. (Shoenfield 1967, 22) gives an axiomatization that has only (implicit) outer universal quantifiers, by dispensing with axiom (3) of **Q** but adding the above three axioms with \< as primitive. That is, Shoenfield's system is **Q+** minus axiom (3), and is strictly weaker than **Q+**, since axiom (3) is independent of the other axioms (for example, the [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) less than $\omega^\omega$ forms a model for all axioms except (3) when *Sv* is interpreted as *v* + 1). Shoenfield's system also appears in (Boolos Burgess Jeffrey 2002, Sec 16.2), where it is called the "*minimal arithmetic*" (also denoted by **Q**). A closely related axiomatization, that uses "≤" instead of "\<", may be found in (Machover 1996, 256–257).

:::

## Metamathematics {#Metamathematics}

On the metamathematics of **Q** see (Boolos Burgess Jeffrey 2002, chpt. 16), (Tarski Mostowski Robinson 1953), (Smullyan 1991), (Mendelson 2015, 202-203) and (Burgess 2005, §§1.5a, 2.2). The [intended interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intended_interpretation) of **Q** is the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers) and their usual arithmetic in which [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) and [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) have their customary meaning, identity is [equality](wikipedia://en/90446), *Sx* = *x* + 1, and **0** is the natural number [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29).

Any model (structure) that satisfies all axioms of **Q** except possibly axiom (3) has a unique submodel ("the standard part") isomorphic to the standard natural numbers (**N**, +, ·, S, 0). (Axiom (3) need not be satisfied; for example the [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) with non-negative integer coefficients forms a model that satisfies all axioms except (3).)

**Q**, like [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic), has [nonstandard models](wikipedia://en/6251420) of all infinite [cardinalities](wikipedia://en/6174). However, unlike Peano arithmetic, [Tennenbaum's theorem](wikipedia://en/13392068) does not apply to **Q**, and it has [computable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computable) non-standard models. For instance, there is a computable model of **Q** consisting of integer-coefficient polynomials with positive leading coefficient, plus the zero polynomial, with their usual arithmetic.

A notable characteristic of **Q** is the absence of the axiom scheme of [induction](wikipedia://en/18881). Hence it is often possible to prove in **Q** every specific instance of a fact about the natural numbers, but not the associated general theorem. For example, 5 + 7 = 7 + 5 is provable in **Q**, but the general statement *x* + *y* = *y* + *x* is not. Similarly, one cannot prove the general statement *Sx* ≠ *x*.[^n3] A model of **Q** that fails many of the standard facts is obtained by adjoining two distinct new elements *a* and *b* to the standard model of natural numbers and defining *Sa* = *a*, *Sb* = *b*, *x* + *a* = *b* and *x* + *b* = *a* for all *x*, *a* + *n* = *a* and *b* + *n* = *b* if *n* is a standard natural number, *x*·0 = 0 for all *x*, *a*·*n* = *b* and *b*·*n* = *a* if *n* is a non-zero standard natural number, *x*·*a* = *a* for all *x* except *x* = *a*, *x*·*b* = *b* for all *x* except *x* = *b*, *a*·*a* = *b*, and *b*·*b* = *a*.[^n4]

**Q** is interpretable in a fragment of [Zermelo's](wikipedia://en/322623) [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory), consisting of [extensionality](wikipedia://en/294056), existence of the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566), and the [axiom of adjunction](wikipedia://en/43548952). This theory is S' in (Tarski Mostowski Robinson 1953, 34) and ST in (Burgess 2005, 90-91,223). See [general set theory](wikipedia://en/9373204) for more details.

**Q** is a finitely axiomatized [first-order theory](wikipedia://en/3690584) that is considerably weaker than [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) (PA), and whose axioms contain only one [existential quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_quantifier). Yet like PA it is incomplete and incompletable in the sense of [Gödel's incompleteness theorems](wikipedia://en/58863), and essentially undecidable. (Robinson 1950) derived the **Q** axioms (1)–(7) above by noting just what PA axioms are required [^n5] to prove that every [computable function](wikipedia://en/1139338) is representable in PA.[^o28] The only use this proof makes of the PA axiom schema of [induction](wikipedia://en/18881) is to prove a statement that is axiom (3) above, and so, all computable functions are representable in **Q**.[^n6][^n7][^n8] The conclusion of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem also holds for **Q**: no consistent recursively axiomatized extension of **Q** can prove its own consistency, even if we additionally restrict Gödel numbers of proofs to a definable cut.[^n9][^n10][^n11]

The first incompleteness theorem applies only to axiomatic systems defining sufficient arithmetic to carry out the necessary coding constructions (of which [Gödel numbering](wikipedia://en/207790) forms a part). The axioms of **Q** were chosen specifically to ensure they are strong enough for this purpose. Thus the usual proof of the first incompleteness theorem can be used to show that **Q** is incomplete and undecidable. This indicates that the incompleteness and undecidability of PA cannot be blamed on the only aspect of PA differentiating it from **Q**, namely the [axiom schema](wikipedia://en/764613) of [induction](wikipedia://en/18881).

Gödel's theorems do not hold when any one of the seven axioms above is dropped. These fragments of **Q** remain undecidable, but they are no longer essentially undecidable: they have consistent decidable extensions, as well as uninteresting models (i.e., models that are not end-extensions of the standard natural numbers).
