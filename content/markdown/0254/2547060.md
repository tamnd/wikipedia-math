# Wassily Hoeffding

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wassily Hoeffding"}

**Wassily Hoeffding** (June 12, 1914 – February 28, 1991) was a Finnish-born American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [probabilist](wikipedia://en/23542). Hoeffding was one of the founders of [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369), in which Hoeffding contributed the idea and basic results on [U-statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-statistics).[^o3][^o4]

:::

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [Hoeffding's inequality](wikipedia://en/1136348) provides an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) for the sum of [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) to deviate from its [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653).[^o5]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hoeffding was born in [Mustamäki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhino,_Leningrad_Oblast), [Grand Duchy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland), although his place of birth is registered as [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg) on his birth certificate. His father was an economist and a disciple of [Peter Struve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Berngardovich_Struve), the Russian social scientist and public figure. His paternal grandparents were [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_people) and his father's uncle was the Danish philosopher [Harald Høffding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Høffding). His mother, née Wedensky, had studied medicine. Both grandfathers had been engineers. In 1918 the family left Tsarskoye Selo for Ukraine and, after traveling through scenes of civil war, finally left Russia for Denmark in 1920, where Wassily entered school.

In 1924 the family settled in Berlin. Hoeffding obtained his PhD in 1940 at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). He migrated with his mother to the United States in 1946. His younger brother, Oleg, became a military historian in the United States. [^o7]

Hoeffding's ashes were buried in a small cemetery on land owned by [George E. Nicholson, Jr.'s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_E._Nicholson,_Jr.) family in Chatham County, NC about 11 miles south of Chapel Hill, NC.

## Work {#Work}

In 1948, Hoeffding introduced the concept of [U-statistics](wikipedia://en/16811057). In 1951, he spent time on the central limit theorem under nonstandard conditions and later proved a combinatorial central limit theorem: If (R1, … , Rn) is a random permutation of the numbers 1, 2, … , n that assumes every permutation with the same probability 1/n!, then $T_{n} = \Sigma_{j} b(j,R_{j})$ is asymptotically normal as n → ∞ under simple conditions that were later proved to be minimal. This result has turned out to be the workhorse of the asymptotic theory of rank tests that often have statistics according to the aforementioned equation or that can be approximated by such statistics.[^o9]

Further details on Hoeffding's work are available in *The Collected Works of Wassily Hoeffding*.[^o10]
