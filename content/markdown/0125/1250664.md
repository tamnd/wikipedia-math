# Deep inference

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deep inference"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **deep inference** names a general idea in [structural proof theory](wikipedia://en/929998) that breaks with the classical [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329) by generalising the notion of [structure](wikipedia://en/359377) to permit inference to occur in contexts of high structural complexity. The term *deep inference* is generally reserved for [proof calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_calculi) where the structural complexity is unbounded; in this article we will use **non-shallow inference** to refer to calculi that have structural complexity greater than the sequent calculus, but not unboundedly so, although this is not at present established terminology.

:::

Deep inference is not important in logic outside of structural proof theory, since the phenomena that lead to the proposal of [formal systems](wikipedia://en/396102) with deep inference are all related to the [cut-elimination theorem](wikipedia://en/910234). The first calculus of deep inference was proposed by [Kurt Schütte](wikipedia://en/16184365),[^o2] but the idea did not generate much interest at the time.

[Nuel Belnap](wikipedia://en/1251311) proposed [display logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_logic) in an attempt to characterise the essence of structural proof theory. The [calculus of structures](wikipedia://en/1250632) was proposed in order to give a cut-free characterisation of [noncommutative logic](wikipedia://en/693282). [Cirquent calculus](wikipedia://en/47037875) was developed as a system of deep inference allowing to explicitly account for the possibility of subcomponent-sharing.
