# Solid torus

*Image: Torus illustration.png.* Solid torus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Solid torus"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **solid torus** is the [solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_%28geometry%29) formed by sweeping a [disk](wikipedia://en/329542) around a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220).[^o2] As a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), it is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $S^1 \times D^2$ of the disk and the circle,[^o3] endowed with the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910).

:::

A standard way to visualize a solid torus is as a [toroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toroid_%28geometry%29), embedded in [3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-space). However, it should be distinguished from a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800), a [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) which has the same visual appearance: the torus is the [two-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/70691232) on the boundary of a toroid, while the solid torus includes also the compact interior space enclosed by the torus. A solid torus is a torus plus the region inside the torus, with a non-zero [volume](wikipedia://en/32498). Real-world objects that approximate a *solid torus* include [O-rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O-ring), non-inflatable [lifebuoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifebuoy), ring [doughnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doughnut), and [bagels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel).

## Topological properties {#Topological_properties}

The solid torus is a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_%28mathematics%29) 3-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with boundary. The boundary is homeomorphic to $S^1 \times S^1$, the ordinary [torus](wikipedia://en/74800).

Since the disk $D^2$ is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible), the solid torus has the [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) type of a circle, $S^1$.[^o5] Therefore the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) and [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) groups are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to those of the circle:

$$\begin{aligned} \pi_1\left(S^1 \times D^2\right) &\cong \pi_1\left(S^1\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}, \\ H_k\left(S^1 \times D^2\right) &\cong H_k\left(S^1\right) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & \text{if } k = 0, 1, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \end{aligned}$$
