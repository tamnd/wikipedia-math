# Suspension (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Suspension"}

*Image: Suspension.svg.* Suspension of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220). The original space is in blue, and the collapsed end points are in green.

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **suspension** of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is intuitively obtained by stretching *X* into a [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) and then collapsing both end faces to points. One views *X* as "suspended" between these end points. The suspension of *X* is denoted by ***SX***[^o2] or **susp(*X*)**.p. 76

:::

There is a variant of the suspension for a [pointed space](wikipedia://en/1236666), which is called the **reduced suspension** and denoted by **Σ*X***. The "usual" suspension *SX* is sometimes called the **unreduced suspension**, **unbased suspension**, or **free suspension** of *X*, to distinguish it from Σ*X.*

## Free suspension {#Free_suspension}

The (free) suspension $SX$ of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ can be defined in several ways.

**\1.** $SX$ is the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) $(X \times [0,1])/(X\times \{0\})\big/ ( X\times \{1\}).$ In other words, it can be constructed as follows:

- Construct the [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) $X \times [0,1]$.
- Consider the entire set $X\times \{0\}$ as a single point ("glue" all its points together).
- Consider the entire set $X\times \{1\}$ as a single point ("glue" all its points together).

**2**. Another way to write this is:

$$SX := v_0 \cup_{p_0}(X \times [0,1])\cup_{p_1} v_1\ =\ \varinjlim_{i \in \{0,1\}} \bigl( (X \times [0,1]) \hookleftarrow (X\times \{i\}) \xrightarrow{p_i} v_i\bigr),$$

Where $v_0, v_1$ are two [points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_%28topology%29), and for each *i* in {0,1}, $p_i$ is the [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) to the point $v_i$ (a function that maps everything to $v_i$). That means, the suspension $SX$ is the result of constructing the [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) $X \times [0,1]$, and then [attaching](wikipedia://en/1240842) it by its faces, $X\times\{0\}$ and $X\times\{1\}$, to the points $v_0, v_1$ along the projections $p_i: \bigl( X\times\{i\} \bigr)\to v_i$.

**\3.** One can view $SX$ as two [cones](wikipedia://en/782162) on *X,* [glued together](wikipedia://en/1240842) at their base.

**\4.** $SX$ can also be defined as the [join](wikipedia://en/3127114) $X\star S^0,$ where $S^0$ is a [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061) with two points.[^o3]p. 76

**5**. In [Homotopy type theory](wikipedia://en/33974223), $SX$ is defined as a higher inductive type generated by

S: $SX$

N:$SX$

$Merid: \bigl( X\bigr)\to (N=S)$[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

In rough terms, *S* increases the dimension of a space by one: for example, it takes an *n*-[sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) to an (*n* + 1)-sphere for *n* ≥ 0.

Given a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) $f:X\rightarrow Y,$ there is a continuous map $Sf:SX\rightarrow SY$ defined by $Sf([x,t]):=[f(x),t],$ where square brackets denote [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260). This makes $S$ into a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) to itself.

:::

## Reduced suspension {#Reduced_suspension}

If *X* is a [pointed space](wikipedia://en/1236666) with basepoint *x*<sub>0</sub>, there is a variation of the suspension which is sometimes more useful. The **reduced suspension** or **based suspension** Σ*X* of *X* is the quotient space:

$$\Sigma X = (X\times I)/(X\times\{0\}\cup X\times\{1\}\cup \{x_0\}\times I).$$

This is the equivalent to taking *SX* and collapsing the line (*x*<sub>0</sub> × *I*hair) joining the two ends to a single point. The basepoint of the pointed space Σ*X* is taken to be the equivalence class of (*x*<sub>0</sub>, 0).

One can show that the reduced suspension of *X* is homeomorphic to the [smash product](wikipedia://en/698010) of *X* with the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) *S*<sup>1</sup>.

$$\Sigma X \cong S^1 \wedge X$$

For [well-behaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-behaved) spaces, such as [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022), the reduced suspension of *X* is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to the unbased suspension.

### Adjunction of reduced suspension and loop space functors {#Adjunction_of_reduced_suspension_and_loop_space_functors}

Σ gives rise to a functor from the [category of pointed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_pointed_spaces) to itself. An important property of this functor is that it is [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint) to the functor $\Omega$ taking a pointed space $X$ to its [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332) $\Omega X$. In other words, we have a [natural isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism)

$$\operatorname{Maps}_*\left(\Sigma X,Y\right) \cong \operatorname{Maps}_*\left(X,\Omega Y\right)$$

where $X$ and $Y$ are pointed spaces and $\operatorname{Maps}_*$ stands for continuous maps that preserve basepoints. This adjunction can be understood geometrically, as follows: $\Sigma X$ arises out of $X$ if a pointed circle is attached to every non-basepoint of $X$, and the basepoints of all these circles are identified and glued to the basepoint of $X$. Now, to specify a pointed map from $\Sigma X$ to $Y$, we need to give pointed maps from each of these pointed circles to $Y$. This is to say we need to associate to each element of $X$ a loop in $Y$ (an element of the loop space $\Omega Y$), and the trivial loop should be associated to the basepoint of $X$: this is a pointed map from $X$ to $\Omega Y$. (The continuity of all involved maps needs to be checked.)

The adjunction is thus akin to [currying](wikipedia://en/6600), taking maps on cartesian products to their curried form, and is an example of [Eckmann–Hilton duality](wikipedia://en/23191477).

This adjunction is a special case of the adjunction explained in the article on [smash products](wikipedia://en/698010).

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

### Applications {#Applications}

The reduced suspension can be used to construct a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) of [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506), to which the [Freudenthal suspension theorem](wikipedia://en/1880440) applies. In [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), the phenomena which are preserved under suspension, in a suitable sense, make up [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655).

:::
