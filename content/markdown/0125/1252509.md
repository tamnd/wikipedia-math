# John Lighton Synge

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Lighton Synge"}

**John Lighton Synge** (; 23 March 1897 – 30 March 1995)[^o5] was an Irish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), whose seven-decade career included significant periods in Ireland, Canada, and the USA. He was a prolific author and influential mentor, and is credited with the introduction of a new geometrical approach to the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001).[^o5]

:::

## Background {#Background}

Synge was born 1897 in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin), Ireland, into a prominent Church of Ireland family. He attended [St. Andrew's College, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrew's_College,_Dublin) and in 1915 entered [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) (TCD). He was [elected a Foundation Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Scholars_of_Trinity_College_Dublin) his first year, which was unusual as it was normally won by more advanced students. While an undergraduate at TCD, he spotted a non-trivial error in *[Analytical Dynamics](wikipedia://en/65496619)*, a textbook by [E. T. Whittaker](wikipedia://en/1077330), who had recently taught there, and notified Whittaker of the error.[^o4] In 1919 he was awarded a B.A. in Mathematics and Experimental Physics, and also a gold medal for outstanding merit. In 1922 he was awarded an M.A., and in 1926 a Sc.D., the latter upon submission of his published papers up to then.[^o5]

In 1918, Synge had married Elizabeth Eleanor Mabel Allen (1896–1985). She was another student at TCD, first of mathematics, then of history, but family finances forced her to leave without graduating. Their daughters Margaret (Pegeen), Cathleen and Isabel were born in 1921, 1923 and 1930 respectively. The middle girl grew up to become the distinguished Canadian mathematician [Cathleen Synge Morawetz](wikipedia://en/1254108).[^o5]

Synge's uncle [John Millington Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Millington_Synge) was a famous playwright. He is more distantly related to the 1952 Nobel prizewinner in chemistry [Richard Laurence Millington Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Laurence_Millington_Synge). He was a great-great-great-grandson of the mathematician and bishop [Hugh Hamilton](wikipedia://en/32401936).[^o5]

His older brother, [Edward Hutchinson Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Hutchinson_Synge) (1890-1957), who was known as Hutchie, also won a Foundation Scholarship in Trinity for Mathematics, though he never graduated. While Hutchie's later independent research was long overlooked, he is now recognised for his pioneering work in optics, particularly in [near field optical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-field_scanning_optical_microscope#History).[^o6][^o7]

He died on 30 March 1995 in Dublin.

## Career in mathematics and physics {#Career_in_mathematics_and_physics}

Synge was appointed to the position of lecturer at Trinity College, and then accepted a position at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1920. From 1920 until 1925, Synge was an assistant professor of mathematics at the University of Toronto. There he attended lectures by [Ludwik Silberstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwik_Silberstein) on the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001), stimulating him to contribute "A system of space-time co-ordinates", a letter in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* in 1921.[^o9][^o10]

Synge returned to Trinity College Dublin, in 1925, where he was elected to a fellowship and was appointed the University Professor of [Natural Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Philosophy)[^o11] (the old name for [physics](wikipedia://en/22939)). He was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). He was treasurer of the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy) in 1929. He went back to [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto) in 1930, where he was appointed Professor of Applied Mathematics and became Head of the Department of Applied Mathematics. In 1940, he supervised three Chinese students, [Guo Yonghuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Yonghuai), [Chien Wei-zang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chien_Wei-zang) and [Chia-Chiao Lin](wikipedia://en/10242665), who later became leading applied mathematicians in China and the United States.

He spent some of 1939 at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), and in 1941, he was a visiting professor at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). In 1943 he was appointed as Chairman of the Mathematics Department of [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University). Three years later he became Head of the Mathematics Department of the [Carnegie Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) (which later became Carnegie Mellon University) in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh), Pennsylvania, where [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash) was one of his students.[^o12] He spent a short time as a ballistic mathematician in the [US Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Air_Force) between 1944 and 1945.

He returned to Ireland in 1948, accepting the position of Senior Professor in the School of Theoretical Physics at the [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies). There, his colleagues included [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger) (a pioneer of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202)) and [Cornelius Lanczos](wikipedia://en/1657748) (an applied mathematician and physicist), both Senior Professors.[^o13]

## His contributions {#His_contributions}

Synge made outstanding contributions to different fields of work including [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), general mechanics and [geometrical optics](wikipedia://en/731780), [gas dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_dynamics), [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics), [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29), [electrical networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_networks), mathematical methods, [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and [Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001). He studied an extensive range of mathematical physics problems, but his best known work revolved around using geometrical methods in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024).

He was one of the first physicists to seriously study the interior of a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650), and his early work[^o15] was cited by both [Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_David_Kruskal) and [Szekeres](wikipedia://en/2563194) in their independent discoveries[^o16][^o17] of the true (so-called maximal) structure of the [Schwarzschild black hole](wikipedia://en/264606). Synge's later derivation of the Szekeres-Kruskal metric solution,[^o18] which was motivated by a desire to avoid "using 'bad' \[Schwarzschild\] coordinates to obtain 'good' \[Szekeres-Kruskal\] coordinates," has been generally under-appreciated in the literature, but was adopted by Chandrasekhar in his black hole monograph.[^o19]

In pure mathematics, he is perhaps best known for [Synge's theorem](wikipedia://en/23524235), which concerns the topology of closed orientable [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) of positive [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618). When such a space is even-dimensional and [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable_manifold), the theorem says it must be [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected). In odd dimensions, it instead says that such a space is necessarily orientable.

He also created the game of [Vish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vish_%28game%29) in which players compete to find circularity ([vicious circles](wikipedia://en/43582)) in dictionary [definitions](wikipedia://en/7964#Limitations_of_definition).[^o20]

### Fields Medal {#Fields_Medal}

While at Toronto, he was a colleague of [John Charles Fields](wikipedia://en/805652) and acted as secretary to the 1924 [International Mathematical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Congress), which was hosted by Fields.[^o22] Fields had been planning the creation of an award for mathematicians, however, when he became ill in 1932, Synge represented Fields at the 1932 International Mathematical Congress, where the medal was approved. After Fields's death, Synge completed arrangements with the sculptor of the medal, [R. Tait McKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Tait_McKenzie) and oversaw the disbursement of Fields's estate. The award is now known as the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859).

## Honours {#Honours}

Synge received many honours for his works. He was elected as a fellow of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) in 1943. He was elected as a fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada), and in 1943 was the first recipient of the society's [Henry Marshall Tory Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Marshall_Tory_Medal), as one of the first mathematicians working in Canada to be internationally recognised for his research in mathematics. In 1954 he was elected an honorary fellow of [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin).[^o24] He was president of the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy) from 1961 until 1964. The Royal Society of Canada established the [John L. Synge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Synge_Award) in his honour in 1986.

John Lighton Synge retired in 1972. During his time at the [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies), about 12% of all workers in the [relativity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativity_theory) studied there. Professor [Hermann Bondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Bondi), who gave the first J. L. Synge Public Lecture in 1992, had this to say: "Every one of the other 88% has been deeply influenced by his geometric vision and the clarity of his expression". He was awarded the [Boyle Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyle_Medal) by the [Royal Dublin Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dublin_Society) in 1972.[^o25]

During his long scientific career, Synge published over 200 papers and 11 books. He proved the result now known as [Synge's theorem](wikipedia://en/23524235).
