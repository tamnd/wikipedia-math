# Joachim Lambek

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Joachim Lambek"}

**Joachim "Jim" Lambek** (5 December 1922 – 23 June 2014) was a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was Peter Redpath [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) [Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mathematics_and_Statistics,_McGill_University) at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), where he earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) degree in 1950 with [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825) as advisor.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Lambek was born in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), where he attended a [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29).[^o5] He came to England in 1938 as a [refugee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee) on the *[Kindertransport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindertransport)*.[^o6] From there he was interned as an [enemy alien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemy_alien) and deported to a [prison work camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_War_II_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_Canada) in [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada). There, he began in his spare time a mathematical apprenticeship with Fritz Rothberger, also interned, and wrote the [McGill Junior Matriculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation_examination) in fall of 1941. In the spring of 1942, he was released and settled in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal), where he entered studies at McGill University, graduating with an [honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honours_degree) mathematics degree in 1945 and an [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSc) a year later.[^o7] In 1950, he completed his doctorate under [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825) becoming McGill's first [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics.

Lambek became assistant professor at McGill; he was made a full professor in 1963. He spent his sabbatical year 1965–66 in at the [Institute for Mathematical Research](wikipedia://en/33084376) at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), where [Beno Eckmann](wikipedia://en/19613365) had gathered together a group of researchers interested in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), including [Bill Lawvere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Lawvere). There Lambek reoriented his research into category theory.[^o8]

Lambek retired in 1992 but continued his involvement at [McGill's mathematics department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mathematics_and_Statistics,_McGill_University). In 2000 a [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) celebrating Lambek's contributions to mathematical structures in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) was published.[^o9] On the occasion of Lambek's 90th birthday, a collection *Categories and Types in Logic, Language, and Physics* was produced in tribute to him.[^o10]

## Scholarly work {#Scholarly_work}

Lambek's wrote *two* short PhD theses on separate topics:[^o12] one studying [quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions) in the foundations of mathematical physics (specifically the [biquaternion](wikipedia://en/1207070) algebra containing [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space)), and another on the problem of embedding a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) into a [group](wikipedia://en/19447). The second component, attacking a question that had been [previously addressed](wikipedia://en/22772051#Embeddability_in_groups) by [Anatoly Maltsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Maltsev) but using a novel geometric approach, was published by the [Canadian Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Journal_of_Mathematics).[^o13] He later returned to [biquaternions](wikipedia://en/1207070) when in 1995 he contributed "If Hamilton had prevailed: Quaternions in Physics", which exhibited the [Riemann–Silberstein](wikipedia://en/36821570) [bivector](wikipedia://en/38379690) to express the free-space electromagnetic equations.

Lambek supervised 17 doctoral students, and has 99 doctoral descendants as of 2025. He has over 100 publications listed in the [Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646), including 6 books. His earlier work was mostly in [module theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_theory), especially torsion theories, non-commutative localization, and [injective modules](wikipedia://en/473775). One of his earliest papers, (Lambek Moser 1954), proved the [Lambek–Moser theorem](wikipedia://en/10620457) about integer sequences. In 1963 he published an important result, now known as Lambek's theorem, on [character modules](wikipedia://en/61665635) characterizing flatness of a module.[^o16] His more recent work is in [pregroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregroup) and [formal languages](wikipedia://en/10939); his earliest works in this field were probably (Lambek 1958) and (Lambek 1979). He is noted, among other things, for the [Lambek calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambek_calculus), an influential effort to capture mathematical aspects of natural language syntax in logical form by reducing [grammaticality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammaticality) to a test of logical entailment in a certain [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329), as well as for developing the connections between [typed lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/1986011) and [cartesian closed categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_closed_categories) (see [Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry–Howard–Lambek_correspondence)). His last works were on [pregroup grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregroup_grammar).
