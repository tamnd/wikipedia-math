# Scott core theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scott core theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Scott core theorem** is a theorem about the [finite presentability](wikipedia://en/31060586#Finite_presentability) of [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) due to [G. Peter Scott](wikipedia://en/58270684), (Scott 1973). The precise statement is as follows:

:::

Given a 3-manifold (not necessarily [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_manifold)) with [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) fundamental group, there is a compact three-dimensional [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095), called the **compact core** or **Scott core**, such that its [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) induces an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) on fundamental groups. In particular, this means a finitely generated 3-manifold group is [finitely presentable](wikipedia://en/99494).

A simplified proof is given in (Rubinstein Swarup 1990), and a stronger uniqueness statement is proven in (Harris Scott 1996).
