# Hartle–Hawking proposal

*Image: Big Bang Singularity and Hartle-Hawking State.jpg.* Comparison of the [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116) concept (left) and a Hartle–Hawking state concept (right) as they are extrapolated to time zero. Diagram shows two space dimensions horizontally and one time dimension vertically.[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hartle–Hawking proposal"}

The **Hartle–Hawking state**, also known as the **no-boundary wave function**, is a [cosmological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological) model that applies [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) to the [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116).[^o10] It is named after [James Hartle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hartle) and [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148), who first proposed it in 1983.[^o3][^o4] The concept can also be considered as an initial condition for models of [quantum cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_cosmology).[^o5]

:::

## Ingredients {#Ingredients}

The Hartle-Hawking proposal includes several ingredients. First it uses [Richard Feynman](wikipedia://en/25523)'s [path integral formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation) of quantum mechanics. In this approach every possible path a particle can take through spacetime contributes to the solution with its own amplitude and phase. Technical challenges with those sums lead to the second ingredient, a transformation to [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697) space-time: a geometry which combines 3 space dimensions with an imaginary time dimension.[^o8] This is related to the [Wick rotation](wikipedia://en/293629), $\tau = it$, and it converts the spacetime metric in to a Euclidean metric, $ds^2 = d\tau^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$. In Hawking's approach, this rotation is applied to every path, not to the background space of the paths as in Wick's approach, and therefore the sum of histories becomes a quantum superposition of spacetimes.[^o10] This curved Euclidean spacetime can be analogous to a sphere in being both finite in extent and yet have no boundary.[^o8]

## History {#History}

The original 1983 paper by Hartle and Hawking grew out of a summer visit by Hawking to [UC Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Santa_Barbara) where Hartle worked. Hawking was exploring the idea that the boundary condition for space time was simply no-boundary at all. With Hartle this idea was converted in to a proposal and published.[^o8] In 1998 Hawking worked with Neil Turok to expand the Hartle-Hawking concept to include a [hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe) or open geometry.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o1]
