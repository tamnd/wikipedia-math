# Calculus of structures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Calculus of structures"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), the **calculus of structures** (CoS) is a [proof calculus](wikipedia://en/1250665) with [deep inference](wikipedia://en/1250664) for studying the [structural proof theory](wikipedia://en/929998) of [noncommutative logic](wikipedia://en/693282). The calculus has since been applied to study [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675), [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868), [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365), and [process calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_calculi), and many benefits are claimed to follow in these investigations from the way in which deep inference is made available in the calculus.

:::

It was first introduced in 2001 in the paper *A System of Interaction and Structure* by Alessio Guglielmo of the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath).[^o2][^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A [**formula**](wikipedia://en/404582) is a ([well-formed](wikipedia://en/404582)) string of logical symbols. For example, $((a \land b ) \land \neg c) \lor d$ is a formula.

An **equational theory** is a list of equations that describe an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on the set of all formulas. The usual equations are associativity, commutativity, and equations for logical constants.

A **structure** is an equivalence class of formulas. The name "structure" emphasizes that CoS does not distinguish sequents and formulas, but uses a single object to do the job of both in a sequent calculus. Specifically, a structure can be regarded as an equivalence class of formulas.

A **context** is a structure with a sub-structure removed. For example, $A \land -$ is a context, where the $-$ denotes a removed sub-structure. Contexts are written as $S\{-\}$, so for example, if $S\{-\} := A \land -$, then $S\{B\}$ is defined as $A \land B$.

An **inference rule** is of form $\frac{S\{A\}}{S\{B\}}$, where $A, B$ are substructures, and $S\{\}$ is not any particular formula, but rather an indication that "any context can go here". We can present it in a simplified form as $\frac{A}{B}$, leaving $S\{\}$ implicit. By default, contexts appearing in inference rules must have positive polarity.

A context has a **polarity**. The polarity of a context is either **positive** or **negative**. For example, $A \lor -$ is a positive context, but $A \lor \neg -$ is a negative context, but $(A \lor \neg -) \to C$ is once again a positive context. The positivity or negativity of a context is called its **polarity**. For example, we say "$A \lor -$ has positive polarity", and "$A \lor \neg -$ has negative polarity".

Two structures may be **duals** to each other. Similarly, two inference rules $\frac{S\{A\}}{S\{B\}}, \; \frac{S\{B'\}}{S\{A'\}}$ can also be **duals** to each other, if it is possible to write $A'$ as a dual to $A$, and $B'$ as a dual to $B$. The classical [contraposition](wikipedia://en/8556497) is an example of this duality.

A convention is to write $()$ for a conjunction, and $[]$ for a disjunction. For example, in linear logic, one writes $(A_1, \dots, A_n)$ for $A_1 \otimes \dots \otimes A_n$, and $[A_1, \dots, A_n]$ for $A_1 \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} \dots \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A_n$.

:::

## Ideas {#Ideas}

### Deep inference {#Deep_inference}

In [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329), each inference rule can only produce/eliminate logical connectives on the outermost level of a formula. In particular, this means that most subformulas remain unchanged. In deep inference, each inference rule can rewrite subformulas on any level.

For example, in the sequent calculus for classical logic, the rule

$$\frac{\Gamma, A , B\vdash \Delta}{\Gamma, A \land B\vdash \Delta}$$

leaves $A, B$ and all their subformulas unchanged. Only the outermost [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) of $A \land B$ is produced.

For deep inference, inference rules may apply to any subformula, arbitrarily deep within the syntax tree. In other words, out of all the nodes in the [syntax tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_syntax_tree) of $A \land B$, an inference rule can only manipulate the outermost node. Deep inference allows a rule to manipulate arbitrary node within the syntax tree.

### Top-down symmetry {#Top-down_symmetry}

In sequent calculus and [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072), a proof is a tree of inference rules. This then produces a fundamental asymmetry: The top of a proof tree are made of many leaf-sequents, but the bottom of a proof tree is a single endsequent. However, many inference rules are symmetric: The top half and the bottom half are mutually derivable.

For example, if one can apply a rule $\frac{\Gamma \vdash A \quad \Gamma \vdash B}{\Gamma \vdash A\& B}(\vdash \&)$ to produce a proof of $\Gamma \vdash A \& B$, then one can also produce a proof of $\Gamma \vdash A$, and a proof of $\Gamma \vdash B$. In this way, the inference rule $\vdash \&$ has a top-down symmetry.

The formalism of sequent calculus makes this top-down symmetry implicit, since the juxtaposition of a sequent $\Gamma \vdash A$ and another sequent $\Gamma \vdash B$ is not itself a sequent. This means this top-down symmetry is not on the [object-level of the proof calculus](wikipedia://en/402929).

In the calculus of sequents, a proof is a line of inference rules. This places the top-down symmetry in the object level.

## SKSg {#SKSg}

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

SKSg is a CoS for classical propositional logic.

The symbols of SKSg consists of:

- The atoms $a_0, \bar a_0, a_1, \bar a_1, \dots$. We say that $a_i, \bar a_i$ are atoms that are dual to each other.
- The connectives $\lor, \land$.
- The units $\top, \bot$.

Negation does not exist in SKSg, since we have demoted negation from a logical connective to merely a pairing between logical atoms.

A structure of SKSg has the following syntax in [Backus–Naur form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backus–Naur_form):

$$F ::= a | \bar a | \top | \bot | F \lor F | F \land F$$

Without negation, all contexts are positive.

Duality for structures is defined by:

$$\begin{aligned} \overline{a} = \bar a, &\quad \overline{\bar a} = a \\ \overline{A \land B} = \overline{A} \lor \overline{B}, & \quad \overline{A \lor B} = \overline{A} \land \overline{B} \end{aligned}$$

The *structural* inference rules come in 3 dual pairs:

::: {#o12 .table cue=table}

| $\frac{\top}{a\lor\bar{a}}\;(\mathrm{i}\downarrow)$ | $\frac{\bot}{a}\;(\mathrm{w}\downarrow)$ | $\frac{a\lor a}{a}\;(\mathrm{c}\downarrow)$ |
|---|---|---|
| *identity* | *weakening* | *contraction* |
| $\frac{a\land\bar{a}}{\bot}\;(\mathrm{i}\uparrow)$ | $\frac{a}{\top}\;(\mathrm{w}\uparrow)$ | $\frac{a}{a\land a}\;(\mathrm{c}\uparrow)$ |
| *cut* | *coweakening* | *cocontraction* |

:::

The 2 *logical* inference rules are self-dual:

::: {#o13 .table cue=table}

| $\frac{A\land(B\lor C)}{(A\land B)\lor C}\;(\mathrm{s})$ | $\frac{(A\land B)\lor(C\land D)}{(A\lor C)\land(B\lor D)}\;(\mathrm{m})$ |
|---|---|
| *switch* | *medial* |

:::

In addition to these rules, there are the following equations:

$$\begin{array}{rcl} A\lor B&=&B\lor A\\ A\land B&=&B\land A\\ (A\lor B)\lor C&=&A\lor(B\lor C)\\ (A\land B)\land C&=&A\land(B\land C) \end{array} \qquad \begin{array}{rcl} A\lor\bot&=&A\\ A\land\top&=&A\\ \top\lor\top&=&\top\\ \bot\land\bot&=&\bot \end{array} \quad$$

All equations of the SKSg system can be replaced by inference rules. The resulting equation-less system is SKS.

:::

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

This is a valid derivation:

$$\begin{array}{c} (a\lor b)\land a\\ \Vert\\ ((a\lor b)\land a)\land((a\lor b)\land a) \end{array} \equiv \left( \frac{ \left(\frac{a}{a\land a}\;(\mathrm{c}\uparrow)\right) \lor \left(\frac{b}{b\land b}\;(\mathrm{c}\uparrow)\right) }{ (a\lor b)\land(a\lor b) }\;(\mathrm{m}) \right) \land \left(\frac{a}{a\land a}\;(\mathrm{c}\uparrow)\right) \quad.$$

This is a general principle in deep inference: [structural rules](wikipedia://en/697666) on generic formulas can be replaced by the same structural rule on atoms. In this case, cocontraction.

A *cut-free* derivation is a derivation where $\mathrm{i}\uparrow$ is not used. One can [eliminate cuts](wikipedia://en/910234) by a technique called **splitting**.[^o16][^o17]

:::

## MLL⁻ {#MLL⁻}

Define MLL⁻ to be the proof system of **multiplicative [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675) without units**.

A formula consists of $F ::= a | \bar a | F \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} F | F \times F$. Here, $a$ and $\bar{a}$ are dual atoms. The equations of duality are

$$(A \otimes B)^\bot = A^\bot \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B^\bot, \quad (A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B)^\bot = A^\bot \otimes B^\bot$$

In particular, negation does not exist anymore, since we have demoted negation from a logical connective to merely a duality between pairs of logical atoms. By definition, $\bar{\bar a} = a$.

The system has the following CoS:[^o17]

$$\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{i}\downarrow\; \frac{}{A^\perp \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A} && \mathrm{i}\uparrow\; \frac{A \otimes A^\perp}{} \\ &\mathrm{i}\downarrow\; \frac{S\{B\}}{S\{(A^\perp \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A) \otimes B\}} && \mathrm{i}\uparrow\; \frac{S\{B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} (A \otimes A^\perp)\}}{S\{B\}} \\ &\sigma\downarrow\; \frac{S\{A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B\}}{S\{B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A\}} && \sigma\uparrow\; \frac{S\{A \otimes B\}}{S\{B \otimes A\}} \\ &\alpha\downarrow\; \frac{S\{A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} (B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C)\}} {S\{(A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B) \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C\}} && \alpha\uparrow\; \frac{S\{A \otimes (B \otimes C)\}} {S\{(A \otimes B) \otimes C\}} \\ &\mathrm{s}\; \frac{S\{A \otimes (B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C)\}} {S\{(A \otimes B) \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C\}} \end{aligned}$$

Each row is a pair of dual rules. The switch rule is dual to itself.

There are 4 initiation rules, two for i↑, and two for i↓. The reason there are two instead of one is that the system has no units $1, \bot$. With the unit $1$ for $\otimes$, one can simply subsume $\frac{}{A^\perp \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A}$ as a special case of $\frac{S\{B\}}{S\{(A^\perp \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A) \otimes B\}}$, where $S\{-\}$ is the empty context, and $B = 1$. Similarly, with the unit $\bot$ for $\mathbin{\mbox{⅋}}$, one can subsume $\frac{A \otimes A^\bot}{}$ under $\frac{S\{B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} (A \otimes A^\perp)\}}{S\{B\}}$.

The rules for association $\alpha$ and commutation $\sigma$ mean that both connectives are associative and commutative. These rules can be replaced with the equations $(A \otimes B) \otimes C = A \otimes (B \otimes C)$, etc.

i↑ corresponds to the identity axiom in sequent calculus: $A \vdash A$, or equivalently, $\vdash A^\bot, A$.

i↓ corresponds to the cut rule: $\frac{\Gamma \vdash \Delta, A \quad A, \Gamma' \vdash \Delta'}{\Gamma, \Gamma' \vdash \Delta, \Delta'}$.

The switch rule $\frac{S\{A \otimes (B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C)\}} {S\{(A \otimes B) \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C\}}$ is more subtle. It corresponds to $S\{A \otimes (B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C)\} \vdash S\{(A \otimes B) \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C\}$. In general, an inference rule of a CoS can be read as a provable sequent in a sequent calculus, by "rotating it 90 degrees".

### Interpretation {#Interpretation}

In MLL⁻, the symbols $\otimes, \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}}$ are logical connectives (conjunction, disjunction), and can only appear on the level of formulas. On the level of sequents, the comma behaves essentially the same as $\mathbin{\mbox{⅋}}$, since we have the following inference rule

$$\frac{\vdash \Gamma, A, B, \Delta}{\vdash \Gamma, A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B, \Delta}$$

but it appears on the level of sequents. Similarly, writing two sequents side-by-side within a proof tree has essentially the same behavior as $\otimes$, since we have the following inference rule

$$\frac{\vdash \Gamma, A \quad \vdash B, \Delta}{\vdash \Gamma, A \otimes B, \Delta}$$

but it appears on the level of proofs.

In the CoS for MLL⁻, the symbol $\otimes$ are manipulated according to rules such that it can do the job of both the logical connective $\otimes$ and the side-by-side placement of sequents. Similarly for $\mathbin{\mbox{⅋}}$.

In particular, given a proof tree in MLL⁻ sequent calculus, one can convert it to a proof in MLL⁻ CoS if one convert each sequent $\vdash A_1, \dots, A_n$ into $A_1\mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} \dots \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A_n$, then convert each side-by-side placement of sequents $\vdash \Gamma_1 \quad \dots \quad \vdash \Gamma_n$ with $\Gamma_1 \otimes \dots \otimes \Gamma_n$. Then replace each use of inference rule in the sequent calculus with a use of several inference rules in the CoS. This shows that structures are not a mere replication of formulas or sequents, since it has features of both.

Cut elimination corresponds to i↓-elimination.

## BV {#BV}

The BV system (Basic System V) can be produced by this CoS:[^o2]

$$\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{ai}\downarrow\; \frac{\circ}{a \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} \bar a} && \mathrm{ai}\uparrow\; \frac{a \otimes \bar a}{\circ} \\ &\mathrm s\; \frac{(A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B) \otimes C}{(A \otimes C) \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B} \\ &\mathrm q\downarrow\; \frac{(A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B) \triangleleft (C \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} D)} {(A \triangleleft C) \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} (B \triangleleft D)} && \mathrm q\uparrow\; \frac{(A \triangleleft B) \otimes (C \triangleleft D)} {(A \otimes C) \triangleleft (B \otimes D)} \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} A \otimes B &= B \otimes A &\qquad (A \otimes B) \otimes C &= A \otimes (B \otimes C) &\qquad A \otimes \circ &= A \\ A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B &= B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} A & (A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} B) \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C &= A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} (B \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} C) & A \mathbin{\mbox{⅋}} \circ &= A \\ && (A \triangleleft B) \triangleleft C &= A \triangleleft (B \triangleleft C) & A \triangleleft \circ &= A = \circ \triangleleft A \end{aligned}$$
