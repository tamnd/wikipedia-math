# Dialectica interpretation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dialectica interpretation"}

In [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), the **Dialectica interpretation**[^o2] is a proof interpretation of intuitionistic logic ([Heyting arithmetic](wikipedia://en/2545815)) into a finite type extension of [primitive recursive arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6637022), the so-called **System T**. It was developed by [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) to provide a [consistency proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_proof) of arithmetic. The name of the interpretation comes from the journal *[Dialectica](wikipedia://en/26204033)*, where Gödel's paper was published in a 1958 special issue dedicated to [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580) on his 70th birthday.

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

Via the [Gödel–Gentzen negative translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel–Gentzen_negative_translation), the consistency of classical [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) had already been reduced to the consistency of intuitionistic [Heyting arithmetic](wikipedia://en/2545815). Gödel's motivation for developing the dialectica interpretation was to obtain a relative [consistency](wikipedia://en/75802) proof for Heyting arithmetic (and hence for Peano arithmetic).

## Intuitionistic logic {#Intuitionistic_logic}

The interpretation has two components: a formula translation and a proof translation. The formula translation describes how each formula $A$ of Heyting arithmetic is mapped to a quantifier-free formula $A_D(x; y)$ of the system T, where $x$ and $y$ are tuples of fresh variables (not appearing free in $A$). Intuitively, $A$ is interpreted as $\exists x \forall y A_D(x; y)$. The proof translation shows how a proof of $A$ has enough information to witness the interpretation of $A$, i.e. the proof of $A$ can be converted into a closed term $t$ and a proof of $A_D(t; y)$ in the system T.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula translation"}

### Formula translation {#Formula_translation}

The quantifier-free formula $A_D(x; y)$ is defined inductively on the logical structure of $A$ as follows, where $P$ is an atomic formula:

$$\begin{array}{lcl} (P)_D & \equiv & P \\ (A \wedge B)_D(x, v; y, w) & \equiv & A_D(x; y) \wedge B_D(v; w) \\ (A \vee B)_D(x, v, z; y, w) & \equiv & (z = 0 \rightarrow A_D(x; y)) \wedge (z \neq 0 \to B_D(v; w)) \\ (A \rightarrow B)_D(f, g; x, w) & \equiv & A_D(x; f x w) \rightarrow B_D(g x; w) \\ (\exists z A)_D(x, z; y) & \equiv & A_D(x; y) \\ (\forall z A)_D(f; y, z) & \equiv & A_D(f z; y) \end{array}$$

:::

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof translation (soundness)"}

### Proof translation (soundness) {#Proof_translation_(soundness)}

The formula interpretation is such that whenever $A$ is provable in Heyting arithmetic then there exists a sequence of closed terms $t$ such that $A_D(t; y)$ is provable in the system T. The sequence of terms $t$ and the proof of $A_D(t; y)$ are constructed from the given proof of $A$ in Heyting arithmetic. The construction of $t$ is quite straightforward, except for the contraction axiom $A \rightarrow A \wedge A$ which requires the assumption that quantifier-free formulas are decidable.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement cue=named-heading name="Characterisation principles"}

### Characterisation principles {#Characterisation_principles}

It has also been shown that Heyting arithmetic extended with the following principles

- [Axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840)
- [Markov's principle](wikipedia://en/14920509)
- [Independence of premise](wikipedia://en/22833480) for universal formulas

is necessary and sufficient for characterising the formulas of HA which are interpretable by the Dialectica interpretation. The choice axiom here is formulated for any 2-ary predicate in the premise and an existence claim with a variable of function type in its conclusion.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

### Extensions {#Extensions}

The basic dialectica interpretation of intuitionistic logic has been extended to various stronger systems. Intuitively, the dialectica interpretation can be applied to a stronger system, as long as the dialectica interpretation of the extra principle can be witnessed by terms in the system T (or an extension of system T).

#### Induction {#Induction}

Given [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem) (which implies that the consistency of PA cannot be proven by [finitistic](wikipedia://en/152900) means) it is reasonable to expect that system T must contain non-finitistic constructions. Indeed, this is the case. The non-finitistic constructions show up in the interpretation of [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881). To give a Dialectica interpretation of induction, Gödel makes use of what is nowadays called Gödel's [primitive recursive functionals](wikipedia://en/22833082), which are [higher-order functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-order_function) with primitive recursive descriptions.

#### Classical logic {#Classical_logic}

Formulas and proofs in classical arithmetic can also be given a Dialectica interpretation via an initial embedding into Heyting arithmetic followed by the Dialectica interpretation of Heyting arithmetic. [Shoenfield](wikipedia://en/48884267), in his book, combines the negative translation and the Dialectica interpretation into a single interpretation of classical arithmetic.

#### Comprehension {#Comprehension}

In 1962 Spector[^o16] extended Gödel's Dialectica interpretation of arithmetic to full mathematical analysis, by showing how the schema of countable choice can be given a Dialectica interpretation by extending system T with [bar recursion](wikipedia://en/29726755).

:::

## Linear logic {#Linear_logic}

The Dialectica interpretation has been used to build a model of [Girard](wikipedia://en/579673)'s refinement of [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) known as [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675), via the so-called [Dialectica spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialectica_spaces).[^o18] Since linear logic is a refinement of intuitionistic logic, the dialectica interpretation of linear logic can also be viewed as a refinement of the dialectica interpretation of intuitionistic logic.

Although the linear interpretation in Shirahata's work[^o19] validates the weakening rule (it is actually an interpretation of [affine logic](wikipedia://en/693233)), de Paiva's dialectica spaces interpretation does not validate weakening for arbitrary formulas.

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variants"}

## Variants {#Variants}

Several variants of the Dialectica interpretation have been proposed since, most notably the Diller-Nahm variant (to avoid the contraction problem) and Kohlenbach's monotone and Ferreira-Oliva bounded interpretations (to interpret [weak Kőnig's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_Kőnig's_lemma)). Comprehensive treatments of the interpretation can be found at,[^o22][^o23] and.[^o24]
