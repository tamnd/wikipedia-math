# Schwartz topological vector space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schwartz topological vector space"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Schwartz spaces** are [topological vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_vector_spaces) (TVS) whose neighborhoods of the origin have a property similar to the definition of [totally bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_bounded) subsets. These spaces were introduced by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space) $X$ with continuous dual $X^{\prime}$, $X$ is called a **Schwartz space** if it satisfies any of the following equivalent conditions:[^n1]

1. For every [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [convex balanced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_convex) neighborhood $U$ of the origin in $X$, there exists a neighborhood $V$ of $0$ in $X$ such that for all real $r > 0$, $V$ can be covered by finitely many translates of $rU$.
1. Every bounded subset of $X$ is [totally bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_bounded) and for every [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238) neighborhood $U$ of the origin in $X$, there exists a neighborhood $V$ of $0$ in $X$ such that for all real $r > 0$, there exists a bounded subset $B$ of $X$ such that $V \subseteq B + rU$.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every [quasi-complete](wikipedia://en/63948433) Schwartz space is a [semi-Montel space](wikipedia://en/1796319). Every [Fréchet](wikipedia://en/233801) Schwartz space is a [Montel space](wikipedia://en/1796319).[^n2]

The [strong dual space](wikipedia://en/63951372) of a [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856) Schwartz space is an [ultrabornological space](wikipedia://en/63904912).

:::

## Examples and sufficient conditions {#Examples_and_sufficient_conditions}

- Vector subspace of Schwartz spaces are Schwartz spaces.
- The quotient of a Schwartz space by a closed vector subspace is again a Schwartz space.
- The [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of any family of Schwartz spaces is again a Schwartz space.
- The weak topology induced on a vector space by a family of linear maps valued in Schwartz spaces is a Schwartz space ***if*** the weak topology is Hausdorff.
- The locally convex strict inductive limit of any countable sequence of Schwartz spaces (with each space TVS-embedded in the next space) is again a Schwartz space.

### Counter-examples {#Counter-examples}

Every infinite-dimensional [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) is *not* a Schwartz space.[^n3]

There exist [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801) that are not Schwartz spaces and there exist Schwartz spaces that are not [Montel spaces](wikipedia://en/1796319).[^n3]
