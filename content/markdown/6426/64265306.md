# József Balogh (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="József Balogh"}

**József Balogh** is a Hungarian-American mathematician, specializing in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Balogh grew up in [Mórahalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mórahalom) and attended secondary school in [Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeged) at Ságvári Endre Gyakorló Gimnázium (a special school for mathematics).[^o4] As a student, he won two silver medals (in 1989 and 1990) at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208). He studied at the University of Szeged (with one year TEMPUS grant at the University of Ghent), where he received his M.S, in mathematics in 1995 with advisor Péter Hajnal and thesis *On the existence of MDS-cyclic codes*. In 2001 Balogh received his doctorate from the [University of Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Memphis) with advisor [Béla Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956) and thesis *Graph properties and Bootstrap percolation*. As a postdoc Balogh was at AT&T Shannon Laboratories in [Florham Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florham_Park,_New_Jersey) and for several months in 2002 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). From 2002 to 2005 he was Zassenhaus Assistant Professor at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University). At the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign) he was an assistant professor from 2005 to 2010 and an associate professor from 2010 to 2013 and is since 2013 a full professor. From 2009 to 2011 he was also an associate professor at [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego).[^o5]

Balogh's research deals with extremal and probabilistic combinatorics (especially graph theory) and bootstrap [percolation](wikipedia://en/187805).[^o4] The latter models the spread of an infection on a d-dimensional grid, whereby nodes are infected in each time step in which at least r neighbors have already been infected. It is based on a randomly chosen starting structure and Bollobás, Balogh, [Hugo Duminil-Copin](wikipedia://en/57492975) and [Robert Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_%28mathematician%29) proved an asymptotic (for large grids) formula for the threshold probability that the whole grid is infected, depending on d and r. He had previously treated the three-dimensional case with r = 3 with Bollobás and Morris.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2007, he received an NSF Career Grant.[^o5] In 2013/14 and 2020 he was a Simons Fellow, in 2013/14 Marie Curie Fellow. In 2016 he received the [George Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya_Prize) in combinatorics with Robert Morris and [Wojciech Samotij](wikipedia://en/47871930).[^o7] In 2018 Balogh was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro).

He was named to the 2023 class of Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), "for contributions to extremal combinatorics, probability and additive number theory, and for graduate mentoring".[^o8] In 2024 he was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research jointly with [Robert Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_%28mathematician%29) and [Wojciech Samotij](wikipedia://en/47871930).[^o9]
