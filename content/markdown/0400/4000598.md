# Polycyclic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polycyclic group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **polycyclic group** is a [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012) that satisfies the maximal condition on [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) (that is, every subgroup is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042)). Polycyclic groups are [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group), which makes them interesting from a computational point of view.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

Equivalently, a group *G* is polycyclic if and only if it admits a [subnormal series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnormal_series) with cyclic factors, that is a finite set of subgroups, let's say *G*<sub>0</sub>, ..., *G*<sub>*n*</sub> such that

- *G*<sub>*n*</sub> coincides with *G*
- *G*<sub>0</sub> is the trivial subgroup
- *G*<sub>*i*</sub> is a normal subgroup of *G*<sub>*i*+1</sub> (for every *i* between 0 and *n* - 1)
- and the quotient group *G*<sub>*i*+1</sub> / *G*<sub>*i*</sub> is a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) (for every *i* between 0 and *n* - 1)

A [metacyclic group](wikipedia://en/7515561) is a polycyclic group with *n* ≤ 2, or in other words an [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of a cyclic group by a cyclic group.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of polycyclic groups include finitely generated abelian groups, finitely generated [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569) groups, and finite solvable groups. [Anatoly Maltsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Maltsev) proved that solvable subgroups of the integer [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) are polycyclic; and later [Louis Auslander](wikipedia://en/22378651) (1967) and Swan proved the converse, that any polycyclic group is up to isomorphism a group of integer matrices.[^o6] The [holomorph](wikipedia://en/2841008) of a polycyclic group is also such a group of integer matrices.

:::

## Strongly polycyclic groups {#Strongly_polycyclic_groups}

A polycyclic group *G* is said to be **strongly polycyclic** if each quotient *G*<sub>*i*+1</sub> / *G*<sub>*i*</sub> is infinite. Any subgroup of a strongly polycyclic group is strongly polycyclic.

## Polycyclic-by-finite groups {#Polycyclic-by-finite_groups}

A **virtually polycyclic group** is a group that has a polycyclic subgroup of finite [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_%28group_theory%29), an example of a [virtual](wikipedia://en/10377971) property. Such a group necessarily has a *normal* polycyclic subgroup of finite index, and therefore such groups are also called **polycyclic-by-finite groups**. Although polycyclic-by-finite groups need not be solvable, they still have many of the finiteness properties of polycyclic groups; for example, they satisfy the maximal condition, and they are finitely presented and [residually finite](wikipedia://en/5780875).

In the textbook (Scott 1964, Ch 7.1) and some papers, an **M-group** refers to what is now called a polycyclic-[by](wikipedia://en/345807)-[finite group](wikipedia://en/323707), which by Hirsch's theorem can also be expressed as a group which has a finite length subnormal series with each factor a finite group or an infinite [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327).

These groups are particularly interesting because they are the only known examples of [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [group rings](wikipedia://en/349223) (Ivanov 1989), or group rings of finite injective dimension.

::: {#o13 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Hirsch length"}

## Hirsch length {#Hirsch_length}

The **Hirsch length** or **Hirsch number** of a polycyclic group *G* is the number of infinite factors in its subnormal series.

If *G* is a polycyclic-by-finite group, then the Hirsch length of *G* is the Hirsch length of a polycyclic [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) *H* of *G*, where *H* has finite [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_of_a_group) in *G*. This is independent of choice of subgroup, as all such subgroups will have the same Hirsch length.

:::
