# Cheeger bound

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cheeger bound"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cheeger bound** is a bound of the second largest eigenvalue of the [transition matrix](wikipedia://en/217313) of a finite-state, discrete-time, reversible stationary [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876). It can be seen as a special case of [Cheeger inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expander_graphs#Cheeger_inequalities) in [expander graphs](wikipedia://en/9313).

:::

Let $X$ be a finite set and let $K(x,y)$ be the transition probability for a reversible Markov chain on $X$. Assume this chain has [stationary distribution](wikipedia://en/2524727) $\pi$.

Define

$$Q(x,y) = \pi(x) K(x,y)$$

and for $A,B \subset X$ define

$$Q(A \times B) = \sum_{x \in A, y \in B} Q(x,y).$$

Define the constant $\Phi$ as

$$\Phi = \min_{S \subset X, \pi(S) \leq \frac{1}{2}} \frac{Q (S \times S^c)}{\pi(S)}.$$

The operator $K,$ acting on the [space of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_of_functions) from $|X|$ to $\mathbb{R}$, defined by

$$(K \phi)(x) = \sum_y K(x,y) \phi(y)$$

has [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) $\lambda_1 \geq \lambda_2 \geq \cdots \geq \lambda_n$. It is known that $\lambda_1 = 1$. The Cheeger bound is a bound on the second largest eigenvalue $\lambda_2$.

::: {#o2 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Cheeger bound)"}

**Theorem (Cheeger bound):**

$$1 - 2 \Phi \leq \lambda_2 \leq 1 - \frac{\Phi^2}{2}.$$

:::
