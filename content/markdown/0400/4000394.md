# Grigore Moisil

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Grigore Moisil"}

**Grigore Constantin Moisil** (; 10 January 1906 – 21 May 1973) was a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) pioneer, and [titular member of the Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy). His research was mainly in the fields of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) ([Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebra](wikipedia://en/43613625)), [algebraic logic](wikipedia://en/7870034), [MV-algebra](wikipedia://en/2483815), and [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations). He is viewed as the father of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) in Romania.

:::

Moisil was also a member of the [Academy of Sciences of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_Bologna) and of the International Institute of Philosophy. In 1996, the [IEEE Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Computer_Society) awarded him posthumously the *Computer Pioneer* Award.

## Biography {#Biography}

Grigore Moisil was born in 1906 in Tulcea into an [intellectual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual) family. His great-grandfather, Grigore Moisil (1814–1891), a [clergyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergyman), was one of the founders of the [first Romanian high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Coșbuc_National_College_%28Năsăud%29) in [Năsăud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Năsăud). His father, [Constantin Moisil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Moisil) (1876–1958), was a history professor, [archaeologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology) and [numismatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numismatics); as a member of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy), he filled the position of Director of the Numismatics Office of the Academy. His mother, Elena (1863–1949), was a teacher in Tulcea, later the director of "Maidanul Dulapului" school in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest) (now "[Ienăchiță Văcărescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ienăchiță_Văcărescu)" school).

Grigore Moisil attended [primary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_school) in Bucharest, then high school in [Vaslui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaslui) and Bucharest (at Spiru Haret High School) between 1916 and 1922. In 1924 he was admitted to the Civil Engineering School of the [Polytechnic University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Bucharest), and also the Mathematics School of the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest). He showed a stronger interest in mathematics, so he quit the Polytechnic University in 1929, despite already having passed all the third-year exams. In 1929 he defended his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) thesis, *La mécanique analytique des systemes continus* (Analytical mechanics of continuous systems), before a commission led by [Gheorghe Țițeica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Țițeica), with [Dimitrie Pompeiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Pompeiu) and [Anton Davidoglu](wikipedia://en/3421491) as members. The thesis was published the same year by the Gauthier-Villars publishing house in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), and received favourable comments from [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra), [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), and [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462).

In 1930 Moisil went to the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) for further study in mathematics, which he finalized the next year with the paper *On a class of systems of equations with partial derivatives from mathematical physics*. In 1931 he returned to Romania, where he was appointed in a teaching position at the Mathematics School of the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University). Shortly after, he left for a one-year [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) scholarship to study in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome). In 1932 he returned to [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași), where he remained for almost 10 years, developing a close relationship with professor [Alexandru Myller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Myller). He taught the first [modern algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) course in Romania, named *Logic and theory of proof*, at the University of Iași. During that time, he started writing a series of papers based on the works of [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064) in [multi-valued logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-valued_logic). His research in mathematical logic laid the foundation for significant work done afterwards in Romania, as well as [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), and [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary). While in Iași, he completed research remarkable for the many new ideas and for his way of finding and using new connections between concepts from different areas of mathematics. He was promoted to Full Professor in November 1939.

In 1941, a position of professor at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest) opened up, and Moisil applied for it. However, [Gheorghe Vrânceanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Vrânceanu), [Dan Barbilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Barbu), and [Miron Nicolescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miron_Nicolescu) also applied for the position, and Vrânceanu got it. Moisil approached the Ministry of Education, arguing that it would be a great opportunity for mathematics in Romania if all four could be appointed. As a result of his appeal, all four mathematicians were hired. Moisil moved to [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), where he became a Professor in the School of Mathematics (later the School of Mathematics and Computer Science) at the University of Bucharest, on 30 December 1941.

From 1946 to 1948, Moisil took a leave of absence, being named [plenipotentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenipotentiary) envoy to [Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara). While in [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey), he gave several series of mathematics lectures at [Istanbul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_University) and [Istanbul Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_Technical_University).

In 1948, he resumed teaching at the University of Bucharest. That same year, he was elected to the Romanian Academy, and a member of the [Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian Academy](wikipedia://en/8609230). After 1965, one of his students, George Georgescu, worked closely with him on multi-valued logics, and after the [Romanian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution) of 1989, became a Professor of Mathematics and Logic at the same university and department as Moisil in 1991.[^o4] His student also published extensive, original work on [algebraic logic](wikipedia://en/7870034), [MV-algebra](wikipedia://en/2483815), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), categories of [MV-algebras](wikipedia://en/2483815), [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) and [Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebra](wikipedia://en/43613625).[^o5][^o6]

## Work {#Work}

*Image: Grigore Moisil, matematician.JPG.* Moisil at his desk

Moisil published papers on [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics), [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). He developed a multi-dimensional extension of Pompeiu's [areolar derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areolar_derivative), and studied [monogenic functions](wikipedia://en/66390572) of one [hypercomplex](wikipedia://en/51438) variable with applications to mechanics. Moisil also introduced some [many-valued algebras](wikipedia://en/2483815), which he called Łukasiewicz algebras (now also named [Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łukasiewicz–Moisil_algebras)), and used them in logic and the study of [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356). He created new methods to analyze [finite automata](wikipedia://en/653404), and had many contributions to the field of automata theory in algebra.

Whereas Moisil's early contributions were in mathematics, he later devoted his scientific activity to [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). He was a professor of mathematical logic and computer science at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), and he also taught [Boolean logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_logic) at the [Politehnica University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politehnica_University_of_Bucharest). In 1957 he assisted in setting up the first Romanian computer in the Institute of Atomic Physics, and he encouraged several of his students to learn computer programming. Thus, he played a fundamental role in the development of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) in Romania, and in raising the first generations of Romanian computer scientists. Furthermore, several of Moisil’s books had an impact on the beginning of computer science: *Încercări Vechi și Noi în Logica Neoclasică* \[New and Old Approaches in Neoclassic Logic\], 1953; *Teoria Algebrică a Mecanismelor Automate* \[Algebraic Theory of Automata\], 1959; and *Circuite cu Tranzistori* \[Transistorized Circuits\], 1961. These books were translated into several languages, including [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_%28language%29) and [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_%28language%29). He furthermore gave lectures at various universities in Europe and North America.[^o8]

Moisil was a titular member of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy) and a member of the Academy of Bologna. In 1996, he was awarded by exception posthumously the [Computer Pioneer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Pioneer_Award) by the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) [Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Computer_Society).
