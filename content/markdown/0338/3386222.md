# Samuel Vince

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Vince"}

*Image: Samuel-Vince.jpg.* Samuel Vince

*Image: Astronomy; Samuel Vince reading in his rooms at Sidney Susse Wellcome V0024858.jpg.* Vince at Cambridge

**Samuel Vince** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)[^o2] (6 April 1749 – 28 November 1821) was an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) clergyman, mathematician and astronomer at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Fressingfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fressingfield). The son of a plasterer, he had laboured with his father up to the age of 12, but came to the attention of a clergyman who saw to it that he entered higher education.[^o4] Vince was admitted as a [sizar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizar) to [Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caius_College,_Cambridge) in 1771.[^o5] In 1775 he was [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609), and Winner of the [Smith Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Prize) at Cambridge. Migrating to [Sidney Sussex College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Sussex_College,_Cambridge) in 1777, he gained his M.A. in 1778 and was ordained a clergyman in 1779.[^o5] He was among seven men of that college who subscribed to the [Abolition Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Effecting_the_Abolition_of_the_Slave_Trade) in 1787.[^o4]

He was awarded the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal) in 1780 and was [Plumian Professor of Astronomy and Experimental Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plumian_Professor_of_Astronomy_and_Experimental_Philosophy) at Cambridge from 1796 until his death. He became [Archdeacon of Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdeacon_of_Bedford) in 1809, and died in [Ramsgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsgate).[^o5]

## Works {#Works}

As a mathematician, Vince wrote on many aspects of his expertise, including [logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithms) and [imaginary numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_numbers). His *Observations on the Theory of the Motion and Resistance of Fluids*[^o7] and *Experiments upon the Resistance of Bodies Moving in Fluids*[^o8] had later importance to [aviation history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_aviation).[^o9] He was also author of the influential *A Complete System of Astronomy* (3 vols. 1797–1808).

Vince also published the pamphlet *The Credibility of Christianity Vindicated, In Answer to Mr. Hume's Objections; In Two Discourses Preached Before the University of Cambridge by the Rev. S. Vince*. In this work, Vince made an apology of the Christian religion and, like [Charles Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698), sought to present rational arguments in favor of the belief in [miracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracles), against [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume)'s criticism. A review of this work with direct quotations can be found in *[The British Critic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_British_Critic)*, Volume 12, 1798.[^o10]
