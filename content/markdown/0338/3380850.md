# Hugh MacColl

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hugh MacColl"}

**Hugh MacColl** (before April 1885 spelled as **Hugh McColl**; 1831–1909) was a Scottish mathematician, [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and novelist.

:::

## Life {#Life}

MacColl was the youngest son of a poor [Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highlands) family that was at least partly [Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic)-speaking. Hugh's father died when he was still an infant, and Hugh was educated thanks to the efforts of his elder brother [Malcolm MacColl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_MacColl), an [Episcopalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Episcopalian) clergyman and friend and political ally of [William Ewart Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone).[^o4] Early in his acquaintanceship with Gladstone, Malcolm MacColl persuaded the [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_%28UK%29) politician to provide funds for Hugh's education at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University). It was proposed to send him to Oxford University's [St. Edmund Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Edmund_Hall,_Oxford), but Gladstone made this conditional on Hugh MacColl agreeing to take orders in the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England). Hugh MacColl refused this condition and, as a result, never obtained a conventional residential university education, which may have limited his contribution to philosophy, and certainly prevented him from ever obtaining a formal academic position.[^o5] He nevertheless earned a BA in mathematics from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) as an external student in 1876.[^o6]

After a few years working in different areas of Great Britain, MacColl relocated to [Boulogne-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulogne-sur-Mer), in France on the Channel, where he developed the greater part of his work and became a [French citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_citizen). MacColl was not obscure during his time. He was a lifelong regular contributor to the *Educational Times*. His correspondents included the logicians [William Stanley Jevons](wikipedia://en/207021) and [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117). He also corresponded, and argued in print, with the young [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163), and reviewed [Alfred North Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395)'s 1898 *Universal Algebra* for *Mind* magazine.

## Works {#Works}

MacColl is known for three main accomplishments in formal and [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939):

- During 1877–1879, while working out a problem involving [integration](wikipedia://en/15532), he published a four-part article establishing the first known variant of the [propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus), terming it the "calculus of equivalent statements", preceding [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416)'s *[Begriffsschrift](wikipedia://en/900125)*. He subsequently published 11 articles in *Mind* magazine, during the period 1880–1908, and a text,[^o8][^o9] in an effort to attract the attention of philosophers to his work.
- Anticipation of [Clarence Irving Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Irving_Lewis)'s systems of [strict implication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict_implication), in work with which Lewis was familiar.[^o10]
- Construction of a logical system including later [modal logics](wikipedia://en/333365), namely the system T of [Robert Feys](wikipedia://en/5154962) and [Georg Henrik von Wright](wikipedia://en/12250), and system S5 of Lewis from 1918.[^o10]

MacColl also published two novels, *[Mr. Stranger's Sealed Packet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Stranger's_Sealed_Packet)* (1889), concerning a journey to Mars and a utopian Martian society, and *Ednor Whitlock* (1891), dealing with a crisis of faith occasioned by exposure to new scientific ideas. While described by a recent critic as "best left unread",[^o11] the novels reveal social and moral values to which the author gave full expression in his 1909 publication *Man's Origin, Destiny, and Duty*, an apology for [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity).

## Legacy {#Legacy}

There is presently a long-term MacColl Project, a joint venture of [Greifswald University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald_University) in Germany and the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo), which intends to publish a critical edition of his work. Furthermore, the group of logic and [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology) at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Lille_Nord_de_France) (France) develop MacColl's suggestions for a dynamic [free logic](wikipedia://en/1635110). The December 1999 issue of the magazine *[Nordic Journal of Philosophical Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Journal_of_Philosophical_Logic)* published the proceedings of a 1998 conference devoted to MacColl's work.
