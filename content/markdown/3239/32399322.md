# Heterogeneity in economics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heterogeneity in economics"}

In [economic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_theory) and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390), the term **heterogeneity** refers to differences across the units being studied. For example, a [macroeconomic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_model) in which consumers are assumed to differ from one another is said to have **heterogeneous agents.**

:::

## Unobserved heterogeneity in econometrics {#Unobserved_heterogeneity_in_econometrics}

In [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390), statistical inferences may be erroneous if, in addition to the observed variables under study, there exist other relevant variables that are unobserved, but correlated with the observed variables; dependent and independent variables .[^o3]

Methods for obtaining valid statistical inferences in the presence of unobserved heterogeneity include the [instrumental variables](wikipedia://en/1514405) method; [multilevel models](wikipedia://en/4521038), including [fixed effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_effects) and [random effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_effects) models; and the [Heckman correction](wikipedia://en/16728089) for [selection bias](wikipedia://en/394392).

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Heterogeneous agent model"}

## Economic models with heterogeneous agents {#Economic_models_with_heterogeneous_agents}

[Economic models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_model) are often formulated by means of a [representative agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_agent). Depending on the application, individual agents can be aggregated to or represented by a single agent. For example, individual demand can be aggregated to market demand if and only if individual preferences are of the [Gorman polar form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorman_polar_form) (or equivalently satisfy linear and parallel [Engel curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engel_curve)). Under this condition, even heterogeneous preferences can be represented by a single aggregate agent simply by summing over individual demand to market demand. However, some questions in economic theory cannot be accurately addressed without considering differences across agents, requiring a **heterogeneous agent model**.

How to solve a heterogeneous agent model depends on the assumptions that are made about the expectations of the agents in the model. Broadly speaking, models with heterogeneous agents fall into the category of [agent-based computational economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent-Based_Computational_Economics) (ACE) if the agents have [adaptive expectations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_expectations) (see [artificial financial market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_financial_market)), or into the category of [dynamic stochastic general equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_stochastic_general_equilibrium) (DSGE) if the agents have [rational expectations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_expectations). DSGE models with heterogeneneous agents are especially difficult to solve, and have only recently become a widespread topic of research; most early DSGE research instead focused on representative agent models.

### Methods for solving DSGE models with heterogeneous agents {#Methods_for_solving_DSGE_models_with_heterogeneous_agents}

- Heathcote, Storesletten and Violante ([AEJ Macro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Journal) 2009) make convenient functional form assumptions that allow for some dimensions of heterogeneity but nonetheless maintain an analytical solution for the general equilibrium.
- [Krusell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Krusell) and Smith ([JPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Political_Economy) 1998) permit an arbitrary distribution of wealth but assume all prices and equilibrium variables are approximately functions of the mean or of a few other statistics of that distribution.
- Algan, Allais, and [den Haan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wouter_den_Haan) (2009) approximate the distribution by a parameterized distributional form at all times.
- Reiter ([JEDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Economic_Dynamics_and_Control) 2009) and Mertens and [Judd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Judd) (mimeo 2011) develop perturbation methods for approximating the dynamics of the distribution under arbitrary distributional forms.
