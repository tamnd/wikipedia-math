# Matrix sign function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matrix sign function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **matrix sign function** is a [matrix function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_function) on [square matrices](wikipedia://en/166022) analogous to the complex [sign function](wikipedia://en/505449).[^o2]

:::

It was introduced by J.D. Roberts in 1971 as a tool for model reduction and for solving [Lyapunov](wikipedia://en/984752) and Algebraic [Riccati](wikipedia://en/16684054) equation in a technical report of [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University), which was later published in a journal in 1980.[^o3][^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The matrix sign function is a generalization of the complex [signum function](wikipedia://en/505449)

$$\operatorname{csgn}(z)= \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \mathrm{Re}(z) > 0, \\ -1 & \text{if } \mathrm{Re}(z) < 0, \end{cases}$$

to the matrix valued analogue $\operatorname{csgn}(A)$. Although the sign function is not [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478), the [matrix function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_function) is well defined for all matrices that have no [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429) on the [imaginary axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_axis), see for example the [Jordan-form-based definition](wikipedia://en/12691877#Jordan_decomposition) (where the derivatives are all zero).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let $A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$, then $\operatorname{csgn}(A)^2 = I$.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let $A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$, then $\operatorname{csgn}(A)$ is [diagonalizable](wikipedia://en/246325) and has eigenvalues that are $\pm 1$.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let $A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$, then $(I+\operatorname{csgn}(A))/2$ is a [projector](wikipedia://en/519182) onto the [invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826) associated with the eigenvalues in the [right-half plane](wikipedia://en/12744687), and analogously for $(I-\operatorname{csgn}(A))/2$ and the [left-half plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-half_plane).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let $A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$, and $A = P\begin{bmatrix}J_+ & 0 \\ 0 & J_-\end{bmatrix}P^{-1}$ be a [Jordan decomposition](wikipedia://en/312877) such that $J_+$ corresponds to eigenvalues with positive real part and $J_-$ to eigenvalue with negative real part. Then $\operatorname{csgn}(A) = P\begin{bmatrix}I_+ & 0 \\ 0 & -I_-\end{bmatrix}P^{-1}$, where $I_+$ and $I_-$ are [identity matrices](wikipedia://en/59718) of sizes corresponding to $J_+$ and $J_-$, respectively.[^o2]

:::

:::

## Computational methods {#Computational_methods}

The function can be computed with generic methods for [matrix functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_function), but there are also specialized methods.

### Newton iteration {#Newton_iteration}

The [Newton iteration](wikipedia://en/22145) can be derived by observing that $\operatorname{csgn}(x) = \sqrt{x^2}/x$, which in terms of matrices can be written as $\operatorname{csgn}(A) = A^{-1}\sqrt{A^2}$, where we use the [matrix square root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_square_root). If we apply the [Babylonian method](wikipedia://en/2929701#By_the_Babylonian_method) to compute the square root of the matrix $A^2$, that is, the iteration $X_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2} \left(X_k + A^{2} X_k^{-1}\right)$, and define the new iterate $Z_k = A^{-1}X_k$, we arrive at the iteration

$$Z_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(Z_k + Z_k^{-1}\right),$$

where typically $Z_0=A$. Convergence is global, and locally it is quadratic.[^o2][^o3]

The Newton iteration uses the explicit inverse of the iterates $Z_k$.

### Newton–Schulz iteration {#Newton–Schulz_iteration}

To avoid the need of an explicit inverse used in the Newton iteration, the inverse can be approximated with one step of the [Newton iteration for the inverse](wikipedia://en/22145#Multiplicative_inverses_of_numbers_and_power_series), $Z_k^{-1}\approx Z_k\left(2I-Z_k^2\right)$, derived by [Schulz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Günther_Schulz_%28mathematician%29)(de) in 1933.[^o16] Substituting this approximation into the previous method, the new method becomes

$$Z_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2}Z_k\left(3I - Z_k^2\right).$$

Convergence is (still) quadratic, but only local (guaranteed for $\|I-A^2\|<1$).[^o2]

## Applications {#Applications}

### Solutions of Sylvester equations {#Solutions_of_Sylvester_equations}

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:[^o3][^o4]** Let $A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and assume that $A$ and $B$ are [stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_matrix), then the unique solution to the [Sylvester equation](wikipedia://en/10105237), $AX +XB = C$, is given by $X$ such that

$$\begin{bmatrix} -I &2X\\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} = \operatorname{csgn} \left( \begin{bmatrix} A &-C\\ 0 & -B \end{bmatrix} \right).$$

*Proof sketch:* The result follows from the similarity transform

$$\begin{bmatrix} A &-C\\ 0 & -B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} I & X \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} A & 0\\ 0 & -B \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I & X \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix}^{-1},$$

since

$$\operatorname{csgn} \left( \begin{bmatrix} A &-C\\ 0 & -B \end{bmatrix} \right) = \begin{bmatrix} I & X \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I & 0\\ 0 & -I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I & -X \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix},$$

due to the stability of $A$ and $B$.

The theorem is, naturally, also applicable to the [Lyapunov equation](wikipedia://en/984752). However, due to the structure the Newton iteration simplifies to only involving inverses of $A$ and $A^T$.

:::

### Solutions of algebraic Riccati equations {#Solutions_of_algebraic_Riccati_equations}

There is a similar result applicable to the [algebraic Riccati equation](wikipedia://en/16684054), $A^H P + P A - P F P + Q = 0$.[^o2][^o3] Define $V,W\in\mathbb{C}^{2n\times n}$ as

$$\begin{bmatrix} V & W \end{bmatrix} = \operatorname{csgn} \left( \begin{bmatrix} A^H &Q\\ F & -A \end{bmatrix} \right) - \begin{bmatrix} I &0\\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix}.$$

Under the assumption that $F,Q\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$ are [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682) and there exists a unique stabilizing solution, in the sense that $A-FP$ is [stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_matrix), that solution is given by the [over-determined](wikipedia://en/10160606), but [consistent](wikipedia://en/45304281), linear system

$$VP=-W.$$

*Proof sketch:* The similarity transform

$$\begin{bmatrix} A^H &Q\\ F & -A \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} P & -I \\ I & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -(A-FP) & -F\\ 0 & (A-FP) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} P & -I \\ I & 0 \end{bmatrix}^{-1},$$

and the stability of $A-FP$ implies that

$$\left( \operatorname{csgn} \left( \begin{bmatrix} A^H &Q\\ F & -A \end{bmatrix} \right) - \begin{bmatrix} I &0\\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} \right) \begin{bmatrix} X & -I \\ I & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} X & -I\\ I & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & Y \\ 0 & -2I \end{bmatrix},$$

for some matrix $Y\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}$.

### Computations of matrix square-root {#Computations_of_matrix_square-root}

The [Denman–Beavers iteration](wikipedia://en/2929701#By_Denman–Beavers_iteration) for the square root of a matrix can be derived from the Newton iteration for the matrix sign function by noticing that $A - PIP=0$ is a degenerate [algebraic Riccati equation](wikipedia://en/16684054)[^o4] and by definition a solution $P$ is the square root of $A$.

:::
