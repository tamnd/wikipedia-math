# Walter Lewis Baily Jr.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Lewis Baily Jr."}

**Walter Lewis Baily Jr.** (July 5, 1930 – January 15, 2013) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Born in [Waynesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynesburg,_Pennsylvania), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania),[^o4] Baily's research focused on areas of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924), [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) and [number-theoretical](wikipedia://en/21527) applications of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146). One of his significant works was with [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672), now known as the [Baily–Borel compactification](wikipedia://en/16083822), which is a [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) of a quotient of a [Hermitian symmetric space](wikipedia://en/4127357) by an [arithmetic group](wikipedia://en/630741) (that is, a [linear algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) over the rational numbers).[^o5] Baily and Borel built on the work of [Ichirō Satake](wikipedia://en/32319985) and others.

Baily became a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) in 1952.[^o6] He studied at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT), receiving a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1952. After that, he attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), receiving a [Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1953 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1955 under the direction of his thesis advisor [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326) (*On the Quotient of a Complex Analytic Manifold by a Discontinuous Group of Complex Analytic Self-Homomorphisms*). Subsequently, he was an instructor at Princeton and then MIT. In 1957 he worked as a mathematician at [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs). In 1957, he was appointed [Assistant Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) and subsequently promoted to [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 1963 at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). He became a Professor [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) at the University of Chicago in 2005.

He was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786). He often visited the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) as a guest of [Shokichi Iyanaga](wikipedia://en/18357287) and [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326), spoke fluent Japanese and in Tokyo, 1963 married Yaeko Iseki, with whom he had a son. He owned an apartment in Tokyo for many years, where he spent his summers. In addition, he often visited [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) and [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) and spoke fluent Russian.

He was awarded an [Alfred P. Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Fellowship) in 1958. In 1962, he was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) (*On the moduli of Abelian varieties with multiplications from an order in a totally real number field*).

His doctoral students include [Paul Monsky](wikipedia://en/12673586), [Timothy J. Hickey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_J._Hickey), and [Daniel Bump](wikipedia://en/50510967).[^o7]

Baily died in [Northbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northbrook,_Illinois), [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois),[^o8] at 82.
