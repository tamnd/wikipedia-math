# Ann E. Bailie

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ann E. Bailie"}

**Dorothy Ann Eckels Bailie** (born 1935) is an American mathematician who worked at [Goddard Space Flight Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddard_Space_Flight_Center) in the 1950s and 1960s. She was one of the three authors of the 1959 report establishing [Earth's shape](wikipedia://en/233668) as asymmetrical and "pear-shaped", based on data from [Vanguard 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard_1).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Dorothy Ann Eckels was born in [Littleton, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littleton,_New_Hampshire) and raised in [Laconia, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laconia,_New_Hampshire), the daughter of John C. Eckels and Dorothy R. Eckels.[^o4] Her father was a surgeon. Her maternal grandfather, Adolph Frederick Erdmann, was a pioneer in the field of [anesthesiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesiology).[^o5][^o6] She earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics at [Middlebury College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlebury_College) in 1957.[^o7] While at Middlebury, she was elected Queen of the school's Winter Carnival, an event she co-chaired.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Bailie worked at the [United States Naval Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Research_Laboratory) after college.[^o10] By 1959 she worked in the Theoretical Division of [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)'s Goddard Space Flight Center, on calculating and analyzing complex orbits for satellites.[^o11] She, R. Kenneth Squires, and [John A. O'Keefe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._O'Keefe_%28astronomer%29) were the team that determined that the Earth was asymmetrical and "pear-shaped",[^o12] based on data from Vanguard 1.[^o13][^o14]

[James E. Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Webb) mentioned Bailie, [Nancy Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Roman), and [Eleanor C. Pressly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_C._Pressly) in his 1961 commencement speech at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University), as examples of women in the space program.[^o15] In 1963, she was named one of the Ten Young Women of the Year by *[Mademoiselle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mademoiselle_%28magazine%29)* magazine.[^o16] Later in her career, she worked at [Analytical Mechanics Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propellant_depot) in Maryland.[^o10][^o17]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Ann Eckels married accountant William J. Bailie in 1959.[^o4] They had three children. Her husband died in 2009.[^o19]
