# Avraham Trahtman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Avraham Trahtman"}

**Avraham Naumovich Trahtman** (Trakhtman) (Абрам Наумович Трахтман; 10 February 1944 – 17 July 2024) was a Soviet-born Israeli mathematician and academic at [Bar-Ilan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-Ilan_University) ([Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel)). In 2007, Trahtman solved a problem in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) that had been open for 37 years, the [Road Coloring Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_Coloring_Conjecture) posed in 1970.[^o3] Trahtman died in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem) on 17 July 2024, at the age of 80.[^o4]

:::

## Road coloring problem posed and solved {#Road_coloring_problem_posed_and_solved}

Trahtman's solution to the [road coloring problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_coloring_problem) was accepted in 2007 and published in 2009 by the *[Israel Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Journal_of_Mathematics)*.[^o6] The problem arose in the subfield of [symbolic dynamics](wikipedia://en/1986263), an abstract part of the field of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). The road coloring problem was raised by [R. L. Adler](wikipedia://en/41177893) and L. W. Goodwyn from the United States, and the Israeli mathematician [B. Weiss](wikipedia://en/40423267).[^o7][^o8] The proof used results from earlier work.[^o9][^o10][^o11]

## Černý conjecture {#Černý_conjecture}

The problem of estimating the length of synchronizing word has a long history and was posed independently by several authors, but it is commonly known as the [Černý conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Černý_conjecture). In 1964 Jan Černý conjectured that $(n-1)^2$ is the upper bound for the length of the shortest synchronizing word for any n-state complete DFA (a DFA with complete state transition graph).[^o14] If this is true, it would be tight: in his 1964 paper, Černý exhibited a class of automata (indexed by the number n of states) for which the shortest reset words have this length. In 2011 Trahtman published a proof[^o15] of upper bound $n (7n^2+6n-16)/48$, but then he found an error in it.[^o16] The conjecture holds in many partial cases, see for instance, Kari[^o17] and Trahtman.[^o18]

:::

## Other work {#Other_work}

The finite basis problem for [semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroups) of order less than six in the theory of semigroups was posed by [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) in 1966,[^o20] and repeated by [Anatoly Maltsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Maltsev) and L. N. Shevrin. In 1983, Trahtman solved this problem by proving that all semigroups of order less than six are finitely based.[^o21][^o22]

In the theory of [varieties](wikipedia://en/1021753) of semigroups and [universal algebras](wikipedia://en/45200) the problem of existence of covering elements in the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) of varieties was posed by Evans in 1971.[^o23] The positive solution of the problem was found by Trahtman.[^o24] He also found a six-element semigroup that generates a variety with a continuum of subvarieties,[^o25] and varieties of semigroups having no irreducible base of identities.[^o26]

The theory of [locally testable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_testable) [automata](wikipedia://en/103356) can be based on the theory of varieties of locally testable semigroups.[^o27] Trahtman found the precise estimation on the order of local testability of finite automata.[^o28]

There are results in theoretical mechanics[^o29] and in the promising area of extracting moisture from the air[^o30] mentioned in "*[New Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Scientist)*".[^o31]
