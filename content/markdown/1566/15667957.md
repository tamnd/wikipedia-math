# Minkowski content

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minkowski content"}

The **Minkowski [content](wikipedia://en/9999200)** (named after [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537)), or the **boundary measure**, of a set is a basic concept that uses concepts from [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) to generalize the notions of [length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of a [smooth curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_curve) in the plane, and [area](wikipedia://en/27725) of a smooth surface in [space](wikipedia://en/9697), to arbitrary [measurable sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set).

:::

It is typically applied to [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) boundaries of domains in the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), but it can also be used in the context of general metric [measure spaces.](wikipedia://en/45270)

It is related to, although different from, the [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For $A \subset \mathbb{R}^{n}$, and each integer *m* with $0 \leq m \leq n$, the ***m*-dimensional upper Minkowski content** is

$$M^{*m}(A) = \limsup_{r \to 0^+} \frac{\mu(\{x: d(x,A) < r\})}{\alpha (n-m)r^{n-m}}$$

and the ***m*-dimensional lower Minkowski content** is defined as

$$M_*^m(A) = \liminf_{r \to 0^+} \frac{\mu(\{x: d(x,A) < r\})}{\alpha (n-m)r^{n-m}}$$

where $\alpha(n-m)r^{n-m}$ is the volume of the [(*n*−*m*)-ball](wikipedia://en/39782) of radius r and $\mu$ is an $n$-dimensional [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198).

If the upper and lower *m*-dimensional Minkowski content of *A* are equal, then their common value is called the Minkowski content *M*<sup>*m*</sup>(*A*).[^o4][^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The Minkowski content is (generally) not a measure. In particular, the *m*-dimensional Minkowski content in **R**<sup>n</sup> is not a measure unless *m* = 0, in which case it is the [counting measure](wikipedia://en/191786). Indeed, clearly the Minkowski content assigns the same value to the set *A* as well as its [closure](wikipedia://en/42309).
- If *A* is a closed *m*-[rectifiable set](wikipedia://en/2644238) in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, given as the image of a bounded set from **R**<sup>*m*</sup> under a [Lipschitz function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function), then the *m*-dimensional Minkowski content of *A* exists, and is equal to the *m*-dimensional [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109) of *A*.[^o8]

:::
