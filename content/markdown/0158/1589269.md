# Cusp neighborhood

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cusp neighborhood"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **cusp neighborhood** is defined as a set of points near a [cusp singularity](wikipedia://en/6851758).[^o2]

:::

## Cusp neighborhood for a Riemann surface {#Cusp_neighborhood_for_a_Riemann_surface}

The cusp neighborhood for a hyperbolic [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) can be defined in terms of its [Fuchsian model](wikipedia://en/1417252).[^o4]

Suppose that the [Fuchsian group](wikipedia://en/471365) *G* contains a [parabolic element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolic_element) g. For example, the element *t* ∈ SL(2,**Z**) where

$$t(z)=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}:z = \frac{1\cdot z+1}{0 \cdot z + 1} = z+1$$

is a parabolic element. Note that all parabolic elements of SL(2,**C**) are [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176) to this element. That is, if *g* ∈ SL(2,**Z**) is parabolic, then $g=h^{-1}th$ for some *h* ∈ SL(2,**Z**).

The set

$$U=\{ z \in \mathbf{H} : \Im z > 1 \}$$

where **H** is the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826) has

$$\gamma(U) \cap U = \emptyset$$

for any $\gamma \in G - \langle g \rangle$ where $\langle g \rangle$ is understood to mean the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) generated by *g*. That is, γ acts [properly discontinuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuously) on *U*. Because of this, it can be seen that the projection of *U* onto **H**/*G* is thus

$$E = U/ \langle g \rangle.$$

Here, *E* is called the **neighborhood of the cusp corresponding to g**.

Note that the hyperbolic area of *E* is exactly 1, when computed using the canonical [Poincaré metric](wikipedia://en/1487868). This is most easily seen by example: consider the intersection of *U* defined above with the [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365)

$$\left\{ z \in H: \left| z \right| > 1,\, \left| \,\mbox{Re}(z) \,\right| < \frac{1}{2} \right\}$$

of the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019), as would be appropriate for the choice of *T* as the parabolic element. When integrated over the [volume element](wikipedia://en/1920694)

$$d\mu=\frac{dxdy}{y^2}$$

the result is trivially 1. Areas of all cusp neighborhoods are equal to this, by the invariance of the area under conjugation.
