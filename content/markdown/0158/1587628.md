# Neal Koblitz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neal Koblitz"}

**Neal I. Koblitz** (born December 24, 1948[^o3]) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer). He is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) and an [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor) with the [Centre for Applied Cryptographic Research](wikipedia://en/999894) at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo). He is the creator of [hyperelliptic curve cryptography](wikipedia://en/561913) and the independent co-creator of [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Neal I. Koblitz was born on December 24, 1948. He received his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1969.[^o3] While at Harvard, he was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1968.[^o5] He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1974 under the direction of [Nick Katz](wikipedia://en/1451427). From 1975 to 1979, he was an instructor at Harvard University.[^o6] In 1979, he began working at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).

Koblitz's 1981 article "Mathematics as Propaganda"[^o7] criticized the misuse of mathematics in the [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science) and helped motivate [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991)'s successful challenge to the nomination of [political scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_scientist) [Samuel P. Huntington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_P._Huntington) to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o8] In *[The Mathematical Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Intelligencer)*, Koblitz,[^o9][^o10][^o11] Steven Weintraub,[^o12] and [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) later criticized the arguments of [Herbert A. Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_A._Simon), who had attempted to defend Huntington's work.[^o13]

He co-invented [elliptic-curve cryptography](wikipedia://en/9966) in 1985[^o14] with [Victor S. Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_S._Miller), and for this was awarded the Levchin Prize[^o15] in 2021.

In 1985, he and his wife, [Ann Hibner Koblitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Hibner_Koblitz), founded the Kovalevskaia Prize to honor women scientists in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries). It was financed from the royalties of Ann Hibner Koblitz's 1983 biography of [Sofia Kovalevskaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kovalevskaia).[^o16]

Koblitz's 2007 article "The uneasy relationship between mathematics and cryptography" discusses the increased contact between mathematics and cryptography in the 1990s. He argues that there is an unjustified "aura" placed onto mathematical proofs in cryptographic competitions and received much ire for the view.[^o17] Koblitz, in co-operation with [Alfred Menezes](wikipedia://en/4356319), went on to write a series of *Another Look* papers that describe errors or weaknesses in existing security proofs, the first being *Another look at HMAC* (2013). The two now maintain a website dedicated to this type of papers.[^o18]

In 2011, Koblitz published "Elliptic curve cryptography: The serpentine course of a [paradigm shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm_shift)" with Ann Hibner Koblitz and Alfred Menezes. Using the history of ECC and shifting attitudes in the cryptographic community, the article argues that the field of cryptography is not as scientific and meritocratic as cryptographers want to show to the outside world; the field is [controlled by social factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_constructivism), especially [path dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_dependence).[^o19]
