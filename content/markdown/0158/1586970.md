# Reuben Goodstein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reuben Goodstein"}

**Reuben Louis Goodstein** (15 December 1912 – 8 March 1985) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) with an interest in the [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Goodstein was educated at [St Paul's School, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_School,_London). He received his Master's degree from [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge). After this, he worked at the [University of Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Reading) but ultimately spent most of his academic career at the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester). He earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) in 1946[^o7] while still working in Reading.

Goodstein also studied under [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741).[^o3][^o4] Wittgenstein selected Goodstein and others to take notes of his lectures, the collection of which later became *[The Blue Book](wikipedia://en/28341040)*.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

He published many works on [finitism](wikipedia://en/152900) and the reconstruction of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) from a finitistic viewpoint, for example "Constructive Formalism. Essays on the foundations of mathematics." [Goodstein's theorem](wikipedia://en/150062) was among the earliest examples of theorems found to be unprovable in [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) but provable in stronger [logical systems](wikipedia://en/396102) (such as [second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454)). He also introduced a variant of the [Ackermann function](wikipedia://en/2925) that is now known as the [hyperoperation sequence](wikipedia://en/33731923), together with the naming convention now used for these operations (*[tetration](wikipedia://en/482379)*, *pentation*, etc.).

Besides [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) (in which he held the first professorial chair in the U.K.), [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), and the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439), Goodstein was keenly interested in the teaching of mathematics. From 1956 to 1962 he was editor of *[The Mathematical Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Gazette)*. In 1962 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) (with an address on *A recursive lattice*) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm). Among his doctoral students are [Martin Löb](wikipedia://en/7268088) and [Alan Bundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Bundy).
