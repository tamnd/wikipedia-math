# Musselman's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Musselman's theorem"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), **Musselman's theorem** is a property of certain [circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_%28geometry%29) defined by an arbitrary [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654).

:::

*Image: Musselman theorem.svg.*

Specifically, let $T$ be a triangle, and $A$, $B$, and $C$ its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417). Let $A^*$, $B^*$, and $C^*$ be the vertices of the [reflection triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_triangle) $T^*$, obtained by mirroring each vertex of $T$ across the opposite side.[^o6] Let $O$ be the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of $T$. Consider the three circles $S_A$, $S_B$, and $S_C$ defined by the points $A\,O\,A^*$, $B\,O\,B^*$, and $C\,O\,C^*$, respectively. The theorem says that these three **Musselman circles** meet in a point $M$, that is the [inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_inversion) with respect to the circumcenter of $T$ of the [isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) or the [nine-point center](wikipedia://en/4517570) of $T$.[^o10]

The common point $M$ is point $X_{1157}$ in [Clark Kimberling's list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberling_center) of [triangle centers](wikipedia://en/12401488).[^o10][^o8]

## History {#History}

The theorem was proposed as an advanced problem by [John Rogers Musselman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rogers_Musselman) and [René Goormaghtigh](wikipedia://en/28902890) in 1939,[^o7] and a proof was presented by them in 1941.[^o9] A generalization of this result was stated and proved by Goormaghtigh.[^o5]

## Goormaghtigh’s generalization {#Goormaghtigh’s_generalization}

The generalization of Musselman's theorem by Goormaghtigh does not mention the circles explicitly.

As before, let $A$, $B$, and $C$ be the vertices of a triangle $T$, and $O$ its circumcenter. Let $H$ be the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of $T$, that is, the intersection of its three [altitude lines](wikipedia://en/161241). Let $A'$, $B'$, and $C'$ be three points on the segments $OA$, $OB$, and $OC$, such that $OA'/OA=OB'/OB=OC'/OC = t$. Consider the three lines $L_A$, $L_B$, and $L_C$, perpendicular to $OA$, $OB$, and $OC$ though the points $A'$, $B'$, and $C'$, respectively. Let $P_A$, $P_B$, and $P_C$ be the intersections of these perpendicular with the lines $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$, respectively.

It had been observed by [Joseph Neuberg](wikipedia://en/19178914), in 1884, that the three points $P_A$, $P_B$, and $P_C$ lie on a common line $R$.[^o13] Let $N$ be the projection of the circumcenter $O$ on the line $R$, and $N'$ the point on $ON$ such that $ON'/ON = t$. Goormaghtigh proved that $N'$ is the inverse with respect to the circumcircle of $T$ of the isogonal conjugate of the point $Q$ on the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) $OH$, such that $QH/QO = 2t$.[^o11][^o12]
