# Orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra** is a pair $(\mathfrak{g}, s)$ consisting of a real [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak{g}$ and an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) $s$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ of order $2$ such that the [eigenspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspace) $\mathfrak{u}$ of *s* corresponding to 1 (i.e., the set $\mathfrak{u}$ of [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738)) is a [compact subalgebra](wikipedia://en/25262324). If "compactness" is omitted, it is called a **symmetric Lie algebra**. An orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra is said to be *effective* if $\mathfrak{u}$ intersects the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) of $\mathfrak{g}$ [trivially](wikipedia://en/193059). In practice, effectiveness is often assumed; we do this in this article as well.

:::

The canonical example is the Lie algebra of a [symmetric space](wikipedia://en/1642877), $s$ being the differential of a symmetry.

Let $(\mathfrak{g}, s)$ be effective orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra, and let $\mathfrak{p}$ denotes the -1 eigenspace of $s$. We say that $(\mathfrak{g}, s)$ is *of compact type* if $\mathfrak{g}$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/25262324) and [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587). If instead it is noncompact, semisimple, and if $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{u}+\mathfrak{p}$ is a Cartan decomposition, then $(\mathfrak{g}, s)$ is *of noncompact type*. If $\mathfrak{p}$ is an Abelian ideal of $\mathfrak{g}$, then $(\mathfrak{g}, s)$ is said to be *of Euclidean type*.

Every effective, orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra decomposes into a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of ideals $\mathfrak{g}_0$, $\mathfrak{g}_-$ and $\mathfrak{g}_+$, each invariant under $s$ and orthogonal with respect to the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386) of $\mathfrak{g}$, and such that if $s_0$, $s_-$ and $s_+$ denote the restriction of $s$ to $\mathfrak{g}_0$, $\mathfrak{g}_-$ and $\mathfrak{g}_+$, respectively, then $(\mathfrak{g}_0,s_0)$, $(\mathfrak{g}_-,s_-)$ and $(\mathfrak{g}_+,s_+)$ are effective orthogonal symmetric Lie algebras of Euclidean type, compact type and noncompact type.
