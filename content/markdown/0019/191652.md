# Otto Hölder

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Otto Hölder"}

**Ludwig Otto Hölder** (December 22, 1859 – August 29, 1937) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) born in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hölder was the youngest of three sons of professor Otto Hölder (1811–1890), and a grandson of professor Christian Gottlieb Hölder (1776–1847); his two brothers also became professors. He first studied at the *Polytechnikum* (which today is the [University of Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Stuttgart)) and then in 1877 went to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) where he was a student of [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450), [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), and [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117).[^o3]

In 1877, he entered the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and took his doctorate from the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen) in 1882. The title of his doctoral thesis was "Beiträge zur Potentialtheorie" ("Contributions to [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342)"). Following this, he went to the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) but was unable to [habilitate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) there, instead earning a second doctorate and habilitation at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), both in 1884.

## Academic career and later life {#Academic_career_and_later_life}

He was unable to get government approval for a faculty position in Göttingen, and instead was offered a position as extraordinary professor at Tübingen in 1889. Temporary mental incapacitation delayed his acceptance but he began working there in 1890. In 1899, he took the former chair of [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) as a full professor at the University of Leipzig. There he served as dean from 1912 to 1913, and as rector in 1918.[^o3]

He married Helene, the daughter of a bank director and politician, in 1899. They had two sons and two daughters. His son [Ernst Hölder](wikipedia://en/31518948) became another mathematician,[^o3] and his daughter Irmgard married mathematician [Aurel Wintner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurel_Wintner).[^o7]

In 1933, Hölder signed the *[Vow of allegiance of the Professors of the German Universities and High-Schools to Adolf Hitler and the National Socialistic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State)*.[^o8]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

[Hölder's inequality](wikipedia://en/191538), named for Hölder, was actually [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) earlier by [Leonard James Rogers](wikipedia://en/9024921). It is named for a paper in which Hölder, citing Rogers, reproves it;[^o10] in turn, the same paper includes a proof of what is now called [Jensen's inequality](wikipedia://en/297811), with some side conditions that were later removed by Jensen.[^o11] Hölder is also noted for many other [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) including the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan–Hölder_theorem), the theorem stating that every [linearly ordered group](wikipedia://en/349014) that satisfies an [Archimedean property](wikipedia://en/264158) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of the additive [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), the classification of [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600) of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) up to 200, the [anomalous outer automorphisms](wikipedia://en/13763478#The_exceptional_outer_automorphism_of_S6) of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>6</sub>, and [Hölder's theorem](wikipedia://en/4883158), which implies that the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) satisfies no [algebraic differential equation](wikipedia://en/9501827). Another idea related to his name is the [Hölder condition](wikipedia://en/4005892) (or Hölder continuity), which is used in many areas of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), including the theories of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630).
