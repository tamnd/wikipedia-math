# List of named matrices

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of named matrices"}

*Image: Taxonomy of Complex Matrices.svg.* Several important classes of matrices are subsets of each other.

This article lists some important classes of [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) used in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering). A **matrix** (plural matrices, or less commonly matrixes) is a rectangular [array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Array_data_structure) of [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) called *entries*. Matrices have a long history of both study and application, leading to diverse ways of classifying matrices. A first group is matrices satisfying concrete conditions of the entries, including constant matrices. Important examples include the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) given by

:::

$$I_n = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

and the [zero matrix](wikipedia://en/1168888) of dimension $m \times n$. For example:

$$O_{2 \times 3} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Further ways of classifying matrices are according to their [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue), or by imposing conditions on the [product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_product) of the matrix with other matrices. Finally, many domains, both in mathematics and other sciences including [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), have particular matrices that are applied chiefly in these areas.

## Matrices satisfying some equations {#Matrices_satisfying_some_equations}

A number of matrix-related notions is about properties of products or inverses of the given matrix. The [matrix product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_product) of a *m*-by-*n* matrix *A* and a *n*-by-*k* matrix *B* is the *m*-by-*k* matrix *C* given by

$(C)_{i,j} = \sum_{r=1}^n A_{i,r}B_{r,j}.$[^o88]

This matrix product is denoted *AB*. Unlike the product of numbers, matrix products are not [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative), that is to say *AB* need not be equal to *BA*.[^o88] A number of notions are concerned with the failure of this commutativity. An [inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_of_a_matrix) of square matrix *A* is a matrix *B* (necessarily of the same dimension as *A*) such that *AB* = *I*. Equivalently, *BA* = *I*. An inverse need not exist. If it exists, *B* is uniquely determined, and is also called *the* inverse of *A*, denoted *A*<sup>−1</sup>.

## Matrices used in statistics {#Matrices_used_in_statistics}

The following matrices find their main application in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

::: {#o150 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bernoulli matrix"}

- [Bernoulli matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_matrix) — a square matrix with entries +1, −1, with equal [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) of each.

:::

::: {#o151 .entry cue=list-entry name="Centering matrix"}

- [Centering matrix](wikipedia://en/11395056) — a matrix which, when multiplied with a vector, has the same effect as subtracting the mean of the components of the vector from every component.

:::

::: {#o152 .entry cue=list-entry name="Correlation matrix"}

- [Correlation matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_matrix) — a symmetric *n×n* matrix, formed by the pairwise [correlation coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_product-moment_correlation_coefficient) of several [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685).

:::

::: {#o153 .entry cue=list-entry name="Covariance matrix"}

- [Covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) — a symmetric *n×n* matrix, formed by the pairwise [covariances](wikipedia://en/157059) of several random variables. Sometimes called a *dispersion matrix*.

:::

::: {#o154 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dispersion matrix"}

- [Dispersion matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersion_matrix) — another name for a *covariance matrix*.

:::

::: {#o155 .entry cue=list-entry name="Doubly stochastic matrix"}

- [Doubly stochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/2906180) — a non-negative matrix such that each row and each column sums to 1 (thus the matrix is both *left stochastic* and *right stochastic*)

:::

::: {#o156 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fisher information matrix"}

- [Fisher information matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_information_matrix) — a matrix representing the variance of the partial derivative, with respect to a parameter, of the log of the likelihood function of a random variable.

:::

::: {#o157 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hat matrix"}

- [Hat matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_matrix) — a square matrix used in statistics to relate fitted values to observed values.

:::

::: {#o158 .entry cue=list-entry name="Orthostochastic matrix"}

- [Orthostochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/13676913) — doubly stochastic matrix whose entries are the squares of the absolute values of the entries of some orthogonal matrix

:::

::: {#o159 .entry cue=list-entry name="Precision matrix"}

- [Precision matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_matrix) — a symmetric *n×n* matrix, formed by inverting the *covariance matrix*. Also called the *information matrix*.

:::

::: {#o160 .entry cue=list-entry name="Stochastic matrix"}

- [Stochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/217313) — a [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) matrix describing a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895). The sum of entries of any row is one.

:::

::: {#o161 .entry cue=list-entry name="Transition matrix"}

- [Transition matrix](wikipedia://en/217313) — a matrix representing the [probabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilities) of conditions changing from one state to another in a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876)

:::

::: {#o162 .entry cue=list-entry name="Unistochastic matrix"}

- [Unistochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/13269420) — a doubly stochastic matrix whose entries are the squares of the absolute values of the entries of some unitary matrix

:::

## Matrices used in graph theory {#Matrices_used_in_graph_theory}

The following matrices find their main application in [graph](wikipedia://en/12401) and [network theory](wikipedia://en/766409).

::: {#o164 .entry cue=list-entry name="Adjacency matrix"}

- [Adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) — a square matrix representing a graph, with *a<sub>ij</sub>* non-zero if vertex *i* and vertex *j* are adjacent.

:::

::: {#o165 .entry cue=list-entry name="Biadjacency matrix"}

- [Biadjacency matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biadjacency_matrix) — a special class of [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) that describes adjacency in [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431).

:::

::: {#o166 .entry cue=list-entry name="Degree matrix"}

- [Degree matrix](wikipedia://en/1449175) — a diagonal matrix defining the degree of each [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) in a graph.

:::

::: {#o167 .entry cue=list-entry name="Edmonds matrix"}

- [Edmonds matrix](wikipedia://en/11415890) — a square matrix of a bipartite graph.

:::

::: {#o168 .entry cue=list-entry name="Incidence matrix"}

- [Incidence matrix](wikipedia://en/420919) — a matrix representing a relationship between two classes of objects (usually [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) in the context of graph theory).

:::

::: {#o169 .entry cue=list-entry name="Laplacian matrix"}

- [Laplacian matrix](wikipedia://en/1448472) — a matrix equal to the degree matrix minus the adjacency matrix for a graph, used to find the number of spanning trees in the graph.

:::

::: {#o170 .entry cue=list-entry name="Seidel adjacency matrix"}

- [Seidel adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/7770329) — a matrix similar to the usual [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) but with −1 for adjacency; +1 for nonadjacency; 0 on the diagonal.

:::

::: {#o171 .entry cue=list-entry name="Skew-adjacency matrix"}

- [Skew-adjacency matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew-adjacency_matrix) — an [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) in which each non-zero *a<sub>ij</sub>* is 1 or −1, accordingly as the direction *i → j* matches or opposes that of an initially specified orientation.

:::

::: {#o172 .entry cue=list-entry name="Tutte matrix"}

- [Tutte matrix](wikipedia://en/18305460) — a generalization of the Edmonds matrix for a balanced bipartite graph.

:::

## Matrices used in science and engineering {#Matrices_used_in_science_and_engineering}

::: {#o174 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix"}

- [Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix](wikipedia://en/1083721) — a unitary matrix used in [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) to describe the strength of *flavour-changing* weak decays.

:::

::: {#o175 .entry cue=list-entry name="Density matrix"}

- [Density matrix](wikipedia://en/62844) — a matrix describing the statistical state of a quantum system. [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682), [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_matrix) and with [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) 1.

:::

::: {#o176 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fundamental matrix (computer vision)"}

- [Fundamental matrix (computer vision)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_matrix_%28computer_vision%29) — a 3 × 3 matrix in [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) that relates corresponding points in stereo images.

:::

::: {#o177 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fuzzy associative matrix"}

- [Fuzzy associative matrix](wikipedia://en/1028755) — a matrix in [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence), used in machine learning processes.

:::

::: {#o178 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gamma matrices"}

- [Gamma matrices](wikipedia://en/2907966) — 4 × 4 matrices in [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).

:::

::: {#o179 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gell-Mann matrices"}

- [Gell-Mann matrices](wikipedia://en/563694) — a [generalization of the Pauli matrices](wikipedia://en/5277589); these matrices are one notable representation of the [infinitesimal generators](wikipedia://en/17945#The_Lie_algebra_associated_to_a_Lie_group) of the [special unitary group](wikipedia://en/173997) SU(3).

:::

::: {#o180 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hamiltonian matrix"}

- [Hamiltonian matrix](wikipedia://en/7852809) — a matrix used in a variety of fields, including [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) and [linear-quadratic regulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear-quadratic_regulator) (LQR) systems.

:::

::: {#o181 .entry cue=list-entry name="Irregular matrix"}

- [Irregular matrix](wikipedia://en/3366265) — a matrix used in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) which has a varying number of elements in each row.

:::

::: {#o182 .entry cue=list-entry name="Overlap matrix"}

- [Overlap matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlap_matrix) — a type of [Gramian matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramian_matrix), used in [quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry) to describe the inter-relationship of a set of [basis vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_vector) of a [quantum](wikipedia://en/25202) system.

:::

::: {#o183 .entry cue=list-entry name="S matrix"}

- [S matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S_matrix) — a matrix in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) that connects asymptotic (infinite past and future) particle states.

:::

::: {#o184 .entry cue=list-entry name="Scattering matrix"}

- [Scattering matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_matrix) - a matrix in Microwave Engineering that describes how the power move in a multiport system.

:::

::: {#o185 .entry cue=list-entry name="State transition matrix"}

- [State transition matrix](wikipedia://en/19079798) — exponent of state matrix in control systems.

:::

::: {#o186 .entry cue=list-entry name="Substitution matrix"}

- [Substitution matrix](wikipedia://en/363225) — a matrix from [bioinformatics](wikipedia://en/4214), which describes mutation rates of [amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid) or [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) sequences.

:::

::: {#o187 .entry cue=list-entry name="Supnick matrix"}

- [Supnick matrix](wikipedia://en/10573340) — a square matrix used in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science).

:::

::: {#o188 .entry cue=list-entry name="Z-matrix"}

- [Z-matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z-matrix_%28chemistry%29) — a matrix in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), representing a molecule in terms of its relative atomic geometry.

:::

## Other matrix-related terms and definitions {#Other_matrix-related_terms_and_definitions}

::: {#o192 .entry cue=list-entry name="Jordan canonical form"}

- [Jordan canonical form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_canonical_form) — an 'almost' diagonalised matrix, where the only non-zero elements appear on the lead and superdiagonals.

:::

::: {#o193 .entry cue=list-entry name="Linear independence"}

- [Linear independence](wikipedia://en/101863) — two or more [vectors](wikipedia://en/879358) are linearly independent if there is no way to construct one from [linear combinations](wikipedia://en/55632) of the others.

:::

::: {#o194 .entry cue=list-entry name="Matrix exponential"}

- [Matrix exponential](wikipedia://en/672731) — defined by the [exponential series](wikipedia://en/9678#Formal_definition).

:::

::: {#o195 .entry cue=list-entry name="Matrix representation of conic sections"}

- [Matrix representation of conic sections](wikipedia://en/189243)

:::

::: {#o196 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pseudoinverse"}

- [Pseudoinverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoinverse) — a generalization of the [inverse matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_matrix).

:::

::: {#o197 .entry cue=list-entry name="Row echelon form"}

- [Row echelon form](wikipedia://en/330215) — a matrix in this form is the result of applying the *forward elimination* procedure to a matrix (as used in [Gaussian elimination](wikipedia://en/13035)).

:::

::: {#o198 .entry cue=list-entry name="Wronskian"}

- [Wronskian](wikipedia://en/302846) — the determinant of a matrix of functions and their derivatives such that row *n* is the (*n*−1)<sup>th</sup> derivative of row one.

:::
