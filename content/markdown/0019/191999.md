# Tangent vector

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tangent vector"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **tangent vector** is a [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometry%29) that is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) or [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) at a given point. Tangent vectors are described in the [differential geometry of curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_geometry_of_curves) in the context of curves in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. More generally, tangent vectors are elements of a *[tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807)* of a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219). Tangent vectors can also be described in terms of [germs](wikipedia://en/352714). Formally, a tangent vector at the point $x$ is a linear [derivation](wikipedia://en/897658) of the algebra defined by the set of germs at $x$.

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

Before proceeding to a general definition of the tangent vector, we discuss its use in [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and its [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) properties.

### Calculus {#Calculus}

Let $\mathbf{r}(t)$ be a parametric [smooth curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_curve). The tangent vector is given by $\mathbf{r}'(t)$ provided it exists and provided $\mathbf{r}'(t)\neq \mathbf{0}$, where we have used a prime instead of the usual dot to indicate differentiation with respect to parameter $t$.[^o4] The unit tangent vector is given by

$$\mathbf{T}(t) = \frac{\mathbf{r}'(t)}{|\mathbf{r}'(t)|}\,.$$

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

#### Example {#Example}

Given the curve

$$\mathbf{r}(t) = \left\{\left(1+t^2, e^{2t}, \cos{t}\right) \mid t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}$$

in $\mathbb{R}^3$, the unit tangent vector at $t = 0$ is given by

$$\mathbf{T}(0) = \frac{\mathbf{r}'(0)}{\|\mathbf{r}'(0)\|} = \left.\frac{(2t, 2e^{2t}, -\sin{t})}{\sqrt{4t^2 + 4e^{4t} + \sin^2{t}}}\right|_{t=0} = (0,1,0)\,.$$

Where the components of the tangent vector are found by taking the derivative of each corresponding component of the curve with respect to $t$.

:::

### Contravariance {#Contravariance}

If $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is given parametrically in the [*n*-dimensional coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-dimensional_coordinate_system) $x^{i}$ (here we have used superscripts as an index instead of the usual subscript) by $\mathbf{r}(t) = (x^1(t), x^2(t), \ldots, x^n(t))$ or

$$\mathbf{r} = x^i = x^i(t), \quad a\leq t\leq b\,,$$

then the tangent vector field $\mathbf{T} = T^i$ is given by

$$T^i = \frac{dx^i}{dt}\,.$$

Under a change of coordinates

$$u^i = u^i(x^1, x^2, \ldots, x^n), \quad 1\leq i\leq n$$

the tangent vector $\bar{\mathbf{T}} = \bar{T}^i$ in the $u^{i}$-coordinate system is given by

$$\bar{T}^i = \frac{du^i}{dt} = \frac{\partial u^i}{\partial x^s} \frac{dx^s}{dt} = T^s \frac{\partial u^i}{\partial x^s}$$

where we have used the [Einstein summation convention](wikipedia://en/195407). Therefore, a tangent vector of a smooth curve will transform as a [contravariant](wikipedia://en/202886) tensor of order one under a change of coordinates.[^o8]

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be a differentiable function and let $\mathbf{v}$ be a vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$. We define the [directional derivative](wikipedia://en/487859) in the $\mathbf{v}$ direction at a point $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ by

$$\nabla_\mathbf{v} f(\mathbf{x}) = \left.\frac{d}{dt} f(\mathbf{x} + t\mathbf{v})\right|_{t=0} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} v_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(\mathbf{x})\,.$$

The tangent vector at the point $\mathbf{x}$ may then be defined[^o11] as

$$\mathbf{v}(f(\mathbf{x})) \equiv (\nabla_\mathbf{v}(f)) (\mathbf{x})\,.$$

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $f,g:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ be differentiable functions, let $\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}$ be tangent vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ at $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n$, and let $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$. Then

1. $$(a\mathbf{v}+b\mathbf{w})(f)=a\mathbf{v}(f)+b\mathbf{w}(f)$$
1. $$\mathbf{v}(af+bg)=a\mathbf{v}(f)+b\mathbf{v}(g)$$
1. $$\mathbf{v}(fg)=f(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{v}(g)+g(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{v}(f)\,.$$

:::

## Tangent vector on manifolds {#Tangent_vector_on_manifolds}

Let $M$ be a differentiable manifold and let $A(M)$ be the algebra of real-valued differentiable functions on $M$. Then the tangent vector to $M$ at a point $x$ in the manifold is given by the [derivation](wikipedia://en/897658) $D_v:A(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ which shall be linear — i.e., for any $f,g\in A(M)$ and $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ we have

$$D_v(af+bg)=aD_v(f)+bD_v(g)\,.$$

Note that the derivation will by definition have the Leibniz property

$$D_v(f\cdot g)(x)=D_v(f)(x)\cdot g(x)+f(x)\cdot D_v(g)(x)\,.$$
