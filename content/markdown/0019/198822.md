# American Mathematical Society

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="American Mathematical Society"}

The **American Mathematical Society** (**AMS**) is an association of professional [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) dedicated to the interests of [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) research and scholarship, and serves the national and international community through its publications, meetings, advocacy and other programs.

:::

The society is one of the four parts of the [Joint Policy Board for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/4675178) and a member of the [Conference Board of the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/13673454).

## History {#History}

The AMS was founded in 1888 as the **New York Mathematical Society**, the brainchild of [Thomas Fiske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fiske), who was impressed by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) on a visit to England. [John Howard Van Amringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howard_Van_Amringe) became the first president while Fiske became secretary.[^o5] The society soon decided to publish a journal, but ran into some resistance over concerns about competing with the *[American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics)*. The result was the *[Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2668304)*, with Fiske as editor-in-chief. The de facto journal, as intended, was influential in increasing membership. The popularity of the *Bulletin* soon led to the launches of the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)* and *[Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)*, which were also de facto journals.

In 1891, [Charlotte Scott](wikipedia://en/8610998) of Britain became the first woman to join the AMS, then called the New York Mathematical Society.[^o6] The society reorganized under its present name (American Mathematical Society) and became a national society in 1894,[^o7] and that year Scott became the first woman on the first council of the society.[^o8] In 1927 [Anna Pell-Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Johnson_Pell_Wheeler) became the first woman to present a lecture at the society's colloquium.[^o9]

In 1951 there was a southeastern sectional meeting of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee).[^o10][^o11][^o12] The citation delivered at the 2007 MAA awards presentation, where [Lee Lorch](wikipedia://en/8942515) received a standing ovation, recorded that:

[Lee Lorch](wikipedia://en/8942515), the chair of the mathematics department at [Fisk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisk_University), and three Black colleagues, [Evelyn Boyd](wikipedia://en/5827598) (now Granville), Walter Brown, and H. M. Holloway came to the meeting and were able to attend the scientific sessions. However, the organizer for the closing banquet refused to honor the reservations of these four mathematicians. (*Letters in Science*, August 10, 1951, pp. 161–162 spell out the details.) Lorch and his colleagues wrote to the governing bodies of the AMS \[American Mathematical Society\] and [MAA](wikipedia://en/198772) seeking bylaws against discrimination. Bylaws were not changed, but non-discriminatory policies were established and have been strictly observed since then.[^o13][^o14][^o15]

Also in 1951, the American Mathematical Society's headquarters moved from [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to [Providence, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island). The society later added an office in [Ann Arbor, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan), in 1965[^o16] and an office in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), in 1992.

In 1954 the society called for the creation of a new teaching degree, a Doctor of Arts in Mathematics, similar to a PhD but without a research thesis.[^o17]

In the 1970s, as reported in "A Brief History of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698): The Presidents' Perspectives" by [Lenore Blum](wikipedia://en/1884319):

In those years the AMS was governed by what could only be called an "old boys network", closed to all but those in the inner circle. [Mary \[W. Gray](wikipedia://en/17848969)\] challenged that by sitting in on the council meeting in Atlantic City. When she was told she had to leave, she refused saying she would wait until the police came. (Mary relates the story somewhat differently: when she was told she had to leave, she responded she could find no rules in the by-laws restricting attendance at council meetings. She was then told it was by "gentlemen's agreement". Naturally Mary replied "Well, obviously I'm no gentleman.") After that time, council meetings were open to observers and the process of democratization of the society had begun.[^o18]

Also, in 1971 the AMS established its Joint Committee on Women in the Mathematical Sciences (JCW), which later became a joint committee of multiple scholarly societies.[^o19]

[Julia Robinson](wikipedia://en/952987) was the first female president of the American Mathematical Society (1983–1984), but was unable to complete her term as she was suffering from [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia).[^o20]

In 1988, the *[Journal of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)* was created, as the flagship journal of the AMS.

## AMS and mathematical research {#AMS_and_mathematical_research}

The American Mathematical Society plays a significant role in advancing mathematical research by fostering collaboration, supporting early-career researchers, and maintaining influential publications and databases.

### Research collaborations and support {#Research_collaborations_and_support}

The AMS facilitates collaboration among mathematicians through a variety of programs aimed at different career stages. The [Mathematical Research Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Communities), established in 2008, provides early-career researchers with opportunities to engage in intensive research workshops, collaborate with peers, and receive mentoring from senior mathematicians. These programs often lead to the formation of long-term research groups that contribute to emerging fields in mathematics.[^o23]

In addition, the AMS supports [Research Experiences for Undergraduates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Experiences_for_Undergraduates) through advocacy and funding partnerships, ensuring that undergraduate students are exposed to high-level mathematical research.[^o24] The AMS also offers travel grants and [fellowships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellowship) to encourage participation in international conferences and collaborative research projects.[^o25]

### Imprints {#Imprints}

[Chelsea Publishing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Publishing_Company) was acquired in 1997 and continues to publish a portion of the company's original catalog under **AMS Chelsea Publishing**. AMS Chelsea Publishing also publishes new titles.[^o27]

## Influence on policy and education {#Influence_on_policy_and_education}

### Advocacy for mathematics funding {#Advocacy_for_mathematics_funding}

The AMS advocates for federal funding for mathematical research.[^o30] It collaborates with organizations such as the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) to promote funding initiatives. The AMS is also a member of the [Joint Policy Board for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/4675178), which works with policymakers to emphasize the role of mathematics in technological advancements and national security.[^o31]

In partnership with the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), the AMS has contributed to discussions on STEM workforce development and the applications of mathematics in areas such as [cybersecurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybersecurity) and [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904). The society has supported initiatives for stable funding in mathematical research, citing its importance in economic growth and scientific development.[^o32]

## Meetings {#Meetings}

The AMS, along with more than a dozen other organizations, holds the largest annual research mathematics meeting in the world, the [Joint Mathematics Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Mathematics_Meeting), in early January. The 2019 Joint Mathematics Meeting in Baltimore drew approximately 6,000 attendees. Each of the four regional sections of the AMS (Central, Eastern, Southeastern, and Western) holds meetings in the spring and fall of each year. The society also co-sponsors meetings with other international mathematical societies.

## Publications {#Publications}

The AMS publishes *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)*, a database of reviews of mathematical publications, various journals, and books, and maintains the associated online version *[MathSciNet](wikipedia://en/2580683)*. In 1997 the AMS acquired [Chelsea Publishing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Publishing_Company), which it uses as an imprint. In 2017, the AMS acquired MAA Press, the book publishing program of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). The AMS has continued to publish books under the MAA Press imprint.[^o39]
