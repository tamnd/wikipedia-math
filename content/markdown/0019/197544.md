# Pentagram

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pentagram"}

*Image: Five Pointed Star Lined.svg.* Pentagram

A **pentagram** (sometimes known as a pentalpha, pentangle, or star pentagon) is a regular five-pointed [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303), formed from the diagonal [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) of a convex (or simple, or non-self-intersecting) [regular pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon). Drawing a circle around the five points creates a similar symbol referred to as the [pentacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentacle),[^o2] which is used widely by [Wiccans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicca) and in [paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism), or as a sign of life and connections, but there is also an inverted version.

:::

The word *pentagram* comes from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) word πεντάγραμμον (*pentagrammon*),[^o3] from πέντε (*pente*), "five" + γραμμή (*grammē*), "line".[^o4] The word pentagram refers to just the star and the word [pentacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentacle) refers to the star within a circle, although there is some overlap in usage.[^o5] The word *pentalpha* is a 17th-century revival of a post-classical Greek name of the shape.[^o6]

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

Early pentagrams have been found on Sumerian pottery from [Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur) c. 3500 [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCE), and the five-pointed star was at various times the symbol of [Ishtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishtar) or [Marduk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marduk).[^o9][^o10]

*Image: Hugieia-pentagram.svg.* A Pythagorean "Hugieia Pentagram"[^o11]

*Image: Right-handed interlaced pentagram.svg.* A right-handed interlaced pentagram, popular with Wiccans and some other neo-pagans. The [Flag of Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Morocco) often bears the left-handed version.

[Flinders Petrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flinders_Petrie) mentioned that the pentagram started to appear as a decoration on [Predynastic Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predynastic_Egyptian) pottery at around "Sequence Date" 64 in his system, corresponding to the early [Naqada III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naqada_III) era.[^o12]

*Image: Japanese Crest Abe no Seimei Hann.svg.* [Abe no Seimei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abe_no_Seimei)'s pentagram [mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon_%28emblem%29) represents the [Wu Xing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxing_%28Chinese_philosophy%29).

Pentagram symbols from about 5,000 years ago were found in the [Liangzhu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangzhu_culture) of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China).[^o13][^o14] A pentagram appeared in a Chinese text on music theory from the [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period) (c. 475 – 221 BCE) as a diagram of the mathematical relations between the five notes in a particular [Chinese musical scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_musicology#Music_scales).[^o14]

The pentagram was known to the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks), with a depiction on a vase possibly dating back to the 7th century BCE.[^o15] [Pythagoreanism](wikipedia://en/291170) originated in the 6th century BCE and used the pentagram as a symbol of mutual recognition, well-being, and good deeds and charity.[^o16]

During 300–150 BCE, preceding the [Hasmonean period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_period), the pentagram was the symbol of the [Judahite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judahite) administration of [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem), the seal of which was denoted with the [Hebrew letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_letters) ירשלם (he).[^o17] In [Modern Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Hebrew), Jerusalem is spelled יְרוּשָׁלַיִם (he).

In the [Serer religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_religion) and the [Serer creation myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_creation_myth), the pentagram, called srr (also spelled *Yoonir*) in the [Serer language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_language), is the symbol of the [Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe).[^o18][^o19] The Serer religion posits that the peak of the five-pointed star represents the Serer [creator deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity), [Roog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roog). The other four points represent the cardinal points of the Universe. The crossing of the lines ("bottom left" and "top right" and "top left and bottom right") pinpoints the axis of the Universe, through which all energies pass. The top point is "the point of departure and conclusion, the origin and the end".[^o20] *Yoonir* also represents "good fortune and destiny"—in a Serer religious sense, and in an ethno-nationalistic sense—following centuries of [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_persecution#Persecution_of_Serers) and [ethnic persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_persecution), it also represents the [Serer people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_people),[^o20][^o21] an ethnoreligious group with members in [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal), [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia), and [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania).[^o22][^o23][^o24][^o25][^o26] The Serer have a detailed [pictorial representation of the Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_creation_myth#Representation_of_the_Universe), representing the three worlds in Serer primordial time: the invisible world, the terrestrial world, and the nocturnal world.[^o27]

### Western symbolism {#Western_symbolism}

#### Middle Ages {#Middle_Ages}

The pentagram was used in [Christendom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christendom) during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) as a symbol for the [five senses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_senses) and of the [Five Holy Wounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Holy_Wounds) of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_in_Christianity).[^o30] The pentagram is featured with a symbolic role in the 14th-century English poem *[Sir Gawain and the Green Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Gawain_and_the_Green_Knight)*, in which the symbol decorates the shield of the hero, [Gawain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gawain). The unnamed [Gawain Poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gawain_Poet) credits the symbol's origin to [King Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Solomon), explaining that each of the five interconnected points represents a virtue tied to a group of five: Gawain is perfect in his five senses and five fingers; faithful to the Five Holy Wounds; takes courage from the [five joys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary) of [Mary, mother of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus); and exemplifies the five virtues of [knighthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight),[^o31] which are generosity, friendship, chastity, chivalry, and piety.[^o32]

*Image: Amiens Rose Nord 1.jpg.* The North rose of [Amiens Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens_Cathedral)

The north-facing rose of [Amiens Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens_Cathedral)—built in the 13th century—exhibits a pentagram-based motif. Some sources interpret the unusual downward-pointing star as symbolizing the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity) descending on celebrants.

#### Renaissance {#Renaissance}

[Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Cornelius_Agrippa) and others perpetuated the pentagram's popularity as a magic symbol, attributing the five neoplatonic elements to its five points, in typical Renaissance fashion. Agrippa depicts the human body inscribed in an 'upright' (point-up) pentagram and another with its hands in rotated pentagrams, among numerous other geometrical figures, in the section on 'the proportions and harmonious measures of the human body', and an 'inverted' (point-down) version of the Pythagorean 'hygeia' pentagram in the section on 'characters, received only by revelation, which no other kind of reasoning can discover', alongside variations of the [Chi-Rho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi-Rho) and the Hebrew word [Makabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_Maccabeus#Origin_of_the_name_"The_Hammer").

'Of this type are the signet shown to [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I), which most people called a cross, inscribed in Latin letters, '[in this conquer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_hoc_signo_vinces)', and another revealed to [Antiochus who was surnamed Soteris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochus_I_Soter), in the shape of a pentagon, which issued health, for resolved into letters, it issued the word ὑγίεα, that is, 'health', in the confidence and virtue of which signs, each of the kings won a notable victory against their enemies. Thus Judas, who for this reason was afterwards known as Maccabeus, was about to fight with the Jews against Antiochus Eupatorus, and received that noble seal מׄכׄבׄיׄ from the angel'.[^o34]

#### Romanticism {#Romanticism}

By the mid-19th century, a further distinction had developed amongst occultists regarding the pentagram's orientation. With a single point upward, it depicted the spirit presiding over the four elements of matter and was essentially "good". However, the influential but controversial writer [Éliphas Lévi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éliphas_Lévi), known for believing that magic was a real science, had called it evil whenever the symbol appeared the other way up:

- "A reversed pentagram, with two points projecting upwards, is a symbol of evil and attracts sinister forces because it overturns the proper order of things and demonstrates the triumph of matter over spirit. It is the goat of lust attacking the heavens with its horns, a sign execrated by initiates."[^o36]
- "The flaming star, which, when turned upside down, is the heirol sign of the goat of [black magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_magic), whose head may be drawn in the star, the two horns at the top, the ears to the right and left, the beard at the bottom. It is a sign of antagonism and fatality. It is the goat of lust attacking the heavens with its horns."[^o37]
- "Let us keep the figure of the Five-pointed Star always upright, with the topmost triangle pointing to heaven, for it is the seat of wisdom, and if the figure is reversed, perversion and evil will be the result."[^o38]

The [apotropaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apotropaic) (protective) use in [German folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_folklore) of the pentagram symbol (called *[Drudenfuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drudenfuss)* in German) is referred to by [Goethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe) in *[Faust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faust,_Part_One)* (1808), where a pentagram prevents [Mephistopheles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mephistopheles) from leaving a room (but did not prevent him from entering by the same way, as the outward pointing corner of the diagram happened to be imperfectly drawn):

Mephistopheles:

I must confess, I'm prevented though

By a little thing that hinders me,

The Druid's-foot on your doorsill– Faust:

The Pentagram gives you pain?

Then tell me, you Son of Hell,

If that's the case, how did you gain

Entry? Are spirits like you cheated?

Mephistopheles:

Look carefully! It's not completed:

One angle, if you inspect it closely

Has, as you see, been left a little open.[^o39]

Also protective is the use in [Icelandic folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_folklore) of a gestured or carved rather than painted pentagram (called smèrhnút in Icelandic), according to 19th century folklorist [Jón Árnason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jón_Árnason_%28author%29):[^o40]

A butter that comes from the fake vomit is called a fake butter; it looks like any other butter; but if one makes a sign of a cross over it, or carves a cross on it, or a figure called a buttermilk-knot,<sup>\*</sup> it all explodes into small pieces and becomes like a grain of dross, so that nothing remains of it, except only particles, or it subsides like foam. Therefore it seems more prudent, if a person is offered a horrible butter to eat, or as a fee,[^o41] to make either mark on it, because a fake butter cannot withstand either a cross mark or a butter-knot.

### Uses in modern occultism {#Uses_in_modern_occultism}

Based on Renaissance-era occultism, the pentagram entered the symbolism of modern occultists. Its primary use is a continuation of the ancient Babylonian use of the pentagram as an [apotropaic charm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apotropaic_charm) to protect against evil forces.[^o43] Éliphas Lévi claimed that "The Pentagram expresses the mind's domination over the elements and it is by this sign that we bind the demons of the air, the spirits of fire, the spectres of water, and the ghosts of earth."[^o44] In this spirit, the [Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermetic_Order_of_the_Golden_Dawn) developed the use of the pentagram in the [lesser banishing ritual of the pentagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_banishing_ritual_of_the_pentagram), which is still used to this day by those who practice Golden Dawn-type magic.

[Aleister Crowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleister_Crowley) made use of the pentagram in the system of [magick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magick) used in [Thelema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thelema): an adverse or inverted pentagram represents the descent of spirit into matter, according to the interpretation of [Lon Milo DuQuette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lon_Milo_DuQuette).[^o45] Crowley contradicted his old comrades in the [Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermetic_Order_of_the_Golden_Dawn), who, following Levi, considered this orientation of the symbol [evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil) and associated it with the triumph of matter over spirit.

### Use in new religious movements {#Use_in_new_religious_movements}

#### Baháʼí Faith {#Baháʼí_Faith}

*Image: Bab-Star-Tablet-Haykal.jpg.* Haykal by the [Báb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Báb) written in his own hand

The five-pointed star is a symbol of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_Faith).[^o48][^o49] In the Baháʼí Faith, the star is known as the *Haykal* ("temple"), and it was initiated and established by the [Báb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Báb). The Báb and [Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahá'u'lláh) wrote various works in the form of a pentagram.[^o50][^o51]

#### The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints {#The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints}

[The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) is theorized to have begun using both upright and inverted five-pointed stars in [Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_%28Latter_Day_Saints%29) architecture, dating from the [Nauvoo Illinois Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauvoo_Illinois_Temple) dedicated on 30 April 1846.[^o53] Other temples decorated with five-pointed stars in both orientations include the [Salt Lake Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_Temple) and the [Logan Utah Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Utah_Temple). These usages come from the symbolism found in [Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation) chapter 12: "And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars."[^o54]

#### Wicca {#Wicca}

Because of a perceived association with Satanism and occultism, many United States schools in the late 1990s sought to prevent students from displaying the pentagram on clothing or jewelry.[^o56] In public schools, such actions by administrators were determined in 2000 to be in violation of students' [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) right to [free exercise of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Exercise_Clause).[^o57]

The encircled pentagram (referred to as a [pentacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentacle) by the plaintiffs) was added to the list of 38 approved religious symbols to be placed on the tombstones of fallen service members at [Arlington National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_National_Cemetery) on 24 April 2007. The decision was made following ten applications from families of fallen soldiers who practiced [Wicca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicca). The government paid the families 225,000 to settle their pending lawsuits.[^o58][^o59]

#### Satanism {#Satanism}

The inverted pentagram is broadly used in [Satanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satanism), sometimes depicted with the [goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat)'s head of [Baphomet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baphomet), as popularized by the [Church of Satan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Satan) since 1968. [LaVeyan Satanists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaVeyan_Satanism) pair the goat head with Hebrew letters at the five points of the pentagram to form the [Sigil of Baphomet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigil_of_Baphomet). The Baphomet sigil was adapted for the [Joy of Satan Ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_of_Satan_Ministries) logo, using [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform) characters at the five points of the pentagram, reflecting the shape's earliest use in [Sumeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumeria). The inverted pentagram also appears in [The Satanic Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Satanic_Temple) logo, with an alternative depiction of Baphomet's head. Other depictions of the Satanic goat's head resemble the inverted pentagram without its explicit outline.

:::

#### Serer religion {#Serer_religion}

The five-pointed star is a symbol of the [Serer religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_religion) and the [Serer people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_people) of West Africa. Called *Yoonir* in [their language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_language), it symbolizes the universe in the [Serer creation myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serer_creation_myth), and also represents the star [Sirius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius).[^o64][^o65]

- A pentagram is featured on the flag of the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) city of [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki), as well on its emblem.

## Geometry {#Geometry}

*Image: Stellamerlata.gif.* [Koch snowflakes](wikipedia://en/46959) drawn with [MSWLogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSWLogo) (in Tartapelago[^o69])

The pentagram is the simplest [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303). The pentagram contains ten points (the five points of the star, and the five vertices of the inner pentagon) and fifteen line segments. It is represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{5/2\}$. Like a regular pentagon, and a regular pentagon with a pentagram constructed inside it, the regular pentagram has as its [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of order 10.

It can be seen as a net of a [pentagonal pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128461) although with isosceles triangles.

::: {#o71 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

The pentagram can be constructed by connecting alternate vertices of a [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014); see [details of the construction](wikipedia://en/20741014#Construction_of_a_regular_pentagon). It can also be constructed as a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of a pentagon, by extending the edges of a pentagon until the lines intersect.

:::

### Golden ratio {#Golden_ratio}

The [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386), $\varphi = \tfrac12\bigl(1 + \sqrt5~\!) = 2\cos 36^\circ,$ plays an important role in regular pentagons and pentagrams. Each intersection of edges sections the edges in the golden ratio: the ratio of the length of the edge to the longer segment is , as is the length of the longer segment to the shorter. Also, the ratio of the length of the shorter segment to the segment bounded by the two intersecting edges (a side of the pentagon in the pentagram's center) is . As the four-color illustration shows:

$$\frac{\color{red}\text{red}}{\color{green}\text{green}} = \frac{\color{green}\text{green}}{\color{blue}\text{blue}} = \frac{\color{blue}\text{blue}}{\color{magenta}\text{magenta}} = \varphi.$$

The pentagram includes ten [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428): five [acute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_triangle) and five [obtuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_triangle) isosceles triangles. In all of them, the ratio of the longer side to the shorter side is . The acute triangles are [golden triangles](wikipedia://en/4525417). The obtuse isosceles triangle highlighted via the colored lines in the illustration is a [golden gnomon](wikipedia://en/4525417).

### Trigonometric values {#Trigonometric_values}

*Image: Pentagram Angles Notation.svg.* Drawing of a pentagram with notations of its angles.

$$\begin{aligned} \sin \tfrac15 \pi & = \sin 36^\circ = \tfrac14 \sqrt{2(5 - \sqrt 5)} = \tfrac12 \sqrt{ \varphi^{-1}\sqrt5 } \\[5pt] \cos \tfrac15 \pi & = \cos 36^\circ = \tfrac14 \bigl(\sqrt 5+1\bigr) = \tfrac12 \varphi \\[5pt] \sin \tfrac25 \pi & = \sin 72^\circ = \tfrac14 \sqrt{2(5 + \sqrt 5)} = \tfrac12 \sqrt{\varphi\sqrt5 } \\[5pt] \cos \tfrac25 \pi & = \cos 72^\circ = \tfrac14 \bigl(\sqrt 5 - 1\bigr)= \tfrac12 \varphi^{-1} \\[5pt] \end{aligned}$$

As a result, in an isosceles triangle with one or two angles of 36°, the longer of the two side lengths is times that of the shorter of the two, both in the case of the acute as in the case of the obtuse triangle.

### Spherical pentagram {#Spherical_pentagram}

A pentagram can be drawn as a [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303) on a sphere, composed of five great circle arcs, whose all internal angles are right angles. This shape was described by [John Napier](wikipedia://en/15993) in his 1614 book *Mirifici logarithmorum canonis descriptio* (Description of the wonderful rule of logarithms) along with rules that link the values of trigonometric functions of five parts of a right spherical triangle (two angles and three sides). It was studied later by [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125).

## Pentagram of Venus {#Pentagram_of_Venus}

*Image: Venus geocentric orbit curve simplified Line (pentagram).svg.* The pentagram of Venus

The pentagram of Venus is the apparent path of the [planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus) as observed from [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228). Successive [inferior conjunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunction_%28astronomy%29#Superior_and_inferior) of Venus repeat with an [orbital resonance](wikipedia://en/22444) of approximately 13:8—that is, Venus orbits the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) approximately 13 times for every eight orbits of Earth—shifting 144° at each inferior conjunction.[^o81] The tips of the five loops at the center of the figure have the same geometric relationship to one another as the five [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), or points, of a pentagram, and each group of five [intersections](wikipedia://en/41780044) [equidistant](wikipedia://en/1532579) from the figure's center have the same geometric relationship.

:::
