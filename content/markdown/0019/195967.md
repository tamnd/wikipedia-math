# Relaxation oscillator

*Image: Animated schmitt-trigger-oscillator.gif.* Simple relaxation oscillator made by [feeding back](wikipedia://en/11545) an inverting [Schmitt trigger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmitt_trigger)'s output voltage through a [RC network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_network) to its input.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Relaxation oscillator"}

In [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), a **relaxation oscillator** is a [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_circuit) [electronic oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_oscillator) circuit that produces a [nonsinusoidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-sinusoidal_waveform) repetitive output signal, such as a [triangle wave](wikipedia://en/52151) or [square wave](wikipedia://en/314652).[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] The circuit consists of a [feedback loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback_loop) containing a switching device such as a [transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor), [comparator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparator), [relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relay),[^o6] [op amp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op_amp), or a [negative resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_resistance) device like a [tunnel diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_diode), that repetitively charges a [capacitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor) or [inductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductor) through a resistance until it reaches a threshold level, then discharges it again.[^o5][^o7] The [period](wikipedia://en/10779) of the oscillator depends on the [time constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_constant) of the capacitor or inductor circuit.[^o3] The active device switches abruptly between charging and discharging modes, and thus produces a discontinuously changing repetitive waveform.[^o3][^o5] This contrasts with the other type of electronic oscillator, the harmonic or [linear oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_oscillator#linear_oscillator), which uses an [amplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplifier) with feedback to excite [resonant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonant) oscillations in a [resonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonator), producing a [sine wave](wikipedia://en/324749).[^o8]

:::

*Image: Turnsignals On.jpg.* The blinking [turn signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_signal) on some motor vehicles is generated by a simple relaxation oscillator powering a [relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relay).

Relaxation oscillators may be used for a wide range of frequencies, but as they are one of the oscillator types suited to low frequencies, below audio, they are typically used for applications such as blinking lights ([turn signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_lighting)) and [electronic beepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzzer), as well as [voltage-controlled oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage-controlled_oscillator) (VCOs), [inverters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverter), [switching power supplies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switching_power_supply), [dual-slope analog to digital converters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-slope_ADC), and [function generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_generator).

The term *relaxation oscillator*, though often used in electronics engineering, is also applied to [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) in many diverse areas of science that produce nonlinear oscillations and can be analyzed using the same mathematical model as electronic relaxation oscillators.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12] For example, geothermal [geysers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geyser),[^o13][^o14] networks of firing [nerve cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_cell),[^o12] [thermostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermostat) controlled heating systems,[^o15] coupled chemical reactions,[^o10] the beating human heart,[^o12][^o15] earthquakes,[^o13] the squeaking of chalk on a blackboard,[^o15] the cyclic populations of predator and prey animals, and [gene activation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_activation) systems[^o10] have been modeled as relaxation oscillators. Relaxation oscillations are characterized by two alternating processes on different time scales: a long [relaxation](wikipedia://en/1780263) period during which the system approaches an [equilibrium point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_point), alternating with a short impulsive period in which the equilibrium point shifts.[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o16] The [period](wikipedia://en/10779) of a relaxation oscillator is mainly determined by the [relaxation time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relaxation_time) constant.[^o12] Relaxation oscillations are a type of [limit cycle](wikipedia://en/452950) and are studied in [nonlinear control](wikipedia://en/3359147) theory.[^o17]

## Electronic relaxation oscillators {#Electronic_relaxation_oscillators}

*Image: Vacuum tube multivibrator calibrating wavemeter 1920.jpg.* A vacuum tube Abraham-Bloch multivibrator relaxation oscillator, France, 1920 *(small box, left)*. Its harmonics are being used to calibrate a wavemeter *(center)*.

*Image: Original Abraham-Bloch multivibrator circuit.png.* Original [vacuum tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_tube) Abraham-Bloch multivibrator oscillator, from their 1919 paper

The first relaxation oscillator circuit, the [astable multivibrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astable_multivibrator), was invented by [Henri Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Abraham) and [Eugene Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Bloch) using [vacuum tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_tube) during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I).[^o19][^o20] [Balthasar van der Pol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balthasar_van_der_Pol) first distinguished relaxation oscillations from harmonic oscillations, originated the term "relaxation oscillator", and derived the first mathematical model of a relaxation oscillator, the influential [Van der Pol oscillator](wikipedia://en/1961786) model, in 1920.[^o20][^o21][^o22] Van der Pol borrowed the term *[relaxation](wikipedia://en/1780263)* from mechanics; the discharge of the capacitor is analogous to the process of *[stress relaxation](wikipedia://en/5202789)*, the gradual disappearance of deformation and return to equilibrium in an [inelastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inelastic) medium.[^o23] Relaxation oscillators can be divided into two classes[^o14]

- ***Sawtooth, sweep, or flyback oscillator***: In this type the energy storage capacitor is charged slowly but discharged rapidly, essentially instantly, by a short circuit through the switching device. Thus there is only one "ramp" in the output waveform which takes up virtually the entire period. The voltage across the capacitor approximates a [sawtooth wave](wikipedia://en/52147), while the current through the switching device is a sequence of short pulses.
- ***[Astable multivibrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astable_multivibrator)***: In this type the capacitor is both charged and discharged slowly through a resistor, so the output waveform consists of two parts, an increasing ramp and a decreasing ramp. The voltage across the capacitor approximates a [triangle waveform](wikipedia://en/52151), while the current through the switching device approximates a square wave.

Before the advent of microelectronics, simple relaxation oscillators often used a [negative resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_resistance) device with [hysteresis](wikipedia://en/147003) such as a [thyratron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyratron) tube,[^o26] [neon lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_lamp),[^o26] or [unijunction transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unijunction_transistor), however today they are more often built with dedicated integrated circuits such as the [555 timer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/555_timer_IC) chip.

::: {#o25 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

### Applications {#Applications}

Relaxation oscillators are generally used to produce low-[frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) signals for such applications as blinking lights and electronic beepers. During the vacuum tube era they were used as oscillators in electronic organs and horizontal deflection circuits and time bases for [CRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube) [oscilloscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscilloscope); one of the most common was the Miller integrator circuit invented by [Alan Blumlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Blumlein), which used vacuum tubes as a constant current source to produce a very linear ramp.[^o26] They are also used in [voltage-controlled oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage-controlled_oscillator) (VCOs),[^o27] [inverters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverter_%28electrical%29) and [switching power supplies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switching_power_supply), [dual-slope analog to digital converters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-slope_ADC), and in [function generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_generator) to produce square and triangle waves. Relaxation oscillators are widely used because they are easier to design than linear oscillators, are easier to fabricate on [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) chips because they do not require inductors like LC oscillators,[^o27][^o28] and can be tuned over a wide frequency range.[^o28] However they have more [phase noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_noise)[^o27] and poorer [frequency stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_stability) than linear oscillators.[^o3][^o27]

:::

## Pearson–Anson oscillator {#Pearson–Anson_oscillator}

*Image: NeonBulbRelaxationOscillator.svg.* [Circuit diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_diagram) of a capacitive relaxation oscillator with a neon lamp threshold device

This example can be implemented with a [capacitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor) or [resistive-capacitive integrating circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_circuit) driven respectively by a constant [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_source) or [voltage source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_source), and a threshold device with [hysteresis](wikipedia://en/147003) ([neon lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_lamp), [thyratron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyratron), [diac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diac), reverse-biased [bipolar transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_transistor),[^o30] or [unijunction transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unijunction_transistor)) connected in parallel to the capacitor. The capacitor is charged by the input source causing the voltage across the capacitor to rise. The threshold device does not conduct at all until the capacitor voltage reaches its threshold (trigger) voltage. It then increases heavily its conductance in an avalanche-like manner because of the inherent positive feedback, which quickly discharges the capacitor. When the voltage across the capacitor drops to some lower threshold voltage, the device stops conducting and the capacitor begins charging again, and the cycle repeats [ad infinitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_infinitum).

If the threshold element is a [neon lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_lamp),[^o31][^o32] the circuit also provides a flash of light with each discharge of the capacitor. This lamp example is depicted below in the typical circuit used to describe the [Pearson&ndash;Anson effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson&ndash;Anson_effect). The discharging duration can be extended by connecting an additional resistor in series to the threshold element. The two resistors form a voltage divider; so, the additional resistor has to have low enough resistance to reach the low threshold.

### Alternative implementation with 555 timer {#Alternative_implementation_with_555_timer}

A similar relaxation oscillator can be built with a [555 timer IC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/555_timer_IC) (acting in astable mode) that takes the place of the neon bulb above. That is, when a chosen capacitor is charged to a design value, (e.g., 2/3 of the power supply voltage) [comparators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparator) within the 555 timer flip a transistor switch that gradually discharges that capacitor through a chosen resistor (which determine the RC time constant) to ground. At the instant the capacitor falls to a sufficiently low value (e.g., 1/3 of the power supply voltage), the switch flips to let the capacitor charge up again. The popular 555's comparator design permits accurate operation with any supply from 5 to 15 volts or even wider.

Other, non-comparator oscillators may have unwanted timing changes if the supply voltage changes.

## Inductive oscillator {#Inductive_oscillator}

*Image: blocking oscillator.jpg.* Basis of solid-state Blocking oscillator

A [blocking oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blocking_oscillator) using the inductive properties of a pulse [transformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer) to generate square waves by driving the transformer into saturation, which then cuts the transformer supply current until the transformer unloads and desaturates, which then triggers another pulse of supply current, generally using a single transistor as the switching element.

## Comparator–based relaxation oscillator {#Comparator–based_relaxation_oscillator}

Alternatively, when the capacitor reaches each threshold, the charging source can be switched from the positive power supply to the negative power supply or vice versa. The earlier inverting [Schmitt trigger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmitt_trigger) animated example operates on the same principle (since the Schmitt trigger internally performs comparison). This section will analyze a similar implementation using a [comparator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparator) as a discrete component.

*Image: OpAmpHystereticOscillator.svg.* A comparator-based hysteretic oscillator.

This relaxation oscillator is a hysteretic oscillator, named this way because of the [hysteresis](wikipedia://en/147003) created by the [positive feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_feedback) loop implemented with the [comparator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparator) (similar to an [operational amplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_amplifier)). A circuit that implements this form of hysteretic switching is known as a [Schmitt trigger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmitt_trigger). Alone, the trigger is a [bistable multivibrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistable_multivibrator). However, the slow [negative feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_feedback) added to the trigger by the RC circuit causes the circuit to oscillate automatically. That is, the addition of the RC circuit turns the hysteretic bistable [multivibrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivibrator) into an [astable multivibrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astable_multivibrator).

### General concept {#General_concept}

The system is in unstable equilibrium if both of the inputs and the output of the comparator are at zero volts. The moment any sort of noise, be it thermal or [electromagnetic](wikipedia://en/9426) [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise) brings the output of the comparator above zero (the case of the comparator output going below zero is also possible, and a similar argument to what follows applies), the positive feedback in the comparator results in the output of the comparator saturating at the positive rail.

In other words, because the output of the comparator is now positive, the non-inverting input to the comparator is also positive, and continues to increase as the output increases, due to the [voltage divider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_divider). After a short time, the output of the comparator is the positive voltage rail, $V_{DD}$.

*Image: Series-RC.svg.* Series RC Circuit

The inverting input and the output of the comparator are linked by a [series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_and_parallel_circuits#Series_circuits) [RC circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_circuit). Because of this, the inverting input of the comparator asymptotically approaches the comparator output voltage with a [time constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_constant) RC. At the point where voltage at the inverting input is greater than the non-inverting input, the output of the comparator falls quickly due to positive feedback.

This is because the non-inverting input is less than the inverting input, and as the output continues to decrease, the difference between the inputs gets more and more negative. Again, the inverting input approaches the comparator's output voltage asymptotically, and the cycle repeats itself once the non-inverting input is greater than the inverting input, hence the system oscillates.

### Example: Differential equation analysis of a comparator-based relaxation oscillator {#Example:_Differential_equation_analysis_of_a_comparator-based_relaxation_oscillator}

*Image: opamprelaxationoscillator.svg.* Transient analysis of a comparator-based relaxation oscillator.

$V_+$ is set by $V_\text{out}$ across a resistive [voltage divider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_divider):

$$V_+ = \frac{V_\text{out}}{2}$$

$V_-$ is obtained using [Ohm's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm's_law) and the [capacitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor) [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309):

$$\frac{V_\text{out}-V_-}{R} = C\frac{dV_-}{dt}$$

Rearranging the $V_-$ differential equation into standard form results in the following:

$$\frac{dV_-}{dt}+\frac{V_-}{RC}=\frac{V_\text{out}}{RC}$$

Notice there are two solutions to the differential equation, the driven or particular solution and the homogeneous solution. Solving for the driven solution, observe that for this particular form, the solution is a constant. In other words, $\, \! V_-=A$ where A is a constant and $\frac{dV_-}{dt}=0$.

$$\frac{A}{RC}=\frac{V_\text{out}}{RC}$$

$$A=V_\text{out}$$

Using the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) to solve the [homogeneous equation](wikipedia://en/1698977) $\frac{dV_-}{dt}+\frac{V_-}{RC}=0$ results in

$$V_-=Be^{\frac{-1}{RC}t}$$

$V_-$ is the sum of the particular and homogeneous solution.

$$V_- = A+Be^{\frac{-1}{RC}t}$$

$$V_- = V_\text{out}+Be^{\frac{-1}{RC}t}$$

Solving for B requires evaluation of the initial conditions. At time 0, $V_\text{out}=V_{dd}$ and $V_-=0$. Substituting into our previous equation,

$$0 = V_{dd}+B$$

$$B = -V_{dd}$$

#### Frequency of oscillation {#Frequency_of_oscillation}

First let's assume that $V_{dd} = -V_{ss}$ for ease of calculation. Ignoring the initial charge up of the capacitor, which is irrelevant for calculations of the frequency, note that charges and discharges oscillate between $\frac{V_{dd}}{2}$ and $\frac{V_{ss}}{2}$. For the circuit above, V<sub>ss</sub> must be less than 0. Half of the period (T) is the same as time that $V_\text{out}$ switches from V<sub>dd</sub>. This occurs when V<sub>−</sub> charges up from $-\frac{V_{dd}}{2}$ to $\frac{V_{dd}}{2}$.

$$V_- = A+Be^{\frac{-1}{RC}t}$$

$$\frac{V_{dd}}{2}=V_{dd}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}e^{\frac{-1}{RC}\frac{T}{2}}\right)$$

$$\frac{1}{3}=e^{\frac{-1}{RC}\frac{T}{2}}$$

$$\ln\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{-1}{RC}\frac{T}{2}$$

$$T=2\ln(3)RC$$

$$f=\frac{1}{2\ln(3)RC}$$

When V<sub>ss</sub> is not the inverse of V<sub>dd</sub> we need to worry about asymmetric charge up and discharge times. Taking this into account we end up with a formula of the form:

$$T = (RC) \left[\ln\left( \frac{2V_{ss}-V_{dd}}{V_{ss}}\right) + \ln\left( \frac{2V_{dd}-V_{ss}}{V_{dd}} \right) \right]$$

Which reduces to the above result in the case that $V_{dd} = -V_{ss}$.
