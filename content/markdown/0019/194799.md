# Helge von Koch

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Helge von Koch"}

**Niels Fabian Helge von Koch** (; 25 January 1870 – 11 March 1924) was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who gave his name to the famous [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) known as the [Koch snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959), one of the earliest fractal curves to be described.

:::

He was born to [Swedish nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_nobility). His grandfather, [Nils Samuel von Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nils_Samuel_von_Koch) (1801–1881), was the [Chancellor of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Justice_%28Sweden%29). His father, [Richert Vogt von Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richert_Vogt_von_Koch) (1838–1913) was a [Lieutenant-Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-Colonel) in the [Life Guards of Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Guards_of_Horse) of the [Swedish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Army). He was enrolled at the newly created [Stockholm University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University_College) in 1887 (studying under [Gösta Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/1438792)), and at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) in 1888, where he also received his bachelor's degree (*filosofie kandidat*) since the non-governmental college in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) had not yet received the rights to issue degrees. He received his PhD in Uppsala in 1892. He was appointed professor of mathematics at the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) in Stockholm in 1905, succeeding [Ivar Bendixson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivar_Bendixson), and became professor of pure mathematics at Stockholm University College in 1911.

Von Koch wrote several papers on [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). One of his results was a 1901 theorem proving that the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) implies what is now known to be the strongest possible form of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).[^o3]

He described the [Koch curve](wikipedia://en/46959) in a 1904 paper entitled Sur une courbe continue sans tangente, obtenue par une construction géométrique élémentaire ("On a continuous curve without tangents constructible from elementary geometry").[^o4]

He was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1900 in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) with talk Sur la distribution des nombres premiers ("On the distribution of prime numbers")[^o5] and in 1912 in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), England, with talk *On regular and irregular solutions of some infinite systems of linear equations*.[^o6]
