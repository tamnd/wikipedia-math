# Jerzy Różycki

*Image: Fot. ES ławeczka Jerzego Różyckiego w Wyszkowie.jpg.* Jerzy Różycki memorial bench in [Wyszków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyszków), near [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jerzy Różycki"}

**Jerzy Witold Różycki** (; 24 July 1909 – 9 January 1942) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [cryptologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptologist) who worked at breaking German [Enigma-machine](wikipedia://en/9256) [ciphers](wikipedia://en/5244) before and during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Różycki was born in what is now [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine), the fourth and youngest child of Zygmunt Różycki, a pharmacist and graduate of [Saint Petersburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_University), and Wanda, née Benita. He attended a Polish school in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv) before moving with his family to Poland in 1918. In 1926 he completed secondary school at [Wyszków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyszków) on eastern Poland's [Bug River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_River).

Różycki studied mathematics from 1927 to 1932 in western Poland, at [Poznań University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań_University)'s Mathematics Institute, graduating with a master's degree on February 19, 1932. He would later earn a second master's degree from Poznań University, in [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography), on December 13, 1937.

In 1929, while still a student, Różycki, proficient in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), was one of twenty-odd Poznań University mathematics students who accepted an invitation to attend a secret [cryptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptology) course organized at a nearby military installation by the Polish [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff)'s [Cipher Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuro_Szyfrów), headquartered in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw).

From September 1932, Różycki served as a civilian cryptologist with the [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff)'s [Cipher Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuro_Szyfrów), housed till 1937 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw)'s [Saxon Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pałac_Saski). He worked there together with fellow [Poznań University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań_University) mathematics alumni and Cipher Bureau cryptology-course graduates [Marian Rejewski](wikipedia://en/4349420) and [Henryk Zygalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Zygalski).

After Rejewski had reconstructed the German military [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256) in December 1932, Różycki and Zygalski likewise worked at ongoing development of methods and equipment to exploit Enigma [decryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decryption) as a source of [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_espionage). Różycki invented the "clock" method, which sometimes made it possible to determine which of the machine's [rotors](wikipedia://en/196223) was at the far right, that is, in the position where the rotor always revolved at every depression of a key.

On 9 January 1942, Różycki perished in the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea) while returning to the [Cadix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadix) center, near [Uzès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzès) in [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France), from a stint at Cadix's branch office at the Château Couba on the outskirts of [Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers). His passenger ship, the *[SS Lamoricière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Lamoricière)*, sank in unclear circumstances near the [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands).[^o4] Fellow victims of the disaster, among the 222 passengers killed, included [Piotr Smoleński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Smoleński) and Captain [Jan Graliński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Graliński), of the prewar [Polish Cipher Bureau](wikipedia://en/625326)'s Russian section, and a French officer accompanying the three Poles, Captain François Lane.

Różycki has a [cenotaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenotaph) (symbolic grave) in the National Pantheon at the [Saints Peter and Paul Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Peter_and_Paul_Church,_Kraków) in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków), Poland.[^o5]

## Family {#Family}

In 1938, aged 29, Różycki married Maria Barbara Mayka. Their son, [Janusz Różycki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janusz_Różycki), born May 10, 1939, completed his studies at the [Academy of Fine Arts in Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Fine_Arts_in_Warsaw) and went on to be a member of the Polish [fencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fencing_%28sport%29) team that won a silver medal at the [1964 Tokyo Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

*Image: 2023-06-15 Cryptologist Monument Różycki.JPG.* 2007 monument to cryptologists [Rejewski](wikipedia://en/4349420), Różycki, and [Zygalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Zygalski) before the [Imperial Castle in Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Castle_in_Poznań)

In 1980 Różycki and his two mathematician colleagues were among the heroes of *Sekret Enigmy* (The Enigma Secret), a Polish [thriller film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_%28genre%29) about their solution of the German Enigma cipher. Late 1980 also saw a similarly-themed Polish [TV series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_series), *Tajemnice Enigmy* (The Secrets of Enigma).[^o8]

On 21 February 2000, [Polish President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Republic_of_Poland) [Aleksander Kwaśniewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksander_Kwaśniewski) posthumously awarded Różycki the Grand Cross of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) for outstanding contributions to his homeland.[^o9]

In 2007 a three-sided memorial was unveiled before the [Imperial Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Castle,_Poznań) in [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań), commemorating the three mathematician-cryptologists (Rejewski, Różycki and Zygalski), alumni of the [University of Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poznań), who reconstructed the German [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256) and developed methods of breaking Enigma ciphers, thus contributing to subsequent Allied victory in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

In 2009 the [Polish Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Post) issued a series of four commemorative stamps, one of which pictured Różycki and fellow mathematician-cryptologists [Marian Rejewski](wikipedia://en/4349420) and [Henryk Zygalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Zygalski).[^o10]

On 5 August 2014 the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) (IEEE) posthumously honored Rejewski, Różycki, and Zygalski with its Milestone Award, given for "First Breaking of Enigma Code by the Team of Polish Cipher Bureau, 1932-1939". The three had built the '[bomba](wikipedia://en/716701)' – the first cryptanalytic machine to break codes. Their work was a foundation of British code breaking efforts which, with later American assistance, helped end World War II.[^o11] In 2014 a commemorative plaque in Polish and English, dedicated to Różycki and his two colleagues, was unveiled before the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) Mathematics Institute in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw).

In 2018 a bench commemorating Różycki was unveiled in a park in the town of [Wyszków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyszków), where Różycki had attended secondary school.[^o12]

In 2021 the [Enigma Cipher Centre](wikipedia://en/70046152), an educational and scientific institution dedicated to the Polish mathematicians who broke the Enigma cipher, including Jerzy Różycki, opened in Poznań.[^o13]
