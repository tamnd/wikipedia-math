# Bernoulli family

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bernoulli family"}

The **Bernoulli family** ( ; ; ) of [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel) was a [patrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrician_%28post-Roman_Europe%29) family, notable for having produced eight mathematically gifted academics who, among them, contributed substantially to the development of mathematics and physics during the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_Switzerland).

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Bernoulli-Wappenn.png.* Coat of arms of the Bernoulli family

Originally from [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp), a branch of the family relocated to Basel in 1620.[^o4] While their origin in Antwerp is certain, proposed earlier connections with the Dutch family of Italian ancestry called *Bornouilla* (*Bernoullie*), or with the Castilian family *de Bernuy* (*Bernoille*, *Bernouille*), are uncertain.[^o4]

The first known member of the family was Leon Bernoulli (d. 1561), a doctor in [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp), at that time part of the [Spanish Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Netherlands). His son, Jacob, emigrated to [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) in 1570 to escape from the [Spanish persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquisition_in_the_Netherlands) of the [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant). Jacob's grandson, a spice trader, also named Jacob, moved to [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) in 1620, and was granted citizenship in 1622.[^o4] His son, Niklaus Bernoulli (Nicolaus, 1623–1708), Leon's great-great-grandson, married Margarethe Schönauer.

### Notable academic members {#Notable_academic_members}

Margarethe and Niklaus had four sons, of whom Johann and Hieronymus became the progenitors of the "greater" and the "lesser" branches of the family, respectively. The four sons of Margarethe and Niklaus were:

- [Jacob Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/64740) (1654–1705; also known as James or Jacques), mathematician after whom [Bernoulli numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_numbers) are named, and author of the early probability text *[Ars Conjectandi](wikipedia://en/17554277)*
- Nicolaus Bernoulli (1662–1716), painter and [alderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alderman) of Basel
- [Johann Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/268886) (1667–1748; also known as Jean), mathematician and early adopter of [infinitesimal calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_calculus)
- Hieronymus Bernoulli (1669–1760), m. Catharina Ebneter

In addition to [Jacob](wikipedia://en/64740) and [Johann](wikipedia://en/268886), the Bernoulli family of mathematicians is generally taken to include:

- [Nicolaus I Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/1151030) (1687–1759), son of Nicolaus, mathematician, worked on curves, [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934); originator of the [St. Petersburg paradox](wikipedia://en/466277)
- [Nicolaus II Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/1150998) (1695–1726), son of Johann
- [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784) (1700–1782), son of Johann, developer of [Bernoulli's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli's_principle) and originator of the concept of [expected utility](wikipedia://en/736803) for resolving the St. Petersburg paradox
- [Johann II Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/21044122) (1710–1790; also known as Jean), son of Johann, mathematician and physicist
- [Johann III Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_III_Bernoulli) (1744–1807; also known as Jean), son of Johann II, astronomer, geographer and mathematician
- [Jakob II Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_II_Bernoulli) (1759–1789; also known as Jacques), son of Johann II, physicist and mathematician

Several more recent prominent scholars are also descended from the family, including:

- Johann Jakob Bernoulli (1831–1913), art historian and archaeologist; noted for his *Römische Ikonographie* (1882 onwards) on Roman Imperial portraits
- [Ludwig Bernoully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Bernoully) (1873–1928), German architect in Frankfurt
- [Hans Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Benno_Bernoulli) (1876–1959), architect and designer of the Bernoullihäuser in Zurich and Grenchen SO
- [Elisabeth Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Bernoulli) (1873–1935), suffragette and campaigner against alcoholism

The surname survives in Switzerland, with 11 entries in the white pages for the city of Basel as of 2026.[^o6]
