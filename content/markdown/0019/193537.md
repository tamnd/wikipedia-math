# Hermann Minkowski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hermann Minkowski"}

**Hermann Minkowski** (22 June 1864 – 12 January 1909) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg), [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), described variously as German,[^o10][^o11][^o12] Polish,[^o13][^o14][^o15] [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania)-German,[^o16] or Russian.[^o3] He created and developed the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153) and elements of [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/4165882), and used [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) methods to solve problems in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), and the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001).

:::

Minkowski is perhaps best known for his foundational work describing space and time as a [four-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/1364622), now known as "[Minkowski spacetime](wikipedia://en/230488)", which facilitated geometric interpretations of [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s [special theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_theory_of_relativity) (1905).

## Personal life and family {#Personal_life_and_family}

Hermann Minkowski was born in the town of [Aleksotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksotas), the [Suwałki Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwałki_Governorate), the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland), since 1864 part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), to Lewin Boruch Minkowski, a merchant who subsidized the building of the [choral synagogue in Kovno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas_Synagogue),[^o5][^o6][^o7] and Rachel Taubmann, both [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jews_in_Poland).[^o8] Hermann was a younger brother of the [medical researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_research) [Oskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Minkowski) (born 1858).[^o9] In different sources Minkowski's nationality is variously given as German,[^o10][^o11][^o12] Polish,[^o13][^o14][^o15] or Lithuanian-German,[^o16] or Russian.[^o3]

To escape Jewish [persecution in the Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Jewish_pogroms_in_the_Russian_Empire), the family moved to Königsberg in 1872,[^o17] where the father became involved in rag export and later in manufacture of mechanical clockwork tin toys (he operated his firm Lewin Minkowski & Son with his eldest son Max).[^o18]

Minkowski studied in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) and taught in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn) (1887–1894), Königsberg (1894–1896) and [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) (1896–1902), and finally in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) from 1902 until his death in 1909. He married Auguste Adler in 1897 with whom he had two daughters; the [electrical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) and inventor [Reinhold Rudenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_Rudenberg) was his son-in-law.

Minkowski died of [appendicitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appendicitis) in Göttingen on 12 January 1909. [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born) delivered the obituary on behalf of the mathematics students at Göttingen.[^o19] [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s obituary of Minkowski illustrates the deep friendship between the two mathematicians:

The main-belt [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) [12493 Minkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12493_Minkowski) and [M-matrices](wikipedia://en/7939066) are named in Minkowski's honor.[^o42]

## Career {#Career}

*Image: Hermann Minkowski.png.* Minkowski in 1883, at the time of being awarded the Mathematics Prize of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences)

Minkowski was educated in [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia) at the *Albertina* University of Königsberg, where he earned his doctorate in 1885 under the direction of [Ferdinand von Lindemann](wikipedia://en/335736). In 1883, while still a student at Königsberg, he was awarded the Mathematics Prize of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) for his manuscript on the theory of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478). Because of his very young age of 18, which was unheard of in the mathematics community and his obscurity as a mathematician at the time, his sharing the award with eminent English mathematician [Henry Smith](wikipedia://en/47123), who was certainly a great deal more famous than Hermann and to whom the prize was awarded posthumously. This caused severe unrest among English mathematicians. The prize committee, despite the numerous complaints, never changed their decision. He also became a friend of another renowned mathematician, [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). His brother, [Oskar Minkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Minkowski) (1858–1931), was a well-known physician and researcher.[^o17]

Minkowski taught at the universities of Bonn, Königsberg, Zürich, and Göttingen. At the [*Eidgenössisches Polytechnikum*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Polytechnic_Institute), today the [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), he was one of Einstein's teachers.

Minkowski explored the arithmetic of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478), especially concerning *n* variables, and his research into that topic led him to consider certain geometric properties in a space of *n* [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398). In 1896, he presented his *[geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153)*, a geometrical method that solved problems in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is also the creator of the [Minkowski Sausage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_Sausage) and the [Minkowski cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_cover) of a curve.

In 1902, he joined the Mathematics Department of [Göttingen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_University) and became a close colleague of David Hilbert, whom he first met at university in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg). [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625) was one of his students there.

## Work on relativity {#Work_on_relativity}

By 1908 Minkowski realized that the special theory of relativity, introduced by his former student [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) in 1905, along with the previous work of [Lorentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lorentz) and [Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740), could best be understood in a four-dimensional space,[^o24] since known as the "Minkowski spacetime", in which [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) and [space](wikipedia://en/27667) are not separated entities but intermingled in a four-dimensional [space–time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space–time), and in which the [Lorentz geometry](wikipedia://en/18404) of special relativity can be effectively represented using the invariant interval $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - c^2 t^2$ (see [History of special relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_special_relativity)).

The mathematical basis of Minkowski space can also be found in the [hyperboloid model](wikipedia://en/4771189) of [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) already known in the 19th century, because [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) (or motions) in hyperbolic space can be related to [Lorentz transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_transformations), which included contributions of [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824) (1880, 1885), [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) (1881), [Homersham Cox](wikipedia://en/55620821) (1881), [Alexander Macfarlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Macfarlane) (1894) and others (see [History of Lorentz transformations](wikipedia://en/7058047)).
