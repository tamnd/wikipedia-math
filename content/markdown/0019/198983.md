# Daniel J. Bernstein

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel J. Bernstein"}

**Daniel Julius Bernstein** (born October 29, 1971) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [cryptologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptologist), and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). He is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of computer science at the [University of Illinois Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago).[^o4] He was a visiting professor in the department of mathematics and computer science at the [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology),[^o5] and a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) at CASA at [Ruhr University Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_University_Bochum) through 2023.[^o6]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Bernstein attended [Bellport High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellport_High_School), a public high school on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), graduating in 1987 at the age of 15.[^o8] The same year, he ranked fifth in the [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search).[^o9] In 1987, he achieved a Top 10 ranking in the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition),[^o10] and was a member of the second-place team from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) the following year.[^o11] Bernstein earned a B.A. in mathematics from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) (1991) and a Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) (1995), where he studied under [Hendrik Lenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lenstra).[^o2]

## *Bernstein v. United States* {#Bernstein_v._United_States}

The [export of cryptography from the United States](wikipedia://en/826003) was controlled as a [munition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munition) starting from the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War) until recategorization in 1996, with further relaxation in the late 1990s.[^o13] In 1995, Bernstein brought the court case *[Bernstein v. United States](wikipedia://en/340772)*. The ruling in the case declared that software was [protected speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_speech) under the [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution), which contributed to regulatory changes reducing controls on encryption.[^o14] Bernstein was originally represented by the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation).[^o15] He later [represented himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_se_legal_representation_in_the_United_States).[^o16]

## Cryptography {#Cryptography}

Bernstein designed the [Salsa20](wikipedia://en/2701329) [stream cipher](wikipedia://en/49007) in 2005 and submitted it to [eSTREAM](wikipedia://en/2287686) for review and possible standardization. He later published the [ChaCha20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChaCha20) variant of Salsa in 2008. In 2005, he proposed the [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/9966) [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444) as a basis for [public-key](wikipedia://en/24222) schemes. He worked as the lead researcher on the [Ed25519](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed25519) version of [EdDSA](wikipedia://en/40775836). The algorithms made their way into popular software. For example, since 2014, when [OpenSSH](wikipedia://en/25490426) is compiled without [OpenSSL](wikipedia://en/438819), they power most of its operations. [OpenBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenBSD) package [signing](wikipedia://en/59644) is based on Ed25519.[^o18][^o19]

Nearly a decade later, [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) [disclosed mass surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_surveillance_disclosures_%282013–present%29) by the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939), and researchers discovered a [backdoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backdoor_%28computing%29) in the Agency's [Dual EC DRBG](wikipedia://en/14259066) algorithm. These events raised suspicions of the elliptic curve parameters proposed by NSA and standardized by [NIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology).[^o20] Many researchers feared[^o21] that the NSA had chosen curves that gave them a [cryptanalytic](wikipedia://en/5715) advantage.[^o22][^o23] [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) selected ChaCha20 along with Bernstein's [Poly1305](wikipedia://en/2017690) [message authentication code](wikipedia://en/567523) for use in [TLS](wikipedia://en/187813), which is widely used for Internet security.[^o24] Many protocols based on his works have been adopted by various [standards organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standards_organization) and are [used in a variety of applications](wikipedia://en/17935444#Applications), such as [Apple iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_iOS),[^o25] the [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux) kernel,[^o26] [OpenSSH](wikipedia://en/25490426),[^o27][^o28] and [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_%28anonymity_network%29).[^o29]

In spring 2005, Bernstein taught a course on "high speed cryptography."[^o30] He introduced new [cache attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side-channel_attacks) against implementations of [AES](wikipedia://en/1260) in the same time period.[^o31]

In April 2008,[^o32] Bernstein's [stream cipher](wikipedia://en/49007) "[Salsa20](wikipedia://en/2701329)" was selected as a member of the final portfolio of the [eSTREAM](wikipedia://en/2287686) project, part of a [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) research directive.

In 2011, Bernstein published RFSB, a variant of the [fast syndrome-based (FSB) hash](wikipedia://en/25587347) function.

He is one of the editors of the 2009 book *Post-Quantum Cryptography*.[^o33]

In 2022, Bernstein filed a second lawsuit against the U.S. government under the [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_%28United_States%29), seeking records on the National Security Agency's role in influencing NIST's [post-quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/26605226) standards, a case that remains ongoing as of 2025.[^o34][^o35]

## Software {#Software}

Starting in the mid-1990s, Bernstein wrote a number of security-aware programs, including [qmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qmail), [ezmlm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezmlm), [djbdns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djbdns), [ucspi-tcp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ucspi-tcp), [daemontools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daemontools), and `publicfile`.

Bernstein criticized the leading [DNS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_Name_System) package at the time, [BIND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIND), and wrote djbdns as a DNS package with security as a primary goal.[^o37] Bernstein offers "security guarantees" for qmail and djbdns in the form of monetary rewards for the identification of flaws.[^o38][^o39] A purported exploit targeting qmail running on [64-bit platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64-bit_computing) was published in 2005,[^o40][^o41] but Bernstein believes that the exploit does not fall within the parameters of his qmail security guarantee. In March 2009, Bernstein awarded \$1000 to Matthew Dempsky for finding a security flaw in [djbdns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djbdns).[^o42]

In August 2008, Bernstein announced[^o43] [DNSCurve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNSCurve), a proposal to secure the [Domain Name System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_Name_System). DNSCurve applies techniques from [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography) with the goal of providing a vast increase in performance over the [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) public-key algorithm used by [DNSSEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNSSEC). It uses the existing DNS hierarchy to propagate trust by embedding public keys into specially formatted, [backward-compatible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_compatibility) DNS records.

Bernstein proposed [Internet Mail 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Mail_2000), an alternative system for electronic mail, which he intended to replace the [Simple Mail Transfer Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_Mail_Transfer_Protocol) (SMTP), the [Post Office Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Office_Protocol) (POP3) and the [Internet Message Access Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Message_Access_Protocol) (IMAP).[^o44]

Bernstein is also known for his [string hashing function](wikipedia://en/4024666#Hashing_strings) *djb2*[^o45][^o46] and the [cdb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cdb_%28software%29) database library.[^o47]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Bernstein has published a number of papers on [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [computation](wikipedia://en/5926). Many of his papers deal with [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) or implementations.

In 2001, Bernstein circulated "Circuits for [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491): a proposal,"[^o49] which suggested that, if physical hardware implementations could be brought close to their theoretical efficiency, the then-popular estimates of adequate security parameters might be off by a factor of three. Since 512-bit [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) was breakable at the time, so might be 1536-bit RSA. Bernstein was careful not to make any actual predictions, and emphasized the importance of correctly interpreting [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/641995) expressions. Several prominent researchers (among them [Arjen Lenstra](wikipedia://en/6850292), [Adi Shamir](wikipedia://en/57314), Jim Tomlinson, and [Eran Tromer](wikipedia://en/78318716)) disagreed strongly with Bernstein's conclusions.[^o50]

Bernstein is also the author of the mathematical [libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_%28computing%29) DJBFFT, a fast [portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_portability) [FFT](wikipedia://en/11512) library, and `primegen`, an asymptotically fast small prime [sieve](wikipedia://en/1035915) with low [memory footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_footprint) based on the [sieve of Atkin](wikipedia://en/2150920) (rather than the more usual [sieve of Eratosthenes](wikipedia://en/73415)). *Sieve of Atkin* was co-authored by Atkin and Bernstein. Both have been used effectively in the search for large [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).

In 2007, Bernstein proposed the use of a [(twisted) Edwards curve](wikipedia://en/25889726), [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444), as a basis for [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography); it is employed in Ed25519 implementation of [EdDSA](wikipedia://en/40775836).

In February 2015, Bernstein and others published a paper on a stateless [post-quantum](wikipedia://en/26605226) [hash-based signature scheme](wikipedia://en/53957696) called [SPHINCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPHINCS).[^o51] In July 2022, [SPHINCS+](wikipedia://en/78931629), a signature scheme adapted from SPHINCS by Bernstein and others, was one of four algorithms selected as winners of the [NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography Standardization](wikipedia://en/56162225) competition. It was the only hash-based algorithm of the four winners.[^o52][^o53]

In April 2017, Bernstein and others published a paper on Post-Quantum RSA that includes an integer factorization algorithm claimed to be "often much faster than [Shor's](wikipedia://en/42674)".[^o54]

## Teaching {#Teaching}

In 2004, Bernstein taught a course on [computer software security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_security) where he assigned each student to find ten [vulnerabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability_%28computing%29) in published software.[^o56] The 25 students discovered 44 vulnerabilities, and the class published [security advisories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability_%28computing%29) about the issues.[^o56]
