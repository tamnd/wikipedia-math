# Trivial group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trivial group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **trivial group** or **zero group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that consists of a single element. All such groups are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397), so one often speaks of the trivial group. The single element of the trivial group is the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) and so it is usually denoted as such: $0$, $1$, or $\mathrm{e}$ depending on the context. If the group operation is denoted $\, \cdot \,$ then it is defined by $\mathrm{e} \cdot \mathrm{e} = \mathrm{e}$.

:::

The similarly defined **trivial monoid** is also a group since its only element is its own inverse, and is hence the same as the trivial group.

The trivial group is distinct from the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566), which has no elements, hence lacks an identity element, and so cannot be a group.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Given any group $G$, the group that consists of only the identity element is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $G$, and, being the trivial group, is called the **trivial subgroup** of $G$.

The term, when referred to "$G$ has no nontrivial proper subgroups" refers to the only subgroups of $G$ being the trivial group $\{ \mathrm{e} \}$ and the group $G$ itself.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The trivial group is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) of order $1$; as such it may be denoted $\mathrm{Z}_1$ or $\mathrm{C}_1$. If the group operation is called addition, the trivial group is usually denoted by $0$. If the group operation is called multiplication then $1$ can be a notation for the trivial group. Combining these leads to the [trivial ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_ring) in which the addition and multiplication operations are identical and $0 = 1$.

Trivial group is the only group with exactly one subgroup. All other groups have at least two subgroups, trivial group and itself. Trivial group has no [proper subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_subgroup).

The trivial group serves as the [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object) in the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092), meaning it is both an [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) and a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object).

The trivial group can be made a (bi-)[ordered group](wikipedia://en/349014) by equipping it with the trivial [non-strict order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-strict_order) $\,\leq$.

:::
