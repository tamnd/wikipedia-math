# Erlang distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erlang distribution"}

The **Erlang distribution** is a two-parameter family of continuous [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) with [support](wikipedia://en/381013) $x \in [0, \infty)$. The two parameters are:

:::

- a positive integer $k,$ the "shape", and
- a positive real number $\lambda,$ the "rate". The "scale", $\beta,$ the reciprocal of the rate, is sometimes used instead.

The Erlang distribution is the distribution of a sum of $k$ [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) [exponential variables](wikipedia://en/45906) with mean $1/\lambda$ each. Equivalently, it is the distribution of the time until the *k*th event of a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) with a rate of $\lambda$. The Erlang and Poisson distributions are complementary, in that while the Poisson distribution counts the events that occur in a fixed amount of time, the Erlang distribution counts the amount of time until the occurrence of a fixed number of events. When $k=1$, the distribution simplifies to the [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906). The Erlang distribution is a special case of the [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079) in which the shape of the distribution is discretized.

The Erlang distribution was developed by [A. K. Erlang](wikipedia://en/2948) to examine the number of telephone calls that might be made at the same time to the operators of the switching stations. This work on telephone [traffic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teletraffic_engineering) has been expanded to consider waiting times in [queueing systems](wikipedia://en/50578) in general. The distribution is also used in the field of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

### Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the Erlang distribution is

$$f(x; k,\lambda)={\lambda^k x^{k-1} e^{-\lambda x} \over (k-1)!}\quad\mbox{for }x, \lambda \geq 0,$$

The parameter *k* is called the shape parameter, and the parameter $\lambda$ is called the rate parameter.

An alternative, but equivalent, parametrization uses the scale parameter $\beta$, which is the reciprocal of the rate parameter (i.e., $\beta = 1/\lambda$):

$$f(x; k,\beta)=\frac{ x^{k-1} e^{-\frac{x}{\beta}} }{\beta^k (k-1)!}\quad\mbox{for }x, \beta \geq 0.$$

When the scale parameter $\beta$ equals 2, the distribution simplifies to the [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424) with 2*k* degrees of freedom. It can therefore be regarded as a [generalized chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/24899099) for even numbers of degrees of freedom.

### Cumulative distribution function (CDF) {#Cumulative_distribution_function_(CDF)}

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) of the Erlang distribution is

$$F(x; k,\lambda) = P(k, \lambda x) = \frac{\gamma(k, \lambda x)}{\Gamma(k)} = \frac{\gamma(k, \lambda x)}{(k-1)!},$$

where $\gamma$ is the lower [incomplete gamma function](wikipedia://en/456050) and $P$ is the [lower regularized gamma function](wikipedia://en/456050#Regularized_Gamma_functions_and_Poisson_random_variables). The CDF may also be expressed as

$$F(x; k,\lambda) = 1 - \sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{n!}e^{-\lambda x}(\lambda x)^n.$$

### Erlang-*k* {#Erlang-k}

The Erlang-*k* distribution (where *k* is a positive integer) $E_k(\lambda)$ is defined by setting *k* in the PDF of the Erlang distribution.[^o7] For instance, the Erlang-2 distribution is $E_2(\lambda) ={\lambda^2 x} e^{-\lambda x} \quad\mbox{for }x, \lambda \geq 0$, which is the same as $f(x; 2,\lambda)$.

### Median {#Median}

An asymptotic expansion is known for the median of an Erlang distribution,[^o9] for which coefficients can be computed and bounds are known.[^o10][^o11] An approximation is $\frac{k}{\lambda}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{3k+0.2}\right),$ i.e. below the mean $\frac{k}{\lambda}.$[^o12]

:::

## Generating Erlang-distributed random variates {#Generating_Erlang-distributed_random_variates}

Erlang-distributed random variates can be generated from uniformly distributed random numbers ($U \in [0,1]$) using the following formula:[^o14]

$$E(k,\lambda) = -\frac{1}\lambda \ln \prod_{i=1}^k U_{i} = -\frac{1}\lambda \sum_{i=1}^k \ln U_{i}$$

## Applications {#Applications}

### Waiting times {#Waiting_times}

Events that occur independently with some average rate are modeled with a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process). The waiting times between *k* occurrences of the event are Erlang distributed. (The related question of the number of events in a given amount of time is described by the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144).)

The Erlang distribution, which measures the time between incoming calls, can be used in conjunction with the expected duration of incoming calls to produce information about the traffic load measured in erlangs. This can be used to determine the probability of packet loss or delay, according to various assumptions made about whether blocked calls are aborted (Erlang B formula) or queued until served (Erlang C formula). The [Erlang-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlang-B) and [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlang_unit#Erlang_C_formula) formulae are still in everyday use for traffic modeling for applications such as the design of [call centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_center).

### Other applications {#Other_applications}

The age distribution of [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) [incidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_incidence) often follows the Erlang distribution, whereas the shape and scale parameters predict, respectively, the number of [driver events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogenesis) and the time interval between them.[^o19][^o20] More generally, the Erlang distribution has been suggested as good approximation of cell cycle time distribution, as result of multi-stage models.[^o21][^o22]

The [kinesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinesin) is a molecular machine with two "feet" that "walks" along a filament. The waiting time between each step is exponentially distributed. When [green fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein) is attached to a foot of the kinesin, then the green dot visibly moves with Erlang distribution of k = 2.[^o23]

It has also been used in marketing for describing interpurchase times.[^o24]

## Properties {#Properties}

- If $X \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(k, \lambda)$ then $a \cdot X \sim \operatorname{Erlang}\left(k, \frac{\lambda}{a}\right)$ with $a \in \mathbb{R}$
- If $X \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(k_1, \lambda)$ and $Y \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(k_2, \lambda)$ then $X + Y \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(k_1 + k_2, \lambda)$ if $X, Y$ are independent

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- The Erlang distribution is the distribution of the sum of *k* [independent and identically distributed random variables](wikipedia://en/30876902), each having an [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906). The long-run rate at which events occur is the reciprocal of the expectation of $X,$ that is, $\lambda/k.$ The (age specific event) rate of the Erlang distribution is, for $k>1,$ monotonic in $x,$ increasing from 0 at $x=0,$ to $\lambda$ as $x$ tends to infinity.[^o28]
  - That is: if $X_i \sim \operatorname{Exponential}(\lambda),$ then $$\sum_{i=1}^k{X_i} \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(k, \lambda)$$
- Because of the factorial function in the denominator of the PDF and CDF, the Erlang distribution is only defined when the parameter *k* is a positive integer. In fact, this distribution is sometimes called the **Erlang-*k* distribution** (e.g., an Erlang-2 distribution is an Erlang distribution with $k=2$). The [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079) generalizes the Erlang distribution by allowing *k* to be any positive real number, using the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) instead of the factorial function.
  - That is: if k is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) and $X \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(k, \lambda),$ then $X \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(k, \lambda)$
- If $U \sim \operatorname{Exponential}(\lambda)$ and $V \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(n, \lambda)$ then $\frac{U}{V}+1 \sim \operatorname{Pareto}(1, n)$
- The Erlang distribution is a special case of the [Pearson type III distribution](wikipedia://en/1804023)
- The Erlang distribution is related to the [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424). If $X \sim \operatorname{Erlang}(k,\lambda),$ then $2\lambda X\sim \chi^2_{2k}.$
- The Erlang distribution is related to the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) by the [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process). More precisely, for $i=1,2,...$, the time $X_i$ between arrivals $i-1$ and $i$ of a [Poisson point process](wikipedia://en/41597450) with arrival rate $\lambda$ on the time interval $t=[0,\infty)$ is an [exponential random variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_random_variable) with parameter $\lambda$ (or equivalently, mean $1/\lambda$) where arrival $i=0$ is identified with time zero. Thus, the amount of time $$S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n{X_i}$$ from time zero until the $n$th arrival of the process has an $\operatorname{Erlang}(n, \lambda)$ distribution. Moreover, letting $N(x)$ denote the number of arrivals in the time interval $[0,x]$, we have $$\operatorname{Pr}(N(x) \leq n - 1) = \operatorname{Pr}(S_n > x) = 1 - F_X(x; n, \lambda) = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{1}{k!}e^{-\lambda x} (\lambda x)^k.$$ Taking the differences over $n$ gives the Poisson distribution.
