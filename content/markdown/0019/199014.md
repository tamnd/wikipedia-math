# Infix notation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infix notation"}

**Infix notation** is the notation commonly used in [arithmetical](wikipedia://en/3118) and [logical](wikipedia://en/46426065) formulae and statements. It is characterized by the placement of [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) between [operands](wikipedia://en/22656)—"infixed operators"—such as the [plus sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plus_sign) in 2 **\+** 2.

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

[Binary relations](wikipedia://en/3931) are often denoted by an infix symbol such as [set membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_membership) *a* ∈ *A* when the set *A* has *a* for an element. In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [perpendicular lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendicular_line) *a* and *b* are denoted $a \perp b \ ,$ and in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) two points *b* and *c* are in [perspective](wikipedia://en/14909067) when $b \ \doublebarwedge \ c$ while they are connected by a projectivity when $b \ \barwedge \ c .$

Infix notation is more difficult to [parse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsing) by computers than [prefix notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefix_notation) (e.g. **\+** 2 2) or [postfix notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postfix_notation) (e.g. 2 2 **\+**). However many [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015) use it due to its familiarity. It is more used in arithmetic, e.g. 5 **×** 6.[^o3]

## Further notations {#Further_notations}

Infix notation may also be distinguished from [function](wikipedia://en/185427) notation, where the name of a function suggests a particular operation, and its [arguments](wikipedia://en/32842052) are the operands. An example of such a [function notation](wikipedia://en/20576545) would be $S(1, 3)$ in which the function $S$ denotes addition ("sum"): $S (1, 3) = 1 + 3 = 4$.

## Order of operations {#Order_of_operations}

In infix notation, unlike in prefix or postfix notations, [parentheses](wikipedia://en/59338#Parentheses) surrounding groups of operands and operators are necessary to indicate the intended order in which operations are to be performed. In the absence of parentheses, certain precedence rules determine the [order of operations](wikipedia://en/212980).
