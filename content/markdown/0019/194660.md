# Leonid Kantorovich

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leonid Kantorovich"}

**Leonid Vitalyevich Kantorovich** (Леонид Витальевич Канторович, ; 19 January 1912 – 7 April 1986) was a Soviet [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist), known for his theory and development of techniques for the optimal allocation of resources. He is regarded as the founder of [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730). He was the winner of the [Stalin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize) in 1949 and the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences) in 1975.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kantorovich was born on 19 January 1912, to a [Russian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia) family.[^o4] His father was a doctor practicing in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg).[^o5] In 1926, at the age of fourteen, he began his studies at [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University). He graduated from the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics in 1930, and began his graduate studies. In 1934, at the age of 22 years, he became a full professor. In 1935 he received his [doctoral degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_degree).[^o6]

Later, Kantorovich worked for the [Soviet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Soviet_Union). He was given the task of [optimizing](wikipedia://en/52033) production in a [plywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plywood) industry. He devised the mathematical technique now known as [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) in 1939, some years before it was advanced by [George Dantzig](wikipedia://en/13111). He authored several books including *The Mathematical Method of Production Planning and Organization* (Russian original 1939), *The Best Uses of Economic Resources* (Russian original 1959), and, with Vladimir Ivanovich Krylov, *Approximate methods of higher analysis* (Russian original 1936).[^o7] For his work, Kantorovich was awarded the [Stalin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize) in 1949.

After 1939, he became a professor at [Military Engineering-Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Engineering-Technical_University). During the [Siege of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad), Kantorovich was a professor at [VITU of Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Engineering-Technical_University) and worked on safety of the [Road of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_of_Life). He calculated the optimal distance between cars on ice in dependence of the thickness of ice and the temperature of the air. In December 1941 and January 1942, Kantorovich walked himself between cars driving on the ice of [Lake Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ladoga) on the [Road of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_of_Life) to ensure that cars did not sink. However, many cars with food for survivors of the siege were destroyed by the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftwaffe) airstrikes. For his feat and courage Kantorovich was awarded the [Order of the Patriotic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Patriotic_War), and was decorated with the medal *For Defense of Leningrad*.

In 1948 Kantorovich was assigned to the atomic project of the USSR.

After 1960, Kantorovich lived and worked in [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk), where he created and took charge of the Department of Computational Mathematics in [Novosibirsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk_State_University).[^o8]

The [Nobel Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences), which he shared with [Tjalling Koopmans](wikipedia://en/1331883), was given "for their contributions to the theory of optimum allocation of resources."

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), Kantorovich had important results in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), and [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675).

In particular, Kantorovich formulated some fundamental results in the theory of [normed](wikipedia://en/21538) [vector lattices](wikipedia://en/8099018), especially in Dedekind complete vector lattices called "K-spaces" which are now referred to as "Kantorovich spaces" in his honor.

Kantorovich showed that [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) could be used in the analysis of [iterative methods](wikipedia://en/15237), obtaining the [Kantorovich inequalities](wikipedia://en/929502) on the [convergence rate](wikipedia://en/999701) of the [gradient method](wikipedia://en/15094152) and of [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) (see the [Kantorovich theorem](wikipedia://en/10349343)).

Kantorovich considered [infinite-dimensional optimization](wikipedia://en/1300939) problems, such as the Kantorovich-Monge problem in [transport theory](wikipedia://en/6631661). His analysis proposed the [Kantorovich–Rubinstein metric](wikipedia://en/6704603), which is used in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), in the theory of the [weak convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures#Weak_convergence_of_measures) of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437).
