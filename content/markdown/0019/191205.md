# Ipso facto

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ipso facto"}

**Ipso facto** is a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) phrase, directly translated as "by the fact itself",[^o2] which means that a specific [phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenon) is a *direct* [consequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality), a resultant *effect*, of the action in question, instead of being brought about by a previous action. (Contrast this with the expressions *"by itself"* or *"per se"*.) It is a [term of art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_of_art) used in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law), and [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science).

:::

Aside from its technical uses, it occurs frequently in literature, particularly in scholarly addenda: e.g., "Faustus had signed his life away, and was, ipso facto, incapable of repentance" (from [Christopher Marlowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Marlowe), *[The Tragical History of Dr. Faustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tragical_History_of_Dr._Faustus)*) or "These prejudices are rooted in the idea that every tramp ipso facto is a blackguard" (from [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell), *[Down and Out in Paris and London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_and_Out_in_Paris_and_London)*).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="In Catholic canon law"}

## In Catholic canon law {#In_Catholic_canon_law}

Ipso facto denotes the automatic character of the loss of membership in a religious body by someone guilty of a specified action.[^o5] Within the [canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law_%28Catholic_Church%29) of the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church), the phrase [latae sententiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latae_sententiae) is more commonly used than ipso facto with regard to [ecclesiastical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical) penalties such as [excommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication_%28Catholic_Church%29). It indicates that the effect follows even if no verdict (in Latin, sententia) is pronounced by an ecclesiastical superior or tribunal.

:::
