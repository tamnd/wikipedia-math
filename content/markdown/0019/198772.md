# Mathematical Association of America

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematical Association of America"}

The **Mathematical Association of America** (**MAA**) is a professional society that focuses on [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) accessible at the undergraduate level. Members include [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University), [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College), and [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school) teachers; graduate and undergraduate students; pure and applied [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902); [computer scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist); [statisticians](wikipedia://en/48661); and many others in academia, government, business, and industry.

:::

The MAA was founded in 1915 and is headquartered at 11 Dupont in the [Dupont Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dupont_Circle,_Washington,_D.C.) neighborhood of [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) The organization publishes mathematics journals and books, including the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* (established in 1894 by [Benjamin Finkel](wikipedia://en/23166485)), the most widely read mathematics journal in the world according to records on [JSTOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JSTOR).[^o3]

## Meetings {#Meetings}

The MAA sponsors the annual summer [MathFest](wikipedia://en/14984993) and cosponsors with the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) the [Joint Mathematics Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Mathematics_Meeting), held in early January of each year. On occasion the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) joins in these meetings. Twenty-nine regional sections also hold regular meetings.

## Publications {#Publications}

The association publishes multiple journals in partnership with [Taylor & Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_&_Francis):[^o6]

- *[The American Mathematical Monthly](wikipedia://en/1179483)* is expository, aimed at a broad audience from undergraduate students to research mathematicians.
- *[Mathematics Magazine](wikipedia://en/4230329)* is expository, aimed at teachers of undergraduate mathematics, especially at the junior-senior level.
- *[The College Mathematics Journal](wikipedia://en/21340776)* is expository, aimed at teachers of undergraduate mathematics, especially at the freshman-sophomore level.
- *[Math Horizons](wikipedia://en/21340642)* is expository, aimed at undergraduate students.

*[MAA FOCUS](wikipedia://en/21147475)* is the association member newsletter. The Association publishes an online resource, Mathematical Sciences Digital Library (Math DL). The service launched in 2001 with the online-only *Journal of Online Mathematics and its Applications* (JOMA) and a set of classroom tools, *Digital Classroom Resources*. These were followed in 2004 by *Convergence*, an online-only history magazine, and in 2005 by *MAA Reviews*, an online book review service, and *Classroom Capsules and Notes*, a set of classroom notes.[^o7]

The MAA publishes several book series, aimed at a broad audience, but primarily for undergraduates majoring in mathematics. The series are: *[Anneli Lax New Mathematical Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anneli_Lax_New_Mathematical_Library)*, *[Carus Mathematical Monographs](wikipedia://en/2941952)*, *Classroom Resource Materials*, *Dolciani Mathematical Expositions*, *MAA Notes*, *MAA Textbooks*, *Problem Books*, and *Spectrum*.[^o8]

## Competitions {#Competitions}

The MAA sponsors numerous competitions for students, including the [William Lowell Putnam Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) for undergraduate students, the online competition series, and the [American Mathematics Competitions](wikipedia://en/665435) (AMC) for middle- and high-school students. This series of competitions is as follows:

- AMC 8: 25 multiple choice questions in 40 minutes
- [AMC 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_10)/[AMC 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_12): 25 multiple choice questions in 75 minutes
- [AIME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Invitational_Mathematics_Examination): 15 short answer questions in a 3-hour period
- [USAMO/USAJMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_Mathematical_Olympiad): 6 questions, 2 days, 9 hours, proof-based olympiad

Through this program, outstanding students are identified and invited to participate in the [Mathematical Olympiad Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Olympiad_Program). Ultimately, six high school students are chosen to represent the U.S. at the [International Mathematics Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematics_Olympiad).

## Memberships {#Memberships}

The MAA is one of four partners in the [Joint Policy Board for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/4675178) (JPBM), and participates in the [Conference Board of the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/13673454) (CBMS), an umbrella organization of sixteen professional societies.

## Historical accounts {#Historical_accounts}

A detailed history of the first fifty years of the MAA appears in (May 1972). A report on activities prior to [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) appears in (Bennett 1967). Further details of its history can be found in (Case 1996). In addition numerous regional sections of the MAA have published accounts of their local history. The MAA was established in 1915. But the roots of the Association can be traced to the 1894 founding of the *American Mathematical Monthly* by [Benjamin Finkel](wikipedia://en/23166485), who wrote "Most of our existing journals deal almost exclusively with subjects beyond the reach of the average student or teacher of mathematics or at least with subjects with which they are familiar, and little, if any, space, is devoted to the solution of problems…No pains will be spared on the part of the Editors to make this the most interesting and most popular journal published in America."

The MAA records are preserved as part of the [Archives of American Mathematics](wikipedia://en/54603387).

## Inclusivity {#Inclusivity}

The MAA has for a long time followed a strict policy of inclusivity and non-discrimination.

In previous periods it was subject to the same problems of discrimination that were widespread across the United States. One notorious incident at a south-eastern sectional meeting in [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee) in 1951 has been documented[^o28] by the American mathematician and equal rights activist [Lee Lorch](wikipedia://en/8942515), who in 2007 received the most prestigious award given by the MAA (the Yueh-Gin Gung and Dr. Charles Y. Hu Award for Distinguished Service to Mathematics).[^o29][^o30] The citation delivered at the 2007 MAA awards presentation, where Lorch received a standing ovation, recorded that:

"*Lee Lorch, the chair of the mathematics department at Fisk University, and three Black colleagues, [Evelyn Boyd](wikipedia://en/5827598) (now Granville), Walter Brown, and H. M. Holloway came to the meeting and were able to attend the scientific sessions. However, the organizer for the closing banquet refused to honor the reservations of these four mathematicians. (Letters in Science, August 10, 1951, pp. 161–162 spell out the details). Lorch and his colleagues wrote to the governing bodies of the AMS and MAA seeking bylaws against discrimination. Bylaws were not changed, but non-discriminatory policies were established and have been strictly observed since then.*"[^o29]

The Association's first woman president was [Dorothy Lewis Bernstein](wikipedia://en/7097637) (1979–1980).[^o31]

In 1980, [Gloria Ford Gilmer](wikipedia://en/42777196) became the first Black woman on the board of governors.[^o32]

[Edray Goins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edray_Goins) became the first African American elected president in its 110-year history (elected 2025; term as president-elect began July 1, 2026; term as president begins July 1, 2027).[^o33]
