# Gilles de Roberval

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gilles de Roberval"}

**Gilles Personne de Roberval** (August 10, 1602 – October 27, 1675) was a [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) born at [Roberval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberval,_Oise) near [Beauvais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauvais), France. His name was originally **Gilles Personne** or **Gilles Personier**, with Roberval the place of his birth.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Like [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525), he was present at the [Siege of La Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_La_Rochelle) in 1627. In the same year he went to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), and in 1631 he was appointed the philosophy chair at [Gervais College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gervais_College), [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). In 1634,[^o5] he was also made the chair of mathematics at the [Royal College of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_France). A condition of tenure attached to this particular chair was that the holder (Roberval, in this case) would propose mathematical questions for solution, and should resign in favour of any person who solved them better than himself. Notwithstanding this, Roberval was able to keep the chair until his death.[^n1]

Roberval was one of those mathematicians who, just before the invention of the [infinitesimal calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_calculus), occupied their attention with problems which are only soluble, or can be most easily solved, by some method involving [limits](wikipedia://en/26551602) or [infinitesimals](wikipedia://en/160990), which would today be solved by calculus. He worked on the [quadrature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrature_%28geometry%29) of surfaces and the [cubature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubature) of solids, which he accomplished, in some of the simpler cases, by an original method which he called the "Method of Indivisibles"; but he lost much of the credit of the discovery as he kept his method for his own use, while [Bonaventura Cavalieri](wikipedia://en/412561) published [a similar method](wikipedia://en/21697672) which he independently invented.[^n1]

Another of Roberval’s discoveries was a very general method of drawing [tangents](wikipedia://en/31482), by considering a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) as described by a moving point whose motion is the resultant of several simpler motions.[^o8] He also discovered a method of deriving one curve from another, by means of which finite areas can be obtained equal to the areas between certain curves and their [asymptotes](wikipedia://en/3107). To these curves, which were also applied to effect some quadratures, [Evangelista Torricelli](wikipedia://en/210413) gave the name "Robervallian lines."[^n1][^o10]

Between Roberval and [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) there existed a feeling of ill-will,[^o11][^o12] owing to the jealousy aroused in the mind of the former by the criticism that Descartes offered to some of the methods employed by him and by [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966); and this led him to criticize and oppose the analytical methods that Descartes introduced into geometry about this time.[^n1]

As results of Roberval’s labours outside of pure mathematics may be noted a work on the system of the universe, in which he supports the [Copernican heliocentric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernican_heliocentric_system) and attributes a mutual attraction to all particles of matter and also the invention of a special kind of [balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighing_scale#Balance), the *[Roberval Balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberval_Balance)*.[^n2]
