# George Gallup

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="George Gallup"}

**George Horace Gallup** (November 18, 1901 – July 26, 1984) was an American pioneer of survey sampling techniques and inventor of the [Gallup poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_poll), a [statistically](wikipedia://en/26685) based [survey sampled](wikipedia://en/27578) measure of [public opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polls).[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

*Image: George Gallup 2001 Romania stamp.jpg.* George Gallup on a 2001 Romanian stamp

*Image: Grave of George H. Gallup.jpg.* Grave in Princeton Cemetery

George Gallup, Jr., was born in [Jefferson, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson,_Iowa), the son of Nettie Quella (Davenport) and George Henry Gallup, a dairy farmer. As a teen, "Ted" would deliver milk and used his earnings to start a newspaper at his high school. He attended the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa), earning his B.A. in 1923, his M.A. in 1925 and his Ph.D. in 1928.[^o5] While there he was a member of the Iowa Beta chapter of the [Sigma Alpha Epsilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Alpha_Epsilon) fraternity, and editor of *[The Daily Iowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Iowan)*, the campus newspaper.

He then moved first to [Des Moines, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines,_Iowa), where he served until 1931 as head of the Department of Journalism at [Drake University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_University), then to [Evanston, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois), as a professor of journalism and advertising at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). He moved to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) in 1932 to join the advertising agency of [Young and Rubicam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_and_Rubicam) as director of research, serving as vice president there from 1937 to 1947. He was also a professor of journalism at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), but relinquished the position shortly after he formed his own polling company, the American Institute of Public Opinion, in 1935.[^o6]

Gallup had first become involved in polling in 1932, when he did some for his mother-in-law, [Ola Babcock Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ola_Babcock_Miller), a longshot candidate for [Iowa Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Secretary_of_State). She was swept in with the Democratic landslide of that year, furthering Gallup's interest in politics.[^o7]

In 1936, his new organization achieved national recognition by correctly predicting that [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt) would defeat [Alf Landon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Landon) in the U.S. Presidential election, besting a poll based on over two million returned questionnaires conducted by the widely-respected *[Literary Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_Digest)* magazine. Gallup's poll was based on a more representative sample of the American electorate reflected in just 50,000 more selectively chosen respondents.

Twelve years later, his organization suffered its moment of greatest ignominy by predicting that [Thomas Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Dewey) would defeat [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) in the [1948 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._presidential_election,_1948) by between 5% and 15%. Truman won the election by 4.5%, with Gallup attributing the error in his results to ending polling three weeks before Election Day, which failed to account for an unexpected Truman's comeback.

In 1947, he launched the [Gallup International Association](wikipedia://en/29811137), an international association of polling organizations.[^o8] With friends-cum-rivals [Elmo Roper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmo_Roper) and [Archibald Crossley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Crossley), he was instrumental in the establishment of the Market Research Council, the National Council on Public Polls, and the [American Association for Public Opinion Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_Public_Opinion_Research).[^o9] In 1948, with [Claude E. Robinson](wikipedia://en/19650017), he founded [Gallup & Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_&_Robinson), an [advertising research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_research) company.

In 1958, Gallup grouped all of his polling operations under what became [The Gallup Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gallup_Organization).

Gallup died in 1984 of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction) at his summer home in [Tschingel ob Gunten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigriswil), a village in the [Bernese Oberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Oberland) of [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland). He was buried in [Princeton Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Cemetery). His wife, the former Ophelia S. Miller, died in 1988, and their son, writer and pollster [George Gallup Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gallup_Jr.), died in 2011.[^o10]
