# Leopold Vietoris

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leopold Vietoris"}

**Leopold Vietoris** ( ; , ; 4 June 1891 – 9 April 2002) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [World War I veteran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_last_surviving_World_War_I_veterans) and [supercentenarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercentenarian). He was born in [Radkersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radkersburg) and died in [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck).

:::

He was known for his contributions to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954)—notably the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](wikipedia://en/378677)—and other fields of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), his interest in [mathematical history](wikipedia://en/14220), and for being a keen [alpinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpinist).

## Biography {#Biography}

Vietoris studied mathematics and geometry at the [Vienna University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Wien).[^o4] He was drafted in 1914 in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) and was wounded in September that same year.[^o4] On 4 November 1918, one week before the [Armistice of Villa Giusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Villa_Giusti), he became an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) [prisoner of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war).[^o4] After returning to Austria, he attended the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), where he earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1920, with a thesis written under the supervision of [Gustav von Escherich](wikipedia://en/29030428) and [Wilhelm Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921).[^o4]

In autumn 1928 he married his first wife Klara Riccabona, who later died while giving birth to their sixth daughter.[^o4] In 1936 he married Klara's sister, Maria Riccabona.[^o4]

Vietoris was survived by his six daughters, 17 grandchildren, and 30 great-grandchildren.[^o6]

He lends his name to a few mathematical concepts:

- **[Vietoris topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietoris_topology)**
- **Vietoris [homology](wikipedia://en/142432)** (see [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory))
- **[Mayer–Vietoris sequence](wikipedia://en/378677)**
- **[Vietoris–Begle mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/2511424)**
- **[Vietoris–Rips complex](wikipedia://en/13056778)**

Vietoris remained scientifically active in his later years, even writing one paper on trigonometric sums at the age of 103.[^o7]

Vietoris lived to be 110 years and 309 days old, and became the oldest verified Austrian man ever.[^o8]

## Decorations and awards {#Decorations_and_awards}

- [Austrian Decoration for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Decoration_for_Science_and_Art) (1973)
- [Grand Gold Decoration for Services to the Republic of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Gold_Decoration_for_Services_to_the_Republic_of_Austria) (1981)
- Honorary member of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973) (1992)
