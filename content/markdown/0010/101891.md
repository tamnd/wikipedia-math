# Gian-Carlo Rota

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gian-Carlo Rota"}

**Gian-Carlo Rota** (April 27, 1932 – April 18, 1999) was an Italian-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher). He spent most of his career at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), where he worked in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), and [phenomenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenology_%28philosophy%29).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rota was born in [Vigevano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigevano), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy). His father, Giovanni, an architect and prominent [antifascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifascist), was the brother of the mathematician Rosetta, who was the wife of the writer [Ennio Flaiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennio_Flaiano).[^o5] Gian-Carlo's family left Italy when he was 13 years old, initially going to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland).

Rota attended the [Colegio Americano de Quito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colegio_Americano_de_Quito) in Ecuador, and graduated with an A.B. in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1953 after completing a senior thesis, titled "On the solubility of linear equations in [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752)", under the supervision of [William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644). He then pursued graduate studies at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), where he received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics in 1956 after completing a doctoral dissertation, titled "Extension Theory Of Ordinary Linear [Differential Operators](wikipedia://en/263886)", under the supervision of [Jacob T. Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_T._Schwartz).[^o6][^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Much of Rota's career was spent as a professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT), where he was and remains the only person ever to be appointed Professor of Applied Mathematics and Philosophy. Rota was also the [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185) Professor of Applied Mathematics.

In addition to his professorships at MIT, Rota held four honorary degrees, from the University of Strasbourg, France (1984); the [University of L'Aquila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_L'Aquila), Italy (1990); the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), Italy (1996); and [Brooklyn Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Polytechnic) (1997). Beginning in 1966 he was a consultant at [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory), frequently visiting to lecture, discuss, and collaborate, notably with his friend [Stanisław Ulam](wikipedia://en/41531). He was also a consultant for the [Rand Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Corporation) (1966–71) and for the [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory) (1969–1973). Rota was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1982, was vice president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) from 1995–97, and was a member of numerous other mathematical and philosophical organizations.[^o9]

He taught a difficult but very popular course in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). He also taught Applications of Calculus, [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), and [Combinatorial Theory](wikipedia://en/5170). His philosophy course in [phenomenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenology_%28philosophy%29) was offered on Friday nights to keep the enrollment manageable. Among his many eccentricities, he would not teach without a can of [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola), and handed out prizes ranging from [Hershey bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey_bar) to [pocket knives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_knives) to students who asked questions in class or did well on tests.[^o10][^o11]

Rota began his career as a [functional analyst](wikipedia://en/11180), but switched to become a distinguished [combinatorialist](wikipedia://en/5170). His series of ten papers on the "Foundations of Combinatorics" in the 1960s is credited with making it a respectable branch of modern mathematics.[^o9] He said that the one combinatorial idea he would like to be remembered for is the correspondence between combinatorial problems and problems of the location of the zeroes of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000).[^o12] He worked on the theory of [incidence algebras](wikipedia://en/166980) (which generalize the 19th-century theory of [Möbius inversion](wikipedia://en/20963)) and popularized their study among combinatorialists, set the [umbral calculus](wikipedia://en/214124) on a rigorous foundation, unified the theory of [Sheffer sequences](wikipedia://en/218596) and [polynomial sequences](wikipedia://en/195990) of [binomial type](wikipedia://en/200077), and worked on fundamental problems in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). His philosophical work was largely in the [phenomenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenology_%28philosophy%29) of [Edmund Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl).[^o13]

Rota founded the *[Advances in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advances_in_Mathematics)* journal in 1961.[^o9]

## Death {#Death}

Rota died of [atherosclerotic cardiac disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atherosclerosis) on April 18, 1999, apparently in his sleep at his home in Cambridge, Massachusetts.
