# Chosen-ciphertext attack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chosen-ciphertext attack"}

A **chosen-ciphertext attack** (**CCA**) is an [attack model](wikipedia://en/3201589) for [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) in which the cryptanalyst can gather information by obtaining the decryptions of chosen ciphertexts. From those pieces of information, the adversary can attempt to recover the secret key used for decryption.

:::

For formal definitions of security against chosen-ciphertext attacks, see, for example, [Michael Luby](wikipedia://en/9552275)[^o2] and [Mihir Bellare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihir_Bellare) et al.[^o3]

## Introduction {#Introduction}

A number of otherwise-secure schemes can be defeated under chosen-ciphertext attack For example, the [El Gamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Gamal) cryptosystem is [semantically secure](wikipedia://en/960585) under [chosen-plaintext attack](wikipedia://en/63852), but the semantic security can be trivially defeated under a chosen-ciphertext attack. Early versions of [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) padding that were used in the [SSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Sockets_Layer) protocol were vulnerable to a sophisticated [adaptive chosen-ciphertext attack](wikipedia://en/426533), which revealed SSL session keys. Chosen-ciphertext attacks have implications for some self-synchronizing [stream ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_ciphers) as well. Designers of tamper-resistant cryptographic [smart cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_cards) must be particularly cognizant of such attacks, as those devices may be completely under the control of an adversary, whuch can issue a large number of chosen-ciphertexts in an attempt to recover the hidden secret key.

It was not clear at all whether public key cryptosystems could withstand the chosen ciphertext attack until the initial breakthrough work of [Moni Naor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moni_Naor) and [Moti Yung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moti_Yung) in 1990, which suggested a mode of dual encryption with [integrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_integrity) proof (now known as the "Naor-Yung" encryption paradigm).[^o5] The work allowed understanding the notion of security against chosen ciphertext attack to become clearer and facilitated research in the direction of constructing systems with various protections against variants of the attack.

When a cryptosystem is vulnerable to chosen-ciphertext attack, implementers must be careful to avoid situations in which an adversary might be able to decrypt chosen-ciphertexts (i.e., avoid providing a decryption oracle). That can be more difficult than it appears, as even partially-chosen ciphertexts can permit subtle attacks. Additionally, other issues exist, and some cryptosystems (such as [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29)) use the same mechanism to sign messages and to decrypt them. That permits attacks when [hashing](wikipedia://en/439526) is not used on the message to be signed.

A better approach is to use a cryptosystem that is [provably secure](wikipedia://en/1182348) under chosen-ciphertext attack, including (among others) [RSA-OAEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_Asymmetric_Encryption_Padding) secure under the random oracle heuristics, [Cramer-Shoup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramer-Shoup_system) which was the first public key practical system to be secure. For symmetric encryption schemes, it is known that [authenticated encryption](wikipedia://en/2244296), which is a primitive based on [symmetric encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_encryption), gives security against chosen ciphertext attacks, as was first shown by [Jonathan Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Katz_%28computer_scientist%29) and [Moti Yung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moti_Yung).[^o6]

## Varieties {#Varieties}

Chosen-ciphertext attacks, like other attacks, may be adaptive or non-adaptive. In an adaptive chosen-ciphertext attack, the attacker can use the results from prior decryptions to inform their choices of which ciphertexts to have decrypted. In a non-adaptive attack, the attacker chooses the ciphertexts to have decrypted without seeing any of the resulting plaintexts. After seeing the plaintexts, the attacker can no longer obtain the decryption of additional ciphertexts.

### Lunchtime attacks {#Lunchtime_attacks}

A specially-noted variant of the chosen-ciphertext attack is the "lunchtime," "midnight," or "indifferent" attack in which an attacker may make adaptive chosen-ciphertext queries but only up until a certain point after which the attacker must demonstrate some improved ability to attack the system.[^o9] The term "lunchtime attack" refers to the idea that a user's computer, with the ability to decrypt, is available to an attacker while the user is out to lunch. That form of the attack was the first one commonly discussed: obviously, if the attacker has the ability to make adaptive chosen ciphertext queries, no encrypted message would be safe, at least until that ability is taken away. The attack is sometimes called the "non-adaptive chosen ciphertext attack";[^o10] Here, "non-adaptive" refers to the fact that the attacker cannot adapt the queries in response to the challenge, which is given after the ability to make chosen ciphertext queries has expired.

### Adaptive chosen-ciphertext attack {#Adaptive_chosen-ciphertext_attack}

A (full) adaptive chosen-ciphertext attack is an attack in which ciphertexts may be chosen adaptively before and after a challenge ciphertext is given to the attacker, with only the stipulation that the challenge ciphertext may not itself be queried. That is a stronger attack notion than the lunchtime attack and is commonly referred to as a CCA2 attack, as compared to a CCA1 (lunchtime) attack.[^o10] Few practical attacks are of this form. Rather, this model is important for its use in proofs of security against chosen-ciphertext attacks. A proof that attacks in this model are impossible implies that any realistic chosen-ciphertext attack cannot be performed.

A practical adaptive chosen-ciphertext attack is the Bleichenbacher attack against [PKCS#1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKCS1).[^o13]

Numerous cryptosystems are proven secure against adaptive chosen-ciphertext attacks. Some prove that the security property is based only on algebraic assumptions. Others additionally require an idealized random oracle assumption. For example, the [Cramer-Shoup system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramer-Shoup_system)[^o9] is secure based on number theoretic assumptions and no idealization, and after a number of subtle investigations, it was also established that the practical scheme [RSA-OAEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA-OAEP) is secure under the RSA assumption in the idealized random oracle model.[^o14]

:::
