# Maxim Kontsevich

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maxim Kontsevich"}

**Maxim Lvovich Kontsevich** (Макси́м Льво́вич Конце́вич, ; born 25 August 1964)[^o3] is a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people)[^o4] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and mathematical physicist. He is a professor at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) and a distinguished professor at the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami). He received the [Henri Poincaré Prize](wikipedia://en/4890712) in 1997, the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1998, the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in 2008, the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) and [Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Fundamental_Physics) in 2012, and the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933) in 2015.[^o5]

:::

## Academic career and research {#Academic_career_and_research}

He was born into the family of [Lev Kontsevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Kontsevich), Soviet orientalist and author of the [Kontsevich system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontsevich_system). After ranking second in the All-Union Mathematics Olympiads, he attended [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) but left without a degree in 1985 to become a researcher at the Institute for Information Transmission Problems in Moscow.[^o7] While at the institute he published papers that caught the interest of the [Max Planck Institute in Bonn](wikipedia://en/1386960) and was invited for three months. Just before the end of his time there, he attended a five-day international meeting, the [Arbeitstagung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbeitstagung), where he sketched a proof of the [Witten conjecture](wikipedia://en/34694574) to the amazement of [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and other mathematicians and his invitation to the institute was subsequently extended to three years.[^o7]

The next year he finished the proof and worked on various topics on mathematical physics and in 1992 received his [Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat.) at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) under [Don Bernard Zagier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bernard_Zagier). His thesis outlines a proof[^o8] of a conjecture by [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) that two [quantum gravitational models](wikipedia://en/25312) are equivalent. In 1992, Kontsevich was appointed to a full professorship in mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), before moving in 1995 to France, where he joined the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in [Bures-sur-Yvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bures-sur-Yvette) as a permanent member.

His work concentrates on geometric aspects of [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), most notably on [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), [quantization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_%28physics%29), and [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671). One of his results is a formal [deformation quantization](wikipedia://en/26049975) that holds for any [Poisson manifold](wikipedia://en/882902). He also introduced the [Kontsevich integral](wikipedia://en/18700023), a topological [invariant of knots (and links)](wikipedia://en/166898) defined by complicated integrals analogous to [Feynman integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation), and generalizing the classical [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss) [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999). In [topological field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_field_theory), he introduced the [moduli space of stable maps](wikipedia://en/2300947), which may be considered a mathematically rigorous formulation of the [Feynman integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation) for [topological string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_string_theory). With Alexei Belov-Kanel he proved that the [Dixmier conjecture](wikipedia://en/12929412) is equivalent to the [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823).[^o9]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 1998, he won the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) "for his contributions to algebraic geometry, topology, and mathematical physics, including the proof of Witten's conjecture of intersection numbers in moduli spaces of stable curves, construction of the [universal Vassiliev invariant of knots](wikipedia://en/3014395#The_universal_Vassiliev_invariant), and formal quantization of Poisson manifolds."[^o11] In July 2012, he was an inaugural awardee of the [Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Fundamental_Physics), the creation of physicist and internet entrepreneur, [Yuri Milner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Milner).[^o12] Also in 2012, he was awarded the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize).[^o13] In 2015, he was awarded [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933).
