# Hypohamiltonian graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypohamiltonian graph"}

*Image: Lindgren hypohamiltonian 15.svg.* A hypohamiltonian graph constructed by (Lindgren 1967)

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) *G* is said to be **hypohamiltonian** if *G* itself does not have a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) but every graph formed by removing a single vertex from *G* is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph).

:::

## History {#History}

Hypohamiltonian graphs were first studied by (Sousselier 1963). (Lindgren 1967) cites (Gaudin Herz Rossi 1964) and (Busacker Saaty 1965) as additional early papers on the subject; another early work is by (Herz Duby Vigué 1967).

(Grötschel 1980) sums up much of the research in this area with the following sentence: “The articles dealing with those graphs ... usually exhibit new classes of hypohamiltonian or hypotraceable graphs showing that for certain orders *n* such graphs indeed exist or that they possess strange and unexpected properties.”

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Hypohamiltonian graphs arise in [integer programming](wikipedia://en/411215) solutions to the [traveling salesman problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_salesman_problem): certain kinds of hypohamiltonian graphs define [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) of the *traveling salesman polytope*, a shape defined as the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the set of possible solutions to the traveling salesman problem, and these facets may be used in [cutting-plane methods](wikipedia://en/1597680) for solving the problem.[^o12] (Grötschel 1980) observes that the [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/7543) of determining whether a graph is hypohamiltonian, although unknown, is likely to be high, making it difficult to find facets of these types except for those defined by small hypohamiltonian graphs; fortunately, the smallest graphs lead to the strongest inequalities for this application.[^o14]

Concepts closely related to hypohamiltonicity have also been used by (Park Lim Kim 2007) to measure the [fault tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault-tolerant_system) of [network topologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_topology) for [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Every hypohamiltonian graph must be 3-[vertex-connected](wikipedia://en/7566175), as the removal of any two vertices leaves a [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437), which is connected. There exist *n*-vertex hypohamiltonian graphs in which the maximum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) is *n*/2, and in which there are approximately *n*<sup>2</sup>/4 edges.[^o18]

*Image: Thomassen girth-3 hypohamiltonian.svg.* Thomassen's (1974b) girth-3 hypohamiltonian graph

(Herz Duby Vigué 1967) [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that every hypohamiltonian graph has [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 5 or more, but this was disproved by (Thomassen 1974b), who found examples with girth 3 and 4. For some time it was unknown whether a hypohamiltonian graph could be [planar](wikipedia://en/24314), but several examples are now known,[^o21] the smallest of which has 40 vertices.[^o22] Every planar hypohamiltonian graph has at least one vertex with only three incident edges.[^o23]

If a [3-regular graph](wikipedia://en/980508) is Hamiltonian, its [edges can be colored](wikipedia://en/690647) with three colors: use alternating colors for the edges on the Hamiltonian cycle (which must have [even](wikipedia://en/143135) length by the [handshaking lemma](wikipedia://en/9607933)) and a third color for all remaining edges. Therefore, all [snarks](wikipedia://en/727811), [bridgeless](wikipedia://en/1305071) cubic graphs that require four edge colors, must be non-Hamiltonian, and many known snarks are hypohamiltonian. Every hypohamiltonian snark is *bicritical*: removing any two vertices leaves a subgraph the edges of which can be colored with only three colors.[^o24] A 3-coloring of this subgraph can be simply described: after removing one vertex, the remaining vertices contain a Hamiltonian cycle. After removing a second vertex, this [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) becomes a [path](wikipedia://en/638889), the edges of which may be colored by alternating between two colors. The remaining edges form a [matching](wikipedia://en/581797) and may be colored with a third color.

The color classes of any 3-coloring of the edges of a 3-regular graph form three matchings such that each edge belongs to exactly one of the matchings. Hypohamiltonian snarks do not have a partition into matchings of this type, but (Häggkvist 2007) conjectures that the edges of any hypohamiltonian snark may be used to form six matchings such that each edge belongs to exactly two of the matchings. This is a special case of the Berge–Fulkerson conjecture that any snark has six matchings with this property.

Hypohamiltonian graphs cannot be [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431): in a bipartite graph, a vertex can only be deleted to form a Hamiltonian subgraph if it belongs to the larger of the graph's two color classes. However, every bipartite graph occurs as an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) of some hypohamiltonian graph.[^o26]

## Examples {#Examples}

The smallest hypohamiltonian graph is the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) (Herz Duby Vigué 1967). More generally, the [generalized Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) GP(*n*,2) is hypohamiltonian when *n* is congruent to 5 [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 6;[^o30] the Petersen graph is the instance of this construction with *n* = 5.

(Lindgren 1967) found another infinite class of hypohamiltonian graphs in which the number of vertices is 4 mod 6. Lindgren's construction consists of a cycle of length 3 mod 6 and a single central vertex; the central vertex is connected to every third vertex of the cycle by edges he calls spokes, and the vertices two positions away from each spoke endpoint are connected to each other. Again, the smallest instance of Lindgren's construction is the Petersen graph.

Additional infinite families are given by (Bondy 1972), (Doyen van Diest 1975), and (Gutt 1977).

:::

::: {#o36 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Enumeration"}

## Enumeration {#Enumeration}

[Václav Chvátal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Václav_Chvátal) proved in 1973 that for all sufficiently large *n* there exists a hypohamiltonian graph with *n* vertices. Taking into account subsequent discoveries,[^o37] “sufficiently large” is now known to mean that such graphs exist for all *n* ≥ 18. A complete list of hypohamiltonian graphs with at most 17 vertices is known:[^o38] they are the 10-vertex Petersen graph, a 13-vertex graph and a 15-vertex graph found by computer searches of (Herz 1968), and four 16-vertex graphs. There exist at least thirteen 18-vertex hypohamiltonian graphs. By applying the flip-flop method of (Chvátal 1973) to the Petersen graph and the [flower snark](wikipedia://en/23759337), it is possible to show that the number of hypohamiltonian graphs, and more specifically the number of hypohamiltonian snarks, grows as an [exponential](wikipedia://en/191933) function of the number of vertices.[^o41]

:::

::: {#o43 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Graph theorists have also studied *hypotraceable graphs*, graphs that do not contain a Hamiltonian path but such that every subset of *n* − 1 vertices may be connected by a path.[^o44] Analogous definitions of hypohamiltonicity and hypotraceability for [directed graphs](wikipedia://en/19721986) have been considered by several authors.[^o45]

An equivalent definition of hypohamiltonian graphs is that their longest cycle has length *n* − 1 and that the intersection of all longest cycles is empty. (Menke Zamfirescu Zamfirescu 1998) investigate graphs with the same property that the intersection of longest cycles is empty but in which the longest cycle length is shorter than *n* − 1. (Herz 1968) defines the *cyclability* of a graph as the largest number *k* such that every *k* vertices belong to a cycle; the hypohamiltonian graphs are exactly the graphs that have cyclability *n* − 1. Similarly, (Park Lim Kim 2007) define a graph to be ƒ-*fault hamiltonian* if the removal of at most ƒ vertices leaves a Hamiltonian subgraph. (Schauerte Zamfirescu 2006) study the graphs with cyclability *n* − 2.

:::
