# Quadratic algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quadratic algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quadratic algebra** is an [algebra over a ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring) for which the algebra extends the ring by a new element that satisfies a [monic](wikipedia://en/314730), [quadratic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_polynomial) with coefficients in the ring.

:::

There are free and graded quadratic algebras.

## Free quadratic algebras {#Free_quadratic_algebras}

Given a commutative [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R*, and the ring of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) *R*\[X\], a free quadratic algebra may be defined as [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) by a polynomial [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977): "An *R*-algebra of the form *R*\[X\]/(X<sup>2</sup> − *a* X − *b*) where X<sup>2</sup> − *a* X − *b* is a monic quadratic polynomial in *R*\[X\] and (X<sup>2</sup> − *a* X − *b*) Is the ideal it generates, is a **free quadratic algebra** over R."[^o3]

Alternatively, a free quadratic extension of *R* is *S* = *R* ⊕ *Rx* with *xx* = *ax* + *b* for some *a* and *b* in R.[^o4] Denote it *S* = (*R, a, b*).

Then (*R, a, b*) ≅ (*R, c, d*) [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) there is a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666) α and an element β of *R* such that[^o4]

*c* = α(*a* − 2 β ) and

*d* = α<sup>2</sup>(β *a* + *b* −β<sup>2</sup>).

If *R* is taken as the ring Z of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), then the quadratic algebra $\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^2 + 1)$ is called the [Gaussian integers](wikipedia://en/48628).

If *R* is taken as the field of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), then there are three [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of $\mathbb{R}[X]/(X^2 - a X - b)$:[^o3]

- if *a*<sup>2</sup> + 4 *b* = 0, the [dual numbers](wikipedia://en/42169)
- if *a*<sup>2</sup> + 4 *b* > 0, the [split-complex numbers](wikipedia://en/893559)
- if *a*<sup>2</sup> + 4 *b* \< 0, the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).

Suppose the quadratic algebra *S* has basis {1,*z*} and $z^2 = a z + b.$ Then an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) σ on S is given by $\sigma(z) = a - z,$ and if $x = \lambda + \mu z,$ then $\sigma(x) = \lambda + \mu a - \mu z.$

Trace and norm are then defined using the involution:[^o5]

$$tr(x) = x + \sigma(x) = 2 \lambda + \mu a \in R ,$$

$$n(x) = x \sigma(x) = \lambda^2 - \lambda \mu a - \mu^2 b \in R.$$

## Graded quadratic algebras {#Graded_quadratic_algebras}

A **graded quadratic algebra** *A* is determined by a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of generators *V* = *A*<sub>1</sub> and a subspace of homogeneous quadratic relations *S* ⊂ *V* ⊗ *V*.[^o7] Thus

$$A=T(V)/\langle S\rangle$$

and inherits its grading from the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) *T*(*V*).

If the subspace of relations is instead allowed to also contain inhomogeneous degree 2 elements, i.e. *S* ⊂ *k* ⊕ *V* ⊕ (*V* ⊗ *V*), this construction results in a **filtered quadratic algebra**.

A graded quadratic algebra *A* as above admits a **quadratic dual**: the quadratic algebra generated by *V*<sup>\*</sup> and with quadratic relations forming the orthogonal complement of *S* in *V*<sup>\*</sup> ⊗ *V*<sup>\*</sup>.

A quadratic algebra may be a [filtered algebra](wikipedia://en/2954244) generated by degree one elements, with defining relations of degree 2. It was pointed out by [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086) that such algebras play an important role in the theory of [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678). The most important class of graded quadratic algebras is [Koszul algebras](wikipedia://en/7987653).

A **quadratic-linear algebra** is an [algebra over a field](wikipedia://en/191788) with a [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) such that all relations are sums of monomials of degrees 1 or 2 in the generators. They were introduced by (txt, p.101). An example is the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), with generators a basis of the Lie algebra and relations of the form *XY* – *YX* – \[*X*, *Y*\] = 0.

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628), [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) and [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) of a finite-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) are graded quadratic (in fact, Koszul) algebras.
- The [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of a finite-dimensional [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is a filtered quadratic algebra.
- The [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) of a finite-dimensional vector space equipped with a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) is a filtered quadratic algebra.
- The [Weyl algebra](wikipedia://en/491095) of a finite-dimensional [symplectic vector space](wikipedia://en/292852) is a filtered quadratic algebra.
