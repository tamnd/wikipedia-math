# Caristi fixed-point theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Caristi fixed-point theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Caristi fixed-point theorem** (also known as the **Caristi–Kirk fixed-point theorem**) generalizes the [Banach fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/46676) for maps of a [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) into itself. Caristi's [fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/1011848) modifies the $\varepsilon$-[variational principle of](wikipedia://en/21341034) [Ekeland](wikipedia://en/31377247) (1974, 1979).[^o2][^o3] The conclusion of Caristi's theorem is equivalent to metric completeness, as proved by Weston (1977).[^o4] The original result is due to the mathematicians [James Caristi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Caristi) and [William Arthur Kirk](wikipedia://en/11968842).[^o5]

:::

Caristi fixed-point theorem can be applied to derive other classical fixed-point results, and also to prove the existence of bounded solutions of a [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377).[^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $(X, d)$ be a complete metric space. Let $T : X \to X$ and $f : X \to [0, +\infty)$ be a [lower semicontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semicontinuous) function from $X$ into the non-negative [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers). Suppose that, for all points $x$ in $X,$

$$d(x, T(x)) \leq f(x) - f(T(x)).$$

Then $T$ has a fixed point in $X;$ that is, a point $x_0$ such that $T(x_0) = x_0.$ The proof of this result utilizes [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442) to guarantee the existence of a [minimal element](wikipedia://en/303398) which turns out to be a desired fixed point.[^o9]

:::
