# Stanley Skewes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stanley Skewes"}

**Stanley Skewes** (; 1899–1988) was a South African [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), best known for his discovery of the [Skewes's number](wikipedia://en/250178) in 1933. He was one of [John Edensor Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371)'s students at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University). Skewes's numbers contributed to the refinement of the [theory of prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23692).

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Skewes obtained a degree in [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) from the University of Cape Town before emigrating to England. He studied mathematics at Cambridge University and obtained a PhD in mathematics in 1938.

He discovered the first Skewes's number in 1933. This is also referred to as the Riemann true Skewes's number owing to its relationship to the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) as related to [prime number theory](wikipedia://en/23692). He discovered the second Skewes's number in 1955. This number was applicable if the Riemann hypothesis is false. Since his original discovery the numbers have been further refined.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

*Image: Young Skewes Schieldrop Tricomi Cartwright Zurich1932.tif.* Ms. Skewes (left, foreground) accompanied Stanley Skewes at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842), Zürich 1932.

Stanley Skewes was born in [Germiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germiston), South Africa in 1899. His parents were Henry (Harry) Skewes, a tin miner and [assayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgical_assay) from [Cury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cury), [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) and Emily Moyle, who was American by birth. His parents moved from [Redruth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redruth), [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall) in 1894 to the [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Colony), South Africa.

He married Ena Allen. She was the daughter of the head chef at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), and a talented opera singer. Among his contemporaries at Cambridge was [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208). They rowed together at Cambridge. Although Skewes returned to South Africa, he revisited Cambridge and Cornwall. He was also a keen rugby player in his youth.

Skewes and his number are discussed by [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov) in his book *Of Matters Great and Small* and in the 20th edition of the *Guinness Book of Records*. A memorandum written by Skewes on his retirement was kept in a glass case in the department of mathematics at the University of Cape Town. The memorandum discuses Skewes's number and further development of it.

He died in 1988 in Cape Town, South Africa.
