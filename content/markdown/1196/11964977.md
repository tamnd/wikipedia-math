# Hans Hamburger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Hamburger"}

**Hans Ludwig Hamburger** (5 August 1889, [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) – 14 August 1956, [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was a professor at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin), the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne), and [Ankara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_University).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hans was the elder son of Karl Hamburger and Margarethe Levy. He was of Jewish heritage, but baptised as a protestant.[^o4] His father was a lawyer and mixed in learned circles in Berlin. Hans attended the [Royal French Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_French_Gymnasium) in Berlin from 1898 to 1907. Hamburger obtained his Ph.D. from the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) in 1914 under the supervision of [Alfred Pringsheim](wikipedia://en/1068126) and after war service obtained his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) for a thesis on *Extensions of the [Stieltjes moment problem](wikipedia://en/3242790)*. He was appointed [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin) in 1921 and professor at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) in 1926. He left Cologne in 1935, after the imposition of the [Nuremberg Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws), and returned to his mother's home in Berlin. In 1939, he left Germany, and from 1941 to 1946 he was lecturer at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton). After the war, he received an invitation to return to the University of Cologne, but instead moved to [Ankara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_University) in 1947. He returned to Cologne in 1953.[^o2][^o6]

He was married briefly twice, to Malla Jessen in 1927 and to Vera Schereschevsky two months prior to his death in 1956.[^o2]

## Research {#Research}

The main results from Hamburger's pre-war research concerned the [Hamburger moment problem](wikipedia://en/3216491), which is named after him, and his work on the [Carathéodory conjecture](wikipedia://en/17228280), beginning in Berlin in 1922.[^o2] His work on this topic was published only 20 years later, a reflection of the difficulty of the problem. He attributed the conjecture to [Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625) and his approach was a reformulation of the conjecture in terms of a bound on the index of an isolated [umbilic point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbilic_point) of a surface. His attribution remains in place today, and his solution of the [Carathéodory conjecture](wikipedia://en/17228280) in the real analytic case is regarded as complete. He also proved a [converse theorem](wikipedia://en/29554685) for the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).[^o2]

After the war, Hamburger's research primarily concerned [linear transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) of [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).[^o2] Hamburger published the textbook, *Linear Transformations in n-Dimensional Vector Space. An Introduction to the Theory of Hilbert Space (1951)* with [Margaret Grimshaw](wikipedia://en/53709478).
