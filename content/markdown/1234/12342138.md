# Marinus of Tyre

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marinus of Tyre"}

*Image: Marinus e Ptolomeu.jpg.* Cover for "Tabulae geographicae" (1578), work of Ptolemy. Depicted are both Ptolemy and Marinus of Tyre, very likely in this order.

**Marinus of Tyre** (Μαρῖνος ὁ Τύριος, *Marînos ho Týrios*; c. AD 70–130) was a [Greek-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) [Roman geographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Graeco-Roman_geographers), [cartographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who founded [mathematical geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_geography) and provided the underpinnings of [Claudius Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy)'s influential *[Geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_%28Ptolemy%29)*.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Marinus was originally from [Tyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre,_Lebanon) in the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia_under_Roman_rule).[^o3] His work was a precursor to that of the great geographer [Claudius Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy), who used Marinus' work as a source for his *[Geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_%28Ptolemy%29)* and acknowledges his great obligations to him.[^n1][^o5] Ptolemy said, "Marinus says of the merchant class generally that they are only intent on their business, and have little interest in exploration, and that often through their love of boasting they magnify distances."[^o6] Later, Marinus was also cited by the [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) geographer [al-Masʿūdī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_al-Hasan_'Alī_al-Mas'ūdī). Beyond this, little is known of his life.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Marinus' geographical treatise is lost and known only from Ptolemy's remarks. He introduced improvements to the construction of maps and developed a system of [nautical charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautical_chart). His chief legacy is that he was the first to assign to each place a proper [latitude](wikipedia://en/17616) and [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude). His [zero meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_meridian) ran through the westernmost land known during his time, the [Isles of the Blessed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isles_of_the_Blessed), around the location of the present-day [Canary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands) or [Cape Verde Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde_Islands). He used the parallel of [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes) for measurements of latitude.

*Image: Marinus LO-IV-178H LTVT.JPG.* Lunar crater Marinus (NASA 1967)

Ptolemy mentions several revisions of Marinus' geographical work, which is often dated to AD 114, although this is uncertain. Marinus estimated a length of 180,000 [stadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_%28unit%29) for the equator, roughly corresponding to a [circumference of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumference_of_the_Earth) of 33,300 km, about 17% less than the actual value.

Marinus also carefully studied the works of his predecessors and the diaries of travelers. His maps were the first in the Roman Empire to show China. He invented [equirectangular projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equirectangular_projection), which is still used in map creation today. A few of Marinus' opinions are also reported by Ptolemy. Marinus was of the opinion that the [World Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanus) was separated into an eastern and a western part by the continents of Europe, Asia and Africa. He thought that the inhabited world stretched in latitude from [Thule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule) (Norway) to [Agisymba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agisymba) (around the [Tropic of Capricorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Capricorn)) and in longitude from the [Isles of the Blessed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isles_of_the_Blessed) (around the [Canaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands)) to [Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serica) (China). Marinus also coined the term [Antarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic), referring to the opposite of the [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic). <https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/binaries/content/assets/metofficegovuk/pdf/research/library-and-archive/library/publications/factsheets/pioneers_scott-bae-1910_1913.pdf>

In 1935, an [impact crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_crater) on the Moon was named after Marinus.
