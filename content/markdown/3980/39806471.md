# Ignatov's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ignatov's theorem"}

In [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), **Ignatov's theorem** is a basic result on the distribution of record values of a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *X*<sub>1</sub>, *X*<sub>2</sub>, ... be an infinite sequence of [independent and identically distributed random variables](wikipedia://en/30876902). The *initial rank* of the *n*th term of this sequence is the value *r* such that *X*<sub>*i*</sub> ≥ *X*<sub>*n*</sub> for exactly *r* values of *i* less than or equal to *n*. Let **Y**<sub>*k*</sub> = (*Y*<sub>*k*,1</sub>, *Y*<sub>*k*,2</sub>, *Y*<sub>*k*,3</sub>, ...) denote the stochastic process consisting of the terms *X*<sub>*i*</sub> having initial rank *k*; that is, *Y*<sub>*k*,*j*</sub> is the *j*th term of the stochastic process that achieves initial rank *k*. The sequence **Y**<sub>*k*</sub> is called the sequence of *k*th **partial records**. Ignatov's theorem states that the sequences **Y**<sub>1</sub>, **Y**<sub>2</sub>, **Y**<sub>3</sub>, ... are independent and identically distributed.

:::

## Note {#Note}

The theorem is named after Tzvetan Ignatov (1942–2024), a Bulgarian professor in probability and mathematical statistics at Sofia University. Due to it and his general contributions to mathematics, Prof. Ignatov was granted a Doctor Honoris Causa degree in 2013 from Sofia University. The recognition is given on extremely rare occasions and only to scholars with internationally landmark results.
