# Sug Woo Shin

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Sug Woo Shin"}

**Sug Woo Shin** (; born 1978) is a professor of mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) working in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [automorphic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_forms), and the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).

:::

## Education {#Education}

From 1994 to 1996 when he was in Seoul Science High School, Shin won two gold medals (including a perfect score in 1995) and one bronze medal while representing South Korea at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208).[^o16][^o6] He graduated from [Seoul National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_National_University) with a Bachelor of Science degree in mathematics in 2000.[^o16] He received his PhD in mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2007 under the supervision of [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931).

## Career {#Career}

Shin was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 2007 to 2008, a Dickson Instructor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) from 2008 to 2010, and again a member at the Institute for Advanced Study from 2010 to 2011.[^o16] He was an assistant professor of mathematics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 2011 to 2014.[^o16] In 2014, Shin moved to the Department of Mathematics at the University of California, Berkeley as an associate professor.[^o16] In 2020, Shin became a full professor of mathematics at the University of California, Berkeley.[^o9]

Shin is a visiting KIAS scholar at the [Korea Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/16984) and a visiting associate member of the [Pohang Mathematics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pohang_Mathematics_Institute).[^o16]

## Research {#Research}

In 2011, [Michael Harris](wikipedia://en/31954452)[^o11] and Shin[^o12] resolved the dependencies on improved forms of the [Arthur–Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/20419621) in the conditional proofs of generalizations of the [Sato–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/597837) by Harris (for products of non-[isogenous](wikipedia://en/1085176) [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225))[^o13] and Barnet-Lamb–Geraghty–Harris–[Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) (for arbitrary non-[CM](wikipedia://en/537183) holomorphic [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) of weight greater than or equal to two).[^o14]

## Awards {#Awards}

Shin received a [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 2013.[^o16]

Shin received a [Samsung Ho-Am Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho-Am_Prize_in_Science) in 2025[^o17] for his contributions to the Langlands Program.

He was elected to the 2026 class of Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o18]
