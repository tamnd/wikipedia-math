# Solution in radicals

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Solution in radicals"}

A **solution in radicals** or **algebraic solution** is an [expression](wikipedia://en/609125) of a solution of a [polynomial equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation) that is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/4283745), that is, relies only on [addition](wikipedia://en/61338), [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845), [division](wikipedia://en/53696), [raising to integer powers](wikipedia://en/99491), and extraction of [$n$th roots](wikipedia://en/235029) ([square roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_roots), [cube roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_roots), etc.).

:::

A well-known example is the [quadratic formula](wikipedia://en/59217)

$$x=\frac{-b \pm \sqrt {b^2-4ac\ }}{2a},$$

which expresses the solutions of the [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175)

$$ax^2 + bx + c =0.$$

There exist algebraic solutions for [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787)[^o2] and [quartic equations](wikipedia://en/251591),[^o3] which are more complicated than the quadratic formula. The [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518),[^o4] and, more generally [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), state that some [quintic equations](wikipedia://en/303368), such as

$$x^5-x+1=0,$$

do not have any algebraic solution. The same is true for every higher degree. However, for any degree there are some polynomial equations that have algebraic solutions; for example, the equation $x^{10} = 2$ can be solved as $x=\pm\sqrt[10]2.$ The eight other solutions are nonreal [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), which are also algebraic and have the form $x=\pm r\sqrt[10]2,$ where $r$ is a fifth [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950), which can be expressed with two [nested square roots](wikipedia://en/2503693). See also Quintic function for various other examples in degree 5.

[Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815) introduced a criterion allowing one to decide which equations are solvable in radicals. See [Radical extension](wikipedia://en/33085116) for the precise formulation of his result.
