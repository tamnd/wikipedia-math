# Mittag-Leffler function

*Image: MittagLeffler GaussToLorentz.gif.* The Mittag-Leffler function can be used to interpolate continuously between a Gaussian and a Lorentzian function.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mittag-Leffler function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Mittag-Leffler functions** are a family of [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function). They are complex-valued functions of a complex argument *z*, and moreover depend on one or two complex parameters.

:::

The **one-parameter Mittag-Leffler function**, introduced by [Gösta Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/1438792) in 1903,[^o2][^o3] can be defined by the Maclaurin series

$$E_{\alpha} (z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{z^k}{\Gamma(\alpha k + 1)},$$

where $\Gamma(x)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), and $\alpha$ is a complex parameter with $\operatorname{Re}\left(\alpha \right)> 0$.

The **two-parameter Mittag-Leffler function**, introduced by Wiman in 1905,[^o4][^o3] is occasionally called the **generalized Mittag-Leffler function**. It has an additional complex parameter $\beta$, and may be defined by the series[^o3][^o5]

$$E_{\alpha, \beta} (z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{z^k}{\Gamma(\alpha k + \beta)},$$

When $\beta=1$, the one-parameter function $E_\alpha = E_{\alpha,1}$ is recovered.

In the case $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are real and positive, the series converges for all values of the argument $z$, so the Mittag-Leffler function is an [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372). This class of functions are important in the theory of the [fractional calculus](wikipedia://en/229939).

See below for three-parameter generalizations.

## Some basic properties {#Some_basic_properties}

For $\alpha >0$, the Mittag-Leffler function $E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)$ is an entire function of [order](wikipedia://en/10372#Order_and_type) $1/\alpha$, and [type](wikipedia://en/10372#Order_and_type) $1$ for any value of $\beta$. In some sense, the Mittag-Leffler function is the simplest entire function of its order. The [indicator function](wikipedia://en/69319810) of $E_{\alpha}(z)$ is[^o7]p. 50

$$h_{E_\alpha}(\theta)=\begin{cases}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right),&\text{for }|\theta|\le\frac 1 2 \alpha\pi;\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$

This result actually holds for $\beta\neq1$ as well with some restrictions on $\beta$ when $\alpha=1$.[^o12]p. 67

The Mittag-Leffler function satisfies the recurrence property (Theorem 5.1 of [^o3])

$$E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\frac{1}{z}E_{\alpha,\beta-\alpha}(z)-\frac{1}{z \Gamma(\beta-\alpha)},$$

from which the following [asymptotic expansion](wikipedia://en/642090) holds : for $0<\alpha<2$ and $\mu$ real such that $\frac{\pi\alpha}{2} < \mu < \min(\pi, \pi\alpha)$ then for all $N\in\mathbb{N}^*, N\neq 1$, we can show the following asymptotic expansions (Section 6. of [^o3]):

\-as $\,|z|\to+\infty, |\text{arg}(z)|\leq \mu$:

$$E_{\alpha}(z) = \frac{1}{\alpha} \exp(z^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}) - \sum\limits_{k=1}^{N} \frac{1}{z^k\, \Gamma(1-\alpha k)} + O\left(\frac{1}{z^{N+1}}\right),$$

\-and as $\,|z|\to+\infty, \mu\leq|\text{arg}(z)|\leq\pi$:

$$E_{\alpha}(z) = - \sum\limits_{k=1}^N \frac{1}{z^k\Gamma(1-\alpha k)} + O\left(\frac{1}{z^{N+1}}\right).$$

A simpler estimate that can often be useful is given, thanks to the fact that the order and type of $E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)$ is $1/\alpha$ and $1$, respectively:[^o12]p. 62

$$|E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)|\le C\exp\left(\sigma|z|^{1/\alpha}\right)$$

for any positive $C$ and any $\sigma>1$.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

For $\alpha=0$, the series above equals the [Taylor expansion](wikipedia://en/30448) of the [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630) and consequently $E_{0,\beta}(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\frac{1}{1-z}$.

For $\alpha=\tfrac12,1,2$ we find: (Section 2 of [^o3])

[Error function](wikipedia://en/177111):

$$E_{\frac{1}{2}}(z) = \exp(z^2)\operatorname{erfc}(-z).$$

[Exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678):

$$E_{1}(z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{z^k}{\Gamma (k + 1)} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{z^k}{k!} = \exp(z).$$

[Hyperbolic functions](wikipedia://en/56567):

$$E_{2}(z) = \cosh(\sqrt{z}), \text{ and } E_{2}(-z^2) = \cos(z).$$

For $\beta=2$, we have

$$E_{1,2}(z) = \frac{e^z-1}{z},$$

$$E_{2,2}(z) = \frac{\sinh(\sqrt{z})}{\sqrt{z}}.$$

For $\alpha=0,1,2$, the integral

$$\int_0^z E_{\alpha}(-s^2) \, {\mathrm d}s$$

gives, respectively: $\arctan(z)$, $\tfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\operatorname{erf}(z)$, $\sin(z)$.

[Hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_functions):

For $p\in \mathbb{N}$ a general formula for a half-integer parameter $\frac{p}{2}$ is

$E_{\frac{p}{2}}(z) = {}_{0}F_{p-1}\bigg(;\frac{1}{p},\frac{2}{p},...,\frac{p-1}{p};\frac{z^2}{p^p}\bigg) + \frac{2^{\frac{p+1}{2}}z}{p!\sqrt{\pi}}{}_{1}F_{2p-1}\bigg(1;\frac{p+2}{2p},\frac{p+3}{2p},...,\frac{3p}{2p};\frac{z^2}{p^p}\bigg)$[^o20]

[Incomplete gamma function](wikipedia://en/456050):

For $p\in \mathbb{N}-\{1\}$

$E_{\frac{1}{p}}(z^{\frac{1}{p}}) = e^{z} \bigg[1+\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}\frac{\gamma(1-\frac{n}{p},z)}{\Gamma(1-\frac{n}{p})}\bigg]$[^o21]

where $\gamma(x,y)$ is the incomplete gamma function.

:::

## Mittag-Leffler's integral representation {#Mittag-Leffler's_integral_representation}

The integral representation of the Mittag-Leffler function is (Section 6 of [^o3])

$$E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{t^{\alpha-\beta}e^t}{t^\alpha-z} \, dt, \Re(\alpha)>0, \Re(\beta)>0,$$

where the contour $C$ starts and ends at $-\infty$ and circles around the singularities and branch points of the integrand.

Related to the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) and [Mittag-Leffler summation](wikipedia://en/37525778) is the expression (Eq (7.5) of [^o3] with $m=0$)

$$\int_0^{\infty}e^{-t z} t^{\beta-1} E_{\alpha,\beta}(\pm r\, t^\alpha) \,dt = \frac{z^{\alpha-\beta}}{z^{\alpha}\mp r}, \Re(z)>0, \Re(\alpha)>0, \Re(\beta)>0.$$

For $\operatorname{Re}(z^{1/\alpha}) < 1$, the Mittag-Leffler function satisfies the integral identity

$\frac{1}{1-z} = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-u} E_{\alpha}(z u^{\alpha}) \, du.$[^o22]

For $\alpha > 0$, the Mellin [Barnes integral](wikipedia://en/19622026) representation using a straight line contour is

$E_{\alpha}(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{\gamma-i\infty}^{\gamma+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha s)}(-z)^{-s} ds,|\arg{z}| < \pi$[^o23]

Which can be written in terms of generalized [Fox–Wright function](wikipedia://en/34287523)

$$E_{\alpha}(z) = {}_{1}\Psi_{1} \left[ \begin{matrix} (1, 1) \\ (1, \alpha) \end{matrix} \bigg| z \right]$$

And in terms of [Fox H-function](wikipedia://en/27106625)

$$E_{\alpha}(z) = H^{1,1}_{1,2}\left[ -z \bigg| \begin{matrix} (0, 1) \\ (0,1),(0, \alpha) \end{matrix} \right]$$

## Three-parameter generalizations {#Three-parameter_generalizations}

One generalization, characterized by three parameters, is

$$E_{\alpha,\beta}^\gamma(z)=\left ( \frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)} \right )\sum\limits_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\Gamma(\gamma + k)z^k}{k! \Gamma(\alpha k + \beta ) },$$

where $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ are complex parameters and $\Re(\alpha)>0$.[^o12]

Another generalization is the [Prabhakar function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabhakar_function)

$$E_{\alpha, \beta}^{\gamma}(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(\gamma)_k z^k}{k!\Gamma(\alpha k + \beta)},$$

where $(\gamma)_k$ is the [Pochhammer symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pochhammer_symbol).

## Applications of Mittag-Leffler function {#Applications_of_Mittag-Leffler_function}

One of the applications of the Mittag-Leffler function is in modeling fractional order viscoelastic materials. Experimental investigations into the time-dependent relaxation behavior of viscoelastic materials are characterized by a very fast decrease of the stress at the beginning of the relaxation process and an extremely slow decay for large times, i.e. it takes a long time to approach a constant asymptotic value. Therefore, many Maxwell elements are required to describe relaxation behavior to sufficient accuracy. This results in a difficult [optimization problem](wikipedia://en/1126536) in order to identify the large number of material parameters required. On the other hand, over the years, the concept of fractional derivatives has been introduced into the theory of [viscoelasticity](wikipedia://en/711288). Among these models, the [fractional Zener](wikipedia://en/7624108) model was found to be very effective for predicting the dynamic nature of rubber-like materials using only a small number of material parameters. The solution of the corresponding [constitutive equation](wikipedia://en/1034699) leads to a relaxation function of the Mittag-Leffler type. It is defined by the [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) with negative arguments. This function represents all essential properties of the relaxation process under the influence of an arbitrary and continuous signal with a jump at the origin.[^o15][^o16]
