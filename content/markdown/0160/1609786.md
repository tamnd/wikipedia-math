# Oskar Anderson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Oskar Anderson"}

**Oskar Johann Viktor Anderson** (Оскар Николаевич Андерсон; 2 August 1887 – 12 February 1960) was a Russian-German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of [Baltic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Germans) descent. He is best known for his work on [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Anderson was born into [Baltic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_German) family in [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk) in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (present-day Belarus), but soon moved to [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan). His father, [Nikolai Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Anderson), was professor in [Finno-Ugric languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finno-Ugric_languages) at the [University of Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kazan).[^o4] His older brothers were the [folklorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklorist) [Walter Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Anderson_%28folklorist%29) and the [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist) [Wilhelm Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Anderson).[^o5]

Oskar Anderson graduated from [Kazan Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Gymnasium) with a gold medal in 1906. After studying [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) for one year at [Kazan Federal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Federal_University), he moved to [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg) to study [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) at [Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great_St._Petersburg_Polytechnic_University).[^o6][^o7] From 1907 to 1915, he was [Aleksandr Chuprov](wikipedia://en/1614831)'s student and assistant. In 1912, he married Margarethe Natalie von Hindenburg-Hirtenberg,[^o9] a granddaughter of Wilhelm Paul von Hindenburg-Hirtenberg,[^o8] who was commemorated in "The Funeral of 'The Universal Man'" in [Fyodor Dostoyevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Dostoyevsky)'s *[A Writer's Diary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Writer's_Diary)*, and started lecturing at a commercial school in St. Petersburg while also studying for a law degree at [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University), graduating in 1914.[^o9]

In 1918, he took on a professorship in [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) but he was forced to flee Russia in 1920 due to the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution), first taking a post in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), Hungary, before becoming a professor at the [University of Economics Varna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Economics_Varna) in [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria), in 1924.

Anderson was one of the charter members of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society),[^o10] whose members also elected him to be a fellow of the society in 1933.[^o11][^o10] In the same year, he also received a fellowship from the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation).[^o12]

Supported by the foundation, in 1935 he established and became director of the Statistical Institute for Economic Research at [Sofia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_University).[^o13] For the remainder of the decade, he also served the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) as an associate member of its Committee of Statistical Experts.[^o14]

In 1942, he joined the [Kiel Institute for the World Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel_Institute_for_the_World_Economy) as the head of the Department of Eastern Studies and also took up a full professorship of statistics at [Kiel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel_University),[^o9] where he was joined by his brother Walter after the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). In 1947, he took a position at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), teaching there until 1956, when he retired.

- *Über die repräsentative Methode und deren Anwendung auf die Aufarbeitung der Ergebnisse der bulgarischen landwirtschaftlichen Betriebszählung vom 31. Dezember 1926*, Munich: Bayerisches Landesamt für Statistik, 1949
- *Die Saisonschwankungen in der deutschen Stromproduktion vor und nach dem Kriege*, Munich: [Inst. f. Wirtschaftsforschung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifo_Institute_for_Economic_Research), 1950
