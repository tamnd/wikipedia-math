# Infinity symbol

2

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Infinity symbol"}

The **infinity symbol** ($\infty$) is a [mathematical symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mathematical_symbols) representing the concept of [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824). This symbol is also called a *lemniscate*, after the [lemniscate](wikipedia://en/5210590) curves of a similar shape studied in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), or "lazy eight", in the terminology of [livestock branding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock_branding).

:::

This symbol was first used mathematically by [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290) in the 17th century, although it has a longer history of other uses. In mathematics, it often refers to infinite processes ([potential infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_infinity)) but may also refer to infinite values ([actual infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actual_infinity)). It has other related technical meanings, such as the use of long-lasting paper in [bookbinding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookbinding), and has been used for its symbolic value of the infinite in modern mysticism and literature. It is a common element of [graphic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_design), for instance in corporate logos as well as in earlier designs such as the [Métis flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Métis_flag).

The infinity symbol and several variations of the symbol are available in various [character encodings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_encoding).

## History {#History}

The lemniscate has been a common decorative motif since ancient times; for instance, it is commonly seen on [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age) combs.

The English mathematician [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290) is credited with introducing the infinity symbol with its mathematical meaning in 1655, in his *De sectionibus conicis*. Wallis did not explain his choice of this symbol. It has been conjectured to be a variant form of a [Roman numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral), but which Roman numeral is unclear. One theory proposes that the infinity symbol was based on the numeral for 100 million, which resembled the same symbol enclosed within a rectangular frame. Another proposes instead that it was based on the notation CIↃ (alternatively reduced to ↀ) used to represent 1,000. Instead of a Roman numeral, it may alternatively be derived from a variant of ω, the lower-case form of [omega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega), the last letter in the [Greek alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet).

Perhaps in some cases because of typographic limitations, other symbols resembling the infinity sign have been used for the same meaning. One paper by [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) was typeset with an open letterform more closely resembling a reflected and sideways S than a lemniscate (something like S), and even "O–O" has been used as a stand-in for the infinity symbol itself.

## Usage {#Usage}

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

In mathematics, the infinity symbol is often used to represent a [potential infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_infinity). For instance, in mathematical expressions with [summations](wikipedia://en/246160) and [limits](wikipedia://en/26551602) such as

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{2^x-1}{2^{x-1}} = 2,$$

the infinity sign is conventionally interpreted as meaning that the variable grows [arbitrarily large](wikipedia://en/1118832) towards infinity, rather than actually taking an infinite value, although other interpretations are possible.

The infinity symbol may also be used in some contexts for an [actual infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actual_infinity), infinite entities taken as [mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961) per se, especially when only a single infinite value is considered. In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the infinity symbol may be used to represent a [point at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139). This usage includes, in particular, the infinite point of a [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578), and the point added to a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) to form its [one-point compactification](wikipedia://en/55611). In [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), the value of a measure is often taken as an [extended real number](wikipedia://en/51698), allowing $+\infty$ as a value; this usage allows sets of infinite measure to be distinguished from nonmeasurable sets. However, other notations are also used for some other kinds of infinite values. For example, $\aleph_0$ ([aleph-nought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleph-nought)) denotes the smallest infinite [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) (representing the size of the set of [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers)), and $\omega$ ([omega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega)) denotes the smallest infinite [ordinal number](wikipedia://en/26547932) (representing the ordering of the natural numbers).

### Other technical uses {#Other_technical_uses}

*Image: Infrarotindex md 300 mm IMGP1196.jpg.* Side view of a camera lens, showing infinity symbol on the focal length indicator

In areas other than mathematics, the infinity symbol may take on other related meanings. For instance, it has been used in [bookbinding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookbinding) to indicate that a book is printed on [acid-free paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid-free_paper) and will therefore be long-lasting. On [cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera) and their [lenses](wikipedia://en/18320), the infinity symbol indicates that the lens's [focal length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focal_length) is [set to an infinite distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_focus), and is "probably one of the oldest symbols to be used on cameras".

### Symbolism and literary uses {#Symbolism_and_literary_uses}

*Image: RWS Tarot 08 Strength.jpg.* The infinity symbol appears on several cards of the [Rider–Waite tarot deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rider–Waite_tarot_deck).

In modern mysticism, the infinity symbol has become identified with a variation of the [ouroboros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouroboros), an ancient image of a snake eating its own tail that has also come to symbolize the infinite, and the ouroboros is sometimes drawn in figure-eight form to reflect this identification—rather than in its more traditional circular form.

In the works of [Vladimir Nabokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Nabokov), including *[The Gift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gift_%28Nabokov_novel%29)* and *[Pale Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_Fire)*, the figure-eight shape is used symbolically to refer to the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) and the infinite, as is the case in these books' descriptions of the shapes of bicycle tire tracks and of the outlines of half-remembered people. Nabokov's poem after which he entitled *Pale Fire* explicitly refers to "the miracle of the lemniscate". Other authors whose works use this shape with its symbolic meaning of the infinite include [James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce), in *[Ulysses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_%28novel%29)*, and [David Foster Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Foster_Wallace), in *[Infinite Jest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Jest)*.

### Graphic design {#Graphic_design}

The well-known shape and meaning of the infinity symbol have made it a common [typographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) element of [graphic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_design). For instance, the [Métis flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Métis_flag), used by the Canadian [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Métis) people since the early 19th century, is based around this symbol. Different theories have been put forward for the meaning of the symbol on this flag, including the hope for an infinite future for Métis culture and its mix of European and [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) traditions, but also evoking the geometric shapes of Métic dances,, [Celtic knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_knot), or [Plains First Nations Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Indian_Sign_Language).

A [rainbow](wikipedia://en/3871014)-coloured infinity symbol is also used by the [autism rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autism_rights_movement), as a way to symbolize the infinite variation of the people in the movement and of human cognition. The [Bakelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakelite) company took up this symbol in its corporate logo to refer to the wide range of varied applications of the synthetic material they produced. Versions of this symbol have been used in other trademarks, corporate logos, and emblems including those of [Fujitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujitsu), [Cell Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_Press), and the [2022 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup).

## Encoding {#Encoding}

*Image: Infinity symbol.svg.* The $\infty$ symbol in several [typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typefaces)

The symbol is encoded in [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) at 221E and in [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) as `\infty`: $\infty$. An encircled version is encoded for use as a symbol for [acid-free paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid-free_paper). 221E

The Unicode set of symbols also includes several variant forms of the infinity symbol that are less frequently available in fonts in the block [Miscellaneous Mathematical Symbols-B](wikipedia://en/5964067). 29DC
