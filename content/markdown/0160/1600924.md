# Inverse-chi-squared distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inverse-chi-squared distribution"}

In probability and statistics, the **inverse-chi-squared distribution** (or **inverted-chi-square distribution**[^o2]) is a [continuous probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_probability_distribution) of a positive-valued random variable. It is closely related to the [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424). It is used in [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571) as [conjugate prior](wikipedia://en/846412) for the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) of the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The inverse chi-squared distribution (or inverted-chi-square distribution[^o2] ) is the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of a random variable whose [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) (reciprocal) has a [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424).

If $X$ follows a chi-squared distribution with $\nu$ [degrees of freedom](wikipedia://en/2321606) then $1/X$ follows the inverse chi-squared distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom.

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the inverse chi-squared distribution is given by

$$f(x; \nu) = \frac{2^{-\nu/2}}{\Gamma(\nu/2)}\,x^{-\nu/2-1} e^{-1/(2 x)}$$

In the above $x>0$ and $\nu$ is the [degrees of freedom](wikipedia://en/2321606) parameter. Further, $\Gamma$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

The inverse chi-squared distribution is a special case of the [inverse-gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/1653565). with shape parameter $\alpha = \frac{\nu}{2}$ and scale parameter $\beta = \frac{1}{2}$.

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- [chi-squared](wikipedia://en/113424): If $X \thicksim \chi^2(\nu)$ and $Y = \frac{1}{X}$, then $Y \thicksim \text{Inv-}\chi^2(\nu)$
- [scaled-inverse chi-squared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaled-inverse-chi-squared_distribution): If $X \thicksim \text{Scale-inv-}\chi^2(\nu, 1/\nu) \,$, then $X \thicksim \text{inv-}\chi^2(\nu)$
- [Inverse gamma](wikipedia://en/1653565) with $\alpha = \frac{\nu}{2}$ and $\beta = \frac{1}{2}$
- Inverse chi-squared distribution is a special case of type 5 [Pearson distribution](wikipedia://en/1804023)
