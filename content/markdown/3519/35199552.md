# Daniel Kubert

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Kubert"}

**Daniel Sion Kubert** (; October 18, 1947[^o4] – January 5, 2010[^o7]) was an American mathematician who introduced [modular units](wikipedia://en/35197301) and [Kubert identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubert_identities).

:::

He grew up in a secular Jewish family in Elkins Park, Pennsylvania, the son of David Kubert, an attorney, and Adele (Sion) Kubert, a high school teacher. Daniel graduated from Philadelphia's Central High School in 1965. Kubert graduated from Brown University in 1969, receiving B.S. and M.A. degrees in the same year.[^o4][^o2] He received his Ph.D. in mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1973,[^o3] where his dissertation "Universal Bounds on the Torsion and Isogenies of Elliptic Curves" was supervised by [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679).

Kubert served as a Gibbs Instructor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) from 1973 to 1975.[^o4] His work on modular units was done in collaboration with Yale mathematician [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991). Kubert was hired as an assistant professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1975,[^o4] and was still there at the end of the decade.[^o5] By the early 1980s, Kubert was at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).[^o6] He also had two stints at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), in 1979–80 and 1984–85.[^o3]

In later life, Kubert was a resident of Philadelphia.[^o7]
