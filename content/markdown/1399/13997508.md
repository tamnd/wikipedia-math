# Carl Hierholzer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carl Hierholzer"}

Carl Hierholzer **Carl Hierholzer** (2 October 1840 – 13 September 1871[^o2]) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hierholzer studied mathematics in [Polytechnic School in Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universität_Karlsruhe_%28TH%29), and he got his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Ruprecht-Karls-Universität Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruprecht-Karls-Universität_Heidelberg) in 1865. His Ph.D. advisor was [Ludwig Otto Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Otto_Hesse) (1811–1874). In 1870 Hierholzer wrote his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) about [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673) (title: *Ueber Kegelschnitte im Raum*) in Karlsruhe, where he later became a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent).

Hierholzer [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that a [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) has an [Eulerian trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulerian_trail) if and only if exactly zero or two of its vertices have an odd [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448). This result had been given, with no proof of the 'if' part, by [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) in 1736. Hierholzer apparently presented his work to a circle of fellow mathematicians not long before his premature death in 1871. A colleague then arranged for its posthumous publication in a paper that appeared in 1873.[^o2]
