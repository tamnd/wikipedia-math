# Peter Winkler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Winkler"}

**Peter Mann Winkler** is a research mathematician, author of books on [mathematical puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_puzzles) and of more than 130 research papers in mathematics[^o3] and patent holder in a broad range of applications, ranging from [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) to marine [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation).[^o4] His research areas include [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492), [theory of computation](wikipedia://en/30402) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). He is currently the William Morrill Professor of Mathematics and Computer Science at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Peter Winkler studied mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and later received his PhD in 1975 from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) under the supervision of [Angus McIntyre](wikipedia://en/11551260).[^o8] He has also served as an assistant professor at [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford), full professor and chair at Emory and as a mathematics research director at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) and [Lucent Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies).[^o4] He was visiting professor at the [Technische Universität Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt).[^o9]

## Puzzle books {#Puzzle_books}

Winkler has published three books on [mathematical puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_puzzles):

- *Mathematical Puzzles: A connoisseur's collection*[^o11][^o12] ([A K Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_K_Peters), 2004, , translated to German and Russian)
- *Mathematical Mind-Benders*[^o13] (A K Peters, 2007, )
- *Mathematical Puzzles* (A K Peters, 2021, ).

Winkler is widely considered to be a pre eminent scholar in this domain. He was the Visiting Distinguished Chair for Public Dissemination of Mathematics at the [National Museum of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mathematics) (MoMath), gave topical talks at the [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner) conferences, and wrote novel papers related to some of these puzzles.

## Bridge at the Enigma Club {#Bridge_at_the_Enigma_Club}

Winkler's book *Bridge at the Enigma Club*[^o15] was a runner up for the 2011 [Master Point Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_Point_Press) Book Of The Year award.[^o16]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2011, Winkler received the [David P. Robbins Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins_Prize) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) as coauthor of one of two papers[^o18] in the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)*.[^o19]

## Paul Erdős anecdote {#Paul_Erdős_anecdote}

According to *The Man Who Loved Only Numbers*, a biography of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), he attended the [bar mitzvah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_mitzvah) celebration for Peter Winkler's twins, and Winkler's mother-in-law tried to throw Erdős out:

"Erdös came to my twins' bar mitzvah, notebook in hand," said Peter Winkler, a colleague of [Graham](wikipedia://en/240694)'s at AT&T. "He also brought gifts for my children--he loved kids--and behaved himself very well. But my mother-in-law tried to throw him out. She thought he was some guy who wandered in off the street, in a rumpled suit, carrying a pad under his arm. It is entirely possible that he proved a theorem or two during the ceremony."[^o21]
