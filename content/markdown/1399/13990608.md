# Uncertainty analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uncertainty analysis"}

**Uncertainty analysis** investigates the uncertainty of variables that are used in [decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-making) problems in which observations and models represent the [knowledge base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_base). In other words, uncertainty analysis aims to make a technical contribution to decision-making through the quantification of uncertainties in the relevant variables.

:::

## Physical experiments {#Physical_experiments}

In physical [experiments](wikipedia://en/59861) **uncertainty analysis**, or **experimental uncertainty assessment**, deals with assessing the [uncertainty](wikipedia://en/63778) in a [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022). An experiment designed to determine an effect, demonstrate a law, or estimate the numerical value of a [physical variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_variable) will be affected by [errors](wikipedia://en/41118) due to instrumentation, methodology, presence of confounding effects and so on. Experimental uncertainty estimates are needed to assess the [confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence) in the results.[^o3] A related field is the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541).

## Mathematical modelling {#Mathematical_modelling}

Likewise in numerical experiments and [modelling](wikipedia://en/20590) uncertainty analysis draws upon a number of techniques for determining the reliability of model predictions, accounting for various sources of uncertainty in model input and design. A related field is [sensitivity analysis](wikipedia://en/620083).

## Calibrated parameters and output {#Calibrated_parameters_and_output}

A [calibrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calibrate) [parameter](wikipedia://en/25065) does not necessarily represent [reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality), as reality is much more complex. Any prediction has its complexities of reality that cannot be represented uniquely in the calibrated model; therefore, there is a potential error. Such errors must be accounted for when making management decisions on the basis of model outcomes. [^o6]
