# Forced convection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Forced convection"}

*Image: Schneekanone.jpg.* Forced convection by a fan in a snow machine

**Forced convection** is a [mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_%28engineering%29), or type of transport, in which fluid motion is generated by an external source (like a pump, fan, suction device, etc.). Alongside [natural convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_convection), [thermal radiation](wikipedia://en/185239), and [thermal conduction](wikipedia://en/72536) it is one of the methods of heat transfer and allows significant amounts of heat energy to be transported very efficiently.

:::

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

This mechanism is found very commonly in everyday life, including [central heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_heating) and [air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning) and in many other machines. Forced convection is often encountered by engineers designing or analyzing [heat exchangers](wikipedia://en/153221), pipe flow, and flow over a plate at a different temperature than the stream (the case of a shuttle wing during re-entry, for example).[^o4]

:::

## Mixed convection {#Mixed_convection}

In any forced convection situation, some amount of natural convection is always present whenever there are [gravitational forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_force) present (i.e., unless the system is in an inertial frame or free-fall). When the [natural convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_convection) is not negligible, such flows are typically referred to as [mixed convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_convection).

## Mathematical analysis {#Mathematical_analysis}

When analyzing potentially mixed convection, a parameter called the [Archimedes number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes_number) (Ar) parametrizes the relative strength of free and forced convection. The Archimedes number is the ratio of [Grashof number](wikipedia://en/58287) and the square of [Reynolds number](wikipedia://en/23868856), which represents the ratio of buoyancy force and inertia force, and which stands in for the contribution of natural convection. When Ar ≫ 1, natural convection dominates and when Ar ≪ 1, forced convection dominates.

$Ar= \frac{Gr}{Re^2}$ [^o7]

When [natural convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_convection) isn't a significant factor, mathematical analysis with forced convection theories typically yields accurate results. The parameter of importance in forced convection is the [Péclet number](wikipedia://en/57526), which is the ratio of advection (movement by currents) and diffusion (movement from high to low concentrations) of heat.

$$Pe=\frac{U L}{\alpha }$$

When the Peclet number is much greater than [unity](wikipedia://en/77591639) (1), advection dominates diffusion. Similarly, much smaller ratios indicate a higher rate of diffusion relative to advection.
