# Conjugate-permutable subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conjugate-permutable subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **conjugate-permutable subgroup** is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) that commutes with all its [conjugate subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_subgroup). The term was introduced by [Tuval Foguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuval_Foguel) in 1997[^o2] and arose in the context of the proof that for [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707), every [quasinormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774267) is a [subnormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774415).

:::

Clearly, every [quasinormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774267) is conjugate-permutable.

In fact, it is true that for a finite group:

- Every maximal conjugate-permutable subgroup is [normal](wikipedia://en/21918).
- Every conjugate-permutable subgroup is a conjugate-permutable subgroup of every intermediate subgroup containing it.
- Combining the above two facts, every conjugate-permutable subgroup is [subnormal](wikipedia://en/2774415).

Conversely, every 2-subnormal subgroup (that is, a subgroup that is a normal subgroup of a normal subgroup) is conjugate-permutable.
