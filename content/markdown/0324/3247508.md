# Frank Wilcoxon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Wilcoxon"}

**Frank Wilcoxon** (2 September 1892 – 18 November 1965) was a [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), known for the development of several [statistical tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Frank Wilcoxon was born to American parents on September 2nd 1892 in [County Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cork), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ireland_%281801–1923%29).[^o5] He grew up in [Catskill, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catskill_%28town%29,_New_York), but received part of his education in England. In 1917, he graduated from [Pennsylvania Military College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Military_College) with a [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) After the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), he entered graduate studies, first at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), where he was awarded an [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) in 1921, and then at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), where he obtained a [Phd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phd) in [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry) in 1924.

Wilcoxon entered a research career, working at the [Boyce Thompson Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyce_Thompson_Institute) for Plant Research from 1925 to 1941. He then moved to the [Atlas Powder Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Powder_Company), where he designed and directed the Control Laboratory, before joining the [American Cyanamid Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cyanamid) in 1943. During this time, he developed an interest in inferential statistics through the study of [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824)'s 1925 text, *[Statistical Methods for Research Workers](wikipedia://en/1645218)*. He retired in 1957 and died on 18 November 1965.

## Research {#Research}

Over his career, Wilcoxon published over 70 papers.[^o7] His best-known paper[^o8] contained the two new statistical tests that still bear his name, the [Wilcoxon rank-sum test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mann–Whitney_U) and the [Wilcoxon signed-rank test](wikipedia://en/1954987). These are [non-parametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric) alternatives to the unpaired and paired [Student's *t*-tests](wikipedia://en/536080), respectively.
