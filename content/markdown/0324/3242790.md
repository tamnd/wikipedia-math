# Stieltjes moment problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stieltjes moment problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Stieltjes [moment problem](wikipedia://en/1244992)**, named after [Thomas Joannes Stieltjes](wikipedia://en/40589), seeks necessary and sufficient conditions for a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) (*m*<sub>0</sub>, *m*<sub>1</sub>, *m*<sub>2</sub>, ...) to be of the form

:::

$$m_n = \int_0^\infty x^n\,d\mu(x)$$

for some [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) *μ*. If such a function *μ* exists, one asks whether it is unique.

The essential difference between this and other well-known [moment problems](wikipedia://en/1244992) is that this is on a half-line \[0, ∞), whereas in the [Hausdorff moment problem](wikipedia://en/3216387) one considers a [bounded interval](wikipedia://en/49172#Definitions) \[0, 1\], and in the [Hamburger moment problem](wikipedia://en/3216491) one considers the whole line (−∞, ∞).

## Existence {#Existence}

Let

$$\Delta_n=\left[\begin{matrix} m_0 & m_1 & m_2 & \cdots & m_{n} \\ m_1 & m_2 & m_3 & \cdots & m_{n+1} \\ m_2& m_3 & m_4 & \cdots & m_{n+2} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ m_{n} & m_{n+1} & m_{n+2} & \cdots & m_{2n} \end{matrix}\right]$$

be a [Hankel matrix](wikipedia://en/377537), and

$$\Delta_n^{(1)}=\left[\begin{matrix} m_1 & m_2 & m_3 & \cdots & m_{n+1} \\ m_2 & m_3 & m_4 & \cdots & m_{n+2} \\ m_3 & m_4 & m_5 & \cdots & m_{n+3} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ m_{n+1} & m_{n+2} & m_{n+3} & \cdots & m_{2n+1} \end{matrix}\right].$$

Then { *m*<sub>*n*</sub> : *n* = 1, 2, 3, ... } is a moment sequence of some measure on $[0,\infty)$ with infinite support if and only if for all *n*, both

$$\det(\Delta_n) > 0\ \mathrm{and}\ \det\left(\Delta_n^{(1)}\right) > 0.$$

{ *m*<sub>*n*</sub> : *n* = 1, 2, 3, ... } is a moment sequence of some measure on $[0,\infty)$ with finite support of size *m* if and only if for all $n \leq m$, both

$$\det(\Delta_n) > 0\ \mathrm{and}\ \det\left(\Delta_n^{(1)}\right) > 0$$

and for all larger $n$

$$\det(\Delta_n) = 0\ \mathrm{and}\ \det\left(\Delta_n^{(1)}\right) = 0.$$

## Uniqueness {#Uniqueness}

There are several sufficient conditions for uniqueness.

**[Carleman's condition](wikipedia://en/9442947)**: The solution is unique if

$$\sum_{n \geq 1} m_n^{-1/(2n)} = \infty~.$$

**[Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599)'s criterion**: If $\mu$ is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) supported on $[0, \infty)$, such that $\mathbb E_{X \sim \mu}[e^{c\sqrt{X}}] < \infty$, then all its moments are finite, and $\mu$ is the unique distribution with these moments.[^o4][^o5][^o6]
