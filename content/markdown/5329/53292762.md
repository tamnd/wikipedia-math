# James Keener

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Keener"}

**James "Jim" Paul Keener** is an American mathematician, currently Distinguished Professor at [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah).[^o3][^o4][^o5] He is recognized as a pioneer in the field of mathematical physiology and cardiology.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jim Keener received his PhD from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1972. Initially intending to work on [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042), he came across a paper by [Otto Rossler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Rossler) that implied that heartbeat can be modeled using [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295). Looking to investigate this claim, he picked up the *Textbook of Medical Physiology* by [Arthur Guyton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Guyton) to build some [foundational knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundational_knowledge) in cardiology and discovered examples of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) that had previously been untouched by the applied mathematics community.[^o7] He was invited to join the faculty at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah) in 1978 by Frank Hoppensteadt to start a new group in [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology). He served as [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of the *[SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Journal_on_Applied_Mathematics)* and was named a [SIAM Fellow](wikipedia://en/1883376#SIAM_Fellows) in 2012.
