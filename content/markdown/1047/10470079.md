# Standard conjectures on algebraic cycles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Standard conjectures on algebraic cycles"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **standard conjectures** about algebraic cycles are several [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) describing the relationship of [algebraic cycles](wikipedia://en/9520954) and [Weil cohomology theories](wikipedia://en/2960213). One of the original applications of these conjectures, envisaged by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), was to prove that his construction of [pure motives](wikipedia://en/430680) gave an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) that is [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_category). Moreover, as he pointed out, the standard conjectures also imply the hardest part of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705), namely Weil's Riemann hypothesis (i.e. an analog over finite fields of the well known [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125)) that remained open at the end of the 1960s and was proved later by [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544); for details on the link between Weil and standard conjectures, see (Kleiman 1968). The standard conjectures remain open problems, so that their application gives only [conditional proofs](wikipedia://en/6562) of results. In quite a few cases, including that of the Weil conjectures, other methods have been found to prove such results unconditionally.

:::

The classical formulations of the standard conjectures involve a fixed Weil cohomology theory $H$. All of the conjectures deal with "algebraic" cohomology classes, which means a morphism on the cohomology of a smooth [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469)

$$H^{\,\ast}(X) \to H^{\,\ast}(X)$$

induced by an algebraic cycle with rational coefficients on the product $X \times X$ via the *[cycle class map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_class_map),* which is part of the structure of a Weil cohomology theory.

Conjecture A is equivalent to Conjecture B (see (Grothendieck 1969), p. 196), and so is not listed.

The situations over the field of complex numbers and over finite fields are completely different. Hodge standard conjecture is true over the field of complex numbers, but largely unknown over finite fields. On the other hand, Standard conjecture C is known over finite fields, but largely unknown over complex numbers.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Lefschetz type Standard Conjecture (Conjecture B)"}

## Lefschetz type Standard Conjecture (Conjecture B) {#Lefschetz_type_Standard_Conjecture_(Conjecture_B)}

One of the axioms of a Weil theory is the so-called **[hard Lefschetz theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Lefschetz_theorem)** (or axiom):

Begin with a fixed smooth [hyperplane section](wikipedia://en/3459767)

$$W = H \cap X,$$

where $X$ is a given smooth projective variety in the ambient projective space $\mathbf{P}^{\,N}$ and $H$ is a hyperplane. Then for $i \le n = \dim(X)$, the Lefschetz operator

$$L : H^{\,i}(X) \to H^{\,i+2}(X),$$

which is defined by intersecting cohomology classes with $W$, gives an isomorphism

$$L^{n-i} : H^{\,i}(X) \to H^{\,2n-i}(X).$$

Now, for $i \le n$ define:

$$\Lambda = (L^{n-i+2})^{-1} \circ L \circ (L^{n-i}) : H^{\,i}(X) \to H^{\,i-2}(X)$$

$$\Lambda = (L^{n-i}) \circ L \circ (L^{n-i+2})^{-1} : H^{\,2n-i+2}(X) \to H^{\,2n-i}(X)$$

The conjecture states that the **Lefschetz operator ($\Lambda$)** is induced by an algebraic cycle.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Künneth type Standard Conjecture (Conjecture C)"}

## Künneth type Standard Conjecture (Conjecture C) {#Künneth_type_Standard_Conjecture_(Conjecture_C)}

It is conjectured that the projectors

$$H^{\,\ast}(X) \twoheadrightarrow H^{i}(X) \rightarrowtail H^{\,\ast}(X)$$

are algebraic, i.e. induced by a cycle $\pi^{\,i} \subset X \times X$ with rational coefficients. This implies that the motive of any smooth projective variety (and more generally, every [pure motive](wikipedia://en/430680)) decomposes as

$$h(X) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{2 \mathrm{dim}(X)} h^i(X).$$

The motives $h^0(X)$ and $h^{2 \mathrm{dim}(X)}$ can always be split off as direct summands. The conjecture therefore immediately holds for curves. It was proved for surfaces by (Murre 1990). (Katz Messing 1974) have used the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705) to show the conjecture for algebraic varieties defined over finite fields, in arbitrary dimension.

(Šermenev 1974) proved the Künneth decomposition for abelian varieties *A*. (Deninger Murre 1991) refined this result by exhibiting a functorial Künneth decomposition of the [Chow motive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_motive) of *A* such that the *n*-multiplication on the abelian variety acts as $n^i$ on the *i*-th summand $h^i(A)$. (de Cataldo Migliorini 2002) proved the Künneth decomposition for the [Hilbert scheme](wikipedia://en/6612581) of points in a smooth surface.

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Conjecture D (numerical equivalence vs. homological equivalence)"}

## Conjecture D (numerical equivalence vs. homological equivalence) {#Conjecture_D_(numerical_equivalence_vs._homological_equivalence)}

Conjecture D states that numerical and homological [equivalence](wikipedia://en/25384599) agree. (It implies in particular the latter does not depend on the choice of the Weil cohomology theory). This conjecture implies the Lefschetz conjecture. If the Hodge standard conjecture holds, then the Lefschetz conjecture and Conjecture D are equivalent.

Over the field of complex numbers, this conjecture was shown by Lieberman for varieties of dimension at most 4, and for [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152).[^o15]

Over finite fields, this conjecture was shown by Clozel for [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) with etale l-adic cohomology, for an infinite set of prime numbers l (which can be stated in terms of Cebotarev density theorem). One crucial idea in the proof of Clozel is to try to reduce to a similar setting as for Abelian varieties over the field of complex numbers: construct a "Hodge decomposition" for the cohomology, using the type of CM field of the endomorphism group.

:::

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="The Hodge Standard Conjecture"}

## The Hodge Standard Conjecture {#The_Hodge_Standard_Conjecture}

The Hodge standard conjecture is modelled on the [Hodge index theorem](wikipedia://en/1242892). It states the definiteness (positive or negative, according to the dimension) of the cup product pairing on primitive algebraic cohomology classes. If it holds, then the Lefschetz conjecture implies Conjecture D. In characteristic zero the Hodge standard conjecture holds, being a consequence of [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). In positive characteristic the Hodge standard conjecture is known for surfaces ((Grothendieck 1958)) and for abelian varieties of dimension 4 ((Ancona 2020)).

The Hodge standard conjecture is not to be confused with the *[Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518)* which states that for smooth projective varieties over $\mathbf{C}$, every rational $(p, p)$-class is algebraic. The Hodge conjecture implies the Lefschetz and Künneth conjectures and conjecture D for varieties over fields of characteristic zero. The [Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/3124804) implies Lefschetz, Künneth, and conjecture D for [ℓ-adic cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) over all fields.

:::

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Permanence properties of the standard conjectures"}

## Permanence properties of the standard conjectures {#Permanence_properties_of_the_standard_conjectures}

For two algebraic varieties *X* and *Y*, (Arapura 2006) has introduced a condition that *Y* is *motivated* by *X*. The precise condition is that the motive of *Y* is (in André's category of motives) expressible starting from the motive of *X* by means of sums, summands, and products. For example, *Y* is motivated if there is a surjective morphism $X^n \to Y$.[^o23] If *Y* is not found in the category, it is *unmotivated* in that context. For smooth projective complex algebraic varieties *X* and *Y*, such that *Y* is motivated by *X*, the standard conjectures D (homological equivalence equals numerical), B (Lefschetz), the [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518) and also the generalized Hodge conjecture hold for *Y* if they hold for all powers of *X*.[^o24] This fact can be applied to show, for example, the Lefschetz conjecture for the [Hilbert scheme](wikipedia://en/6612581) of points on an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311).

:::

::: {#o26 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Relation to other conjectures"}

## Relation to other conjectures {#Relation_to_other_conjectures}

(Beilinson 2012) has shown that the (conjectural) existence of the so-called motivic t-structure on the triangulated category of motives implies the Lefschetz and Künneth standard conjectures B and C.

:::
