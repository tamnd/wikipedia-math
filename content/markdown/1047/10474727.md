# Index group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Index group"}

In [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), a branch of mathematics, every [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665) can be associated with a group called its **abstract index group**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* be a Banach algebra and *G* the group of invertible elements in *A*. The set *G* is open and a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315). Consider the [identity component](wikipedia://en/1088787)

*G*<sub>0</sub>,

or in other words the [connected component](wikipedia://en/6233) containing the identity 1 of *A*; *G*<sub>0</sub> is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*. The [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526)

Λ<sub>*A*</sub> = *G*/*G*<sub>0</sub>

is the **abstract index group** of *A*. Because *G*<sub>0</sub>, being the component of an open set, is both open and closed in *G*, the index group is a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Let *L*(*H*) be the Banach algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space. The set of invertible elements in *L*(*H*) is path connected. Therefore, Λ<sub>*L*(*H*)</sub> is the trivial group.

Let **T** denote the unit circle in the complex plane. The algebra *C*(**T**) of continuous functions from **T** to the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) is a Banach algebra, with the topology of uniform convergence. A function in *C*(**T**) is invertible (meaning that it has a [pointwise](wikipedia://en/4786797) [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940), not that it is an [invertible function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible_function)) if it does not map any element of **T** to zero. The group *G*<sub>0</sub> consists of elements [homotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopic), in *G*, to the identity in *G*, the constant function **1**. One can choose the functions *f<sub>n</sub>*(*z*) = *z<sup>n</sup>* as representatives in G of distinct homotopy classes of maps **T**→**T**. Thus the index group Λ<sub>*C*(**T**)</sub> is the set of homotopy classes, indexed by the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) of its members. Thus Λ<sub>*C*(**T**)</sub> is isomorphic to the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of **T**. It is a countable discrete group.

The [Calkin algebra](wikipedia://en/945565) *K* is the quotient [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) of *L*(*H*) with respect to the [compact operators](wikipedia://en/6690773). Suppose π is the quotient map. By [Atkinson's theorem](wikipedia://en/10533603), an invertible elements in *K* is of the form π(*T*) where *T* is a [Fredholm operators](wikipedia://en/279722). The index group Λ<sub>*K*</sub> is again a countable discrete group. In fact, Λ<sub>*K*</sub> is isomorphic to the additive group of integers **Z**, via the [Fredholm index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredholm_index). In other words, for Fredholm operators, the two notions of index coincide.
