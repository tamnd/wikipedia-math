# Differentially closed field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differentially closed field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [differential field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_field) *K* is **differentially closed** if every finite system of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) with a solution in some differential field extending *K* already has a solution in *K*. This concept was introduced by (Robinson 1959). Differentially closed fields are the analogues for differential equations of [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018) for polynomial equations.

:::

## The theory of differentially closed fields {#The_theory_of_differentially_closed_fields}

We recall that a [differential field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_field) is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) equipped with a [derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29) operator. Let *K* be a differential field with derivation operator ∂.

- A **differential polynomial** in *x* is a polynomial in the formal expressions *x*, ∂*x*, ∂<sup>2</sup>*x*, ... with coefficients in *K*.
- The **order** of a non-zero differential polynomial in *x* is the largest *n* such that ∂<sup>*n*</sup>*x* occurs in it, or −1 if the differential polynomial is a constant.
- The **separant** *S*<sub>*f*</sub> of a differential polynomial of order *n*≥0 is the derivative of *f* with respect to ∂<sup>*n*</sup>*x*.
- The **field of constants** of *K* is the subfield of elements *a* with ∂*a*=0.
- In a differential field *K* of nonzero [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*, all *p*th powers are constants. It follows that neither *K* nor its field of constants is [perfect](wikipedia://en/505088), unless ∂ is trivial. A field *K* with derivation ∂ is called **differentially perfect** if it is either of characteristic 0, or of characteristic *p* and every constant is a *p*th power of an element of *K*.
- A **differentially closed field** is a differentially perfect differential field *K* such that if *f* and *g* are differential polynomials such that *S*<sub>*f*</sub>≠ 0 and *g*≠0 and *f* has order greater than that of *g*, then there is some *x* in *K* with *f*(*x*)=0 and *g*(*x*)≠0. (Some authors add the condition that *K* has characteristic 0, in which case *S*<sub>*f*</sub> is automatically non-zero, and *K* is automatically perfect.)
- **DCF<sub>*p*</sub>** is the theory of differentially closed fields of characteristic *p* (where *p* is 0 or a prime).

Taking *g*=1 and *f* any ordinary [separable polynomial](wikipedia://en/362400) shows that any differentially closed field is [separably closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separably_closed). In characteristic 0 this implies that it is algebraically closed, but in characteristic *p*>0 differentially closed fields are never algebraically closed.

Unlike the complex numbers in the theory of algebraically closed fields, there is no natural example of a differentially closed field. Any differentially perfect field *K* has a **differential closure**, a [prime model](wikipedia://en/1663421) extension, which is differentially closed. Shelah showed that the differential closure is unique up to isomorphism over *K*. Shelah also showed that the prime differentially closed field of characteristic 0 (the differential closure of the rationals) is not [minimal](wikipedia://en/18374778); this was a rather surprising result, as it is not what one would expect by analogy with algebraically closed fields.

The theory of DCF<sub>*p*</sub> is [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335) and [model complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_complete) (for *p*=0 this was shown by Robinson, and for *p*>0 by (Wood 1973)). The theory DCF<sub>*p*</sub> is the [model companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_companion) of the theory of differential fields of characteristic *p*. It is the model completion of the theory of differentially perfect fields of characteristic *p* if one adds to the language a symbol giving the *p*th root of constants when *p*>0. The theory of differential fields of characteristic *p*>0 does not have a model completion, and in characteristic *p*=0 is the same as the theory of differentially perfect fields so has DCF<sub>0</sub> as its model completion.

The number of differentially closed fields of some infinite [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) κ is 2<sup>κ</sup>; for κ uncountable this was proved by (Shelah 1973), and for κ countable by Hrushovski and Sokolovic.

## The Kolchin topology {#The_Kolchin_topology}

The *Kolchin topology* on *K* <sup>m</sup> is defined by taking sets of solutions of systems of differential equations over *K* in *m* variables as basic closed sets. Like the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744), the Kolchin topology is [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/2936080).

A d-constructible set is a finite union of closed and open sets in the Kolchin topology. Equivalently, a d-constructible set is the set of solutions to a quantifier-free, or [atomic](wikipedia://en/4472066), formula with parameters in *K*.

## Quantifier elimination {#Quantifier_elimination}

Like the theory of algebraically closed fields, the theory DCF<sub>0</sub> of differentially closed fields of characteristic 0 [eliminates quantifiers](wikipedia://en/2150441). The geometric content of this statement is that the projection of a d-constructible set is d-constructible. It also eliminates imaginaries, is complete, and model complete.

In characteristic *p*>0, the theory DCF<sub>p</sub> eliminates quantifiers in the language of differential fields with a unary function *r* added that is the *p*th root of all constants, and is 0 on elements that are not constant.

## Differential Nullstellensatz {#Differential_Nullstellensatz}

The differential Nullstellensatz is the analogue in differential algebra of Hilbert's [nullstellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullstellensatz).

- A **differential ideal** or ∂-ideal is an ideal closed under ∂.
- An ideal is called **radical** if it contains all roots of its elements.

Suppose that *K* is a differentially closed field of characteristic 0. . Then Seidenberg's **differential nullstellensatz** states there is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between

- Radical differential ideals in the ring of differential polynomials in *n* variables, and
- ∂-closed subsets of *K*<sup>*n*</sup>.

This correspondence maps a ∂-closed subset to the ideal of elements vanishing on it, and maps an ideal to its set of zeros.

:::

## Omega stability {#Omega_stability}

In characteristic 0 Blum showed that the theory of differentially closed fields is [ω-stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ω-stable) and has [Morley rank](wikipedia://en/1401941) ω. In non-zero characteristic (Wood 1973) showed that the theory of differentially closed fields is not ω-stable, and (Shelah 1973) showed more precisely that it is [stable](wikipedia://en/15828771) but not [superstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstable).
