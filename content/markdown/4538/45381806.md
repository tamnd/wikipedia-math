# Toroidal embedding

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toroidal embedding"}

In algebraic geometry, a **toroidal embedding** is an open embedding of algebraic varieties that locally looks like the embedding of the open torus into a [toric variety](wikipedia://en/737087). The notion was introduced by Mumford to prove the existence of [semistable reductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistable_reduction) of algebraic varieties over one-dimensional bases.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *X* be a [normal variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_variety) over an algebraically closed field $\bar{k}$ and $U \subset X$ a smooth open subset. Then $U \hookrightarrow X$ is called a **toroidal embedding** if for every [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408) *x* of *X*, there is an isomorphism of local $\bar{k}$-algebras:

$$\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X, x} \simeq \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\sigma}, t}$$

for some affine toric variety $X_{\sigma}$ with a torus *T* and a point *t* such that the above isomorphism takes the ideal of $X - U$ to that of $X_{\sigma} - T$.

Let *X* be a [normal variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_variety) over a field *k*. An open embedding $U\hookrightarrow X$ is said to a toroidal embedding if $U_{\bar{k}}\hookrightarrow X_{\bar{k}}$ is a toroidal embedding.
