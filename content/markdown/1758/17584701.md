# Arithmetic dynamics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic dynamics"}

**Arithmetic dynamics**[^o2] is a field that amalgamates two areas of mathematics, [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Part of the inspiration comes from [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518), the study of the [iteration](wikipedia://en/1448702) of self-maps of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) or other complex [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties). Arithmetic dynamics is the study of the number-theoretic properties of [integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_point), [rational](wikipedia://en/2960200), [$p$-adic](wikipedia://en/51423), or algebraic points under repeated application of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) or [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210). A fundamental goal is to describe arithmetic properties in terms of underlying geometric structures.

:::

*Global arithmetic dynamics* is the study of analogues of classical [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) in the setting of discrete dynamical systems, while *local arithmetic dynamics*, also called [p-adic or nonarchimedean dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_dynamics), is an analogue of complex dynamics in which one replaces the complex numbers $\mathbf{C}$ by a $p$-adic field such as $\mathbf{Q}_{p}$ or $\mathbf{C}_{p}$ and studies chaotic behavior and the [Fatou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_set) and [Julia sets](wikipedia://en/56434).

The following table describes a rough correspondence between Diophantine equations, especially [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties), and dynamical systems:

::: {#o3 .table cue=table}

| Diophantine equations | Dynamical systems |
|---|---|
| Rational and integer points on a variety | Rational and integer points in an orbit |
| Points of finite order on an abelian variety | [Preperiodic points](wikipedia://en/4941621) of a rational function |

:::

## Definitions and notation from discrete dynamics {#Definitions_and_notation_from_discrete_dynamics}

Let $S$ be a set and let $F : S \to S$ be a map from $S$ to itself. The iterate of $F$ with itself $n$ times is denoted

$$F^{(n)} = F \circ F \circ \cdots \circ F.$$

A point $P \in S$ is *periodic* if $F^{(n)}(P) = P$ for some $n \ge 1$.

The point is *preperiodic* if $F^{(k)}(P)$ is periodic for some $k \ge 1$.

The (forward) *orbit of* $P$ is the set

$$O_F(P) = \left \{ P, F(P), F^{(2)}(P), F^{(3)}(P), \cdots\right\}.$$

Thus $P$ is preperiodic if and only if its orbit $O_{F}(P)$ is finite.

## Number theoretic properties of preperiodic points {#Number_theoretic_properties_of_preperiodic_points}

Let $F(x)$ be a rational function of degree at least two with coefficients in $\mathbf{Q}$. A theorem of [Douglas Northcott](wikipedia://en/9368121)[^o7] says that $F$ has only finitely many $\mathbf{Q}$-rational preperiodic points, i.e., $F$ has only finitely many preperiodic points in $\mathbf{P}^{1}(\mathbf{Q})$. The **uniform boundedness conjecture for preperiodic points**[^o8] of Patrick Morton and [Joseph Silverman](wikipedia://en/2288703) says that the number of preperiodic points of $F$ in $\mathbf{P}^{1}(\mathbf{Q})$ is bounded by a constant that depends only on the degree of $F$.

More generally, let $F : \mathbf{P}^{N} \to \mathbf{P}^{N}$ be a morphism of degree at least two defined over a number field $K$. Northcott's theorem says that $F$ has only finitely many preperiodic points in $\mathbf{P}^{N}(K)$, and the general Uniform Boundedness Conjecture says that the number of preperiodic points in $\mathbf{P}^{N}(K)$ may be bounded solely in terms of $N$, the degree of $F$, and the degree of $K$ over $\mathbf{Q}$.

The Uniform Boundedness Conjecture is not known even for quadratic polynomials $F_{c}(x) = x^{2} + c$ over the rational numbers $\mathbf{Q}$. It is known in this case that $F_{c}(x)$ cannot have periodic points of period four,[^o9] five,[^o10] or six,[^o11] although the result for period six is contingent on the validity of the [conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch_Swinnerton-Dyer_Conjecture). [Bjorn Poonen](wikipedia://en/16283918) has conjectured that $F_{c}(x)$ cannot have rational periodic points of any period strictly larger than three.[^o12]

:::

## Integer points in orbits {#Integer_points_in_orbits}

The orbit of a rational map may contain infinitely many integers. For example, if $F(x)$ is a polynomial with integer coefficients and if $a$ is an integer, then it is clear that the entire orbit $O_{F}(a)$ consists of integers. Similarly, if $F(x)$ is a rational map and some iterate $F^{(n)}(x)$ is a polynomial with integer coefficients, then every $n$-th entry in the orbit is an integer. An example of this phenomenon is the map $F(x) = x^{-d}$, whose second iterate is a polynomial. It turns out that this is the only way that an orbit can contain infinitely many integers.

**Theorem.**[^o14] Let $F(x) \in \mathbf{Q}(x)$ be a rational function of degree at least two, and assume that no iterate[^o15] of $F$ is a polynomial. Let $a \in \mathbf{Q}$. Then the orbit $O_{F}(a)$ contains only finitely many integers.

## Dynamically defined points lying on subvarieties {#Dynamically_defined_points_lying_on_subvarieties}

There are general conjectures due to [Shouwu Zhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shouwu_Zhang)[^o17] and others concerning subvarieties that contain infinitely many periodic points or that intersect an orbit in infinitely many points. These are dynamical analogues of, respectively, the [Manin–Mumford conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manin-Mumford_conjecture), proven by [Michel Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928), and the [Mordell–Lang conjecture](wikipedia://en/172199), proven by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226). The following conjectures illustrate the general theory in the case that the subvariety is a curve.

**Conjecture.** Let $F : \mathbf{P}^{N} \to \mathbf{P}^{N}$ be a morphism and let $C \subset \mathbf{P}^{N}$ be an irreducible algebraic curve. Suppose that there is a point $P \in \mathbf{P}^{N}$ such that $C$ contains infinitely many points in the orbit $O_{F}(P)$. Then $C$ is periodic for $F$ in the sense that there is some iterate $F^{(k)}$ of $F$ that maps $C$ to itself.

## *p*-adic dynamics {#p-adic_dynamics}

The field of [$p$-adic (or nonarchimedean) dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_dynamics) is the study of classical dynamical questions over a field $K$ that is complete with respect to a nonarchimedean absolute value. Examples of such fields are the field of $p$-adic rationals $\mathbf{Q}_{p}$ and the completion of its algebraic closure $\mathbf{C}_{p}$. The metric on $K$ and the standard definition of equicontinuity leads to the usual definition of the [Fatou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_set) and [Julia sets](wikipedia://en/56434) of a rational map $F(x) \in K(x)$. There are many similarities between the complex and the nonarchimedean theories, but also many differences. A striking difference is that in the nonarchimedean setting, the Fatou set is always nonempty, but the Julia set may be empty. This is the reverse of what is true over the complex numbers. Nonarchimedean dynamics has been extended to [Berkovich space](wikipedia://en/34420832),[^o19] which is a compact connected space that contains the totally disconnected non-locally compact field $\mathbf{C}_{p}$.

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

There are natural generalizations of arithmetic dynamics in which $\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{Q}_{p}$ are replaced by number fields and their $p$-adic completions. Another natural generalization is to replace self-maps of $\mathbf{P}^{1}$ or $\mathbf{P}^{N}$ with self-maps (morphisms) $V \to V$ of other affine or [projective varieties](wikipedia://en/320469).

:::

## Other areas in which number theory and dynamics interact {#Other_areas_in_which_number_theory_and_dynamics_interact}

There are many other problems of a number theoretic nature that appear in the setting of dynamical systems, including:

- dynamics over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615).
- dynamics over [function fields](wikipedia://en/288358) such as $\mathbf{C}(x)$.
- iteration of formal and $p$-adic [power series](wikipedia://en/59958).
- dynamics on [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).
- arithmetic properties of dynamically defined [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609).
- [equidistribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equidistribution)[^o23] and invariant [measures](wikipedia://en/19873), especially on $p$-adic spaces.
- dynamics on [Drinfeld modules](wikipedia://en/5578871).
- number-theoretic iteration problems that are not described by rational maps on varieties, for example, the [Collatz problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collatz_problem).
- symbolic codings of dynamical systems based on explicit arithmetic expansions of real numbers.[^o24]

The [Arithmetic Dynamics Reference List](https://www.math.brown.edu/~jhs/ADSBIB.pdf) gives an extensive list of articles and books covering a wide range of arithmetical dynamical topics.
