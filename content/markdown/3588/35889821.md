# Transcendental law of homogeneity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transcendental law of homogeneity"}

In mathematics, the **transcendental law of homogeneity** (**TLH**) is a heuristic principle enunciated by [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) most clearly in a 1710 text entitled *Symbolismus memorabilis calculi algebraici et infinitesimalis in comparatione potentiarum et differentiarum, et de lege homogeneorum transcendentali*.[^o2] [Henk J. M. Bos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henk_J._M._Bos) describes it as the principle to the effect that in a sum involving [infinitesimals](wikipedia://en/160990) of different orders, only the lowest-order term must be retained, and the remainder discarded.[^o3] Thus, if $a$ is finite and $dx$ is infinitesimal, then one sets

:::

$$a+dx=a.$$

Similarly,

$$u\,dv+v\,du+du\,dv=u\,dv+v\,du,$$

where the higher-order term *du* *dv* is discarded in accordance with the TLH. A 2012 study argues that Leibniz's TLH was a precursor of the [standard part function](wikipedia://en/19145800) over the [hyperreals](wikipedia://en/51429).[^o4]
