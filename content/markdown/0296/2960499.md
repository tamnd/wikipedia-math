# Gustave Bertrand

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gustave Bertrand"}

**Gustave Bertrand** (1896–1976) was a French [military intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_intelligence) officer who made a vital contribution to the [decryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decryption), by [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)'s [Cipher Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuro_Szyfrów), of German [Enigma](wikipedia://en/9256) [ciphers](wikipedia://en/5244), beginning in December 1932. This achievement would in turn lead to [Britain's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) celebrated [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) [Ultra](wikipedia://en/31748) operation.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Bertrand joined the French military as a private in 1914 and was wounded in 1915 at the [Dardanelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardanelles). From 1926, he worked in [radio intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGINT).

In the 1920s, French radio intelligence was decentralized. Decryption of foreign, chiefly German and Italian, ciphers and codes was the responsibility of a General Staff cryptology department, while radio monitoring was conducted by the intelligence service, *Service de Renseignement* or *S.R.* At the end of 1930, decryption was turned over to the *S.R.*, which created a Section *D* (for *Decryptement*), of which Bertrand became chief. He later took over all of French radio intelligence.[^o4]

Bertrand's [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_intelligence) associates had purchased documents pertaining to the [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256) from [Hans-Thilo Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Thilo_Schmidt) (codenamed "Asché" by the French), an employee at the German Armed Forces' Cryptographic Agency. In December 1932, then-Captain (later, General) Bertrand turned these documents over to the [Polish Cipher Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuro_Szyfrów)'s chief, Major [Gwido Langer](wikipedia://en/2919669). Asché's documents, according to cryptologist [Marian Rejewski](wikipedia://en/4349420)'s testimony, proved in practice crucial to his mathematical solution of the military [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256)'s wiring. During his work with the Poles, Bertrand used the code name *Bolek*, given him by the Poles.

Bertrand was to learn of the Poles' success against Enigma only six and a half years later, at a trilateral Polish-French-British conference held in the [Kabaty Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaty), south of [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), on 25 July 1939, just five weeks before the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).[^o5] After Germany's invasion of Poland in September 1939, then-Major Bertrand from October 1939 to November 1942 sponsored the continued work of prewar Cipher Bureau personnel: first at *[PC Bruno](wikipedia://en/621987)*, outside [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989); then, after Germany's invasion of France (May–June 1940), at the [Cadix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadix) center in southern France's [Vichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) "Free Zone."

Over a year after the Cadix center had been scattered to avert capture by the Germans, on 5 January 1944, Bertrand was captured by the Germans as he waited at the famous Church of [Sacré Cœur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Sacré_Cœur), in Paris' [Montmartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montmartre) district, for a courier from London. The Germans suggested that he work for them. Pretending to agree, Bertrand was allowed to return with his wife Mary to [Vichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy) to contact British intelligence. There he sent his underground comrades into hiding and went into hiding himself. On 2 June 1944, four days before the [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day) [Normandy landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_landings), at an improvised airstrip in France's [Massif Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif_Central), Bertrand, his wife and a Jesuit priest who served as a courier of the Polish Resistance climbed into a small, unarmed [Lysander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Lysander) III aircraft that flew them to southern England. Bertrand and his wife moved into a house in the [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire) village of [Boxmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxmoor) just a short walk from the Polish radio-intercept station and cipher office at the nearby village of [Felden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felden) where [Marian Rejewski](wikipedia://en/4349420) and [Henryk Zygalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Zygalski) were working.

Bertrand retired from the French Secret Service in 1950 and went on to become mayor of [Théoule-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Théoule-sur-Mer) in southern France.

In 1973, the Paris publishing house Plon published his book, *Enigma ou la plus grande énigme de la guerre 1939-1945* (Enigma, or the Greatest Enigma of the War of 1939-1945). The book, one of the principal primary sources on the history of [Enigma](wikipedia://en/9256) [decryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decryption), for the first time gave a detailed account of the some eleven years of Franco-Polish collaboration in breaking and reading Enigma before and during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).
