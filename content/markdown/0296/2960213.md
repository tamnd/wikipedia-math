# Weil cohomology theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weil cohomology theory"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **Weil cohomology** or **Weil cohomology theory** is a [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) satisfying certain axioms concerning the interplay of [algebraic cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_cycles) and cohomology groups. The name is in honor of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019). Any Weil cohomology theory factors uniquely through the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [Chow motives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_motive), but the category of Chow motives itself is not a Weil cohomology theory, since it is not an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Fix a base field *k* of arbitrary characteristic and a "coefficient field" *K* of characteristic zero. A *Weil cohomology theory* is a [contravariant functor](wikipedia://en/10987#Covariance_and_contravariance)

$$H^*: \{\text{smooth projective varieties over } k \} \longrightarrow \{\text{graded } K\text{-algebras}\}$$

satisfying the axioms below. For each smooth [projective algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/320469) *X* of dimension *n* over *k*, the [graded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) [*K*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-algebra)

$$H^*(X) = \bigoplus\nolimits_i H^i(X)$$

is required to satisfy the following:

- $H^i(X)$ is a finite-dimensional *K*-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) for each integer *i*.
- $H^i(X) = 0$ for each *i* \< 0 or *i* > 2*n*.
- $H^{2n}(X)$ is isomorphic to *K* (the so-called orientation map).
- [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732): there is a perfect pairing $$H^i(X) \times H^{2n-i}(X) \to H^{2n}(X) \cong K.$$
- There is a canonical [Künneth](wikipedia://en/918047) isomorphism $$H^*(X) \otimes H^*(Y) \to H^*(X\times Y).$$
- For each integer *r*, there is a *cycle map* defined on the group $Z^r(X)$ of algebraic cycles of codimension *r* on *X*, $$\gamma_X : Z^r(X) \to H^{2r}(X),$$ satisfying certain compatibility conditions with respect to functoriality of *H* and the Künneth isomorphism. If *X* is a point, the cycle map is required to be the inclusion **Z** ⊂ *K*.
- *Weak Lefschetz axiom*: For any smooth [hyperplane section](wikipedia://en/3459767) *j*: *W* ⊂ *X* (i.e. *W* = *X* ∩ *H*, *H* some hyperplane in the ambient projective space), the maps $$j^*: H^i(X) \to H^i(W)$$ are isomorphisms for $i \leqslant n-2$ and injections for $i \leqslant n-1$.
- *Hard Lefschetz axiom*: Let *W* be a hyperplane section and $w =\gamma_X(W) \in H^2(X)$ be its image under the cycle class map. The *Lefschetz operator* is defined as $$\begin{cases} L: H^i(X) \to H^{i+2}(X) \\ x \mapsto x \cdot w, \end{cases}$$ where the dot denotes the product in the algebra $H^*(X)$. Then $$L^i : H^{n-i}(X) \to H^{n+i}(X)$$ is an isomorphism for *i* = 1, ..., *n*.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

There are four so-called classical Weil cohomology theories:

- [singular (= Betti) cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betti_cohomology), regarding varieties over **C** as topological spaces using their analytic topology (see [GAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAGA)),
- [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) over a base field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero: over **C** defined by [differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms) and in general by means of the complex of Kähler differentials (see [algebraic de Rham cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_de_Rham_cohomology)),
- [$\ell$-adic cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) for varieties over fields of characteristic different from $\ell$,
- [crystalline cohomology](wikipedia://en/10220067).

The proofs of the axioms for Betti cohomology and de Rham cohomology are comparatively easy and classical. For $\ell$-adic cohomology, for example, most of the above properties are deep theorems.

The vanishing of Betti cohomology groups exceeding twice the dimension is clear from the fact that a (complex) manifold of complex dimension *n* has real dimension 2*n*, so these higher cohomology groups vanish (for example by comparing them to [simplicial (co)homology](wikipedia://en/1060236)).

The de Rham cycle map also has a down-to-earth explanation: Given a subvariety *Y* of complex codimension *r* in a complete variety *X* of complex dimension *n*, the real dimension of *Y* is 2*n*−2*r*, so one can integrate any differential (2*n*−2*r*)-form along *Y* to produce a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826). This induces a linear functional $\textstyle\int_Y \colon \; H^{2n-2r}_{\text{dR}}(X) \to \mathbf{C}$. By Poincaré duality, to give such a functional is equivalent to giving an element of $H^{2r}_{\text{dR}}(X)$; that element is the image of *Y* under the cycle map.
