# Weil reciprocity law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weil reciprocity law"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Weil reciprocity law** is a result of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) holding in the [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) *K*(*C*) of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) *C* over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *K*. Given functions *f* and *g* in *K*(*C*), i.e. rational functions on *C*, then

:::

*f*((*g*)) = *g*((*f*))

where the notation has this meaning: (*h*) is the [divisor](wikipedia://en/1693055) of the function *h*, or in other words the [formal sum](wikipedia://en/498958) of its zeroes and poles counted with [multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790); and a function applied to a formal sum means the product (with multiplicities, poles counting as a negative multiplicity) of the values of the function at the points of the divisor. With this definition there must be the side-condition, that the divisors of *f* and *g* have disjoint support (which can be removed).

In the case of the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578), this can be proved by manipulations with the [resultant](wikipedia://en/2195020) of polynomials.

To remove the condition of disjoint support, for each point *P* on *C* a *local symbol*

(*f*, *g*)<sub>*P*</sub>

is defined, in such a way that the statement given is equivalent to saying that the product over all *P* of the local symbols is 1. When *f* and *g* both take the values 0 or ∞ at *P*, the definition is essentially in limiting or [removable singularity](wikipedia://en/81644) terms, by considering (up to sign)

*f*<sup>*a*</sup>*g*<sup>*b*</sup>

with *a* and *b* such that the function has neither a zero nor a pole at *P*. This is achieved by taking *a* to be the multiplicity of *g* at *P*, and −*b* the multiplicity of *f* at *P*. The definition is then

(*f*, *g*)<sub>*P*</sub> = (−1)<sup>*ab*</sup> *f*<sup>*a*</sup>*g*<sup>*b*</sup>.

See for example [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), *Groupes algébriques et corps de classes*, pp. 44–46, for this as a special case of a theory on mapping algebraic curves into commutative groups.

There is a generalisation of [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) to [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties) (Lang, *Abelian Varieties*).
