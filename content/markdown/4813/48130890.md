# Denjoy–Riesz theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Denjoy–Riesz theorem"}

*Image: Julia set (Rev formula 02).jpg.* A totally disconnected [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434). By the Denjoy–Riesz theorem, there exists an arc passing through all the points in this set.

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **Denjoy–Riesz theorem** states that every compact set of [totally disconnected](wikipedia://en/303356) points in the Euclidean plane can be covered by a continuous image of the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324), without self-intersections (a [Jordan arc](wikipedia://en/267444)).

:::

## Definitions and statement {#Definitions_and_statement}

A topological space is [zero-dimensional](wikipedia://en/517682) according to the [Lebesgue covering dimension](wikipedia://en/398886) if every finite [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) has a refinement that is also an open cover by disjoint sets. A topological space is [totally disconnected](wikipedia://en/303356) if it has no nontrivial connected subsets; for points in the plane, being totally disconnected is equivalent to being zero-dimensional. The Denjoy–Riesz theorem states that every compact totally disconnected subset of the plane is a subset of a Jordan arc.[^o3]

## History {#History}

(Kuratowski 1968) credits the result to publications by [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126) in 1906, and [Arnaud Denjoy](wikipedia://en/1485520) in 1910, both in *[Comptes rendus de l'Académie des sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptes_rendus_de_l'Académie_des_sciences)*.[^o6] As (Moore Kline 1919) describe,[^o8] Riesz actually gave an incorrect argument that every totally disconnected set in the plane is a subset of a Jordan arc. This generalized a previous result of L. Zoretti, which used a more general class of sets than Jordan arcs, but Zoretti found a flaw in Riesz's proof: it incorrectly presumed that one-dimensional projections of totally disconnected sets remained totally disconnected. Then, Denjoy (citing neither Zoretti nor Riesz) claimed a proof of Riesz's theorem, with little detail. Moore and Kline state and prove a generalization that completely characterizes the subsets of the plane that can be subsets of Jordan arcs, and that includes the Denjoy–Riesz theorem as a special case.[^o8]

## Applications and related results {#Applications_and_related_results}

By applying this theorem to a two-dimensional version of the [Smith–Volterra–Cantor set](wikipedia://en/922382), it is possible to find an [Osgood curve](wikipedia://en/26717031), a Jordan arc or closed Jordan curve whose [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) is positive.[^o10]

A related result is the [analyst's traveling salesman theorem](wikipedia://en/28177884), describing the point sets that form subsets of curves of finite [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401). Not every compact totally disconnected set has this property, because some compact totally disconnected sets require any arc that covers them to have infinite length.
