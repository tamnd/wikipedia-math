# Lighthill's eighth power law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lighthill's eighth power law"}

In [aeroacoustics](wikipedia://en/2518272), **Lighthill's eighth power law** states that power of the sound created by a turbulent motion, far from the turbulence, is proportional to eighth power of the characteristic turbulent velocity, derived by [Sir James Lighthill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_James_Lighthill) in 1952.[^o2][^o3] This is used to calculate the total acoustic power of the [jet noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_noise). The law reads as

:::

$$W = K \frac{\rho_o}{c_o^5} L^2 U^8,$$

where

- $W$ is the acoustic power in the far-field,
- $K$ is the proportionality constant (or Lighthill's constant),
- $\rho_o$ is the uniform fluid [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density),
- $c_o$ is the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853),
- $L$ is the characteristic length scale of the turbulent source and
- $U$ is the characteristic velocity scale of the turbulent source.

The eighth power is experimentally verified and found to be accurate for low speed flows, i.e., [Mach number](wikipedia://en/20051) is small, $M<1$. And also, the source has to be compact to apply this law.
