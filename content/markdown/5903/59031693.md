# Lisl Gaal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lisl Gaal"}

**(Ilse) Lisl Novak Gaal** (January 17, 1924 – October 7, 2024)[^o15] was an Austrian-born American mathematician known for her contributions to [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316). She was the first woman to hold a [tenure-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenure-track) position in mathematics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), and was an associate professor emeritus at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota).[^o2] Gaal died in 2024, at the age of 100.[^o3]

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Gaal's dissertation work was in the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358). It proved that two different systems for [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) that had previously been proposed as foundational were [equiconsistent](wikipedia://en/3435716): either both are valid or both lead to contradictions. These two systems were [Zermelo set theory](wikipedia://en/322623) and [Von Neumann set theory](wikipedia://en/528491). They differed from each other in that von Neumann had added to Zermelo's theory a notion of [classes](wikipedia://en/20696), collections of mathematical objects that are defined by some property but do not necessarily form a set. (Often, intuitively, proper classes are "too big" to form sets; for instance, the collection of all sets cannot itself be a set, by [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095), but it can be a class.) Gaal's work showed that introducing this extra notion of a class is a safe step, one that does not introduce any new inconsistencies into the system.

Gaal was also the author of two books:

- *Classical Galois Theory with Examples* (Markham Publishing, 1971; third ed., Chelsea Publishing, 1979; reprinted 1998)
- *A Mathematical Gallery* (American Mathematical Society, 2017)

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gaal was born in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) on January 17, 1924, the daughter of a gynecologist and the sister of [Gertrude M. Novak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_M._Novak), who became a physician in Chicago. She and her two sisters escaped Nazi Germany, and moved with their family to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City).

After graduating from [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) with an A.B. in 1944, Gaal earned a doctorate in 1948 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), through [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College). Her dissertation, *On the Consistency of Goedel's Axioms for Class and Set Theory Relative to a Weaker Set of Axioms*, was jointly supervised by [Lynn Harold Loomis](wikipedia://en/35267335) and [Willard Van Orman Quine](wikipedia://en/33833).

## Later career {#Later_career}

Gaal lived in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California) from 1950 to 1951. She and her husband, mathematician [Steven Gaal](wikipedia://en/24774553), both moved to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), beginning as instructors in 1953 but then in 1954 being promoted to assistant professors. This step was the first time the Cornell mathematics department had offered a tenure-track position to a woman. She also became the first woman at Cornell to advise the doctorate of a mathematics student, Angelo Margaris.

The Gaals moved again in 1957, to the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), where she became an associate professor emeritus.

In later life, Gaal became a [lithographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithography), making prints that combined mathematical themes with Minnesota scenes. Her book *A Mathematical Gallery* collects some of her mathematical illustrations.
