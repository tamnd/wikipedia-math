# Karl Holzinger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karl Holzinger"}

**Karl John Holzinger** (August 9, 1892 – January 15, 1954)[^o3] was an American educational psychologist known for his work in [psychometrics](wikipedia://en/24982).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Holzinger received his [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.B.) and [A.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degrees from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) in 1915 and 1917, respectively.[^o5] He then attended the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), from which he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1922. He subsequently studied at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) with both [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695) and [Charles Spearman](wikipedia://en/1290799). Holzinger became interested in [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence) testing through his work with Spearman.[^o6][^o7]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Holzinger spent almost his entire academic career at the University of Chicago, teaching in the Department of Education there for thirty-two years. He was elected vice president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1933 and president of the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500) in 1940. From 1949 until his death, he was co-[editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of the *[Journal of Educational Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Psychology)*.[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Holzinger is known for his research on the use of [factor analysis](wikipedia://en/253492) to study human intelligence.[^o11] He developed the theory that human intelligence consists of three types of abilities, or factors. This theory has since become the basis of many contemporary hierarchical theories of intelligence.[^o12] He conducted much of his research as a member of the Unitary Traits Committee, which he and [Edward Thorndike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Thorndike) had established in 1931.[^o13][^o14] He also collaborated with [Horatio Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Newman) and [Frank N. Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_N._Freeman) on an early, large-scale [twin study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_study) that began in 1927. According to [M. Susan Lindee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Susan_Lindee), this study "...was extraordinary in its depth and complexity".[^o15]
