# Cole Prize

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cole Prize"}

The **Frank Nelson Cole Prize**, or **Cole Prize** for short, is one of two [prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize) awarded to [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), one for an outstanding contribution to [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and the other for an outstanding contribution to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o2] The prize is named after [Frank Nelson Cole](wikipedia://en/3417701), who served the Society for 25 years. The Cole Prize in algebra was funded by Cole himself, from funds given to him as a retirement gift; the prize fund was later augmented by his son, leading to the double award.[^o2][^n1]

:::

The prizes recognize a notable research work in algebra (given every three years) or number theory (given every three years) that has appeared in the last six years. The work must be published in a recognized, peer-reviewed venue. The first award for algebra was made in 1928 to [L. E. Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._E._Dickson), while the first award for number theory was made in 1931 to [H. S. Vandiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._S._Vandiver).[^n2]
