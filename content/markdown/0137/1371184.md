# Werner Boy

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Werner Boy"}

*Image: BoysSurfaceTopView.PNG.* Boy's surface from near the top ([R. Bryant's](wikipedia://en/24204031) parameterization)

**Werner Boy** (; 4 May 1879 − 6 September 1914) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was the discoverer and [eponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) of [Boy's surface](wikipedia://en/61361)—a [three-dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) projection of the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) without [singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity), the first of its kind. He discovered it in 1901 after his thesis adviser, [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), asked him to prove that it was not possible to immerse the real projective plane in three-dimensional space. Boy sketched several models of the surface, and discovered that it could have 3-fold rotational symmetry, but was unable to find a [parametric model](wikipedia://en/576108) for the surface. It was not until 1978 that [Bernard Morin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Morin) found the first parametrisation, with the [aid of computers](wikipedia://en/2840305).[^o3][^o4]

:::

After completing his dissertation, Boy worked as a high school teacher in [Krefeld, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krefeld,_Germany). He later returned to his birth town of Barmen (today [Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuppertal)) to teach. He died as a soldier in the first weeks of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) on 6 September 1914.
