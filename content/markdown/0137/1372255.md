# Highly cototient number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Highly cototient number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **highly cototient number** is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $k$ which is above 1 and has more solutions to the [equation](wikipedia://en/9284)

:::

$$x - \phi(x) = k$$

than any other integer below $k$ and above 1. Here, $\phi$ is [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452). There are infinitely many solutions to the equation for

$k$ = [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29)

so this value is excluded in the definition. The first few highly cototient numbers are:[^o2]

[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29), [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_%28number%29), [8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_%28number%29), [23](wikipedia://en/362203), [35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_%28number%29), [47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47_%28number%29), [59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/59_%28number%29), [63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63_%28number%29), [83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/83_%28number%29), [89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89_%28number%29), [113](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/113_%28number%29), [119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/119_%28number%29), [167](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/167_%28number%29), [209](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/209_%28number%29), [269](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/269_%28number%29), 299, 329, 389, 419, 509, 629, 659, 779, 839, 1049, 1169, 1259, 1469, 1649, 1679, 1889, ...

Many of the highly cototient numbers are odd.[^o2]

The concept is somewhat analogous to that of [highly composite numbers](wikipedia://en/208732). Just as there are infinitely many highly composite numbers, there are also infinitely many highly cototient numbers. Computations become harder, since [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491) becomes harder as the numbers get larger.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The **cototient** of $x$ is defined as $x - \phi(x)$, i.e. the number of positive integers less than or equal to $x$ that have at least one prime factor in common with $x$. For example, the cototient of 6 is 4 since these four positive integers have a [prime factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) in common with 6: 2, 3, 4, 6. The cototient of 8 is also 4, this time with these integers: 2, 4, 6, 8. There are exactly two numbers, 6 and 8, which have cototient 4. There are fewer numbers which have cototient 2 and cototient 3 (one number in each case), so 4 is a highly cototient number.
