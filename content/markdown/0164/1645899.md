# Uniform 4-polytope

*Image: Schlegel half-solid truncated 120-cell.png.* [Schlegel diagram](wikipedia://en/7201595) for the [truncated 120-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_120-cell) with [tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/30606) cells visible

*Image: 120-cell t01 H3.svg.* [Orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052) of the truncated 120-cell, in the $H_{3}$ [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) ($D_{10}$ symmetry). Only vertices and edges are drawn.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform 4-polytope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **uniform 4-polytope** (or **uniform polychoron**)[^o2] is a 4-dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) which is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) and whose cells are [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223), and faces are [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306).

:::

There are 47 non-[prismatic](wikipedia://en/283007) [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) uniform 4-polytopes. There are two infinite sets of convex prismatic forms, along with 17 cases arising as prisms of the convex uniform polyhedra. There are also an unknown number of non-convex star forms.

## History of discovery {#History_of_discovery}

- Convex **[Regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693)**:
  - **1852**: [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) proved in his manuscript *Theorie der vielfachen Kontinuität* that there are exactly 6 regular polytopes in 4 [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398) and only 3 in 5 or more dimensions.
- **[Regular star 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli-Hess_polychoron)** ([star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/7087334) cells and/or [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719))
  - **1852**: [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) also found 4 of the 10 regular star 4-polytopes, discounting 6 with cells or vertex figures [{<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,5}](wikipedia://en/3029843) and [{5,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>}](wikipedia://en/2860441).
  - **1883**: [Edmund Hess](wikipedia://en/9191575) completed the list of 10 of the nonconvex regular 4-polytopes, in his book (in German) *Einleitung in die Lehre von der Kugelteilung mit besonderer Berücksichtigung ihrer Anwendung auf die Theorie der Gleichflächigen und der gleicheckigen Polyeder* [Einleitung in die Lehre von der Kugelteilung mit besonderer Berücksichtigung ihrer Anwendung auf die Theorie der Gleichflächigen und der gleicheckigen Polyeder, von dr. Edmund Hess. Mit sechzehn lithographierten tafeln.](http://www.hti.umich.edu/cgi/b/bib/bibperm?q1=ABN8623.0001.001).
- **Convex [semiregular polytopes](wikipedia://en/11684875)**: (Various definitions before Coxeter's **uniform** category)
  - **1900**: [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) enumerated the list of nonprismatic semiregular convex polytopes with regular cells ([Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905)) in his publication *On the Regular and Semi-Regular Figures in Space of n Dimensions*. In four dimensions, this gives the [rectified 5-cell](wikipedia://en/2720430), the [rectified 600-cell](wikipedia://en/2720121), and the [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756).[^o4]
  - **1910**: [Alicia Boole Stott](wikipedia://en/3325459), in her publication *Geometrical deduction of semiregular from regular polytopes and space fillings*, expanded the definition by also allowing [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847) and [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) cells. This construction enumerated 45 semiregular 4-polytopes, corresponding to the nonprismatic forms listed below.[^o5] The snub 24-cell and [grand antiprism](wikipedia://en/4058047) were missing from her list.[^o6]
  - **1911**: [Pieter Hendrik Schoute](wikipedia://en/6022750) published *Analytic treatment of the polytopes regularly derived from the regular polytopes*, followed Boole-Stott's notations, enumerating the convex uniform polytopes by symmetry based on [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821), [8-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-cell)/[16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422), and [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485).[^o7][^o8]
  - **1912**: [E. L. Elte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._L._Elte) independently expanded on Gosset's list with the publication *The Semiregular Polytopes of the Hyperspaces*, polytopes with one or two types of semiregular facets.[^o9]
- **Convex uniform polytopes**:
  - **1940**: The search was expanded systematically by [H.S.M. Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) in his publication *Regular and Semi-Regular Polytopes*.
  - **Convex uniform 4-polytopes**:
    - **1965**: The complete list of convex forms was finally enumerated by [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Michael Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Guy_%28computer_scientist%29), in their publication *Four-Dimensional Archimedean Polytopes*, established by computer analysis, adding only one non-Wythoffian convex 4-polytope, the grand antiprism.
    - **1966** [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) completes his Ph.D. dissertation *The Theory of Uniform Polytopes and Honeycombs* under advisor Coxeter, completes the basic theory of uniform polytopes for dimensions 4 and higher.
    - **1986** Coxeter published a paper *Regular and Semi-Regular Polytopes II* which included analysis of the unique [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756) structure, and the symmetry of the anomalous grand antiprism.
    - **1998[^o10]-2000**: The 4-polytopes were systematically named by Norman Johnson, and given by George Olshevsky's online indexed enumeration (used as a basis for this listing). Johnson named the 4-polytopes as polychora, like polyhedra for 3-polytopes, from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) roots *poly* ("many") and *choros* ("room" or "space").[^o11] The names of the uniform polychora started with the 6 regular polychora with prefixes based on rings in the Coxeter diagrams; truncation t<sub>0,1</sub>, cantellation, t<sub>0,2</sub>, runcination t<sub>0,3</sub>, with single ringed forms called rectified, and bi, tri-prefixes added when the first ring was on the second or third nodes.[^o12]
    - **2004**: A proof that the Conway-Guy set is complete was published by Marco Möller in his dissertation, *Vierdimensionale Archimedische Polytope*. Möller reproduced Johnson's naming system in his listing.[^o14]
    - **2008**: *The Symmetries of Things*[^o15] was published by [John H. Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and contains the first print-published listing of the convex uniform 4-polytopes and higher dimensional polytopes by Coxeter group family, with general [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) diagrams for each ringed [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) permutation—snub, grand antiprism, and duoprisms—which he called proprisms for product prisms. He used his own *ijk*-ambo naming scheme for the indexed ring permutations beyond truncation and bitruncation, and all of Johnson's names were included in the book index.
- **Nonregular uniform star 4-polytopes**: (similar to the [nonconvex uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2850448#Nonconvex_forms_with_convex_faces))
  - **1966**: Johnson describes three nonconvex uniform antiprisms in 4-space in his dissertation.[^o16]
  - **1990-2006**: In a collaborative search, up to 2005 a total of 1845 uniform 4-polytopes (convex and nonconvex) had been identified by Jonathan Bowers and George Olshevsky,[^o17] with an additional four discovered in 2006 for a total of 1849. The count includes the 74 prisms of the 75 [non-prismatic uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2850448) (since that is a finite set – the cubic prism is excluded as it duplicates the tesseract), but not the infinite categories of duoprisms or prisms of antiprisms.[^o18]
  - **2020-2023**: 342 new polychora were found, bringing up the total number of known uniform 4-polytopes to 2191. The list has not been proven complete.[^o18][^o19]

## Regular 4-polytopes {#Regular_4-polytopes}

Regular 4-polytopes are a subset of the uniform 4-polytopes, which satisfy additional requirements. [Regular 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#4-polytopes_%28polychora%29) can be expressed with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {*p*,*q*,*r*} have cells of type {*p*,*q*}, faces of type {*p*}, edge figures {*r*}, and [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) {*q*,*r*}.

The existence of a regular 4-polytope {*p*,*q*,*r*} is constrained by the existence of the regular polyhedra {*p*,*q*} which becomes cells, and {*q*,*r*} which becomes the [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719).

Existence as a finite 4-polytope is dependent upon an inequality:[^o21]

$$\sin \left ( \frac{\pi}{p} \right ) \sin \left(\frac{\pi}{r}\right) > \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{q}\right).$$

The 16 [regular 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#Four_dimensional_regular_polytopes), with the property that all cells, faces, edges, and vertices are congruent:

- 6 [regular convex 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_convex_4-polytope): [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821) {3,3,3}, [8-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-cell) {4,3,3}, [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) {3,3,4}, [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485) {3,4,3}, [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358) {5,3,3}, and [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447) {3,3,5}.
- 10 [regular star 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_star_4-polytope): [icosahedral 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604585) {3,5,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>}, [small stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604577) {<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,5,3}, [great 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604660) {5,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,5}, [grand 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604599) {5,3,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>}, [great stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604610) {<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,3,5}, [grand stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604676) {<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,5,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>}, [great grand 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604627) {5,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,3}, [great icosahedral 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604644) {3,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,5}, [grand 600-cell](wikipedia://en/8604538) {3,3,<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>}, and [great grand stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604513) {<sup>5</sup>/<sub>2</sub>,3,3}.

Each reflective uniform 4-polytope can be constructed in one or more reflective point group in 4 dimensions by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777), represented by rings around permutations of nodes in a [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram). Mirror [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) can be grouped, as seen by colored nodes, separated by even-branches. Symmetry groups of the form \[a,b,a\], have an extended symmetry, \[\[a,b,a\]\], doubling the symmetry order. This includes \[3,3,3\], \[3,4,3\], and \[*p*,2,*p*\]. Uniform polytopes in these group with symmetric rings contain this extended symmetry.

If all mirrors of a given color are unringed (inactive) in a given uniform polytope, it will have a lower symmetry construction by removing all of the inactive mirrors. If all the nodes of a given color are ringed (active), an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) operation can generate a new 4-polytope with chiral symmetry, shown as "empty" circled nodes", but the geometry is not generally adjustable to create uniform solutions.

### Enumeration {#Enumeration}

There are 64 convex uniform 4-polytopes, including the 6 regular convex 4-polytopes, and excluding the infinite sets of the [duoprisms](wikipedia://en/2114155) and the [antiprismatic prisms](wikipedia://en/8813490).

- 5 are polyhedral prisms based on the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) (1 overlap with regular since a cubic hyperprism is a [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112))
- 13 are polyhedral prisms based on the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847)
- 9 are in the self-dual regular A<sub>4</sub> \[3,3,3\] group ([5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821)) family.
- 9 are in the self-dual regular F<sub>4</sub> \[3,4,3\] group ([24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485)) family. (Excluding snub 24-cell)
- 15 are in the regular B<sub>4</sub> \[3,3,4\] group ([tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112)/[16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422)) family (3 overlap with 24-cell family)
- 15 are in the regular H<sub>4</sub> \[3,3,5\] group ([120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358)/[600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447)) family.
- 1 special snub form in the \[3,4,3\] group ([24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485)) family.
- 1 special non-Wythoffian 4-polytope, the grand antiprism.
- TOTAL: 68 − 4 = 64

These 64 uniform 4-polytopes are indexed below by George Olshevsky. Repeated symmetry forms are indexed in brackets.

In addition to the 64 above, there are 2 infinite prismatic sets that generate all of the remaining convex forms:

- Set of [uniform antiprismatic prisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_antiprismatic_hyperprism) - sr{*p*,2}×{ } - Polyhedral prisms of two [antiprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiprisms).
- Set of uniform [duoprisms](wikipedia://en/2114155) - {*p*}×{*q*} - A [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two polygons.

### The B<sub>4</sub> family {#The_B4_family}

This family has [*diploid hexadecachoric* symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexadecachoric_symmetry),[^o12] \[4,3,3\], of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_order) 24×16=384: 4!=24 permutations of the four axes, 2<sup>4</sup>=16 for reflection in each axis. There are 3 small index subgroups, with the first two generate uniform 4-polytopes which are also repeated in other families, \[1<sup>\+</sup>,4,3,3\], \[4,(3,3)<sup>\+</sup>\], and \[4,3,3\]<sup>\+</sup>, all order 192.

### The grand antiprism {#The_grand_antiprism}

There is one non-Wythoffian uniform convex 4-polytope, known as the [grand antiprism](wikipedia://en/4058047), consisting of 20 [pentagonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/2860091) forming two perpendicular rings joined by 300 [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606). It is loosely analogous to the three-dimensional [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851), which consist of two parallel [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621) joined by a band of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654). Unlike them, however, the grand antiprism is not a member of an infinite family of uniform polytopes.

Its symmetry is the *ionic diminished Coxeter group*, \[\[10,2<sup>\+</sup>,10\]\], order 400.

### Prismatic uniform 4-polytopes {#Prismatic_uniform_4-polytopes}

A prismatic polytope is a [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two polytopes of lower dimension; familiar examples are the 3-dimensional [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), which are products of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) and a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860). The prismatic uniform 4-polytopes consist of two infinite families:

- *Polyhedral prisms*: products of a line segment and a uniform polyhedron. This family is infinite because it includes prisms built on 3-dimensional prisms and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851).
- *Duoprisms*: products of two polygons.

#### Convex polyhedral prisms {#Convex_polyhedral_prisms}

The most obvious family of prismatic 4-polytopes is the *polyhedral prisms,* i.e. products of a polyhedron with a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860). The cells of such a 4-polytopes are two identical uniform polyhedra lying in parallel [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) (the *base* cells) and a layer of prisms joining them (the *lateral* cells). This family includes prisms for the 75 nonprismatic [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223) (of which 18 are convex; one of these, the cube-prism, is listed above as the *tesseract*).

There are *18 convex polyhedral prisms* created from 5 [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) and 13 [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847) as well as for the infinite families of three-dimensional [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851). The symmetry number of a polyhedral prism is twice that of the base polyhedron.

#### Tetrahedral prisms: A<sub>3</sub> × A<sub>1</sub> {#Tetrahedral_prisms:_A3_×_A1}

#### Duoprisms: \[p\] × \[q\] {#Duoprisms:_[p]_×_[q]}

*Image: 3-3 duoprism.png.* The simplest of the duoprisms, the 3,3-duoprism, in [Schlegel diagram](wikipedia://en/7201595), one of 6 [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584) cells shown.

The second is the infinite family of [uniform duoprisms](wikipedia://en/2114155), products of two [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306). A duoprism's [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) is . Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is a [disphenoid tetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disphenoid_tetrahedron),

*Image: Pq-duoprism verf.png.*

.

This family overlaps with the first: when one of the two "factor" polygons is a square, the product is equivalent to a hyperprism whose base is a three-dimensional prism. The symmetry number of a duoprism whose factors are a *p*-gon and a *q*-gon (a "*p,q*-duoprism") is 4*pq* if *p*≠*q*; if the factors are both *p*-gons, the symmetry number is 8*p*<sup>2</sup>. The tesseract can also be considered a 4,4-duoprism.

The extended [f-vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-vector) of {*p*}×{*q*} is (*p*,*p*,1)\*(*q*,*q*,1) = (*pq*,2*pq*,*pq*+*p*+*q*,*p*+*q*).

- Cells: *p* *q*-gonal prisms, *q* *p*-gonal prisms
- Faces: *pq* squares, *p* *q*-gons, *q* *p*-gons
- Edges: *2pq*
- Vertices: *pq*

There is no uniform analogue in four dimensions to the infinite family of three-dimensional [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851).

### Nonuniform alternations {#Nonuniform_alternations}

*Image: Snubcubes in grCO.svg.* Like the 3-dimensional [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249), , an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) removes half the vertices, in two chiral sets of vertices from the ringed form , however the *uniform* solution requires the vertex positions be adjusted for equal lengths. In four dimensions, this adjustment is only possible for 2 alternated figures, while the rest only exist as nonequilateral alternated figures.

Coxeter showed only two uniform solutions for rank 4 Coxeter groups with all rings [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) (shown with empty circle nodes). The first is , s{2<sup>1,1,1</sup>} which represented an index 24 subgroup ([symmetry](wikipedia://en/32088502) \[2,2,2\]<sup>\+</sup>, order 8) form of the [demitesseract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demitesseract), , h{4,3,3} (symmetry \[1<sup>\+</sup>,4,3,3\] = \[3<sup>1,1,1</sup>\], order 192). The second is , s{3<sup>1,1,1</sup>}, which is an index 6 subgroup (symmetry \[3<sup>1,1,1</sup>\]<sup>\+</sup>, order 96) form of the [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756), , s{3,4,3}, (symmetry \[3<sup>\+</sup>,4,3\], order 576).

Other alternations, such as , as an alternation from the [omnitruncated tesseract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncated_tesseract) , can not be made uniform as solving for equal edge lengths are in general [overdetermined](wikipedia://en/10160606) (there are six equations but only four variables). Such nonuniform alternated figures can be constructed as [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) 4-polytopes by the removal of one of two half sets of the vertices of the full ringed figure, but will have unequal edge lengths. Just like uniform alternations, they will have half of the symmetry of uniform figure, like \[4,3,3\]<sup>\+</sup>, order 192, is the symmetry of the *alternated omnitruncated tesseract*.[^o86]

Wythoff constructions with alternations produce [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) figures that can be made equilateral, but not uniform because the alternated gaps (around the removed vertices) create cells that are not regular or semiregular. A proposed name for such figures is **scaliform polytopes**.[^o87] This category allows a subset of [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) as cells, for example the [triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1128465).

Each [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) within a Johnson solid must exist within the vertex figure. For example, a square pyramid has two vertex configurations: 3.3.4 around the base, and 3.3.3.3 at the apex.

### Geometric derivations for 46 nonprismatic Wythoffian uniform polychora {#Geometric_derivations_for_46_nonprismatic_Wythoffian_uniform_polychora}

The 46 Wythoffian 4-polytopes include the six [convex regular 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_regular_4-polytope). The other forty can be derived from the regular polychora by geometric operations which preserve most or all of their [symmetries](wikipedia://en/53741), and therefore may be classified by the [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186) that they have in common.

:::

The geometric operations that derive the 40 uniform 4-polytopes from the regular 4-polytopes are *truncating* operations. A 4-polytope may be truncated at the vertices, edges or faces, leading to addition of cells corresponding to those elements, as shown in the columns of the tables below.

The *[Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram)* shows the four mirrors of the Wythoffian kaleidoscope as nodes, and the edges between the nodes are labeled by an integer showing the angle between the mirrors ([π](wikipedia://en/23601)/*n* [radians](wikipedia://en/26003) or 180/*n* degrees). Circled nodes show which mirrors are active for each form; a mirror is active with respect to a vertex that does not lie on it.

## Uniform star polychora {#Uniform_star_polychora}

Other than the aforementioned infinite duoprism and antiprism prism families, which have infinitely many nonconvex members, many uniform star polychora have been discovered. In 1852, Ludwig Schläfli discovered four *regular* star polychora: {5,3,5/2}, {5/2,3,5}, {3,3,5/2}, and {5/2,3,3}. In 1883, Edmund Hess found the other six: {3,5,5/2}, {5/2,5,3}, {5,5/2,5}, {5/2,5,5/2}, {5,5/2,3}, and {3,5/2,5}. Norman Johnson described three uniform antiprism-like star polychora in his doctoral dissertation of 1966: they are based on the three [ditrigonal polyhedra](wikipedia://en/40779564) sharing the edges and vertices of the regular dodecahedron. Many more have been found since then by other researchers, including Jonathan Bowers and George Olshevsky, creating a total count of 2127 known uniform star polychora at present (not counting the infinite set of duoprisms based on star polygons). There is currently no proof of the set's completeness.
