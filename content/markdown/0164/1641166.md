# Simon P. Norton

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Simon P. Norton"}

**Simon Phillips Norton** (28 February 1952 – 13 February 2019)[^o3] was a mathematician in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), England, who worked on finite [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Simon Norton was born into a [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi) family of [Iraqi descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Iraq), the youngest of three brothers.[^o5]

From 1964 he was a [King's Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Scholar) at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College), where he earned a reputation as an eccentric mathematical genius and was taught by [Norman Routledge](wikipedia://en/63664151). He obtained an external first-class degree in Pure Mathematics at the University of London while still at the school, commuting to [Royal Holloway College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway,_University_of_London).

He also represented the United Kingdom at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) thrice consecutively starting from 1967, winning a gold medal each time and two special prizes in 1967 and 1969.[^o6]

He then went up to [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), and achieved a first in the final examinations.

## Career and life {#Career_and_life}

He stayed at Cambridge, working on [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707). Norton was one of the authors of the [ATLAS of Finite Groups](wikipedia://en/22659407). He constructed the [Harada–Norton group](wikipedia://en/1196772) and in 1979, together with [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) proved there is a connection between the [Monster group](wikipedia://en/47422) and the [*j*-function](wikipedia://en/451988) in number theory. They dubbed this "[monstrous moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168)", and made some conjectures later proved by [Richard Borcherds](wikipedia://en/292169). Norton also made several early discoveries in [Conway's Game of Life](wikipedia://en/37035),[^o8] and invented the game [Snort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snort_%28game%29).

In 1985, Cambridge University did not renew his contract.

Norton is the subject of the biography *The Genius in My Basement*, written by his Cambridge tenant, [Alexander Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Masters),[^o9] which describes his eccentric lifestyle and his life-long obsession with buses. He was also an occasional contributor to *[Word Ways: The Journal of Recreational Linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_Ways:_The_Journal_of_Recreational_Linguistics)*.

Norton was very interested in transport issues and was a member of Subterranea Britannica. He coordinated the local group of the [Campaign for Better Transport (United Kingdom)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Better_Transport_%28United_Kingdom%29), and had done so since the organisation was known as Transport 2000, writing most of the newsletter for the local Cambridge group[^o10] and tirelessly campaigning for efficient, inclusive and environmentally friendly public transport in the region and across the United Kingdom.

He collapsed and died in north London, aged 66, of a heart condition on 13 February 2019.[^o3][^o11]
