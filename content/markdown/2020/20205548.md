# Leo the Mathematician

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leo the Mathematician"}

**Leo the Mathematician**, **the Grammarian** or **the Philosopher** (Λέων ὁ Μαθηματικός or ὁ Φιλόσοφος, *Léōn ho Mathēmatikós* or *ho Philósophos*; c. 790 – after January 9, 869[^o2]) was a [Byzantine philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_philosophy) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) associated with the [Macedonian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Renaissance) and the end of [the Second Byzantine Iconoclasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm). His only preserved writings are some notes contained in manuscripts of [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954)'s dialogues. He has been called a "true [Renaissance man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath)"[^o3] and "the cleverest man in Byzantium in the 9th century".[^o4] He was [archbishop of Thessalonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Thessalonica) and later became the head of the [Magnaura School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnaura) of philosophy in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople),[^o5] where he taught [Aristotelian logic](wikipedia://en/499019).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Leo was born in [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly), a cousin of the [Patriarch of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople), [John the Grammarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_VII_of_Constantinople). Little is known about his early life. In his youth he was educated at Constantinople, but found higher education options limited in the city and thus travelled to the monasteries of [Andros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andros,_Greece), where he could obtain rare manuscripts and was taught mathematics by an old monk.[^o7][^o8] He originally taught privately in obscurity in Constantinople. The story goes that when one of his students[^o9] was captured during the [Byzantine–Arab Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine–Arab_Wars), the [Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph_of_Baghdad) [al-Mamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mamun) was so impressed by his knowledge of mathematics, providing proofs that his own scholars were unable to complete, and predictive abilities that he offered Leo great riches to come to [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad).[^o10] Leo took the letter from the caliph to the [Byzantine emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_emperor) [Theophilos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophilos_%28emperor%29), who, impressed by his international repute, conferred on him a school (*ekpaideutērion*) in either the Magnaura or the church of the [Forty Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste).[^o11]

In the version of the story recorded by [Theophanes Continuatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophanes_Continuatus), the caliph, upon receiving Leo's letter of refusal, sent a letter requesting answers to some difficult questions of geometry and astrology, which Leo obliged. Al-Mamun then offered two thousands pounds of gold and a perpetual peace to Theophilos, if only he could borrow Leo's services briefly; the request was declined. The emperor then honoured Leo by having John the Grammarian consecrate him [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of [Thessalonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessalonica), which post he held from the spring of 840 to 843. There is a discrepancy in this account, however, in that the caliph died in 833. It has been suggested that either the connection between the caliph's final letter and Leo's appointment as metropolitan is in error, or the caliph in question was actually al-Mutasim. This latter option squares with the account of [Symeon the Logothete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symeon_the_Logothete), who makes Leo teach at the Magnaura from late 838 to early 840 and was paid handsomely.[^o12]

Leo, an iconoclast sometimes accused of paganism, lost his metropolitancy with the end of the [Iconoclasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm) in 843.[^o13] Despite this, he delivered a sermon favourable of icons within months of Theophilos' death.[^o14] Around 855, Leo was appointed at the head of a newly established Magnaura School by [Bardas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardas).[^o15] He was renowned for his philosophical, mathematical, medical, scientific, literary, philological, seismological, astronomic, and astrological learning, and was patronised by [Theoktistos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoktistos) and befriended by [Photios I of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photios_I_of_Constantinople).[^o16][^o17] [Cyril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius) was his student. Leo has been credited with a [system of beacons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_beacon_system) (an optical telegraph) stretching across [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor) from [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia) to Constantinople, which gave advance warning of Arab raids, as well as diplomatic communication.[^o5][^o18] Leo also invented several [automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automata), such as trees with moving birds, roaring lions, and a levitating imperial throne.[^o3] The throne was in operation a century later, when [Liutprand of Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona) witnessed it during his visit to Constantinople.[^o19][^o20]

## Works {#Works}

Most of Leo's writings have been lost. He wrote book-length works, poems, and many [epigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigrams),[^o22] and was also a compiler, who brought together a wide range of philosophical, medical, and astronomic texts. His library can at least partially be reconstructed: [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844), [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331), [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954), [Paul of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_of_Alexandria), [Theon of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theon_of_Alexandria), [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus), [Porphyry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_%28philosopher%29), [Apollonius of Perga](wikipedia://en/257242), the lost *Mechanics* of Quirinus and Marcellus, and possibly [Thucydides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thucydides).[^o7] He composed his own medical encyclopaedia. Later Byzantine scholars sometimes confused Leo with the scholar [Leo Choirosphaktes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Choirosphaktes) and the emperor [Leo VI the Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_VI_the_Wise), and ascribe to him [oracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle).
