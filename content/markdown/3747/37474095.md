# Adolph Winkler Goodman

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Adolph Winkler Goodman"}

**Adolph Winkler Goodman** (July 20, 1915 – July 30, 2004) was an American mathematician who contributed to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and to the theory of [univalent functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univalent_functions):[^o4] [The conjecture](wikipedia://en/71506875) on the coefficients of multivalent functions named after him is considered the most interesting challenge in the area after the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture), proved by [Louis de Branges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Branges) in 1985.[^o5]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

In 1948, he made a mathematical conjecture on coefficients of $\rho$-valent functions, first published in his [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) dissertation thesis[^o7] and then in a closely following paper.[^o8] After the proof of the Bieberbach conjecture by Louis de Branges, this conjecture is considered the most interesting challenge in the field,[^o5] and he himself and coauthors answered affirmatively to the conjecture for some classes of $\rho$-valent functions.[^o9] His researches in the field continued in the paper *Univalent functions and nonanalytic curves*, published in 1957:[^o10] in 1968, he published the survey *Open problems on univalent and multivalent functions*,[^o11] which eventually led him to write the two-volume book *Univalent Functions*.[^o12][^o13]

Apart from his research activity, He was actively involved in teaching: he wrote several college and high school textbooks including *Analytic Geometry and the Calculus*, and the five-volume set *Algebra from A to Z*.[^o4]

He retired in 1993, became a Distinguished Professor Emeritus in 1995, and died in 2004.[^o4]
