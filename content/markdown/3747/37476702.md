# Sesquipower

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sesquipower"}

In mathematics, a **sesquipower** or **Zimin word** is a [string](wikipedia://en/27701) over an alphabet with identical [prefix and suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefix_%28computer_science%29). Sesquipowers are [unavoidable patterns](wikipedia://en/37477193), in the sense that all sufficiently long strings contain one.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Formally, let *A* be an alphabet and *A*<sup>∗</sup> be the [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) of finite strings over *A*. Every non-empty word *w* in *A*<sup>\+</sup> is a sesquipower of order 1. If *u* is a sesquipower of order *n* then any word *w* = *uvu* is a sesquipower of order *n* + 1.[^o4] The *degree* of a non-empty word *w* is the largest integer *d* such that *w* is a sesquipower of order *d*.[^o5]

:::

## Bi-ideal sequence {#Bi-ideal_sequence}

A **bi-ideal sequence** is a sequence of words *f*<sub>*i*</sub> where *f*<sub>1</sub> is in *A*<sup>\+</sup> and

$$f_{i+1} = f_i g_i f_i \$$

for some *g*<sub>*i*</sub> in *A*<sup>∗</sup> and *i* ≥ 1. The degree of a word *w* is thus the length of the longest bi-ideal sequence ending in *w*.[^o5]

## Unavoidable patterns {#Unavoidable_patterns}

For a finite alphabet *A* on *k* letters, there is an integer *M* depending on *k* and *n*, such that any word of length *M* has a factor which is a sesquipower of order at least *n*. We express this by saying that the sesquipowers are *unavoidable patterns*.[^o8][^o9]

## Sesquipowers in infinite sequences {#Sesquipowers_in_infinite_sequences}

Given an infinite bi-ideal sequence, we note that each *f*<sub>*i*</sub> is a prefix of *f*<sub>*i*+1</sub> and so the *f*<sub>*i*</sub> converge to an infinite sequence

$$f = f_1 g_1 f_1 g_2 f_1 g_1 f_1 g_3 f_1 \cdots \$$

We define an infinite word to be a sesquipower if it is the limit of an infinite bi-ideal sequence.[^o11] An infinite word is a sesquipower if and only if it is a [recurrent word](wikipedia://en/37478359),[^o11][^o13] that is, every factor occurs infinitely often.[^o12]

Fix a finite alphabet *A* and assume a [total order](wikipedia://en/30330) on the letters. For given integers *p* and *n*, every sufficiently long word in *A*<sup>∗</sup> has either a factor which is a *p*-power or a factor which is an *n*-sesquipower; in the latter case the factor has an *n*-[factorisation](wikipedia://en/37206260) into [Lyndon words](wikipedia://en/3919355).[^o13]
