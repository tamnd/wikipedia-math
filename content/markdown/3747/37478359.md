# Recurrent word

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Recurrent word"}

In mathematics, a **recurrent word** or **sequence** is an infinite word over a finite alphabet in which every factor occurs infinitely many times.[^o2][^o3][^o4] An infinite word is recurrent if and only if it is a [sesquipower](wikipedia://en/37476702).[^o5][^o6]

:::

A **uniformly recurrent word** is a recurrent word in which for any given factor *X* in the sequence, there is some length *n*<sub>*X*</sub> (often much longer than the length of *X*) such that *X* appears in *every* block of length *n*<sub>*X*</sub>.[^o2][^o7][^o8] The terms **minimal sequence**[^o9] and **almost periodic sequence** (Muchnik, Semenov, Ushakov 2003) are also used.

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The easiest way to make a recurrent sequence is to form a [periodic sequence](wikipedia://en/810250), one where the sequence repeats entirely after a given number *m* of steps. Such a sequence is then uniformly recurrent and *n*<sub>*X*</sub> can be set to any multiple of *m* that is larger than twice the length of *X*. A recurrent sequence that is ultimately periodic is purely periodic.[^o3]
- The [Thue–Morse sequence](wikipedia://en/491903) is uniformly recurrent *without* being periodic, nor even eventually periodic (meaning periodic after some nonperiodic initial segment).[^o12]
- All [Sturmian words](wikipedia://en/1701956) are uniformly recurrent.[^o13]

:::
