# Isbell duality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isbell duality"}

In mathematics, **Isbell conjugacy** (a.k.a. **Isbell duality** or **Isbell adjunction**) (named after [John R. Isbell](wikipedia://en/31277386)[^o2]) is a fundamental construction of [enriched category theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enriched_category_theory) formally introduced by [William Lawvere](wikipedia://en/872760) in 1986.[^o3][^o4] That is a duality between covariant and contravariant representable [presheaves](wikipedia://en/4840944) associated with an objects of categories under the Yoneda embedding.[^o5][^o6] In addition, Lawvere[^o7] says; "Then the conjugacies are the first step toward expressing the duality between space and quantity fundamental to mathematics".[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Yoneda embedding {#Yoneda_embedding}

The (covariant) [Yoneda embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda_embedding) is a [covariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_functor) from a small category $\mathcal{A}$ into the [category of presheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_presheaves) $\left[\mathcal{A}^{op}, \mathcal{V} \right]$ on $\mathcal{A}$, taking $X \in \mathcal{A}$ to the contravariant [representable functor](wikipedia://en/844461): [^o12][^o13]

$$y \; (h^{\bullet}) :\mathcal{A} \rightarrow \left[\mathcal{A}^{op}, \mathcal{V} \right]$$

$$X \mapsto \mathrm{hom} (-,X).$$

and the co-Yoneda embedding (a.k.a. dual Yoneda embedding[^o14]) is a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from a small category $\mathcal{A}$ into the [opposite](wikipedia://en/372399#Properties) of the [category of co-presheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_co-presheaves) $\left[\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{V} \right]^{op}$ on $\mathcal{A}$, taking $X \in \mathcal{A}$ to the covariant representable functor:

$$z \; ({h_{\bullet}}^{op}): \mathcal{A} \rightarrow \left[\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{V} \right]^{op}$$

$$X \mapsto \mathrm{hom} (X,-).$$

### Isbell duality {#Isbell_duality}

*Image: Isbell duality.svg.* Origin of symbols $\mathcal{O}$ (“[ring of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_functions)”) and $\mathrm{Spec}$ (“spectrum”): (Lawvere 1986, 169) says that; "$\mathcal{O}$" assigns to each general space the algebra of functions on it, whereas "$\mathrm{Spec}$" assigns to each algebra its “spectrum” which is a general space.

*Image: Nerve and realization (ver. left kan extension).svg.* note:In order for this commutative diagram to hold, it is required that $\mathcal{A}$ is small and E is co-complete.[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20]

Every functor $F \colon \mathcal{A}^\mathrm{op}\to \mathcal{V}$ has an Isbell conjugate of a functor $F^{\ast} \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{V}$, given by

$$F^{\ast} (X) = \mathrm{hom} (F , y(X)).$$

In contrast, every functor $G \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{V}$ has an Isbell conjugate of a functor $G^{\ast} \colon \mathcal{A}^\mathrm{op} \to \mathcal{V}$ given by

$$G^{\ast} (X) = \mathrm{hom} (z(X) , G).$$

These two functors are not typically inverses, or even [natural isomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism). Isbell duality asserts that the relationship between these two functors is an adjunction.

**Isbell duality** is the relationship between Yoneda embedding and co-Yoneda embedding；

Let $\mathcal{V}$ be a [symmetric monoidal closed category](wikipedia://en/1396924), and let $\mathcal{A}$ be a small category enriched in $\mathcal{V}$.

The **Isbell duality** is an [adjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunction_%28category_theory%29) between the functor categories; $\left(\mathcal{O} \dashv \mathrm{Spec} \right) \colon \left[\mathcal{A}^{op}, \mathcal{V} \right] {\underset{\mathrm{Spec}}{\overset{\mathcal{O}}{\rightleftarrows}}} \left[\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{V} \right]^{op}$.[^o21][^o22][^o23][^o24]

Applying the nerve construction, the functors $\mathcal{O} \dashv \mathrm{Spec}$ of Isbell duality are such that $\mathcal{O} \cong \mathrm{Lan_{y}z}$ and $\mathrm{Spec} \cong \mathrm{Lan_{z}y}$.[^o25][^o26]
