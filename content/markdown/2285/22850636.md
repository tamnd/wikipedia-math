# Generalized trigonometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized trigonometry"}

Ordinary [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) studies [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^2$. There are a number of ways of defining the ordinary [Euclidean geometric](wikipedia://en/9417) [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) on [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), for example [right-angled triangle definitions](wikipedia://en/30367#Right-angled_triangle_definitions), [unit circle definitions](wikipedia://en/30367#Unit-circle_definitions), [series definitions](wikipedia://en/30367#Power_series_expansion), [definitions via differential equations](wikipedia://en/30367#Definition_by_differential_equations), and [definitions using functional equations](wikipedia://en/30367#Definitions_using_functional_equations). **Generalizations of trigonometric functions** are often developed by starting with one of the above methods and adapting it to a situation other than the real numbers of Euclidean geometry. Generally, trigonometry can be the study of triples of points in any kind of [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_geometry_topics#Types,_methodologies,_and_terminologies_of_geometry) or [space](wikipedia://en/5308894). A triangle is the [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with the smallest number of vertices, so one direction to generalize is to study higher-dimensional analogs of [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) and polygons: [solid angles](wikipedia://en/94102) and [polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytopes) such as [tetrahedrons](wikipedia://en/30606) and [$n$-simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-simplex).

:::

## Trigonometry {#Trigonometry}

- In [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405), triangles on the surface of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) are studied. The spherical triangle identities are written in terms of the ordinary trigonometric functions but differ from the plane [triangle identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_identities).
- Hyperbolic trigonometry:
  1. Study of [hyperbolic triangles](wikipedia://en/1283865) in [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291) with [hyperbolic functions](wikipedia://en/56567).
  1. [Hyperbolic functions](wikipedia://en/56567) in Euclidean geometry: The [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) is parameterized by (cos *t*, sin *t*) whereas the equilateral [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) is parameterized by (cosh *t*, sinh *t*).
  1. [Gyrotrigonometry](wikipedia://en/20295286#Gyrotrigonometry): A form of trigonometry used in the [gyrovector space](wikipedia://en/20295286) approach to [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), with applications to [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) and [quantum computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computation).
- Trigonometry for [taxicab geometry](wikipedia://en/408354)[^o3]
- Spacetime trigonometries[^o4]
- Fuzzy qualitative trigonometry[^o5]
- Operator trigonometry[^o6]
- Lattice trigonometry[^o7]
- Trigonometry on symmetric spaces[^o8][^o9][^o10]

## Higher dimensions {#Higher_dimensions}

- [Schläfli orthoschemes](wikipedia://en/20740340) - right simplexes (right triangles generalized to *n* dimensions) - studied by [Schoute](wikipedia://en/6022750) who called the generalized trigonometry of *n* Euclidean dimensions **[polygonometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonometry)**.
  - Pythagorean theorems for [*n*-simplices with an "orthogonal corner"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-simplex#Simplices_with_an_"orthogonal_corner")
- [Trigonometry of a tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/48757693)[^o12]
  - [De Gua's theorem](wikipedia://en/6469973) – a Pythagorean theorem for a tetrahedron with a cube corner
  - [A law of sines for tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606#A_law_of_sines_for_tetrahedra_and_the_space_of_all_shapes_of_tetrahedra)
- [Polar sine](wikipedia://en/17101042)

## Trigonometric functions {#Trigonometric_functions}

- Trigonometric functions can be defined for [fractional differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_differential_equation).[^o14]
- In [time scale calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_scale_calculus), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [difference equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_equation) are unified into dynamic equations on time scales which also includes [q-difference equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-difference_equation). Trigonometric functions can be defined on an arbitrary time scale (a subset of the real numbers).
- The [series definitions](wikipedia://en/30367#Series_definitions) of sin and cos define these functions on any [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) where the [series](wikipedia://en/15287) [converge](wikipedia://en/1743431) such as [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/30367#Relationship_to_exponential_function_%28Euler's_formula%29), [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/291327), [matrices](wikipedia://en/49554717), and various [Banach algebras](wikipedia://en/4665).
