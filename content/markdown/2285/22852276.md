# Newey–West estimator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Newey–West estimator"}

A **Newey–West estimator** is used in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) to provide an estimate of the [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) of the parameters of a [regression-type](wikipedia://en/48758386) model where the standard assumptions of [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997) do not apply.[^o2] It was devised by [Whitney K. Newey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_K._Newey) and [Kenneth D. West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_D._West) in 1987, although there are a number of later variants.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6] The estimator is used to try to overcome [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724) (also called serial correlation), and [heteroskedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroskedasticity) in the [error terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) in the models, often for regressions applied to [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) data. The abbreviation "HAC," sometimes used for the estimator, stands for "heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent."[^o3] There are a number of HAC estimators described in,[^o13] and HAC estimator does not refer uniquely to Newey–West. One version of Newey–West Bartlett requires the user to specify the bandwidth and usage of the Bartlett kernel from [Kernel density estimation](wikipedia://en/2090057)[^o13]

:::

Regression models estimated with time series data often exhibit autocorrelation; that is, the [error terms](wikipedia://en/4229275) are correlated over time. The [heteroscedastic consistent estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroscedasticity-consistent_standard_errors) of the error covariance is constructed from a term $X^{\operatorname{T}}\Sigma X$, where $X$ is the [design matrix](wikipedia://en/949189) for the regression problem and $\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix of the residuals. The least squares estimator $b$ is a [consistent estimator](wikipedia://en/1497569) of $\beta$. This implies that the [least squares](wikipedia://en/82359) [residuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) $e_i$ are "point-wise" consistent estimators of their population counterparts $E_i$. The general approach, then, will be to use $X$ and $e$ to devise an estimator of $X^{\operatorname{T}}\Sigma X$.[^o7] This means that as the time between error terms increases, the correlation between the error terms decreases. The estimator thus can be used to improve the [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) (OLS) [regression](wikipedia://en/826997) when the residuals are heteroscedastic and/or autocorrelated.

$$X^{\operatorname{T}}\Sigma X=\frac{1}{T} \sum^T_{t=1} e_t^2 x_t x^{\operatorname{T}}_t + \frac{1}{T} \sum^L_{\ell=1} \sum^T_{t=\ell+1} w_\ell e_t e_{t-\ell}(x_t x^{\operatorname{T}}_{t-\ell} + x_{t-\ell} x^{\operatorname{T}}_t)$$

$$w_\ell=1 - \frac\ell {L+1}$$

where *T* is the sample size, $e_t$ is the $t^\text{th}$ residual and $x_t$ is the $t^\text{th}$ row of the design matrix, and $w_\ell$ is the Bartlett kernel [^o8] and can be thought of as a weight that decreases with increasing separation between samples. Disturbances that are farther apart from each other are given lower weight, while those with equal subscripts are given a weight of 1. This ensures that second term converges (in some appropriate sense) to a finite matrix. This weighting scheme also ensures that the resulting covariance matrix is [positive semi-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_symmetric_matrix).[^o3] *L* = 0 reduces the Newey–West estimator to [Huber–White standard error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huber–White_standard_error).[^o9] *L* specifies the "maximum lag considered for the control of autocorrelation. A common choice for *L*" is $T^{1/4}$.[^o9][^o10]

## Software implementations {#Software_implementations}

In [Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_%28programming_language%29), the CovarianceMatrices.jl package [^o12] supports several types of heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent covariance matrix estimation including Newey–West, White, and Arellano.

In [R](wikipedia://en/376707), the packages `sandwich`[^o13] and `plm`[^o14] include a function for the Newey–West estimator.

In [Stata](wikipedia://en/1809002), the command `newey` produces Newey–West standard errors for coefficients estimated by OLS regression.[^o15]

In [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412), the command `hac` in the Econometrics toolbox produces the Newey–West estimator (among others).[^o16]

In [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29), the `statsmodels`[^o17] module includes functions for the covariance matrix using Newey–West.

In [Gretl](wikipedia://en/1166251), the option `--robust` to several estimation commands (such as `ols`) in the context of a time-series dataset produces Newey–West standard errors.[^o18]

In [SAS](wikipedia://en/990677), the Newey–West corrected standard errors can be obtained in PROC AUTOREG and PROC MODEL [^o19]
