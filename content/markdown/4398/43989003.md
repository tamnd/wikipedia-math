# Katalin Marton

Marton Katalin

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Katalin Marton"}

**Katalin Marton** (9 December 1941 – 13 December 2019) was a Hungarian mathematician, born in Budapest.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Marton obtained her PhD from [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in 1965 and worked at the Department of Numerical Mathematics, Central Research Institute for Physics, Budapest from 1965 to 1973. Important influences on her early career were her attendance at the combinatorics seminar organised by [Alfréd Rényi](wikipedia://en/614988) from 1966, meeting [Roland Dobrushin](wikipedia://en/6368767) in Debrecen in 1967 (which led to her visiting the Institute for Problems in Information Transmission in Moscow in 1969[^o5]), and her collaboration with [Imre Csiszár](wikipedia://en/13008077) which began in 1972. From 1973 she worked at the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), visiting the United States in 1977 (for the [International Symposium on Information Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_International_Symposium_on_Information_Theory) in Ithaca) and in 1979–80 (meeting [Robert Gallager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gallager) at MIT and [Robert M. Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Gray) at Stanford).

## Research interests {#Research_interests}

Marton worked on various areas of mathematics, including [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [concentration of measure](wikipedia://en/788497) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). In a 1974 paper on information theory she used a combinatorics approach to characterize error in discrete memoryless sources under distortion.[^o2] She was particularly well known for her two-page proof, based on an information-theoretic coupling inequality, of the blowing-up lemma,[^o7] published in 1986. This result, which arose out of work of [Grigory Margulis](wikipedia://en/1104948) in 1974[^o8] and which was developed further by [Rudolf Ahlswede](wikipedia://en/18251788), Peter Gács and [János Körner](wikipedia://en/63058245),[^o9] shows that (in product measures) the neighbourhood of a set of greater than exponentially small size has size close to 1. This result is used in a variety of contexts including strong converse results for coding theorems, classification and model selection.

Marton was also responsible for the polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa conjecture,[^o10] a central question of [additive combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243392), now also called [Freiman's theorem](wikipedia://en/2240299). This was published by [Imre Ruzsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Z._Ruzsa) but as he mentions[^o11] this conjecture came from Marton. It states that if a subset $A$ of a group $G$ (a power of a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327)) has small [doubling constant](wikipedia://en/18243392#Doubling_constant) then $A$ lies in the union of a bounded polynomial number of cosets of some subgroup $H$. This conjecture is deeply characteristic to the way Marton fed back particular information-theoretic results into the mainstream of mathematics. In 2012 [Tom Sanders](wikipedia://en/36766438) gave an almost polynomial bound of the conjecture for abelian groups.[^o12][^o14] In 2023 a solution over $G=\mathbb F_2^n$ a field of characteristic 2 has been posted as a preprint by [Tim Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715), [Ben Green](wikipedia://en/1927952), Freddie Manners and [Terry Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498).[^o13][^o14]

Marton's other major contributions included coding theorems for the broadcast channel[^o15][^o16] (with the former paper proving the best-known inner bound on the capacity region of the two-receiver general broadcast channel, often referred to as "Marton's inner bound"[^o17]) and many other results in concentration of measure,[^o18][^o19] [rate-distortion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate-distortion_theory)[^o20][^o21] and graph capacity.[^o22][^o23] Marton had an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 2, for example via her collaboration[^o24] with [Imre Csiszár](wikipedia://en/13008077) and [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938).

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

In 1996, Marton won the [Alfréd Rényi Prize](wikipedia://en/26466805) from the Alfréd Rényi Institute. In 2013, she was the first (and so far only) female winner of the [Claude E. Shannon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award), the top prize in [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), from the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE). As a result, she delivered the Shannon Lecture at the International Symposium on Information Theory in Istanbul in 2013, with her talk being entitled Distance-Divergence Inequalities.[^o26][^o27][^o28] The citation and biographical sketch[^o29] paid tribute to her scientific contributions, with Fields Medallist [Cédric Villani](wikipedia://en/27172341) writing:

> "Marton is one of the leading authorities about the applications of information theory techniques to concentration theory, in particular in the setting of [Markov Chains](wikipedia://en/60876). Most importantly, in the mid-nineties, Marton pointed out the interest and importance of entropy inequalities in the study of the concentration phenomena. Talagrand has acknowledged the influence of Marton in this respect, and this motivated him to establish the famous [Talagrand](wikipedia://en/11091583) inequality[^o30] controlling the [Wasserstein distance](wikipedia://en/6704603) by the square root of the [Boltzmann-Shannon information](wikipedia://en/15445). In turn, the Talagrand inequality triggered the development a whole field, which I explored with [Otto](wikipedia://en/4248548), [McCann](wikipedia://en/60947361), [Lott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lott_%28mathematician%29) and others, involving entropy, concentration, [transport](wikipedia://en/6631661), [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582), with very far reaching geometric consequences."

In 2013, Marton was also awarded the József Eötvös Wreath by the [Hungarian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Science).[^o32]
