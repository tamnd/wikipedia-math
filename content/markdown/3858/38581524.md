# Rudolf Halin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rudolf Halin"}

**Rudolf Halin** (February 3, 1934 – November 14, 2014) was a German [graph theorist](wikipedia://en/12401), known for defining the [ends](wikipedia://en/33989041) of infinite graphs,[^n1] for [Halin's grid theorem](wikipedia://en/38580904),[^n2][^o4] for extending [Menger's theorem](wikipedia://en/1155738) to infinite graphs,[^n3] and for his early research on [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) and [tree decomposition](wikipedia://en/159023).[^n4] He is also the namesake of [Halin graphs](wikipedia://en/19658548), a class of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) constructed from [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) by adding a cycle through the leaves of the given tree; earlier researchers had studied the subclass of [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) Halin graphs but Halin was the first to study this class of graphs in full generality.[^n5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Halin was born on February 3, 1934, in [Uerdingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uerdingen).[^o9] He earned his doctorate from the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) in 1962, under the supervision of [Klaus Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Wagner) and Karl Dörge, after which he joined the faculty of the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg). He died on November 14, 2014, in [Mölln, Schleswig-Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mölln,_Schleswig-Holstein).[^o9]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In February 1994, a colloquium was held at the University of Hamburg in honor of Halin's 60th birthday.[^o12] In 2017, a special issue of the journal *Abhandlungen aus dem Mathematischen Seminar der Universität Hamburg* was published in his memory.[^o13]
