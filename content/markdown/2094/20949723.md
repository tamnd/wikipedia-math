# Topical logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topical logic"}

**Topical logic** is the [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) of **topical argument**, a branch of [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447) developed in the [Late Antique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Antique) period from earlier works, such as [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s *[Topics](wikipedia://en/1692819)* and [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero)'s *Topica*. It consists of [heuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristics) for developing [arguments](wikipedia://en/13018310), which are in the first place plausible rather than rigorous, from commonplaces (*topoi* or *loci*). In other words, therefore, it consists of standardized ways of thinking up debating techniques from existing, thought-through positions. The actual practice of topical argument was much developed by Roman lawyers.[^o2][^o3] Cicero took the theory of Aristotle to be an aspect of rhetoric.[^o4] As such it belongs to *inventio* in the classic fivefold division of rhetoric.

:::

The standard classical work on topical logic was the *De Topicis Differentiis* (On Topical Differentiae) by [Boethius](wikipedia://en/195684). *Differentiae* refer to [case analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_study), being the differentiations used to distinguish the cases into which a question is divided. Besides Aristotle and Cicero, Boethius built on [Themistius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Themistius).[^o5] In terminology, the Greek *axioma* and *topos* in Boethius became the Latin *maxima propositio* ([maxim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_%28philosophy%29), universal truth) and *locus*.

In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), topical logic became a theory of [inference](wikipedia://en/317465), for which the name "axiomatic topics" has been suggested.[^o6] [Abelard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelard) wanted to complete a theory of [entailment](wikipedia://en/37531624) by invoking the *loci* in Boethius to fill in conditionals, a flawed if bold development.[^o7][^o8] [Peter of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_of_Spain_%28author%29), in his *De locis*, developed the ideas of Boethius.[^o9]

The *De inventione dialectica* of [Rodolphus Agricola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolphus_Agricola) (1479) made large claims for this method as an aspect of [dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813) (traditionally contrasted with rhetoric) subordinated to *inventio*.[^o10] The precise relationship of "dialectic" and "rhetoric" remained vexed well into the sixteenth century, hinging on the role assigned to *loci*. It was expounded in different fashions by [Philipp Melanchthon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Melanchthon) and [Petrus Ramus](wikipedia://en/311650).[^o11] The debate fed into the later development of [Ramism](wikipedia://en/1691461).
