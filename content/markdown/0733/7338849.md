# Standardized coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Standardized coefficient"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **standardized (regression) coefficients**, also called **beta coefficients** or **beta weights**, are the estimates resulting from a [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997) where the underlying data have been [standardized](wikipedia://en/221722) so that the [variances](wikipedia://en/32344) of [dependent and independent variables](wikipedia://en/437701) are equal to 1.[^o2] Therefore, standardized coefficients are [unitless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitless) and refer to how many standard deviations a dependent variable will change, per standard deviation increase in the predictor variable.

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

Standardization of the coefficient is usually done to answer the question of which of the independent variables have a greater effect on the [dependent variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_variable) in a [multiple regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_regression) analysis where the variables are measured in different [units of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement) (for example, income measured in dollars and family size measured in number of individuals). It may also be considered a general measure of [effect size](wikipedia://en/437276), quantifying the "magnitude" of the effect of one variable on another. For simple linear regression with orthogonal predictors, the standardized regression coefficient equals the [correlation](wikipedia://en/29018709) between the independent and dependent variables.

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Implementation"}

## Implementation {#Implementation}

A [regression](wikipedia://en/826997) carried out on original (unstandardized) variables produces unstandardized coefficients. A regression carried out on standardized variables produces standardized coefficients. Values for standardized and unstandardized coefficients can also be re-scaled to one another subsequent to either type of analysis. Suppose that $\beta$ is the regression coefficient resulting from a [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) (predicting $y$ by $x$). The standardized coefficient simply results as $\beta^\ast = \frac{s_x}{s_y} \beta$, where $s_x$ and $s_y$ are the [(estimated) standard deviations](wikipedia://en/27590#Estimation) of $x$ and $y$, respectively.[^o2]

Sometimes, standardization is done only without respect to the standard deviation of the [regressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regressor) (the independent variable $x$).[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Advantages and disadvantages {#Advantages_and_disadvantages}

Standardized coefficients' advocates note that the coefficients are independent of the involved variables' [units of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement) (i.e., standardized coefficients are *[unitless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitless)*), which makes comparisons easy.[^o7]

Critics voice concerns that such a standardization can be very misleading.[^o6][^o9] Due to the re-scaling based on sample standard deviations, any effect apparent in the standardized coefficient may be due to [confounding](wikipedia://en/3105999) with the particularities (especially: [variability](wikipedia://en/23773247)) of the involved data sample(s). Also, the interpretation or meaning of a "*one standard deviation change*" in the regressor $x$ may vary markedly between non-[normal distributions](wikipedia://en/21462) (e.g., when [skewed](wikipedia://en/28212), [asymmetric](wikipedia://en/39284103) or [multimodal](wikipedia://en/1068378)).

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

Some [statistical software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_statistical_packages) packages like [PSPP](wikipedia://en/16653967), [SPSS](wikipedia://en/179088) and [SYSTAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SYSTAT_%28statistics%29) label the standardized regression coefficients as "Beta" while the unstandardized coefficients are labeled "B". Others, like [DAP](wikipedia://en/16671308)/[SAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_System) label them "Standardized Coefficient". Sometimes the unstandardized variables are also labeled as "b".

:::
