# Barrow's inequality

*Image: Barrow inequality.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Barrow's inequality"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Barrow's inequality** is an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) relating the [distances](wikipedia://en/53932) between an arbitrary point within a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), the vertices of the triangle, and certain points on the sides of the triangle. It is named after [David Francis Barrow](wikipedia://en/38017423).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *P* be an arbitrary point inside the [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) *ABC*. From *P* and *ABC*, define *U*, *V*, and *W* as the points where the [angle bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector) of *BPC*, *CPA*, and *APB* intersect the sides *BC*, *CA*, *AB*, respectively. Then Barrow's inequality states that[^o8]

$$PA+PB+PC\geq 2(PU+PV+PW),\,$$

with equality holding only in the case of an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) and *P* is the center of the triangle.[^o8]

:::

## Generalisation {#Generalisation}

Barrow's inequality can be extended to convex polygons. For a convex polygon with vertices $A_1,A_2,\ldots ,A_n$ let $P$ be an inner point and $Q_1, Q_2,\ldots ,Q_n$ the intersections of the angle bisectors of $\angle A_1PA_2,\ldots,\angle A_{n-1}PA_n,\angle A_nPA_1$ with the associated polygon sides $A_1A_2,\ldots ,A_{n-1}A_n, A_nA_1$, then the following inequality holds:[^o5][^o6]

$$\sum_{k=1}^n|PA_k|\geq \sec\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) \sum_{k=1}^n|PQ_k|$$

Here $\sec(x)$ denotes the [secant function](wikipedia://en/30367#Right-angled_triangle_definitions). For the triangle case $n=3$ the inequality becomes Barrow's inequality due to $\sec\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)=2$.

## History {#History}

*Image: Ungleichung barrow2.svg.* Barrow strengthening Erdős-Mordell$\begin{aligned}&\quad\, |PA|+|PB|+|PC| \\ &\ge 2 (|PQ_a|+|PQ_b|+|PQ_c|)\\ &\ge 2 (|PF_a|+|PF_b|+|PF_c|)\end{aligned}$

Barrow's inequality strengthens the [Erdős–Mordell inequality](wikipedia://en/13006855), which has identical form except with *PU*, *PV*, and *PW* replaced by the three distances of *P* from the triangle's sides. It is named after [David Francis Barrow](wikipedia://en/38017423). Barrow's proof of this inequality was published in 1937, as his solution to a problem posed in the [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) of proving the Erdős–Mordell inequality.[^o8] This result was named "Barrow's inequality" as early as 1961.[^o9]

A simpler proof was later given by [Louis J. Mordell](wikipedia://en/991484).[^o10]
