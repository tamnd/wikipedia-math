# Joseph Wolstenholme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Wolstenholme"}

**Joseph Wolstenholme** (30 September 1829 – 18 November 1891) was an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Wolstenholme was born in [Eccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccles,_Greater_Manchester) near [Salford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salford,_Greater_Manchester), [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), the son of a Methodist minister, Joseph Wolstenholme, and his wife, Elizabeth, *née* Clarke.[^o3] He graduated from [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) as Third [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1850 and was elected a fellow of [Christ's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge) in 1852. Collaborating with [Percival Frost](wikipedia://en/31113891), a *Treatise on Solid Geometry* was published in 1863.[^o4]

Wolstenholme served as Examiner in 1854, 1856, and 1863 for [Cambridge Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Tripos), and according to Andrew Forsyth his book *Mathematical Problems*[^o5] made a significant contribution to [mathematical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_education):

...gathered together from many examination papers to form a volume, which was considerably amplified in later editions, they [exercised](wikipedia://en/35557313) a very real influence upon successive generations of undergraduates; and "Wolstenholme's Problems" have proved a help and stimulus to many students.

In 1869 he resigned his fellowship to marry Térèse Kraus, his Swiss bride. He became a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Royal Indian Engineering College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Engineering_College) at Cooper's Hill, [Egham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egham), [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey) from 1871 to 1889. In 1878 he published an expanded version of *Mathematical Problems*, and in 1888 *Examples for Practice in the Use of Seven-figure Logarithms*.

He was a close friend of [Leslie Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Stephen) from his undergraduate studies at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge). [Virginia Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf) used his personality for the character Augustus Carmichael in her novel *[To the Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_the_Lighthouse)*. His sister was the [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist) [Elizabeth Clarke Wolstenholme Elmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Clarke_Wolstenholme_Elmy).[^o6]
