# Bartel Leendert van der Waerden

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bartel Leendert van der Waerden"}

van der Waerden Van der Waerden

**Bartel Leendert van der Waerden** (; 2 February 1903 – 12 January 1996) was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Education and early career {#Education_and_early_career}

Van der Waerden learned advanced mathematics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), from 1919 until 1926. He was much influenced by [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). Amsterdam awarded him a Ph.D. for a thesis on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), supervised by Hendrick de Vries. Göttingen awarded him the [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1928. In that year, at the age of 25, he accepted a professorship at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen).

At 27, Van der Waerden published his *[Moderne Algebra](wikipedia://en/37252648)*, an influential two-volume treatise on [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), still cited, and perhaps the first treatise to treat the subject as a comprehensive whole. This work systematized an ample body of research by [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896), [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106), and [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351). In the following year, 1931, he was appointed professor at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig).

In July 1929, he married Camilla Juliana Anna Rellich, the sister of mathematician [Franz Rellich](wikipedia://en/4172897). They had three children.

### Nazi Germany {#Nazi_Germany}

After the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) [seized power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung), and through [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Van der Waerden remained at Leipzig, and passed up opportunities to leave Nazi Germany for [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University). However, he was critical of the Nazis and refused to give up his Dutch nationality, both of which led to difficulties for him.[^o7]

### Postwar career {#Postwar_career}

Following the war, Van der Waerden was repatriated to the Netherlands rather than returning to [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) (then under Soviet control), but struggled to find a position in the Dutch academic system, in part because his time in Germany made his politics suspect and in part due to [Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitzen_Egbertus_Jan_Brouwer)'s opposition to Hilbert's school of mathematics. After a year visiting [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) and two years as a part-time professor, in 1950, Van der Waerden filled the chair in mathematics at the University of Amsterdam.[^o9] In 1951, he moved to the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich), where he spent the rest of his career, supervising more than 40 [Ph.D. students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy).

In 1949, Van der Waerden became member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), in 1951 this was changed to a foreign membership.[^o10] In 1973 he received the *[Pour le Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite)*.[^o11]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Van der Waerden is mainly remembered for his work on [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). He also wrote on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) (he and [Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg) had been colleagues at Leipzig). In later years, he turned to the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science). His historical writings include *Ontwakende wetenschap* (1950), which was translated into English as *Science Awakening* (1954),[^o13] *Sources of Quantum Mechanics* (1967),[^o14] *Geometry and Algebra in Ancient Civilizations* (1983), and *A History of Algebra* (1985).[^o15]

Van der Waerden has over 1000 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy), most of them through three of his students, [David van Dantzig](wikipedia://en/528853) (Ph.D. Groningen 1931), [Herbert Seifert](wikipedia://en/637024) (Ph.D. Leipzig 1932), and Hans Richter (Ph.D. Leipzig 1936, co-advised by [Paul Koebe](wikipedia://en/4313643)).[^o5]
