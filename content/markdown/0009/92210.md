# Len Sassaman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Len Sassaman"}

**Leonard Harris Sassaman** (April 9, 1980 – July 3, 2011) was an American technologist, [information privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_privacy) advocate, and the maintainer of the [Mixmaster anonymous remailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixmaster_anonymous_remailer) code and operator of the *randseed* [remailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remailer). Much of his career gravitated towards [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and protocol development.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sassaman graduated in 1998 from [The Hill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_School), [Pottstown, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottstown,_Pennsylvania). He was diagnosed with [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder) as a teenager.[^o4] By 18, he was on the [Internet Engineering Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Engineering_Task_Force) responsible for the TCP/IP protocol underlying the Internet. In 1999, Len moved to the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area), quickly became a regular in the [cypherpunk](wikipedia://en/37314) community, and moved in with [Bram Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bram_Cohen).

## Career {#Career}

Sassaman was employed as the security architect and senior systems engineer for [Anonymizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymizer_%28company%29). He was a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) candidate at the [Katholieke Universiteit Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven) in Belgium, as a researcher with the [Computer Security and Industrial Cryptography](wikipedia://en/8116603) (COSIC) research group, led by [Bart Preneel](wikipedia://en/1520490). [David Chaum](wikipedia://en/239365) and Bart Preneel were his advisors.

Sassaman was a well-known [cypherpunk](wikipedia://en/37314), cryptographer and privacy advocate. He worked for [Network Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_Associates) on the [PGP](wikipedia://en/23080) encryption software, was a member of the Shmoo Group, a contributor to the [OpenPGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenPGP) [IETF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IETF) [working group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_group), the [GNU Privacy Guard](wikipedia://en/38809) project, and frequently appeared at technology conferences like [DEF CON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEF_CON). Sassaman was the co-founder of [CodeCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CodeCon) along with [Bram Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bram_Cohen), co-founder of the HotPETS workshop (with [Roger Dingledine](wikipedia://en/48434704) of [Tor](wikipedia://en/20556944) and Thomas Heydt-Benjamin), co-author of the [Zimmermann–Sassaman key-signing protocol](wikipedia://en/424757), and at the age of 21, was an organizer of the protests following the arrest of Russian programmer [Dmitry Sklyarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Sklyarov).[^o6]

*Image: Len Sassaman 4.jpg.* Len slips a blue cable-tie ring on [Meredith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_L._Patterson)'s finger

On February 11, 2006, at the fifth CodeCon, Sassaman proposed to returning speaker and noted computer scientist [Meredith L. Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_L._Patterson) during the Q&A after her presentation, and they were married.[^o7] The couple worked together on several research collaborations, including a critique of privacy flaws in the [OLPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OLPC) [Bitfrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitfrost) security platform,[^o8] and a proposal of formal methods of analysis of computer insecurity in February 2011.[^o9]

Sassaman and Patterson founded the startup Osogato[^o10], aiming to commercialize Patterson's [Support Vector Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Support_Vector_Machine)-based "Query By Example" (later "OBELisQ"[^o11]) project. Sassaman and Patterson announced Osogato's first products, a music console application and a music analysis library, at [SuperHappyDevHouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperHappyDevHouse) #21 in San Francisco.[^o12]

In 2009, [Dan Kaminsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Kaminsky) presented joint work with Sassaman and Patterson at [Black Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hat_Briefings) in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley), showing multiple methods for attacking the [X.509](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X.509) [certificate authority](wikipedia://en/296370) infrastructure. Using these techniques, the team demonstrated how an attacker could obtain a certificate that clients would treat as valid for domains the attacker did not control.[^o13][^o14]

In 2021, developer Evan Hatch proposed Sassaman as a possible candidate to be [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto).[^o15] Sassaman had been mentioned on bitcointalk on 15 March 2013 when a user suggested Sassaman was Satoshi.[^o16] A presentation given by Kaminsky at the 2011 [Black Hat Briefings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hat_Briefings) revealed that a testimonial in honor of Sassaman had been permanently embedded into bitcoin's [blockchain](wikipedia://en/28249265#Blockchain).[^o17]

## Death {#Death}

*Image: LenSassaman-Bitcoin-Tribute.png.* Len memorial on the Bitcoin blockchain

Sassaman was reported dead on July 3, 2011.[^o19][^o20] Patterson stated on the same day after speaking to Belgian police that her husband's death was "unambiguously [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide)".[^o21][^o22] After he had stopped responding to [instant messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_messaging) from Patterson while she was in the United States, she asked a friend to fly to Belgium to check up on him and the friend "found him hanging in the closet".[^o23] He was laid to rest on July 9, 2011, at De Jacht Cemetery at [Heverlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heverlee) in Leuven, Belgium.[^o24]

An ASCII art tribute by Dan Kaminsky in honor of Len, to be permanently embedded into Bitcoin's blockchain, was first announced during "The Wake for Len Sassaman" held at the [DNA Lounge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_Lounge) in San Francisco on July 30, 2011. Kaminsky revealed it publicly during Black Hat USA 2011 at the Caesars Palace venue in Las Vegas.[^o25]
