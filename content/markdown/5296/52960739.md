# Alexander Its

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Its"}

**Alexander R. Its** is a Distinguished Professor of Mathematical Sciences at [Indiana University-Purdue University Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_–_Purdue_University_Indianapolis).[^o3] He completed his doctorate from [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University), then known as Leningrad University, in 1977. Afterwards, he continued his career as a lecturer at the [Steklov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute) in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) before becoming a professor at his alma mater. He remained there until 1993, when he assumed his current role at Indiana University. His research focuses on [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213), examining asymptotic analysis of matrix models using [Riemann–Hilbert](wikipedia://en/9710396) and [isomonodromy](wikipedia://en/16987249) methods, asymptotic analysis of correlation functions related to aspects of theoretical [Fredholm](wikipedia://en/279722) and [Toeplitz operators](wikipedia://en/14598730), and the theory of integrable nonlinear partial and ordinary [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) of [KdV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation) and [Painlevé](wikipedia://en/2033003) types.[^o3]

:::

Awards received by Its over the course of his career include the Prize of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9098251) (1976), the Prize of the [Leningrad Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Mathematical_Society) (1981), the Hardy Fellowship of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) (2002), the Batsheva de Rothschild Fellowship of the [Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) (2009),[^o4] and Fellowship in the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (2012).[^o5] In 2012, a conference on "Integrable Systems and Random Matrices" was held in his honor at the [Institut Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/10513595) in Paris.[^o6]
