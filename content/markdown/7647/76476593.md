# Paula Williamson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paula Williamson"}

**Paula Ruth Williamson** is a British medical statistician who specialises in medical statistics and the use of methodology within the field, particularly clinical trials methodology. A graduate from the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield), she originally worked as a senior statistician in healthcare before moving to the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), where she became Professor of Medical Statistics and served as head of the Department of Biostatistics from 2002 until 2018. She was elected [Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Academy_of_Medical_Sciences) in 2018.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Paula Ruth Williamson[^o4] was educated at the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield), where she got her Bachelor of Science degree with special honours in Probability and Statistics and eventually her [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Probability and Statistics.[^o5] Her doctoral dissertation was titled *Prognostic prediction within Cox's proportional hazards regression model*.[^o5]

She worked abroad as a senior research assistant at the [National Centre in HIV Epidemiology and Clinical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirby_Institute) in Australia from 1990 until 1991, and having worked as a statistical tutor while studying for her PhD in Sheffield, later continued her tutor work abroad at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney)'s Department of Mathematical Statistics in 1990.[^o5] She worked as a senior statistician at the Yorkshire Clinical Trials and Research Unit (1992-1994) and the Central Manchester Healthcare Trust Department of [Medical Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_genetics) (1994-1996).[^o5] She also worked as a geographer, during which she had some experience in statistics; she recalled this during a November 1999 meeting organised by the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664)'s Merseyside Local Group.[^o6]

In 1996, Williamson moved to the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool) Department of Mathematical Sciences and started as a lecturer, before being promoted to senior lecturer in 2000 and reader at the Centre for Medical Statistics and Health Evaluation in 2002.[^o5] In 2005, she was promoted to professor at the Department of Biostatistics,[^o5] where she served as head from 2002 to 2018.[^o8] She later moved to the University's Institute of [Population Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_health) and became Professor of Medical Statistics.[^o7] She has also held several positions in government institutions, including chair of the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28United_Kingdom%29)'s Network of Hubs for Trials Methodology Research (2008-2018) and [National Institute for Health and Care Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Health_and_Care_Research) Senior Investigator.[^o8] In 2021, she was elected to the Council of the [Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Medical_Sciences).[^o7]

As an academic, Williamson specialises in medical statistics and the use of methodology within the field, particularly clinical trials methodology.[^o10][^o9] In 2017, she was the speaker for the 26th [Bradford Hill Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Hill_Memorial_Lecture), "Improving health by improving trials: from outcomes to recruitment and back again".[^o9] She was elected [Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Academy_of_Medical_Sciences) in 2018.[^o10]
