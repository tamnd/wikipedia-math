# Asplund space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Asplund space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) — an **Asplund space** or **strong differentiability space** is a type of [well-behaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-behaved) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). Asplund spaces were introduced in 1968 by the [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Edgar Asplund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Asplund), who was interested in the [Fréchet differentiability](wikipedia://en/2735727) properties of [Lipschitz functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function) on Banach spaces.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Equivalent definitions"}

## Equivalent definitions {#Equivalent_definitions}

There are many equivalent definitions of what it means for a Banach space *X* to be an **Asplund space**:

- *X* is Asplund if and only if every [separable subspace](wikipedia://en/27855) *Y* of *X* has separable [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) *Y*<sup>∗</sup>.
- *X* is Asplund if and only if every [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) on any [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [convex subset](wikipedia://en/6292) *U* of *X* is Fréchet differentiable at the points of a [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) [*G*<sub>*δ*</sub>-subset](wikipedia://en/252076) of *U*.
- *X* is Asplund if and only if its dual space *X*<sup>∗</sup> has the [Radon–Nikodým property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Nikodym_property). This property was established by Namioka & Phelps in 1975 and Stegall in 1978.
- *X* is Asplund if and only if every non-empty [bounded subset](wikipedia://en/48258) of its dual space *X*<sup>∗</sup> has [weak-∗-slices](wikipedia://en/33662) of arbitrarily small diameter.
- *X* is Asplund if and only if every non-empty weakly-∗ [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) subset of the dual space *X*<sup>∗</sup> is the weakly-∗ closed [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of its weakly-∗ strongly [exposed points](wikipedia://en/45357429). In 1975, Huff & Morris showed that this property is equivalent to the statement that every bounded, closed and convex subset of the dual space *X*<sup>∗</sup> is closed convex hull of its extreme points.

:::

## Properties of Asplund spaces {#Properties_of_Asplund_spaces}

- The class of Asplund spaces is closed under topological isomorphisms: that is, if *X* and *Y* are Banach spaces, *X* is Asplund, and *X* is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to *Y*, then *Y* is also an Asplund space.
- Every [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of an Asplund space is an Asplund space.
- Every [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) of an Asplund space is an Asplund space.
- The class of Asplund spaces is closed under extensions: if *X* is a Banach space and *Y* is an Asplund subspace of *X* for which the quotient space *X* ⁄ *Y* is Asplund, then *X* is Asplund.
- Every locally Lipschitz function on an open subset of an Asplund space is Fréchet differentiable at the points of some dense subset of its domain. This result was established by [Preiss](wikipedia://en/19372849) in 1990 and has applications in optimization theory.
- The following theorem from Asplund's original 1968 paper is a good example of why non-Asplund spaces are badly behaved: if *X* is not an Asplund space, then there exists an equivalent norm on *X* that fails to be Fréchet differentiable at every point of *X*.
- In 1976, Ekeland & Lebourg showed that if *X* is a Banach space that has an equivalent norm that is Fréchet differentiable away from the origin, then *X* is an Asplund space. However, in 1990, Haydon gave an example of an Asplund space that does not have an equivalent norm that is [Gateaux differentiable](wikipedia://en/2253139) away from the origin.
