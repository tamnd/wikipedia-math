# Dorothy Maharam

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dorothy Maharam"}

**Dorothy Maharam Stone** (July 1, 1917[^o3] – September 27, 2014[^o4]) was an American mathematician born in [Parkersburg, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkersburg,_West_Virginia), who made important contributions to [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and became the namesake of [Maharam's theorem](wikipedia://en/18759825) and [Maharam algebra](wikipedia://en/43218839).[^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Maharam earned her B.S. degree at [Carnegie Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institute_of_Technology) in 1937 and her Ph.D. in 1940 under [Anna Johnson Pell Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Johnson_Pell_Wheeler) from [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College) with a dissertation entitled *On measure in abstract sets*. Part of her thesis was published in the [Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937). Then she went on to a postdoc at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey),[^o8] where she first met fellow mathematician [Arthur Harold Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Harold_Stone). They married in April 1942.[^o9]

Stone and Maharam both lectured at various universities in the US and the United Kingdom, including Northeastern University and University of Manchester,[^o10] and were faculty at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester) for many years. She was an invited speaker at a [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) conference at [Northern Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Illinois_University) in 1980.[^o11] Their two children, David and Ellen, both became mathematicians as well.[^o12]

She retired in 2001. Her husband, Arthur Stone, died August 6, 2000, and her son, David Stone, died August 27, 2014. She died a month later in [Brookline, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline,_Massachusetts) and was buried at Kehillath Jacob Cemetery in [West Roxbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Roxbury).[^o4]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Maharam pioneered the research of [finitely additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_additive) measures on integers. [Maharam's theorem](wikipedia://en/18759825) about the decomposability of complete measure spaces plays an important role in the theory of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). Maharam published it in the *[Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_United_States_of_America)* in 1942. Another paper of Maharam, in 1947 in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*, introduced [Maharam algebras](wikipedia://en/43218839), which are complete Boolean algebras with continuous submeasures.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Maharam was named a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1976.[^o15]
