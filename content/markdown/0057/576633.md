# Nicholas Rescher

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Nicholas Rescher"}

**Nicholas Rescher** (; ; 15 July 1928 – 5 January 2024) was a German-born American philosopher and author who was a professor of philosophy at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) from 1961. He was chairman of the [Center for Philosophy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Philosophy_of_Science) and chairman of the philosophy department.[^o14]

:::

Rescher served as president of the [American Catholic Philosophical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Catholic_Philosophical_Association), [Leibniz Society of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_Society_of_North_America), [American Metaphysical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Metaphysical_Society), [American Philosophical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Association), and [Charles S. Peirce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_S._Peirce) Society.[^o9] He was the founder of *[American Philosophical Quarterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Quarterly)*,[^o5] *[History of Philosophy Quarterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Philosophy_Quarterly)*, and *[Public Affairs Quarterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Affairs_Quarterly)*.[^o6] He died in Pittsburgh on January 5, 2024, at the age of 95.[^o7]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rescher was born in [Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen) in the [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia) region of Germany.[^o9] In his autobiography he traces his descent to Nehemias Rescher (1735–1801), a founder of the Hochberg-Remseck Jewish community in Swabian Germany.[^o10] He relocated to the United States when he was 10 and became a naturalized United States citizen in 1944.[^o11] In 1949 he obtained a degree in mathematics at [Queens College, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_College,_New_York),[^o12][^o13] thereafter attending [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and graduating with a Ph.D. in philosophy in 1951 at the age of 22, the youngest person to earn a Ph.D. in that department.[^o14][^o9] From 1952 to 1954 during the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War) he served a term in the [United States Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps), and then from 1954 to 1957 he worked for the [Rand Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Corporation)'s mathematics division.[^o12] After a time at [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University), he taught philosophy at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) starting in 1961. The orientalist [Oskar Rescher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Rescher) is the first cousin of his father.

## Career {#Career}

Rescher began his career as an academic at Princeton University in 1951.[^o12][^o16] He joined the philosophy department at the University of Pittsburgh in 1961, becoming the first associate director of its new Center for Philosophy of Science the following year.[^o17] In 1964, he founded the *American Philosophical Quarterly*.[^o18] From 1980 to 1981, Rescher served as the chairman of the philosophy department.[^o12] In July 1988, Rescher changed roles at the Center for Philosophy of Science, resigning as its director and becoming its chairman.[^o19] In 2010, he donated his philosophy collection to the [Hillman Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillman_Library).[^o12]

An honorary member of [Corpus Christi College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Oxford), he was elected to membership in the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the [Royal Asiatic Society of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Asiatic_Society_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland), the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea), the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada), and the Institut International de Philosophie, among others.[^o14]

Rescher was a prolific writer, with over 100 books and 400 articles, generating the jest that Rescher is not a single person, but a committee sharing the name.[^o9][^o5][^o20] Philosopher [Michele Marsonet](wikipedia://en/32526581), who has published extensively on Rescher's philosophy, writes that his prolific publication is in itself the most common objection against Rescher, adding "it is, indeed, a leitmotiv of all those unwilling to discuss his ideas".[^o20]

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

Rescher's university biography describes his philosophical work thus:[^o14]

> His work envisions a dialectical tension between our synoptic aspirations for useful knowledge and our human limitations as finite inquirers. The elaboration of this project represents a many-sided approach to fundamental philosophical issues that weaves together threads of thought from the philosophy of science, and from continental idealism and American pragmatism.

He is known for his system of [pragmatic idealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatic_idealism), which synthesizes [British idealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_idealism) with the [pragmatism](wikipedia://en/95170) of the U.S.[^o23] The original term he used for his system was **conceptual idealism**, an attempt to renew the idealist approach to knowledge in the domain of modern science.[^o24]

In the mid and late 1960s, his studies were focused on medieval Arabic logic, but he soon broadened his areas of inquiry in [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), moving toward the [methodological pragmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodological_pragmatism) he would define.[^o25] In the 1970s, he began working more extensively with American pragmatism with a focus on the writings of [C. S. Peirce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._S._Peirce), who was to number among his major influences.[^o26] In 1966, Rescher collaborated with [Herbert A. Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_A._Simon) on a ground-breaking paper on the theory of causality.[^o27]

Rescher had contributed to [futuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futuristics), and with [Olaf Helmer](wikipedia://en/22911700)[^o28] and Norman Dalkey, invented the [Delphi method](wikipedia://en/337523) of forecasting.[^o14] A lifelong aficionado of the philosophy of G. W. Leibniz, Rescher has been instrumental in the reconstruction of Leibniz's *machina deciphratoria*, an ancestor of the famous Enigma cipher machine. Rescher was also responsible for two further items of historical rediscovery and reconstruction: the model of cosmic evolution in Anaximander,[^o29] and the medieval Islamic theory of modal syllogistic.[^o30]

:::

## Honors {#Honors}

Rescher was honored for his work. In 1984, he received the [Humboldt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize) for Humanistic Scholarship.[^o9] In 2005, he received the [Cardinal Mercier Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Mercier_Prize), and in 2007 the American Catholic Philosophical Society's Aquinas Medal. In 2011, his contributions as a German-American to philosophy were recognized with the premier cross of the [Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany), the Founder's Medal of the American Metaphysical Society (2016), and the Helmholtz Medal of the German Academy of Sciences [Berlin-Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Brandenburg).[^o14] He holds eight honorary degrees. Having held visiting lectureships at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), the [University of Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Konstanz), the [University of Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Salamanca), [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich), and [Marburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg_University), he has been awarded fellowships by the [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation), [Guggenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship), and [National Science Foundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation).[^o14] In April 2021, the [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran) held a session in his honor where [Nadia Maftouni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Maftouni)[^o32] asserted:

> Rescher's *A Journey through Philosophy in 101 Anecdotes* is a successful framework to reach a broader audience in the field. At first glance it seems an easy book to write. But at least in philosophy, it's easy to write in a complicated style and it's hard to write in a simple, clear, and readable fashion.[^o33]

## Prize and medal {#Prize_and_medal}

In 2010, the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) created the Dr. Nicholas Rescher Fund for the Advancement of the Department of Philosophy which bestows the Nicholas Rescher Prize for Contributions to Systematic Philosophy.[^o12] The first recipient of the prize was Rescher's former student, [Ernest Sosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Sosa). As of 2012, the prize included a gold medal and \$25,000, subsequently raised to \$30,000. Later awardees have included [Alvin Plantinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Plantinga), [Jürgen Mittelstraß](wikipedia://en/3773510), [Hilary Putnam](wikipedia://en/75233), [Ruth Millikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Millikan), and [Thomas Nagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Nagel).[^o35] When the American Philosophical Association inaugurated its own [Rescher Prize for Systematic Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Rescher_Prize_for_Systematic_Philosophy) in 2018, the University of Pittsburgh redesignated its award as the Rescher Medal.
