# Image (mathematics)

*Image: FunctionMappingPersonToFavoriteFood.svg.* For the function that maps a Person to their Favorite Food, the image of Gabriela is Apple. The preimage of Apple is the set {Gabriela, Maryam}. The preimage of Fish is the empty set. The image of the subset {Richard, Maryam} is {Rice, Apple}. The preimage of {Rice, Apple} is {Gabriela, Richard, Maryam}.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Image"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **image** of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f: X \to Y$ is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all $f(x)$ such that $x$ belongs to the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of $f$. The *image* by $f$ of an element $x$ of the domain of $f$ is $f(x)$, that is, the output corresponding to the input $x$. The *image by* $f$ of a subset $S$ of the domain of $f$ is the set of all $f(x)$ such that $x$ is in $S$, that is, the set of the images of the elements of $S$. Equivalently, it is the image of the [restriction](wikipedia://en/1589032) of $f$ to $S$.

:::

**Preimages** or **inverse images** are defined similarly, by exchanging the roles of the domain and the **[codomain](wikipedia://en/50264)**:

The *preimage* of an element $y$ of the codomain of $f$ is the set of all elements $x$ of the domain of $f$ such that $f(x)=y$; it is [empty](wikipedia://en/9566) if $y$ does not belong to the image of $f$. The preimage of a subset $T$ of the codomain of $f$ is the set of all elements $x$ of the domain of $f$ such that $f(x)\in T$. The preimage of the codomain of $f$ is, by definition of a function, the domain of $f$.

Images and inverse images may also be defined similarly for general [binary relations](wikipedia://en/3931#Operations). in this generalization, images and preimages play symmetric roles: the images and the preimages of a relation are respectively the preimages and the images of the [opposite relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_relation).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: ImagePreimageOfElement.png.* $f$ is a function from domain $X$ to codomain $Y$. The image of element $x$ is element $y$. The preimage of element $y$ is the set {$x, x'$}. The preimage of element $y'$ is $\varnothing$.

*Image: ImagePreimageofaSet.png.* $f$ is a function from domain $X$ to codomain $Y$. The image of all elements in subset $A$ is subset $B$. The preimage of $B$ is subset $C$

*Image: Codomain2.SVG.* $f$ is a function from domain $X$ to codomain $Y$. The yellow oval inside $Y$ is the image of $f$. The preimage of $Y$ is the entire domain $X$

The word "image" is used in three related ways. In these definitions, $f : X \to Y$ is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) from the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $X$ to the set $Y$.

### Image of an element {#Image_of_an_element}

If $x$ is a member of $X$, then the image of $x$ under $f$, denoted $f(x)$, is the [value](wikipedia://en/4095147) of $f$ when applied to $x$. $f(x)$ is alternatively known as the output of $f$ for argument $x$.

Given $y$, the function $f$ is said to take the value $y$ or take $y$ as a value if there exists some $x$ in the function's domain such that $f(x) = y$. Similarly, given a set $S,$ $f$ is said to take a value in $S$ if there exists some $x$ in the function's domain such that $f(x) \in S$. However, $f$ takes \[all\] values in $S$ and $f$ is valued in $S$ means that $f(x) \in S$ for every point $x$ in the domain of $f$.

### Image of a subset {#Image_of_a_subset}

Throughout, let $f : X \to Y$ be a function. The image under $f$ of a subset $A$ of $X$ is the set of all $f(a)$ for $a\in A$. It is denoted by $f[A]$, or by $f(A)$ when there is no risk of confusion. Using [set-builder notation](wikipedia://en/220089), this definition can be written as[^o6][^o7]

$$f[A] = \{f(a) : a \in A\}$$

This induces a function $f[\,\cdot\,] : \mathcal P(X) \to \mathcal P(Y)$, where $\mathcal P(S)$ denotes the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of a set $S$; that is the set of all [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of $S$. See below for more.

### Image of a function {#Image_of_a_function}

The *image* of a function is the image of its entire [domain](wikipedia://en/50263), also known as the [range](wikipedia://en/275991) of the function.[^o9] This last usage should be avoided because the word "range" is also commonly used to mean the [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) of $f$.

### Generalization to binary relations {#Generalization_to_binary_relations}

If $R$ is an arbitrary [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) on $X \times Y$, then the set $\{ y \in Y : x R y \text{ for some } x \in X \}$ is called the image, or the range, of $R$. Dually, the set $\{ x \in X : x R y \text{ for some } y \in Y \}$ is called the domain of $R$.

:::

::: {#o12 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Inverse image"}

## Inverse image {#Inverse_image}

Let $f$ be a function from $X$ to $Y.$ The **preimage** or **inverse image** of a set $B \subseteq Y$ under $f,$ denoted by $f^{-1}[B],$ is the subset of $X$ defined by

$$f^{-1}[ B ] = \{ x \in X \,:\, f(x) \in B \}.$$

Other notations include $f^{-1}(B)$ and $f^{-}(B).$[^n1] The inverse image of a [singleton set](wikipedia://en/381798), denoted by $f^{-1}[\{ y \}]$ or by $f^{-1}(y),$ is also called the [fiber](wikipedia://en/6603892) (or fibre) over $y$, or the [level set](wikipedia://en/559622) of $y.$ The set of all the fibers over the elements of $Y$ is a [family of sets](wikipedia://en/723043) indexed by $Y.$

For example, for the function $f(x) = x^2,$ the inverse image of $\{ 4 \}$ would be $\{ -2, 2 \}.$ Again, if there is no risk of confusion, $f^{-1}[B]$ can be denoted by $f^{-1}(B),$ and $f^{-1}$ can also be thought of as a function from the power set of $Y$ to the power set of $X.$ The notation $f^{-1}$ should not be confused with that for [inverse function](wikipedia://en/14907), although it coincides with the usual one for bijections in that the inverse image of $B$ under $f$ is the image of $B$ under $f^{-1}.$

:::

## Notation for image and inverse image {#Notation_for_image_and_inverse_image}

The traditional notations used in the previous section do not distinguish the original function $f : X \to Y$ from the image-of-sets function $f : \mathcal{P}(X) \to \mathcal{P}(Y)$; likewise they do not distinguish the inverse function (assuming one exists) from the inverse image function (which again relates the powersets). Given the right context, this keeps the notation light and usually does not cause confusion. But if needed, an alternative[^n2] is to give explicit names for the image and preimage as functions between power sets:

### Arrow notation {#Arrow_notation}

- $f^\rightarrow : \mathcal{P}(X) \to \mathcal{P}(Y)$ with $f^\rightarrow(A) = \{ f(a)\;|\; a \in A\}$
- $f^\leftarrow : \mathcal{P}(Y) \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ with $f^\leftarrow(B) = \{ a \in X \;|\; f(a) \in B\}$

### Star notation {#Star_notation}

- $f_\star : \mathcal{P}(X) \to \mathcal{P}(Y)$ instead of $f^\rightarrow$
- $f^\star : \mathcal{P}(Y) \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ instead of $f^\leftarrow$

### Other terminology {#Other_terminology}

- An alternative notation for $f[A]$ used in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) is $f\,''A.$[^o19][^o20]
- Some texts refer to the image of $f$ as the range of $f,$[^o21] but this usage should be avoided because the word "range" is also commonly used to mean the [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) of $f.$

::: {#o23 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

1. $f : \{ 1, 2, 3 \} \to \{ a, b, c, d \}$ defined by $\left\{\begin{matrix} 1 \mapsto a, \\ 2 \mapsto a, \\ 3 \mapsto c. \end{matrix}\right.$ The *image* of the set $\{ 2, 3 \}$ under $f$ is $f(\{ 2, 3 \}) = \{ a, c \}.$ The *image* of the function $f$ is $\{ a, c \}.$ The *preimage* of $a$ is $f^{-1}(\{ a \}) = \{ 1, 2 \}.$ The *preimage* of $\{ a, b \}$ is also $f^{-1}(\{ a, b \}) = \{ 1, 2 \}.$ The *preimage* of $\{ b, d \}$ under $f$ is the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) $\{ \ \} = \emptyset.$
1. $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by $f(x) = x^2.$ The *image* of $\{ -2, 3 \}$ under $f$ is $f(\{ -2, 3 \}) = \{ 4, 9 \},$ and the *image* of $f$ is $\mathbb{R}^+$ (the set of all [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571) and zero). The *preimage* of $\{ 4, 9 \}$ under $f$ is $f^{-1}(\{ 4, 9 \}) = \{ -3, -2, 2, 3 \}.$ The *preimage* of set $N = \{ n \in \mathbb{R} : n < 0 \}$ under $f$ is the empty set, because the negative numbers do not have square roots in the set of reals.
1. $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2.$ The [*fibers*](wikipedia://en/6603892) $f^{-1}(\{ a \})$ are [concentric circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric_circles) about the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432), the origin itself, and the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) (respectively), depending on whether $a > 0, \ a = 0, \text{ or } \ a < 0$ (respectively). (If $a \ge 0,$ then the *fiber* $f^{-1}(\{ a \})$ is the set of all $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ satisfying the equation $x^2 + y^2 = a,$ that is, the origin-centered circle with radius $\sqrt{a}.$)
1. If $M$ is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) and $\pi : TM \to M$ is the canonical [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) from the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) $TM$ to $M,$ then the *fibers* of $\pi$ are the [tangent spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_spaces) $T_x(M) \text{ for } x \in M.$ This is also an example of a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526).
1. A [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) is a homomorphic *image*.

:::

### General {#General}

For every function $f : X \to Y$ and all subsets $A \subseteq X$ and $B \subseteq Y,$ the following properties hold:

::: {#o28 .table cue=table}

| Image | Preimage |
|---|---|
| $f(X) \subseteq Y$ | $f^{-1}(Y) = X$ |
| $f\left(f^{-1}(Y)\right) = f(X)$ | $f^{-1}(f(X)) = X$ |
| $f\left(f^{-1}(B)\right) \subseteq B$ (equal if $B \subseteq f(X);$ for instance, if $f$ is surjective)[^o29][^o30] | $f^{-1}(f(A)) \supseteq A$ (equal if $f$ is injective)[^o29][^o30] |
| $f(f^{-1}(B)) = B \cap f(X)$ | $\left(f \vert_A\right)^{-1}(B) = A \cap f^{-1}(B)$ |
| $f\left(f^{-1}(f(A))\right) = f(A)$ | $f^{-1}\left(f\left(f^{-1}(B)\right)\right) = f^{-1}(B)$ |
| $f(A) = \varnothing \,\text{ if and only if }\, A = \varnothing$ | $f^{-1}(B) = \varnothing \,\text{ if and only if }\, B \subseteq Y \setminus f(X)$ |
| $f(A) \supseteq B \,\text{ if and only if } \text{ there exists } C \subseteq A \text{ such that } f(C) = B$ | $f^{-1}(B) \supseteq A \,\text{ if and only if }\, f(A) \subseteq B$ |
| $f(A) \supseteq f(X \setminus A) \,\text{ if and only if }\, f(A) = f(X)$ | $f^{-1}(B) \supseteq f^{-1}(Y \setminus B) \,\text{ if and only if }\, f^{-1}(B) = X$ |
| $f(X \setminus A) \supseteq f(X) \setminus f(A)$ | $f^{-1}(Y \setminus B) = X \setminus f^{-1}(B)$[^o29] |
| $f\left(A \cup f^{-1}(B)\right) \subseteq f(A) \cup B$[^o31] | $f^{-1}(f(A) \cup B) \supseteq A \cup f^{-1}(B)$[^o31] |
| $f\left(A \cap f^{-1}(B)\right) = f(A) \cap B$[^o31] | $f^{-1}(f(A) \cap B) \supseteq A \cap f^{-1}(B)$[^o31] |

:::

Also:

- $$f(A) \cap B = \varnothing \,\text{ if and only if }\, A \cap f^{-1}(B) = \varnothing$$

### Multiple functions {#Multiple_functions}

For functions $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to Z$ with subsets $A \subseteq X$ and $C \subseteq Z,$ the following properties hold:

- $$(g \circ f)(A) = g(f(A))$$
- $$(g \circ f)^{-1}(C) = f^{-1}(g^{-1}(C))$$

### Multiple subsets of domain or codomain {#Multiple_subsets_of_domain_or_codomain}

For function $f : X \to Y$ and subsets $A, B \subseteq X$ and $S, T \subseteq Y,$ the following properties hold:

::: {#o34 .table cue=table}

| Image | Preimage |
|---|---|
| $A \subseteq B \,\text{ implies }\, f(A) \subseteq f(B)$ | $S \subseteq T \,\text{ implies }\, f^{-1}(S) \subseteq f^{-1}(T)$ |
| $f(A \cup B) = f(A) \cup f(B)$[^o31][^o35] | $f^{-1}(S \cup T) = f^{-1}(S) \cup f^{-1}(T)$ |
| $f(A \cap B) \subseteq f(A) \cap f(B)$[^o31][^o35] (equal if $f$ is injective[^o36]) | $f^{-1}(S \cap T) = f^{-1}(S) \cap f^{-1}(T)$ |
| $f(A \setminus B) \supseteq f(A) \setminus f(B)$[^o31] (equal if $f$ is injective[^o36]) | $f^{-1}(S \setminus T) = f^{-1}(S) \setminus f^{-1}(T)$[^o31] |
| $f\left(A \triangle B\right) \supseteq f(A) \triangle f(B)$ (equal if $f$ is injective) | $f^{-1}\left(S \triangle T\right) = f^{-1}(S) \triangle f^{-1}(T)$ |

:::

The results relating images and preimages to the ([Boolean](wikipedia://en/3959)) algebra of [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) and [union](wikipedia://en/47949) work for any collection of subsets, not just for pairs of subsets:

- $$f\left(\bigcup_{s\in S}A_s\right) = \bigcup_{s\in S} f\left(A_s\right)$$
- $$f\left(\bigcap_{s\in S}A_s\right) \subseteq \bigcap_{s\in S} f\left(A_s\right)$$
- $$f^{-1}\left(\bigcup_{s\in S}B_s\right) = \bigcup_{s\in S} f^{-1}\left(B_s\right)$$
- $$f^{-1}\left(\bigcap_{s\in S}B_s\right) = \bigcap_{s\in S} f^{-1}\left(B_s\right)$$

(Here, $S$ can be infinite, even [uncountably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountably_infinite).)

With respect to the algebra of subsets described above, the inverse image function is a [lattice homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_homomorphism), while the image function is only a [semilattice](wikipedia://en/755647) homomorphism (that is, it does not always preserve intersections).

:::
