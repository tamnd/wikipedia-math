# Jean-Yves Girard

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Yves Girard"}

**Jean-Yves Girard** (; born 1947) is a French [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) working in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478). He is a research director ([emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus)) at the mathematical institute of [University of Aix-Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aix-Marseille), at [Luminy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculte_des_Sciences_de_Luminy).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jean-Yves Girard is an alumnus of the [École normale supérieure de Saint-Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_Saint-Cloud).

He made a name for himself in the 1970s with his proof of [strong normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_normalization) in a system of [second-order logic](wikipedia://en/341428) called [System F](wikipedia://en/767637). This result gave a new proof of [Takeuti's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2372007), which was proven a few years earlier by [William W. Tait](wikipedia://en/17002765), Motō Takahashi and [Dag Prawitz](wikipedia://en/911318). For this purpose, he introduced the notion of "reducibility candidate" ("candidat de réductibilité"). He is also credited with the discovery of [Girard's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girard's_paradox), [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675), the [geometry of interaction](wikipedia://en/8561592), [ludics](wikipedia://en/902801), and (satirically) the mustard watch.[^o4]

He obtained the [CNRS Silver Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Silver_Medal) in 1983 and is a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).
