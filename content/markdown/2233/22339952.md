# Polyominoid

*Image: Polyominoids.svg.* The polyominoids for *n* = 1 through *n* = 3

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polyominoid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **polyominoid** (or **minoid** for short) is a set of equal [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) in [3D](wikipedia://en/3054853) space, joined edge to edge at 90- or 180-degree angles. The polyominoids include the [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634), which are just the planar polyominoids. The surface of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is an example of a *hexominoid,* or 6-cell polyominoid, and many other [polycubes](wikipedia://en/710426) have polyominoids as their boundaries. Polyominoids appear to have been first proposed by [Richard A. Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Arnold_Epstein).[^o2]

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

90-degree connections are called *hard*; 180-degree connections are called *soft*. This is because, in manufacturing a model of the polyominoid, a hard connection would be easier to realize than a soft one.[^o4] Polyominoids may be classified as *hard* if every junction includes a 90° connection, *soft* if every connection is 180°, and *mixed* otherwise, except in the unique case of the monominoid, which has no connections of either kind. The set of soft polyominoids is equal to the set of [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634).

As with other [polyforms](wikipedia://en/691765), two polyominoids that are mirror images may be distinguished. *One-sided* polyominoids distinguish mirror images; *free* polyominoids do not.

## Generalization to higher dimensions {#Generalization_to_higher_dimensions}

In general one can define an *n,k-polyominoid* as a [polyform](wikipedia://en/691765) made by joining *k*-dimensional hypercubes at 90° or 180° angles in *n*-dimensional space, where 1≤*k*≤*n*.

- [Polysticks](wikipedia://en/710384) are 2,1-polyominoids.
- [Polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634) are 2,2-polyominoids.
- The polyforms described above are 3,2-polyominoids.
- [Polycubes](wikipedia://en/710426) are 3,3-polyominoids.
