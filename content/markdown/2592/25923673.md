# Small complex icosidodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Small complex icosidodecahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **small complex icosidodecahedron** is a degenerate [uniform star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8464397). Its edges are doubled, making it degenerate. The star has 32 faces (20 [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) and 12 [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014)), 60 (doubled) edges and 12 vertices and 4 sharing faces. The faces in it are considered as two overlapping edges as topological polyhedron.

:::

A small complex icosidodecahedron can be [constructed](wikipedia://en/2950777) from a number of different [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719).

A very similar figure emerges as a geometrical truncation of the [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878), where the pentagram faces become doubly-wound pentagons (t{5/2} = {10/2}), making the internal pentagonal planes, and the three meeting at each vertex become triangles, making the external triangular planes.

## As a compound {#As_a_compound}

The small complex icosidodecahedron can be seen as a [compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedron_compound) of the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) {3,5} and the [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441) {5,5/2} where all vertices are precise and edges coincide. The small complex icosidodecahedron resembles an icosahedron, because the great dodecahedron is completely contained inside the icosahedron.

Its two-dimensional analogue would be the compound of a regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014), {5}, representing the icosahedron as the *n*-dimensional [pentagonal polytope](wikipedia://en/33020517), and regular [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544), {5/2}, as the *n*-dimensional star. These shapes would share vertices, similarly to how its 3D equivalent shares edges.
