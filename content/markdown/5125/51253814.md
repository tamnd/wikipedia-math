# Roland P. Falkner

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Roland P. Falkner"}

**Roland Post Falkner** (April 14, 1866 – November 27, 1940) was an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Robert P. Falkner was born in [Bridgeport, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport,_Connecticut) on April 14, 1866.[^o5]

He graduated from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (Wharton School of Finance and Economy) in 1885; studied economics at [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin), [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) and [Halle-on-Saale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle), Germany; studied at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France); was instructor in accounting and statistics in the University of Pennsylvania in 1888–91, and professor of statistics 1891–1900.[^o3]

He served also as statistician of the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate) Committee of Finance in 1891; as secretary of the United States delegation to the [International Monetary Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Monetary_Conference_%281892%29) in [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels); and as secretary of that conference in 1892.[^o6] He left the University of Pennsylvania in 1900 for the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress), where he was chief of the division of documents.[^o3] From 1904 to 1907 he served as commissioner of education in [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico); from 1908 to 1911 he was statistician in charge of school inquiries for the [United States Immigration Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Immigration_Commission), and in 1911-12 was assistant director of the [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census).[^o6] From 1915 until 1926 he was associated with the [Alexander Hamilton Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton_Institute), and from 1926 onward with the [National Industrial Conference Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Industrial_Conference_Board).[^o3]

In 1894 he was elected to the International Institute of Statistics.[^o3] In 1920 he was elected as a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988).[^o7]

He died at [Memorial Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Hospital_%28New_York_City,_New_York%29) in New York on November 27, 1940.[^o8]

## Works {#Works}

He is author of numerous essays on criminology, sociology, etc. to economic, statistical and other journals; and was editor of *Annals* of the [American Academy of Political and Social Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Political_and_Social_Science) 1890–1900.[^o6] He did a notable study of prices and wages in the United States for the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate). It was published as "Retail Prices and Wages" (1892) and "Wholesale Prices, Wages, and Transportation" (1893).[^o3][^o10] He published a translation of [August Meitzen](wikipedia://en/1880598)'s *History, Theory, and Technique of Statistics* (1893).[^o6][^o11]
