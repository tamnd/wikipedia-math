# Nauru graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nauru graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Nauru graph** is a [symmetric](wikipedia://en/1577896), [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431), [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) with 24 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 36 edges. It was named by [David Eppstein](wikipedia://en/4015871) after the twelve-pointed star in the [flag of Nauru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Nauru).[^o9]

:::

It has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 2, [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3, [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) 4, radius 4 and [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 6.[^o3] It is also a 3-[vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) and 3-[edge-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph) graph. It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o4]

The Nauru graph requires at least eight crossings in any drawing of it in the plane. It is one of three non-[isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) graphs tied for being the smallest cubic graph that requires eight crossings. Another of these three graphs is the [McGee graph](wikipedia://en/23714466), also known as the (3-7)-[cage](wikipedia://en/7735022).[^o5][^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The Nauru graph is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) and can be described by the [LCF notation](wikipedia://en/24186720) : \[5, −9, 7, −7, 9, −5\]<sup>4</sup>.[^o9]

The Nauru graph can also be constructed as the [generalized Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/23716451) *G*(12, 5) which is formed by the vertices of a [dodecagon](wikipedia://en/508486) connected to the vertices of a twelve-point star in which each point of the star is connected to the points five steps away from it.

There is also a combinatorial construction of the Nauru graph. Take three distinguishable objects and place them in four distinguishable boxes, no more than one object per box. There are 24 ways to so distribute the objects, corresponding to the 24 vertices of the graph. If it is possible to go from one state to another state by moving exactly one object from its present location to an empty box, then the vertices corresponding to the two states are joined by an edge. The resulting state-transition graph is the Nauru graph. In other words, it is the [arrangement graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrangement_graph) $A_{4,3}$.

:::

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The automorphism group of the Nauru graph is a group of order 144.[^o12] It is isomorphic to the [direct product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_direct_product) of the [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>4</sub> and *S*<sub>3</sub> and acts transitively on the vertices, on the edges and on the arcs of the graph. Therefore, the Nauru graph is a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896) (though not [distance transitive](wikipedia://en/7768222)). It has automorphisms that take any vertex to any other vertex and any edge to any other edge. According to the *Foster census*, the Nauru graph is the only cubic symmetric graph on 24 vertices.[^o3]

The generalized Petersen graph *G*(*n,k*) is vertex-transitive if and only if *n* = 10 and *k* =2 or if *k*<sup>2</sup> ≡ ±1 (mod *n*) and is edge-transitive only in the following seven cases: (*n,k*) = (4,1), (5,2), (8,3), (10,2), (10,3), (12,5), (24,5).[^o13] So the Nauru graph is one of only seven symmetric Generalized Petersen graphs. Among these seven graphs are the [cubical graph](wikipedia://en/6285) $G(4,1)$, the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) $G(5,2)$, the [Möbius–Kantor graph](wikipedia://en/13396591) $G(8,3)$, the [dodecahedral graph](wikipedia://en/8407) $G(10,2)$ and the [Desargues graph](wikipedia://en/1578059) $G(10,3)$.

The Nauru graph is a [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of *S*<sub>4</sub>, the symmetric group of permutations on four elements, generated by the three different ways of swapping the first element with one of the three others : (1 2), (1 3) and (1 4).

Using the signed permutations of (1,1,3) as vertices, the Nauru graph is obtained by joining pairs at Euclidean distance 6.

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Nauru graph is equal to

$$(x-3) (x-2)^6 (x-1)^3 x^4 (x+1)^3 (x+2)^6 (x+3),\$$

making it an [integral graph](wikipedia://en/24845923)—a graph whose [spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914) consists entirely of integers.

:::

## Topological properties {#Topological_properties}

*Image: Nauru graph 3d.png.* A symmetric embedding of the Nauru graph on a genus-4 surface, with six dodecagonal faces.

The Nauru graph has two different [embeddings](wikipedia://en/8149170) as a [generalized regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/21533914): a topological surface partitioned into edges, vertices, and faces in such a way that there is a symmetry taking any [flag](wikipedia://en/9352497) (an incident triple of a vertex, edge, and face) into any other flag.[^o15]

One of these two embeddings forms a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800), so the Nauru graph is a [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057): it consists of 12 hexagonal faces together with the 24 vertices and 36 edges of the Nauru graph. The [dual graph](wikipedia://en/2536864) of this embedding is a symmetric 6-regular graph with 12 vertices and 36 edges.

The other symmetric embedding of the Nauru graph has six [dodecagonal](wikipedia://en/508486) faces, and forms a surface of [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_%28topology%29) 4. Its dual is not a [simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph), since each face shares three edges with four other faces, but a [multigraph](wikipedia://en/1774970). This dual can be formed from the graph of a regular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) by replacing each edge with a bundle of three parallel edges.

The set of faces of any one of these two embeddings is the set of [Petrie polygons](wikipedia://en/18571869) of the other embedding.

## Geometric properties {#Geometric_properties}

*Image: Nauru unit distance.svg.* The Nauru graph as a unit distance graph, from (Žitnik Horvat Pisanski 2010).

Like all generalized Petersen graphs, the Nauru graph can be represented by points in the plane in such a way that adjacent vertices are at unit distance apart; that is, it is a [unit distance graph](wikipedia://en/7158665).[^o18] It and the prisms are the only generalized Petersen graphs *G*(*n*,*p*) that cannot be so represented in such a way that the symmetries of the drawing form a cyclic group of order *n*. Instead, its unit distance graph representation has the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) Dih<sub>6</sub> as its symmetry group.

## History {#History}

The first person to write about the Nauru graph was [R. M. Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._M._Foster), in an effort to collect all the cubic symmetric graphs.[^o20] The whole list of cubic symmetric graphs is now named after him the *[Foster Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_Census)* and inside this list the Nauru graph is numbered graph F24A but has no specific name.[^o21] In 1950, [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) cited the graph a second time, giving the Hamiltonian representation used to illustrate this article and describing it as the [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of a [projective configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_configuration) discovered by Zacharias.[^o22][^o23]

In 2003, [Ed Pegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Pegg,_Jr.) wrote in his online MAA column that F24A deserves a name but did not propose one.[^o24] Finally, in 2007, David Eppstein used the name *Nauru graph* because the [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Nauru) of the [Republic of Nauru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Nauru) has a 12-point star similar to the one that appears in the construction of the graph as a generalized Petersen graph.[^o9]
