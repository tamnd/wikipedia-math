# List of countries by wealth per adult

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of countries by wealth per adult"}

This is a **list of countries of the world by wealth per adult**, from [UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS)'s *Global Wealth Databook.*[^o3] [Wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth) includes both financial and non-financial assets.

:::

[UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS) publishes various statistics relevant for calculating net wealth. These figures are influenced by real estate prices, equity market prices, exchange rates, liabilities, debts, adult percentage of the population, [human resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_resources), [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resources) and [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_%28economics%29) and technological advancements, which may create new assets or render others worthless in the future.

In nations where wealth is highly concentrated in a small percentage of people (a higher [Gini](wikipedia://en/12883) % in the tables below), the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) (obtained by dividing the total aggregate wealth by the number of adults) can be much higher than the [median](wikipedia://en/18837) (the amount that divides the population into two equal groups). From [Investopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investopedia): "Wealth distribution often results in higher Gini coefficients compared to income distribution, reflecting more pronounced inequality in wealth even in affluent countries."[^o4]

### By country {#By_country}

**Gini:** Higher [Gini coefficients](wikipedia://en/12883) signify greater inequality in wealth distribution. A Gini coefficient of 0 reflects perfect wealth equality, where all wealth values are the same, while a Gini coefficient of 1 (or 100%) reflects maximal wealth inequality, a situation where a single individual has all the wealth while all others have none.
