# Ole Barndorff-Nielsen

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Ole Barndorff-Nielsen"}

**Ole Eiler Barndorff-Nielsen** (18 March 1935 – 26 June 2022) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who has contributed to many areas of [statistical science](wikipedia://en/26685).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He was born in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen), and became interested in statistics when, as a student of [actuarial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuarial_mathematics) at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen), he worked part-time at the Department of [Biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) of the [Danish State Serum Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statens_Serum_Institut). He graduated from [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University) (Denmark) in 1960, where he has spent most of his academic life, and where he became professor of statistics in 1973. However, from 1962 to 1963 and from 1963 to 1964, he stayed at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), respectively, and from August 1974 to February 1975, he was an Overseas Fellow at [Churchill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), and visitor at Statistical Laboratory, [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).

Barndorff-Nielsen became Professor Emeritus at Aarhus University at the Thiele Centre for Applied Mathematics in Natural Science and affiliated with the *Center for Research in Econometric Analysis of Time Series* (CREATES) on a part-time basis[^o5] and since 2008 also affiliated to the Institute of Advanced Studies at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich).

## Works of Barndorff-Nielsen {#Works_of_Barndorff-Nielsen}

Among Barndorff-Nielsen's early scientific contributions are his work on [exponential families](wikipedia://en/339174) and on the [foundations of statistics](wikipedia://en/15515301), in particular [sufficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficiency_%28statistics%29) and conditional inference. In 1977, he introduced the [hyperbolic distribution](wikipedia://en/6331574) as a mathematical model of the size distribution of [sand grains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_grain), formalising heuristic ideas proposed by [Ralph Alger Bagnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Alger_Bagnold). He also derived the larger class of [generalised hyperbolic distributions](wikipedia://en/3420188). These distributions, in particular the [normal-inverse Gaussian](wikipedia://en/6533836) (NIG) distribution, have later turned out to be useful in many other areas of science, in particular [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) and [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance). The NIG-distribution is now widely used to describe the distribution of [returns from financial assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modeling_and_analysis_of_financial_markets).

In 1984, he produced a short film[^o7][^o8] on the physics of blown sand and the life of the British scientist and explorer Brigadier [Ralph Alger Bagnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Alger_Bagnold). A follow-up to the film was produced in 2011 on the studies of stochastics in the physical sciences carried out by Barndorff-Nielsen and colleagues at the Faculty of Science, Aarhus University by the initiative of the President of the Bernoulli Society for Mathematical Statistics and Probability, Professor Victor Pérez-Abreu.[^o9]

Later Barndorff-Nielsen played a leading role in the application of [differential geometry to investigate statistical models](wikipedia://en/487312). Another main contribution is his work on [asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_theory) methods in statistics, not least his formula for the [conditional distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_distribution) of the [maximum likelihood estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood_estimator) given an [ancillary statistic](wikipedia://en/670398) that generalizes a formula by [Ronald A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_A._Fisher) (originally called the $p^*$-formula, but now known as the Barndorff-Nielsen formula).[^o10][^o11] He has jointly with [David Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771) written two influential books on asymptotic techniques in statistics.[^o12][^o13] Since the mid-90s Barndorff-Nielsen has worked on [stochastic models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_model) in finance (often with [Neil Shephard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Shephard)) and turbulence, on statistical methods for the analysis of data from experiments in [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics), and has contributed to the theory of [Lévy processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lévy_processes).

## Notable honors and positions held {#Notable_honors_and_positions_held}

Barndorff-Nielsen is a member of the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters) and of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea). He has received [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) degrees from the [Université Paul Sabatier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toulouse), Toulouse and the [Katholieke Universiteit Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven). In 1993-1995 he was a very influential president of the [Bernoulli Society for Mathematical Statistics and Probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_Society_for_Mathematical_Statistics_and_Probability). He was the editor of *International Statistical Review* in 1980-1987 and of the journal *Bernoulli* in 1994–2000. From 1 April 1998 to 31 March 2003 he was Scientific Director of MaPhySto (the Centre for Mathematical Physics and Stochastics), which was a centre devoted to advanced research and training in the fields of mathematical physics and stochastics, as well as some closely related areas. In 2001 he received the [Humboldt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize) and in 2010 the Faculty Price from Faculty of Science, Aarhus University.[^o15][^o16]
