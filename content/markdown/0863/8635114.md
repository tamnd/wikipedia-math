# Pompeiu's theorem

*Image: Pompeiu theorem1.svg.*

*Image: Pompeiu theorem2.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pompeiu's theorem"}

**Pompeiu's theorem** is a result of [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), discovered by the Romanian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Dimitrie Pompeiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Pompeiu). The theorem is simple, but not classical. It states the following:

:::

*Given an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) ABC in the plane, and a point P in the plane of the triangle ABC, the lengths PA, PB, and PC form the sides of a (maybe, degenerate) triangle.*[^o3][^o2]

*Image: Satz von Pompeiu.svg.* Proof of Pompeiu's theorem with Pompeiu triangle $\triangle PCP'$

The proof is quick. Consider a rotation of 60° about the point *B*. Assume *A* maps to *C*, and *P* maps to *P*′. Then *PB* = *P*′*B*, and ∠*PBP*′ = 60°. Hence triangle *PBP*′ is equilateral and *PP*′ = *PB*. Then *PA* = *P*′*C*. Thus, triangle *PCP*′ has sides equal to *PA*, *PB*, and *PC* and the [proof by construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_by_construction) is complete (see drawing).[^o3]

Further investigations reveal that if *P* is not in the interior of the triangle, but rather on the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432), then *PA*, *PB*, *PC* form a degenerate triangle, with the largest being equal to the sum of the others; this observation is also known as [Van Schooten's theorem](wikipedia://en/59937621).[^o3]

Generally, by the point *P* and the lengths to the vertices of the equilateral triangle – *PA*, *PB*, and *PC* two equilateral triangles (the larger and the smaller) with sides *a*<sub>1</sub> and *a*<sub>2</sub> are defined:

$$a_{1,2}^2 = \frac{1}{2} \left( PA^2 + PB^2 + PC^2 \pm 4\sqrt{3}\triangle_{(PA,PB,PC)} \right) .$$

The symbol △ denotes the area of the triangle whose sides have lengths *PA*, *PB*, *PC*.[^o4]

Pompeiu published the theorem in 1936; however [August Ferdinand Möbius](wikipedia://en/37875) had already published a more general theorem about four points in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) in 1852. In this paper Möbius also derived the statement of Pompeiu's theorem explicitly as a special case of his more general theorem. For this reason, the theorem is also known as the *Möbius–Pompeiu theorem*.[^o5]
