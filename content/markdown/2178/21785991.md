# Adjusted mutual information

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adjusted mutual information"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), **adjusted mutual information**, a variation of [mutual information](wikipedia://en/427282) may be used for comparing [clusterings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_Analysis).[^o2] It corrects the effect of agreement solely due to chance between clusterings, similar to the way the [adjusted rand index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjusted_rand_index) corrects the [Rand index](wikipedia://en/4813359). It is closely related to [variation of information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variation_of_information):[^o3] when a similar adjustment is made to the VI index, it becomes equivalent to the AMI.[^o2] The adjusted measure however is no longer metrical.[^o4]

:::

## Mutual information of two partitions {#Mutual_information_of_two_partitions}

Given a set *S* of *N* elements $S=\{s_1, s_2,\ldots s_N\}$, consider two [partitions](wikipedia://en/340240) of *S*, namely $U=\{U_1, U_2,\ldots, U_R\}$ with *R* clusters, and $V=\{V_1, V_2,\ldots, V_C\}$ with *C* clusters. It is presumed here that the partitions are so-called *hard clusters;* the partitions are pairwise disjoint:

$$U_i\cap U_j = \varnothing = V_i\cap V_j$$

for all $i\ne j$, and complete:

$$\cup_{i=1}^RU_i=\cup_{j=1}^C V_j=S$$

The [mutual information](wikipedia://en/427282) of cluster overlap between *U* and *V* can be summarized in the form of an *R*x*C* [contingency table](wikipedia://en/935515) $M=[n_{ij}]^{i=1 \ldots R}_{j=1 \ldots C}$, where $n_{ij}$ denotes the number of objects that are common to clusters $U_i$ and $V_j$. That is,

$$n_{ij}=\left|U_i\cap V_j\right|$$

Suppose an object is picked at random from *S*; the probability that the object falls into cluster $U_i$ is:

$$P_U(i)=\frac{|U_i|}{N}$$

The [entropy](wikipedia://en/15445) associated with the partitioning *U* is:

$$H(U)=-\sum_{i=1}^R P_U(i)\log P_U(i)$$

*H(U)* is non-negative and takes the value 0 only when there is no uncertainty determining an object's cluster membership, *i.e.*, when there is only one cluster. Similarly, the entropy of the clustering *V* can be calculated as:

$$H(V)=-\sum_{j=1}^C P_V(j)\log P_V(j)$$

where $P_V(j)={|V_j|}/{N}$. The [mutual information](wikipedia://en/427282) (MI) between two partitions:

$$MI(U,V)=\sum_{i=1}^R \sum_{j=1}^C P_{UV}(i,j)\log \frac{P_{UV}(i,j)}{P_U(i)P_V(j)}$$

where $P_{UV}(i,j)$ denotes the probability that a point belongs to both the cluster $U_i$ in *U* and cluster $V_j$ in *V*:

$$P_{UV}(i,j)=\frac{|U_i \cap V_j|}{N}$$

MI is a non-negative quantity upper bounded by the entropies *H*(*U*) and *H*(*V*). It quantifies the information shared by the two clusterings and thus can be employed as a clustering [similarity measure](wikipedia://en/1004743).

## Adjustment for chance {#Adjustment_for_chance}

Like the [Rand index](wikipedia://en/4813359), the baseline value of mutual information between two random clusterings does not take on a constant value, and tends to be larger when the two partitions have a larger number of clusters (with a fixed number of set elements *N*). By adopting a [hypergeometric](wikipedia://en/180841) model of randomness, it can be shown that the expected mutual information between two random clusterings is:

$$\begin{aligned} E\{MI(U,V)\} = & \sum_{i=1}^R \sum_{j=1}^C \sum_{n_{ij}=(a_i+b_j-N)^+}^{\min(a_i, b_j)} \frac{n_{ij}}{N} \log \left( \frac{ N\cdot n_{ij}}{a_i b_j}\right) \times \\ & \frac{a_i!b_j!(N-a_i)!(N-b_j)!} {N!n_{ij}!(a_i-n_{ij})!(b_j-n_{ij})!(N-a_i-b_j+n_{ij})!} \\ \end{aligned}$$

where $(a_i+b_j-N)^+$ denotes $\max(0,a_i+b_j-N)$. The variables $a_i$ and $b_j$ are partial sums of the [contingency table](wikipedia://en/935515); that is,

$$a_i=\sum_{j=1}^Cn_{ij}$$

and

$$b_j=\sum_{i=1}^Rn_{ij}$$

The adjusted measure[^o2] for the mutual information may then be defined to be:

$$AMI(U,V)= \frac{MI(U,V)-E\{MI(U,V)\}} {\max{\{H(U),H(V)\}}-E\{MI(U,V)\}}.$$

The AMI takes a value of 1 when the two partitions are identical and 0 when the MI between two partitions equals the value expected due to chance alone.
