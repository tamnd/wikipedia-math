# Glenda Lappan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glenda Lappan"}

**Glenda T. Lappan** (born 1939) is a [professor emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emerita) of mathematics at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University). She is known for her work in [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) and in particular for developing the widely used [Connected Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9472437) curriculum for middle school mathematics in the US.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Lappan grew up as an only child on a farm in southern Georgia. She did her undergraduate studies at [Mercer University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_University), graduating in 1961, and taught at the high school level in Georgia before completing a doctorate at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) in 1965. She taught at Michigan State for 50 years, from 1965 until her retirement in 2015.[^o2]

From 1986 to 1991, Lappan directed the middle school portion of a project by the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691) to set curriculum and evaluation standards for mathematics. Following that work, she began the Connected Mathematics Project, initially envisioned as a five-year effort to implement the NCTM standards.[^o6] She served as president of the NCTM from 1998 to 2000, and later as chair of the Mathematical Sciences Education Board of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o4]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1996 the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) gave her their [Louise Hay Award](wikipedia://en/51270001).[^o6] She was named a University Distinguished Professor at Michigan State in 1998.[^o2] In 2002, the Connected Mathematics project endowed the Lappan-Phillips-Fitzgerald Endowed Chair in Mathematics Education at Michigan State, named after Lappan and the other two founders of the project.[^o2][^o7] The [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691) gave her a lifetime achievement award in 2004.[^o4] In 2008 she and Elizabeth Phillips shared the [International Society for Design and Development in Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Design_and_Development_in_Education) Prize for Excellence in Educational Design for their work on Connected Mathematics.[^o8] She was inducted into the [Michigan Women's Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Women's_Hall_of_Fame) in 2009.[^o9]
