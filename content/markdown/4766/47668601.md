# David J. Foulis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David J. Foulis"}

**David James Foulis** (July 26, 1930 – April 3, 2018) was an American mathematician known for his research on the [algebraic](wikipedia://en/19616384) foundations of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). He spent much of his career at the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst),[^o3] retiring in 1997 but continuing to be very active in mathematics as [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus). He is the namesake of [Foulis semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foulis_semigroup), an algebraic structure that he studied extensively under the alternative name of Baer \*-semigroups.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Foulis was born on July 26, 1930, in [Hinsdale, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinsdale,_Illinois), the son of professional golfer [Jim Foulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Foulis). As a teenager he moved with his family to Florida, and he graduated magna cum laude from the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami) with a degree in physics in 1952. He stayed at Miami for a master's degree in mathematics in 1953. He spent the following year as a graduate student at [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University), and then visited the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) for two years with [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky) and [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598) as mentors.[^o2][^o3] Returning to Tulane, he completed his Ph.D. in 1958 under the supervision of one of Kaplansky's students, Fred Boyer Wright, Jr.[^o2][^o8]

## Career {#Career}

After completing his doctorate, Foulis taught for one year in the mathematics department at [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University), four years at [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University), and two years at the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida) before moving to the University of Massachusetts as a professor of mathematics and statistics in 1965. He retired in 1997, but continued to be active as a researcher after retirement.[^o2][^o3][^o10]

Foulis's doctoral students at Massachusetts have included [DIMACS](wikipedia://en/3684858) associate director [Melvin Janowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Janowitz), [graph theorist](wikipedia://en/12401) [David Sumner](wikipedia://en/47676305), and mathematics and statistics educator and textbook author [Patti Frazer Lock](wikipedia://en/62922552).[^o7]

## Publications {#Publications}

The most highly cited of Foulis's research papers is "Effect algebras and unsharp quantum logics" (*Foundations of Physics*, 1994) which he wrote with his former student and later University of Massachusetts colleague Mary K. Bennett. Foulis is also the author of seven undergraduate textbooks in mathematics.[^o2] One of his texts, a large red book on calculus, was used as a prop in the 1985 romantic comedy movie *[The Sure Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sure_Thing)*.[^o3]
