# Jan Brinkhuis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jan Brinkhuis"}

**Jan Brinkhuis** (born 1952) is a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and associate professor of Finance and Mathematical Methods and Techniques at the [Econometric Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Institute) of [Erasmus University Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_University_Rotterdam), specialized in the theory and application of [optimization theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_theory) and [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924).[^o2]

:::

Born in [Veenendaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veenendaal), Brinkhuis received his PhD in mathematics in 1981 at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) under supervisor of [Albrecht Fröhlich](wikipedia://en/1096590) with the thesis *Embedding Problems and [Galois Modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module)*. After graduation, he started his academic career as research associate at King's College London on a SERC grant. In 1983 he moved back to The Netherlands and joined the [Econometric Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Institute) of [Erasmus University Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_University_Rotterdam). Since early 2000s Brinkhuis is also teacher at the [Tinbergen Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinbergen_Institute).

In 2000 Brinkhuis was awarded Education and Research award by the Erasmus University Rotterdam,[^o3] and in 2009 he was elected Tinbergen Professor of the Year 2008/2009 by the first-year students.[^o4] In 2005 Brinkhuis and Vladimir Tikhomirov wrote *Optimization: Insights and Applications* an introduction to [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033),[^o5] published by Princeton University Press.
