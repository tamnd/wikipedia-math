# Adjoint bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adjoint bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **adjoint bundle** [^o2] is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) naturally associated with any smooth [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950). The fibers of the adjoint bundle carry a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) structure making the adjoint bundle into a (nonassociative) [algebra bundle](wikipedia://en/1244529). Adjoint bundles have important applications in the theory of [connections](wikipedia://en/308815) as well as in [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *G* be a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) with [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak g$, and let *P* be a [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) over a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *M*. Let

$$\mathrm{Ad}: G\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak g)\subset\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak g)$$

be the (left) [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of *G*. The **adjoint bundle** of *P* is the [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874)

$$\mathrm{ad} P = P\times_{\mathrm{Ad}}\mathfrak g$$

The adjoint bundle is also commonly denoted by $\mathfrak g_P$. Explicitly, elements of the adjoint bundle are [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of pairs \[*p*, *X*\] for *p* ∈ *P* and *X* ∈ $\mathfrak g$ such that

$$[p\cdot g,X] = [p,\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(X)]$$

for all *g* ∈ *G*. Since the [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group) of the adjoint bundle consists of Lie algebra [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160), the fibers naturally carry a Lie algebra structure making the adjoint bundle into a bundle of Lie algebras over *M*.

:::

## Restriction to a closed subgroup {#Restriction_to_a_closed_subgroup}

Let *G* be any Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and let *H* be a closed subgroup of G. Via the (left) adjoint representation of G $\mathfrak g$, G becomes a topological transformation group $\mathfrak g$. By restricting the adjoint representation of G to the subgroup H,

$$\mathrm{Ad\vert_H}: H \hookrightarrow G \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak g)$$

also H acts as a topological transformation group on $\mathfrak g$. For every h in H, $Ad\vert_H(h): \mathfrak g \mapsto \mathfrak g$ is a Lie algebra automorphism.

Since H is a closed subgroup of Lie group G, the homogeneous space M=G/H is the base space of a principal bundle $G \to M$ with total space G and structure group H. So the existence of H-valued transition functions $g_{ij}: U_{i}\cap U_{j} \rightarrow H$ is assured, where $U_{i}$ is an open covering for M, and the transition functions $g_{ij}$ form a cocycle of transition function on M. The associated fibre bundle $\xi= (E,p,M,\mathfrak g) = G[(\mathfrak g, \mathrm{Ad\vert_H})]$ is a bundle of Lie algebras, with typical fibre $\mathfrak g$, and a continuous mapping $\Theta :\xi \oplus \xi \rightarrow \xi$ induces on each fibre the Lie bracket.[^o6]

## Properties {#Properties}

[Differential forms](wikipedia://en/3772116) on *M* with values in $\mathrm{ad} P$ are in one-to-one correspondence with [horizontal, *G*-equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensorial_form) [Lie algebra-valued forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra-valued_form) on *P*. A prime example is the [curvature](wikipedia://en/489440) of any [connection](wikipedia://en/470632) on *P* which may be regarded as a 2-form on *M* with values in $\mathrm{ad} P$.

The space of sections of the adjoint bundle is naturally an (infinite-dimensional) Lie algebra. It may be regarded as the Lie algebra of the infinite-dimensional Lie group of [gauge transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation) of *P* which can be thought of as sections of the bundle $P \times_{\mathrm conj} G$ where conj is the action of *G* on itself by (left) [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_theory%29).

If $P=\mathcal{F}(E)$ is the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) of a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) $E\to M$, then $P$ has fibre in the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $\operatorname{GL}(r)$ (either real or complex, depending on $E$) where $\operatorname{rank}(E) = r$. This structure group has Lie algebra consisting of all $r\times r$ matrices $\operatorname{Mat}(r)$, and these can be thought of as the endomorphisms of the vector bundle $E$. Indeed, there is a natural isomorphism $\operatorname{ad} \mathcal{F}(E) \cong \operatorname{End}(E)$.

:::
