# 1/2 − 1/4 + 1/8 − 1/16 + ⋯

*Image: Halfminusquarter.png.* Demonstration that $\tfrac{1}{2}$ − $\tfrac{1}{4}$ + $\tfrac{1}{8}$ − $\tfrac{1}{16}$ + ⋯ = $\tfrac{1}{3}$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="1/2 − 1/4 + 1/8 − 1/16 + ⋯"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) 1/2 − 1/4 + 1/8 − 1/16 + ⋯ is a simple example of an [alternating series](wikipedia://en/494308) that [converges absolutely](wikipedia://en/61532).

:::

It is a [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630) whose first term is $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and whose common ratio is −$\tfrac{1}{2}$, so its sum is

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2^n}=\frac12-\frac14+\frac18-\frac{1}{16}+\cdots=\frac{\frac12}{1-(-\frac12)} = \frac13.$$

## Hackenbush and the surreals {#Hackenbush_and_the_surreals}

*Image: Hackenbush two thirds.svg.* Demonstration of $\tfrac{2}{3}$ via a zero-value game

A slight rearrangement of the series reads

$$1-\frac12-\frac14+\frac18-\frac{1}{16}+\cdots=\frac13.$$

The series has the form of a positive integer plus a series containing every negative [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948) with either a positive or negative sign, so it can be translated into the infinite blue-red [Hackenbush](wikipedia://en/1613052) string that represents the [surreal number](wikipedia://en/51432) $\tfrac{1}{3}$:

LRRLRLR... = $\tfrac{1}{3}$.[^n1]

A slightly simpler Hackenbush string eliminates the repeated R:

LRLRLRL... = $\tfrac{2}{3}$.[^n2]

In terms of the Hackenbush game structure, this equation means that the board depicted on the right has a value of 0; whichever player moves second has a winning strategy.

## Related series {#Related_series}

- The statement that $\tfrac{1}{2}$ − $\tfrac{1}{4}$ + $\tfrac{1}{8}$ − $\tfrac{1}{16}$ + ⋯ is absolutely convergent means that the series [$\tfrac{1}{2}$ + $\tfrac{1}{4}$ + $\tfrac{1}{8}$ + $\tfrac{1}{16}$ + ⋯](wikipedia://en/10073693) is convergent. In fact, the latter series converges to [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29), and it proves that one of the [binary expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_expansion) of 1 is 0.111....
- Pairing up the terms of the series $\tfrac{1}{2}$ − $\tfrac{1}{4}$ + $\tfrac{1}{8}$ − $\tfrac{1}{16}$ + ⋯ results in another geometric series with the same sum, [$\tfrac{1}{4}$ + $\tfrac{1}{16}$ + $\tfrac{1}{64}$ + $\tfrac{1}{256}$ + ⋯](wikipedia://en/10073845). This series is one of the first to be summed in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220); it was used by [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) circa 250–200 BC.[^n3]
- The [Euler transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_transform) of the divergent series [1 − 2 + 4 − 8 + ⋯](wikipedia://en/9939158) is $\tfrac{1}{2}$ − $\tfrac{1}{4}$ + $\tfrac{1}{8}$ − $\tfrac{1}{16}$ + ⋯. Therefore, even though the former series does not have a sum in the usual sense, it is [Euler summable](wikipedia://en/9780066) to $\tfrac{1}{3}$.[^n4]
