# Roberto Bachi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Roberto Bachi"}

**Roberto Bachi** (רוברטו בקי; 16 January 1909 – 26 November 1995) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy)-[Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685) and [demographer](wikipedia://en/45642), and founder of the [Israel Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics). His research focused on the [graphical presentation of statistics](wikipedia://en/15934463) and the renewal of [Jewish demographic studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_population) in the [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Roberto Bachi was born to an [Italian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Jews) family in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), the son of Clelia Lampronti and economist Riccardo Bachi. He studied law and statistics at the [University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), receiving a [Doctor of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Law) degree in 1931. He taught statistics at the [University of Sassari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sassari) from 1934 to 1936, and at the [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa) from 1936, becoming [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_%28highest_academic_rank%29) in 1937. He [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah) to [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) following the introduction of the [Italian racial laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_racial_laws) in 1938, where he became an active *[Mapainik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapai)*.

Bachi worked as a statistician in the [Hadassah Medical Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadassah_Medical_Center) (where he founded a Medical Central Bureau of Statistics), and during 1945–47 in the Department of Statistics of the [Mandatory Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine). He began teaching statistics at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) in the early 1940s. He studied the issue of [sub-replacement fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-replacement_fertility) the [Yishuv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishuv) and corresponded extensively with major Zionist leaders of the time, conveying comparative data on the Jewish and Arab populations to [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion) and others.[^o5][^o6]

After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), the [Italian Foreign Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_%28Italy%29) invited Bachi to resume his post in Genoa, which Bachi declined.[^o7] By 1945, Bachi had become associate professor of statistics at the Hebrew University, and was promoted to full professor in 1947. He was also appointed Statistician General of Israel with the foundation of the state in 1948, and founded Israel's [Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) in 1949, which he directed until 1971.

At the same time Bachi was among the founders of the Faculty of Social Sciences of the Hebrew University and served as its first dean from 1953 to 1956.[^o8] He served as [prorector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prorector) of the Hebrew University in 1959–60. After his retirement in 1977, Bachi concentrated primarily on [geostatistics](wikipedia://en/51531) and graphical representation of [statistical data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_data).[^o9] He was awarded the [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize) in 1982.

Bachi died on November 26, 1995.[^o10]
