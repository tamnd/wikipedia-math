# Spherical Bernstein's problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical Bernstein's problem"}

The **spherical Bernstein's problem** is a possible generalization of the original [Bernstein's problem](wikipedia://en/30486183) in the field of global [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), first proposed by [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905) in 1969, and then later in 1970, during his plenary address at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice).

:::

## The problem {#The_problem}

Are the equators in $\mathbb{S}^{n+1}$ the only smooth embedded minimal hypersurfaces which are topological $n$-dimensional spheres?

Additionally, the **spherical Bernstein's problem**, while itself a generalization of the original Bernstein's problem, can also be generalized further by replacing the ambient space $\mathbb{S}^{n+1}$ with a simply connected, compact symmetric space. Some results in this direction are due to [Wu-Yi Hsiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu-Yi_Hsiang), [Wu-Teh Hsiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu-Teh_Hsiang), and [P. Tomter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._Tomter).

## Alternative formulations {#Alternative_formulations}

Below are two alternative ways to express the problem:

### The second formulation {#The_second_formulation}

Let the [(*n* − 1) sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) be embedded as a minimal hypersurface in $S^n$(1). Is it necessarily an equator?

By the [Almgren](wikipedia://en/12998276)–[Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000) theorem, it's true when *n* = 3 (or *n* = 2 for the 1st formulation).

[Wu-Yi Hsiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu-Yi_Hsiang) proved it for *n* ∈ {4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14} (or *n* ∈ {3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13}, respectively)

In 1987, [Per Tomter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Tomter) proved it for all even *n* (or all odd *n*, respectively).

Thus, it only remains unknown for all odd *n* ≥ 9 (or all even *n* ≥ 8, respectively)

### The third formulation {#The_third_formulation}

Is it true that an embedded, minimal hypersphere inside the Euclidean $n$-sphere is necessarily an equator?

Geometrically, the problem is analogous to the following problem:

Is the local topology at an isolated singular point of a minimal hypersurface necessarily different from that of a disc?

For example, the affirmative answer for spherical Bernstein problem when *n* = 3 is equivalent to the fact that the local topology at an isolated singular point of any minimal hypersurface in an arbitrary Riemannian 4-manifold must be different from that of a disc.
