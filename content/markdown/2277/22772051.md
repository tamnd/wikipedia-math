# Cancellative semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cancellative semigroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **cancellative semigroup** (also called a **cancellation semigroup**) is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) having the [cancellation property](wikipedia://en/490646).[^o2] In intuitive terms, the cancellation property asserts that from an [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) of the form *a*·*b* = *a*·*c*, where · is a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948), one can cancel the element *a* and deduce the equality *b* = *c*. In this case the element being cancelled out is appearing as the left factors of *a*·*b* and *a*·*c* and hence it is a case of the **left cancellation property**. The **right cancellation property** can be defined analogously. Prototypical examples of cancellative semigroups are the [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) under [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) or [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845). Cancellative semigroups are considered to be very close to being [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) because cancellability is one of the necessary conditions for a semigroup to be [embeddable](wikipedia://en/52119) in a group. Moreover, every finite cancellative semigroup is a group. One of the main problems associated with the study of cancellative semigroups is to determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for embedding a cancellative semigroup in a group.

:::

The origins of the study of cancellative semigroups can be traced to the first substantial paper on semigroups, (Suschkewitsch 1928).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definitions"}

## Formal definitions {#Formal_definitions}

Let *S* be a semigroup. An element *a* in *S* is **left cancellative** (or, is *left cancellable*, or, has the *left cancellation property*) if *ab* = *ac* implies *b* = *c* for all *b* and *c* in *S*. If every element in *S* is left cancellative, then *S* is called a **left cancellative semigroup**.

Let *S* be a semigroup. An element *a* in *S* is **right cancellative** (or, is *right cancellable*, or, has the *right cancellation property*) if *ba* = *ca* implies *b* = *c* for all *b* and *c* in *S*. If every element in *S* is right cancellative, then *S* is called a **right cancellative semigroup**.

Let *S* be a semigroup. If every element in *S* is both left cancellative and right cancellative, then *S* is called a **cancellative semigroup**.

:::

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Alternative definitions"}

## Alternative definitions {#Alternative_definitions}

It is possible to restate the characteristic property of a cancellative element in terms of a property held by the corresponding left multiplication *L*<sub>*a*</sub> : *S* → *S* and right multiplication *R*<sub>*a*</sub> : *S* → *S* maps defined by *L*<sub>*a*</sub>(*b*) = *ab* and *R*<sub>*a*</sub>(*b*) = *ba*: an element *a* in *S* is **left cancellative** [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *L*<sub>*a*</sub> is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective), an element *a* is **right cancellative** if and only if *R*<sub>*a*</sub> is injective.

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

1. Every [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is a cancellative semigroup.
1. The set of [positive integers](wikipedia://en/21474) under addition is a cancellative semigroup.
1. The set of nonnegative integers under addition is a cancellative [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652).
1. The set of positive integers under multiplication is a cancellative monoid.
1. A [left zero semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_zero_semigroup) is right cancellative but not left cancellative, unless it is trivial.
1. A [right zero semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_zero_semigroup) is left cancellative but not right cancellative, unless it is trivial.
1. A [null semigroup](wikipedia://en/22694038) with more than one element is neither left cancellative nor right cancellative. In such a semigroup there is no element that is either left cancellative or right cancellative.
1. Let *S* be the semigroup of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [square](wikipedia://en/166022) [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) of order *n* under [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280). Let *a* be any element in *S*. If *a* is [nonsingular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) then *a* is both left cancellative and right cancellative. If *a* is singular then *a* is neither left cancellative nor right cancellative.

:::

## Finite cancellative semigroups {#Finite_cancellative_semigroups}

It is an elementary result in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) that a finite cancellative semigroup is a group. Let *S* be a finite cancellative semigroup.

1. Cancellativity and finiteness taken together imply that *Sa* = *aS* = *S* for all *a* in *S*. So given an element *a* in *S*, there is an element *e*<sub>*a*</sub>, depending on *a*, in *S* such that *ae*<sub>*a*</sub> = *a*. Cancellativity now further implies that this *e*<sub>*a*</sub> is independent of *a* and that *xe*<sub>*a*</sub> = *e*<sub>*a*</sub>*x* = *x* for all *x* in *S*. Thus *e*<sub>*a*</sub> is the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of *S*, which may from now on be denoted by *e*.
1. Using the property *Sa* = *S* one now sees that there is *b* in *S* such that *ba* = *e*. Cancellativity can be invoked to show that *ab* = *e* also, thereby establishing that every element *a* in *S* has an inverse in *S*. Thus *S* must necessarily be a group.

Furthermore, every cancellative [epigroup](wikipedia://en/37300268) is also a group.[^o12]

## Embeddability in groups {#Embeddability_in_groups}

A [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) semigroup can be embedded in a group (i.e., is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_homomorphism) to a [subsemigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsemigroup#Subsemigroups_and_ideals) of a group) if and only if it is cancellative. The procedure for doing this is similar to that of [embedding an integral domain in a field](wikipedia://en/25271) (Clifford Preston 1961, 34) – it is called the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) construction, and is the universal mapping from a commutative semigroup to [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) that is an embedding if the semigroup is cancellative.

For the embeddability of noncommutative semigroups in groups, cancellativity is obviously a [necessary condition](wikipedia://en/169319). However, it is not sufficient: there are (noncommutative and infinite) cancellative semigroups that cannot be embedded in a group.[^o15] To obtain a sufficient (but not necessary) condition, it may be observed that the proof of the result that a finite cancellative semigroup *S* is a group critically depended on the fact that *Sa* = *S* for all *a* in *S*. The paper (Dubreil 1941) generalized this idea and introduced the concept of a *right reversible* semigroup. A semigroup *S* is said to be *right reversible* if any two principal ideals of *S* intersect, that is, *Sa* ∩ *Sb* ≠ Ø for all *a* and *b* in *S*. The sufficient condition for the embeddability of semigroups in groups can now be stated as follows: ([Ore's Theorem](wikipedia://en/3100560)) Any right reversible cancellative semigroup can be embedded in a group, (Clifford Preston 1961, 35).

The first set of necessary and sufficient conditions for the embeddability of a semigroup in a group were given in (Malcev 1939).[^o19] Though theoretically important, the conditions are countably infinite in number and no finite subset will suffice, as shown in (Malcev 1940).[^o21] A different (but also countably infinite) set of necessary and sufficient conditions were given in (Lambek 1951), where it was shown that a semigroup can be embedded in a group if and only if it is cancellative and satisfies a so-called "polyhedral condition". The two embedding theorems by Malcev and Lambek were compared in (Bush 1963) and later revisited and generalized by (Johnstone 2008), who also explained the close relationship between the semigroup embeddability problem and the more general problem of embedding a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) into a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543).
