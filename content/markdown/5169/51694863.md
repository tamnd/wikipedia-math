# Edward Neuman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Neuman"}

**Edward Neuman** (born September 19, 1943[^o3] in [Rydułtowy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rydułtowy), [Silesian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Voivodeship), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland))[^o4] is a Polish-American mathematician, currently a professor emeritus of mathematics at [Southern Illinois University Carbondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University_Carbondale).[^o5]

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Neuman received his Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław) in 1972[^o7] under the supervision of [wmpl:Stefan Paszkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wmpl:Stefan_Paszkowski).[^o8] His dissertation was entitled "Projections in Uniform Polynomial Approximation." He held positions at the Institute of Mathematics and the Institute of Computer Sciences of the [University of Wroclaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wroclaw),[^o9] and the Institute of Applied Mathematics Bonn[^o10] in Germany. In 1986, he took a permanent faculty position at [Southern Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Neuman has contributed 130 journal articles[^o12] in computational mathematics and mathematical inequalities such as the [Ky Fan inequality](wikipedia://en/2143560), on bivariate means,[^o13] and mathematical approximations and expansions. Neuman also developed software for computing with spline functions[^o14] and wrote several tutorials for the [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) programing software.[^o15] Among the mathematical concepts named after Edward Neuman are Neuman–Sándor Mean and Neuman Means, which are useful tools for advancing the theory of [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) including [Jacobi elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/450004):

- The [Neuman–Sándor mean](wikipedia://en/55729546)[^o16]
- The Neuman Means of the first kind[^o17]
- The Neuman Means of the second kind[^o18]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Neuman was named the Outstanding Teacher in 2001 in the College of Science at [Southern Illinois University Carbondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University_Carbondale). Neuman worked as a Validator[^o3] for the original release and publication of the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology) (NIST) Handbook and [Digital Library of Mathematical Functions](wikipedia://en/12647205). He serves on the Editorial Boards of the Journal of Inequalities in Pure and Applied Mathematics,[^o20] the Journal of Inequalities and Special Functions,[^o21] and Journal of Mathematical Inequalities.[^o22]

## Selected works {#Selected_works}

The most frequently cited works by Neuman include:

- "On the Schwab–Borchardt mean" *Math Pannon* 14(2) (2003), 253–266.[^o24]
- "On the Schwab–Borchardt mean II" *Math Pannon* 17(1) (2006), 49–59.[^o25]
- "The weighted logarithmic mean" *J. Math. Anal. Appl.* 188 (1994), 885–900.[^o26]
