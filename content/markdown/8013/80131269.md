# Algebra (book)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Algebra"}

***Algebra*** is a [graduate-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_education) textbook on [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) written by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) and was originally published by [Addison-Wesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addison-Wesley) in 1965. Its intended audience is students in graduate-level courses and readers who have previously attended undergraduate-level algebra courses.

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

The third edition is divided into four parts.

- The first part, *The Basic Objects of [Algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923)*, covers [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), and [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000).
- The second part, *Algebraic Equations*, focuses on [field theory](wikipedia://en/10603) and includes a chapter on [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129) and [modules](wikipedia://en/542431).
- The third part, *[Linear Algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and Representations*, contains chapters on the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of modules and [semi-simplicity](wikipedia://en/45256442).
- The fourth part, *[Homological Algebra](wikipedia://en/139410)*, covers [general homology theory](wikipedia://en/142432) and finite free [resolutions](wikipedia://en/19745659).

## Audience and reception {#Audience_and_reception}

The [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) strongly recommends that undergraduate mathematics libraries have a copy of Lang's *Algebra* available.

Reviews of *Algebra* appeared in *[The Mathematical Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Gazette)* in 1967 and 2003.[^o5][^o6]

Professor [George Bergman](wikipedia://en/41052556) of [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) wrote *Companion to Lang's Algebra*, a 222-page book of notes collected when teaching Berkeley's basic graduate algebra course from Lang’s book.
