# Dirac structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dirac structure"}

In mathematics a **Dirac structure** is a geometric structure generalizing both [symplectic structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_structure) and [Poisson structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_structure), and having several applications to mechanics. It is based on the notion of the [Dirac bracket](wikipedia://en/13657747) constraint introduced by [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) and was first introduced by [Ted Courant](wikipedia://en/22117952) and [Alan Weinstein](wikipedia://en/7344764).

:::

## Linear Dirac structures {#Linear_Dirac_structures}

Let *$V$* be a real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), and *$V^*$* its [dual](wikipedia://en/7988). A (linear) *Dirac structure* on *$V$* is a [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) *$D$* of $V\times V^*$ satisfying

- for all $(v,\alpha)\in D$ one has $\left\langle\alpha,\,v\right\rangle=0$,
- *$D$* is maximal with respect to this property.

In particular, if *$V$* is finite dimensional, then the second criterion is satisfied if $\dim D = \dim V$. Similar definitions can be made for vector spaces over other fields.

An alternative (equivalent) definition often used is that $D$ satisfies $D=D^\perp$, where orthogonality is with respect to the symmetric [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) on $V\times V^*$ given by $\bigl\langle(v,\alpha),\,(u,\beta)\bigr\rangle = \left\langle\alpha,u\right\rangle + \left\langle\beta,v\right\rangle$.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

If $U\subset V$ is a vector subspace, then $D=U\times U^\circ$ is a Dirac structure on $V$, where $U^\circ$ is the annihilator of $U$; that is, $U^\circ=\left\{\alpha\in V^*\mid \alpha_{\vert U}=0\right\}$.

Let $\omega:V\to V^*$ be a skew-symmetric linear map, then the graph of $\omega$ is a Dirac structure. Similarly, if $\Pi:V^*\to V$ is a skew-symmetric linear map, then its graph is a Dirac structure.

:::

## Dirac structures on manifolds {#Dirac_structures_on_manifolds}

A ***Dirac structure*** $\mathfrak{D}$ on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *$M$* is an assignment of a (linear) Dirac structure on the tangent space to *$M$* at *$m$*, for each $m \in M$. That is,

- for each $m\in M$, a Dirac subspace $D_m$ of the space $T_mM\times T^*_mM$.

Many authors, in particular in geometry rather than the mechanics applications, require a Dirac structure to satisfy an extra *integrability condition* as follows:

- suppose $(X_i,\alpha_i)$ are sections of the Dirac bundle $\mathfrak{D} \to M$ ($i=1,2,3$) then $\left\langle L_{X_1}(\alpha_2),\,X_3\right\rangle + \left\langle L_{X_2}(\alpha_3),\,X_1\right\rangle +\left\langle L_{X_3}(\alpha_1),\,X_2\right\rangle = 0.$

In the mechanics literature this would be called a *closed* or *integrable* Dirac structure.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

Let $\Delta$ be a [smooth distribution](wikipedia://en/2976052) of constant rank on a manifold *$M$*, and for each $m\in M$ let $D_m=\{(u,\alpha)\in T_mM\times T_m^*M \mid u\in\Delta(m),\,\alpha\in \Delta(m)^\circ\}$, then the union of these subspaces over *$m$* forms a Dirac structure on *$M$*.

Let $\omega$ be a [symplectic form](wikipedia://en/28356) on a manifold $M$, then its graph is a (closed) Dirac structure. More generally, this is true for any closed 2-form. If the 2-form is not closed, then the resulting Dirac structure is not closed.

Let $\Pi$ be a [Poisson structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_structure) on a manifold $M$, then its graph is a (closed) Dirac structure.

Any submanifold of a Poisson manifold induces a Dirac structure.[^o8]

:::
