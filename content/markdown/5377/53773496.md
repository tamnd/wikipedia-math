# Elongated gyrobifastigium

*Image: Elongated gyrobifastigium.stl.*

*Image: Elongated gyrobifastigium.gif.*

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Elongated gyrobifastigium"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **elongated gyrobifastigium** or **gabled rhombohedron** is a space-filling [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) with 4 rectangles and 4 right-angled [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/20741014) faces.

:::

## Name {#Name}

The first name is from the regular-faced [gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/1177963) but [elongated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29) with 4 triangles expanded into pentagons. The name of the gyrobifastigium comes from the Latin *fastigium*, meaning a sloping roof. In the standard naming convention of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034), *bi-* means two solids connected at their bases, and *gyro-* means the two halves are twisted with respect to each other. The gyrobifastigium is first in a series of [gyrobicupola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrobicupola), so this solid can also be called an *elongated digonal gyrobicupola*. Geometrically, it can also be constructed as the dual of a digonal [gyrobianticupola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrobianticupola). This construction is space-filling.

The second name, *gabled [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406)*, is from Michael Goldberg's paper on space-filling octahedra, model 8-VI, the 6th of at least 49 space-filling octahedra. A [gable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gable) is the triangular portion of a wall between the edges of intersecting roof pitches.

## Geometry {#Geometry}

The elongated gyrobifastigium is the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of a [snub disphenoid](wikipedia://en/1133565), one of 92 Johnson solids, sharing the same [three-dimensional dihedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2922089) as antiprismatic $\mathrm{D}_{2\mathrm{d}}$ of order 8. If the underlying [rectangular cuboid](wikipedia://en/38848329) is distorted into a [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406), the symmetry is reduced to two-fold rotational symmetry, C<sub>2</sub>, order 2.

### Related figures {#Related_figures}

The elongated gyrobifastigium is the cell of the [isochoric](wikipedia://en/6286120) tridecachoron, a polychoron constructed from the dual of the 13-5 step prism, which has a snub disphenoid vertex figure.

## Variations {#Variations}

A topologically distinct *[elongated gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/16034#Elongated_bicupolae)* has square and equilateral triangle faces, seen as 2 [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584) augmented to a central [cube](wikipedia://en/6285). This is a failed [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) for not being strictly convex.[^o8]

This is also a space-filling polyhedron, and matches the geometry of the [gyroelongated triangular prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroelongated_triangular_prismatic_honeycomb) if the *elongated gyrobifastigium* is [dissected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) back into cubes and triangular prisms.

The *elongated gyrobifastigium* must be based on a [rectangular cuboid](wikipedia://en/38848329) or [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406) to fill-space, while the angle of the roof is free, including allowing concave forms. If the roof has zero angle, the geometry becomes a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) or [rectangular cuboid](wikipedia://en/38848329).

The pentagons can also be made regular and the rectangles become trapezoids, and it will no longer be space-filling.

## Honeycomb {#Honeycomb}

Like the gyrobifastigium, it can [self-tessellate space](wikipedia://en/58607583). Polyhedra are tessellated by translation in the plane, and are stacked with alternate orientations. The cross-section of the polyhedron must be square or rhombic, while the [roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof) angle is free, and can be negative, making a concave polyhedron. Rhombic forms require chiral (mirror image) polyhedral pairs to be space-filling.
