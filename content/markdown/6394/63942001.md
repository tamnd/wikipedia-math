# Caroline Klivans

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Caroline Klivans"}

**Caroline Jane (Carly) Klivans** is an American mathematician specializing in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), including work on [cell complexes](wikipedia://en/35238282) associated with [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321) and on [chip-firing games](wikipedia://en/54561208). She is a professor of applied mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), and associate director of the [Institute for Computational and Experimental Research in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31112547) at Brown.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

After graduating from [Mt. Lebanon High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Lebanon_High_School),[^o4] Kilvan attended [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) where she was the 1999 winner of the [Alice T. Schafer Prize](wikipedia://en/57181252) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698). Her undergraduate research project involved robot navigation algorithms. She graduated from Cornell in 1999, and completed her Ph.D. at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 2003. Her dissertation, *Combinatorial Properties of Shifted Complexes*, was supervised by [Richard P. Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397).

After postdoctoral research at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) and the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), where she was an L. E. Dickson Instructor, and positions as a researcher and lecturer at the University of Chicago and Brown University, she became associate director of the [Institute for Computational and Experimental Research in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31112547) at Brown in 2015, and obtained a regular-rank faculty position as associate professor there in 2018. In 2022, she was elected member-at-large of the AWM Executive Committee.[^o5]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Klivans is the author of the book *[The Mathematics of Chip-Firing](wikipedia://en/63953698)* (CRC Press, 2018).

Her research contributions include a disproof of a 50-year-old conjecture of Richard Stanley that every [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) whose face ring is a [Cohen–Macaulay ring](wikipedia://en/497407) can be partitioned into disjoint intervals, each including a facet of the complex. Such a partition generalizes a [shelling](wikipedia://en/32457161) and (if it always existed) would have been helpful in understanding the [$h$-vectors](wikipedia://en/11503563) of these complexes.
