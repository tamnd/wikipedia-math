# Associated graded ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Associated graded ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **associated graded ring** of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* with respect to a proper [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* is the [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094):

:::

$$\operatorname{gr}_I R = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty I^n/I^{n+1}.$$

Similarly, if *M* is a left *R*-module, then the **associated graded module** is the [graded module](wikipedia://en/103094) over $\operatorname{gr}_I R$:

$$\operatorname{gr}_I M = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty I^n M/ I^{n+1} M.$$

## Basic definitions and properties {#Basic_definitions_and_properties}

For a ring *R* and ideal *I*, multiplication in $\operatorname{gr}_IR$ is defined as follows: First, consider [homogeneous elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_element) $a \in I^i/I^{i + 1}$ and $b \in I^j/I^{j + 1}$ and suppose $a' \in I^i$ is a representative of *a* and $b' \in I^j$ is a representative of *b*. Then define $ab$ to be the [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of $a'b'$ in $I^{i + j}/I^{i + j + 1}$. Note that this is [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined) modulo $I^{i + j + 1}$. Multiplication of inhomogeneous elements is defined by using the [distributive property](wikipedia://en/103118).

A ring or module may be related to its associated graded ring or module through the **initial form map**. Let *M* be an *R*-module and *I* an ideal of *R*. Given $f \in M$, the **initial form** of *f* in $\operatorname{gr}_I M$, written $\mathrm{in}(f)$, is the equivalence class of *f* in $I^mM/I^{m+1}M$ where *m* is the maximum [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) such that $f\in I^mM$. If $f \in I^mM$ for every *m*, then set $\mathrm{in}(f) = 0$. The initial form map is only a map of sets and generally not a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/474118). For a [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) $N \subset M$, $\mathrm{in}(N)$ is defined to be the submodule of $\operatorname{gr}_I M$ generated by $\{\mathrm{in}(f) | f \in N\}$. This may not be the same as the submodule of $\operatorname{gr}_IM$ generated by the only initial forms of the generators of *N*.

A ring inherits some "good" properties from its associated graded ring. For example, if *R* is a [noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391), and $\operatorname{gr}_I R$ is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), then *R* is itself an integral domain.[^o3]

## gr of a quotient module {#gr_of_a_quotient_module}

Let $N \subset M$ be left modules over a ring *R* and *I* an ideal of *R*. Since

$${I^n(M/N) \over I^{n+1}(M/N)} \simeq {I^n M + N \over I^{n+1}M + N} \simeq {I^n M \over I^n M \cap (I^{n+1} M + N)} = {I^n M \over I^n M \cap N + I^{n+1} M}$$

(the last equality is by [modular law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_law)), there is a canonical identification:[^o5]

$$\operatorname{gr}_I (M/N) = \operatorname{gr}_I M / \operatorname{in}(N)$$

where

$$\operatorname{in}(N) = \bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty} {I^n M \cap N + I^{n+1} M \over I^{n+1} M},$$

called the *submodule* generated by the initial forms of the elements of $N$.

## Examples {#Examples}

Let *U* be the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field *k*; it is filtered by degree. The [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](wikipedia://en/762696) implies that $\operatorname{gr} U$ is a polynomial ring; in fact, it is the [coordinate ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) $k[\mathfrak{g}^*]$.

The associated graded algebra of a [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) is an exterior algebra; i.e., a [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) [degenerates](wikipedia://en/50650350) to an [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537).

:::

## Generalization to multiplicative filtrations {#Generalization_to_multiplicative_filtrations}

The associated graded can also be defined more generally for multiplicative [descending filtrations](wikipedia://en/726014) of *R* (see also [filtered ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_ring).) Let *F* be a descending chain of ideals of the form

$$R = I_0 \supset I_1 \supset I_2 \supset \dotsb$$

such that $I_jI_k \subset I_{j + k}$. The graded ring associated with this filtration is $\operatorname{gr}_F R = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty I_n/ I_{n+1}$. Multiplication and the initial form map are defined as above.
