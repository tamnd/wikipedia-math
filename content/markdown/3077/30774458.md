# Auslander–Buchsbaum formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Auslander–Buchsbaum formula"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), the **Auslander–Buchsbaum formula**, introduced by (txt, theorem 3.7), states that if *R* is a commutative [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) and *M* is a non-zero [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) *R*-module of finite [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension), then:

:::

$$\mathrm{pd}_R(M) + \mathrm{depth}(M) = \mathrm{depth}(R).$$

Here pd stands for the projective dimension of a module, and depth for the [depth](wikipedia://en/9770927) of a module.

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Auslander–Buchsbaum theorem implies that a Noetherian local ring is [regular](wikipedia://en/506959) if, and only if, it has finite [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739). In turn this implies that the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) of a regular local ring is regular.

If *A* is a local finitely generated [*R*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) (over a regular local ring *R*), then the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula implies that *A* is [Cohen–Macaulay](wikipedia://en/497407) if, and only if, pd<sub>*R*</sub>*A* = codim<sub>*R*</sub>*A*.

:::
