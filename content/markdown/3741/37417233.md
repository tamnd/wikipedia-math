# Betrothed numbers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Betrothed numbers"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **betrothed numbers** or **quasi-amicable numbers** are two positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) such that the [sum](wikipedia://en/61338) of the [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisor) of either number is one more than the value of the other number. In other words, (*m*, *n*) are a pair of betrothed numbers if *s*(*m*) = *n* + 1 and s(*n*) = *m* + 1, where s(*n*) is the [aliquot sum](wikipedia://en/12374274) of *n*: an equivalent condition is that σ(*m*) = σ(*n*) = *m* + *n* + 1, where σ denotes the [sum-of-divisors function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum-of-divisors_function).

:::

The first few pairs of betrothed numbers are: (48, 75), (140, 195), (1050, 1925), (1575, 1648), (2024, 2295), (5775, 6128), which (removing brackets) may be sorted by magnitude .

All known pairs of betrothed numbers have opposite [parity](wikipedia://en/143135). Any pair of the same parity must exceed 10<sup>13</sup>.

## Quasi-sociable numbers {#Quasi-sociable_numbers}

Quasi-sociable numbers or reduced sociable numbers are numbers whose [aliquot sums](wikipedia://en/12374274) minus one form a cyclic sequence that begins and ends with the same number. They are generalizations of the concepts of betrothed numbers and [quasiperfect numbers](wikipedia://en/321913). The first quasi-sociable sequences, or quasi-sociable chains, were discovered by Mitchell Dickerman in 1997:

- $$1215571544 = 2^3\cdot 11\cdot 13813313$$
- $$1270824975 = 3^2\cdot 5^2\cdot 7\cdot 19\cdot 42467$$
- $$1467511664 = 2^4\cdot 19\cdot 599\cdot 8059$$
- $$1530808335 = 3^3\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 1619903$$
- $$1579407344 = 2^4\cdot 31^2\cdot 59\cdot 1741$$
- $$1638031815 = 3^4\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 521\cdot 1109$$
- $$1727239544 = 2^3\cdot 2671\cdot 80833$$
- $$1512587175 = 3\cdot 5^2\cdot 11\cdot 1833439$$
