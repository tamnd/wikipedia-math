# Studentized range

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Studentized range"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **studentized range**, denoted *q*, is the difference between the largest and smallest data in a [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29) [normalized](wikipedia://en/2978513) by the [sample standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590). It is named after [William Sealy Gosset](wikipedia://en/105651) (who wrote under the pseudonym "*Student*"), and was introduced by him in 1927.[^o2] The concept was later discussed by Newman (1939),[^o3] Keuls (1952),[^o4] and [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974) in some unpublished notes. Its statistical distribution is the *[studentized range distribution](wikipedia://en/14261888)*, which is used for [multiple comparison](wikipedia://en/9444220) procedures, such as the single step procedure [Tukey's range test](wikipedia://en/14653762), the [Newman–Keuls method](wikipedia://en/23857094), and the Duncan's step down procedure, and establishing [confidence intervals](wikipedia://en/280911) that are still valid after [data snooping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_snooping) has occurred.[^o5]

:::

## Description {#Description}

The value of the **studentized range**, most often represented by the variable *q*, can be defined based on a random sample *x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub> from the *N*(0, 1) distribution of numbers, and another random variable *s* that is independent of all the *x<sub>i</sub>*, and *νs*<sup>2</sup> has a *χ*<sup>2</sup> distribution with *ν* degrees of freedom. Then

$$q _{n,\nu}= \frac{\max\{\,x_1,\ \dots, \ x_n\,\} - \min\{\,x_1,\ \dots,\ x_n\}}{s} = \max_{i,j=1, \dots, n} \left\{\frac{x_i - x_j}{s}\right\}$$

has the Studentized range distribution for *n* groups and *ν* degrees of freedom. In applications, the *x<sub>i</sub>* are typically the means of samples each of size *m*, *s*<sup>2</sup> is the [pooled variance](wikipedia://en/10910481), and the degrees of freedom are *ν* = *n*(*m* − 1).

The critical value of *q* is based on three factors:

1. *α* (the probability of rejecting a true [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis))
1. *n* (the number of observations or groups)
1. *ν* (the degrees of freedom used to estimate the [sample variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_variance))

## Distribution {#Distribution}

If *X*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *X*<sub>*n*</sub> are [independent identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_identically_distributed) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) that are [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462), the probability distribution of their studentized range is what is usually called the *studentized range distribution*. Note that the definition of *q* does not depend on the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) or the [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590) of the distribution from which the sample is drawn, and therefore its probability distribution is the same regardless of those parameters.

## *Studentization* {#Studentization}

Generally, the term *studentized* means that the variable's scale was adjusted by dividing by an [estimate](wikipedia://en/1565926) of a population [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590) (see also [studentized residual](wikipedia://en/609197)). The fact that the standard deviation is a *sample* standard deviation rather than the *population* standard deviation, and thus something that differs from one random sample to the next, is essential to the definition and the distribution of the *Studentized* data. The variability in the value of the *sample* standard deviation contributes additional uncertainty into the values calculated. This complicates the problem of finding the probability distribution of any statistic that is *studentized*.
