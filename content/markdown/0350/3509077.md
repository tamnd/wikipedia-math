# Ply (game theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ply"}

In two-or-more-player [sequential games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequential_game), a **ply** is one turn taken by one of the players. The word is used to clarify what is meant when one might otherwise say "turn".

:::

The word "turn" can be a problem since it means different things in different traditions. For example, in standard [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) terminology, one *move* consists of a turn by each player; therefore a ply in chess is a *half-move*. Thus, after 20 moves in a chess game, 40 plies have been completed—20 by white and 20 by black. In the game of [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_%28game%29), by contrast, a ply is the normal unit of counting moves; so for example to say that a game is *250 moves long* is to imply 250 plies.

In poker with *n* players the word "street" is used for a full betting round consisting of *n* plies; each dealt card may sometimes also be called a "street". For instance, in heads up [Texas hold'em](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_hold'em), a street consists of 2 plies, with possible plays being check/raise/call/fold: the first by the player at the big blind, and the second by the dealer, who posts the small blind; and there are 4 streets: preflop, flop, turn, river (the latter 3 corresponding to [community cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_cards)). The terms "half-street" and "half-street game" are sometimes used to describe, respectively, a single bet in a heads up game, and a simplified heads up poker game where only a single player bets.[^o2]

The word "ply" used as a synonym for "layer" goes back to the 15th century.[^o3] [Arthur Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Samuel_%28computer_scientist%29) first used the term in its game-theoretic sense in his seminal paper on [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) in [checkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkers) in 1959,[^o4] but with a slightly different meaning: the "ply", in Samuel's terminology, is actually the depth of analysis ("Certain expressions were introduced which we will find useful. These are: Ply, defined as the number of moves ahead, where a ply of two consists of one proposed move by the machine and one anticipated reply by the opponent"[^o5]).

In computing, the concept of a ply is important because one ply corresponds to one level of the [game tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_tree). The [Deep Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_%28chess_computer%29) [chess computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_computer) which defeated [Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov) in 1997 would typically search to a depth of between six and sixteen plies to a maximum of forty plies in some situations.[^o6]
