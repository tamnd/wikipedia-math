# Centre de Recherches Mathématiques

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centre de Recherches Mathématiques"}

The **Centre de recherches mathématiques** (**CRM**)[^o2] is the first mathematical research institute in Canada, located at the [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal).

:::

The CRM has ten research laboratories, one in each of: [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/1134), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [symbolic computation](wikipedia://en/23659805), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), [neuroimaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroimaging), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220).[^o3]

Each year it awards four of the main mathematical sciences prizes in Canada: the [CRM–Fields–PIMS prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRM–Fields–PIMS_prize),[^o4] which is the most prestigious award given in Canada in mathematics; the [Aisenstadt Prize](wikipedia://en/8534576),[^o5] awarded to a young outstanding Canadian mathematician; the CRM–SSC Prize,[^o6] awarded in collaboration with the [Statistical Society of Canada](wikipedia://en/15572327) to an exceptional young Canadian statistician; and the [CAP–CRM Prize in Theoretical and Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAP–CRM_Prize_in_Theoretical_and_Mathematical_Physics) awarded in collaboration with the [Canadian Association of Physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Association_of_Physicists) in recognition of exceptional achievements in theoretical and mathematical physics.
