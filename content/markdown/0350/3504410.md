*Image: Tetragonal trapezohedron.stl.* 3D model of a tetragonal trapezohedron.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **tetragonal trapezohedron**, or **[deltohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deltohedron)**, is the second in an infinite series of [trapezohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezohedra), which are [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) to the [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851). It has eight faces, which are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [kites](wikipedia://en/84840), and is dual to the [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045).

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

### In mesh generation {#In_mesh_generation}

This shape has been used as a test case for hexahedral [mesh generation](wikipedia://en/3050716),[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15] simplifying an earlier test case posited by mathematician Robert Schneiders in the form of a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) with its boundary subdivided into 16 quadrilaterals. In this context the tetragonal trapezohedron has also been called the **cubical octahedron**,[^o13] **quadrilateral octahedron**,[^o14] or **octagonal spindle**,[^o15] because it has eight quadrilateral faces and is uniquely defined as a combinatorial polyhedron by that property.[^o13] Adding four cuboids to a mesh for the cubical octahedron would also give a mesh for Schneiders' pyramid.[^o12] As a simply-connected polyhedron with an even number of quadrilateral faces, the cubical octahedron can be decomposed into topological cuboids with curved faces that meet face-to-face without subdividing the boundary quadrilaterals,[^o11][^o15][^o16] and an explicit mesh of this type has been constructed.[^o14] However, it is unclear whether a decomposition of this type can be obtained in which all the cuboids are convex polyhedra with flat faces.[^o11][^o15]

### In art {#In_art}

A tetragonal trapezohedron appears in the upper left as one of the polyhedral "stars" in [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127)'s 1948 wood engraving [Stars](wikipedia://en/1170784).

:::

## Spherical tiling {#Spherical_tiling}

The tetragonal trapezohedron also exists as a [spherical tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_tiling), with 2 vertices on the poles, and alternating vertices equally spaced above and below the equator.

*Image: Spherical tetragonal trapezohedron.svg.*

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

The *tetragonal trapezohedron* is first in a series of dual snub polyhedra and tilings with [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) V3.3.4.3.*n*.
