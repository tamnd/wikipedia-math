# Congruum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Congruum"}

*Image: Congruum.svg.* The two right triangles with leg and [hypotenuse](wikipedia://en/251366) (7,13) and (13,17) have equal third sides of length $\sqrt{120}$. The square of this side, 120, is a congruum: it is the difference between consecutive values in the [arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/168389) of squares 7<sup>2</sup>, 13<sup>2</sup>, 17<sup>2</sup>. Equivalently, the two [annuli](wikipedia://en/356158) between the three yellow [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) have equal areas, $\pi$ times the congruum.

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **congruum** (plural *congrua*) is the difference between successive [square numbers](wikipedia://en/213919) in an [arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/168389) of three squares. The **congruum problem** is the problem of finding squares in arithmetic progression and their associated congrua. It can be formalized as a [Diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109).

:::

[Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) solved the congruum problem by finding a parameterized formula for generating all congrua, together with their associated arithmetic progressions. According to this formula, each congruum is four times the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a [Pythagorean triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_triangle), a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420) whose sides are [integers](wikipedia://en/14563). Congrua are also closely connected with [congruent numbers](wikipedia://en/2334769), the areas of right triangles whose sides are [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). Every congruum is a congruent number, and every congruent number is a congruum multiplied by the square of a rational number.

Fibonacci claimed without [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) that it is impossible for a congruum to be a square number. This was later proven by [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) as [Fermat's right triangle theorem](wikipedia://en/29559494).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A *congruum* is defined to be any number that can form the [difference](wikipedia://en/1102297) between successive square numbers in an [arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/168389) of three squares. That is, if $x^2$, $y^2$, and $z^2$ (for integers $x$, $y$, and $z$) are three square numbers that are equally spaced apart from each other, then the spacing between them, $y^2-x^2=z^2-y^2$, is called a congruum.[^o14]

The *congruum problem* is the problem of finding squares in arithmetic progression and their associated congrua.[^o14] It can be formalized as a [Diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109): find integers $x$, $y$, and $z$ such that $y^2 - x^2 = z^2 - y^2.$ When this equation is satisfied, both sides of the equation equal the congruum.[^o11]

## History {#History}

The congruum problem was originally studied and solved by [Islamic mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematician) of the 10th century, mainly [Abū Ja'far al-Khāzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_Ja'far_al-Khāzin).[^o7][^o8]

In 1225, the congruum problem was part of a mathematical tournament held by [Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), and answered correctly at that time by [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949), who recorded his work on this problem in his *[Book of Squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Squares)*,[^o9] without crediting al-Khāzin.

Fibonacci was already aware that it is impossible for a congruum to itself be a square, but did not give a satisfactory proof of this fact.[^o10] [Geometrically](wikipedia://en/18973446), this means that it is not possible for the pair of legs of a Pythagorean triangle to be the leg and hypotenuse of another Pythagorean triangle. A proof was eventually given by [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966), and the result is now known as [Fermat's right triangle theorem](wikipedia://en/29559494). Fermat also [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138), and [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) proved, that there is no sequence of four squares in arithmetic progression.[^o11][^o12]

## Parameterized solution {#Parameterized_solution}

The congruum problem may be solved by choosing two distinct positive integers $m$ and $n$ (with $m>n$); then the number $4mn(m^2-n^2)$ is a congruum. The middle square of the associated arithmetic progression of squares is $(m^2+n^2)^2$, and the other two squares may be found by adding or subtracting the congruum. Additionally, multiplying a congruum by a square number produces another congruum, whose progression of squares is multiplied by the same factor. All solutions arise in one of these two ways.[^o14] For instance, the congruum 96 can be constructed by these formulas with $m=3$ and $n=1$, while the congruum 216 is obtained by multiplying the smaller congruum 24 by the square number 9.

An equivalent formulation of this solution, given by [Bernard Frénicle de Bessy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Frénicle_de_Bessy), is that for the three squares in arithmetic progression $x^2$, $y^2$, and $z^2$, the middle number $y$ is the hypotenuse of a [Pythagorean triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_triangle) and the other two numbers $x$ and $z$ are the difference and sum respectively of the triangle's two legs.[^o15] The congruum itself is four times the area of the same Pythagorean triangle. The example of an arithmetic progression with the congruum 96 can be obtained in this way from a right triangle with side and hypotenuse lengths 6, 8, and 10.

## Relation to congruent numbers {#Relation_to_congruent_numbers}

A [congruent number](wikipedia://en/2334769) is defined as the area of a right triangle with rational sides. Because every congruum can be obtained (using the parameterized solution) as the area of a Pythagorean triangle, it follows that every congruum is congruent. Every congruent number is a congruum multiplied by the square of a rational number.[^o17] However, testing whether a number is a congruum is much easier than testing whether a number is congruent. For the congruum problem, the parameterized solution reduces this testing problem to checking a finite set of parameter values, as the two parameters $m$ and $n$ must both [divide](wikipedia://en/49492) the given number. In contrast, for the congruent number problem, a finite testing procedure is known only conjecturally, via [Tunnell's theorem](wikipedia://en/6144888), under the assumption that the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) is true.[^o18]
