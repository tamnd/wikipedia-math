# Robert Riley (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Riley"}

**Robert F. Riley** (December 22, 1935–March 4, 2000[^o2]) was an American mathematician. He is known for his work in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774) using computational tools and [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), being one of the inspirations for [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s later breakthroughs in [3-dimensional topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-dimensional_topology).[^n1]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Riley earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) in 1957; shortly thereafter he dropped out of the graduate program and went on to work in industry, eventually moving to Amsterdam in 1966. In 1968 he took a temporary position at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton).[^n2] He defended his Ph.D. at this institution in 1980, under the nominal direction of David Singerman. For the next two years he occupied a postdoctoral position in [Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulder,_Colorado) where William Thurston was employed at the time, before moving on to Binghamton University as a professor.[^n2]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Riley's research was in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), especially in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), where he mostly studied [representations](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [knot groups](wikipedia://en/1164126). Early on, following work of [Ralph Fox](wikipedia://en/1343918), he was interested in morphisms to finite groups. Later on in Southampton, considering $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$-representations sending peripheral elements to parabolics led him to discover the [hyperbolic structure](wikipedia://en/1237700) on the complement of the [figure-eight knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-eight_knot) and some others.[^n3][^n4] This was one of the few examples of hyperbolic 3-manifolds that were available at the time, and as such it was one of the motivations which led to William Thurston's [geometrisation conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrisation_conjecture), which includes as a particular case a criterion for a knot complement to support a hyperbolic structure.[^n5] One notable feature of Riley's work is that it relied much on the assistance of a computer.[^n6]
