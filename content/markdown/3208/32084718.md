# Ian Agol

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Ian Agol"}

**Ian Agol** (; born May 13, 1970) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who deals primarily with the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [three-dimensional manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Agol graduated with B.S. in mathematics from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1992 and obtained his Ph.D. in 1998 from the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego). His faculty advisor was [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970) and his thesis was *Topology of Hyperbolic 3-Manifolds*.[^o6] He is a professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley)[^o7] and a former professor at the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago).[^o8]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In 2004, Agol proved the [Marden tameness conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marden_tameness_conjecture), a conjecture of [Albert Marden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Marden).[^o10] It states that a hyperbolic 3-manifold with finitely generated [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the interior of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) 3-manifold. The conjecture was also independently proven by [Danny Calegari](wikipedia://en/21907908) and [David Gabai](wikipedia://en/6129609), and implies the [Ahlfors measure conjecture](wikipedia://en/31197103).[^o10]

In 2012, he announced a proof of the [virtually Haken conjecture](wikipedia://en/4692183), which was published a year later.[^o11] The conjecture (now theorem) states that every aspherical 3-manifold is finitely covered by a [Haken manifold](wikipedia://en/236567).

In 2022, he posted on the [ArXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) a proof of [Cameron Gordon](wikipedia://en/4695115)'s 1981 conjecture on [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) saying that ribbon concordance forms a partial ordering on the set of knots.[^o12][^o13]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Agol, Calegari, and Gabai received the 2009 [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) for their proof of the Marden tameness conjecture.[^o10]

In 2005, Agol was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o15] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16]

In 2013, Agol was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075), along with [Daniel Wise](wikipedia://en/38193678).[^o17]

In 2015, he was awarded the 2016 [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933), "for spectacular contributions to [low dimensional topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_dimensional_topology) and [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), including work on the solutions of the [tameness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tameness_conjecture), [virtually Haken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_Haken_conjecture) and [virtual fibering](wikipedia://en/2823145) conjectures."[^o18]

In 2016, he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o19][^o20]

## Personal {#Personal}

His identical twin brother, [Eric Agol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Agol),[^o22][^o23][^o24] is an astronomy professor at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle).[^o25]
