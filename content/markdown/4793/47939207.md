# Colin P. Rourke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Colin P. Rourke"}

**Colin Rourke** (1 January 1943 – 18 December 2024) was a British mathematician who worked in [PL topology](wikipedia://en/2174184), [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774), [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology). He was an emeritus professor at the Mathematics Institute at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) and a founding editor of the journals *[Geometry & Topology](wikipedia://en/2862181)* and *[Algebraic & Geometric Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_&_Geometric_Topology)*, published by [Mathematical Sciences Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Publishers), where he was a permanent member of its board of directors.[^o2]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Rourke obtained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1965 under the direction of [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415).[^o4] Most of his early work was carried out in collaboration with Brian Sanderson. They solved a number of outstanding problems: the provision of [normal bundles](wikipedia://en/2370200) for the PL category (which they called "Block bundles")[^o5], and the non-existence of normal [microbundles](wikipedia://en/19072600) (top and PL)[^o6]. In collaboration with Sandro Buoncristiano, he geometric interpretation for all (generalized) [homology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theories)[^o7].

Rourke was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1970 at [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice).[^o8][^o9] From 1976 to 1981, he was acting professor of pure mathematics at the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University) (on secondment from Warwick) where he oversaw the rewriting of the pure mathematics course.

Rourke died in 2024, aged 81.[^o10]

## Poincaré Conjecture {#Poincaré_Conjecture}

In September 1986 Rourke and his graduate student, Eduardo Rêgo (later at [University of Oporto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oporto)), claimed to have solved the [Poincaré Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_Conjecture).[^o13] Reaction by the topological community at the time was highly skeptical, and during a special seminar at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) given by Rourke, a fatal error was found in the proof.[^o14][^o15]

The part of the proof that was salvaged was a constructive characterization and enumeration of [Heegaard diagrams](wikipedia://en/864418) for [homotopy 3-spheres](wikipedia://en/1202074).[^o16] A later-discovered algorithm of [J. Hyam Rubinstein](wikipedia://en/4983922) and [Abigail Thompson](wikipedia://en/44841077) identified when a homotopy 3-sphere was a topological 3-sphere.[^o17] Together, the two algorithms provided an algorithm that would find a counterexample to the Poincaré Conjecture, if one existed.[^o18]

In 2002, [Martin Dunwoody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Dunwoody) posted a claimed proof of the Poincaré Conjecture.[^o19] Rourke identified its fatal flaw.[^o20][^o21][^o22]

:::

## Geometry & Topology {#Geometry_&_Topology}

In 1996, dissatisfied with the rapidly rising fees charged by the major publishers of mathematical research journals, Rourke decided to start his own journal, and was assisted by [Robion Kirby](wikipedia://en/2932424), John Jones, and Brian Sanderson. The resulting journal, *[Geometry & Topology](wikipedia://en/2862181)*, became a leading journal in its field. It was joined in 1998 by a proceedings and monographs series, *Geometry & Topology Monographs*, and in 2000 by a sister journal, *[Algebraic & Geometric Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_&_Geometric_Topology)*. Rourke wrote the software and managed these publications until around 2005 when he cofounded [Mathematical Sciences Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Publishers) (with Rob Kirby) to take over the running.

## Cosmology {#Cosmology}

In 2000, Rourke started taking an interest in [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology) and posted his first paper on the topic on the arXiv preprint server in 2003. He collaborated with Robert MacKay, also of [Warwick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_University), with papers on [redshift](wikipedia://en/26262), [gamma-ray bursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma-ray_burst) and natural observer fields. He proposed a new paradigm for the universe, one that involves neither [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter) nor a [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116). This new paradigm was presented in "A new paradigm for the universe" (see bibliography).

The main idea is that the principal objects in the universe form a spectrum unified by the presence of a massive or hypermassive [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650). These objects are variously called [quasars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasar), [active galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_galactic_nucleus) and [spiral galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_galaxy). The key to understanding their dynamics is [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839) and the key tool is a proper formulation of "[Mach's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach's_principle)" using Sciama's ideas. This is added to standard [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) in the form of hypothesized "inertial drag fields" which carry the forces that realize Mach's principle. This formulation solves the causal problems that occur in a naive formulation of the principle.

The new approach provides an explanation for the observed dynamics of spiral galaxies without needing [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter) and gives a framework that fits the observations of [Halton Arp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halton_Arp) and others that show that [quasars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasar) typically exhibit [instrinsic redshift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrinsic_redshift). An accessible version was published by World Scientific in it its "Knots and everything" series no 71, titled "The geometry of the universe".
