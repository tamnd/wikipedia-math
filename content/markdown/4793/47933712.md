# Fixed-point subring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fixed-point subring"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **fixed-point subring** $R^f$ of an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) *f* of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is the [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of the [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738) of *f*, that is,

:::

$$R^f = \{ r \in R \mid f(r) = r \}.$$

More generally, if *G* is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) [acting](wikipedia://en/12781) on *R*, then the subring of *R*

$$R^G = \{ r \in R \mid g \cdot r = r \ \text{ for all } \ g \in G \}$$

is called the **fixed subring** or, more traditionally, the **ring of invariants** under $G$. If *S* is a set of automorphisms of *R*, the elements of *R* that are fixed by the elements of *S* form the ring of invariants under the group generated by *S*. In particular, the fixed-point subring of an automorphism *f* is the ring of invariants of the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) generated by *f*.

In [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), when *R* is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) and *G* is a group of field automorphisms, the fixed ring is a [subfield](wikipedia://en/11634) called the **fixed field** of the automorphism group; see [Fundamental theorem of Galois theory](wikipedia://en/1947635).

Along with a [module of covariants](wikipedia://en/42992981), the **ring of invariants** is a central object of study in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762). Geometrically, the rings of invariants are the coordinate rings of (affine or projective) [GIT quotients](wikipedia://en/41139312) and they play fundamental roles in the constructions in [geometric invariant theory](wikipedia://en/1639003).

::: {#o2 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example**: Let $R = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) in *n* variables. The [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) S<sub>*n*</sub> acts on *R* by permuting the variables. Then the ring of invariants $R^G = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]^{\operatorname{S}_n}$ is the [ring of symmetric polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_symmetric_polynomials). If a [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) *G* acts on *R*, then the [fundamental theorem of invariant theory](wikipedia://en/52945125) describes the generators of *R*<sup>*G*</sup>.

[Hilbert's fourteenth problem](wikipedia://en/1695231) asks whether the ring of invariants is finitely generated or not (the answer is affirmative if *G* is a reductive algebraic group by Nagata's theorem.) The finite generation is easily seen for a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) *G* acting on a [finitely generated algebra](wikipedia://en/11985661) *R*: since *R* is [integral](wikipedia://en/9478630) over *R*<sup>*G*</sup>,[^o3] the [Artin–Tate lemma](wikipedia://en/45586929) implies *R*<sup>*G*</sup> is a finitely generated algebra. The answer is negative for some [unipotent groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipotent_group).

Let *G* be a finite group. Let *S* be the symmetric algebra of a finite-dimensional [*G*-module](wikipedia://en/26068776). Then *G* is a reflection group if and only if $S$ is a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) (of finite [rank](wikipedia://en/326454#Definition)) over *S*<sup>*G*</sup> (Chevalley's theorem).

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), if *G* is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and $\mathfrak{g} = \operatorname{Lie}(G)$ its [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), then each principal *G*-bundle on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* determines a [graded](wikipedia://en/103094) [algebra homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_homomorphism) (called the [Chern–Weil homomorphism](wikipedia://en/970447))

$$\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^G \to \operatorname{H}^{2*}(M; \mathbb{C})$$

where $\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]$ is the [ring of polynomial functions](wikipedia://en/38608533) on $\mathfrak{g}$ and *G* acts on $\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]$ by [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188).

:::
