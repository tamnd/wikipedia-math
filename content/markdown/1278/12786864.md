# Tetradecahedron

*Image: Space-filling tetrakaidecahedron.png.* A tetradecahedron with D<sub>2d</sub>-symmetry, existing in the [Weaire–Phelan structure](wikipedia://en/2795211)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tetradecahedron"}

A **tetradecahedron** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with 14 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). There are numerous topologically distinct forms of a tetradecahedron, with many constructible entirely with [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) faces.

:::

A tetradecahedron is sometimes called a **tetrakaidecahedron**.[^o3] No difference in meaning is ascribed.[^o5] The Greek word *[kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_%28conjunction%29)* means 'and'. There is evidence that mammalian [epidermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermis) cells are shaped like flattened tetrakaidecahedra, an idea first suggested by [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin).[^o6] The polyhedron can also be found in soap bubbles and in [sintered ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintering), due to its ability to [tesselate](wikipedia://en/6008654) in 3D space.[^o7][^o8]

## Convex {#Convex}

There are 1,496,225,352 topologically distinct *convex* tetradecahedra, excluding mirror images, having at least 9 vertices.[^o10] (Two polyhedra are "topologically distinct" if they have intrinsically different arrangements of faces and vertices, such that it is impossible to distort one into the other simply by changing the lengths of edges or the angles between edges or faces.)

## Examples {#Examples}

An incomplete list of forms includes:

Tetradecahedra having all [regular polygonal](wikipedia://en/333306) faces (all exist in irregular-faced forms as well):

- [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847):
  - [Cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) (8 [triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangles), 6 [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29))
  - [Truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529) (8 triangles, 6 [octagons](wikipedia://en/314575))
  - [Truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654) (6 squares, 8 [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733))
- [Prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851):
  - [Dodecagonal prism](wikipedia://en/3929468) (12 squares, 2 [dodecagons](wikipedia://en/508486))
  - [Hexagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860125) (12 triangles, 2 hexagons)
- [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034):
  - J<sub>18</sub>: [Elongated triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1196215) (4 triangles, 9 squares, 1 hexagon)
  - J<sub>27</sub>: [Triangular orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1196250) (8 triangles, 6 squares)
  - J<sub>51</sub>: [Triaugmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/728019) (14 triangles)
  - J<sub>55</sub>: [Parabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177980) (8 triangles, 4 squares, 2 hexagons)
  - J<sub>56</sub>: [Metabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177982) (8 triangles, 4 squares, 2 hexagons)
  - J<sub>65</sub>: [Augmented truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/1200120) (8 triangles, 3 squares, 3 hexagons)
  - J<sub>86</sub>: [Sphenocorona](wikipedia://en/1142750) (12 triangles, 2 squares)
  - J<sub>91</sub>: [Bilunabirotunda](wikipedia://en/1170286) (8 triangles, 2 squares, 4 pentagons)

Tetradecahedra having at least one irregular face:

- [Heptagonal bipyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptagonal_bipyramid) (14 triangles) (see *[Dipyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipyramid)*)
- [Heptagonal trapezohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptagonal_trapezohedron) (14 [kites](wikipedia://en/84840)) (see *[Trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/719845)*)
- [Tridecagonal pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tridecagonal_pyramid) (13 triangles, 1 regular [tridecagon](wikipedia://en/654601)) (see *[Pyramid (geometry)](wikipedia://en/3307045)*)
- [Dissected regular icosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissected_regular_icosahedron) (the vertex figure of the [grand antiprism](wikipedia://en/4058047)) (12 equilateral triangles and 2 [trapezoids](wikipedia://en/201826))
- [Hexagonal truncated trapezohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_truncated_trapezohedron): (12 [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014), 2 hexagons) Includes an optimal space-filling shape in foams (see *[Weaire–Phelan structure](wikipedia://en/2795211)*) and in the crystal structure of [clathrate hydrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clathrate_hydrate) (see illustration, next to label 5<sup>12</sup>6<sup>2</sup>)
- [Hexagonal bifrustum](wikipedia://en/3521756) (12 trapezoids, 2 hexagons)
- The [British £1 coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_pound_%28British_coin%29) in circulation from 2017 – with twelve edges and two faces – is an irregular dodecagonal prism, when one disregards the edging and relief features.[^o13]
