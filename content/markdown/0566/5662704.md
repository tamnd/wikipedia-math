# Dietrich Stoyan

*Image: StoyanD.jpg.* Dietrich Stoyan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dietrich Stoyan"}

**Dietrich Stoyan** (born November 26, 1940, [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin)) is a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who made contributions to [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), [stochastic geometry](wikipedia://en/22037813), and [spatial statistics](wikipedia://en/9467349).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Stoyan studied mathematics at [Technical University Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Dresden); applied research at [Deutsches Brennstoffinstitut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Brennstoffinstitut) [Freiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiberg,_Saxony), 1967 PhD, 1975 Habilitation. Since 1976 at [TU Bergakademie Freiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiberg_University_of_Mining_and_Technology), Rektor of that university in 1991—1997. His statistical research of the diffusion of euro coins in Germany and Europe after the introduction of the [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_euro#Launch) in 2002 drew public attention.[^o3] In 2024, he criticized together with Sung Nok Chiu the statistical data analysis methods employed in a paper by a team of researchers led by [Michael Worobey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Worobey) that had concluded that the [origin of the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_SARS-CoV-2) was a [market in Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanan_Seafood_Wholesale_Market). Worobey stated that he was working on a response.[^o4][^o5]

Stoyan is an honorary doctor of the Technical University of Dresden (2000) and the University of Jyväskylä (2004). He was a member of the [German Academy of Sciences at Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_at_Berlin) (1990), which disappeared in 1991. At present he is a member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) (since 1992), the Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Sciences (since 2000) and the German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina (since 2002). He is also a Fellow of the Institute of Mathematical Statistics (since 1997).

## Research {#Research}

### Queueing Theory {#Queueing_Theory}

Qualitative theory, in particular inequalities, for [queueing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queuing_theory) and related [stochastic models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_model). The books *Comparison Methods for Queues and other Stochastic Models* (1983) and *Comparison Methods for Stochastic Models and Risks* (2002) report on the results. The work goes back to 1969 when he discovered the [monotonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonicity) of the GI/G/1 waiting times with respect to the convex order.

### Stochastic Geometry {#Stochastic_Geometry}

Stereological formulae, applications for marked point processes, development of stochastic models. Successful joint work with [Joseph Mecke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Mecke) led to the first exact proof of the fundamental stereological formulae.

A book entitled *Stochastic Geometry and its Applications* (2013) reports on these results. Its 3rd edition is the key reference for applied stochastic geometry.

### Spatial Statistics {#Spatial_Statistics}

Statistical methods for point processes, random sets and many other random geometrical structures such as fibre processes. Results can be found in the 2013 book on stochastic geometry and in the book, *Fractals, Random Shapes and Point Fields* (1994).

Stoyan is very active in demonstrating non-mathematicians and non-statisticians the potential of statistical and stochastic geometrical methods. He published many papers in journals of physics, materials science, forestry, and geology. One topic of particular interest was random packings of hard spheres. He co-organized together with Klaus Mecke conferences where physicists, geometers and statisticians met. See the books *Statistical Physics and Spatial Statistics* (2000) and *Morphology of Condensed Matter* (2002).
