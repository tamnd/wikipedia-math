# Shinzo Watanabe

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shinzo Watanabe"}

**Shinzō Watanabe** (渡辺 信三 Watanabe Shinzō, born 23 December 1935) is a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who has made fundamental contributions to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes) and [stochastic differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_differential_equations).[^o3] He is revered as a luminary in the field of modern probability theory and [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001). The pioneering book “Stochastic Differential Equations and Diffusion Processes” he wrote with [Nobuyuki Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobuyuki_Ikeda) has attracted a lot of researchers into the area and is known as the “Ikeda-Watanabe” for researchers in the field of [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis). He serves as the editor of [Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Publishing) Mathematics.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Watanabe received his bachelor's degree from [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) in 1958 and completed his Ph.D. under [Kiyosi Itô](wikipedia://en/1329240) in 1963. Watanabe subsequently became a professor at Kyoto University. After that, he moved to [Ritsumeikan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritsumeikan_University) and hold the full-time faculty position there until his retirement. He was also a visiting professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and participated in the organizing committees of international Japanese/Soviet seminars on probability theory. He has one daughter Shiori Watanabe.

## Scientific contributions {#Scientific_contributions}

Watanabe has made many important contributions to stochastic analysis and the theory of stochastic processes. In an important work with H. Kunita, he extended K. Ito's theory of stochastic integration, initially developed by Ito for Markov processes, to square integrable martingales.[^o7] This theory, known as the *Kunita-Watanabe extension* is based on the crucial *[Kunita–Watanabe inequality](wikipedia://en/33744493)* for the stochastic integral.[^o8]

Another important contribution of Watanabe has been to use the [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875) to establish a theory of generalized functionals on [Wiener space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_space), by analogy to [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485)'s theory of distributions, and apply this theory to obtain expansions of heat kernels.[^o9]

Watanabe also made important contributions to the study of multidimensional diffusion processes with boundary conditions[^o10] and continuous-time branching processes.[^o11]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1989 he received the Autumn Prize of the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786).[^o13]

In 1983 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) (*Excursion point processes and diffusion*). In 1996 he received the [Japan Academy Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy_Prize_%28academics%29) in Mathematics.[^o14]
