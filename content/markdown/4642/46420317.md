# Client-side encryption

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Client-side encryption"}

**Client-side encryption** is the [cryptographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic) technique of [encrypting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encrypting) data on the sender's side, before it is transmitted to a [server](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_%28computing%29) such as a [cloud storage service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_storage_service).[^o21] Client-side encryption features an encryption key that is not available to the service provider, making it difficult or impossible for service providers to decrypt hosted data. Client-side encryption allows for the creation of applications whose providers cannot access the data its users have stored, thus offering a high level of privacy.[^o21]

:::

Applications utilizing client-side encryption are sometimes marketed under the misleading or incorrect term *"zero-knowledge"*,[^o22] but this is a misnomer, as the term [zero-knowledge](wikipedia://en/450541) describes something entirely different in the context of cryptography.

## Details {#Details}

Client-side encryption seeks to eliminate the potential for data to be viewed by service providers (or third parties that compel service providers to deliver access to data), client-side encryption ensures that data and files that are stored in the cloud can only be viewed on the client-side of the exchange. This prevents data loss and the unauthorized disclosure of private or personal files, providing increased peace of mind for its users.[^o21]

Current recommendations by industry professionals as well as academic scholars offer great vocal support for developers to include client-side encryption to protect the confidentiality and integrity of information. [^o3][^o4] [^o5]

- [Tresorit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tresorit)[^o12]
- [MEGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_%28service%29)[^o13]
- [Cryptee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptee)[^o14]
- [Cryptomator](wikipedia://en/75068654)[^o23]

### Examples of services that optionally support client-side encryption {#Examples_of_services_that_optionally_support_client-side_encryption}

- [Apple iCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICloud) offers optional client-side encryption when "Advanced Data Protection for iCloud" is enabled.[^o15][^o16]
- [Google Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Drive),[^o18] [Google Docs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Docs),[^o18] [Google Meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Meet),[^o19] [Google Calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Calendar),[^o19] and [Gmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmail)[^o19] — However, as of Jul 2024, optional client-side encryption features are only available to paid users.[^o20]

### Examples of services that do not support client-side encryption {#Examples_of_services_that_do_not_support_client-side_encryption}

- [Dropbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dropbox_%28service%29)[^o17]

### Examples of client-side encrypted services that no longer exist {#Examples_of_client-side_encrypted_services_that_no_longer_exist}
