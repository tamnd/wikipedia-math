# Genus g surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Genus g surface"}

In mathematics, a **genus *g* surface** (also known as a ***g*-torus** or ***g*-holed torus**) is a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) formed by the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of *g* distinct [tori](wikipedia://en/74800): the interior of a disk is removed from each of *g* distinct tori and the boundaries of the *g* many disks are identified (glued together), forming a *g*-torus. The [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) of such a surface is *g*.

:::

A genus *g* surface is a [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). The [classification theorem for surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865#Classification_of_closed_surfaces) states that every [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) two-dimensional manifold is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to either the sphere, the connected sum of tori, or the connected sum of [real projective planes](wikipedia://en/411325).

## Definition of genus {#Definition_of_genus}

The genus of a connected orientable surface is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) representing the maximum number of cuttings along non-intersecting [closed simple curves](wikipedia://en/89246#Topological_curve) without rendering the resultant [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) disconnected.[^o3] It is equal to the number of [handles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handle_%28mathematics%29) on it. Alternatively, it can be defined in terms of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) *χ*, via the relationship *χ* = 2 − 2*g* for [closed surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865#Closed_surfaces), where *g* is the genus.

The genus (sometimes called the demigenus or Euler genus) of a connected non-orientable closed surface is a positive integer representing the number of [cross-caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-cap) attached to a sphere. Alternatively, it can be defined for a closed surface in terms of the Euler characteristic *χ*, via the relationship *χ* = 2 − *g*, where *g* is the non-orientable genus.

## Genus 0 {#Genus_0}

An [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) surface of genus zero is the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) ***S*<sup>*2*</sup>**. Another surface of genus zero is the [disc](wikipedia://en/329542).

## Genus 1 {#Genus_1}

A genus one orientable surface is the ordinary torus. A non-orientable surface of genus one is the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350).[^o6]

[Elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) can be identified with genus 1 surfaces. The formulation of elliptic curves as the embedding of a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) follows naturally from a property of [Weierstrass's elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass's_elliptic_functions) that allows elliptic curves to be obtained from the quotient of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) by a [lattice](wikipedia://en/1605743).[^o7]

## Genus 2 {#Genus_2}

The term **double torus** is occasionally used to denote a genus 2 surface.[^o11] A non-orientable surface of genus two is the [Klein bottle](wikipedia://en/17412).

The [Bolza surface](wikipedia://en/12576332) is the most symmetric [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 2, in the sense that it has the largest possible [conformal](wikipedia://en/50627) [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772).[^o12]

:::

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Genus three surface"}

## Genus 3 {#Genus_3}

The term **triple torus** is also occasionally used to denote a genus 3 surface.[^o11]

The [Klein quartic](wikipedia://en/390976) is a compact [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) $3$ with the highest possible order [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) for compact Riemann surfaces of genus 3. It has $168$ orientation-preserving automorphisms, and $336$ automorphisms altogether.

:::
