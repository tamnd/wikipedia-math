# Chinese multiplication table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chinese multiplication table"}

*Image: Qinghuajian, Suan Biao.jpg.* The [Tsinghua Bamboo Slips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_Bamboo_Slips), containing the world's earliest [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) [multiplication table](wikipedia://en/57122), dated 305 BC during the [Warring States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States) period

The **Chinese [multiplication table](wikipedia://en/57122)** is the first requisite for using the [Rod calculus](wikipedia://en/11919629) for carrying out multiplication, division, the extraction of square roots, and the solving of equations based on place value decimal notation. It was known in China as early as the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period), and survived through the age of the [abacus](wikipedia://en/655); pupils in elementary school today still must memorise it.[^o2]

:::

The Chinese multiplication table consists of eighty-one terms. It was often called the **nine-nine table**, or simply **nine-nine**, because in ancient times, the nine nine table started with 9 × 9:[^o3] nine nines beget eighty-one, eight nines beget seventy-two ... seven nines beget sixty three, *etc.* two ones beget two. In the opinion of [Wang Guowei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Guowei), a noted scholar, the nine-nine table probably started with nine because of the "worship of nine" in ancient China; the emperor was considered the "nine five supremacy" in the [Book of Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ching). See also Numbers in Chinese culture#Nine.

It is also known as **nine-nine song** (or poem),[^o3] as the table consists of eighty-one lines with four or five Chinese characters per lines; this thus created a constant [metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_%28poetry%29) and render the multiplication table as a poem. For example, 9 × 9 = 81 would be rendered as "九九八十一", or "nine nine eighty one", with the world for "begets" "得" implied. This makes it easy to learn by heart.[^o2] A shorter version of the table consists of only forty-five sentences, as terms such as "nine eights beget seventy-two" are identical to "eight nines beget seventy-two" so there is no need to learn them twice. When the [abacus](wikipedia://en/655) replaced the counting rods in the Ming dynasty, many authors on the abacus advocated the use of the full table instead of the shorter one. They claimed that memorising it without needing a moment of thinking makes abacus calculation much faster.

The existence of the Chinese multiplication table is evidence of an early positional decimal system: otherwise a much larger multiplication table would be needed with terms beyond 9×9.

## The Nine-nine table in Chinese literature {#The_Nine-nine_table_in_Chinese_literature}

*Image: Ming suanpan.JPG.* Nine nine song in [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) [Cheng Dawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_Dawei) *[Suanfa tongzong](wikipedia://en/52418196)* Volume II

Many Chinese classics make reference to the nine-nine table:

- [Zhoubi Suanjing](wikipedia://en/6131715): "nine nine eighty one"
- [Guan Zi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guan_Zi) has sentences of the form "three eights beget twenty four, three sevens beget twenty-one"
- [The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207): "[Fu Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_Xi) invented the art of nine-nine".
- In [Huainanzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huainanzi), there were eight sentences: "nine nines beget eighty one", "eight nines beget seventy two", all the way to "two nines beget eighteen".
- A nine-nine table manuscript was discovered in [Dun Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dun_Huang).[^o3]
- [Xia Houyang's Computational Canons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_Houyang's_Computational_Canons): "To learn the art of multiplication and division, one must understand nine-nine".
- The Song dynasty author Hong Zhai's Notebooks said: "three threes as nine, three fours as twelve, two eights as sixteen, four fours as sixteen, three nines as twenty seven, four nines as thirty six, six sixes as thirty six, five eights as forty, five nines as forty five, seven nines as sixty three, eight nines as seventy two, nine nines as eighty one". This suggests that the table has begun with the smallest term since the Song dynasty.
- [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) mathematician [Yang Hui](wikipedia://en/1716898)'s mathematics text book: *Suan fa tong bian ben mo*, meaning "You must learn nine nine song from one one equals one to nine nine eighty one, in small to large order"
- [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) mathematician [Zhu Shijie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Shijie)'s *Suanxue qimeng* (Elementary mathematics): "one one equals one, two by two equals four, one by three equals three, two by three equals six, three by three equals nine, one by four equals four... nine by nine equals eight one"

## Archeological artifacts {#Archeological_artifacts}

- At the end of the 19th century, archeologists unearthed pieces of written bamboo script from the Han dynasty in [Xin Jiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xin_Jiang). One such Han dynasty bamboo script, from Liusha, is a remnant of the nine-nine table. It starts with nine: nine nine eighty one, eight nine seventy two, seven nine sixty three, eight eight sixty four, seven eight fifty six, six eight forty eight, ... two two gets four, altogether 1100 Chinese words.
- In 2002, Chinese archeologists unearthed a written wood script from a two-thousand-year-old site from the [Warring States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States), on which was written: "four eight thirty two, five eight forty, six eight forty eight." This is the earliest artifact of the nine-nine table that has been unearthed, indicating that the nine-nine table, as well as a positional decimal system, had appeared by the [Warring States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States) period.
- Tsinghua Bamboo Slips Calculation Table, is an ancient calculator artifact from the Warring States period in 305 BC. It is included in the "Tsinghua University Collection of Warring States Bamboo Slips (Part IV)," predating the previously discovered Liye Qin Bamboo Slips and Zhangjiashan Han Bamboo Slips nine-nine tables by a century.
- In 2023, a bamboo slip from the 4th century BC, containing a multiplication formula, was found in a Jingzhou tomb in Hubei Province, China. The formula was deciphered using infrared scanning, revealing calculations such as "five times seven is thirty plus five, four times seven is twenty plus eight, three times seven is twenty plus one." As of December 2023, this represents the earliest discovery of a nine-nine table artifact.
- The nine-nine table was transmitted to Japan, and appeared in a Japanese primary mathematics book in the 10th century, beginning with 9×9.
