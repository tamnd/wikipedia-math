# Paul Émile Appell

***M. P. Appell****is the same person: it stands for Monsieur Paul Appell*.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Émile Appell"}

**Paul Émile Appell** (27 September 1855 in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) – 24 October 1930 in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989)) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [Rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) of the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). [Appell polynomials](wikipedia://en/1649947) and Appell's equations of motion are named after him, as is rue Paul Appell in the [14th arrondissement of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_arrondissement_of_Paris) and the minor planet [988 Appella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/988_Appella).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Paul Appell entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1873. He was elected to the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1892.

In 1895, he became a Professor at the [École Centrale Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Centrale_Paris). Between 1903 and 1920 he was [Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) of the Faculty of Science of the University of Paris, then Rector of the University of Paris from 1920 to 1925.

Appell was the President of the [Société astronomique de France (SAF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_astronomique_de_France), the French astronomical society, from 1919 to 1921.[^o4]

His daughter Marguerite Appell (1883–1969), who married the mathematician [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161), is known as a novelist under her pen-name [Camille Marbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Marbo).

Appell was an atheist.[^o5] He was awarded [Order of the White Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_White_Eagle_%28Serbia%29).[^o6] and was also elected to honorary membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society).[^o7]

## Work {#Work}

He worked first on [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) in the line of [Chasles](wikipedia://en/2252716), then on [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). Appell was the editor of the collected works of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740). Jules Drach was co-editor of the first volume.[^o9]

### Appell series {#Appell_series}

He introduced a set of four hypergeometric series *F*<sub>1</sub>, *F*<sub>2</sub>, *F*<sub>3</sub>, *F*<sub>4</sub> of two variables, now called [Appell series](wikipedia://en/19783560), that generalize [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss)'s [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series).

He established the set of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) of which these functions are solutions, and found formulas and expressions of these series in terms of [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series) of one variable. In 1926, with Professor [Joseph-Marie Kampé de Fériet](wikipedia://en/20796560), he authored a treatise on generalized hypergeometric series.

### Mechanics {#Mechanics}

In mechanics, he proposed an alternative formulation of analytical mechanics known as [Appell's equation of motion](wikipedia://en/11717900).

He discovered a physical interpretation of the [imaginary](wikipedia://en/14554) [period](wikipedia://en/76848) of the [doubly periodic function](wikipedia://en/3643495) whose restriction to [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) arguments describes the motion of an ideal [pendulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendulum_%28mathematics%29).
