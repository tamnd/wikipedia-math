# Orthogonal wavelet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal wavelet"}

An **orthogonal wavelet** is a [wavelet](wikipedia://en/50903) whose associated [wavelet transform](wikipedia://en/857564) is [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221). That is, the inverse wavelet transform is the [adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_of_an_operator) of the wavelet transform. If this condition is weakened one may end up with [biorthogonal wavelets](wikipedia://en/4555949).

:::

## Basics {#Basics}

The [scaling function](wikipedia://en/50903#Scaling_function) is a [refinable function](wikipedia://en/4557519). That is, it is a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377), called the **refinement equation** (**twin-scale relation** or **dilation equation**):

$$\phi(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} a_k\phi(2x-k),$$

where the sequence $(a_0,\dots, a_{N-1})$ of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is called a scaling sequence or scaling mask. The wavelet proper is obtained by a similar [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632),

$$\psi(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{M-1} b_k\phi(2x-k),$$

where the sequence $(b_0,\dots, b_{M-1})$ of real numbers is called a wavelet sequence or wavelet mask.

A necessary condition for the *orthogonality* of the wavelets is that the scaling sequence is orthogonal to any shifts of it by an even number of coefficients:

$$\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} a_n a_{n+2m}=2\delta_{m,0},$$

where $\delta_{m,n}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890).

In this case there is the same number *M=N* of coefficients in the scaling as in the wavelet sequence, the wavelet sequence can be determined as $b_n=(-1)^n a_{N-1-n}$. In some cases the opposite sign is chosen.

## Vanishing moments, polynomial approximation and smoothness {#Vanishing_moments,_polynomial_approximation_and_smoothness}

A necessary condition for the existence of a solution to the refinement equation is that there exists a positive integer *A* such that (see [Z-transform](wikipedia://en/171589)):

$$(1+Z)^A | a(Z):=a_0+a_1Z+\dots+a_{N-1}Z^{N-1}.$$

The maximally possible power *A* is called **polynomial approximation order** (or pol. app. power) or **number of vanishing moments**. It describes the ability to represent polynomials up to degree *A*-1 with linear combinations of [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) translates of the scaling function.

In the biorthogonal case, an approximation order *A* of $\phi$ corresponds to *A* **vanishing moments** of the dual wavelet $\tilde\psi$, that is, the [scalar products](wikipedia://en/157093) of $\tilde\psi$ with any polynomial up to degree *A-1* are zero. In the opposite direction, the approximation order *Ã* of $\tilde\phi$ is equivalent to *Ã* vanishing moments of $\psi$. In the orthogonal case, *A* and *Ã* coincide.

A sufficient condition for the existence of a scaling function is the following: if one decomposes $a(Z)=2^{1-A}(1+Z)^Ap(Z)$, and the estimate

$$1\le\sup_{t\in[0,2\pi]} \left |p(e^{it}) \right |<2^{A-1-n},$$

holds for some $n\in\mathbb{N}$, then the refinement equation has a *n* times continuously differentiable solution with compact support.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- Suppose $p(Z) =1$ then $a(Z)=2^{1-A}(1+Z)^A$, and the estimate holds for *n*=*A*-2. The solutions are Schoenbergs [B-splines](wikipedia://en/21834) of order *A*-1, where the (*A*-1)-th derivative is piecewise constant, thus the (*A*-2)-th derivative is [Lipschitz-continuous](wikipedia://en/51731). *A*=1 corresponds to the index function of the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324).
- *A*=2 and *p* linear may be written as

$$a(Z)=\frac14(1+Z)^2((1+Z)+c(1-Z)).$$

Expansion of this degree 3 polynomial and insertion of the 4 coefficients into the orthogonality condition results in $c^2=3.$ The positive root gives the scaling sequence of the D4-wavelet, see below.

:::
