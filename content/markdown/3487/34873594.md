# Exsphere (polyhedra)

*Image: Exesfera cubo.png.* Exsphere of a cube, tangent to one of its faces and to the extensions of four others

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exsphere"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **exsphere** of a face of a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299) is the sphere outside the polyhedron which touches the face and the planes defined by extending the adjacent faces outwards. It is tangent to the face externally and tangent to the adjacent faces internally.

:::

It is the 3-dimensional equivalent of the [excircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle).

The sphere is more generally well-defined for any face which is a regular polygon and delimited by faces with the same dihedral angles at the shared edges. Faces of semi-regular polyhedra often have different types of faces, which define exspheres of different size with each type of face.

## Parameters {#Parameters}

The exsphere touches the face of the regular polyedron at the center of the incircle of that face. If the exsphere radius is denoted $r_{\operatorname{ex}}$, the radius of this incircle $r_{\text{in}}$ and the [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) between the face and the extension of the adjacent face $\delta$, the center of the exsphere is located from the viewpoint at the middle of one edge of the face by bisecting the dihedral angle. Therefore

$$\tan\frac{\delta}{2} = \frac{r_{\mathrm{ex}}}{r_{\mathrm{in}}}.$$

$\delta$ is the 180-degree complement of the internal face-to-face angle.

### Tetrahedron {#Tetrahedron}

Applied to the geometry of the [Tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) of edge length $a$, we have an [incircle radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) $r_{\text{in}} = a/(2\sqrt{3})$ (derived by dividing twice the face area $(a^{2}\sqrt{3})/4$ through the perimeter $3a$), a dihedral angle $\delta = \pi - \arccos(1/3)$, and in consequence $r_{\operatorname{ex}} = a/\sqrt{6}$.

### Cube {#Cube}

The radius of the exspheres of the 6 faces of the [Cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is the same as the radius of the inscribed sphere, since $\delta$ and its complement are the same, 90 degrees.

### Icosahedron {#Icosahedron}

The dihedral angle applicable to the [Icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) is derived by considering the coordinates of two triangles with a common edge, for [example](wikipedia://en/43856084#Cartesian_coordinates) one face with vertices at

$$(0,-1,g), (g,0,1), (0,1,g),$$

the other at

$$(1,-g,0), (g,0,1), (0,-1,g),$$

where $g$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). Subtracting vertex coordinates defines edge vectors,

$$(g,1,1-g), (-g,1,g-1)$$

of the first face and

$$(g-1,g,1), (-g,-1,g-1)$$

of the other. [Cross products](wikipedia://en/157092) of the edges of the first face and second face yield (not normalized) face [normal vectors](wikipedia://en/173224)

$$(2g-2,0,2g) \sim (g-1,0,g)$$

of the first and

$$(g^2-g+1,-g-(g-1)^2,1-g+g^2) = (2,-2,2)\sim (1,-1,1)$$

of the second face, using $g^{2}=1+g$. The [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) between these two face normals yields the cosine of the dihedral angle,

$$\cos\delta = \frac{(g-1)\cdot 1+g\cdot 1}{\sqrt{(g-1)^2+g^2} \sqrt{3}} =\frac{2g-1}{3} =\frac{\surd 5}{3}\approx 0.74535599.$$

$$\therefore \delta \approx 0.72973 \,\mathrm{rad} \approx 41.81^\circ$$

$$\therefore \tan\frac{\delta}{2} = \frac{\sin\delta}{1+\cos\delta} =\frac{2}{3+\surd 5} \approx 0.3819660$$

For an icosahedron of edge length $a$, the incircle radius of the triangular faces is $r_{\text{in}} = a/(2\sqrt{3})$, and finally the radius of the 20 exspheres

$$r_{\mathrm{ex}} = \frac{a}{(3+\sqrt{5})\sqrt 3} \approx 0.1102641 a.$$
