# Lie theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lie theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) ( ) initiated lines of study involving integration of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [automorphism groups](wikipedia://en/56661772) and [contact](wikipedia://en/396068) of [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) that have come to be called **Lie theory**.[^o2] For instance, the latter subject is [Lie sphere geometry](wikipedia://en/16646944). This article addresses his approach to transformation groups, which is one of the [areas of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areas_of_mathematics), and was worked out by [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824) and [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274).

:::

The foundation of Lie theory is the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/44612290) relating [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) to [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) which is called the [Lie group–Lie algebra correspondence](wikipedia://en/43302095). The subject is part of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) since Lie groups are [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219). Lie groups evolve out of the identity (1) and the [tangent vectors](wikipedia://en/191999) to [one-parameter subgroups](wikipedia://en/754851) generate the Lie algebra. The structure of a Lie group is implicit in its algebra, and the structure of the Lie algebra is expressed by [root systems](wikipedia://en/277087) and [root data](wikipedia://en/6825924).

Lie theory has been particularly useful in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) since it describes the standard transformation groups: the [Galilean group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean_group), the [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489), the [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487) and the [conformal group of spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_group_of_spacetime).

## Elementary Lie theory {#Elementary_Lie_theory}

The [one-parameter groups](wikipedia://en/754851) are the first instance of Lie theory. The [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) case arises through [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/9613) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). Other one-parameter groups occur in the [split-complex number](wikipedia://en/893559) plane as the [unit hyperbola](wikipedia://en/26205122)

$$\lbrace \exp(j t) = \cosh(t) + j \sinh(t) : t \in R \rbrace,\quad j^2 = +1$$

and in the [dual number](wikipedia://en/42169) plane as the line $\lbrace \exp(\varepsilon t) = 1 + \varepsilon t : t \in R \rbrace \quad \varepsilon^2 = 0.$ In these cases the Lie algebra parameters have names: [angle](wikipedia://en/1196), [hyperbolic angle](wikipedia://en/1139981), and [slope](wikipedia://en/29368). These species of angle are useful for providing [polar decompositions](wikipedia://en/1369241#Alternative_planar_decompositions) which describe the planar [subalgebras](wikipedia://en/45239) of 2 x 2 real matrices.

There is a classical 3-parameter Lie group and algebra pair: the [quaternions of unit length](wikipedia://en/2644621) which can be identified with the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792). Its Lie algebra is the subspace of [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) vectors. Since the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) ij − ji = 2k, the Lie bracket in this algebra is twice the [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) of ordinary [vector analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_analysis).

Another elementary 3-parameter example is given by the [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518) and its Lie algebra. Standard treatments of Lie theory often begin with the [classical groups](wikipedia://en/10172878).

## History and scope {#History_and_scope}

Early expressions of Lie theory are found in books composed by [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) with [Friedrich Engel](wikipedia://en/5686283) and [Georg Scheffers](wikipedia://en/31778680) from 1888 to 1896.

In Lie's early work, the idea was to construct a theory of *continuous groups*, to complement the theory of [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536) that had developed in the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), in the hands of [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) and [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740). The initial application that Lie had in mind was to the theory of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). On the model of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) and [polynomial equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation), the driving conception was of a theory capable of unifying, by the study of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741), the whole area of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753).

According to [Thomas W. Hawkins Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_W._Hawkins_Jr.), it was [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) who made Lie theory what it is:

While Lie had many fertile ideas, Cartan was primarily responsible for the extensions and applications of his theory that have made it a basic component of modern mathematics. It was he who, with some help from [Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), developed the seminal, essentially algebraic ideas of [Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824) into the theory of the structure and representation of [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587) that plays such a fundamental role in present-day Lie theory. And although Lie envisioned applications of his theory to geometry, it was Cartan who actually created them, for example through his theories of symmetric and generalized spaces, including all the attendant apparatus ([moving frames](wikipedia://en/439082), exterior [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519), etc.)[^o7]

## Lie's three theorems {#Lie's_three_theorems}

In his work on transformation groups, Sophus Lie proved three theorems relating the groups and algebras that bear his name. The first theorem exhibited the basis of an algebra through [infinitesimal transformations](wikipedia://en/658068).[^o10] The second theorem exhibited [structure constants](wikipedia://en/20285142) of the algebra as the result of [commutator products](wikipedia://en/7193#Ring_theory) in the algebra.[^o10] The [third theorem](wikipedia://en/10875756) showed these constants are anti-symmetric and satisfy the [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370).[^o10] As Robert Gilmore wrote:

Lie's three theorems provide a mechanism for constructing the Lie algebra associated with any Lie group. They also characterize the properties of a Lie algebra. ¶ The converses of Lie’s three theorems do the opposite: they supply a mechanism for associating a Lie group with any finite dimensional Lie algebra ... [Taylor's theorem](wikipedia://en/51714) allows for the construction of a canonical analytic structure function φ(β,α) from the Lie algebra. ¶ These seven theorems – the three theorems of Lie and their converses, and Taylor's theorem – provide an essential equivalence between Lie groups and algebras.[^o10]

:::

## Aspects of Lie theory {#Aspects_of_Lie_theory}

Lie theory is frequently built upon a study of the classical [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885). Special branches include [Weyl groups](wikipedia://en/296332), [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004), and [buildings](wikipedia://en/275015). The classical subject has been extended to [Groups of Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490).

In 1900 [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) challenged Lie theorists with his [Fifth Problem](wikipedia://en/152760) presented at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Paris.
