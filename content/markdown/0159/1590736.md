# Statistical genetics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical genetics"}

**Statistical genetics** is a scientific field concerned with the development and application of statistical methods for drawing inferences from [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) data. The term is most commonly used in the context of [human genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genetics). Research in statistical genetics generally involves developing theory or methodology to support research in one of three related areas:

:::

- [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268) - Study of evolutionary processes affecting genetic variation between organisms
- [genetic epidemiology](wikipedia://en/16017459) - Studying effects of genes on diseases[^o2]
- [quantitative genetics](wikipedia://en/297382) - Studying the effects of genes on 'normal' [phenotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotypes)

Statistical geneticists tend to collaborate closely with [geneticists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticist), [molecular biologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology), [clinicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_genetics) and [bioinformaticians](wikipedia://en/4214). Statistical genetics is a type of [computational biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology).

## History {#History}

In the early 20th century, R. A. Fisher's 1918 work helped reconcile Mendelian and biometric approaches to continuous traits and introduced the infinitesimal model, which became foundational in quantitative genetics.[^o4][^o5]
