# Dilation (metric space)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dilation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **dilation** is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ from a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) $M$ into itself that satisfies the identity

:::

$$d(f(x),f(y))=rd(x,y)$$

for all points $x, y \in M$, where $d(x, y)$ is the distance from $x$ to $y$ and $r$ is some positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438).[^o2]

In [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), such a dilation is a [similarity](wikipedia://en/39377) of the space.[^o3] Dilations change the size but not the shape of an object or figure.

Every dilation of a Euclidean space that is not a [congruence](wikipedia://en/39330) has a unique [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738)[^o4] that is called the *center of dilation*.[^o5] Some congruences have fixed points and others do not.[^o6]
