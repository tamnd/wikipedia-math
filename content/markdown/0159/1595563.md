# Rodolphe Radau

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rodolphe Radau"}

**Jean Charles Rodolphe Radau** (22 January 1835 – 21 December 1911) was a German [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) at the *Revue des deux Mondes* for most of his life. He was the co-founder of the [Bulletin Astronomique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_Astronomique).

:::

Radau was born in Angerburg, [Province of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Prussia) (now [Węgorzewo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Węgorzewo) in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)), and after studying in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) and working on the [Three-body problem](wikipedia://en/1208420), he moved to Paris to collaborate with other scientists. In 1871 he was awarded a Ph.D. in recognition of his work in mathematics.

Radau won the [Prix Damoiseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Damoiseau) of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1892 working on planetary perturbations in the motion of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon). This work was of such a high quality that he was elected to the Academy in 1897.

A [crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radau_%28crater%29) on [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars) is named in Radau's honor.

His publications include the *Wonders of Acoustics* ([1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1867)).
