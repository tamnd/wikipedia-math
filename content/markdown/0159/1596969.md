# Categorification

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Categorification"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **categorification** is the process of replacing [set-theoretic](wikipedia://en/27553) [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) with [category-theoretic](wikipedia://en/5869) analogues. Categorification, when done successfully, replaces [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) with [categories](wikipedia://en/53759), [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) with [functors](wikipedia://en/10987), and [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) with [natural isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/45022) of functors satisfying additional properties. The term was coined by [Louis Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Crane).[^o2][^o3]

:::

The reverse of categorification is the process of *decategorification*. Decategorification is a systematic process by which [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) objects in a category are identified as [equal](wikipedia://en/90446). Whereas decategorification is a straightforward process, categorification is usually much less straightforward. In the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), [modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_over_a_ring) over specific algebras are the principal objects of study, and there are several frameworks for what a categorification of such a module should be, e.g., so called (weak) abelian categorifications.[^o4]

Categorification and decategorification are not precise mathematical procedures, but rather a class of possible analogues. They are used in a similar way to words like '[generalization](wikipedia://en/12746)', and not like '[sheafification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheafification)'.[^o5]

## Examples {#Examples}

One form of categorification takes a structure described in terms of sets, and interprets the sets as [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of objects in a category. For example, the set of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) can be seen as the set of [cardinalities](wikipedia://en/6174) of [finite sets](wikipedia://en/11742) (and any two sets with the same cardinality are isomorphic). In this case, operations on the set of natural numbers, such as addition and multiplication, can be seen as carrying information about [coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889) and [products](wikipedia://en/237868) of the [category of finite sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_finite_sets). Less abstractly, the idea here is that manipulating sets of actual objects, and taking coproducts (combining two sets in a union) or products (building arrays of things to keep track of large numbers of them) came first. Later, the concrete structure of sets was abstracted away – taken "only up to isomorphism", to produce the abstract theory of arithmetic. This is a "decategorification" – categorification reverses this step.

Other examples include [homology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) gave the modern formulation of homology as the [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) of certain [free abelian groups](wikipedia://en/247296) by categorifying the notion of a [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262).[^n1] See also [Khovanov homology](wikipedia://en/2639906) as a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008).

An example in [finite group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_group_theory) is that the [ring of symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/364460) is categorified by the [category of representations](wikipedia://en/55872661) of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901). The decategorification map sends the [Specht module](wikipedia://en/5122533) indexed by partition $\lambda$ to the [Schur function](wikipedia://en/41513534) indexed by the same partition,

$$S^\lambda \,\stackrel{\varphi}{\to}\; s_\lambda,$$

essentially following the [character](wikipedia://en/293418) map from a favorite basis of the associated [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) to a representation-theoretic favorite basis of the ring of [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359). This map reflects how the structures are similar; for example

$$\left[\operatorname{Ind}_{S_m \otimes S_n}^{S_{n+m}}(S^{\mu} \otimes S^{\nu})\right] \qquad\text{and}\qquad s_\mu s_\nu$$

have the same decomposition numbers over their respective bases, both given by [Littlewood–Richardson coefficients](wikipedia://en/16004359).

## Abelian categorifications {#Abelian_categorifications}

For an abelian category $\mathcal{B}$, let $K(\mathcal{B})$ be the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) of $\mathcal{B}$.

Let $A$ be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) which is [free as an abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296), and let $\mathbf{a} = \{a_i\}_{i \in I}$ be a basis of $A$ such that the multiplication is positive in $\mathbf{a}$, i.e.

$a_i a_j = \sum_{k} c_{ij}^k a_k,$ with $c_{ij}^k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.$

Let $B$ be an $A$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). Then a (weak) abelian categorification of $(A, \mathbf{a}, B)$ consists of an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) $\mathcal{B}$, an isomorphism $\phi: K(\mathcal{B}) \to B$, and exact [endofunctors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor) $F_i: \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{B}$ such that

1. the functor $F_i$ lifts the action of $a_i$ on the module $B$, i.e. $\phi [F_i] = a_i \phi$, and
1. there are isomorphisms $F_i F_j \cong \bigoplus_{k} F_k^{c_{ij}^k},$, i.e. the composition $F_i F_j$ decomposes as the direct sum of functors $F_k$ in the same way that the product $a_i a_j$ decomposes as the linear combination of basis elements $a_k$.
