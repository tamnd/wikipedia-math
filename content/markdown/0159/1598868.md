# Digit sum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Digit sum"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **digit sum** of a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) in a given [number base](wikipedia://en/701207) is the sum of all its [digits](wikipedia://en/280582). For example, the digit sum of the [decimal number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_number) $9045$ would be $9 + 0 + 4 + 5 = 18.$

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $n$ be a natural number. We define the **digit sum** for base $b > 1$, $F_b : \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{N}$ to be the following:

$$F_b(n) = \sum_{i=0}^{k} d_i$$

where $k = \lfloor \log_{b}{n} \rfloor$ is one less than the number of digits in the number in base $b$, and

$$d_i = \frac{n \bmod{b^{i+1}} - n \bmod b^i}{b^i}$$

is the value of each digit of the number.

For example, in [base 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_10), the digit sum of 84001 is $F_{10}(84001) = 8 + 4 + 0 + 0 + 1 = 13.$

For any two bases $2 \leq b_1 < b_2$ and for sufficiently large natural numbers $n,$

$\sum_{k=0}^n F_{b_1}(k) < \sum_{k=0}^n F_{b_2}(k).$[^o4]

The sum of the base 10 digits of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) 0, 1, 2, ... is given by in the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004). (Borwein Borwein 1992) use the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of this [integer sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) (and of the analogous sequence for [binary](wikipedia://en/238686) digit sums) to derive several rapidly [converging](wikipedia://en/1743431) [series](wikipedia://en/15287) with [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) and [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325) sums.[^o6]

:::

## Extension to negative integers {#Extension_to_negative_integers}

The digit sum can be extended to the negative integers by use of a [signed-digit representation](wikipedia://en/1181818) to represent each integer.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The number of n-digit numbers with digit sum q can be calculated using:

$$f(n, q) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } n = 1 \\ f(n, 9n-q+1) & \text{if } q > \lceil\frac{9n}{2}\rceil \\ \sum_{i=\max(q-9,1)}^{q} f(n-1, i) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

For any natural number $n$ in base $b$, the digit sum is at most:

$d$ ≤ $(b-1)$ × log<sub>b</sub>(n)

The maximum for a given digit length is given by b<sup>n</sup>−1, where all $n$ digits are each equal to $b-1$, the highest digit in base $b$.

:::

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The concept of a decimal digit sum is closely related to, but not the same as, the [digital root](wikipedia://en/8286632), which is the result of repeatedly applying the digit sum operation until the remaining value is only a single digit. The decimal digital root of any non-zero integer will be a number in the range 1 to 9, whereas the digit sum can take any value. Digit sums and digital roots can be used for quick [divisibility tests](wikipedia://en/991210): a natural number is [divisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility) by 3 or 9 [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its digit sum (or digital root) is divisible by 3 or 9, respectively. For divisibility by 9, this test is called the **rule of nines** and is the basis of the [casting out nines](wikipedia://en/575641) technique for checking calculations.

Digit sums are also a common ingredient in [checksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checksum) algorithms to check the arithmetic operations of early computers.[^o12] Earlier, in an era of hand calculation, (Edgeworth 1888) suggested using sums of 50 digits taken from mathematical [tables of logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_logarithms) as a form of [random number generation](wikipedia://en/2736939); if one assumes that each digit is random, then by the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406), these digit sums will have a random distribution closely approximating a [Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution).[^o14]

The digit sum of the [binary representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_representation) of a number is known as its [Hamming weight](wikipedia://en/1127884) or population count; algorithms for performing this operation have been studied, and it has been included as a built-in operation in some computer architectures and some [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015). These operations are used in computing applications including [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869), and [computer chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_chess).

[Harshad numbers](wikipedia://en/641307) are defined in terms of divisibility by their digit sums, and [Smith numbers](wikipedia://en/631045) are defined by the equality of their digit sums with the digit sums of their [prime factorizations](wikipedia://en/15491).
