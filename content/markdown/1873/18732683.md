# X-ray transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="X-ray transform"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **X-ray transform** (also called **ray transform**[^o2] or **John transform**) is an [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912) introduced by [Fritz John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_John) in 1938[^o3] that is one of the cornerstones of modern [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336). It is very closely related to the [Radon transform](wikipedia://en/503378), and coincides with it in two dimensions. In higher dimensions, the X-ray transform of a function is defined by integrating over [lines](wikipedia://en/946975) rather than over [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) as in the Radon transform. The X-ray transform derives its name from X-ray [tomography](wikipedia://en/385523) (used in [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan)) because the X-ray transform of a function *ƒ* represents the attenuation data of a tomographic scan through an inhomogeneous medium whose density is represented by the function *ƒ*. [Inversion](wikipedia://en/203956) of the X-ray transform is therefore of practical importance because it allows one to reconstruct an unknown density *ƒ* from its known attenuation data.

:::

In detail, if *ƒ* is a [compactly supported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support) [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) on the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, then the X-ray transform of *ƒ* is the function *Xƒ* defined on the set of all lines in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> by

$$Xf(L) = \int_L f = \int_{\mathbf{R}} f(x_0+t\theta)dt$$

where *x*<sub>0</sub> is an initial point on the line and *θ* is a unit vector in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> giving the direction of the line *L*. The latter integral is not regarded in the oriented sense: it is the integral with respect to the 1-dimensional [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) on the Euclidean line *L*.

The X-ray transform satisfies an [ultrahyperbolic wave equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrahyperbolic_wave_equation) called [John's equation](wikipedia://en/4154269).

The Gaussian or ordinary [hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/2055223) can be written as an X-ray transform (Gelfand Gindikin Graev 2003, 2.1.2).
