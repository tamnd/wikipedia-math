# George Edward Hughes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="George Edward Hughes"}

**George Edward Hughes** (8 June 1918 – 4 March 1994) was an Irish-born New Zealand [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) whose principal scholarly works were concerned with [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) and [medieval philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_philosophy).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hughes was born on 8 June 1918 in [Waterford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterford), Ireland. His English parents George James Hughes and Gertrude Sparks moved to Scotland in the early 1920s, as a result of the [Irish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_War_of_Independence). George graduated MA with First Class Honours in Philosophy and English, and then in pure Philosophy, from the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow). He then studied for a year at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), before being called back to Glasgow as an [assistant lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer). Subsequently, he held [lectureships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) at the [University College of South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University) at [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff), and then the [University College of North Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_University) at [Bangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor,_Gwynedd). In 1951 he was appointed to the first Chair in Philosophy at the [Victoria University of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Wellington) in New Zealand, a position from which he retired in 1984. He died in [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington) on 4 March 1994.

## Career {#Career}

Notable influences on Hughes' philosophical development included [John Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wisdom) and [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), from whom he took classes at Cambridge; [J. L. Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._L._Austin), a leading exponent of [ordinary language philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_language_philosophy); and [Arthur Prior](wikipedia://en/308764), with whom he found much in common when they met in New Zealand.

His early interests were in [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics) and the [philosophy of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_religion), but he is most widely known for books on modal logic co-authored with his colleague and former student [Max Cresswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Cresswell). In 1968 they published *An Introduction to Modal Logic*, the first modern textbook in the area. This book, which has been translated into German, Italian, Japanese and Spanish, was influential in introducing many generations of students and researchers to [Kripke semantics](wikipedia://en/911960), a mathematical theory of meaning that revolutionised the study of modal logics and led to applications ranging from the semantics of [natural languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing) to [reasoning](wikipedia://en/31103500) about the behaviour of [computer programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program). [Vaughan Pratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughan_Pratt), the creator of [dynamic logic](wikipedia://en/31631871), has written in reference to his own motivation that "a weekend with Hughes and Cresswell convinced me that a most harmonious union between modal logic and programs was possible".[^o5]

Hughes' other special interest was in [medieval philosophical logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_philosophy), where his main projects were the preparation of [philosophical commentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commentary_of_a_philosophical_text) on [Latin manuscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_literature) of [John Buridan](wikipedia://en/172466) and [Paul of Venice](wikipedia://en/2364425), as well as English translations of the originals.

He was also a priest in the Anglican ([Episcopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Episcopal_Church)) Church, having been ordained in [Bangor Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_Cathedral) in 1950. At that time there was a need for clergy who could conduct services in both Welsh and English, so the then Bishop of Bangor ordained several men whom he considered suitable, but who had not had the usual theological training. Hughes had a flair for languages that enabled him to quickly learn how to pronounce the set words of the service even though he was not a Welsh speaker.

He was married with five children. His wife Beryl Hughes (1920 – 2015), an historian, taught in the History Department of Victoria University for 25 years, and was one of the founders of the [Women's Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Studies) programme there.
