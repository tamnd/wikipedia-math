# Nassif Ghoussoub

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nassif Ghoussoub"}

**Nassif A. Ghoussoub** is a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the fields of non-linear [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_%28mathematics%29) and [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). He is a Professor of Mathematics and a Distinguished University Scholar at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Ghoussoub was born to Lebanese parents in Western Africa (now [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali)).[^o3][^o5]

He completed his doctorat 3ème cycle (PhD) in 1975, and a Doctorat d'Etat in 1979 at the [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University), where his advisors were [Gustave Choquet](wikipedia://en/17301193) and Antoine Brunel.

## Career {#Career}

Ghoussoub completed his post-doctoral fellowship at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) during 1976–77. He then joined the University of British Columbia, where he currently holds a position of Professor of Mathematics and a Distinguished University Scholar.[^o3][^o5][^o7][^o8] Ghoussoub is known for his work in functional analysis, non-linear analysis, and partial differential equations.[^o8]

He was vice-president of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) from 1994 to 1996, the founding director of the [Pacific Institute for the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/34978739) (PIMS) for the period 1996–2003, the co-editor-in-chief of the Canadian Journal of Mathematics during 1993–2002, a co-founder of the MITACS Network of Centres of Excellence, and is the founder and scientific director (2001 - 2020) of the [Banff International Research Station](wikipedia://en/39151817) (BIRS).[^o5][^o8] In 1994, Ghoussoub became a fellow of the Royal Society of Canada, and in 2012, a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9]

Ghoussoub has been awarded multiple awards and distinctions, including the Coxeter-James prize in 1990, and the Jeffrey-Williams prize in 2007. He holds honorary doctorates from the Université Paris-Dauphine (France), and the University of Victoria (Canada). He was awarded the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal in 2012, and appointed to the [Order of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Canada) in 2015, with the grade of officer for contributions to mathematics, research, and education.[^o5][^o10]

In 2018, Ghoussoub was elected a faculty representative on the University of British Columbia's Board of Governors.[^o11] He will serve until February 29, 2020.[^o11] Ghoussoub has previously served two consecutive terms in this role from 2008 to 2014.[^o11][^o12][^o8]

Ghoussoub's scholarly work has been cited over 5,900 times and has an h-index of 40.[^o13]
