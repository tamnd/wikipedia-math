# Brun sieve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brun sieve"}

In the field of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Brun sieve** (also called **Brun's pure sieve**) is a technique for estimating the size of "sifted sets" of [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) which satisfy a set of conditions which are expressed by [congruences](wikipedia://en/48167#Modular_arithmetic). It was developed by [Viggo Brun](wikipedia://en/40647) in 1915 and later generalized to the [fundamental lemma of sieve theory](wikipedia://en/18826584) by others.

:::

## Description {#Description}

In terms of [sieve theory](wikipedia://en/1035915) the Brun sieve is of *combinatorial type*; that is, it derives from a careful use of the [inclusion–exclusion principle](wikipedia://en/342684).

Let $A$ be a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) of positive integers. Let $P$ be some set of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). For each prime $p$ in $P$, let $A_p$ denote the set of elements of $A$ that are divisible by $p$. This notation can be extended to other integers $d$ that are products of distinct primes in $P$. In this case, define $A_d$ to be the intersection of the sets $A_p$ for the prime factors $p$ of $d$. Finally, define $A_1$ to be $A$ itself. Let $z$ be an arbitrary positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438). The object of the sieve is to estimate:

$$S(A,P,z) = \biggl\vert A \setminus \bigcup_{p \in P\atop p \le z} A_p \biggr\vert ,$$

where the notation $|X|$ denotes the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of a set $X$, which in this case is just its number of elements. Suppose in addition that $|A_d|$ may be estimated by

$$\left\vert A_d \right\vert = \frac{w(d)}{d} |A| + R_d,$$

where $w$ is some [multiplicative function](wikipedia://en/20059), and $R_d$ is some [error function](wikipedia://en/177111). Let

$$W(z) = \prod_{p \in P\atop p \le z} \left(1 - \frac{w(p)}{p} \right) .$$

### Brun's pure sieve {#Brun's_pure_sieve}

This formulation is from [Cojocaru & Murty](https://books.google.com/books?id=1swo9Yf3d2YC), Theorem 6.1.2. With the notation as above, suppose that

- $| R_d | \leq w(d)$ for any squarefree $d$ composed of primes in $P$;
- $w(p) < C$ for all $p$ in $P$;
- There exist constants $C, D, E$ such that, for any positive real number $z$, $$\sum_{p \in P\atop p \le z} \frac{w(p)}{p} < D \log\log z + E.$$

Then

$$S(A,P,z) = X \cdot W(z) \cdot \left({1 + O\left((\log z)^{-b \log b}\right)}\right) + O\left(z^{b \log\log z}\right)$$

where $X$ is the cardinal of $A$, $b$ is any positive integer and the $O$ invokes [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578). In particular, letting $x$ denote the maximum element in $A$, if $\log z< c\log x/\log\log x$ for a suitably small $c$, then

$$S(A,P,z) = X \cdot W(z) (1+o(1)) .$$

## Applications {#Applications}

- [Brun's theorem](wikipedia://en/2796181): the sum of the reciprocals of the [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997) converges;
- [Schnirelmann's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnirelmann's_theorem): every even number is a sum of at most $C$ primes (where $C$ can be taken to be 6);
- There are infinitely many pairs of integers differing by 2, where each of the member of the pair is the product of at most 9 primes;
- Every even number is the sum of two numbers each of which is the product of at most 9 primes.

The last two results were superseded by [Chen's theorem](wikipedia://en/1400840), and the second by [Goldbach's weak conjecture](wikipedia://en/147164) ($C=3$).

:::
