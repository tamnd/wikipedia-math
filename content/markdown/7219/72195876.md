# Indira Chatterji

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Indira Chatterji"}

*Image: Indira Chatterji Oberwolfach 2026.jpg.* Chatterji at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematisches_Forschungsinstitut_Oberwolfach) in 2026

**Indira Lara Chatterji** (born 25 January 1973) is a Swiss-Indian mathematician working in France as a professor of mathematics in the J. A. Dieudonné Laboratory of the [University of Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Côte_d'Azur). Her research involves low-dimensional geometry, [cubical complexes](wikipedia://en/54829672), and [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164). She has also studied [sexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexism) and [institutional bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_bias) in mathematics.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Chatterji was born in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), where her father, Indian [probability theorist](wikipedia://en/23542) Srishti Dhar Chatterji, worked; her mother was a Swiss woman from [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino), Carla Bolognini. She is a Swiss citizen, and holds [Overseas Citizenship of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Citizenship_of_India). After entering the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne) intending to study [criminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminology) and then [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology), she switched to mathematics, earning a [license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_%28degree%29) in 1995 and a [diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom) in 1997. She completed her doctorate in mathematics at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) in 2001. Her dissertation, *On property (RD) for certain discrete groups*, studied the property of "rapid decay" in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), introduced by [Vincent Lafforgue](wikipedia://en/27229287) in connection with the [Baum–Connes conjecture](wikipedia://en/11774498). It was jointly supervised by Marc Burger and Alain Valette.

After working as an H. C. Wang Assistant Professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), she took a tenure-track faculty position at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) in 2005, and remained there until 2011, earning tenure in 2010. In 2007, she was one of 16 US mathematicians to win an [NSF CAREER Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation_CAREER_Awards). Meanwhile, in 2007, she began working as a professor at the [University of Orléans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Orléans) in France (on leave from Ohio State), and in 2010 was promoted to [professor 1st class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_France). In 2014 she moved to her present position in the J. A. Dieudonné Laboratory, originally associated with the [University of Nice Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nice_Sophia_Antipolis) and currently with the University of Côte d'Azur.

She also serves on the scientific council of the Fondation sciences mathématiques de Paris.

## Animation {#Animation}

Along with her mathematical research, Chatterji has made animated drawings that illustrate concepts in geometric group theory for general-audience talks, and exhibited her drawings in the [Institute for Computational and Experimental Research in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31112547) "Illustrating Mathematics" program.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Chatterji was a junior member of the [Institut Universitaire de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Universitaire_de_France) from 2014 to 2019.
