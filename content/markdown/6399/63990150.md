# Abstract L-space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abstract L-space"}

In mathematics, specifically in [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), an **abstract *L*-space**, an **AL-space**, or an **abstract Lebesgue space** is a [Banach lattice](wikipedia://en/54391334) $(X, \| \cdot \|)$ whose norm is additive on the positive cone of *X*.[^n1]

:::

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), it means the [standard probability space](wikipedia://en/10406190).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The strong dual of an [AM-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AM-space) with unit is an AL-space.[^n1]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The reason for the name abstract *L*-space is because every AL-space is isomorphic (as a Banach lattice) with some subspace of $L^1(\mu).$[^n1] Every AL-space *X* is an [order complete](wikipedia://en/63989777) [vector lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_lattice) of [minimal type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_vector_lattice); however, the [order dual](wikipedia://en/12259232) of *X*, denoted by *X*<sup>\+</sup>, is *not* of minimal type unless *X* is finite-dimensional.[^n1] Each order interval in an AL-space is weakly compact.[^n1]

The strong dual of an AL-space is an [AM-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AM-space) with unit.[^n1] The continuous dual space $X^{\prime}$ (which is equal to *X*<sup>\+</sup>) of an AL-space *X* is a [Banach lattice](wikipedia://en/54391334) that can be identified with $C_{\mathbb{R}} ( K )$, where *K* is a compact [extremally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremally_disconnected) topological space; furthermore, under the evaluation map, *X* is isomorphic with the band of all real [Radon measures](wikipedia://en/521933) 𝜇 on *K* such that for every [majorized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorized) and directed subset *S* of $C_{\mathbb{R}} ( K ),$ we have $\lim_{f \in S} \mu ( f ) = \mu ( \sup S ).$[^n1]

:::
