# Band (order theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Band"}

In mathematics, specifically in [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **band** in a [vector lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_lattice) $X$ is a subspace $M$ of $X$ that is [solid](wikipedia://en/63989659) and such that for all $S \subseteq M$ such that $x = \sup S$ exists in $X,$ we have $x \in M.$[^n1] The smallest band containing a subset $S$ of $X$ is called the **band generated by $S$** in $X.$[^n1] A band generated by a singleton set is called a **principal band**.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For any subset $S$ of a vector lattice $X,$ the set $S^{\perp}$ of all elements of $X$ disjoint from $S$ is a band in $X.$[^n1]

If $\mathcal{L}^p(\mu)$ ($1 \leq p \leq \infty$) is the usual space of real valued functions used to define [Lp spaces](wikipedia://en/45194) $L^p,$ then $\mathcal{L}^p(\mu)$ is countably order complete (that is, each subset that is bounded above has a supremum) but in general is not [order complete](wikipedia://en/63989777). If $N$ is the vector subspace of all $\mu$-null functions then $N$ is a [solid subset](wikipedia://en/63989659) of $\mathcal{L}^p(\mu)$ that is not a band.[^n1]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The intersection of an arbitrary family of bands in a vector lattice $X$ is a band in $X.$[^n2]

:::
