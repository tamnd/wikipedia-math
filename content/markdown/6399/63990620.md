# Locally convex vector lattice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally convex vector lattice"}

In mathematics, specifically in [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **locally convex vector lattice (LCVL)** is a [topological vector lattice](wikipedia://en/63989962) that is also a [locally convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex) space.[^n1] LCVLs are important in the theory of [topological vector lattices](wikipedia://en/63989962).

:::

## Lattice semi-norms {#Lattice_semi-norms}

The [Minkowski functional](wikipedia://en/3510908) of a convex, [absorbing](wikipedia://en/1728179), and [solid](wikipedia://en/63989659) set is a called a **lattice semi-norm**. Equivalently, it is a [semi-norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-norm) $p$ such that $|y| \leq |x|$ implies $p(y) \leq p(x).$ The topology of a locally convex vector lattice is generated by the family of all continuous lattice semi-norms.[^n1]

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every locally convex vector lattice possesses a neighborhood base at the origin consisting of [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238) [solid](wikipedia://en/63989659) [absorbing](wikipedia://en/1728179) sets.[^n1]

The strong dual of a locally convex vector lattice $X$ is an [order complete](wikipedia://en/63989777) locally convex vector lattice (under its canonical order) and it is a [solid](wikipedia://en/63989659) subspace of the [order dual](wikipedia://en/12259232) of $X$; moreover, if $X$ is a [barreled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barreled_space) then the continuous dual space of $X$ is a band in the order dual of $X$ and the strong dual of $X$ is a complete locally convex TVS.[^n1]

If a locally convex vector lattice is [barreled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barreled_space) then its strong dual space is complete (this is not necessarily true if the space is merely a locally convex barreled space but not a locally convex vector lattice).[^n1]

If a locally convex vector lattice $X$ is [semi-reflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-reflexive) then it is order complete and $X_b$ (that is, $\left( X, b\left(X, X^{\prime}\right) \right)$) is a complete TVS; moreover, if in addition every positive linear functional on $X$ is continuous then $X$ is of $X$ is of [minimal type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_vector_lattice), the [order topology](wikipedia://en/63989892) $\tau_{\operatorname{O}}$ on $X$ is equal to the [Mackey topology](wikipedia://en/1790627) $\tau\left(X, X^{\prime}\right),$ and $\left(X, \tau_{\operatorname{O}}\right)$ is [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091).[^n1] Every [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) locally convex vector lattice is [order complete](wikipedia://en/63989777) and a complete locally convex TVS whose strong dual is a [barreled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barreled_space) reflexive locally convex TVS that can be identified under the canonical evaluation map with the strong bidual (that is, the strong dual of the strong dual).[^n1]

If a locally convex vector lattice $X$ is an [infrabarreled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrabarreled) TVS then it can be identified under the evaluation map with a topological vector sublattice of its strong bidual, which is an order complete locally convex vector lattice under its canonical order.[^n1]

If $X$ is a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [metrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078) [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) [ordered topological vector space](wikipedia://en/63988132) whose positive cone $C$ is a complete subset of $X,$ then the set of [quasi-interior points](wikipedia://en/63990101) of $C$ is dense in $C.$[^n1]

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem[^n1].**

Suppose that $X$ is an [order complete](wikipedia://en/63989777) locally convex vector lattice with topology $\tau$ and endow the bidual $X^{\prime\prime}$ of $X$ with its natural topology (that is, the topology of uniform convergence on equicontinuous subsets of $X^{\prime}$) and canonical order (under which it becomes an order complete locally convex vector lattice). The following are equivalent:

The evaluation map $X \to X^{\prime\prime}$ induces an isomorphism of $X$ with an order complete sublattice of $X^{\prime\prime}.$ For every majorized and directed subset $S$ of $X,$ the section filter of $S$ converges in $(X, \tau)$ (in which case it necessarily converges to $\sup S$). Every [order convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_convergent) filter in $X$ converges in $(X, \tau)$ (in which case it necessarily converges to its order limit).

:::

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=math-theorem name="Corollary"}

**Corollary[^n1].**

Let $X$ be an [order complete](wikipedia://en/63989777) vector lattice with a [regular order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_order_%28mathematics%29). The following are equivalent:

$X$ is of [minimal type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_vector_lattice). For every [majorized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorized) and direct subset $S$ of $X,$ the section filter of $S$ converges in $X$ when $X$ is endowed with the [order topology](wikipedia://en/63989892). Every [order convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_convergent) filter in $X$ converges in $X$ when $X$ is endowed with the [order topology](wikipedia://en/63989892).

Moreover, if $X$ is of minimal type then the order topology on $X$ is the finest locally convex topology on $X$ for which every order convergent filter converges.

:::

If $(X, \tau)$ is a locally convex vector lattice that is [bornological](wikipedia://en/1804457) and [sequentially complete](wikipedia://en/64126151), then there exists a family of compact spaces $\left(X_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha \in A}$ and a family of $A$-indexed vector lattice embeddings $f_{\alpha} : C_{\mathbb{R}}\left(K_{\alpha}\right) \to X$ such that $\tau$ is the finest locally convex topology on $X$ making each $f_{\alpha}$ continuous.[^n2]

:::

::: {#o20 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every [Banach lattice](wikipedia://en/54391334), [normed lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_lattice), and [Fréchet lattice](wikipedia://en/63990644) is a locally convex vector lattice.
