# Elementary mathematics

article

*Image: Similar-geometric-shapes.svg.* A collection of geometric [shapes](wikipedia://en/169191). All shapes of a given color are [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) to each other. Shapes and basic geometry are important topics in elementary mathematics.

*Image: Commutative Addition.svg.* Both groups are equal to 5. Apples are frequently used to explain arithmetic in textbooks for children.[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Elementary mathematics"}

**Elementary mathematics**, also known as [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_school) or [secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school) [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), is the study of mathematics topics that are commonly taught at the primary or secondary school levels around the world. It includes a wide range of mathematical concepts and skills, including [number sense](wikipedia://en/4642495), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022), and [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954). These concepts and skills form the foundation for more advanced mathematical study and are essential for success in many fields and everyday life. The study of elementary mathematics is a crucial part of a student's education and lays the foundation for future academic and career success.

:::

## Strands of elementary mathematics {#Strands_of_elementary_mathematics}

### Number sense and numeration {#Number_sense_and_numeration}

Number sense is an understanding of numbers and operations. In the 'Number Sense and Numeration' strand students develop an understanding of numbers by being taught various ways of representing numbers, as well as the relationships among numbers.[^o5]

Properties of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) such as [divisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility) and the distribution of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666), are studied in basic [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), another part of elementary mathematics.

Elementary Focus:

- [Abacus](wikipedia://en/655)
- [LCM](wikipedia://en/17961) and [GCD](wikipedia://en/12354)
- [Fractions](wikipedia://en/1704824) and [Decimals](wikipedia://en/8214)
- [Place Value](wikipedia://en/437052) & [Face Value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_Value)
- [Addition](wikipedia://en/61338) and [Subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141)
- [Multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) and [Division](wikipedia://en/53696)
- [Counting](wikipedia://en/275768)
- Counting [Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money)
- [Factorial](wikipedia://en/10606)

- [Combination](wikipedia://en/5308) with repetition
- [Permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) without repetition
- [Permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) with repetition

- [Inequality](wikipedia://en/89489)
- Representing and ordering numbers
- [Estimating](wikipedia://en/36687154)

### Spatial sense {#Spatial_sense}

'Measurement skills and concepts' or 'Spatial Sense' are directly related to the world in which students live. Many of the concepts that students are taught in this strand are also used in other subjects such as science, social studies, and physical education[^o7] In the measurement strand students learn about the measurable attributes of objects, in addition to the basic metric system.

Elementary Focus:

- [Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_%28metrology%29) and [non-standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardization) [units of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_of_measurement)
- telling [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) using [12 hour clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_hour_clock) and [24 hour clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-hour_clock)
- [comparing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison) objects using measurable attributes
- [measuring](wikipedia://en/19022) [height](wikipedia://en/239972), [length, width](wikipedia://en/18542)
- [centimetres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centimetre) and [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre)
- [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) and [capacity](wikipedia://en/32498)
- [temperature change](wikipedia://en/20647050)
- [days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day), [months](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Month), [weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Week), [years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year)
- [distances](wikipedia://en/39378) using [kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometre)
- measuring [kilograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogram) and [litres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litre)
- determining [area](wikipedia://en/1209) and [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636)
- determining [grams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram) and millilitre
- determining measurements using [shapes](wikipedia://en/169191) such as a [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584)

The measurement strand consists of multiple forms of measurement, as Marian Small states: "Measurement is the process of assigning a qualitative or quantitative description of size to an object based on a particular attribute."[^o8]

### Equations and formulas {#Equations_and_formulas}

A formula is an entity constructed using the symbols and formation rules of a given [logical language](wikipedia://en/10939).[^o11] For example, determining the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) requires a significant amount of [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus) or its geometrical analogue, the [method of exhaustion](wikipedia://en/1721284);[^o12] but, having done this once in terms of some [parameter](wikipedia://en/25065) (the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) for example), mathematicians have produced a formula to describe the volume.

An equation is a [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_formula) of the form *A* = *B*, where *A* and *B* are [expressions](wikipedia://en/609125) that may contain one or several [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) called **unknowns**, and "=" denotes the [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931). Although written in the form of [proposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposition_%28mathematics%29), an equation is not a [statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_%28logic%29) that is either true or false, but a problem consisting of finding the values, called **solutions**, that, when substituted for the unknowns, yield equal values of the expressions *A* and *B*. For example, 2 is the unique *solution* of the *equation* *x* + 2 = 4, in which the *unknown* is *x*.[^o13]

:::

### Data {#Data}

*Image: Black cherry tree histogram.svg.* An example [histogram](wikipedia://en/13266) of the heights of 31 [Black Cherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prunus_serotina) trees. Histograms are a common tool used to represent data.

Data is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_%28computer_science%29) of [qualitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualitative_data) or [quantitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_data) [variables](wikipedia://en/30423282); restated, pieces of data are individual pieces of [information](wikipedia://en/18985062). Data in [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) (or [data processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing)) is represented in a [structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure) that is often [tabular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_%28information%29) (represented by [rows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row_%28database%29) and [columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_%28database%29)), a [tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_structure) (a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node_%28graph_theory%29) with [parent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parent)-[children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children) [relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relation_%28database%29)), or a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) (a set of [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interconnected) nodes). Data is typically the result of [measurements](wikipedia://en/19022) and can be [visualized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_visualisation) using [graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_%28data_structure%29) or [images](wikipedia://en/71925).

Data as an [abstract](wikipedia://en/2972) [concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept) can be viewed as the lowest level of [abstraction](wikipedia://en/2972), from which [information](wikipedia://en/18985062) and then [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge) are derived.

### Basic two-dimensional geometry {#Basic_two-dimensional_geometry}

Two-dimensional geometry is a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) concerned with questions of shape, size, and relative position of two-dimensional figures. Basic topics in elementary mathematics include polygons, circles, perimeter and areas.

A [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) is a shape that is bounded by a finite chain of straight [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) closing in a loop to form a [closed chain](wikipedia://en/12056032) or *circuit*. These segments are called its *edges* or *sides*, and the points where two edges meet are the polygon's *[vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417)* (singular: vertex) or *corners*. The interior of the polygon is sometimes called its *body*. An ***n*-gon** is a polygon with *n* sides. A polygon is a 2-dimensional example of the more general [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) in any number of dimensions.

A [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) is a simple [shape](wikipedia://en/169191) of [two-dimensional geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) that is the set of all [points](wikipedia://en/593693) in a [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) that are at a given distance from a given point, the [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_%28geometry%29). The distance between any of the points and the center is called the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815). It can also be defined as the locus of a point equidistant from a fixed point.

A [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) is a path that surrounds a [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional) [shape](wikipedia://en/169191). The term may be used either for the path or its length - it can be thought of as the length of the outline of a shape. The perimeter of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) or [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) is called its [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916).

[Area](wikipedia://en/1209) is the [quantity](wikipedia://en/691277) that expresses the extent of a [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional) figure or [shape](wikipedia://en/169191). There are several well-known [formulas](wikipedia://en/164040) for the areas of simple shapes such as [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654), [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048), and [circles](wikipedia://en/6220).

### Proportions {#Proportions}

Two quantities are proportional if a change in one is always accompanied by a change in the other, and if the changes are always related by use of a constant multiplier. The constant is called the [coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203) of proportionality or **proportionality constant**.

- If one quantity is always the product of the other and a constant, the two are said to be *directly proportional*. *x* and *y* are directly proportional if the [ratio](wikipedia://en/87837) $\tfrac yx$ is constant.
- If the product of the two quantities is always equal to a constant, the two are said to be *inversely proportional*. *x* and *y* are inversely proportional if the product $xy$ is constant.

### Analytic geometry {#Analytic_geometry}

*Image: Cartesian-coordinate-system.svg.* Cartesian coordinates

[Analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) is the study of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) using a [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931). This contrasts with [synthetic geometry](wikipedia://en/267484).

Usually the [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) is applied to manipulate [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) for [planes](wikipedia://en/19469852), [straight lines](wikipedia://en/946975), and [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), often in two and sometimes in three dimensions. Geometrically, one studies the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) (2 dimensions) and [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) (3 dimensions). As taught in school books, analytic geometry can be explained more simply: it is concerned with defining and representing geometrical shapes in a numerical way and extracting numerical information from shapes' numerical definitions and representations.

Transformations are ways of shifting and scaling functions using different algebraic formulas.

### Negative numbers {#Negative_numbers}

A [negative number](wikipedia://en/154616) is a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) that is [less than](wikipedia://en/89489) [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29). Such numbers are often used to represent the amount of a loss or absence. For example, a [debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt) that is owed may be thought of as a negative asset, or a decrease in some quantity may be thought of as a negative increase. Negative numbers are used to describe values on a scale that goes below zero, such as the Celsius and [Fahrenheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit) scales for temperature.

### Exponents and radicals {#Exponents_and_radicals}

Exponentiation is a [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) [operation](wikipedia://en/4140245), written as ***b*<sup>*n*</sup>**, involving two numbers, the **[base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_%28exponentiation%29)** *b* and the **exponent** (or **power**) *n*. When *n* is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) (i.e., a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563)), exponentiation corresponds to repeated [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) of the base: that is, *b<sup>n</sup>* is the [product](wikipedia://en/24977) of multiplying *n* bases:

$$b^n = \underbrace{b \times \cdots \times b}_n$$

Roots are the opposite of exponents. The [nth root](wikipedia://en/235029) of a [number](wikipedia://en/21690) *x* (written $\sqrt[n]{x}$) is a number *r* which when raised to the power *n* yields *x*. That is,

$$\sqrt[n]{x} = r \iff r^n = x,$$

where *n* is the *degree* of the root. A root of degree 2 is called a *[square root](wikipedia://en/29208)* and a root of degree 3, a *[cube root](wikipedia://en/292506)*. Roots of higher degree are referred to by using ordinal numbers, as in *fourth root*, *twentieth root*, etc.

For example:

- 2 is a square root of 4, since 2<sup>2</sup> = 4.
- −2 is also a square root of 4, since (−2)<sup>2</sup> = 4.

### Compass-and-straightedge {#Compass-and-straightedge}

Compass-and-straightedge, also known as ruler-and-compass construction, is the construction of lengths, [angles](wikipedia://en/1196), and other geometric figures using only an [idealized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealization_%28science_philosophy%29) [ruler](wikipedia://en/65593) and [compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_%28drafting%29).

The idealized ruler, known as a [straightedge](wikipedia://en/147854), is assumed to be infinite in length, and has no markings on it and only one edge. The compass is assumed to collapse when lifted from the page, so may not be directly used to transfer distances. (This is an unimportant restriction since, using a multi-step procedure, a distance can be transferred even with a collapsing compass, see [compass equivalence theorem](wikipedia://en/12613184).) More formally, the only permissible constructions are those granted by the [first three postulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_postulates) of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331).

### Congruence and similarity {#Congruence_and_similarity}

Two figures or objects are congruent if they have the same [shape](wikipedia://en/169191) and size, or if one has the same shape and size as the mirror image of the other.[^o22] More formally, two sets of [points](wikipedia://en/593693) are called **congruent** if, and only if, one can be transformed into the other by an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777), i.e., a combination of **rigid motions**, namely a [translation](wikipedia://en/204682), a [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789), and a [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829). This means that either object can be repositioned and reflected (but not resized) so as to coincide precisely with the other object. So two distinct plane figures on a piece of paper are congruent if we can cut them out and then match them up completely. Turning the paper over is permitted.

Two geometrical objects are called [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) if they both have the same [shape](wikipedia://en/169191), or one has the same shape as the mirror image of the other. More precisely, one can be obtained from the other by uniformly [scaling](wikipedia://en/651752) (enlarging or shrinking), possibly with additional [translation](wikipedia://en/204682), [rotation](wikipedia://en/410009) and [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829). This means that either object can be rescaled, repositioned, and reflected, so as to coincide precisely with the other object. If two objects are similar, each is [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to the result of a uniform scaling of the other.

### Three-dimensional geometry {#Three-dimensional_geometry}

[Solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960) was the traditional name for the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). **Stereometry** deals with the [measurements](wikipedia://en/19022) of [volumes](wikipedia://en/32498) of various **solid figures** ([three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional) figures) including [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045), [cylinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29), [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29), [truncated cones](wikipedia://en/89405), [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859), and [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007).

### Rational numbers {#Rational_numbers}

[Rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) is any [number](wikipedia://en/21690) that can be expressed as the [quotient](wikipedia://en/332640) or fraction *p*/*q* of two [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), with the [denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominator) *q* not equal to zero.[^o25] Since *q* may be equal to 1, every integer is a rational number. The [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all rational numbers is usually denoted by a boldface **Q** (or [blackboard bold](wikipedia://en/40276) $\mathbb{Q}$).

### Patterns, relations and functions {#Patterns,_relations_and_functions}

A [pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern) is a discernible regularity in the world or in a manmade design. As such, the elements of a pattern repeat in a predictable manner. A **geometric pattern** is a kind of pattern formed of geometric shapes and typically repeating like a [wallpaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallpaper) pattern.

A [relation](wikipedia://en/3931) on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *A* is a collection of [ordered pairs](wikipedia://en/22362) of elements of *A*. In other words, it is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) *A*<sup>2</sup> = *A* × *A*. Common relations include divisibility between two numbers and inequalities.

A **function**[^o27] is a [relation](wikipedia://en/3931) between a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of inputs and a set of permissible outputs with the property that each input is related to exactly one output. An example is the function that relates each real number *x* to its square *x*<sup>2</sup>. The output of a function *f* corresponding to an input *x* is denoted by *f*(*x*) (read "*f* of *x*"). In this example, if the input is −3, then the output is 9, and we may write *f*(−3) = 9. The input variable(s) are sometimes referred to as the argument(s) of the function.

### Slopes and trigonometry {#Slopes_and_trigonometry}

The [slope of a line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope_of_a_line) is a number that describes both the *direction* and the *steepness* of the line.[^o29] Slope is often denoted by the letter ***m***.[^o30]

[Trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) is a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) that studies relationships involving lengths and [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654). The field emerged during the 3rd century BC from applications of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) to astronomical studies.[^o31]

## United States {#United_States}

In the United States, there has been considerable concern about the low level of elementary mathematics skills on the part of many students, as compared to students in other developed countries.[^o33] The [No Child Left Behind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Child_Left_Behind) program was one attempt to address this deficiency, requiring that all American students be tested in elementary mathematics.[^o34]
