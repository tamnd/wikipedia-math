# James G. Oldroyd

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James G. Oldroyd"}

**James Gardner Oldroyd** (25 April 1921 – 22 November 1982[^o2]) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and noted [rheologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheologist). He formulated the [Oldroyd-B model](wikipedia://en/33558176) to describe the [viscoelastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscoelastic) behaviour of [non-Newtonian fluids](wikipedia://en/75367).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Oldroyd was born in 1921 and educated at [Bradford Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Grammar_School), and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). On graduation, during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), he worked for the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply). After the war he joined the Research Laboratory of [Courtaulds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtaulds). In 1946 he married Marged Katryn Evans.[^o4] In 1953 he became professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Wales, Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wales,_Swansea) until 1965 and head of the Applied Mathematics department from 1957.[^o4] In 1965 he moved to [Liverpool University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_University), becoming head of the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics in 1973 until his death.[^o5] He died 22 November 1982, and was survived by his wife Katryn and three sons.[^o2]

## Work {#Work}

In 1950 he published a paper "On the Formulation of Rheological Equations of State"[^o7] which has been described as "probably the most important single paper in theoretical rheology"[^o8] as it established the basic requirements for mathematical models of rheology.[^o4] For this and other major papers he received international recognition, including the Gold Medal of the [British Society of Rheology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Society_of_Rheology), and a tribute issue of its Bulletin on his sixtieth birthday.[^o9]
