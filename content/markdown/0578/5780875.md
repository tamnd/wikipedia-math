# Residually finite group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Residually finite group"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* is **residually finite** or **finitely approximable** if for every element *g* that is not the identity in *G* there is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) *h* from *G* to a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707), such that $h(g) \neq 1.\,$[^n1]

:::

There are a number of equivalent definitions:

- A group is residually finite if for each non-identity element in the group, there is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of finite [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_%28group_theory%29) not containing that element.
- A group is residually finite if and only if the intersection of all its [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of finite index is [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059).
- A group is residually finite if and only if the intersection of all its normal subgroups of finite index is trivial.
- A group is residually finite if and only if it can be embedded inside the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of a family of finite groups.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ is residually finite if, for every $1_G \neq g \in G$, there exists some [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) $F$ and some [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $\phi : G \to F$ such that $\phi(g) \neq 1_F$.[^n2] There are other equivalent characterizations of residually finite groups:

- A group $G$ is residually finite if and only if, for every $g, h \in G$ where $g \neq h$, there exists some finite group $F$ and some group homomorphism $\phi : G \to F$ such that $\phi(g) \neq \phi(h)$.[^n3]
- A group is residually finite if and only if its [residual subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residual_subgroup) (or profinite kernel) is trivial. The residual subgroup of a group is the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of all [subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroups) that have a [finite index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_index), or equivalently, the intersection of all [normal subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_subgroups) of finite index.[^n4]
- A group is residually finite if and only if it is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a subgroup of a [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) of a family of finite groups.[^n5]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Every finite group is residually finite. This can be shown by considering the group itself as the finite group, and its identity homomorphism as the homomorphism to a finite group.[^n3]

The [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers) are an example of an [infinite](wikipedia://en/4587030) residually finite group. Given any non-zero integer $k$ and letting $m$ be an integer with $m > |k|$, the canonical homomorphism from the integers to the group of integers modulo $m$, $\phi : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$, does not map $k$ onto $0$.[^n3] a similar technique done on the entries of the matrices of $GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$, for $n \geq 1$, shows that this group is also residually finite.[^n3] More generally, all [finitely generated abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_abelian_groups) are residually finite.[^n5] Furthermore, the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of any finitely generated residually finite group will be residually finite.[^n6]

Subgroups of a residually finite group are themselves residually finite.[^n5] The direct product[^n5] and direct sum[^n5] of residually finite groups will also be residually finite.

Any [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of residually finite groups is residually finite.[^n7] In particular, all [profinite groups](wikipedia://en/24266) are residually finite.[^n7] One example of a profinite group is the [p-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integers).[^n7]

If a group has a subgroup of finite index which is residually finite (that is, a [virtually](wikipedia://en/10377971) residually finite group), then said group is also residually finite.[^n7]

More examples of groups that are residually finite are [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735)[^n8], [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569), [polycyclic groups](wikipedia://en/4000598)[^n9], [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564)[^n10], and [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of compact [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257)[^o26].

### Nonexamples {#Nonexamples}

A [divisible group](wikipedia://en/472147) is a group $G$ where every $g \in G$ and integer $n \geq 1$ has an element $h \in G$ where $h^n = g$. Examples of nontrivial divisible groups include the [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers), the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers), the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers), the [additive group](wikipedia://en/332229) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the rationals, and the additive group of every field with [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_%28field_theory%29) 0.[^n3] Every nontrivial divisible group fails to be residually finite as every homomorphism from a divisible group to a finite group is trivial.[^n3]

Examples of non-residually finite groups can be constructed using the fact that all finitely generated residually finite groups are [Hopfian groups](wikipedia://en/11486001)[^n11]. For example the [Baumslag–Solitar group](wikipedia://en/11485892) $B(1,m)$ for $|m| \geq 2$ is finitely generated, residually finite, and Hopfian[^n12] , but $B(2,3)$ is finitely generated yet not Hopfian, and therefore not residually finite.[^n13]

Every infinite [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) is not residually finite because the only normal subgroup with a finite index will be the group itself.[^n14] This implies that the [group of permutations](wikipedia://en/28901) on an infinite set with [finite support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_support) is not residually finite as the subgroup with the permutations of signature 1 is an infinite simple group.[^n14] This can be used to show that the subgroup of permutation on the integers generated by the translation $n \mapsto n+1$ and the transposition of $0$ and $1$ is a finitely generated Hopfian group that is not residually finite.[^n14]

[Group extensions](wikipedia://en/345807) of residually finite groups also need not be residually finite.[^n15] One counter example is the [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625) of $A_5$, the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) of degree 5, with the integers, both of which are residually finite.[^n15]

## Properties {#Properties}

Finitely presented residually finite groups have a solvable [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563)[^n16], meaning there is a procedure where, given the group's generators, one can find the words that equate to the identity element[^n17].

:::

## Topology {#Topology}

Every group *G* may be made into a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) by taking as a [basis of open neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_of_open_neighbourhoods) of the identity, the collection of all normal subgroups of finite index in *G*. The resulting [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) is called the [profinite topology](wikipedia://en/24266) on *G*[^n18]. A group is residually finite if, and only if, its profinite topology is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637).[^n18] If this group is also infinite and finitely generated, then said topology is [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected), and the [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28topology%29) is the inverse limit of a sequence of finite quotients of this group, making it a profinite group.[^n16]

A group whose [cyclic subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_subgroup) are closed in the profinite topology is said to be $\Pi_C\,$. Groups each of whose finitely generated subgroups are closed in the profinite topology are called **subgroup separable** (also **LERF**, for *locally extended residually finite*). A group in which every [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) is closed in the profinite topology is called **conjugacy separable**.

## Varieties of residually finite groups {#Varieties_of_residually_finite_groups}

One question is: what are the properties of a [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) all of whose groups are residually finite? Two results about these are:

- Any variety comprising only residually finite groups is generated by an [A-group](wikipedia://en/4938898).
- For any variety comprising only residually finite groups, it contains a finite group such that all members are embedded in a direct product of that finite group.
