# Extremally disconnected space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Extremally disconnected space"}

In mathematics, an **extremally disconnected space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) in which the closure of every open set is open. (The term "extremally disconnected" is correct, even though the word "extremally" does not appear in most dictionaries, and is sometimes mistaken by spellcheckers for the [homophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophone) *extremely disconnected*.)

:::

An extremally disconnected space that is also [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) is sometimes called a **Stonean space**.[^o3][^n1] This is not the same as a [Stone space](wikipedia://en/380219), which is a [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected) compact Hausdorff space. Every Stonean space is a Stone space, but not vice versa. In the duality between Stone spaces and [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959), the Stonean spaces correspond to the [complete Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/1839944).

An extremally disconnected [first-countable](wikipedia://en/904940) [collectionwise Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/5075324) must be [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061). In particular, for [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018), the property of being extremally disconnected (the closure of every open set is open) is equivalent to the property of being discrete (every set is open).

## Examples and non-examples {#Examples_and_non-examples}

- Every [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061) is extremally disconnected. Every [indiscrete space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_space) is both extremally disconnected and connected.
- The [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) of a discrete space is extremally disconnected.
- The [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of an [abelian von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/1496379) is extremally disconnected.
- Any commutative [AW\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/39435930) is isomorphic to $C(X)$, for some space $X$ which is extremally disconnected, compact and Hausdorff.
- Any infinite space with the [cofinite topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite_topology) is both extremally disconnected and [connected](wikipedia://en/6233). More generally, every [hyperconnected space](wikipedia://en/7556651) is extremally disconnected.
- The space on three points with [base](wikipedia://en/59220) $\{\{x,y\},\{x,y,z\}\}$ provides a [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) example of a space that is both extremally disconnected and connected. Another example is given by the [Sierpinski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_space), since it is finite, connected, and hyperconnected.

The following spaces are not extremally disconnected:

- The [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172) is not extremally disconnected. However, it is totally disconnected.

## Equivalent characterizations {#Equivalent_characterizations}

A theorem due to (Gleason 1958) says that the [projective objects](wikipedia://en/3017382) of the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of compact Hausdorff spaces are exactly the extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces. A simplified proof of this fact is given by (Rainwater 1959).

A compact Hausdorff space is extremally disconnected if and only if it is a [retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retract_%28topology%29) of the Stone–Čech compactification of a discrete space.[^o9]

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

(Hartig 1983) proves the [Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem](wikipedia://en/38948504) by reducing it to the case of extremally disconnected spaces, in which case the representation theorem can be proved by elementary means.
