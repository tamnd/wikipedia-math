# Henricus Grammateus

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Henricus Grammateus"}

**Henricus Grammateus** (also known as **Henricus Scriptor**, **Heinrich Schreyber** or **Heinrich Schreiber**; 1495 – 1525 or 1526[^o3]) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was born in [Erfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erfurt). In 1507 he started to study at the University of Vienna, where he subsequently taught. [Christoph Rudolff](wikipedia://en/23511221) was one of his students. From 1514 to 1517 he studied in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) and then returned to Vienna. But when the plague affected Vienna Schreiber left the city and went to [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg).

:::

In 1518, he published details of a new [musical temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_temperament),[^o4] which is now named after him, for the [harpsichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpsichord). It was a precursor of the [equal temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_temperament).

In 1525 Schreiber was back in Vienna, where he is listed as "Examinator", i.e. eligible to work holding exams.

## Works {#Works}

- *Algorithmus proportionum una cum monochordi generalis dyatonici compositione*, pub. Volfgangvm De Argentina, Kraków, 1514
- *Libellus de compositione regularum pro vasorum mensuratione. Deque arte ista tota theoreticae et practicae*, Vienna, 1518
- *Ayn new Kunstlich Buech* (*A New Skill Book*), Vienna 1518, Nuremberg 1521 - contains (besides [Johannes Widmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Widmann)) the earliest-known use of the [plus and minus signs](wikipedia://en/245206) for addition and subtraction[^o6] and is the earliest German text on bookkeeping[^o7]
