# David Francis Barrow

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Francis Barrow"}

**David Francis Barrow** (November 14, 1888 – February 4, 1970) was an American mathematician who introduced [Barrow's inequality](wikipedia://en/7333103) in 1937.

:::

Barrow's father, [David Crenshaw Barrow Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Crenshaw_Barrow_Jr.), was also a mathematician, and served as chancellor of the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) from 1906 to 1925. His son, David F. Barrow, did his undergraduate studies at the University of Georgia and then studied at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1913. After a year abroad, he taught for two years at the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas), and then at the [Sheffield Scientific School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Scientific_School). After a brief stint in the U.S. armed services, he joined the faculty of his father's university in 1920. He became a full professor in 1923, and chaired the mathematics department in 1944–1945.[^o3]
