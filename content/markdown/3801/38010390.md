# Richard Shute

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Shute"}

**Richard Shute** (6 November 1849 – 22 September 1886) was a British classicist and logician.

:::

Richard Shute was the only son of Richard Shute of [Sydenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydenham,_London), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent). He was educated at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College), and matriculated at [Trinity Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Hall,_Cambridge) in 1868. However, he transferred to [New Inn Hall, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Inn_Hall,_Oxford), where he matriculated in 1870, graduating B.A. in 1872. He was a senior student at [Christ Church, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford) from 1872 to 1886, and tutor from 1876 to 1882. He died of [consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_%28disease%29) at Oxford in 1886. At the time of his death he had been appointed professor of logic at [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay).[^o2]

Shute's *Discourse on Truth* (1877) was adapted into German by [Goswin Karl Uphues](wikipedia://en/38010100).[^o3] Shute's [Conington Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conington_Prize) essay on the Aristotelian writings was published posthumously: there Shute held that the surviving works were not written by [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308) himself, but had been "filtered at least through other minds".[^o4]

## Works {#Works}

- *Essays on certain logical questions*, Oxford, London: James Parker and Co., 1874.
- *A Discourse on Truth*, London, 1877.
- (ed.) *Aristotle's Physics, book VII, a transcript of the Paris ms. 1859 collated with the Paris mss. 1861 and 2633 and a manuscript in the Bodleian library*, Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1882.
- *On the History of the Process by which the Aristotelian Writings arrived at their Present Form: with a Brief Mention of the Author*, Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1888. ([Conington Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conington_Prize) essay for 1882.)
