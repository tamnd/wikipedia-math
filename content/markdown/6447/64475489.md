# Peano surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peano surface"}

*Image: Modell einer Peanoschen Fläche -Schilling XLIX, 1-.jpg.* Model of the Peano surface in the Dresden collection

In mathematics, the **Peano surface** is the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of the [two-variable function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_calculus)

:::

$$f(x,y)=(2x^2-y)(y-x^2).$$

It was proposed by [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043) in 1899 as a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to a conjectured criterion for the existence of [maxima and minima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima) of functions of two variables.[^o2][^o3]

The surface was named the Peano surface (Peanosche Fläche) by [Georg Scheffers](wikipedia://en/31778680) in his 1920 book *Lehrbuch der darstellenden Geometrie*.[^o2][^o4] It has also been called the **Peano saddle**.[^o5][^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Peano-flaeche-12.svg.* Peano surface and its level curves for level 0 (parabolas, green and purple)

The function $f(x,y)=(2x^2-y)(y-x^2)$ whose graph is the surface takes positive values between the two [parabolas](wikipedia://en/23231) $y=x^2$ and $y=2x^2$, and negative values elsewhere (see diagram). At the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432), the three-dimensional point $(0,0,0)$ on the surface that corresponds to the intersection point of the two parabolas, the surface has a [saddle point](wikipedia://en/640249).[^o17] The surface itself has positive [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) in some parts and negative curvature in others, separated by another parabola,[^o5][^o6] implying that its [Gauss map](wikipedia://en/378881) has a [Whitney cusp](wikipedia://en/581763).[^o6]

*Image: Peano intersection.png.* Intersection of the Peano surface with a vertical plane. The intersection curve has a local maximum at the origin, to the right of the image, and a global maximum on the left of the image, dipping shallowly between these two points.

Although the surface does not have a local maximum at the origin, its intersection with any vertical plane through the origin (a plane with equation $y=mx$ or $x=0$) is a curve that has a local maximum at the origin,[^o2] a property described by [Earle Raymond Hedrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_Raymond_Hedrick) as "paradoxical".[^o9] In other words, if a point starts at the origin $(0,0)$ of the plane, and moves away from the origin along any straight line, the value of $(2x^2-y)(y-x^2)$ will decrease at the start of the motion. Nevertheless, $(0,0)$ is not a local maximum of the function, because moving along a parabola such as $y=\sqrt{2}\,x^2$ (in diagram: red) will cause the function value to increase.

The Peano surface is a [quartic surface](wikipedia://en/20133775).

:::

## As a counterexample {#As_a_counterexample}

In 1886 [Joseph Alfred Serret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Alfred_Serret) published a textbook[^o11] with a proposed criterion for the extremal points of a surface given by $z=f(x_0+h,y_0+k)$

"the maximum or the minimum takes place when for the values of $h$ and $k$ for which $d^2f$ and $d^3f$ (third and fourth terms) vanish, $d^4f$ (fifth term) has constantly the sign − , or the sign +."

Here, it is assumed that the linear terms vanish and the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) of $f$ has the form $z=f(x_0,y_0)+Q(h,k)+C(h,k)+F(h,k)+\cdots$ where $Q(h,k)$ is a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) like $a h^2+b h k+c k^2$, $C(h,k)$ is a [cubic form](wikipedia://en/6680683) with cubic terms in $h$ and $k$, and $F(h,k)$ is a quartic form with a [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/1698977) quartic polynomial in $h$ and $k$. Serret proposes that if $F(h,k)$ has constant sign for all points where $Q(h,k)=C(h,k)=0$ then there is a local maximum or minimum of the surface at $(x_0,y_0)$.

In his 1884 notes to [Angelo Genocchi](wikipedia://en/9134562)'s Italian textbook on [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), *Calcolo differenziale e principii di calcolo integrale*, Peano had already provided different correct conditions for a function to attain a local minimum or local maximum.[^o2][^o12] In the 1899 German translation of the same textbook, he provided this surface as a counterexample to Serret's condition. At the point $(0,0,0)$, Serret's conditions are met, but this point is a saddle point, not a local maximum.[^o2][^o3] A related condition to Serret's was also criticized by [Ludwig Scheeffer](wikipedia://en/69989987), who used Peano's surface as a counterexample to it in an 1890 publication, credited to Peano.[^o17][^o13]

## Models {#Models}

Models of Peano's surface are included in the Göttingen Collection of Mathematical Models and Instruments at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen),[^o15] and in the mathematical model collection of [TU Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dresden) (in two different models).[^o16] The Göttingen model was the first new model added to the collection after [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), and one of the last added to the collection overall.[^o17]
