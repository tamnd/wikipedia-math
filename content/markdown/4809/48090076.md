# Tatiana Toro

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tatiana Toro"}

**Tatiana Toro** (born 1964) is a Colombian-American mathematician at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).[^o3] Her research is "at the interface of [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564)".[^o4] Toro was appointed director of the [Simons Laufer Mathematical Sciences Institute](wikipedia://en/401367) for 2022–2027.[^o5]

:::

## Education and employment {#Education_and_employment}

Toro was born in 1964 in [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia),[^o4][^o7] and attended the [Lycée Français Louis Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_français_Louis_Pasteur_%28Colombia%29) in [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogotá).[^o8] She competed for Colombia in the 1981 [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208),[^o9] and earned a bachelor's degree from the [National University of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Colombia).[^o10] In 1992, she was awarded her PhD at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), under the supervision of [Leon Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Simon). After short-term positions at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), and [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), she joined the University of Washington faculty in 1996.[^o3] Since August 2022, Toro serves as the director of [Simons Laufer Mathematical Sciences Institute](wikipedia://en/401367) (formerly MSRI).[^o7] She will maintain her tenure at the University of Washington throughout her term.[^o12]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Toro was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2010.[^o14] She became a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow) in 2015.[^o4] She was elected as a member of the 2017 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to geometric measure theory, [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), and free boundary theory".[^o15] At the University of Washington, she was the Robert R. & Elaine F. Phelps Professor in Mathematics from 2012 to 2016[^o16] and is currently the Craig McKibben and Sarah Merner Professor. Toro was named [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) Chancellor's Professor for 2016–17.[^o17] She was awarded the 2020 [Blackwell-Tapia Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwell-Tapia_Prize).[^o18] She was elected fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences (AAAS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2020.[^o19] Toro was awarded the [ICMAM Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICMAM_Prize) 2022 of the research organization ICMAM Latin America.[^o20] Toro was honored as the [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer) in 2023.[^o21]
