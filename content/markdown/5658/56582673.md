# Helen F. Cullen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helen F. Cullen"}

**Helen Frances Cullen** (January 4, 1919 – August 25, 2007) was an American mathematician specializing in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). She worked for many years as a professor of mathematics at the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst) and was the first female faculty member in the mathematics department at Amherst. She was known as the author of the book *Introduction to General Topology* (Heath, 1968), as well as for her outspoken [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Cullen was born in [Dorchester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorchester,_Massachusetts), and studied at [Girls' Latin School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Latin_Academy) and [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College). She earned a master's degree at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1944, and completed her Ph.D. at Michigan in 1950. Her dissertation, *A Set of Parabolic Regular Curve Families Filling the Plane and Certain Related Reimann Surfaces*, was supervised by [Wilfred Kaplan](wikipedia://en/46189346). She was a faculty member in the department of mathematics at Amherst from 1949 until her retirement as a [professor emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emerita) in 1992.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1998 the Girls' Latin School – Boston Latin Academy Association listed her as one of their outstanding alumnae.
