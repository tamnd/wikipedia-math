# Udayadivākara

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Udayadivākara"}

**Udayadivākara** (c. 1073 CE) was an Indian astronomer and mathematician who authored an influential and elaborate commentary, called *Sundari*, on *Laghu-bhāskarīya* of [Bhāskara I](wikipedia://en/1158626). No personal details about Udayadivākara are known. Since the commentary *Sundari* takes the year 1073 CE as its epoch, probably the commentary was completed about that year. *Sundari* has not yet been published and is available only in manuscript form. Some of these manuscripts are preserved in the manuscript depositories in Thiruvananthapuram. According to [K. V. Sarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._V._Sarma), historian of the astronomy and mathematics of the Kerala school, Udayadivākara probably hailed from [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Historical significance of *Sundari* {#Historical_significance_of_Sundari}

Apart from the fact that *Sundari* is an elaborate commentary, it has some historical significance. It has quoted extensively from a now lost work by a little-known mathematician [Jayadeva](wikipedia://en/1158578). The quotations relate to the *[cakravala](wikipedia://en/4050658)* method for solving indeterminate integral equations of the form $Nx^2+1=y^2$. This shows that the method predates [Bhaskara II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskara_II) contrary to generally held beliefs. Another important reference to Jayadeva’s work is the solution of the indeterminate equation of the form $Nx^2 + C = y^2$, $C$ being positive or negative.[^o3]

## A problem and its solution {#A_problem_and_its_solution}

Udayadivākara used his method for solving the equation $Nx^2 + C = y^2$ to obtain some particular solutions to a certain algebraic problem. The problem and Udayadivākara's solution to the problem are presented below only to illustrate the techniques used by Indian astronomers for solving algebraic equations.[^o3]

### Problem {#Problem}

Find positive integers $x$ and $y$ satisfying the following conditions:

$$\begin{aligned} x+y & = \text{a prefect square},\\ x-y & = \text{a prefect square},\\ xy+1 & = \text{a prefect square}. \end{aligned}$$

### Solution {#Solution}

To solve the problem, Udayadivākara makes a series of apparently arbitrary assumptions all aimed at reducing the problem to one of solving an indeterminate equation of the form $Nx^2+C=y^2$.

Udayadivākara begins by assuming that $xy+1=(2y+1)^2$ which can be written in the form $x-y=3y+4$. He next assumes that $3y+4 = (3z+2)^2$ which, together with the earlier equation, yields

$$\begin{aligned} x & =12z^2+16z+4,\\ y &= 3z^2+4z,\\ x+y & = 15z^2+20z+4. \end{aligned}$$

Now, Udayadivākara puts

$$15z^2+20z+4 = u^2$$

which is then transformed to the equation

$$(30z+20)^2= 60u^2+160.$$

This equation is of the form $Nx^2+C=\lambda^2$ with $N=60$, $C=160$ and $\lambda = 30z+20$. Using the method for solving the equation $Nx^2+C=y^2$, Udayadivākara finds the following solutions $( u=2, \lambda=20)$, $( u=18, \lambda=140)$ and $( u=8802, \lambda=68180)$ from which the values of $x$ and $y$ are obtained by back substitution.
