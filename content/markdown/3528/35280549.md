# Alexander Schrijver

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Schrijver"}

*Image: Alexander Schrijver 2010.jpg.* Lex Schrijver at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) in 2010

**Alexander (Lex) Schrijver** (born 4 May 1948 in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam))[^o2] is a Dutch mathematician and computer scientist, a professor of discrete mathematics and optimization at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) and a fellow at the [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063) in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam).[^o3] Since 1993 he has been co-editor in chief of the journal *[Combinatorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorica)*.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Schrijver earned his Ph.D. in 1977 from the [Vrije Universiteit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit) in Amsterdam, under the supervision of Pieter Cornelis Baayen. He worked for the Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica (under its former name as the Mathematisch Centrum) in pure mathematics from 1973 to 1979, and was a professor at [Tilburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilburg_University) from 1983 to 1989. In 1989 he rejoined the Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica, and in 1990 he also became a professor at the University of Amsterdam. In 2005, he stepped down from management at CWI and instead became a CWI Fellow.[^o3][^o19]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Schrijver was one of the winners of the [Delbert Ray Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 1982 for his work with [Martin Grötschel](wikipedia://en/55085835) and [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938) on applications of the [ellipsoid method](wikipedia://en/4331203) to [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555); he won the same prize in 2003 (shared with Satoru Iwata, Lisa Fleischer, and Satoru Fujishige) for showing [submodular minimization](wikipedia://en/33273315#Optimization_problems) to be strongly polynomial.[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o14] He won the [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences) [Frederick W. Lanchester Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_W._Lanchester_Prize) in 1986 for his book *Theory of Linear and Integer Programming*, and again in 2004 for his book *Combinatorial Optimization: Polyhedra and Efficiency*. He was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in 1986 in Berkeley[^o12] and of the ICM in 1998 in Berlin.[^o13] In 2003, he won the George B. Dantzig Prize of the [Mathematical Programming Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Programming_Society) and [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) for "deep and fundamental research contributions to discrete optimization".[^o14] In 2006, he was a joint winner of the INFORMS John von Neumann Theory Prize with Grötschel and Lovász for their work in combinatorial optimization, and in particular for their joint work in the book *Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization* showing the polynomial-time equivalence of separation and optimization.[^o15] In 2008, his work with Adri Steenbeek on scheduling the [Dutch train system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen) was honored with INFORMS' [Franz Edelman Award for Achievement in Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Edelman_Award_for_Achievement_in_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences).[^o20][^o16] He won the SIGMA prize of the Dutch [SURF foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SURFnet) in 2008, for a mathematics education project.[^o17] In 2015 he won the [EURO Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EURO_Gold_Medal), the highest distinction within [Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research) in Europe.

In 2005 Schrijver won the [Spinoza Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinoza_Prize) of the [NWO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Organisatie_voor_Wetenschappelijk_Onderzoek), the highest scientific award in the Netherlands, for his research in combinatorics and algorithms.[^o18] Later in the same year he became a Knight of the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion).[^o19] In 2002, Schrijver received an honorary doctorate from the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada), and in 2011 he received another one from [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary).[^o20]

Schrijver became a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1995.[^o21] He became a corresponding member of the North Rhine-Westphalia Academy for Sciences and Arts in 2005,[^o22] joined the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina) in 2006,[^o23] and was elected to the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2008.[^o24] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o25]
