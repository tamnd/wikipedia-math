# S-unit

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="S-unit"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), an ***S*-unit** generalises the idea of [unit](wikipedia://en/289666) of the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of the field. Many of the results which hold for units are also valid for *S*-units.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *K* be a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) with ring of integers *R*. Let *S* be a finite set of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) of *R*. An element *x* of *K* is an *S*-unit if the [principal fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) (*x*) is a product of primes in *S* (to positive or negative powers). For the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [rational integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_integer) **Z** one may take *S* to be a finite set of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) and define an *S*-unit to be a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) whose numerator and denominator are [divisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisible) only by the primes in *S*.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The *S*-units form a multiplicative [group](wikipedia://en/19447) containing the units of *R*.

[Dirichlet's unit theorem](wikipedia://en/460227) holds for *S*-units: the group of *S*-units is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/58527), with [rank](wikipedia://en/244541) (maximal number of multiplicatively independent elements) equal to *r* + *s*, where *r* is the rank of the unit group and *s* = |*S*|.

:::

::: {#o7 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="S-unit equation"}

## S-unit equation {#S-unit_equation}

The ***S*-unit equation** is a [Diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109)

*u* + *v* = 1

with *u* and *v* restricted to being *S*-units of *K* (or more generally, elements of a finitely generated subgroup of the multiplicative group of any field of characteristic zero). The number of solutions of this equation is finite[^o8] and the solutions are effectively determined using estimates for [linear forms in logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_forms_in_logarithms) as developed in [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698). A variety of Diophantine equations are reducible in principle to some form of the *S*-unit equation: a notable example is [Siegel's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel's_theorem) on integral points on [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225), and more generally [superelliptic curves](wikipedia://en/35809693) of the form *y*<sup>*n*</sup> = *f*(*x*).

A computational solver for *S*-unit equation is available in the software [SageMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SageMath).[^o9]

:::
