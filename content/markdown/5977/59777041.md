# Joan & Joseph Birman Research Prize in Topology and Geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joan & Joseph Birman Research Prize in Topology and Geometry"}

The **Joan & Joseph Birman Research Prize in Topology and Geometry** is a prize given every other year by the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) to an outstanding young female researcher in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) or [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). The prize fund for the award was endowed by a donation in 2013 from [Joan Birman](wikipedia://en/8625257) and her husband, [Joseph Birman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Birman), and first awarded in 2015.

:::

## Winners {#Winners}

- [Elisenda Grigsby](wikipedia://en/54248237) (2015), for her research in low-dimensional [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), particularly in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) and categorified invariants.
- [Emmy Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Murphy) (2017), for her research in [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) where she developed new techniques for studying [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356) and [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398).
- [Kathryn Mann](wikipedia://en/61738901) (2019), for "major breakthroughs in the theory of dynamics of group actions on manifolds".
- [Emily Riehl](wikipedia://en/56785970) (2021), for "deep and foundational work in category theory and homotopy theory".[^o3]
- [Kristen Hendricks](wikipedia://en/73852031) (2023), for "highly influential work on equivariant aspects of Floer homology theories".[^o4]
- [Mona Merling](wikipedia://en/79646095) (2025), for "innovative and impactful research in algebraic K-theory, equivariant homotopy theory, and their applications to manifold theory".[^o5]
