# Talitha Washington

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Talitha Washington"}

**Talitha Washington** (born 1974) is an American mathematician and academic who specializes in applied mathematics and [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663) education policy.[^o3] She was recognized by [Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006) as a [Black History Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_History_Month) 2018 Honoree.[^o3] Washington became the 26th president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in 2023.[^o11][^o22]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Washington was born in [Frankfort, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort,_Indiana), and adopted at a young age by Ruthanne and [Walter Wangerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Wangerin_Jr.).[^o5][^o6] She was raised in [Evansville, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana), and attended [Benjamin Bosse High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Bosse_High_School).[^o7] After serving in [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica) with the [American Field Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFS_Intercultural_Programs), she earned a Bachelor of Science in Mathematics from [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College) in 1996. She then attended the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut), earning a master's degree in 1998 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 2001. Her doctoral thesis was *Mathematical Model of Proteins Acting as On/Off Switches,* under the supervision of Yung-Sze Choi.

Washington served on the faculty of [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) from 2001 to 2003, the [College of New Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_New_Rochelle) from 2003 to 2005, the [University of Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Evansville) from 2005 to 2011, and [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University), starting in 2011, where she became associate professor of mathematics.[^o3] After a few years on leave as a program director at the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation),[^o10] in 2020 she became the inaugural director of the [Atlanta University Center Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_University_Center_Consortium) (AUCC) Data Science Initiative. In 2022 she became president-elect of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) and assumed the presidency on February 1, 2023.[^o11][^o22] In 2025, Washington was named the executive director of the Howard University Center for Applied Data Science and Analytics and the Sean McCleese Endowed Chair in Computer Science, Race, and Social Justice.[^o12]

Washington's research interests include [nonstandard finite difference](wikipedia://en/16801509) (NSFD) schemes for certain systems of differential equations, including [population models](wikipedia://en/11397469), [one-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-dimensional) systems, and the [Black–Scholes equation](wikipedia://en/17656561).[^o3][^o13]

## Education policy and awards {#Education_policy_and_awards}

Washington is active in education policy, especially best practices on achieving racial and ethnic diversity in STEM.[^o15] At the NSF, she has served as co-lead of the Hispanic-Serving Institutions Program and is a graduate of the STEM diversity organization [SACNAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SACNAS).[^o16] She serves on the Council of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and served on the Executive Committee of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM).[^o17]

Washington helped to champion the once-forgotten mathematician [Elbert Frank Cox](wikipedia://en/2814552) (1895–1969) from her hometown of [Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana),[^o3] leading to the November 2006 unveiling of a plaque honoring the longtime Howard professor as the first [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American) scholar to earn a doctorate in mathematics.[^o18] She received the 2019 Black Engineer of the Year Awards STEM Innovator Award.[^o19]

Talitha Washington was named a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society), Class of 2021. Her citation read "For contributions to broadening the participation of underrepresented groups, and service to the mathematical profession".[^o20] Washington was also named a Fellow of the AWM, Class of 2021, "for her dedication to raise awareness of African American women in STEM; for her lifelong promotion of Historically Black Colleges and Universities; and for her unwavering dedication to the [National Association of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/58449356)."[^o21] On February 1, 2022, she became president-elect of the AWM.[^o22] She was elected to the 2022 class of Fellows of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (AAAS).[^o23]
