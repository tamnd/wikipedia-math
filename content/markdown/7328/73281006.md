# Stochastic analysis on manifolds

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stochastic analysis on manifolds"}

In mathematics, **stochastic analysis on manifolds** or **stochastic differential geometry** is the study of [stochastic analysis](wikipedia://en/228001) over [smooth manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219). It is therefore a synthesis of stochastic analysis (the extension of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) to [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895)) and of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

The connection between [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and stochastic processes stems from the fundamental relation that the [infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/12193437) of a continuous [strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Markov_property) [Markov process](wikipedia://en/60876) is a second-order [elliptic operator](wikipedia://en/662787). The infinitesimal generator of [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) is the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706) and the [transition probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_probability) density $p(t,x,y)$ of Brownian motion is the minimal [heat kernel](wikipedia://en/3904336) of the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415). Interpreting the paths of Brownian motion as [characteristic curves](wikipedia://en/751933) of the operator, Brownian motion can be seen as a stochastic counterpart of a flow to a second-order partial differential operator.

Stochastic analysis on manifolds investigates stochastic processes on non-linear state spaces or [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). Classical theory can be reformulated in a [coordinate-free](wikipedia://en/18858579) representation. In that, it is often complicated (or not possible) to formulate objects with coordinates of $\mathbb{R}^d$. Thus, we require an additional structure in form of a linear [connection](wikipedia://en/308815) or [Riemannian metric](wikipedia://en/144652) to define martingales and Brownian motion on manifolds. Therefore, controlled by the Riemannian metric, Brownian motion will be a local object by definition. However, its stochastic behaviour determines global aspects of the topology and geometry of the manifold.

Brownian motion is defined to be the [diffusion process](wikipedia://en/1667059) generated by the [Laplace-Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/15659323) $\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_M$ with respect to a manifold $M$ and can be constructed as the solution to a non-canonical [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454) on a Riemannian manifold. As there is no Hörmander representation of the operator $\Delta_M$ if the manifold is not *parallelizable*, i.e. if the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) is not trivial, there is no canonical procedure to construct Brownian motion. However, this obstacle can be overcome if the manifold is equipped with a connection: We can then introduce the *stochastic horizontal lift* of a [semimartingale](wikipedia://en/9653775) and the *stochastic development* by the so-called *Eells-Elworthy-Malliavin construction*.[^o2][^o3]

The latter is a generalisation of a [horizontal lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_lift) of smooth curves to horizontal curves in the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550), such that the *anti-development* and the horizontal lift are connected by a stochastic differential equation. Using this, we can consider an SDE on the [orthonormal frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), whose solution is Brownian motion, and projects down to the (base) manifold via *stochastic development*. A visual representation of this construction corresponds to the construction of a [spherical Brownian motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_Brownian_motion) by *[rolling without slipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_without_slipping)* the manifold along the paths (or footprints) of Brownian motion left in Euclidean space.[^o4]

Stochastic differential geometry provides insight into classical analytic problems, and offers new approaches to prove results by means of probability. For example, one can apply Brownian motion to the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/423504) at infinity for [Cartan-Hadamard manifolds](wikipedia://en/12471936)[^o5] or give a probabilistic proof of the [Atiyah-Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752).[^o6] Stochastic differential geometry also applies in other areas of mathematics (e.g. [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680)). For example, we can convert classical [arbitrage theory](wikipedia://en/3162) into differential-geometric language (also called *[geometric arbitrage theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_arbitrage_theory)*).[^o7]

## Preface {#Preface}

section For the reader's convenience and if not stated otherwise, let $(\Omega,\mathcal{A},(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\geq 0},\mathbb P)$ be a [filtered](wikipedia://en/57854817) [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) and $M$ be a smooth manifold. The filtration satisfies the usual conditions, i.e. it is [right-continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-continuous) and complete. We use the [Stratonovich integral](wikipedia://en/2314574) which obeys the classical [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113) (compared to [Itô calculus](wikipedia://en/1498680)). The main advantage for us lies in the fact that stochastic differential equations are then stable under [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) $f:M\to N$ between manifolds, i.e. if $X$ is a solution, then also $f(X)$ is a solution under transformations of the stochastic differential equation.

Notation:

- $TM$ is. the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of $M$.
- $T^*M$ is the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of $M$.
- $\Gamma(TM)$ is the $C^{\infty}(M)$-module of [vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_fields) on $M$.
- $X \circ dZ$ is the Stratonovich integral.
- $C^{\infty}_c(M)$ is the space of [test functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_functions) on $M$, i.e. $f\in C^{\infty}_c(M)$ is smooth and has compact support.
- $\widehat{M}:=M\cup \{\infty\}$ is the [one-point compactification](wikipedia://en/55611) (or Alexandroff compactification).

## Flow processes {#Flow_processes}

*Flow processes* (also called $L$*\-diffusions*) are the probabilistic counterpart of [integral curves](wikipedia://en/1322061) ([flow lines](wikipedia://en/2619735)) of vector fields. In contrast, a flow process is defined with respect to a [second-order differential operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-order_differential_equation), and thus, generalises the notion of deterministic flows being defined with respect to a [first-order operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_order_differential_equation).

### Partial differential operator in Hörmander form {#Partial_differential_operator_in_Hörmander_form}

Let $A\in \Gamma(TM)$ be a vector field, understood as a derivation by the $C^{\infty}(M)$-[isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)

$$\Gamma(TM)\to \operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{R}} C^{\infty}(M),\quad A\mapsto (f\mapsto Af)$$

for some $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$. The map $Af:M\to \mathbb{R}$ is defined by $Af(x):=A_x(f)$. For the composition, we set $A^2:=A(A(f))$ for some $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$.

A [partial differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) (PDO) $L:C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)$ is given in *Hörmander form* if and only there are vector fields $A_0,A_1,\dots,A_r\in \Gamma(TM)$ and $L$ can be written in the form

$$L=A_0+\sum\limits_{i=1}^r A_i^2.$$

### Flow process {#Flow_process}

Let $L$ be a PDO in Hörmander form on $M$ and $x\in M$ a starting point. An adapted and continuous $M$-valued process $X$ with $X_0=x$ is called a *flow process to $L$ starting in $x$*, if for every test function $f\in C^{\infty}_c(M)$ and $t\in\mathbb{R}_+$ the process

$$N(f)_t:=f(X_t)-f(X_0)-\int_0^t Lf(X_r)\mathrm{d}r$$

is a martingale, i.e.

$$\mathbb{E}\left(N(f)_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s\right)=N(f)_s,\quad \forall s\leq t.$$

### Remark {#Remark}

For a test function $f\in C^{\infty}_c(M)$, a PDO $L$ in Hörmander form and a flow process $X_t^x$ (starting in $x$) also holds the flow equation, but in comparison to the deterministic case *only in mean*

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{E} f(X_t^x) = \mathbb{E}\left[Lf(X_t^x)\right].$$

and we can recover the PDO by taking the [time derivative](wikipedia://en/1783069) at time 0, i.e.

$$\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\mathbb{E}f(X_t^x)=Lf(x).$$

### Lifetime and explosion time {#Lifetime_and_explosion_time}

Let $\emptyset \neq U\subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be open und $\xi>0$ a predictable [stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193). We call $\xi$ the *lifetime* of a continuous semimartingale $X=(X_t)_{0\leq t<\xi}$ on $U$ if

- there is a sequence of stopping times $(\xi_n)$ with $\xi_n\nearrow\xi$, such that $\xi_n< \xi$ $\mathbb P$-almost surely on $\{0<\xi<\infty\}$.
- the stopped process $(X_{t\wedge \xi_n})$ is a semimartingale.

Moreover, if $X_{\xi_n(\omega)}\to \partial U$ for almost all $\omega\in\{\xi<\infty\}$, we call $\xi$ *explosion time*.

A flow process $X$ can have a finite lifetime $\xi$. By this we mean that $X=(X)_{t<\xi}$ is defined such that if $t\to \xi$, then $\mathbb P$-almost surely on $\{\xi<\infty\}$ we have $X_t\to \infty$ in the one-point compactification $\widehat{M}:=M\cup \{\infty\}$. In that case we extend the process path-wise by $X_t:=\infty$ for $t\geq \xi$.

### Semimartingales on a manifold {#Semimartingales_on_a_manifold}

A process $X$ is a s*emimartingale on $M$*, if for every $f\in C^2(M)$ the random variable $f(X)$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-semimartingale, i.e. the composition of any smooth function $f$ with the process $X$ is a real-valued semimartingale. It can be shown that any $M$-semimartingale is a solution of a stochastic differential equation on $M$. If the semimartingale is only defined up to a finite lifetime $\xi$, we can always construct a semimartingale with infinite lifetime by a transformation of time. A semimartingale has a quadratic variation with respect to a section in the bundle of [bilinear forms](wikipedia://en/449477) on $TM$.

Introducing the *Stratonovich Integral of a [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) $\alpha$ along the semimartingale $X$* we can study the so called *winding behaviour* of $X$, i.e. a generalisation of the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113).

### Stratonovich integral of a 1-form {#Stratonovich_integral_of_a_1-form}

Let $X$ be an $M$-valued semimartingale and $\alpha\in\Gamma(T^*M)$ be a [1-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-form). We call the integral $\int_X\alpha:=\int \alpha (\circ dX)$ the *Stratonovich integral of $\alpha$ along $X$*. For $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$ we define $f(X)\circ \alpha(\circ dX):=f(X)\circ d(\int_X\alpha)$.

## SDEs on a manifold {#SDEs_on_a_manifold}

A *stochastic differential equation on a manifold* $M$, denoted *SDE on $M$*, is defined by the pair $(A,Z)$ including a bundle homomorphism (i.e. a *homomorphism of vector bundles*) or the ($r+1$)-tuple $(A_1,\dots,A_r,Z)$ with vector fields $A_1,\dots,A_r$ given. Using the [Whitney embedding](wikipedia://en/477578), we can show that there is a unique maximal solution to every SDE on $M$ with [initial condition](wikipedia://en/1186804) $X_0=x$. If we have identified the maximal solution, we recover directly a flow process $X^x$ to the operator $L$.

::: {#o18 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

An *SDE on $M$* is a pair $(A,Z)$, where

- $Z=(Z_t)_{t\in\mathbb{R}_+}$ is a continuous semimartingale on a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{R}$-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $E$; and
- $A:M\times E\to TM$ is a (smooth) [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over $M$

$$A:(x,e)\mapsto A(x)e$$

where$A(x):E\to TM$ is a linear map.

The stochastic differential equation $(A,Z)$ is denoted by

$$dX=A(X)\circ dZ$$

or

$$dX=\sum\limits_{i=1}^r A_i(X)\circ dZ^i.$$

The latter follows from setting $A_i:=A(\cdot)e_i$ with respect to a basis $(e_i)_{i=1,\dots,r}$ and $\mathbb{R}$-valued semimartingales $(Z^{i})_{i=1,\dots,r}$ with $Z=\sum\limits_{i=1}^rZ^{i}e_i$.

As for given vector fields $A_1,\dots,A_r\in \Gamma(TM)$ there is exactly one bundle homomorphism $A$ such that $A_i:=A(\cdot)e_i$, our definition of an SDE on $M$ as $(A_1,\dots,A_r,Z)$ is plausible.

If $Z$ has only finite life time, we can transform the time horizon into the infinite case.[^o19]

:::

### Solutions {#Solutions}

Let $(A,Z)$ be an SDE on $M$ and $x_0:\Omega\to M$ an $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685). Let $(X_t)_{t<\zeta}$ be a continuous adapted $M$-valued process with life time $\zeta$ on the same probability space such as $Z$. Then $(X_t)_{t<\zeta}$ is called a *solution* to the SDE

$$dX=A(X)\circ dZ$$

with initial condition $X_0=x_0$ up to the life time $\zeta$, if for every test function $f \in C^{\infty}_c(M)$ the process $f(X)$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-valued semimartingale and for every stopping time $\tau$ with $0\leq \tau < \zeta$, it holds $\mathbb P$-almost surely

$$f(X_\tau) = f(x_0) + \int_0^\tau (df)_{X_s} A(X_s)\circ \mathrm{d}Z_s,$$

where $(df)_X:T_xM\to T_{f(x)}M$ is the [push-forward](wikipedia://en/1558510) (or differential) at the point $X$. Following the idea from above, by definition $f(X)$ is a semimartingale for every test function $f\in C_c^{\infty}(M)$, so that $X$ is a *semimartingale on $M$*.

If the lifetime is maximal, i.e.

$$\{\zeta <\infty\}\subset\left\{\lim\limits_{t\nearrow \zeta}X_t=\infty \text{ in }\widehat{M}\right\}$$

$\mathbb P$-almost surely, we call this solution the *maximal solution*. The lifetime of a maximal solution $X$ can be extended to all of $\mathbb{R}_+$ , and after extending $f$ to the whole of $\widehat{M}$, the equation

$$f(X_{t})=f(X_0)+\int_0^t (df)_X A(X)\circ dZ, \quad t\geq 0,$$

holdsup to indistinguishability.[^o21]

### Remark {#Remark}

Let $Z=(t,B)$ with a $d$-dimensional Brownian motion $B=(B_1,\dots,B_d)$, then we can show that every maximal solution starting in $x_0$ is a flow process to the operator

$$L=A_0+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^d A_i^2.$$

## Martingales and Brownian motion {#Martingales_and_Brownian_motion}

Brownian motion on manifolds are stochastic flow processes to the [Laplace-Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/15659323). It is possible to construct Brownian motion on [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M,g)$. However, to follow a canonical [ansatz](wikipedia://en/1359360), we need some additional structure. Let $\mathcal{O}(d)$ be the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954); we consider the canonical SDE on the [orthonormal frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) $O(M)$ over $M$, whose solution is Brownian motion. The orthonormal frame bundle is the collection of all sets $O_x(M)$ of [orthonormal frames](wikipedia://en/366550) of the tangent space $T_xM$

$$O(M):=\bigcup\limits_{x\in M}O_x(M)$$

or in other words, the $\mathcal{O}(d)$-[principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) associated to $TM$.

*Image: Eells-Elworthy-Malliavin-construction.jpg.* The Eells-Elworthy-Malliavin construction of the Brownian motion on manifolds

Let $W$ be an $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued semimartingale. The solution $U$ of the SDE

$$dU_t = \sum\limits_{i=1}^d A_i(U_t)\circ dW_t^i,\quad U_0=u_0,$$

defined by the projection $\pi:O(M)\to M$ of a Brownian motion $X$ on the Riemannian manifold, is the *stochastic development* from $W$ on $M$. Conversely we call $W$ the *anti-development* of $U$ or, respectively, $\pi(U)=X$. In short, we get the following relations: $W\leftrightarrow U \leftrightarrow X$, where

- $U$ is an $O(M)$-valued semimartingale; and
- $X$ is an $M$-valued semimartingale.

For a Riemannian manifold we always use the [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176) and the corresponding Laplace-Beltrami operator $\Delta_M$. The key observation is that there exists a lifted version of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the orthonormal frame bundle. The fundamental relation reads, for $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$,

$$\Delta_M f(x)=\Delta_{O(M)}(f\circ \pi)(u)$$

for all $u\in O(M)$ with $\pi u=x$, and the operator $\Delta_{O(M)}$ on $O(M)$ is well-defined for so-called *horizontal vector fields*. The operator $\Delta_{O(M)}$ is called *Bochner's horizontal Laplace operator*.

### Martingales with linear connection {#Martingales_with_linear_connection}

To define martingales, we need a linear connection $\nabla$. Using the connection, we can characterise $\nabla$-martingales, if their anti-development is a local martingale. It is also possible to define $\nabla$-martingales without using the anti-development.

We write $\stackrel{m}{=}$ to indicate that equality holds [modulo](wikipedia://en/679987) differentials of [local martingales](wikipedia://en/7302795).

Let $X$ be an $M$-valued semimartingale. Then $X$ is a *martingale* or *$\nabla$-martingale*, if and only if for every $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$, it holds that

$$d(f\circ X)\,\,\stackrel{m}{=}\,\,\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla df)(dX,dX).$$

### Brownian motion on a Riemannian manifold {#Brownian_motion_on_a_Riemannian_manifold}

Let $(M,g)$ be a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with Laplace-Beltrami operator $\Delta_{M}$. An adapted $M$-valued process $X$ with maximal lifetime $\xi$ is called a *Brownian motion$(M,g)$*, if for every $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$

$$f(X)-\frac{1}{2}\int\Delta_{M} f(X)\mathrm{d}t$$

is a local $\mathbb{R}$-martingale with life time $\xi$. Hence, Brownian motion Bewegung is the diffusion process to $\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{M}$. Note that this characterisation does not provide a canonical procedure to define Brownian motion.
