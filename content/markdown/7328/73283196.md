# Ascon (cipher)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Ascon"}

**Ascon** is a family of [lightweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightweight_cipher) [authenticated ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authenticated_cipher) and [hash functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hash_function) that have been selected by the U.S. [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology) (NIST) for cryptography on resource-constrained devices in 2025, specified in NIST SP 800-232.[^n1][^o5][^o6]

:::

## History {#History}

Ascon was developed in 2014 by a team of researchers from [Graz University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz_University_of_Technology), [Infineon Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infineon_Technologies), Lamarr Security Research, and [Radboud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University).[^n2] The cipher family was chosen as a finalist of the [CAESAR Competition](wikipedia://en/70035770)[^n2] in February 2019.

NIST announced its decision on February 7, 2023[^n2] with the following steps that lead to its standardization:[^n1]

- Publication of NIST IR 8454[^o12] describing the process of evaluation and selection that was used;
- Preparation of a new draft[^o13] for public comments[^o14];
- Public workshop held on June 21–22, 2023.<https://csrc.nist.gov/events/2023/lightweight-cryptography-workshop-2023>

NIST finalized the standard on August 13, 2025, releasing it as "Ascon-Based Lightweight Cryptography Standards for Constrained Devices" (NIST Special Publication 800-232).[^o15]

## Design {#Design}

The design is based on a [sponge construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge_construction) along the lines of SpongeWrap and MonkeyDuplex. This design makes it easy to reuse Ascon in multiple ways (as a cipher, [hash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_hash), or a [MAC](wikipedia://en/567523)).[^n3] As of February 2023, the Ascon suite contained seven ciphers,[^n2] including:[^n4]

- Ascon-128 and Ascon-128a authenticated ciphers;
- Ascon-Hash cryptographic hash;
- Ascon-Xof [extendable-output function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extendable-output_function);
- Ascon-80pq cipher with an "increased" 160-bit key.

The main components have been borrowed from other designs:[^n3]

- substitution layer utilizes a modified [S-box](wikipedia://en/240991) from the $\chi$ function of [Keccak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keccak);
- permutation layer functions are similar to the $\Sigma$ of [SHA-2](wikipedia://en/1638777).

### Parameterization {#Parameterization}

The ciphers are parameterizable by the [key length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_length) *k* (up to 128 bits), "rate" ([block size](wikipedia://en/294065)) *r*, and two numbers of [rounds](wikipedia://en/73297602) *a*, *b*. All algorithms support [authenticated encryption](wikipedia://en/2244296) with plaintext P and [additional authenticated data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_authenticated_data) A (that remains unencrypted). The encryption input also includes a public [nonce](wikipedia://en/6988390) N, the output - [authentication tag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentication_tag) T, size of the ciphertext C is the same as that of P. The decryption uses N, A, C, and T as inputs and produces either P or signals verification failure if the message has been altered. Nonce and tag have the same size as the key K (*k* bits).[^n5]

In the CAESAR submission, two sets of parameters were recommended:[^n5]

*Suggested parameters, bits*

::: {#o24 .table cue=table name="Suggested parameters, bits"}

| Name | *k* | *r* | *a* | *b* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ascon-128 | 128 | 64 | 12 | 6 |
| Ascon-128a | 128 | 128 | 12 | 8 |

:::

### Padding {#Padding}

The data in both A and P is padded with a single bit with the value of 1 and a number of zeros to the nearest multiple of $r$ bits. As an exception, if A is an empty string, there is no padding at all.[^n6]

### State {#State}

The state consists of 320 bits, so the capacity $c=320-r$.[^n7] The state is initialized by an initialization vector IV (constant for each cipher type, e.g., hex 80400c0600000000 for Ascon-128) concatenated with K and N.[^n8]

### Transformation {#Transformation}

The initial state is transformed by applying $a$ times the transformation function $p$ ($p^a$). On encryption, each word of A || P is XORed into the state and the $p$ is applied $b$ times ($p^b$). The ciphertext C is contained in the first $r$ bits of the result of the XOR. Decryption is near-identical to encryption.[^n7] The final stage that produces the tag T consists of another application of $p^a$; the special values are XORed into the last $c$ bits after the initialization, the end of A, and before the finalization.[^n6]

Transformation $p$ consists of three layers:

- $p_C$, XORing the [round constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_constant);
- $p_S$, application of 5-bit [S-boxes](wikipedia://en/240991);
- $p_L$, application of [linear diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_diffusion).
