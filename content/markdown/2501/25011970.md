# Michael Saks (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Saks"}

**Michael Ezra Saks** is an American mathematician. He is currently the Department Chair of the Mathematics Department at Rutgers University (2017–) and from 2006 until 2010 was director of the Mathematics Graduate Program at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University). Saks received his Ph.D. from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1980 after completing his dissertation titled *Duality Properties of Finite Set Systems*[^o2] under his advisor [Daniel J. Kleitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_J._Kleitman).

:::

A list of his publications and collaborations may be found at [DBLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DBLP).

In 2016 he became a [Fellow of the Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Association_for_Computing_Machinery).[^o4][^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Saks' research in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) has contributed to the study of lower bounds in [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924), [randomized computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_computation), and [space–time tradeoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space–time_tradeoff).

In 1984, Saks and [Jeff Kahn](wikipedia://en/26919476) showed that there exist a tight information-theoretical lower bound for sorting under [partially ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partially_ordered) information up to a multiplicative constant.[^o7]

In <http://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.114.5448&rep=rep1&type=pdf> the first super-linear lower bound for the [noisy broadcast problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noisy_broadcast_problem) was proved. In a noisy broadcast model, $n+1$ processors $P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_n$ are assigned a local input bit $x_i$. Each processor may perform a *noisy broadcast* to all other processors where the received bits may be independently flipped with a fixed probability. The problem is for processor $P_0$ to determine $f(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$ for some function $f$. Saks et al. showed that an existing protocol by Gallager was indeed optimal by a reduction from a generalized noisy [decision tree](wikipedia://en/232602) and produced a $\Omega(n\log(n))$ lower bound on the depth of the tree that learns the input.[^o8]

In 2003, P. Beame, Saks, X. Sun, and E. Vee published the first time–space lower bound trade-off for randomized computation of decision problems was proved.[^o9]
