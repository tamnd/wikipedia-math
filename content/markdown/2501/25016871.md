# Up tack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Up tack"}

"**Up tack**" is the [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) name for a symbol (**⊥**, `\bot` in [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX), U+22A5 in Unicode[^o2]) that is also called "**bottom**",[^o3][^o4] "**falsum**",[^o5] "**absurdum**",[^o6] or "**absurdity**",[^o7][^o8][^o4] depending on context. It is used to represent:

:::

- The [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) ['false'](wikipedia://en/229823), or a logical constant denoting a proposition in logic that is always false. (The names "falsum", "absurdum" and "absurdity" come from this context.)
- The [bottom element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_element) in [wheel theory](wikipedia://en/634240) and [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory), which also represents absurdum when used for logical semantics
- The [bottom type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_type) in [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282), which is the bottom element in the subtype relation. This may coincide with the [empty type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_type), which represents absurdum under the [Curry–Howard correspondence](wikipedia://en/254299)
- The "undefined value" in quantum physics interpretations that reject [counterfactual definiteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfactual_definiteness), as in (**r<sub>0</sub>**,⊥)

as well as

- [Mixed radix](wikipedia://en/249726) decoding in the [APL programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APL_%28programming_language%29)

The [glyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) of the up tack appears as an upside-down [tee symbol](wikipedia://en/19145299), and as such is sometimes called **eet** (the word "tee" in reverse).[^o9][^o10] Tee plays a complementary or [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28mathematics%29) role in many of these theories.

The similar-looking **perpendicular symbol** (**⟂**, `\perp` in LaTeX, U+27C2 in Unicode) is a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) symbol used to represent:

- [Perpendicularity](wikipedia://en/76944) of lines in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446)
- [Orthogonality](wikipedia://en/102221) in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422)
- [Independence](wikipedia://en/27593) of [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542)
- [Coprimality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprimality) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527)

Historically, in character sets before Unicode 4.1 (March 2005), such as Unicode 4.0[^o11] and JIS X 0213, the perpendicular symbol was encoded with the same code point as the up tack, specifically U+22A5 in Unicode 4.0.[^o12] This overlap is reflected in the fact that both HTML entities `&perp;` and `&bot;` refer to the same code point U+22A5, as shown in the [HTML entity list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_XML_and_HTML_character_entity_references#cite_ref-perp_50-0). In March 2005, Unicode 4.1 introduced the distinct symbol "⟂" (U+27C2 "PERPENDICULAR") with a reference back to ⊥ (U+22A5 "UP TACK") and a note that "typeset with additional spacing."[^o13]

The **double tack up** symbol (**⫫**, U+2AEB in [Unicode](wikipedia://en/5921376)[^o2]) is a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) symbol used to represent:

- [Conditional independence](wikipedia://en/801135) of [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542)[^o14]
