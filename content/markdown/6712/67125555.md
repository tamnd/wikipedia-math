# Eugenie Maria Morenus

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eugenie Maria Morenus"}

**Eugenie Maria Morenus** (February 21, 1881 – October 15, 1966) was an American mathematician and college professor and one of the few women to earn a PhD in math before World War II. She taught Latin and mathematics at [Sweet Briar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Briar_College) from 1909 to 1946.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Morenus was born in [Cleveland, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_New_York), the daughter of Eugene Morenus and Maria Euphemia Van Blarcom Morenus.[^o4] Her father managed a glassworks. She graduated from [Monogahela High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela,_Pennsylvania) in 1898.[^o5] She earned a bachelor's degree from [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College) in 1904, and a master's degree from the same school in 1905.[^o6] She completed doctoral studies in mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1922. Her dissertation under [Edward Kasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kasner) was titled "Geometric properties completely characterizing the set of all the curves of constant pressure in a field of force".[^o7][^o8]

Morenus was also a student for briefer periods at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), and at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities).[^o9]

## Career {#Career}

Morenus taught mathematics and Latin at a school in [Watertown, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watertown_%28city%29,_New_York) and at [Poughkeepsie High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poughkeepsie_High_School) after her master's degree.[^o6] She was a Latin instructor at Sweet Briar College from 1909 to 1916, and was a mathematics professor at the same school from 1916 to 1946.[^o11][^o12] She was head of the mathematics department for much of that time. While at the school she was prominent in campus events, as a chorister, photographer, and play director. Her horse, October or "Toby", was a familiar figure on campus, and Morenus would lead ten-day rides for students over spring breaks.[^o13]

Morenus was a charter member of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), belonged to the [Virginia Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Academy_of_Science), and was active in the [American Association of University Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Women) (AAUW).[^o6] She was active in the [Order of the Eastern Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Eastern_Star) and the [Daughters of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughters_of_the_American_Revolution).[^o14] She received an [Anna Brackett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Brackett) Fellowship by the AAUW in 1927, to study at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o15][^o16] After her retirement from Sweet Briar College in 1946, she taught briefly at Connecticut College for Women, and spent her winters in Florida.[^o6]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Morenus died in [Lake Wales, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Wales,_Florida) in 1966, aged 85 years.[^o18][^o19] There was a scholarship endowment fund named for Morenus at Sweet Briar College, beginning in 1960.[^o6]
