# Rokhlin's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rokhlin's theorem"}

In 4-dimensional topology, a branch of mathematics, **Rokhlin's theorem** states that if a [smooth](wikipedia://en/2119219), orientable, closed 4-[manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* has a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) (equivalently, if the second [Stiefel–Whitney class](wikipedia://en/877761) $w_2(M)$ vanishes), then the [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) of its [intersection form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form_%284-manifold%29), a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) on the second [cohomology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) $H^2(M)$, is divisible by 16. The theorem is named for [Vladimir Rokhlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Rokhlin_%28Soviet_mathematician%29), who proved it in 1952.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [intersection form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form_%284-manifold%29) on *M*

$$Q_M\colon H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})\times H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow \mathbb{Z}$$

is [unimodular](wikipedia://en/1648605) on $\mathbb{Z}$ by [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732), and the vanishing of $w_2(M)$ implies that the intersection form is even. By a theorem of [Cahit Arf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahit_Arf), any even unimodular lattice has signature divisible by 8, so Rokhlin's theorem forces one extra factor of 2 to divide the signature.

- A [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116) is compact, 4 dimensional, and $w_2(M)$ vanishes, and the signature is −16, so 16 is the best possible number in Rokhlin's theorem.
- A complex surface in $\mathbb{CP}^3$ of degree $d$ is spin [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $d$ is even. It has signature $(4-d^2)d/3$, which can be seen from [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495)'s [signature theorem](wikipedia://en/5574857). The case $d=4$ gives back the last example of a [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116).
- [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970)'s [E8 manifold](wikipedia://en/6091657) is a [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) compact [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193) with vanishing $w_2(M)$ and intersection form $E_8$ of signature 8. Rokhlin's theorem implies that this manifold has no [smooth structure](wikipedia://en/1542238). This manifold shows that Rokhlin's theorem fails for the set of merely topological (rather than smooth) manifolds.
- If the manifold *M* is simply connected (or more generally if the first homology group has no 2-torsion), then the vanishing of $w_2(M)$ is equivalent to the intersection form being even. This is not true in general: an [Enriques surface](wikipedia://en/3094760) is a compact smooth 4 manifold and has even intersection form II<sub>1,9</sub> of signature −8 (not divisible by 16), but the class $w_2(M)$ does not vanish and is represented by a [torsion element](wikipedia://en/3039330) in the second cohomology group.

:::

## Proofs {#Proofs}

Rokhlin's theorem can be deduced from the fact that the third [stable homotopy group of spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_group_of_spheres) $\pi^S_3$ is cyclic of order 24; this is Rokhlin's original approach.

## The Rokhlin invariant {#The_Rokhlin_invariant}

Since Rokhlin's theorem states that the signature of a spin smooth manifold is divisible by 16, the definition of the **Rokhlin invariant** is deduced as follows:

For 3-manifold $N$ and a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) $s$ on $N$, the Rokhlin invariant $\mu(N,s)$ in $\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}$ is defined to be the signature of any smooth compact spin [4-manifold](wikipedia://en/1362795) with spin boundary $(N,s)$.

If *N* is a [spin](wikipedia://en/2844303) 3-manifold then it bounds a spin 4-manifold *M*. The signature of *M* is divisible by 8, and an easy application of Rokhlin's theorem shows that its value mod 16 depends only on *N* and not on the choice of *M*. Homology 3-spheres have a unique [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) so we can define the Rokhlin invariant of a homology [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) to be the element $\operatorname{sign}(M)/8$ of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, where *M* any spin 4-manifold bounding the homology sphere.

For example, the [Poincaré homology sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_homology_sphere) bounds a spin 4-manifold with intersection form $E_8$, so its Rokhlin invariant is 1. This result has some elementary consequences: the Poincaré homology sphere does not admit a smooth embedding in $S^4$, nor does it bound a [Mazur manifold](wikipedia://en/14204445).

More generally, if *N* is a [spin](wikipedia://en/2844303) 3-manifold (for example, any $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ homology sphere), then the signature of any spin 4-manifold *M* with boundary *N* is well defined mod 16, and is called the Rokhlin invariant of *N*. On a topological 3-manifold *N*, the **generalized Rokhlin invariant** refers to the function whose domain is the [spin structures](wikipedia://en/2844303) on *N*, and which evaluates to the Rokhlin invariant of the pair $(N,s)$ where *s* is a spin structure on *N*.

The Rokhlin invariant of M is equal to half the [Casson invariant](wikipedia://en/7066452) mod 2. The Casson invariant is viewed as the **Z**-valued lift of the Rokhlin invariant of integral homology 3-sphere.

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The **Kervaire–Milnor theorem** (Kervaire Milnor 1960) states that if $\Sigma$ is a characteristic sphere in a smooth compact 4-manifold *M*, then

$$\operatorname{signature}(M) = \Sigma \cdot \Sigma \bmod 16.$$

A characteristic sphere is an embedded 2-sphere whose homology class represents the Stiefel–Whitney class $w_2(M)$. If $w_2(M)$ vanishes, we can take $\Sigma$ to be any small sphere, which has self intersection number 0, so Rokhlin's theorem follows.

The **Freedman–Kirby theorem** (Freedman Kirby 1978) states that if $\Sigma$ is a characteristic surface in a smooth compact 4-manifold *M*, then

$$\operatorname{signature}(M) = \Sigma \cdot \Sigma + 8\operatorname{Arf}(M,\Sigma) \bmod 16.$$

where $\operatorname{Arf}(M,\Sigma)$ is the [Arf invariant](wikipedia://en/6950659) of a certain quadratic form on $H_1(\Sigma, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$. This Arf invariant is obviously 0 if $\Sigma$ is a sphere, so the Kervaire–Milnor theorem is a special case.

A generalization of the Freedman-Kirby theorem to topological (rather than smooth) manifolds states that

$$\operatorname{signature}(M) = \Sigma \cdot \Sigma + 8\operatorname{Arf}(M,\Sigma) + 8\operatorname{ks}(M) \bmod 16,$$

where $\operatorname{ks}(M)$ is the [Kirby–Siebenmann invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirby–Siebenmann_invariant) of *M*. The Kirby–Siebenmann invariant of *M* is 0 if *M* is smooth.

[Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) and [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) proved the following theorem: If *X* is a smooth compact [spin manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold) of dimension divisible by 4 then the [Â genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Â_genus) is an integer, and is even if the dimension of *X* is 4 mod 8. This can be deduced from the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752): [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Isadore Singer](wikipedia://en/324564) showed that the Â genus is the index of the Atiyah–Singer operator, which is always integral, and is even in dimensions 4 mod 8. For a 4-dimensional manifold, the [Hirzebruch signature theorem](wikipedia://en/7192444) shows that the signature is −8 times the Â genus, so in dimension 4 this implies Rokhlin's theorem.

(Ochanine 1980) proved that if *X* is a compact oriented smooth spin manifold of dimension 4 mod 8, then its signature is divisible by 16.
