# Donald Rubin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Donald Rubin"}

**Donald Bruce Rubin** (born December 22, 1943) is an American statistician, Emeritus Professor of Statistics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University),[^o3] where he chaired the department of statistics for 13 years.[^o4] He also works at [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University) in China and at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) in Philadelphia.[^o5]

:::

He is most well known for the [Rubin causal model](wikipedia://en/5700418), a set of methods designed for [causal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal) [inference](wikipedia://en/317465) with [observational data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_data), and for his methods for dealing with [missing data](wikipedia://en/7859676).

## Biography {#Biography}

Rubin was born in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) into a Jewish family of lawyers.[^o7] As an undergraduate Rubin attended the accelerated [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) program where he was one of a cohort of 20 students mentored by the [physicist John Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler) (the intention of the program was to confer degrees within 5 years of [freshman](wikipedia://en/11541500) [matriculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation)). He switched to [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) and graduated in 1965. He began graduate school in psychology at Harvard with a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) fellowship, but because his statistics background was considered insufficient, he was asked to take introductory statistics courses.

Rubin became a PhD student again, this time in statistics under [William Cochran](wikipedia://en/2223833) at the Harvard Statistics Department. After graduating from Harvard in 1970, he began working at the [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service) in 1971, and served as a visiting faculty member at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University)'s new statistics department. He published his major papers on the [Rubin causal model](wikipedia://en/5700418) in 1974–1980, seminal papers on [propensity score matching](wikipedia://en/16722927) in the early 1980s with [Paul Rosenbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_R._Rosenbaum), and a textbook on the subject with Nobel prize winning econometrician [Guido Imbens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Imbens) in 2015.[^o8]

In 1977 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o9] In 2007, Rubin was awarded the [George W. Snedecor Award](wikipedia://en/47945620) by the [Committee of Presidents of Statistical Societies](wikipedia://en/44155344).[^o10]
