# Homoeoid and focaloid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Homoeoid and focaloid"}

*Image: homoeoid3d.jpg.* Cut view of a homoeoid in 3D

*Image: Focaloid3d.jpg.* Cut view of a focaloid in 3D

A **homoeoid** or **homeoid** is a shell (a bounded region) bounded by two [concentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric), [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) [ellipses](wikipedia://en/9277) (in 2D) or [ellipsoids](wikipedia://en/145381) (in 3D).[^o2][^o3] When the thickness of the shell becomes negligible, it is called a **thin homoeoid**. The name homoeoid was coined by [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin) and [Peter Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Tait_%28physicist%29).[^o4] Closely related is the **focaloid**, a shell between concentric, [confocal](wikipedia://en/39739) ellipses or ellipsoids.[^o5]

:::

## Mathematical definition {#Mathematical_definition}

If the outer shell is given by

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 1$$

with semiaxes $a,b,c$, the inner shell of a homoeoid is given for $0 \leq m \leq 1$ by

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} = m^2,\quad {\displaystyle {\frac{x^2}{a^2 + \lambda }}+{\frac{y^2}{b^2 + \lambda }}+{\frac{z^2}{c^2 + \lambda }}=1.}$$

and a focaloid is defined for $\lambda \geq 0$ by

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2 + \lambda} + \frac{y^2}{b^2 + \lambda} + \frac{z^2}{c^2 + \lambda} = 1.$$

The thin homoeoid is then given by the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602) $m \to 1$, and the thin focaloid is the limit $\lambda \to 0$.[^o4]

## Physical properties {#Physical_properties}

Thin focaloids and homoeoids can be used as elements of an ellipsoidal matter or charge distribution that generalize the [shell theorem](wikipedia://en/2025989) for spherical shells. The gravitational or electromagnetic [potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential) of a homoeoid homogeneously filled with matter or charge is constant inside the shell, so there is no force on a test particle inside of it.[^o8] Meanwhile, two uniform, concentric focaloids with the same mass or charge exert the same potential on a test particle outside of both.[^o5][^o2]
