# Boolean-valued function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boolean-valued function"}

A **Boolean-valued function** (sometimes called a [predicate](wikipedia://en/285109) or a [proposition](wikipedia://en/81094)) is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of the type f : X → **B**, where X is an arbitrary [set](wikipedia://en/26691) and where **B** is a [Boolean domain](wikipedia://en/3954077), i.e. a generic two-element set, (for example **B** = {0, 1}), whose elements are interpreted as [logical values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_value), for example, 0 = [false](wikipedia://en/229823) and 1 = [true](wikipedia://en/161711), i.e., a single [bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit) of [information](wikipedia://en/18985062).

:::

In the [formal sciences](wikipedia://en/3694774), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), and their applied disciplines, a Boolean-valued function may also be referred to as a characteristic function, [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790), predicate, or proposition. In all of these uses, it is understood that the various terms refer to a mathematical object and not the corresponding [semiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotic) sign or syntactic expression.

In [formal semantic](wikipedia://en/29107) theories of [truth](wikipedia://en/29920), a **truth predicate** is a predicate on the [sentences](wikipedia://en/2129527) of a [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939), interpreted for logic, that formalizes the intuitive concept that is normally expressed by saying that a sentence is true. A truth predicate may have additional domains beyond the formal language domain, if that is what is required to determine a final [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711).
