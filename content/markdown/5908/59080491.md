# Anne Bosworth Focke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anne Bosworth Focke"}

**Anne Lucy Bosworth Focke** (September 29, 1868 – May 15, 1907) was an American mathematician who became the first mathematics professor at what is now the [University of Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rhode_Island), and later became the first female doctoral student of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Bosworth was originally from [Woonsocket, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woonsocket,_Rhode_Island). When she was four, her father and a younger sister died, and she grew up in a family of women: her mother (a librarian), her grandmother (also widowed), and her aunt.

## Undergraduate education and academic work {#Undergraduate_education_and_academic_work}

Bosworth attended [Woonsocket High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woonsocket_High_School), and graduated from [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) in 1890. At Wellesley, her classmates included mathematicians [Grace Andrews](wikipedia://en/31861687) and [Clara Latimer Bacon](wikipedia://en/58789158).

She worked for two years as a teacher at [Amesbury High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amesbury_High_School) in Massachusetts, and was appointed as an instructor of mathematics at the Rhode Island College of Agriculture and Mechanic Arts (later to become the [University of Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rhode_Island)) in early 1892, the first year the school became a college. One month later she became its professor of mathematics and physics.

## Graduate education {#Graduate_education}

While continuing to work at the college, Bosworth earned a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) from 1894 through 1896 through summer study with [E. H. Moore](wikipedia://en/1532860) and [Oskar Bolza](wikipedia://en/10217078).

In 1898, taking a leave from her work for the college, Bosworth traveled to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in Germany, where she worked under the supervision of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). She defended her dissertation there in 1899, and was awarded the [Ph.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis). in 1900.[^o6] Her dissertation was *Begründung einer vom Parallelenaxiome unabhängigen Streckenrechnung*, and concerned [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610). She was David Hilbert's first female doctoral student, part of a group that later included [Nadeschda Gernet](wikipedia://en/61303124) (1902), [Vera Myller](wikipedia://en/59097885) (1906), [Margarete Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarete_Kahn) (1909), [Klara Löbenstein](wikipedia://en/41642433) (1910), and Eva Koehler (1912).

## Later life {#Later_life}

In 1901 Bosworth married Theodore Moses Focke, an American civil engineer, [materials scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science), and applied mathematician whom she had met in Göttingen. Soon afterwards she followed her husband to [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio), leaving her academic work (except for assisting her husband in grading) to raise a family of three children. She caught [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia) in 1907 and died of it.
