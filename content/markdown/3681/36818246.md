# Pythagoras number

Pythagoras's constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pythagoras number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Pythagoras number** or **reduced height** of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) describes the structure of the set of squares in the field. The Pythagoras number $p(K)$ of a field $K$ is the smallest positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $p$ such that every sum of squares in $K$ is a sum of $p$ squares.

:::

A *[Pythagorean field](wikipedia://en/31032774)* is a field with Pythagoras number 1: that is, every sum of squares is already a square.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every non-negative [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) is a square, so $p(\mathbb{R})=1$.
- For a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) of odd [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621), not every element is a square, but all are the sum of two squares,[^n1] so $p=2$.
- By [Lagrange's four-square theorem](wikipedia://en/326395), every positive [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) is a sum of four squares, and not all are sums of three squares, so $p(\mathbb{Q})=4$.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every positive integer occurs as the Pythagoras number of some [formally real field](wikipedia://en/1067223).[^n2]
- The Pythagoras number is related to the [Stufe](wikipedia://en/35551843) by $p(F)\leq s(F)+1$.[^n3] If $F$ is not formally real then $s(F)\leq p(F)\leq s(F)+1$,[^n4] and both cases are possible: for $F=\mathbb{C}$ we have $s=p=1$, whereas for $F=\mathbb{F}_5$ we have $s=1$, $p=2$.[^n5]
- As a consequence, the Pythagoras number of a non-formally-real field is either a power of 2, or 1 more than a power of 2. All such cases occur: i.e., for each pair $(s,p)$ of the form $(2^k,2^k)$ or $(2^k,2^k+1)$, there exists a field $F$ such that $(s(F),p(F)) = (s,p)$.[^n6] For example,
  - [quadratically closed fields](wikipedia://en/36823185) and fields of characteristic 2 give $(s(F),p(F))=(1,1)$;
  - for primes $p\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\pmod{4}$, $\mathbb{F}_p$ and the [*p*-adic field](wikipedia://en/51423) $\mathbb{Q}_p$ give $(1,2)$;
  - for primes $p\equiv 3\!\!\!\!\pmod{4}$, $\mathbb{F}_p$ gives $(2,2)$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p$ gives $(2,3)$;
  - $\mathbb{Q}_2$ gives $(4,4)$, and
  - the [function field](wikipedia://en/447645) $\mathbb{Q}_2(t)$ gives $(4,5)$.
- The Pythagoras number is related to the [height of a field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_of_a_field) $F$: if $F$ is formally real then $h(F)$ is the smallest power of 2 which is not less than $p(F)$; if $F$ is not formally real then $h(F)=2s(F)$.[^n7]

:::
