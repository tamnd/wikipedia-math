# Squaregraph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Squaregraph"}

*Image: Squaregraph.svg.* A squaregraph.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **squaregraph** is a type of [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) that can be [drawn in the plane](wikipedia://en/325813) in such a way that every bounded [face](wikipedia://en/149926) is a [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) and every [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) with three or fewer neighbors is incident to an unbounded face.

:::

## Related graph classes {#Related_graph_classes}

The squaregraphs include as special cases [trees](wikipedia://en/48560), [grid graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_graph), [gear graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_graph), and the graphs of [polyominos](wikipedia://en/72634).

As well as being [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), squaregraphs are [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848), meaning that for every three vertices *u*, *v*, and *w* there is a unique median vertex *m*(*u*,*v*,*w*) that lies on shortest paths between each pair of the three vertices.[^o3] As with median graphs more generally, squaregraphs are also [partial cubes](wikipedia://en/30697444): their vertices can be labeled with [binary strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitvector) such that the [Hamming distance](wikipedia://en/41227) between strings is equal to the shortest path distance between vertices.

The graph obtained from a squaregraph by making a vertex for each zone (an equivalence class of parallel edges of quadrilaterals) and an edge for each two zones that meet in a quadrilateral is a [circle graph](wikipedia://en/1399873) determined by a [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996) [chord diagram](wikipedia://en/69127586) of the unit disk.[^o6]

## Characterization {#Characterization}

Squaregraphs may be characterized in several ways other than via their planar embeddings:[^o6]

- They are the [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848) that do not contain as an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) any member of an infinite family of [forbidden graphs](wikipedia://en/13547826). These forbidden graphs are the cube (the [simplex graph](wikipedia://en/20752774) of *K*<sub>3</sub>), the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of an edge and a [claw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claw_%28graph_theory%29) *K*<sub>1,3</sub> (the simplex graph of a claw), and the graphs formed from a [gear graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_graph) by adding one more vertex connected to the hub of the wheel (the simplex graph of the disjoint union of a cycle with an isolated vertex).
- They are the graphs that are connected and [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431), such that (if an arbitrary vertex *r* is picked as a [root](wikipedia://en/1373168)) every vertex has at most two neighbors closer to *r*, and such that at every vertex *v*, the link of *v* (a graph with a vertex for each edge incident to *v* and an edge for each 4-cycle containing *v*) is either a cycle of length greater than three or a disjoint union of paths.
- They are the [dual graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_dual) of [arrangements of lines](wikipedia://en/864149) in the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/318742) that do not have three mutually-crossing lines.

:::

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

The characterization of squaregraphs in terms of distance from a root and links of vertices can be used together with [breadth first search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadth_first_search) as part of a [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) for testing whether a given graph is a squaregraph, without any need to use the more complex linear-time algorithms for [planarity testing](wikipedia://en/15972636) of arbitrary graphs.[^o6]

Several algorithmic problems on squaregraphs may be computed more efficiently than in more general planar or median graphs; for instance, (Chepoi Dragan Vaxès 2002) and (Chepoi Fanciullini Vaxès 2004) present linear time algorithms for computing the [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of squaregraphs, and for finding a vertex minimizing the maximum distance to all other vertices.

:::
