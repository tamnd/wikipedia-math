# Monostatic polytope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monostatic polytope"}

*Image: R. K. Guy and J. H. Conway monostatic polyhedron b=1 a=19.stl.* Monostatic polyhedron described (Conway Goldberg Guy 1969)

*Image: Reshetov monostatic polyhedron.stl.* 3D model of Reshetov's monostatic polyhedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **monostatic polytope** or **unistable polyhedron** is a $d$-[polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) which "can stand on only one face". They were described in 1969 by [J. H. Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), M. Goldberg, [R. K. Guy](wikipedia://en/816308) and [K. C. Knowlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Knowlton). The monostatic polytope in [3-space](wikipedia://en/3054853) (a **monostatic polyhedron**) constructed independently by Guy and Knowlton has 19 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). In 2011 Andras Bezdek discovered an 18-face solution, and in 2014 Alex Reshetov published a 14-face [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A polytope is called monostatic if, when filled homogeneously, it is stable on only one [facet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29). Alternatively, a polytope is monostatic if its [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) (the [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961)) has an [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) in the interior of only one facet.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- No [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019) in the plane is monostatic. This was shown by [V. Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490) via reduction to the [four-vertex theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-vertex_theorem).
- There are no monostatic [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) in dimension up to eight. In dimension 3, this is due to Conway. In dimensions up to 6, this is due to R. J. M. Dawson. Dimensions 7 and 8 were ruled out by R. J. M. Dawson, W. Finbow, and P. Mak.
- (R. J. M. Dawson) There exist monostatic simplices in dimension 10 and up.
- There are monostatic polytopes in dimension 3 whose shapes are arbitrarily close to a sphere, and three-dimensional with $k$-fold rotational symmetry for an arbitrary positive integer $k$.
