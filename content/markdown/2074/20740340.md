# Schläfli orthoscheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schläfli orthoscheme"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **Schläfli orthoscheme** is a type of [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781). The orthoscheme is the generalization of the [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420) to simplex figures of any number of dimensions. Orthoschemes are defined by a sequence of [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) $(v_0v_1), (v_1v_2), \dots, (v_{d-1}v_d) \,$ that are mutually [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal). They were introduced by [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408), who called them *orthoschemes* and studied their [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) in [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697), [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291), and [spherical](wikipedia://en/174026) geometries. [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) later named them after Schläfli. As right triangles provide the basis for [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), orthoschemes form the basis of a trigonometry of *n* dimensions, as developed by [Schoute](wikipedia://en/6022750) who called it [polygonometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonometry). [J.-P. Sydler](wikipedia://en/20493975) and [Børge Jessen](wikipedia://en/20754801) studied orthoschemes extensively in connection with [Hilbert's third problem](wikipedia://en/152703).

:::

Orthoschemes, also called **path-simplices** in the [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) literature, are a special case of a more general class of simplices studied by [Fiedler](wikipedia://en/5539109), and later rediscovered by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter). These simplices are the [convex hulls](wikipedia://en/40634) of [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) in which all edges are mutually perpendicular. In an orthoscheme, the underlying tree is a [path](wikipedia://en/4587190).

In three dimensions, an orthoscheme is also called a [birectangular tetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectangular_tetrahedron) (because its path makes two right angles at vertices each having two right angles) or a [quadrirectangular tetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrirectangular_tetrahedron) (because it contains four right angles).

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Heronian\_birectangular\_tetrahedron.svg.* [Isometric projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_projection) of Euler's [Heronian birectangular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/426551) in its [minimum bounding box](wikipedia://en/12087300)

- All [2-faces](wikipedia://en/149926) are [right triangles](wikipedia://en/30654#By_internal_angles).
- All [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) of a *d*-dimensional orthoscheme are (*d* − 1)-dimensional orthoschemes.
- The $d$ [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046) that are disjoint from edges of the path have [acute angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_angle); the remaining $\tbinom{d}{2}$ dihedral angles are all [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956).
- The [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979) of the longest [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) is the center of the [circumscribed sphere](wikipedia://en/991784).
- The case when $|v_0v_1|=|v_1v_2|=\cdots = |v_{d-1}v_d|$ is a generalized [Hill tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/20700599).
- Every [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) in *d*-dimensional space can be dissected into *d*! congruent orthoschemes. A similar dissection into the same number of orthoschemes applies more generally to every [hyperrectangle](wikipedia://en/1688550) but in this case the orthoschemes may not be congruent.
- Every [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) can be dissected radially into *g* congruent orthoschemes that meet at its center, where *g* is the *order* of the regular polytope's symmetry group.
- Every orthoscheme can be trisected into three smaller orthoschemes.
- In 3-dimensional hyperbolic and spherical spaces, the volume of orthoschemes can be expressed in terms of the [Lobachevsky function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky_function), or in terms of [dilogarithms](wikipedia://en/2398437).

:::

## Dissection into orthoschemes {#Dissection_into_orthoschemes}

*Image: Triangulated cube.svg.* A cube dissected into six orthoschemes.

[Hugo Hadwiger](wikipedia://en/630451) conjectured in 1956 that every simplex can be [dissected](wikipedia://en/2915839) into finitely many orthoschemes. The conjecture has been proven in spaces of five or fewer dimensions, but remains unsolved in higher dimensions.

Hadwiger's conjecture implies that every convex polytope can be dissected into orthoschemes.

## Characteristic simplex of the general regular polytope {#Characteristic_simplex_of_the_general_regular_polytope}

Coxeter identifies various orthoschemes as the **characteristic simplexes** of the polytopes they generate by reflections. The characteristic simplex is the polytope's fundamental building block. It can be replicated by reflections or rotations to construct the polytope, just as the polytope can be dissected into some integral number of it. The characteristic simplex is [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral) (it comes in two mirror-image forms which are different), and the polytope is dissected into an equal number of left- and right-hand instances of it. It has dissimilar edge lengths and faces, instead of the equilateral triangle faces of the regular simplex. When the polytope is regular, its characteristic simplex is an orthoscheme, a simplex with only right triangle faces.

Every regular polytope has its **characteristic orthoscheme** which is its [fundamental region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_region), the irregular simplex which has exactly the same [symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_%28mathematics%29) characteristics as the regular polytope but captures them all without repetition. For a regular *k*-polytope, the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) of the characteristic *k-*orthoscheme is the *k*-polytope's diagram without the [generating point ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram#Application_with_uniform_polytopes). The regular *k-*polytope is subdivided by its symmetry (*k*-1)-elements into *g* instances of its characteristic *k*-orthoscheme that surround its center, where *g* is the *order* of the *k*-polytope's [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/297004). This is a [barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651).
