# (G, X)-manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="(G, X)-manifold"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), if *X* is a manifold with an action of a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) *G* by analytical diffeomorphisms, the notion of a **(*G*, *X*)-structure** on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is a way to formalise it being locally isomorphic to *X* with its *G*-invariant structure; spaces with a (*G*, *X*)-structure are always [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) and are called **(*G*, *X*)-manifolds**. This notion is often used with *G* being a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and *X* a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) for *G*. Foundational examples are [hyperbolic manifolds](wikipedia://en/1589135) and [affine manifolds](wikipedia://en/9996788).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and examples"}

## Definition and examples {#Definition_and_examples}

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

### Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $X$ be a [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connectedness_%28topology%29) [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) and $G$ be a subgroup of the group of [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) of $X$ which act analytically in the following sense:

if $g_1, g_2 \in G$ and there is a nonempty open subset $U \subset X$ such that $g_1, g_2$ are equal when restricted to $U$ then $g_1 = g_2$

(this definition is inspired by the [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) property of analytic diffeomorphisms on an [analytic manifold](wikipedia://en/3183798)).

A $(G, X)$-structure on a topological space $M$ is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) structure on $M$ whose [atlas](wikipedia://en/47436)'s charts have values in $X$ and [transition maps](wikipedia://en/47436#Transition_maps) belong to $G$. This means that there exists:

- a covering of $M$ by open sets $U_i, i \in I$ (i.e. $M = \bigcup_{i \in I} U_i$);
- open [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) $\varphi_i : U_i \to X$ called charts;

such that every transition map $\varphi_i \circ \varphi_j^{-1} : \varphi_j(U_i\cap U_j) \to \varphi_i(U_i \cap U_j)$ is the restriction of a diffeomorphism in $G$.

Two such structures $(U_i, \varphi_i), (V_j, \psi_j)$ are equivalent when they are contained in a maximal one, equivalently when their union is also a $(G, X)$ structure (i.e. the maps $\varphi_i \circ \psi_j^{-1}$ and $\psi_j \circ \varphi_i^{-1}$ are restrictions of diffeomorphisms in $G$).

:::

### Riemannian examples {#Riemannian_examples}

If $G$ is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and $X$ a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with a [faithful action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_action) of $G$ by [isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometry_%28Riemannian_geometry%29) then the action is analytic. Usually one takes $G$ to be the full isometry group of $X$. Then the category of $(G, X)$ manifolds is equivalent to the category of Riemannian manifolds which are locally isometric to $X$ (i.e. every point has a neighbourhood isometric to an open subset of $X$).

Often the examples of $X$ are [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/363325) under $G$, for example one can take $X = G$ with a left-invariant metric. A particularly simple example is $X = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $G$ the group of [euclidean isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_isometry). Then a $(G,X)$ manifold is simply a [flat manifold](wikipedia://en/12756616).

A particularly interesting example is when $X$ is a Riemannian [symmetric space](wikipedia://en/1642877), for example [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742). The simplest such example is the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane), whose isometry group is isomorphic to $G = \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{R})$.

### Pseudo-Riemannian examples {#Pseudo-Riemannian_examples}

When $X$ is [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) and $G$ the [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489) the notion of a $(G, X)$-structure is the same as that of a flat [Lorentzian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold).

### Other examples {#Other_examples}

When $X$ is the [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) and $G$ the group of affine transformations then one gets the notion of an [affine manifold](wikipedia://en/9996788).

When $X = \mathbb P^n(\mathbb{R})$ is the n-dimensional real [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) and $G = \mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})$ one gets the notion of a projective structure.[^o9]

:::

## Developing map and completeness {#Developing_map_and_completeness}

### Developing map {#Developing_map}

Let $M$ be a $(G,X)$-manifold which is connected (as a topological space). The developing map is a map from the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) $\tilde M$ to $X$ which is only well-defined up to composition by an element of $G$.

A developing map is defined as follows:[^n1] fix $p\in \tilde M$ and let $q \in \tilde M$ be any other point, $\gamma$ a path from $p$ to $q$, and $\varphi: U \to X$ (where $U$ is a small enough neighbourhood of $p$) a map obtained by composing a chart of $M$ with the projection $\tilde M \to M$. We may use analytic continuation along $\gamma$ to extend $\varphi$ so that its domain includes $q$. Since $\tilde M$ is [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) the value of $\varphi(q)$ thus obtained does not depend on the original choice of $\gamma$, and we call the (well-defined) map $\varphi: \tilde M \to X$ a *developing map* for the $(G,X)$-structure. It depends on the choice of base point and chart, but only up to composition by an element of $G$.

### Monodromy {#Monodromy}

Given a developing map $\varphi$, the *monodromy* or *holonomy*[^n2] of a $(G,X)$-structure is the unique morphism $h : \pi_1(M) \to G$ which satisfies

$$\forall \gamma \in \pi_1(M), p\in \tilde M : \varphi(\gamma\cdot p) = h(\gamma)\cdot \varphi(p).$$

It depends on the choice of a developing map but only up to an [inner automorphism](wikipedia://en/61839) of $G$.

### Complete (*G*,*X*)-structures {#Complete_(G,X)-structures}

A $(G,X)$ structure is said to be *complete* if it has a developing map which is also a [covering map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_map) (this does not depend on the choice of developing map since they differ by a diffeomorphism). For example, if $X$ is simply connected the structure is complete [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the developing map is a diffeomorphism.

::: {#o17 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

#### Riemannian (*G*,*X*)-structures {#Riemannian_(G,X)-structures}

If $X$ is a Riemannian manifold and $G$ its full group of isometry, then a $(G,X)$-structure is complete if and only if the underlying Riemannian manifold is [geodesically complete](wikipedia://en/144652#Geodesic_completeness) (equivalently metrically complete). In particular, in this case if the underlying space of a $(G,X)$-manifold is compact then the latter is automatically complete.

In the case where $X$ is the hyperbolic plane the developing map is the same map as given by the [Uniformisation Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformisation_Theorem).

#### Other cases {#Other_cases}

In general compactness of the space does not imply completeness of a $(G,X)$-structure. For example, an affine structure on the torus is complete if and only if the monodromy map has its image inside the [translations](wikipedia://en/204682). But there are many affine tori which do not satisfy this condition, for example any quadrilateral with its opposite sides glued by an affine map yields an affine structure on the torus, which is complete if and only if the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

Interesting examples of complete, noncompact affine manifolds are given by the Margulis spacetimes.

:::

## (*G*,*X*)-structures as connections {#(G,X)-structures_as_connections}

In the work of [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108) $(G, X)$-structures on a manifold $M$ are viewed as flat [Ehresmann connections](wikipedia://en/5731754) on [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526) with fiber $X$ over $M$, whose [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) maps lie in $G$.
