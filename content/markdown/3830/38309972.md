# McKean–Vlasov process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="McKean–Vlasov process"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **McKean–Vlasov process** is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) described by a [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454) where the coefficients of the [diffusion](wikipedia://en/645335) depend on the distribution of the solution itself.[^o2][^o3] The equations are a model for [Vlasov equation](wikipedia://en/1991528) and were first studied by [Henry McKean](wikipedia://en/35051081) in 1966.[^o4] It is an example of **propagation of chaos**, in that it can be obtained as a limit of a mean-field system of interacting particles: as the number of particles tends to infinity, the interactions between any single particle and the rest of the pool will only depend on the particle itself.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider a measurable function $\sigma:\mathbb{R}^d \times \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^d)\to \mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{R})$ where $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ is the space of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) on $\mathbb{R}^d$ equipped with the [Wasserstein metric](wikipedia://en/6704603) $W_2$ and $\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{R})$ is the space of [square matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) of dimension $d$. Consider a measurable function $b:\mathbb{R}^d\times \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^d)\to \mathbb{R}^d$. Define $a(x,\mu) := \sigma(x,\mu)\sigma(x,\mu)^T$.

A stochastic process $(X_t)_{t\geq 0}$ is a McKean–Vlasov process if it solves the following system:[^o4][^o13]

- $X_0$ has law $f_0$
- $$dX_t = \sigma(X_t, \mu_t) dB_t + b(X_t, \mu_t) dt$$

where $\mu_t = \mathcal{L}(X_t)$ describes the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_%28stochastic_processes%29) of $X$ and $B_t$ denotes a $d$-dimensional [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984). This process is non-linear, in the sense that the associated [Fokker–Planck equation](wikipedia://en/166896) for $\mu_t$ is a non-linear [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564).[^o13][^o14]

:::

## Existence of a solution {#Existence_of_a_solution}

The following Theorem can be found in.[^o5]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Existence of a solution"}

**Existence of a solution.**

Suppose $b$ and $\sigma$ are [globally Lipschitz](wikipedia://en/51731), that is, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that:

$$|b(x,\mu)-b(y,\nu)| + |\sigma(x,\mu)-\sigma(y,\nu)| \leq C(|x-y|+W_2(\mu,\nu))$$

where $W_2$ is the [Wasserstein metric](wikipedia://en/6704603).

Suppose $f_0$ has finite variance.

Then for any $T>0$ there is a unique strong solution to the McKean-Vlasov system of equations on $[0,T]$. Furthermore, its law is the unique solution to the non-linear [Fokker–Planck equation](wikipedia://en/166896):

$$\partial_t \mu_t(x) = -\nabla \cdot \{b(x,\mu_t)\mu_t\} + \frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^d \partial_{x_i}\partial_{x_j}\{a_{ij}(x,\mu_t)\mu_t\}$$

:::

## Propagation of chaos {#Propagation_of_chaos}

The McKean-Vlasov process is an example of **propagation of chaos**.[^o5] What this means is that many McKean-Vlasov process can be obtained as the limit of discrete systems of stochastic differential equations $(X_t^i)_{1\leq i\leq N}$.

Formally, define $(X^i)_{1\leq i\leq N}$ to be the $d$-dimensional solutions to:

- $(X_0^i)_{1\leq i\leq N}$ are i.i.d with law $f_0$
- $$dX_t^i = \sigma(X_t^i, \mu_{X_t}) dB_t^i + b(X_t^i, \mu_{X_t}) dt$$

where the $(B^i)_{1\leq i\leq N}$ are i.i.d [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), and $\mu_{X_t}$ is the [empirical measure](wikipedia://en/5647670) associated with $X_t$ defined by $\mu_{X_t} := \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq N} \delta_{X_t^i}$ where $\delta$ is the [Dirac measure](wikipedia://en/1019002).

Propagation of chaos is the property that, as the number of particles $N\to +\infty$, the interaction between any two particles vanishes, and the random empirical measure $\mu_{X_t}$ is replaced by the deterministic distribution $\mu_t$.

Under some regularity conditions,[^o5] the mean-field process just defined will converge to the corresponding McKean-Vlasov process.

## Applications {#Applications}

- [Mean-field theory](wikipedia://en/555768)
- [Mean-field game theory](wikipedia://en/38306273)[^o13]
- [Random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices): including Dyson's model on eigenvalue dynamics for random symmetric matrices and the [Wigner semicircle distribution](wikipedia://en/1028321)[^o14]
