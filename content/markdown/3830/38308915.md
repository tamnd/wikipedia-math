# William J. Cook

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William J. Cook"}

**William John Cook** (born October 18, 1957 in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey)) is an American [operations researcher](wikipedia://en/43476) and mathematician, and Professor of Combinatorics and Optimization at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo).

:::

He was elected a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in 2011 for theoretical and computational contributions to discrete optimization.[^o3]

He is known for his work on the [traveling salesman problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_salesman_problem) and is one of the authors of the [Concorde TSP Solver](wikipedia://en/16645361).

## Professional career {#Professional_career}

Cook did his undergraduate studies at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), graduating in 1979 with a bachelor's degree in mathematics. After earning a master's degree in operations research from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1980, he moved to the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo), where he earned a Ph.D. in combinatorics and optimization in 1983 under the supervision of [U. S. R. Murty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U._S._R._Murty).[^o6] After postdoctoral studies at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), he joined the [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) faculty in 1985, moved to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1987, and in 1988 joined the research staff of [Bell Communications Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Communications_Research). In 1994 he returned to academia as John von Neumann Professor at the University of Bonn, and in 1996 he moved to [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) as Noah Harding Professor of Computational and Applied Mathematics. In 2002 he took his position at Georgia Tech.[^o6] In January 2013, he moved to the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) as the John Swanson Professor of Industrial Engineering, before returning to the University of Waterloo in June 2013 as a professor in the Department of Combinatorics and Optimization, and subsequently University Professor.[^o6] From 2018-2020 he worked at Johns Hopkins University as a Professor of Applied Mathematics and Statistics.

He is the founding editor-in-chief of the journal *Mathematical Programming Computation* (since 2008), and the former editor-in-chief of *Mathematical Programming* (Series B from 1993 to 2003, and Series A from 2003 to 2007).[^o6]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o8] He won the Beale–Orchard-Hays Prize of the [Mathematical Programming Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Programming_Society) in 2000,[^o9] and his book *The Traveling Salesman Problem: A Computational Study* won the [Frederick W. Lanchester Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_W._Lanchester_Prize) of INFORMS in 2007.[^o10]

He became a fellow of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) in 2009, and of [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFORMS) in 2010.[^o6] He was elected to the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in 2011.[^o3] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11]
