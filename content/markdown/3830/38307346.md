# Chow's moving lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chow's moving lemma"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Chow's moving lemma**, proved by (txt), states: given [algebraic cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_cycles) *Y*, *Z* on a [nonsingular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) [quasi-projective variety](wikipedia://en/1321785) *X*, there is another algebraic cycle *Z'* which is [rationally equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationally_equivalent) to *Z* on *X,* such that *Y* and *Z'* intersect properly. The lemma is one of the key ingredients in developing [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602) and the [Chow ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_ring), as it is used to show the uniqueness of the theory.

:::

Even if *Z* is an effective cycle, it is not, in general, possible to choose *Z'* to be effective.
