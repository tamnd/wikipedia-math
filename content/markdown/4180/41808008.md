# Hamnet Holditch

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hamnet Holditch"}

Rev. **Hamnet Holditch**, also spelled **Hamnett Holditch** (1800 – 12 December 1867), was an English mathematician who was President of [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge). In 1858, he introduced the result in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) now known as [Holditch's theorem](wikipedia://en/3521151).

:::

Hamnet Holditch was born in 1800 in [King's Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Lynn), the son of George Holditch, [pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_pilot) and harbour-master. Educated at King's Lynn Grammar School under Rev. Martin Coulcher,[^o3] he matriculated at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge) in 1818, and graduated [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1822 ([Senior Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) and 1st [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize)), [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxford,_Cambridge,_and_Dublin%29) in 1825.

At Gonville and Caius College, Holditch was a junior fellow from 1821 and a senior fellow from 1823, and held the college posts of lecturer in Hebrew and Greek, registrar, steward, salarist (1823–28), bursar (1828–31), and President (1835–67).[^o8][^o5]

He died at Gonville and Caius College on 12 December 1867, aged 67,[^o6] and was buried at [North Wootton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wootton,_Norfolk).[^o3][^o8]

Although Holditch produced ten mathematical papers, he was extremely idle as a tutor.[^o7] [John Venn](wikipedia://en/251482), an undergraduate at Caius in the 1850s then a Caius Fellow from 1857, noted that Holditch, despite his succession of college offices, "beyond a few private pupils, never took part in educational work":[^o8]
