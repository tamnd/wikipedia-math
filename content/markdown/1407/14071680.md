# Eric Schechter

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eric Schechter"}

**Eric Schechter** (born August 1, 1950) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), retired from [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) with the title of professor emeritus. His interests started primarily in [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_%28mathematics%29) but moved into [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). Schechter is best known for his 1996 book *Handbook of Analysis and its Foundations*, which provides a novel approach to mathematical analysis and related topics at the graduate level.

:::

## Important works {#Important_works}

Schechter has authored a number of articles in [analysis](wikipedia://en/1134), [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), and [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553). He is best known for writing two textbooks covering advanced material but written at an introductory level:

- (2005) *Classical and Nonclassical Logics* () [(more information)](http://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/~schectex/logics/)
- (1996) *Handbook of Analysis and its Foundations* () [(more information)](http://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/~schectex/ccc/)

*Handbook of Analysis and its Foundations* was reviewed at length by the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) review, which wrote:

*Every once in a while a book comes along that so effectively redefines an educational enterprise -- in this case, graduate mathematical training -- and so effectively reexamines the hegemony of ideas prevailing in a discipline -- in this case, mathematical analysis -- that it deserves our careful attention. This is such a book.*[^o4]

Schechter also maintains two webpages that are frequently cited:

- *Common Errors in Undergraduate Mathematics* <http://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/~schectex/commerrs/>
- *A Home Page for the Axiom of Choice* <http://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/~schectex/ccc/choice.html>

## Politics {#Politics}

Schechter is involved in political activism of the [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism) variety. His mathematical homepage includes a few anti-war statements,[^o6] and his political home page includes a long essay about progressive ideology.[^o7] He has worked as an organizer for the Nashville Peace Coalition, protesting the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan.[^o8] At a meeting for the [living wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_wage) movement on Vanderbilt's campus, he remarked that it is hard to bring up politics in a non-political environment, and expressed that people did not talk much about politics in the mathematics department at Vanderbilt.[^o9] His father, Henry Schechter, was a deputy of the [AFL-CIO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL-CIO).[^o10]

In 2010, Schechter ran for Tennessee's 5th Congressional District seat against incumbent congressman Jim Cooper,[^o11] but was defeated in the [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_%28United_States%29) primary. Schechter describes himself as "a different kind of Democrat."[^o12]
