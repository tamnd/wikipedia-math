# Mazur manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mazur manifold"}

In [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), a branch of mathematics, a **Mazur manifold** is a contractible, [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), smooth four-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470)-with-boundary which is not [diffeomorphic](wikipedia://en/8564) to the standard [4-ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-ball_%28mathematics%29). Usually these manifolds are further required to have a handle decomposition with a single $1$-handle, and a single $2$-handle; otherwise, they would simply be called contractible manifolds. The boundary of a Mazur manifold is necessarily a [homology 3-sphere](wikipedia://en/585388).

:::

## History {#History}

[Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679)[^o3] and [Valentin Poénaru](wikipedia://en/8527892)[^o4] discovered these manifolds simultaneously. [Selman Akbulut](wikipedia://en/4558855) and [Robion Kirby](wikipedia://en/2932424) showed that the [Brieskorn homology spheres](wikipedia://en/585388) $\Sigma(2,5,7)$, $\Sigma(3,4,5)$, and $\Sigma(2,3,13)$ are boundaries of Mazur manifolds, effectively coining the term \`Mazur Manifold.'[^o5] These results were later generalized to other contractible manifolds by [Andrew Casson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Casson), John Harer, and [Ronald Stern](wikipedia://en/46991782).[^o6][^o7][^o8] One of the Mazur manifolds is also an example of an [Akbulut cork](wikipedia://en/17974424) which can be used to construct exotic 4-manifolds.[^o9]

Mazur manifolds have been used by [Ronald Fintushel](wikipedia://en/62176391) and Stern[^o10] to construct exotic actions of a group of order 2 on the [4-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782).

Mazur's discovery was surprising for several reasons:

- Every smooth homology sphere in dimension $n \geq 5$ is homeomorphic to the boundary of a compact contractible smooth manifold. This follows from the work of [Michel Kervaire](wikipedia://en/13644049)[^o11] and the [h-cobordism](wikipedia://en/681049) theorem. Slightly more strongly, every smooth homology 4-sphere is diffeomorphic to the boundary of a compact contractible smooth 5-manifold (also by the work of Kervaire). But not every homology 3-sphere is diffeomorphic to the boundary of a contractible compact smooth 4-manifold. For example, the [Poincaré homology sphere](wikipedia://en/585388) does not bound such a 4-manifold because the [Rokhlin invariant](wikipedia://en/7031816) provides an obstruction.
- The [h-cobordism Theorem](wikipedia://en/681049) implies that, at least in dimensions $n \geq 6$ there is a unique contractible $n$-manifold with simply-connected boundary, where uniqueness is up to diffeomorphism. This manifold is the unit ball $D^n$. It's an open problem as to whether or not $D^5$ admits an exotic smooth structure, but by the h-cobordism theorem, such an exotic smooth structure, if it exists, must restrict to an exotic smooth structure on $S^4$. Whether or not $S^4$ admits an exotic smooth structure is equivalent to another open problem, the smooth [Poincaré conjecture in dimension four](wikipedia://en/8536216). Whether or not $D^4$ admits an exotic smooth structure is another open problem, closely linked to the [Schoenflies problem](wikipedia://en/25148935) in dimension four.

## Mazur's observation {#Mazur's_observation}

Let $M$ be a Mazur manifold that is constructed as $S^1 \times D^3$ union a 2-handle. Here is a sketch of Mazur's argument that the [double](wikipedia://en/17375166) of such a Mazur manifold is $S^4$. $M \times [0,1]$ is a contractible 5-manifold constructed as $S^1 \times D^4$ union a 2-handle. The 2-handle can be unknotted since the attaching map is a framed knot in the 4-manifold $S^1 \times S^3$. So $S^1 \times D^4$ union the 2-handle is diffeomorphic to $D^5$. The boundary of $D^5$ is $S^4$. But the boundary of $M \times [0,1]$ is the [double](wikipedia://en/17375166) of $M$.
