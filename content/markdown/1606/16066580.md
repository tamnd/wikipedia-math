# Tarski's plank problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tarski's plank problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Tarski's plank problem** is a question about coverings of convex regions in *n*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) by "planks": regions between two [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862). [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) asked if the sum of the widths of the planks must be at least the minimum width of the convex region. The question was answered affirmatively by ().[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

*Image: Tarski plank problem.svg.*

Given a [convex body](wikipedia://en/8652020) *C* in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) *H*, the width of *C* parallel to *H*, *w*(*C*,*H*), is the distance between the two [supporting hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/4165181) of *C* that are parallel to *H*. The smallest such distance (i.e. the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) over all possible hyperplanes) is called the minimal width of *C*, *w*(*C*).

The (closed) set of points *P* between two distinct, parallel hyperplanes in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is called a plank, and the distance between the two hyperplanes is called the width of the plank, *w*(*P*). Tarski conjectured that if a convex body *C* of minimal width *w*(*C*) was covered by a collection of planks, then the sum of the widths of those planks must be at least *w*(*C*). That is, if *P*<sub>1</sub>,…,*P*<sub>*m*</sub> are planks such that

$$C\subseteq P_1\cup\ldots\cup P_m\subset \mathbb{R}^n,$$

then

$$\sum_{i=1}^m w(P_i)\geq w(C).$$

Bang proved this is indeed the case.

:::

## Nomenclature {#Nomenclature}

The name of the problem, specifically for the sets of points between parallel hyperplanes, comes from the visualisation of the problem in **R**<sup>2</sup>. Here, hyperplanes are just straight lines and so planks become the space between two parallel lines. Thus the planks can be thought of as (infinitely long) [planks of wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plank_%28wood%29), and the question becomes how many planks does one need to completely cover a [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) tabletop of minimal width *w*? Bang's theorem shows that, for example, a circular [table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_%28furniture%29) of [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) *d* feet can't be covered by fewer than *d* planks of wood of width one foot each.
