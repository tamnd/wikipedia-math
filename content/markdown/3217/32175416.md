# Faustina Pignatelli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Faustina Pignatelli"}

**Faustina Pignatelli Carafa**, princess of Colubrano (9 December 1705 – 30 December 1769),[^o3] was an Italian mathematician and scientist from Naples. She became the second woman (after the Bolognese physicist [Laura Bassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bassi))[^o5] to be elected to the [Academy of Sciences of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_Bologna) on 20 November 1732.[^o4]

:::

In 1734, Faustina published a paper titled *Problemata Mathematica* using the name "anonima napolitana" (a Latin phrase meaning "anonymous female from Naples"), in the German scientific journal *[Nova Acta Eruditorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Acta_Eruditorum)*, which was published entirely in Latin.[^o5]

Alongside her brother Peter, she was educated by [Nicola De Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_De_Martino) and was instrumental in introducing the theories of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) to Naples. She was an important participator in the scientific debate in Italy and corresponded with the French Academy of Sciences.[^o5][^o6]

Upon her marriage to the poet Francesco Domenico [Carafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Carafa) in 1724, she was given the principality Colubrano in southern Italy as a dowry by her father.[^o5]

[Francesco Maria Zanotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_Zanotti), secretary of the [Academy of Sciences of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Institute_of_Bologna) from 1723 to 1766, mentioned her as a gifted mathematician in 1745.[^o5][^o6]

She was a Dame of the [Order of the Starry Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Starry_Cross) from 3 May 1732.[^o7]
