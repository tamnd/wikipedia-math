# Ioannis Kigalas

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ioannis Kigalas"}

**Ioannis Kigalas** (Ιωάννης Κιγάλας;[^o3] Giovanni Cigala, Cicala;[^o4] Joannes Cigala; c. 1622 – 5 November 1687)[^o5] was a [Greek Cypriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Cypriots)[^n1] [scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholar) and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of philosophy and logic[^n2] who was largely active in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) in the 17th-century [Italian Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ioannis Kigalas was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks)[^o7] born in 1622 in the city of [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia) on the island of [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus), then [constituting a province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Cyprus) in the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire).[^o8] His father was Matthaios. Ioannis moved to [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) in his youth to pursue his education. From 1635 he was enrolled in the [college of Saint Athanasios in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Greek_College_of_Saint_Athanasius), where he was soon joined by his brothers Dimtrios and Ieronymos who studied at the same college. In 1642 he graduated as [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) and [Doctor of Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Theology) and was employed as a teacher of the [Greek language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) from 1642 to 1650.[^o8] Kigalas soon moved to [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) where he briefly practiced law. He later moved to [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), where in 1666 he was appointed professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua).[^o8] He was selected as professor to the second chair of philosophy in 1678, and by 1687 he was appointed professor to the first chair of philosophy.[^o8] He wrote several [epigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigrams) in Greek. Several of his works have survived in books of other scholars. Kigalas died on 5 November 1687.[^o8]
