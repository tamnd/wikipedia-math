# Ulam's game

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ulam's game"}

**Ulam's game**, or the **Rényi–Ulam game**, is a [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math) game similar to the popular game of [twenty questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty_questions). In Ulam's game, a player attempts to guess an unnamed object or number by asking [yes–no questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes–no_question) of another, but *one* of the answers given may be a [lie](wikipedia://en/229723).[^o2]

:::

(txt) introduced the game in a 1961 paper, based on Hungary's [Bar Kokhba game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_bar_Kokhba#The_Bar_Kokhba_game), but the paper was overlooked for many years.

[Stanisław Ulam](wikipedia://en/41531) rediscovered the game, presenting the idea that there are a million objects and the answer to one question can be wrong, and considered the minimum number of questions required, and the strategy that should be adopted.[^n1][^o5] Pelc gave a survey of similar games and their relation to [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773).[^n2]
