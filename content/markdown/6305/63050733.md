# Difference Equations: From Rabbits to Chaos

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Difference Equations: From Rabbits to Chaos"}

***Difference Equations: From Rabbits to Chaos*** is an undergraduate-level textbook on [difference equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_difference_equation), a type of [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) in which the values of a sequence are determined by equations involving differences of successive terms of the sequence. It was written by Paul Cull, [Mary Flahive](wikipedia://en/56636978), and Robby Robson, and published by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer-Verlag) in their [Undergraduate Texts in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8871375) series (Vol. 111, 2005, doi:10.1007/0-387-27645-9, ).

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

After an introductory chapter on the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) and the rabbit [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466) example based on these numbers that [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) introduced in his book *[Liber Abaci](wikipedia://en/167699)*, the book includes chapters on homogeneous linear equations, finite difference equations and [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993), nonnegative difference equations and roots of [characteristic polynomials](wikipedia://en/218268), the [Leslie matrix](wikipedia://en/1545816) in [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466), [matrix difference equations](wikipedia://en/27567102) and [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876), recurrences in [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087), [algorithmic](wikipedia://en/775) applications of [fast Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/11512), and nonlinear difference equations and [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087). Four appendices include a set of worked problems, background on [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), and a method of [Morris Marden](wikipedia://en/74073006) for testing whether the sequence defined by a difference equation converges to zero.

## Reception and related reading {#Reception_and_related_reading}

Other books on similar topics include *A Treatise on the Calculus of Finite Differences* by [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298), *Introduction to Difference Equations* by S. Goldberg, *Difference Equations: An Introduction with Applications* by W. G. Kelley and A. C. Peterson, *An Introduction to Difference Equations* by S. Elaydi, *Theory of Difference Equations: An Introduction* by V. Lakshmikantham and D. Trigiante, and *Difference Equations: Theory and Applications* by R. E. Mickens. However, *From Rabbits to Chaos* places a greater emphasis on computation than theory compared to some of these other books. Reviewer Henry Ricardo writes that the book is "more suitable to an undergraduate course" than its alternatives, despite being less in-depth, because of its greater accessibility and connection to application areas. Similarly, reviewer [Shandelle Henson](wikipedia://en/63051838) calls *From Rabbits to Chaos* "well written and easy to read" but adds that it is not "comprehensive or up-to-date".
