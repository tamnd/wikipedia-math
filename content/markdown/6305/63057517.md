# Generalized pencil-of-function method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized pencil-of-function method"}

*Image: GPOF fit to noisy data.png.* Extraction of two sinusoids from a noisy data through the GPOF method

**Generalized pencil-of-function method** (**GPOF**), also known as **matrix pencil method**, is a [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) technique for estimating a signal or extracting information with [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [exponentials](wikipedia://en/9678). Being similar to [Prony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prony_method) and original pencil-of-function methods, it is generally preferred to those for its robustness and computational efficiency.[^o15]

:::

The method was originally developed by Yingbo Hua and [Tapan Sarkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapan_Kumar_Sarkar) for estimating the behaviour of electromagnetic systems by its transient response, building on Sarkar's past work on the original pencil-of-function method.[^o15][^o13] The method has a plethora of applications in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), particularly related to problems in [computational electromagnetics](wikipedia://en/3849994), [microwave engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_engineering) and [antenna theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_%28radio%29).[^o15]

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Method"}

## Method {#Method}

### Mathematical basis {#Mathematical_basis}

A transient signal can be represented as:[^o14]

$$y(t)=x(t)+n(t) \approx \sum_{i=1}^{M}R_i e^{s_i t} + n(t); 0 \leq t \leq T,$$

where

$y(t)$ is the observed time-domain signal,

$n(t)$ is the [signal noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_%28signal_processing%29),

$x(t)$ is the actual signal,

$R_i$ are the [residues](wikipedia://en/73102) ($R_i$),

$s_i$ are the [poles](wikipedia://en/81560) of the system, defined as $s_i=-\alpha_i+j \omega_i$,

$z_i=e^{(- \alpha_i + j \omega_i) T_s}$ by the identities of [Z-transform](wikipedia://en/171589),

$\alpha_i$ are the [damping factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damping_ratio) and

$\omega_i$ are the [angular frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency).

The same sequence, [sampled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_%28signal_processing%29) by a period of $T_s$, can be written as the following:

$$y[kT_s]=x[kT_s]+n[kT_s] \approx \sum_{i=1}^{M}R_i z_i^{k} + n[kT_s]; k=0,...,N-1; i=1,2,...,M,$$

Generalized pencil-of-function estimates the optimal $M$ and $z_i$'s.[^o12]

### Noise-free analysis {#Noise-free_analysis}

For the noiseless case, two $(N-L) \times L$ matrices, $Y_1$ and $Y_2$, are produced:[^o14]

$[Y_1]= \begin{bmatrix} x(0) & x(1) & \cdots & x(L-1)\\ x(1) & x(2) & \cdots & x(L)\\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ x(N-L-1) & x(N-L) & \cdots & x(N-2) \end{bmatrix}_{(N-L) \times L};$$[Y_2]= \begin{bmatrix} x(1) & x(2) & \cdots & x(L)\\ x(2) & x(3) & \cdots & x(L+1)\\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ x(N-L) & x(N-L+1) & \cdots & x(N-1) \end{bmatrix}_{(N-L) \times L}$

where $L$ is defined as the [pencil](wikipedia://en/7058352) parameter. $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ can be decomposed into the following matrices:[^o14]

$$[Y_1]=[Z_1][B][Z_2]$$

$$[Y_2]=[Z_1][B][Z_0][Z_2]$$

where

$[Z_1]= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1\\ z_1 & z_2 & \cdots & z_M\\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ z_1^{(N-L-1)} & z_2^{(N-L-1)} & \cdots & z_M^{(N-L-1)} \end{bmatrix}_{(N-L) \times M};$$[Z_2]= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & z_1 & \cdots & z_1^{L-1}\\ 1 & z_2 & \cdots & z_2^{L-1}\\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ 1 & z_M & \cdots & z_M^{L-1} \end{bmatrix}_{M \times L}$

$[Z_0]$ and $[B]$ are $M \times M$ [diagonal matrices](wikipedia://en/174080) with sequentially-placed $z_i$ and $R_i$ values, respectively.[^o14]

If $M \leq L \leq N-M$, the [generalized eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_eigenvalue_problem) of the [matrix pencil](wikipedia://en/7058352)

$$[Y_2]-\lambda[Y_1]=[Z_1][B]([Z_0]-\lambda[I])[Z_2]$$

yield the poles of the system, which are $\lambda=z_i$. Then, the generalized eigenvectors $p_i$ can be obtained by the following identities:[^o14]

$[Y_1]^+[Y_1]p_i=p_i;$ $i=1,...,M$

$[Y_1]^+[Y_2]p_i=z_i p_i;$ $i=1,...,M$

where the $^+$ denotes the [Moore–Penrose inverse](wikipedia://en/331258), also known as the pseudo-inverse. [Singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207) can be employed to compute the pseudo-inverse.

### Noise filtering {#Noise_filtering}

If noise is present in the system, $[Y_1]$ and $[Y_2]$ are combined in a general data matrix, $[Y]$:[^o14]

$$[Y]= \begin{bmatrix} y(0) & y(1) & \cdots & y(L)\\ y(1) & y(2) & \cdots & y(L+1)\\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ y(N-L-1) & y(N-L) & \cdots & y(N-1) \end{bmatrix}_{(N-L) \times (L+1)}$$

where $y$ is the noisy data. For efficient [filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_filter), L is chosen between $\frac{N}{3}$ and $\frac{N}{2}$. A singular value decomposition on $[Y]$ yields:

$$[Y]=[U][\Sigma][V]^H$$

In this decomposition, $[U]$ and $[V]$ are [unitary matrices](wikipedia://en/129222) with respective [eigenvectors](wikipedia://en/2161429) $[Y][Y]^H$ and $[Y]^H[Y]$ and $[\Sigma]$ is a diagonal matrix with [singular values](wikipedia://en/709116) of $[Y]$. Superscript $H$ denotes the [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548).[^o14][^o12]

Then the parameter $M$ is chosen for filtering. Singular values after $M$, which are below the filtering threshold, are set to zero; for an arbitrary singular value $\sigma_c$, the threshold is denoted by the following formula:[^o15]

$$\frac{\sigma_c}{\sigma_{max}}=10^{-p},$$

$\sigma_{max}$ and $p$ are the maximum singular value and [significant decimal digits](wikipedia://en/317062), respectively. For a data with significant digits accurate up to $p$, singular values below $10^{-p}$ are considered noise.[^o12]

$[V_1']$ and $[V_2']$ are obtained through removing the last and first row and column of the filtered matrix $[V']$, respectively; $M$ columns of $[\Sigma]$ represent $[\Sigma']$. Filtered $[Y_1]$ and $[Y_2]$ matrices are obtained as:[^o12]

$$[Y_1]=[U][\Sigma'][V_1']^H$$

$$[Y_2]=[U][\Sigma'][V_2']^H$$

Prefiltering can be used to combat noise and enhance [signal-to-noise ratio](wikipedia://en/41706) (SNR).[^o15] Band-pass matrix pencil (BPMP) method is a modification of the GPOF method via [FIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_impulse_response) or [IIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_impulse_response) [band-pass filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band-pass_filter).[^o15][^o16]

GPOF can handle up to 25 dB SNR. For GPOF, as well as for BPMP, variance of the estimates approximately reaches [Cramér–Rao bound](wikipedia://en/581124).[^o14][^o16][^o12]

### Calculation of residues {#Calculation_of_residues}

Residues of the complex poles are obtained through the [least squares](wikipedia://en/82359) problem:[^o15]

$$\begin{bmatrix} y(0) \\ y(1) \\ \vdots \\ y(N-1) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\ z_1 & z_2 & \cdots & z_M \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ z_1^{N-1} & z_2^{N-1} & \cdots & z_M^{N-1} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R_1 \\ R_2 \\ \vdots \\ R_M \end{bmatrix}$$

## Applications {#Applications}

The method is generally used for the closed-form evaluation of Sommerfeld integrals in discrete complex image method for [method of moments](wikipedia://en/68572267) applications, where the spectral [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001) is approximated as a sum of complex exponentials.[^o15][^o17] Additionally, the method is used in [antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_%28radio%29) analysis, [S-parameter](wikipedia://en/1972332)-estimation in [microwave integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolithic_microwave_integrated_circuit), wave propagation analysis, [moving target indication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_target_indication), [radar signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_signal_characteristics),[^o15][^o19][^o18] and [series acceleration](wikipedia://en/8737421) in electromagnetic problems.[^o20]
