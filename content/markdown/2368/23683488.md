# Aurifeuillean factorization

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aurifeuillean factorization"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **aurifeuillean factorization**, named after [Léon-François-Antoine Aurifeuille](wikipedia://en/44178951), is [factorization](wikipedia://en/82341) of certain [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) values of the [cyclotomic polynomials](wikipedia://en/171992).[^o2] Because cyclotomic polynomials are [irreducible polynomials](wikipedia://en/188725) over the integers, such a factorization cannot come from an [algebraic factorization of the polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_factorization). Nevertheless, certain families of integers coming from cyclotomic polynomials have factorizations given by formulas applying to the whole family, as in the examples below.

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Numbers of the form $a^4 + 4b^4$ have the following factorization ([Sophie Germain's identity](wikipedia://en/74081567)): $$a^4 + 4b^4 = (a^2 - 2ab + 2b^2)\cdot (a^2 + 2ab + 2b^2).$$ Setting $a=1$ and $b=2^k$, one obtains the following aurifeuillean factorization of $\Phi_4(2^{2k+1})=2^{4k+2}+1$, where $\Phi_4(x)=x^2+1$ is the fourth cyclotomic polynomial: $$2^{4k+2}+1 = (2^{2k+1}-2^{k+1}+1)\cdot (2^{2k+1}+2^{k+1}+1).$$
- Numbers of the form $a^6 + 27b^6$ have the following factorization, where the first factor ($a^2 + 3b^2$) is the algebraic factorization of [sum of two cubes](wikipedia://en/72077175): $$a^6 + 27b^6 = (a^2 + 3b^2)\cdot (a^2 - 3ab + 3b^2)\cdot (a^2 + 3ab + 3b^2).$$ Setting $a=1$ and $b=3^k$, one obtains the following factorization of $3^{6k+3}+1$:[^o5] $$3^{6k+3}+1 = (3^{2k+1}+1)\cdot (3^{2k+1}-3^{k+1}+1)\cdot (3^{2k+1}+3^{k+1}+1).$$ Here, the first of the three terms in the factorization is $\Phi_2(3^{2k+1})$ and the remaining two terms provide an aurifeuillean factorization of $\Phi_6(3^{2k+1})$, where $\Phi_6(x)=x^2-x+1$.
- Numbers of the form $b^n - 1$ or their factors $\Phi_n(b)$, where $b = s^2 \cdot t$ with [square-free](wikipedia://en/29525) $t$, have aurifeuillean factorization [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) one of the following conditions holds:
  - $t\equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $n\equiv t \pmod{2t}$
  - $t\equiv 2, 3 \pmod 4$ and $n\equiv 2t \pmod{4t}$

Thus, when $b = s^2\cdot t$ with square-free $t$, and $n$ is [congruent](wikipedia://en/20087) to $t$ modulo $2t$, then if $t$ is congruent to 1 mod 4, $b^n-1$ have aurifeuillean factorization, otherwise, $b^n+1$ have aurifeuillean factorization.

- When the number is of a particular form (the exact expression varies with the base), aurifeuillean factorization may be used, which gives a product of two or three numbers. - [Lucas numbers](wikipedia://en/398404) $L_{10k+5}$ have the following aurifeuillean factorization:[^o10]

$$L_{10k+5} = L_{2k+1}\cdot (5{F_{2k+1}}^2-5F_{2k+1}+1)\cdot (5{F_{2k+1}}^2+5F_{2k+1}+1)$$

where $L_n$ is the $n$th Lucas number, and $F_n$ is the $n$th [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number).

## History {#History}

In 1869, before the discovery of aurifeuillean factorizations, Fortuné Landry, through a tremendous manual effort,[^o12][^o13][^o14] obtained the following factorization into [primes](wikipedia://en/23666):

$$2^{58}+1 = 5 \cdot 107367629 \cdot 536903681.$$

Three years later, in 1871, [Aurifeuille](wikipedia://en/44178951) discovered the nature of this factorization; the number $2^{4k+2}+1$ for $k=14$, with the formula from the previous section, factors as:[^o5][^o12]

$$2^{58}+1 = (2^{29}-2^{15}+1)(2^{29}+2^{15}+1) = 536838145 \cdot 536903681.$$

Landry's full factorization follows from this (taking out the obvious factor of 5). The general form of the factorization was later discovered by [Lucas](wikipedia://en/150102).[^o5]

536903681 is an example of a [Gaussian Mersenne](wikipedia://en/18908#Gaussian_Mersenne_primes) norm.[^o13][^o14]
