# Jakob Levitzki

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jakob Levitzki"}

**Jakob Levitzki**, also known as **Yaakov Levitsky** (יעקב לויצקי; 17 August 1904 – 25 February 1956), was an Israeli [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Levitzki was born in 1904 in the Ukrainian city, [Kherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson), then part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). In 1912 he emigrated to then [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire)-ruled [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_%28region%29).[^o5] After completing his studies at the [Herzliya Gymnasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Hebrew_High_School), he travelled to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) and, in 1929, obtained a doctorate in mathematics from the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) under the supervision of [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896). In 1931, after two years at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), in [New Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven), [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut), Levitzki returned to then British-ruled [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) to join the faculty at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem).

## Awards {#Awards}

Levitzki together with [Shimshon Amitsur](wikipedia://en/19937535), who had been one of his students at the Hebrew University, were each awarded the [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize) in [exact sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_science) in 1953, the inaugural year of the prize, for their work on the laws of noncommutative rings.[^o8]

Levitzki's son [Alexander Levitzki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Levitzki), a recipient of the Israel Prize in 1990, in [life sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_science), established the [Levitzki Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levitzki_Prize) in the name of his parents, Jacob and Charlotte, for Israeli research in the field of algebra.
