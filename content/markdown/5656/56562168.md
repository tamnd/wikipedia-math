# Ruth Silverman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth Silverman"}

**Ruth Silverman** (born 1936 or 1937, died April 25, 2011) was an American mathematician and computer scientist known for her research in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927). She was one of the original founders of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in 1971.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Silverman completed a Ph.D. in 1970 at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington). She was a faculty member at the [New Jersey Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Institute_of_Technology), an associate professor at [Southern Connecticut State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Connecticut_State_University), a computer science instructor at the [University of the District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_District_of_Columbia), and a researcher in the Center for Automation Research at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Silverman's dissertation, *Decomposition of plane convex sets*, concerned the characterization of [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) that cannot be formed as [Minkowski sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_sum) of simpler sets.

She became known for her research in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) and particular for highly cited publications on [k-means clustering](wikipedia://en/1860407)KM and [nearest neighbor search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest_neighbor_search).NN Other topics in Silverman's research include [robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691)LT and small sets of points that meet every line in finite [projective planes](wikipedia://en/24350).IP
