# Robert S. Doran

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Robert S. Doran"}

**Robert Stuart Doran** (born December 21, 1937) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He held the John William and Helen Stubbs Potter Professorship in mathematics at [Texas Christian University (TCU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Christian_University) from 1995 until his retirement in 2016. Doran served as chair of the TCU mathematics department for 21 years.[^o3] He has also held visiting appointments at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, N.J.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_N.J.) He was elected to the board of trustees of the Association of Members of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), serving as president of the organization for 10 years.[^o4][^o5] He has been an editor for the *Encyclopedia of Mathematics and its Applications*, [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press), a position he has held since 1988.[^o6] Doran is known for his research-level books, his award-winning teaching, and for his solution to a long-standing open problem due to [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky) on a [symmetric \*-algebra](wikipedia://en/654098).[^o7]

:::

## Personal background {#Personal_background}

Robert Stuart Doran was born on December 21, 1937, in [Winthrop, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winthrop,_Iowa).[^o9] In 1959, he married Shirley Ann Lange. They have two sons, Bruce and Brad.

**Military service**

In 1956, Doran served in the [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division) at [Fort Bragg, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bragg,_North_Carolina). He served as a [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Forces) Instructor at the US Army Jungle Survival Center at [Fort Sherman, Colón, Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sherman).[^o10] He left active duty in the military in 1958 and he served in the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) reserves until 1962 when he was honorably discharged.

**Educational background**

Doran, a member of Phi Beta Kappa, studied mathematics at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa) from 1959 until 1964, earning a [Bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) and a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) degree. He received a Ph.D in mathematics from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1968, under the direction of [J. M. G. Fell](wikipedia://en/56434958) and [Ramesh Gangolli](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~gangolli/).[^o11] His doctoral dissertation,[^o12][^o13] titled Representations of C\*-algebras by Uniform CT-bundles and Operator Theory, dealt with topological representations in spaces of cross-sections of [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526) of a non-commutative [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184). This work was motivated in part by the classical [Gelfand–Naimark theorem](wikipedia://en/315414) for [C\*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebras) and by the work of [M. Takesaki](wikipedia://en/41007469) and J. Tomiyama.[^o14][^o15]

## Professional background {#Professional_background}

Doran held the John William and Helen Stubbs Potter Professor of Mathematics at [Texas Christian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Christian_University) from 1995 to 2016. He was faculty sponsor of the TCU chapter of [Campus Crusade for Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_Crusade_for_Christ) ([Cru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cru_%28Christian_organization%29)) for 43 years, and he was the founding faculty sponsor in 1989 of the TCU chapter of [Brothers Under Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Upsilon_Chi) ([Beta Upsilon Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Upsilon_Chi)).

He has held visiting appointments at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (1980), [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) in England (1988), and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (1981).[^o17] His areas of research involve [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) characterizations, the notion of an [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628) in a [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665), and [Banach bundle](wikipedia://en/3468491) theory.[^o18][^o19]

Doran taught at both the undergraduate and graduate levels. In 1988 he published an article titled "A care package for undergraduate mathematics students" that outlined his teaching methods.[^o20] He has received national, statewide, and local awards for his teaching.[^o21][^o22]

Doran was chair of the TCU Mathematics Department for 21 years (1990–2011). In 1986 he was elected to the board of trustees the Association of Members of the Institute for Advanced Study.[^o17] He was president of the association for 10 years (1990-1999). Doran has been an editor for [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) since 1988.[^o23] He has also organized many [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) special sessions and conferences for CBMS, the [Conference Board of the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/13673454). His first such conference was held at TCU in 1970 with [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598) as principal speaker.[^o24] The most recent conference was held at TCU, June 2011 with [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414), former Director of the Institute for Advanced Study as principal speaker.

Doran is known for his elegant solution[^o25] of a long-standing unsolved problem on a symmetric [\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/293426) left open by [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky) in the *[Duke Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Mathematical_Journal)* in 1949.[^o26]

## Published works {#Published_works}

Doran has published books, articles, and reviews on a variety of topics, often collaborating with other mathematicians, including [J. M. G. Fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._M._G._Fell), [Richard Kadison](wikipedia://en/25798650), [Calvin Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_C._Moore), [Jonathan Rosenberg](wikipedia://en/41108807), [Paul Sally](wikipedia://en/2789475), [Robert Zimmer](wikipedia://en/4335903), and [V. S. Varadarajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._S._Varadarajan).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- 1989 National CASE-Carnegie Gold Medalist for outstanding teaching (awarded by CASE, the [Council for Advancement and Support of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_Advancement_and_Support_of_Education) based in Washington DC).[^o31][^o32]
- 1989 CASE-Carnegie Texas Professor of the Year (awarded by CASE)[^o32]
- 1992 Texas MAA Professor of the Year (awarded by MAA, the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)).[^o33]
- 1993 TCU Honors Professor of the Year (awarded by the TCU Honors College)[^o34]
- 1989 Piper Professor (Texas statewide award; given by the Minnie Stevens Piper Foundation, San Antonio, Texas)[^o35][^o36]
- 1988 Chancellor's Award at TCU for Distinguished Teaching, the top faculty honor at TCU[^o37]
- 1986 [Burlington Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Northern) Faculty Achievement Award, awarded by the Burlington Northern Corporation[^o10]
