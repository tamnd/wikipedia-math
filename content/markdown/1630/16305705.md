# Milnor–Thurston kneading theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Milnor–Thurston kneading theory"}

The **Milnor–Thurston kneading theory** is a [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) theory which analyzes the iterates of piecewise monotone [mappings](wikipedia://en/516931) of an [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) into itself. The emphasis is on understanding the properties of the mapping that are invariant under [topological conjugacy](wikipedia://en/5506149).

:::

The theory had been developed by [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) and [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) in two widely circulated and influential [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) preprints from 1977 that were revised in 1981 and finally published in 1988. Applications of the theory include piecewise linear models, counting of [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738), computing the [total variation](wikipedia://en/683561), and constructing an [invariant measure](wikipedia://en/7635201) with maximal [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891).

## Short description {#Short_description}

Kneading theory provides an effective calculus for describing the qualitative behavior of the [iterates](wikipedia://en/1448702) of a piecewise [monotone](wikipedia://en/48260) mapping *f* of a [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) *I* of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) into itself. Some quantitative invariants of this [discrete dynamical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_dynamical_system), such as the *lap numbers* of the iterates and the [Artin–Mazur zeta function](wikipedia://en/1342156) of *f* are expressed in terms of certain [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) and [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012).

The basic invariant of *f* is its **kneading matrix**, a rectangular matrix with coefficients in the ring $\mathbb{Z}[[t]]$ of integer formal power series. A closely related **kneading determinant** is a formal power series

$$D(t) = 1 + D_1 t + D_2 t^2 + \cdots\,$$

with odd integer coefficients. In the simplest case when the map is [unimodal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimodal_function), with a maximum at *c*, each coefficient $D_k$ is either $+1$ or $-1$, according to whether the $(k+1)$th iterate $f^{k+1}$ has [local maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_maximum) or local minimum at *c*.
