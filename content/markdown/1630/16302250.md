# Carpus of Antioch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carpus of Antioch"}

**Carpus of [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch)** (Κάρπος) was an ancient [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). It is not certain when he lived; he may have lived any time between the 2nd century BC and the 2nd century AD. He wrote on [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus) quotes from an *Astronomical Treatise* by Carpus concerning whether problems should come before theorems, in which Carpus may (or may not) have been criticising [Geminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geminus).[^o2] Proclus also quotes the view of Carpus that "an [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) is a quantity, namely a distance between the lines of surfaces containing it."[^o3] According to [Pappus](wikipedia://en/509300), Carpus made use of mathematics for practical applications.[^o4] According to [Iamblichus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iamblichus_of_Chalcis), Carpus also constructed a curve for the purpose of [squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359), which he calls a curve generated by a double motion.[^o5]
