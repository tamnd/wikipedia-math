# Free category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **free category** or **path category** generated by a [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) or [quiver](wikipedia://en/355100) is the [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) that results from freely concatenating arrows together, whenever the target of one arrow is the source of the next.

:::

More precisely, the objects of the category are the vertices of the quiver, and the morphisms are paths between objects. Here, a **path** is defined as a [finite sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_sequence)

$$V_0\xrightarrow{\;\;E_0\;\;} V_1\xrightarrow{\;\;E_1\;\;} \cdots \xrightarrow{E_{n-1}} V_n$$

where $V_k$ is a vertex of the quiver, $E_k$ is an edge of the quiver, and *n* ranges over the non-negative integers. For every vertex $V$ of the quiver, there is an "empty path" which constitutes the identity morphisms of the category.

The composition operation is concatenation of paths. Given paths

$$V_0\xrightarrow{E_0}\cdots\xrightarrow{E_{n-1}} V_n,\quad V_n\xrightarrow{F_0}W_0\xrightarrow{F_1}\cdots\xrightarrow{F_{m}} W_m,$$

their composition is

$\left(V_n\xrightarrow{F_0}W_0\xrightarrow{F_1}\cdots\xrightarrow{F_{m}} W_m \right) \circ \left(V_0\xrightarrow{E_0}\cdots\xrightarrow{E_{n-1}} V_n \right) := V_0\xrightarrow{E_0}\cdots\xrightarrow{E_{n-1}} V_n\xrightarrow{F_0}W_0\xrightarrow{F_1}\cdots\xrightarrow{F_{m}} W_m$.[^o2][^o3]

Note that the result of the composition starts with the right operand of the composition, and ends with its left operand.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If $Q$ is the quiver with one vertex and one edge $f$ from that object to itself, then the free category on $Q$ has as arrows $1$, $f$, $f$∘$f$,$f$∘$f$∘$f$, etc.[^o3]
- Let $Q$ be the quiver with two vertices $a$, $b$ and two edges $e$, $f$ from $a$ to $b$ and $b$ to $a$, respectively. Then the free category on $Q$ has two identity arrows and an arrow for every finite sequence of alternating $e$s and $f$s, including: $e$, $f$, $e$∘$f$, $f$∘$e$, $f$∘$e$∘$f$, $e$∘$f$∘$e$, etc.[^o2]
- If $Q$ is the quiver $a\xrightarrow{f}b\xrightarrow{g}c$, then the free category on $Q$ has (in addition to three identity arrows), arrows $f$, $g$, and $g$∘$f$.
- If a quiver $Q$ has only one vertex, then the free category on $Q$ has only one object, and corresponds to the [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) on the edges of $Q$.[^o2]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The [category of small categories](wikipedia://en/2072741) **Cat** has a [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) $U$ into the quiver category **Quiv**:

$U$ : **Cat** → **Quiv**

which takes objects to vertices and morphisms to arrows. Intuitively, $U$ "\[forgets\] which arrows are composites and which are identities".[^o3] This forgetful functor is [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to the functor sending a quiver to the corresponding free category.

### Universal property {#Universal_property}

The free category on a quiver can be described [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/379051) by a [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245). Let $C$ : **Quiv** → **Cat** be the functor that takes a quiver to the free category on that quiver (as described above), let $U$ be the forgetful functor defined above, and let $G$ be any quiver. Then there is a [graph homomorphism](wikipedia://en/676328) $I$ : $G$ → $U$($C$($G$)) and given any category **D** and any graph homomorphism $F$ : $G$ → $U(D)$, there is a unique functor $F'$ : $C$($G$) → **D** such that $U$($F'$)∘$I$=$F$, i.e. the following diagram [commutes](wikipedia://en/138484):

The functor $C$ is [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint) to the forgetful functor $U$.[^o2][^o3]
