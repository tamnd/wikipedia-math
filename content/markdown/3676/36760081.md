# Rota's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rota's conjecture"}

**Rota's excluded minors conjecture** is one of a number of conjectures made by the mathematician [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891). Some members of the structural combinatorics community consider it an important problem. Rota [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) in 1971 that, for every [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), the family of [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321) that can be [represented](wikipedia://en/37165960) over that field has only finitely many [excluded minors](wikipedia://en/36749660).[^o2] A proof of the conjecture was announced, but not published, in 2014 by Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Statement of the conjecture"}

## Statement of the conjecture {#Statement_of_the_conjecture}

If $S$ is a set of points in a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) defined over a field $F$, then the linearly independent subsets of $S$ form the independent sets of a [matroid](wikipedia://en/244321) $M$; $S$ is said to be a [representation](wikipedia://en/37165960) of any matroid isomorphic to $M$. Not every matroid has a representation over every field, for instance, the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404) is representable only over fields of characteristic two. Other matroids are representable over no fields at all. The matroids that are representable over a particular field form a proper subclass of all matroids.

A [minor](wikipedia://en/36749660) of a matroid is another matroid formed by a sequence of two operations: deletion and contraction. In the case of points from a vector space, deleting a point is simply the removal of that point from $S$; contraction is a dual operation in which a point is removed and the remaining points are projected onto a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) that does not contain the removed point. It follows from this that if a matroid is representable over a field, then so are all its minors. A matroid that is not representable over $F$, and is minor-[minimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_element) with that property, is called an "excluded minor"; a matroid $M$ is representable over $F$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it does not contain one of the forbidden minors.

For representability over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), there are infinitely many forbidden minors.[^o6] Rota's conjecture is that, for every finite field $F$, there is only a finite number of forbidden minors.

:::

## Partial results {#Partial_results}

[W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021) proved that the [binary matroids](wikipedia://en/36848165) (matroids representable over the field of two elements) have a single forbidden minor, the [uniform matroid](wikipedia://en/27967069) $U{}^2_4$ (geometrically, a line with four points on it).[^o8][^o9]

A matroid is representable over the ternary field GF(3) if and only if it does not have one or more of the following four matroids as minors: a five-point line $U{}^2_5$, its [dual matroid](wikipedia://en/23515837) $U{}^3_5$ (five points in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) in three dimensions), the Fano plane, or the dual of the Fano plane. Thus, Rota's conjecture is true in this case as well.[^o10][^o11] As a consequence of this result and of the forbidden minor characterization by (Tutte 1958) of the [regular matroids](wikipedia://en/13415343) (matroids that can be represented over all fields) it follows that a matroid is regular if and only if it is both binary and ternary.[^o11]

There are seven forbidden minors for the matroids representable over GF(4).[^o13] They are:

- The six-point line $U{}^2_6$.
- The dual $U{}^4_6$ to the six-point line, six points in general position in four dimensions.
- A self-dual six-point rank-three matroid with a single three-point line.
- The non-Fano matroid formed by the seven points at the vertices, edge midpoints, and centroid of an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410). This configuration is one of two known sets of planar points with fewer than $n/2$ [two-point lines](wikipedia://en/1052632).[^o14]
- The dual of the non-Fano matroid.
- The eight-point matroid of a [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045).
- The matroid obtained by relaxing the unique pair of disjoint circuit-hyperplanes of the square antiprism.

This result won the 2003 [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) for its authors [Jim Geelen](wikipedia://en/24393682), A. M. H. Gerards, and A. Kapoor.[^o15]

For GF(5), several forbidden minors on up to 12 elements are known,[^o16] but it is not known whether the list is complete.

## Reported proof {#Reported_proof}

Geoff Whittle announced during a 2013 visit to the UK that he, [Jim Geelen](wikipedia://en/24393682), and Bert Gerards had solved Rota's conjecture. The collaboration involved intense visits where the researchers sat in a room together, all day every day, in front of a whiteboard.[^o18] It would take them years to write up their research in its entirety and publish it.[^o19][^o20] An outline of the proof appeared 2014 in the *Notices of the American Mathematical Society*.[^o21] Only one paper by the same authors, related to this conjecture, has subsequently appeared.[^o22]
