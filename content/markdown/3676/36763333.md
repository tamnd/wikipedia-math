# Schwarz minimal surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schwarz minimal surface"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Schwarz minimal surfaces** are [periodic](wikipedia://en/31526142) [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) originally described by [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834).

:::

In the 1880s Schwarz and his student E. R. Neovius described periodic minimal surfaces.[^o2][^o3] They were later named by [Alan Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Schoen) in his seminal report that described the [gyroid](wikipedia://en/3905163) and other triply periodic minimal surfaces.[^o4]

The surfaces were generated using symmetry arguments: given a solution to [Plateau's problem](wikipedia://en/338129) for a polygon, reflections of the surface across the boundary lines also produce valid minimal surfaces that can be continuously joined to the original solution. If a minimal surface meets a plane at right angles, then the mirror image in the plane can also be joined to the surface. Hence given a suitable initial polygon inscribed in a unit cell periodic surfaces can be constructed.[^o5]

The Schwarz surfaces have [topological genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 3, the minimal genus of triply periodic minimal surfaces.[^o6]

They have been considered as models for periodic [nanostructures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanostructures) in [block copolymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_copolymer), electrostatic equipotential surfaces in crystals,[^o7] and hypothetical negatively curved graphite phases.[^o8]

## Schwarz P ("Primitive") {#Schwarz_P_("Primitive")}

*Image: Schwarz P Surface.png.* Schwarz P surface

Schoen named this surface 'primitive' because it has two intertwined congruent labyrinths, each with the shape of an inflated tubular version of the simple cubic lattice. While the standard P surface has cubic symmetry the unit cell can be any rectangular box, producing a family of minimal surfaces with the same topology.[^o10]

It can be approximated by the implicit surface

$\cos(x) + \cos(y) + \cos(z) = 0 \$.[^o11]

The P surface has been considered for prototyping [tissue scaffolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_engineering) with a high surface-to-volume ratio and porosity.[^o12]

## Schwarz D ("Diamond") {#Schwarz_D_("Diamond")}

*Image: Schwarz D Surface.png.* Schwarz D surface

Schoen named this surface 'diamond' because it has two intertwined congruent labyrinths, each having the shape of an inflated tubular version of the [diamond bond structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_lattice). It is sometimes called the F surface in the literature.

It can be approximated by the implicit surface

$$\cos(x)\cos(y)\cos(z) - \sin(x)\sin(y)\sin(z) = 0\.$$

Some sources give the alternate implicit surface

$$\sin(x)\sin(y)\sin(z) + \sin(x)\cos(y)\cos(z) + \cos(x)\sin(y)\cos(z) + \cos(x)\cos(y)\sin(z) = 0$$

which is equivalent up to a translation in all coordinates by $\frac{\pi}{4}$.

An exact expression exists in terms of [elliptic integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_integrals), based on the [Weierstrass representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_representation).[^o14]

## Schwarz H ("Hexagonal") {#Schwarz_H_("Hexagonal")}

*Image: Schwarz H Surface.png.* Schwarz H surface

The H surface is similar to a [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329) with a triangular boundary, allowing it to tile space.
