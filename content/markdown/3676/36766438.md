# Tom Sanders (mathematician)

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Tom Sanders"}

**Tom Sanders** is an English mathematician, working on problems in [additive combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243392) at the interface of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) and [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Sanders studied mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he was awarded a PhD in 2007 for research on [arithmetic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243381) supervised by [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715).[^o2][^o8]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

He held a Junior [Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Fellow) at [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge) from 2006 until 2011, in addition to visiting fellowships at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 2007, the [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in 2008, and the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) in 2009. Since 2011, he has held a [Royal Society University Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_University_Research_Fellowship) (URF)[^o3] at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), where he is also a senior research fellow at the [Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/7905850), and a Tutorial Fellow at [St Hugh's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Hugh's_College,_Oxford).[^o10]

Among other results, he has improved the theorem of [Klaus Friedrich Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Friedrich_Roth) on three-term [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389),[^o11] coming close to breaking the so-called logarithmic barrier. More precisely, he has shown that any subset of {1, 2, ..., *N*} of maximal cardinality containing no non-trivial three-term arithmetic progression is of size $O\!\left(\frac{N (\log \log N)^5}{\log N}\right)\;\!\!\!.$[^o12]

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In February 2011, he was awarded the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110) (jointly with [Harald Helfgott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Helfgott)) for having "employed deep harmonic analysis to understand arithmetic progressions and answer long-standing conjectures in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527)".[^o14] In July 2012, he was awarded a Prize of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) for his "fundamental results in additive combinatorics and harmonic analysis, which combine in a masterful way deep known techniques with the invention of new methods to achieve spectacular results."[^o15] In July 2013, he was awarded the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097)[^o16] for his "spectacular results in additive combinatorics and related areas"*,*in particular "for his paper obtaining the best known upper bounds for sets of integers containing no 3-term arithmetic progressions, for his work dramatically improving bounds connected with [Freiman's theorem](wikipedia://en/2240299) on sets with small doubling, and for other results in additive combinatorics and harmonic analysis."[^o17] In September 2013, he was awarded the [European Prize in Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Prize_in_Combinatorics).[^o18]
