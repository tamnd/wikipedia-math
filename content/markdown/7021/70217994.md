# Drug liking

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Drug liking"}

**Drug liking** is a measure of the [pleasurable (hedonic) experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasure) when a person consumes [drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug).[^o2] It is commonly used to study the [misuse liability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misuse_liability) of drugs.[^o3][^o4][^o5] Drug liking is often measured using unipolar and bipolar [visual analogue scales](wikipedia://en/17920455) (VAS), such as the Drug Liking VAS, the High VAS, the Take Drug Again (TDA) VAS, and the Overall Drug Liking (ODL) VAS.[^o3][^o5] There is a dissociation of drug liking from drug wanting (unconscious attribution of [incentive salience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incentive_salience)).[^o2][^o6] Drugs that increase scores on drug-liking measures include [amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substituted_amphetamine), [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine), [methylphenidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylphenidate), [MDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDMA), [opioids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid), [benzodiazepines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzodiazepine), [Z-drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z-drug), [barbiturates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbiturate), [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_%28drug%29), [nicotine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicotine), and [caffeine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffeine) (limitedly), among others.[^o4][^o7][^o5][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13]

:::
