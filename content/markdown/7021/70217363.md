# Continuity in probability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuity in probability"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) is said to be **continuous in probability** or **stochastically continuous** if its distributions converge whenever the values in the index set converge. [^o7][^o6]

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X=(X_t)_{t \in T}$ be a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The process $X$ is continuous in probability when $X_r$ [converges in probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_probability) to $X_s$ whenever $r$ converges to $s$.[^o6]

:::

## Examples and Applications {#Examples_and_Applications}

[Feller processes](wikipedia://en/6891551) are continuous in probability at $t=0$. Continuity in probability is a sometimes used as one of the defining property for [Lévy process](wikipedia://en/1025748).[^o7] Any process that is continuous in probability and has [independent increments](wikipedia://en/877315) has a [version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Version_%28probability_theory%29) that is [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839).[^o6] As a result, some authors immediately define Lévy process as being càdlàg and having independent increments.[^o8]
