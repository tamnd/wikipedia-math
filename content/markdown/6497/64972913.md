# Expected mean squares

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Expected mean squares"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **expected mean squares (EMS)** are the expected values of certain statistics arising in partitions of sums of squares in the [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634) (ANOVA). They can be used for ascertaining which statistic should appear in the denominator in an [F-test](wikipedia://en/318976) for testing a [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) that a particular effect is absent.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

When the total corrected sum of squares in an ANOVA is partitioned into several components, each attributed to the effect of a particular predictor variable, each of the sums of squares in that partition is a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) that has an [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653). That expected value divided by the corresponding number of degrees of freedom is the expected [mean square](wikipedia://en/634#Partitioning_of_the_sum_of_squares) for that predictor variable.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The following example is from *Longitudinal Data Analysis* by Donald Hedeker and [Robert D. Gibbons](wikipedia://en/76006335).[^o6]

Each of *s* treatments (one of which may be a placebo) is administered to a sample of (capital) *N* randomly chosen patients, on whom certain measurements $Y_{hij}$ are observed at each of (lower-case) *n* specified times, for $h=1,\ldots,s, \quad i=1,\ldots,N_h$ (thus the numbers of patients receiving different treatments may differ), and $j=1,\ldots, n.$ We assume the sets of patients receiving different treatments are disjoint, so patients are [nested](wikipedia://en/17941446#Nested_random_effects) within treatments and not crossed with treatments. We have

$$Y_{hij} = \mu + \gamma_h + \tau_j + (\gamma\tau)_{hj} + \pi_{i(h)} + \varepsilon_{hij}$$

where

- $\mu$ = [grand mean](wikipedia://en/8132847), (fixed)
- $\gamma_h$ = effect of treatment $h$, (fixed)
- $\tau_j$ = effect of time $j$, (fixed)
- $(\gamma\tau)_{hj}$ = interaction effect of treatment $h$ and time $j$, (fixed)
- $\pi_{i(h)}$ = individual difference effect for patient $i$ nested within treatment $h$, (random)
- $\varepsilon_{hij}$ = error for patient $i$ in treatment $h$ at time $j$. (random)
- $\sigma_\pi^2$ = variance of the random effect of patients nested within treatments,
- $\sigma_\varepsilon$ = error variance.

The total corrected sum of squares is

$$\sum_{hij} (Y_{hij} - \overline Y)^2 \quad\text{where } \overline Y = \frac 1 n \sum_{hij} Y_{hij}.$$

The ANOVA table below partitions the sum of squares (where $N = \sum_h N_h$):

::: {#o7 .table cue=table}

| source of variability | degrees of freedom | sum of squares | mean square | expected mean square |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| treatment | $s - 1$ | $\text{SS}_\text{Tr} = n\sum_{h=1}^s N_h(\overline Y_{h\cdot\cdot} - \overline Y_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2$ | $\dfrac{\text{SS}_\text{Tr}}{s-1}$ | $\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + n \sigma_\pi^2 + D_\text{Tr}$ |
| time | $n - 1$ | $\text{SS}_\text{T} = N \sum_{j=1}^n (\overline Y_{\cdot\cdot j} - \overline Y_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2$ | $\dfrac{\text{SS}_\text{T}}{n-1}$ | $\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + D_\text{T}$ |
| treatment × time | $(s-1)(n-1)$ | $\text{SS}_\text{Tr T} = \sum_{h=1}^s \sum_{j=1}^n N_h (\overline Y_{h\cdot j} - \overline Y_{h\cdot\cdot} - \overline Y_{\cdot\cdot j} + \overline Y_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2$ | $\dfrac{\text{SS}_\text{Tr T}}{(n-1)(s-1)}$ | $\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + D_\text{Tr T}$ |
| patients within treatments | $N - s$ | $\text{SS}_{\text{S}(\text{Tr})} = n\sum_{h=1}^s \sum_{i=1}^{N_h} (\overline Y_{hi\cdot} - \overline Y_{h\cdot\cdot})^2$ | $\dfrac{\text{SS}_{\text{S}(\text{Tr})}}{N-s}$ | $\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + n\sigma_\pi^2$ |
| error | $(N - s)(n - 1)$ | $\text{SS}_\text{E} = \sum_{h=1}^s \sum_{i=1}^{N_h} \sum_{j=1}^n (Y_{hij} - \overline Y_{h\cdot j} - \overline Y_{hi\cdot} + \overline Y_{h\cdot\cdot})^2$ | $\dfrac{\text{SS}_\text{E}}{(N-s)(n-1)}$ | $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ |

:::

### Use in F-tests {#Use_in_F-tests}

A null hypothesis of interest is that there is no difference between effects of different treatments—thus no difference among treatment means. This may be expressed by saying $D_\text{Tr}=0,$ (with the notation as used in the table above). Under this null hypothesis, the expected mean square for effects of treatments is $\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + n \sigma_\pi^2.$

The numerator in the F-statistic for testing this hypothesis is the mean square due to differences among treatments, i.e. it is $\left. \text{SS}_\text{Tr} \right/(s-1).$ The denominator, however, is **not** $\left. \text{SS}_\text{E}\right/ \big( (N-s)(n-1) \big).$ The reason is that the random variable below, although under the null hypothesis it has an [F-distribution](wikipedia://en/332116), is not observable—it is not a statistic—because its value depends on the unobservable parameters $\sigma_\pi^2$ and $\sigma_\varepsilon^2.$

$$\frac{\left.\frac{\text{SS}_\text{Tr}}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + n\sigma_\pi^2} \right/(s-1)}{ \left. \frac{\text{SS}_\text{E}}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2} \right/ \big( (N-s)(n-1) \big)} \ne \frac{\text{SS}_\text{Tr}/(s-1)}{\text{SS}_\text{E}/\big((N-s)(n-1)\big)}$$

Instead, one uses as the [test statistic](wikipedia://en/1416975) the following random variable that is not defined in terms of $\text{SS}_\text{E}$:

$$F = \frac{\left.\frac{\text{SS}_\text{Tr}}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2 + n\sigma_\pi^2} \right/(s-1)}{ \left. \frac{\text{SS}_{\text{S}(\text{Tr})}}{\sigma_\varepsilon^2+ n\sigma_\pi^2} \right/ (N-s)} = \frac{\left. \text{SS}_\text{Tr} \right/(s-1)}{ \left. \text{SS}_\text{S(Tr)} \right/ (N-s)}$$

:::
