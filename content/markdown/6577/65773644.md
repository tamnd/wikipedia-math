# Vivanti–Pringsheim theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vivanti–Pringsheim theorem"}

The **Vivanti–Pringsheim theorem** is a mathematical statement in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), that determines a specific [singularity](wikipedia://en/54240) for a function described by certain type of [power series](wikipedia://en/59958). The theorem was originally formulated by [Giulio Vivanti](wikipedia://en/34327454) in 1893 and proved in the following year by [Alfred Pringsheim](wikipedia://en/1068126).

:::

More precisely the [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) states the following:

A complex function defined by a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958)

$$f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nz^n$$

with non-negative real coefficients $a_n$ and a [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) $R$ has a singularity at $z=R$.

A simple example is the (complex) [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)

$$f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^n =\frac{1}{1-z}$$

with a singularity at $z=1$.

The example of the geometric series gives an isolated singularity. An example of a series with a non-isolated singularity at $z=1$ is

$$f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{n!}.$$
