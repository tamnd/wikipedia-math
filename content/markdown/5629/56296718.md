# Konstantinos Papaioannou

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Konstantinos Papaioannou"}

**Konstantinos Papaioannou** (Greek: Κωνσταντίνος Παπαϊωάννου; 1899 – 6 October 1979)[^o2] was a Greek physicist, mathematician, and professor of mechanics at the [University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Athens).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in Athens in 1899. He completed secondary school in Athens and then studied at the [National Technical University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technical_University_of_Athens). He graduated there in 1919 with a degree from the School of Mechanical and Electrical Engineering. He continued his studies at the University of Athens, where he obtained a degree in mathematics and then a doctorate in mathematics. He was appointed a professor of the University of Athens and became a member of the [Academy of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Athens_%28modern%29).

Papaioannou was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1928 in Bologna, in 1932 in Zurich,[^o4] and in 1936 in Oslo.[^o5] He died on October 6, 1979, the same day that [Anastasios Orlandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasios_Orlandos) died.[^o6][^o7]

Papaionnou was the (unofficial) referee for the famous 154-page doctoral thesis of [Christos Papakyriakopoulos](wikipedia://en/1463636) and had the mathematical ability to immediately foresee Papakyriakopoulos's successful research career.[^o8]
