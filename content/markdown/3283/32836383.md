# Enrico Giusti

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Enrico Giusti"}

**Enrico Giusti** (28 October 1940 – 26 March 2024) was an Italian mathematician mainly known for his contributions to the fields of [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), regularity theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), [minimal surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surfaces) and [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220). He was professor of mathematics at the [Università di Firenze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florence);[^o3] he also taught and conducted research at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) at Canberra, at the [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o4] After retirement, he devoted himself to the managing of the "Giardino di Archimede",[^o5] a museum entirely dedicated to mathematics and its applications. Giusti was also the editor-in-chief of the international journal dedicated to the history of mathematics *Bollettino di storia delle scienze matematiche* (*Bulletin of the history of the mathematical sciences*).[^o6][^o7]

:::

One of Giusti's most famous results, obtained with [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475) and [Ennio De Giorgi](wikipedia://en/1752401), concerned the minimality of [Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Harris_Simons)' cones, and made it possible to disprove the validity of [Bernstein's theorem](wikipedia://en/30486183) in dimensions larger than 8. The work on minimal surfaces was mentioned in the citation of the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), eventually awarded to Bombieri in 1974.

Giusti had a sustained interest in the history of mathematics, e.g. the mathematics of [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) (see Giusti 2009). At the time of his death, he was the director of the [Garden of Archimedes](wikipedia://en/51333951), a museum devoted to mathematics in Florence, Italy.[^o8]

Giusti died in Florence on 26 March 2024, at the age of 83.[^o9]

## Awards {#Awards}

Giusti won the [Caccioppoli Prize](wikipedia://en/23538352) of the [Italian Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/11969092) in 1978[^o11] and in 2003 was awarded the national medal for mathematics by the Accademia Nazionale delle Scienze (dei XL).
