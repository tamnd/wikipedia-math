# Boris Shapiro

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boris Shapiro"}

**Boris Z. Shapiro** (born 1957, [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)) is a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia)-[Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) mathematician, whose research concerns [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [Schubert calculus](wikipedia://en/3061740). The Shapiro–Shapiro conjecture (or simply the Shapiro conjecture) was named after Michael Shapiro and him[^o2] (it is now the well-known Mukhin–Tarasov–[Varchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Varchenko) theorem[^o3]).

:::

Shapiro enrolled in the PhD program at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1985 as a student of [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490), but his thesis defense was rejected by the examining committee. He then defended the same thesis at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) in 1990, and was awarded his PhD. He became the most prolific PhD student of Arnold, in terms of academic descendance. He has been a professor at Stockholm University since 1993.[^o5][^o6]
