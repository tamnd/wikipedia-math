# Jerome Levine

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jerome Levine"}

**Jerome Paul Levine** (May 4, 1937 – April 8, 2006) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who contributed to the understanding of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), Levine received his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) from [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1958, and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1962, studying under [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod). He began his career as an instructor at M.I.T., after which he spent a year at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) under a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) [fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow). He became a professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1964, and in 1966 he left for [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University). His early work helped to develop [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115) as a powerful tool in knot theory and in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732). In 1970 he was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Nice.[^o3]

Jerome Levine died after a long and hard-fought battle with [lymphatic cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphatic_cancer) at the age of 68. He was an active mathematician at Brandeis until his death, with his last paper[^o4] published four months after he died.
