# N-group (finite group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="N-group"}

In mathematical [finite group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_group_theory), an **N-group** is a group all of whose [local subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_subgroup) (that is, the [normalizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) of nontrivial [*p*-subgroups](wikipedia://en/24664)) are [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012). The non-solvable ones were classified by [Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189) during his work on finding all the minimal finite simple groups.

:::

## Simple N-groups {#Simple_N-groups}

The simple N-groups were classified by (txt) in a series of 6 papers totaling about 400 pages.

The simple N-groups consist of the [special linear groups](wikipedia://en/144823) PSL<sub>2</sub>(*q*), PSL<sub>3</sub>(3), the [Suzuki groups](wikipedia://en/29628738) Sz(2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>), the [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) U<sub>3</sub>(3), the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) *A*<sub>7</sub>, the [Mathieu group](wikipedia://en/380207) M<sub>11</sub>, and the [Tits group](wikipedia://en/606719). (The Tits group was overlooked in Thomson's original announcement in 1968, but Hearn pointed out that it was also a simple N-group.) More generally Thompson showed that any non-solvable N-group is a subgroup of Aut(*G*) containing *G* for some simple N-group *G*.

(Gorenstein Lyons 1976) generalized Thompson's theorem to the case of groups where all 2-local subgroups are solvable. The only extra simple groups that appear are the unitary groups U<sub>3</sub>(*q*).

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

(Gorenstein 1980, 16.5) gives a summary of Thompson's classification of N-groups.

The primes dividing the order of the group are divided into four classes π<sub>1</sub>, π<sub>2</sub>, π<sub>3</sub>, π<sub>4</sub> as follows

- π<sub>1</sub> is the set of primes *p* such that a [Sylow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_group) *p*-subgroup is nontrivial and cyclic.
- π<sub>2</sub> is the set of primes *p* such that a Sylow *p*-subgroup *P* is non-cyclic but SCN<sub>3</sub>(*P*) is empty.
- π<sub>3</sub> is the set of primes *p* such that a Sylow *p*-subgroup *P* has SCN<sub>3</sub>(*P*) nonempty and normalizes a nontrivial abelian subgroup of order prime to *p*.
- π<sub>4</sub> is the set of primes *p* such that a Sylow *p*-subgroup *P* has SCN<sub>3</sub>(*P*) nonempty but does not normalize a nontrivial abelian subgroup of order prime to *p*.

The proof is subdivided into several cases depending on which of these four classes the prime 2 belongs to, and also on an integer *e*, which is the largest [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) for which there is an [elementary abelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_abelian) subgroup of rank *e* normalized by a nontrivial 2-subgroup intersecting it trivially.

- (Thompson 1968) Gives a general introduction, stating the main theorem and proving many preliminary lemmas.
- (Thompson 1970) characterizes the groups *E*<sub>2</sub>(3) and *S*<sub>4</sub>(3) (in Thompson's notation; these are the exceptional group *G*<sub>2</sub>(3) and the symplectic group *Sp*<sub>4</sub>(3)) which are not N-groups but whose characterizations are needed in the proof of the main theorem.
- (Thompson 1971) covers the case where 2∉π<sub>4</sub>. Theorem 11.2 shows that if 2∈π<sub>2</sub> then the group is PSL<sub>2</sub>(*q*), M<sub>11</sub>, *A*<sub>7</sub>, U<sub>3</sub>(3), or PSL<sub>3</sub>(3). The possibility that 2∈π<sub>3</sub> is ruled out by showing that any such group must be a C-group and using Suzuki's classification of C-groups to check that none of the groups found by Suzuki satisfy this condition.
- (Thompson 1973) and (Thompson 1974) cover the cases when 2∈π<sub>4</sub> and *e*≥3, or *e*=2. He shows that either *G* is a [C-group](wikipedia://en/29529118) so a Suzuki group, or satisfies his characterization of the groups *E*<sub>2</sub>(3) and *S*<sub>4</sub>(3) in his second paper, which are not N-groups.
- (Thompson 1974) covers the case when 2∈π<sub>4</sub> and *e*=1, where the only possibilities are that *G* is a [C-group](wikipedia://en/29529118) or the [Tits group](wikipedia://en/606719).

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

A **minimal simple group** is a non-cyclic simple group all of whose proper subgroups are solvable. The complete list of minimal finite simple groups is given as follows (Thompson 1968, corollary 1)

- PSL<sub>2</sub>(2<sup>*p*</sup>), *p* a prime.
- PSL<sub>2</sub>(3<sup>*p*</sup>), *p* an odd prime.
- PSL<sub>2</sub>(*p*), *p* > 3 a prime congruent to 2 or 3 mod 5
- Sz(2<sup>*p*</sup>), *p* an odd prime.
- PSL<sub>3</sub>(3)

In other words a non-cyclic [finite simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) must have a [subquotient](wikipedia://en/1639294) isomorphic to one of these groups.
