# Feller's coin-tossing constants

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Feller's coin-tossing constants"}

**Feller's coin-tossing constants** are a set of numerical constants which describe [asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic) [probabilities](wikipedia://en/22934) that in *n* independent tosses of a [fair coin](wikipedia://en/9597100), no run of *k* consecutive heads (or, equally, tails) appears.

:::

[William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644) showed[^o2] that if this probability is written as *p*(*n*,*k*) then

$$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty} p(n,k) \alpha_k^{n+1}=\beta_k$$

where α<sub>*k*</sub> is the smallest positive real root of

$$x^{k+1}=2^{k+1}(x-1)$$

and

$$\beta_k={2-\alpha_k \over k+1-k\alpha_k}.$$

## Values of the constants {#Values_of_the_constants}

| k | $\alpha_k$ | $\beta_k$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | \1.23606797... | \1.44721359... |
| 3 | \1.08737802... | \1.23683983... |
| 4 | \1.03758012... | \1.13268577... |

For $k=2$ the constants are related to the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386), $\varphi$, and [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_numbers); the constants are $\sqrt{5}-1=2\varphi-2=2/\varphi$ and $1+1/\sqrt{5}$. The exact probability *p*(n,2) can be calculated either by using [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number), *p*(n,2) = $\tfrac{F_{n+2}}{2^n}$ or by solving a direct [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) leading to the same result. For higher values of $k$, the constants are related to [generalizations of Fibonacci numbers](wikipedia://en/8006956#Fibonacci_numbers_of_higher_order) such as the tribonacci and tetranacci numbers. The corresponding exact probabilities can be calculated as *p*(n,k) = $\tfrac{F^{(k)}_{n+2}}{2^n}$. [^o4]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

If we toss a fair coin ten times then the exact probability that no pair of heads come up in succession (i.e. *n* = 10 and *k* = 2) is *p*(10,2) = $\tfrac{9}{64}$ = 0.140625. The approximation $p(n,k) \approx \beta_k / \alpha_k^{n+1}$ gives 1.44721356...×1.23606797...<sup>−11</sup> = 0.1406263...

:::
