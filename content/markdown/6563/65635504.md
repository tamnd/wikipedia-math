# Kenmotsu manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kenmotsu manifold"}

In the mathematical field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Kenmotsu manifold** is an [almost-contact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost-contact_manifold) endowed with a certain kind of [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric). They are named after the Japanese mathematician Katsuei Kenmotsu.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $(M, \varphi, \xi, \eta)$ be an [almost-contact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost-contact_manifold). One says that a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) $g$ on $M$ is adapted to the almost-contact structure $(\varphi, \xi, \eta)$ if:

$$\begin{aligned} g_{ij}\xi^j&=\eta_i\\ g_{pq}\varphi_i^p\varphi_j^q&=g_{ij}-\eta_i\eta_j. \end{aligned}$$

That is to say that, relative to $g_p,$ the vector $\xi_p$ has length one and is orthogonal to $\ker \left(\eta_p\right);$ furthermore the restriction of $g_p$ to $\ker \left(\eta_p\right)$ is a [Hermitian metric](wikipedia://en/2640459) relative to the [almost-complex structure](wikipedia://en/514258) $\varphi_p\big\vert_{\ker \left(\eta_p\right)}.$ One says that $(M, \varphi, \xi, \eta, g)$ is an *almost-contact metric manifold*.

An almost-contact metric manifold $(M, \varphi, \xi, \eta, g)$ is said to be a **Kenmotsu manifold** if

$$\nabla_i\varphi_j^k=-\eta_j\varphi_i^k-g_{ip}\varphi_j^p\xi^k.$$

:::
