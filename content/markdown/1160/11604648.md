# Rose Rand

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Rose Rand"}

**Rose Rand** (born **Rozalia Rand**, ; June 14, 1903 – July 28, 1980) was an Austrian-American [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher). She was a member of the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Rose (Rozalia) Rand was born in [Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemberg) in the Austrian crown land of [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria) (today, Lviv, [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)).[^o5][^o6] After her family had moved to [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) she studied at the Polish [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866).[^o5] In 1924 she enrolled in [Vienna University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_University), her teachers included [Heinrich Gomperz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Gomperz), [Moritz Schlick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Schlick), and [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987).[^o7] She graduated with her first degree in 1928.[^o6] During her post-graduation years, she remained in contact with Vienna Circle colleagues such as Schlick.[^o7]

As a PhD candidate, Rand participated regularly in the Vienna Circle discussions, and kept records of these discussions, she was most active in the Vienna Circle from 1930 to 1935.[^o7][^o9] Between 1930 and 1937 she worked, and took part in research, at the Psychiatric-neurological Clinic of Vienna University.[^o8] She also earned money by tutoring students, giving adult education classes, and translating Polish articles on logic.[^o5][^o8]

In 1937 her doctoral thesis on [Tadeusz Kotarbiński](wikipedia://en/1022909)'s philosophy was approved and she completed her PhD [viva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis_defense).[^o6] In 1938, on the same day she completed her final doctoral exam, she was awarded her PhD.[^o8] As a [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew), however, she was barred from her profession.[^o9]

Rose Rand, unemployed and of Jewish descent, suffered great difficulties in pre-[World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) Vienna. In 1939, with the assistance of [Susan Stebbing](wikipedia://en/3783415), she finally emigrated to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) as a Jew [without nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statelessness).[^o6]

After a period of time in England in which she worked as a [nurse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse) she was admitted as "distinguished foreigner" at the faculty of Moral Science at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).[^o6] There she attended the seminars of [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741).[^o7] In 1943 she lost her privileges and had to work at a metal factory, and teach night classes in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) and [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) in the Luton Technical College and Tottenham Technical College.[^o5][^o9] Between 1943 and 1950 she also worked in practical [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering).[^o6] [Karl Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Popper) helped her to get a small research grant, so she could attend [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) as a "recognized student" [^o6] from 1950 to 1954.[^o8]

Rand moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1954. There she sought academic employment and initially attempted to pursue her research at the libraries of [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) universities.[^o7] Between 1955 and 1959 she held temporary positions teaching elementary math, ancient philosophy and logic, and was a research associate, in the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), [Indiana University Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Northwest) in [Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary,_Indiana), and [Notre Dame University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_University).[^o6][^o7]

In 1959 she returned to [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) and after that to [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey).[^o7] In the following years she earned her living from grants and fellowships which were given to her mostly for her work on translations of Polish and Russian logicians.[^o7] When not supported by grants Rand operated on private loans and other financial assistance, freelance translation work, or sporadic temporary employment.[^o6][^o7]

Rose Rand died on 28 July 1980 in Princeton, aged 77.[^o6][^o7]

Rand's records at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) contain, among other things, her research, the records of the discussions in the Vienna Circle and over 1,600 letters to [Otto Neurath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Neurath), [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) and others.[^o7] Some of her correspondence (from her time as an *émigré* in England) is also held by [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University)'s [Bodleian Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodleian_Library).[^o10][^o11]
