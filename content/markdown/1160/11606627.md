# Isaak Yaglom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isaak Yaglom"}

**Isaak Moiseevich Yaglom**[^o3] (Исаа́к Моисе́евич Ягло́м; 6 March 1921 – 17 April 1988)[^o4] was a Soviet [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and author of popular mathematics books, some with his twin [Akiva Yaglom](wikipedia://en/28210480).

:::

Yaglom received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1945 as student of [Veniamin Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veniamin_Kagan). As the author of several books, translated into English, that have become academic standards of reference, he has an international stature. His attention to the necessities of learning ([pedagogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy)) make his books pleasing experiences for students. The seven authors of his Russian obituary recount "…the breadth of his interests was truly extraordinary: he was seriously interested in history and philosophy, passionately loved and had a good knowledge of literature and art, often came forward with reports and lectures on the most diverse topics (for example, on [Alexander Blok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Blok), [Anna Akhmatova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Akhmatova), and the Dutch painter [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127)), actively took part in the work of the cinema club in [Yaroslavl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslavl) and the music club at the House of Composers in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), and was a continual participant of conferences on [mathematical linguistics](wikipedia://en/78756037) and on [semiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotics)."[^o6]

## University life {#University_life}

Yaglom started his higher education at Moscow State University in 1938. During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he volunteered, but due to [myopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myopia) he was deferred from military service. In the evacuation of Moscow he went with his family to [Sverdlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg) in the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains). He studied at [Sverdlovsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_State_University), graduated in 1942, and when the usual Moscow faculty assembled in Sverdlovsk during the war, he took up graduate study. Under the geometer [Veniamin Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veniamin_Kagan) he developed his Ph.D. thesis which he defended in Moscow in 1945. It is reported that this thesis "was devoted to projective metrics on a plane and their connections with different types of complex numbers $a + jb$ (where $jj=-1$, or $jj=+1$, or else $jj=0$)."[^o6]

## Institutes and titles {#Institutes_and_titles}

During his career, Yaglom was affiliated with these institutions:[^o6]

- Moscow Energy Institute (1946) – lecturer in mathematics
- [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) (1946–1949) – lecturer, department of analysis and differential geometry
- Orekhovo-Zuevo Pedagogical Institute (1949–1956) – lecturer in mathematics
- Lenin State Pedagogical Institute (Moscow) (1956–1968) – obtained D.Sc. 1965
- Moscow Evening Metallurgical Institute (1968–1974) – professor of mathematics
- [Yaroslavl State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslavl_State_University) (1974–1983) – professor of mathematics
- Academy of Pedagogical Sciences (1984–1988) – technical consultant

## Affine geometry {#Affine_geometry}

In 1962 Yaglom and Vladimir G. Ashkinuse published *Ideas and Methods of Affine and Projective Geometry*, in Russian. The text is limited to [affine geometry](wikipedia://en/243890) since projective geometry was put off to a second volume that did not appear. The concept of [hyperbolic angle](wikipedia://en/1139981) is developed through area of [hyperbolic sectors](wikipedia://en/1139926). A treatment of [Routh's theorem](wikipedia://en/4639094) is given at page 193. This textbook, published by the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_%28Soviet_Union%29), includes 234 exercises with hints and solutions in an appendix.

## English translations {#English_translations}

Isaac Yaglom wrote over 40 books and many articles. Several were translated, and appeared in the year given:

### Complex numbers in geometry (1968) {#Complex_numbers_in_geometry_(1968)}

Translated by Eric J. F. Primrose, published by Academic Press (N.Y.). The [trinity of complex number planes](wikipedia://en/51438#Two-dimensional_real_algebras) is laid out and exploited. Topics include [line coordinates](wikipedia://en/21252759#With_complex_numbers) in the Euclidean and Lobachevski planes, and [inversive geometry](wikipedia://en/295844).

### Geometric Transformations (1962, 1968, 1973, 2009) {#Geometric_Transformations_(1962,_1968,_1973,_2009)}

The first three books were originally published in English by Random House as part of the series [New Mathematical Library](wikipedia://en/1700591) (Volumes 8, 21, and 24). They were keenly appreciated by proponents of the [New Math](wikipedia://en/404988) in the U.S.A., but represented only a part of Yaglom's two-volume original published in Russian in 1955 and 56. More recently the final portion of Yaglom's work was translated into English and published by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). All four volumes are now available from the MAA in the series **Anneli Lax New Mathematical Library** (Volumes 8, 21, 24, and 44).

### A simple non-euclidean geometry and its physical basis (1979) {#A_simple_non-euclidean_geometry_and_its_physical_basis_(1979)}

Subtitle: *An elementary account of [Galilean geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean_geometry) and the Galilean principle of [relativity](wikipedia://en/26962)*. Translated by Abe Shenitzer, published by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer-Verlag). In his prefix, the translator says the book is "a fascinating story which flows from one geometry to another, from geometry to algebra, and from geometry to [kinematics](wikipedia://en/65914), and in so doing crosses artificial boundaries separating one area of mathematics from another and mathematics from physics." The author's own prefix speaks of "the important connection between Klein's [Erlanger Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlanger_Program) and the principles of relativity."

The approach taken is elementary; simple manipulations by [shear mapping](wikipedia://en/799405) lead on page 68 to the conclusion that "the difference between the Galilean geometry of points and the Galilean geometry of lines is just a matter of terminology".

The concepts of the [dual number](wikipedia://en/42169) and its "imaginary" ε, ε<sup>2</sup> = 0, do not appear in the development of Galilean geometry. However, Yaglom shows that the common [slope](wikipedia://en/29368) concept in analytic geometry corresponds to the *Galilean angle*. Yaglom extensively develops his non-Euclidean geometry including the theory of [cycles](wikipedia://en/42169#Cycles) (pp. 77–79), [duality](wikipedia://en/539925), and the circumcycle and incycle of a triangle (p. 104).

Yaglom continues with his Galilean study to the *inversive Galilean plane* by including a special line at infinity and showing the topology with a stereographic projection. The Conclusion of the book delves into the *Minkowskian geometry* of hyperbolas in the plane, including the [nine-point hyperbola](wikipedia://en/18388859). Yaglom also covers the *inversive Minkowski plane*.

### Probability and information (1983) {#Probability_and_information_(1983)}

Co-author: [A. M. Yaglom](wikipedia://en/28210480). Russian editions in 1956, 59 and 72. Translated by V. K. Jain, published by D. Reidel and the Hindustan Publishing Corporation, India. The channel capacity work of [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) is developed from first principles in four chapters: probability, entropy and information, information calculation to solve logical problems, and applications to information transmission. The final chapter is well-developed including code efficiency, [Huffman codes](wikipedia://en/13883), natural language and biological information channels, influence of noise, and error detection and correction.

### Challenging Mathematical Problems With Elementary Solutions (1987) {#Challenging_Mathematical_Problems_With_Elementary_Solutions_(1987)}

Co-author: [A. M. Yaglom](wikipedia://en/28210480). Two volumes. Russian edition in 1954. First English edition 1964–1967

### Felix Klein and Sophus Lie (1988) {#Felix_Klein_and_Sophus_Lie_(1988)}

Translated from the Russian by Sergei Sossinsky. Subtitle: "The evolution of the idea of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) in the 19th century". In his chapter on "Felix Klein and his Erlangen Program", Yaglom says that "finding a general description of all geometric systems \[was\] considered by mathematicians the central question of the day."[^o17] A great number of mathematicians are credited in this account of the modern tools and methods of symmetry.

In 2009 the book was republished by [Ishi Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishi_Press) as *Geometries, Groups and Algebras in the Nineteenth Century*.[^o18]
