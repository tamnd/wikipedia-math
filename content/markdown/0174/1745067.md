# Equianharmonic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equianharmonic"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and in particular the study of [Weierstrass elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/447181), the **equianharmonic case** occurs when the Weierstrass invariants satisfy *g*<sub>2</sub> = 0 and *g*<sub>3</sub> = 1.[^o2] This page follows the terminology of [Abramowitz and Stegun](wikipedia://en/1206236); see also the [lemniscatic case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemniscatic_elliptic_function). (These are special examples of [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183).)

:::

In the equianharmonic case, the minimal half period ω<sub>2</sub> is real and equal to

$$\frac{\Gamma^3(1/3)}{4\pi}$$

where $\Gamma$ is the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). The half period is

$$\omega_1=\tfrac{1}{2}(-1+\sqrt3i)\omega_2.$$

Here the [period lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_lattice) is a real multiple of the [Eisenstein integers](wikipedia://en/2353035).

The [constants](wikipedia://en/48779551) *e*<sub>1</sub>, *e*<sub>2</sub> and *e*<sub>3</sub> are given by

$$e_1=4^{-1/3}e^{(2/3)\pi i},\qquad e_2=4^{-1/3},\qquad e_3=4^{-1/3}e^{-(2/3)\pi i}.$$

The case *g*<sub>2</sub> = 0, *g*<sub>3</sub> = *a* may be handled by a scaling transformation.
