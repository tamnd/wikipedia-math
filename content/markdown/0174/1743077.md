# Sparkline

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sparkline"}

A **sparkline** is a very small line chart, typically drawn without axes or coordinates. It presents the general shape of a variation (typically over time) in some measurement, such as [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050) or [stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market) price, in a simple and highly condensed way. Whereas a typical [chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chart) is designed to professionally show as much data as possible, and is set off from the flow of text, sparklines are intended to be succinct, memorable, and located where they are discussed. Sparklines are small enough to be embedded in text, or several sparklines may be grouped together as elements of a [small multiple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_multiple).

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Sparkline of the narrative structure of the first three volumes of Tritram Shandy, accouring to the author.png.* Illustration from the "Life and Opinions of Tristram Shandy, Gentleman"

In 1762 [Laurence Sterne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Sterne) used typographical devices in his sixth volume of [The Life and Opinions of Tristram Shandy, Gentleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_and_Opinions_of_Tristram_Shandy,_Gentleman) to illustrate his narrative proceeding: "These were the four lines I moved through my first, second, third, and fourth volumes,–".[^o3]

The 1888 monograph describing the 1883 eruption of [Krakatoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakatoa) shows barometric signatures of the event obtained at various stations around the world in the same fashion, but in separate plates (VII & VIII), not within the text.[^o4]

[Edward Tufte](wikipedia://en/263977) documented a compact style in 1983 called "intense continuous time-series".[^o5] He introduced the term *sparkline* in 2006 for "small, high resolution graphics embedded in a context of words, numbers, images",[^o6][^o7] which are "data-intense, design-simple, word-sized graphics".[^o8] Later in 2020, Tufte attributed the idea to [Donald Knuth's](wikipedia://en/8095) ["METAFONTbook"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computers_and_Typesetting).[^o9]

*Image: Screenshot of Sparklines in Medved QuoteTracker, 1998.png.* Sparklines in Medved QuoteTracker[^o10]

The first software sparkline was programmed in 1999 by [Peter Zelchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Zelchenko).[^o12] He introduced "an inline-chart" feature for Mike Medved's QuoteTracker.[^o11][^o12] TD Ameritrade later discontinued QuoteTracker.[^o13]

On May 7, 2008, Microsoft employees filed a patent application for the implementation of sparklines in [Microsoft Excel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Excel) 2010. The application was published on November 12, 2009,[^o14] prompting Tufte[^o15] to express concerns about patent breadth and non-novelty.[^o16] On 23 January, 2009, MultiRacio Ltd. published an [OpenOffice.org Calc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenOffice.org_Calc) extension named "EuroOffice Sparkline".[^o17] On March 3, 2022, Tomaž Vajngerl implemented sparklines in [LibreOffice Calc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LibreOffice_Calc) version 7.4, including support for importing sparklines from the [OOXML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OOXML) Workbook format.[^o18]

## Usage {#Usage}

Sparklines are frequently used in line with text. For example:

> The [Dow Jones Industrial Average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Jones_Industrial_Average) for February 7, 2006
>
> *Image: Sparkline dowjones new.svg.*
>
> .

The sparkline should be about the same height as the text around it. [Tufte](wikipedia://en/263977) offers some useful design principles for the sizing of sparklines to maximize their readability.[^o7]
