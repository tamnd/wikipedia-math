# Smith graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smith graph"}

In the mathematical field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **Smith graph** is either of two kinds of graph.

:::

- It is a graph whose [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) has largest eigenvalue at most 2,[^o2] or has spectral radius 2[^o3] or at most 2.[^o4] The graphs with spectral radius 2 form two infinite families and three sporadic examples; if we ask for spectral radius at most 2 then there are two additional infinite families and three more sporadic examples. The infinite families with spectral radius less than 2 are the [paths](wikipedia://en/638889) and the [paths](wikipedia://en/638889) with one extra edge attached to the vertex next to an endpoint; the infinite families with spectral radius exactly 2 are the [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609) and the [paths](wikipedia://en/638889) with an extra edge attached to each of the vertices next to an endpoint.

These are also the simply laced [affine (and finite, if the spectral radius may be less than 2) Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/277109#Affine_Dynkin_diagrams).

- It is a [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) with certain kinds of parameter values.[^o5]
