# Athir al-Din al-Abhari

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Athir al-Din al-Abhari"}

**Athīr al-Dīn al-Mufaḍḍal ibn ʿUmar ibn al-Mufaḍḍal al-Samarqandī al-Abharī** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language)): اثیرالدین مُفَضَّل بن عمر بن مَفَضَّل سمرقندی ابهری; d. 1262 or 1265[^o6][^o4] also known as **Athīr al-Dīn al-Munajjim** (اثیرالدین منجم) was an Iranian Muslim [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Islamic_philosophy), [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomer), [astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astrologer) and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematician). Other than his influential writings, he had many disciples.

:::

## Life {#Life}

His birthplace is contested among sources. According to the *[Encyclopaedia of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopaedia_of_Islam)*[^o6] and the *[Encyclopaedia Islamica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopaedia_Islamica)*,[^o7] he was born in [Abhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhar), a small town between [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin) and [Zanjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanjan,_Iran) in the North-West of Iran. The claim of G.C. Anawati making him a native of Mosul in Iraq, taken from the fact that al-Abharī was educated by a scientist from Mosul, [Kamāl al-Dīn ibn Yūnus al-Mawṣilī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamāl_al-Dīn_ibn_Yūnus), must also been dismissed.[^o4] None of his oldest biographers mentioned Mosul as his birthplace,[^o7] and al-Abharī himself indicated that he had gone to Mosul for this purpose.[^o4] Beside the city of Abhar, the epithet al-Abharī could suggest that he or his ancestors originally stem from the Abhar tribe.[^n1]

In his youth al-Abharī was a student of the theologian [Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhr_al-Din_al-Razi), probably in the city of [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni) or [Herat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herat). Beside philosophy and logic, from al-Rāzī it is likely that al-Abharī received an orthodox Sunni instruction in theology (kalām), jurisprudence (fiqh), and Qur’anic exegesis (tafsīr).[^o4] When Mongol took [Khwarezmian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_the_Khwarazmian_Empire), al-Abharī, in 1228 he flew to [Erbil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erbil), then to [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus), where he studied to Muḥyī al-Dīn Muḥammad b. Sa‘īd b. Nadī.[^o4] Then he went to Mosul, where he studied mathematics, especially astronomy, under the direction of Kamāl al-Dīn al-Mawṣilī.[^o6][^o4]

Among his students were [Najm al-Din al-Qazwini al-Katibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najm_al-Din_al-Qazwini_al-Katibi), [Abū Zakariya al-Qazwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakariya_al-Qazwini), and [Ibn Khallikān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khallikan).[^o9][^o6]

According to most accounts, al-Abharī died in Mosul between 660/1261–62 and 663/1264–65,[^o6] during the reign of [Khān Hülegü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulegu_Khan).[^o4]

## Works {#Works}

**Astronomy**

- Risāla fī al-hayʾa (رساله الهیئة; lit. Treatise on astronomy).
- Mukhtaṣar fī al-hayʾa (مختصر فی علم الهیئة; lit. Epitome on astronomy).
- Kashf al-ḥaqāʾiq fī taḥrīr al-daqāʾiq (كشف الحقائق فی تحریر الدقائق), where he accepts the view that the [celestial bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_bodies) do not change and maintains that stars have volition and it is the source of their motion.[^n1]

**Mathematics**

- Several works on Iṣlāḥ (اصلاح; lit. Correction) of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331), one of which is an attempt to prove the [parallel postulate](wikipedia://en/33731493), which was commented upon and criticized by [Shams al-Dīn al-Samarqandī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams_al-Dīn_al-Samarqandī).[^n1]

**Philosophy**

- ar (هدایةالحکمه; lit. Guide on Philosophy): a book dealing with the complete cycle of Hikmat, i.e., [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy), and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics).
- *ar* (ایساغوجی فی المنطق; Commentary on [Porphyry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_%28philosopher%29)'s *Isagoge*), a treatise on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065). Latin Translation by [Thomas Obicini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Obicini); *Īsāghūkhī, Isagoge. Id est, breve Introductorium Arabicum in Scientiam Logices: cum versione latina: ac theses Sanctae Fidei. R. P. F. Thomae Novariensis* (1625).
