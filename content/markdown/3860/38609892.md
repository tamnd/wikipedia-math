# Alessio Figalli

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alessio Figalli"}

**Alessio Figalli** (; born 2 April 1984) is an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working primarily on the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564).

:::

He was awarded the Peccot-Vimont Prize and the [Peccot Lectures](wikipedia://en/60897406) in 2012, the [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize) in 2012,[^o3] the [Stampacchia Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stampacchia_Medal) in 2015,[^o4] the [Feltrinelli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feltrinelli_Prize) in 2017, and the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2018. He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) 2014.[^o5] In 2016 he was awarded a [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) (ERC) grant, and in 2018 he received the Doctorate *Honoris Causa* from the [Université Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Côte_d'Azur). In 2019, he received the Doctorate *Honoris Causa* from the [Polytechnic University of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Catalonia).

## Biography {#Biography}

Figalli received his master's degree from the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa)[^o7] in 2006 (as a student of the [Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa)), and earned his doctorate in 2007 under the supervision of [Luigi Ambrosio](wikipedia://en/25337146) at the [Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa) and [Cédric Villani](wikipedia://en/27172341) at the [École Normale Supérieure de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure_de_Lyon). In 2007, he was appointed Chargé de recherche at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research), and in 2008 he went to the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) as Professeur Hadamard.[^o8]

In 2009, he moved to the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) as an associate professor. He became full professor in 2011, and [R. L. Moore](wikipedia://en/1077133) Chair holder in 2013. Since 2016, he is a chaired professor at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich).[^o8]

Amongst his several recognitions, Figalli has won an [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize) in 2012, he has been awarded the Peccot-Vimont Prize 2011 and Cours Peccot 2012 of the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) and has been appointed Nachdiplom Lecturer in 2014 at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich).[^o9] He has won the 2015 edition of the [Stampacchia Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stampacchia_Medal), and the 2017 edition of the [Feltrinelli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feltrinelli_Prize) for mathematics.

In 2018, he won the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) "for his contributions to the theory of optimal transport, and its application to partial differential equations, metric geometry, and probability".[^o10]

## Work {#Work}

Figalli has worked in the theory of [optimal transport](wikipedia://en/6631661), with particular emphasis on the regularity theory of optimal transport maps and its connections to [Monge–Ampère equations](wikipedia://en/4914066). Amongst the results he obtained in this direction, there stand out an important higher integrability property of the second derivatives of solutions to the Monge–Ampère equation[^o12] and a partial regularity result for Monge–Ampère type equations,[^o13] both proved together with [Guido de Philippis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_de_Philippis). He used optimal transport techniques to get improved versions of the anisotropic [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182), and obtained several other important results on the stability of functional and geometric inequalities. In particular, together with Francesco Maggi and Aldo Pratelli, he proved a sharp quantitative version of the anisotropic [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182).[^o14]

Then, in a joint work with [Eric Carlen](wikipedia://en/50605220), he addressed the stability analysis of some [Gagliardo–Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/38500906) and logarithmic [Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequalities](wikipedia://en/4665038) to obtain a quantitative rate of convergence for the critical mass Keller–Segel equation.[^o15] He also worked on [Hamilton–Jacobi equations](wikipedia://en/897539) and their connections to weak [Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theory](wikipedia://en/16972). In a paper with Gonzalo Contreras and Ludovic Rifford, he proved generic hyperbolicity of Aubry sets on compact surfaces.[^o16]

In addition, he has given several contributions to the Di Perna–Lions' theory, applying it both to the understanding of [semiclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiclassical_physics) limits of the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) with very rough potentials,[^o17] and to study the Lagrangian structure of weak solutions to the [Vlasov–Poisson equation](wikipedia://en/1991528).[^o18] More recently, in collaboration with [Alice Guionnet](wikipedia://en/49986739), he introduced and developed new transportation techniques in the topic of [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices) to prove universality results in several-matrix models.[^o19] Also, together with Joaquim Serra, he proved the [De Giorgi](wikipedia://en/1752401)'s conjecture for boundary reaction terms in dimension lower than five, and he improved the classical results by [Luis Caffarelli](wikipedia://en/2070173) on the structure of singular points in the [obstacle problem](wikipedia://en/19487617).[^o20]
