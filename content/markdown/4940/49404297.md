# Browder fixed-point theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Browder fixed-point theorem"}

The **Browder fixed-point theorem** is a refinement of the [Banach fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/46676) for [uniformly convex Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/9133418). It asserts that if $K$ is a nonempty [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) closed bounded set in uniformly convex [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) and $f$ is a mapping of $K$ into itself such that $\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\|x-y\|$ (i.e. $f$ is *non-expansive*), then $f$ has a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738).

:::

## History {#History}

Following the publication in 1965 of two independent versions of the theorem by [Felix Browder](wikipedia://en/10201445) and by [William Kirk](wikipedia://en/11968842), a new proof of Michael Edelstein showed that, in a uniformly convex Banach space, every iterative sequence $f^nx_0$ of a non-expansive map $f$ has a unique asymptotic center, which is a fixed point of $f$. (An *asymptotic center* of a sequence $(x_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}$, if it exists, is a limit of the [Chebyshev centers](wikipedia://en/16895496) $c_n$ for truncated sequences $(x_k)_{k\ge n}$.) A stronger property than asymptotic center is [Delta-limit](wikipedia://en/49394107) of Teck-Cheong Lim, which in the uniformly convex space coincides with the weak limit if the space has the [Opial property](wikipedia://en/11988974).
