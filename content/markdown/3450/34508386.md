# Park test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Park test"}

In [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390), the **Park test** is a test for [heteroscedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroscedasticity). The test is based on the method proposed by Rolla Edward Park for estimating [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) parameters in the presence of [heteroscedastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroscedastic) [error terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics).[^o2]

:::

## Background {#Background}

In [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997), [heteroscedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroscedasticity) refers to unequal [variances](wikipedia://en/32344) of the [random error terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) $\epsilon_i$, such that

$$\operatorname{Var}(\epsilon_i)=E(\epsilon_i^2)-E(\epsilon_i)^2=E(\epsilon_i^2)=\sigma_i^2.$$

It is assumed that $\operatorname{E}(\epsilon_i)=0$. The above variance varies with $i$, or the $i^{th}$ trial in an experiment or the $i^{th}$case or observation in a dataset. Equivalently, heteroscedasticity refers to unequal conditional variances in the response variables $Y_i$, such that

$$\operatorname{Var}(Y_i|X_i)=\sigma_i^2,$$

again a value that depends on $i$ – or, more specifically, a value that is conditional on the values of one or more of the regressors $X$. [Homoscedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoscedasticity), one of the basic [Gauss–Markov](wikipedia://en/170353) assumptions of [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) modeling, refers to equal variance in the random error terms regardless of the trial or observation, such that

$\operatorname{Var}(\epsilon_i)=\sigma^2$, a constant.

### Test description {#Test_description}

Park, on noting a standard recommendation of assuming proportionality between error term variance and the square of the regressor, suggested instead that analysts 'assume a structure for the variance of the error term' and suggested one such structure:[^o2]

$$\operatorname{ln}(\sigma_{\epsilon i}^2)=\operatorname{ln}(\sigma^2)=\gamma\operatorname{ln}(X_i)+v_i$$

in which the error terms $v_i$ are considered well behaved.

This relationship is used as the basis for this test.

The modeler first runs the unadjusted regression

$$Y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1X_{i1}+...+\beta_{p-1}X_{i,p-1}+\epsilon_i$$

where the latter contains *p* − 1 regressors, and then squares and takes the natural logarithm of each of the [residuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) ($\hat{\epsilon_i}$), which serve as estimators of the $\epsilon_i$. The squared residuals $\hat{\epsilon_i}^2$ in turn estimate $\sigma_{\epsilon i}^2$.

If, then, in a regression of $\ln{(\epsilon_i^2)}$ on the natural logarithm of one or more of the regressors $X_i$, we arrive at statistical significance for non-zero values on one or more of the $\hat\gamma_i$, we reveal a connection between the residuals and the regressors. We reject the null hypothesis of homoscedasticity and conclude that heteroscedasticity is present.

## Notes {#Notes}

The test has been discussed in econometrics textbooks.[^o7][^o8] [Stephen Goldfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Goldfeld) and [Richard E. Quandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Quandt) raise concerns about the assumed structure, cautioning that the v<sub>i</sub> may be heteroscedastic and otherwise violate assumptions of ordinary least squares regression.[^o9]
