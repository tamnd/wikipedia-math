# William Allen Whitworth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Allen Whitworth"}

**William Allen Whitworth** (1 February 1840 – 12 March 1905) was an English mathematician and a priest in the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Education and mathematical career {#Education_and_mathematical_career}

Whitworth was born in [Runcorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runcorn); his father, William Whitworth, was a school headmaster, and he was the oldest of six siblings. He was schooled at the Sandicroft School in Northwich and then at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), earning a B.A. in 1862 as 16th [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29). He taught mathematics at the Portarlington School and the Rossall School, and was a professor of mathematics at Queen's College in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) from 1862 to 1864. He returned to Cambridge to earn a master's degree in 1865, and was a fellow there from 1867 to 1882.[^o2]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

As an undergraduate, Whitworth became the founding editor in chief of the *[Messenger of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_of_Mathematics)*, and he continued as its editor until 1880.[^o2] He published works about the [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) and about [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790), but his most famous mathematical publication is the book *Choice and Chance: An Elementary Treatise on Permutations, Combinations, and Probability* (first published in 1867 and extended over several later editions).[^o2] The first edition of the book treated the subject primarily from the point of view of arithmetic calculations, but had an appendix on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and was based on lectures he had given at Queen's College.[^o3] Later editions added material on [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533) (the numbers of ways of arranging items into groups with various constraints), [derangements](wikipedia://en/162132), [frequentist probability](wikipedia://en/10869), [life expectancy](wikipedia://en/18669), and the fairness of bets, among other topics.[^o3]

Among the other contributions in this book, Whitworth was the first to use [ordered Bell numbers](wikipedia://en/6992164) to count the number of [weak orderings](wikipedia://en/828131) of a set, in the 1886 edition. These numbers had been studied earlier by [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034), but for a different problem.[^o6] He was the first to publish [Bertrand's ballot theorem](wikipedia://en/1043036), in 1878; the theorem is [misnamed](wikipedia://en/6695781) after [Joseph Louis François Bertrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_François_Bertrand), who rediscovered the same result in 1887.[^o7] He is the inventor of the E\[*X*\] notation for the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of a random variable *X*, still commonly in use,[^o8] and he coined the name "subfactorial" for the number of [derangements](wikipedia://en/162132) of *n* items.[^o9]

Another of Whitworth's contributions, in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), concerns [equable shapes](wikipedia://en/11435202), shapes whose area has the same numerical value (with a different set of units) as their perimeter. As Whitworth showed with D. Biddle in 1904, there are exactly five equable triangles with integer sides: the two [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420) with side lengths (5,12,13) and (6,8,10), and the three triangles with side lengths (6,25,29), (7,15,20), and (9,10,17).[^o10]

## Religious career {#Religious_career}

Whitworth was ordained as a [deacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) in 1865, and became a priest in 1866. He served as the [curate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curate) of St Anne's Church in [Birkenhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkenhead) in 1865, of the [Church of St Luke, Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St_Luke,_Liverpool) from 1866 to 1870 and of Christ Church in Liverpool from 1870 to 1875. He was then a [vicar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicar) in London at St John the Evangelist's in [Hammersmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammersmith). From 1886 to 1905 he was vicar of [All Saints, Margaret Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Saints,_Margaret_Street).[^o2]

He was the [Hulsean Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulsean_Lectures) in 1903.[^o2]
