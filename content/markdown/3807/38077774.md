# Matlis duality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matlis duality"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), **Matlis duality** is a [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) between [Artinian](wikipedia://en/542336) and [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/542431) [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a complete Noetherian [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391). In the special case when the local ring contains a field mapping to the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) it is closely related to earlier work by [Francis Sowerby Macaulay](wikipedia://en/3592252) on [polynomial rings](wikipedia://en/373065) and is sometimes called **Macaulay duality**, and the general case was introduced by (txt).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that *R* is a Noetherian complete local ring with residue field *k*, and choose *E* to be an [injective hull](wikipedia://en/665821) of *k* (sometimes called a **Matlis module**). The dual *D*<sub>*R*</sub>(*M*) of a module *M* is defined to be Hom<sub>*R*</sub>(*M*,*E*). Then Matlis duality states that the duality functor *D*<sub>*R*</sub> gives an anti-equivalence between the categories of Artinian and Noetherian *R*-modules. In particular the duality functor gives an anti-equivalence from the category of finite-length modules to itself.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Suppose that the Noetherian complete local ring *R* has a subfield *k* that maps onto a subfield of finite index of its residue field *R*/*m*. Then the Matlis dual of any *R*-module is just its dual as a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) over *k*, if the module is given its *m*-adic topology. In particular the dual of *R* as a topological vector space over *k* is a Matlis module. This case is closely related to work of Macaulay on graded polynomial rings and is sometimes called Macaulay duality.

If *R* is a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) with [quotient field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_field) *K* then the Matlis module is *K*/*R*. In the special case when *R* is the ring of [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423), the Matlis dual of a [finitely-generated module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_module) is the [Pontryagin dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_dual) of it considered as a [locally compact](wikipedia://en/1651204) [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974).

If *R* is a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of dimension *d* with [dualizing module](wikipedia://en/38513558) Ω, then the Matlis module is given by the [local cohomology](wikipedia://en/3212091) group H${}^{d}_{R}$(Ω). In particular if *R* is an Artinian local ring then the Matlis module is the same as the dualizing module.

:::

## Explanation using adjoint functors {#Explanation_using_adjoint_functors}

Matlis duality can be conceptually explained using the language of [adjoint functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) and [derived categories](wikipedia://en/985897):[^o8] the functor between the derived categories of *R*- and *k*-modules induced by regarding a *k*-module as an *R*-module, admits a right adjoint (derived [internal Hom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Hom))

$$D(k) \gets D(R) : R\operatorname{Hom}_R(k, -).$$

This right adjoint sends the injective hull $E(k)$ mentioned above to *k*, which is a [dualizing object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualizing_object) in $D(k)$. This abstract fact then gives rise to the above-mentioned equivalence.
