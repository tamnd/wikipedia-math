# Warped product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Warped product"}

**Warped product** $F\times_f B$ of two [Riemannian](wikipedia://en/144652) (or [pseudo-Riemannian](wikipedia://en/287555)) manifolds $F=(F,h)$ and $B=(B,g)$ with respect to a function $f\colon B\to\mathbb{R}$ is the product space $F\times B$ with the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) $g\oplus (f^2\cdot h)$.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Warped geometries are useful in that [separation of variables](wikipedia://en/387750) can be used when solving [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) over them.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Warped geometries acquire their full meaning when we substitute the variable *y* for *t*, time and *x*, for *s*, space. Then the *f*(*y*) factor of the spatial dimension becomes the effect of time that in words of Einstein "curves space". How it curves space will define one or other solution to a space-time world. For that reason, different models of space-time use warped geometries. Many basic solutions of the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540) are warped geometries, for example, the [Schwarzschild solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzschild_solution) and the [Friedmann–Lemaitre–Robertson–Walker models](wikipedia://en/343031).

Also, warped geometries are the key building block of [Randall–Sundrum models](wikipedia://en/671821) in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

:::
