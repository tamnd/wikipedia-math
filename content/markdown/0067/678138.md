# Cabtaxi number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cabtaxi number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the $n$-th **cabtaxi number**, typically denoted $\operatorname{Cabtaxi}(n)$, is defined as the smallest positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) that can be written as the sum of two *positive or negative or 0* [cubes](wikipedia://en/451445) in $n$ ways. Such numbers exist for all $n$, which follows from the analogous result for [taxicab numbers](wikipedia://en/513117).

:::

## History {#History}

Cabtaxi(2) was known to [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238) and [Pietro Bongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Bongo) in the late 16th century in the equivalent form $3^3+4^3+5^3=6^3$. The existence of Cabtaxi(3) was known to [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), but its actual solution was not found until later, by Edward B. Escott in 1902.

Cabtaxi(4) through Cabtaxi(7) were found by [Randall L. Rathbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randall_L._Rathbun) in 1992; Cabtaxi(8) was found by [Daniel J. Bernstein](wikipedia://en/198983) in 1998. Cabtaxi(9) was found by Duncan Moore in 2005, using Bernstein's method. Cabtaxi(10) was first reported as an upper bound by [Christian Boyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Boyer) in 2006 and verified as Cabtaxi(10) by [Uwe Hollerbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uwe_Hollerbach) and reported on the [NMBRTHRY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMBRTHRY) mailing list on May 16, 2008.
