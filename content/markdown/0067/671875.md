# Conformal symmetry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conformal symmetry"}

Conformal symmetry is a property of spacetime that ensures angles remain unchanged even when distances are altered. If you stretch, compress, or otherwise distort spacetime, the local angular relationships between lines or curves stay the same. This idea extends the familiar [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487) —which accounts for rotations, translations, and boosts—into the more comprehensive conformal group.

:::

Conformal symmetry encompasses [special conformal transformations](wikipedia://en/24675202) and [dilations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilation_%28affine_geometry%29). In three spatial plus one temporal dimensions, conformal symmetry has 15 [degrees of freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_freedom_%28physics_and_chemistry%29): ten for the Poincaré group, four for special conformal transformations, and one for a dilation.

[Harry Bateman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Bateman) and [Ebenezer Cunningham](wikipedia://en/11767545) were the first to study the conformal symmetry of [Maxwell's equations](wikipedia://en/19737). They called a generic expression of conformal symmetry a [spherical wave transformation](wikipedia://en/42475403).[^o2]

## Generators {#Generators}

The [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of the conformal group has the following [representation](wikipedia://en/12695):[^n1]

$$\begin{aligned} & M_{\mu\nu} \equiv i(x_\mu\partial_\nu-x_\nu\partial_\mu) \,, \\ &P_\mu \equiv-i\partial_\mu \,, \\ &D \equiv-ix_\mu\partial^\mu \,, \\ &K_\mu \equiv i(x^2\partial_\mu-2x_\mu x_\nu\partial^\nu) \,, \end{aligned}$$

where $M_{\mu\nu}$ are the [Lorentz](wikipedia://en/230489) [generators](wikipedia://en/99945), $P_\mu$ generates [translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_%28physics%29), $D$ generates scaling transformations (also known as dilatations or dilations) and $K_\mu$ generates the [special conformal transformations](wikipedia://en/24675202).

## Commutation relations {#Commutation_relations}

The [commutation](wikipedia://en/7193) relations are as follows:[^n1]

$$\begin{aligned} &[D,K_\mu]= -iK_\mu \,, \\ &[D,P_\mu]= iP_\mu \,, \\ &[K_\mu,P_\nu]=2i (\eta_{\mu\nu}D-M_{\mu\nu}) \,, \\ &[K_\mu, M_{\nu\rho}] = i ( \eta_{\mu\nu} K_{\rho} - \eta_{\mu \rho} K_\nu ) \,, \\ &[P_\rho,M_{\mu\nu}] = i(\eta_{\rho\mu}P_\nu - \eta_{\rho\nu}P_\mu) \,, \\ &[M_{\mu\nu},M_{\rho\sigma}] = i (\eta_{\nu\rho}M_{\mu\sigma} + \eta_{\mu\sigma}M_{\nu\rho} - \eta_{\mu\rho}M_{\nu\sigma} - \eta_{\nu\sigma}M_{\mu\rho})\,, \end{aligned}$$

other commutators vanish. Here $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ is the [Minkowski metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_metric) tensor.

Additionally, $D$ is a scalar and $K_\mu$ is a covariant vector under the [Lorentz transformations](wikipedia://en/18404).

The special conformal transformations are given by[^n2]

$$x^\mu \to \frac{x^\mu-a^\mu x^2}{1 - 2a\cdot x + a^2 x^2}$$

where $a^{\mu}$ is a parameter describing the transformation. This special conformal transformation can also be written as $x^\mu \to x'^\mu$, where

$$\frac{{x}'^\mu}{{x'}^2}= \frac{x^\mu}{x^2} - a^\mu,$$

which shows that it consists of an inversion, followed by a translation, followed by a second inversion.

In two-dimensional [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758), the transformations of the conformal group are the [conformal transformations](wikipedia://en/295907). There are [infinitely many](wikipedia://en/364774#Two_dimensions) of them.

In more than two dimensions, [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697) conformal transformations map circles to circles, and hyperspheres to hyperspheres with a straight line considered a degenerate circle and a hyperplane a degenerate hypercircle.

In more than two [Lorentzian dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space), conformal transformations map null rays to null rays and [light cones](wikipedia://en/310474) to light cones, with a null [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) being a degenerate light cone.

## Applications {#Applications}

### Conformal field theory {#Conformal_field_theory}

In relativistic quantum field theories, the possibility of symmetries is strictly restricted by [Coleman–Mandula theorem](wikipedia://en/1372610) under physically reasonable assumptions. The largest possible global [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of a non-[supersymmetric](wikipedia://en/224636) [interacting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_interaction) [field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) is a [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of the conformal group with an [internal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_group).[^o11] Such theories are known as [conformal field theories](wikipedia://en/364774).

### Second-order phase transitions {#Second-order_phase_transitions}

One particular application involves [critical phenomena](wikipedia://en/376800) in systems with local interactions. At the critical point, the fluctuations become conformally invariant, meaning they look the same regardless of scale or local stretching. That allows for classification of universality classes of phase transitions in terms of conformal field theories.

Conformal invariance is also present in two-dimensional turbulence at high [Reynolds number](wikipedia://en/23868856).[^o13]

### High-energy physics {#High-energy_physics}

Many theories studied in [high-energy physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-energy_physics) admit conformal symmetry due to it typically being implied by local [scale invariance](wikipedia://en/364774#Scale_invariance_vs_conformal_invariance). A famous example is d=4, [N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/36625367) due its relevance for [AdS/CFT correspondence](wikipedia://en/644443). Also, the [worldsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldsheet) in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) is described by a [two-dimensional conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913) coupled to two-dimensional gravity.

:::

## Mathematical proofs of conformal invariance in lattice models {#Mathematical_proofs_of_conformal_invariance_in_lattice_models}

Physicists have found that many lattice models become conformally invariant in the critical limit. However, mathematical proofs of these results have only appeared much later, and only in some cases.

In 2010, the mathematician [Stanislav Smirnov](wikipedia://en/22752379) was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) "for the proof of conformal invariance of [percolation](wikipedia://en/187805) and the planar [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) in statistical physics".[^o16]

In 2020, the mathematician [Hugo Duminil-Copin](wikipedia://en/57492975) and his collaborators proved that [rotational invariance](wikipedia://en/424202) exists at the boundary between phases in many physical systems.[^o17][^o18]
