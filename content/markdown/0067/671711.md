# Hilbert–Smith conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert–Smith conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hilbert–Smith conjecture** is concerned with the possible [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_group) of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). The conjecture says that if a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) *G* [act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) faithfully and continuous on a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) manifold *M*, then *G* must be a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945).

:::

The naming of the conjecture is for [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), and [Paul A. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_A._Smith).[^o2] It is considered by some to be a better formulation of [Hilbert's fifth problem](wikipedia://en/152760), than the characterisation of topological groups being also a topological manifolds as [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) often cited as a solution.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

Because of known structural results on *G*, it is enough to deal with the case where *G* is the additive group $\mathbb{Z}_p$ of [p-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer), for some [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*. An equivalent form of the conjecture is that $\mathbb{Z}_p$ has no faithful group action on a topological manifold.

In 1997, [Dušan Repovš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dušan_Repovš) and Evgenij Ščepin proved the Hilbert–Smith conjecture for groups acting by Lipschitz maps on a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) using [covering](wikipedia://en/398886), [fractal](wikipedia://en/285907), and [cohomological dimension theory](wikipedia://en/9880197).[^o5]

In 1999, [Gaven Martin](wikipedia://en/45145995) extended their dimension-theoretic argument to quasiconformal actions on a Riemannian manifold and gave applications concerning unique analytic continuation for Beltrami systems.[^o6]

In 2013, [John Pardon](wikipedia://en/48651594) proved the three-dimensional case of the Hilbert–Smith conjecture.[^o7]
