# Generalized taxicab number

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

Does there exist any number that can be expressed as a sum of two positive fifth powers in at least two different ways, i.e., $a^5+b^5=c^5+d^5$?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized taxicab number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **generalized taxicab number** $\operatorname{Taxicab}(k, j, n)$ is the smallest number — if it exists — that can be expressed as [the sum](wikipedia://en/12537045) of $j$ numbers to the $k$th positive [power](wikipedia://en/99491) in $n$ different ways. For $k = 3$ and $j = 2$, they coincide with the [taxicab number](wikipedia://en/513117).

:::

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Taxicab}(1, 2, 2) &= 4 = 1 + 3 = 2 + 2 \\ \mathrm{Taxicab}(2, 2, 2) &= 50 = 1^2 + 7^2 = 5^2 + 5^2 \\ \mathrm{Taxicab}(3, 2, 2) &= 1729 = 1^3 + 12^3 = 9^3 + 10^3 \end{aligned}$$

The latter example is [1729](wikipedia://en/512549), as first noted by [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan).

[Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler) showed that

$$\mathrm{Taxicab}(4, 2, 2) = 635318657 = 59^4 + 158^4 = 133^4 + 134^4.$$

However, $\operatorname{Taxicab}(5, 2, n)$ is not known for any $n \ge 2$: No positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) is known that can be written as the sum of two 5th powers in more than one way, and it is not known whether such a number exists.[^o3]
