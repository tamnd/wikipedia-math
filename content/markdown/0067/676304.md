# Uniquely colorable graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniquely colorable graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **uniquely colorable graph** is a $k$-chromatic [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) that has only one possible (proper) $k$-[coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) up to [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) of the colors. Equivalently, there is only one way to [partition](wikipedia://en/11973947) its [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) into $k$ [independent sets](wikipedia://en/524501) and there is no way to partition them into $k - 1$ independent sets.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) is uniquely colorable, because the only proper coloring is one that assigns each vertex a different color.

Every [*k*-tree](wikipedia://en/31104438) is uniquely (*k* + 1)-colorable. The uniquely 4-colorable [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) are known to be exactly the [Apollonian networks](wikipedia://en/31104610), that is, the planar 3-trees.[^n1]

Every connected [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) is uniquely 2-colorable. Its 2-coloring can be obtained by choosing a starting vertex arbitrarily, coloring the vertices at even distance from the starting vertex with one color, and coloring the vertices at odd distance from the starting vertex with the other color.[^n2]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A uniquely $k$-colorable graph $G$ with $n$ vertices has at least $m \ge (k-1)n - k(k-1)/2$ edges. Equality holds when $G$ is a $(k-1)$-tree.[^o8]

:::

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

### Minimal imperfection {#Minimal_imperfection}

A *minimal imperfect graph* is a graph in which every subgraph is [perfect](wikipedia://en/670531). The deletion of any vertex from a minimal imperfect graph leaves a uniquely colorable subgraph.

### Unique edge colorability {#Unique_edge_colorability}

*Image: Generalized Petersen 9 2 edge coloring.svg.* The unique 3-edge-coloring of the [generalized Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/23716451) *G*(9,2)

A **uniquely edge-colorable graph** is a [*k*-edge-chromatic](wikipedia://en/690647) graph that has only one possible [(proper) *k*-edge-coloring](wikipedia://en/690647) up to permutation of the colors. The only uniquely 2-edge-colorable graphs are the paths and the cycles. For any *k*, the [stars](wikipedia://en/19310129) *K*<sub>1,*k*</sub> are uniquely *k*-edge-colorable. Moreover, (Wilson 1976) conjectured and (Thomason 1978) proved that, when *k* ≥ 4, they are also the only members in this family. However, there exist uniquely 3-edge-colorable graphs that do not fit into this classification, such as the graph of the [triangular pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_pyramid).

If a [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) is uniquely 3-edge-colorable, it must have exactly three [Hamiltonian cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle), formed by the edges with two of its three colors, but some cubic graphs with only three Hamiltonian cycles are not uniquely 3-edge-colorable.[^n3] Every simple [planar](wikipedia://en/24314) cubic graph that is uniquely 3-edge-colorable contains a triangle,[^n1] but (txt) observed that the [generalized Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/23716451) *G*(9,2) is [non-planar](wikipedia://en/24314), triangle-free, and uniquely 3-edge-colorable. For many years it was the only known such graph, and it had been conjectured to be the only such graph[^o17] but now infinitely many triangle-free non-planar cubic uniquely 3-edge-colorable graphs are known.[^n4]

### Unique total colorability {#Unique_total_colorability}

A **uniquely total colorable graph** is a [*k*-total-chromatic graph](wikipedia://en/690702) that has only one possible [(proper) *k*-total-coloring](wikipedia://en/690702) up to permutation of the colors.

[Empty graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_graph), [paths](wikipedia://en/4587190), and [cycles](wikipedia://en/878962) of length divisible by 3 are uniquely total colorable graphs. (Mahmoodian Shokrollahi 1995) conjectured that they are also the only members in this family.

Some properties of a uniquely *k*-total-colorable graph *G* with *n* vertices:

1. χ″(*G*) = Δ(*G*) + 1 unless *G* = *K*<sub>2</sub>.[^n5]
1. Δ(*G*) ≤ 2 δ(*G*).[^n5]
1. Δ(*G*) ≤ n/2 + 1.[^n6]

Here χ″(*G*) is the [total chromatic number](wikipedia://en/690702); Δ(*G*) is the [maximum degree](wikipedia://en/325802#Degree); and δ(*G*) is the [minimum degree](wikipedia://en/325802#Degree).
