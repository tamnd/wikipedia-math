# Erhard Schmidt

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Erhard Schmidt"}

**Erhard Schmidt** (13 January 1876 – 6 December 1959) was a [Baltic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_German) mathematician whose work significantly influenced the direction of mathematics in the twentieth century. Schmidt was born in [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu) (Dorpat), in the [Governorate of Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_of_Livonia) (now [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia)).

:::

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

His advisor was [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)[^o2] and he was awarded his doctorate from [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1905. His doctoral dissertation was entitled *Entwickelung willkürlicher Funktionen nach Systemen vorgeschriebener* and was a work on [integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_equations).[^o2] Together with [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) he made important contributions to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). [Ernst Zermelo](wikipedia://en/178655) credited conversations with Schmidt for the idea and method for his classic 1904 proof of the [Well-ordering theorem](wikipedia://en/33458) from an "[Axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840)", which has become an integral part of modern [set theory](wikipedia://en/152214).[^o6]

After the war, in 1948, Schmidt founded and became the first editor-in-chief of the journal *[Mathematische Nachrichten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematische_Nachrichten)*.[^o4]

## National Socialism {#National_Socialism}

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) Schmidt held positions of authority at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and had to carry out various [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) resolutions against the Jews—a job that he apparently did not do well, since he was criticized at one point for not understanding the "[Jewish question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_question)". At the celebration of Schmidt's 75th birthday in 1951 a prominent Jewish mathematician, [Hans Freudenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Freudenthal), who had survived the Nazi years, spoke of the difficulties that Schmidt faced during that period without criticism.[^o4] Schmidt was, however, a conservative and a nationalist, and, in 1938, defended [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler) after [Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht), telling Jewish mathematician [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522): "Suppose we had to fight a war to rearm Germany, [unite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss) with [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria), liberate the [Saar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_the_Saar_Basin) and the [German part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetenland) of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia). Such a war would have cost us half a million young men. But everybody would have admired our victorious leader. Now, Hitler has sacrificed half a million Jews and has achieved great things for Germany. I hope some day you will be recompensed but I am still grateful to Hitler".[^o8]
