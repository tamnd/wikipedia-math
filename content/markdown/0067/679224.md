# J. Barkley Rosser

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="J. Barkley Rosser"}

**John Barkley Rosser Sr.** (December 6, 1907 – September 5, 1989) was an American [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), a student of [Alonzo Church](wikipedia://en/49731), and known for his part in the [Church–Rosser theorem](wikipedia://en/150035) in [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203). He also developed what is now called the "[Rosser sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosser_sieve)" in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He was part of the mathematics department at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) from 1936 to 1963, chairing it several times.[^o4] He was later director of the Army Mathematics Research Center at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) and the first director of the [Communications Research Division of IDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses#Center_for_Communications_and_Computing). Rosser also authored mathematical textbooks.

:::

In 1936, he proved [Rosser's trick](wikipedia://en/19460821), a stronger version of [Gödel's first incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_first_incompleteness_theorem), showing that the requirement for [ω-consistency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ω-consistency) may be weakened to consistency. Rather than using the [liar paradox](wikipedia://en/17967) sentence equivalent to "I am not provable," he used a sentence that stated "For every proof of me, there is a shorter proof of my negation".

In prime number theory, he proved [Rosser's theorem](wikipedia://en/2165486).

The [Kleene–Rosser paradox](wikipedia://en/14258729) showed that the original lambda calculus was inconsistent.

Rosser died of an [aneurysm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneurysm) September 5, 1989, at his home in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin).[^o3][^o4]

Rosser's son, [John Barkley Rosser Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Barkley_Rosser_Jr.) (1948–2023), was a [mathematical economist](wikipedia://en/18967255) and professor at James Madison University in Harrisonburg, Virginia.
