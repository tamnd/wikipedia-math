# Faddeev–Popov ghost

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Faddeev–Popov ghost"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), **Faddeev–Popov ghosts** (also called **Faddeev–Popov gauge ghosts** or **Faddeev–Popov ghost fields**) are extraneous [fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) which are introduced into [gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_theories) [quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/25267) to maintain the consistency of the [path integral formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation). They are named after [Ludvig Faddeev](wikipedia://en/4854639) and [Victor Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Popov).[^o2][^o3]

:::

A more general meaning of the word "ghost" in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) is discussed in [Ghost (physics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_%28physics%29).

## Overcounting in Feynman path integrals {#Overcounting_in_Feynman_path_integrals}

The necessity for Faddeev–Popov ghosts follows from the requirement that [quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/25267) yield unambiguous, non-singular solutions. This is not possible in the [path integral formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation) when a [gauge symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_symmetry) is present since there is no procedure for selecting among physically equivalent solutions related by gauge transformation. The path integrals overcount field configurations corresponding to the same physical state; the [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) of the path integrals contains a factor which does not allow obtaining various results directly from the [action](wikipedia://en/312881).

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Faddeev-Popov procedure"}

### Faddeev–Popov procedure {#Faddeev–Popov_procedure}

It is possible, however, to modify the action, such that methods such as [Feynman diagrams](wikipedia://en/11617) will be applicable by adding *ghost fields* which break the gauge symmetry. The ghost fields do not correspond to any real particles in external states: they appear as [virtual particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_particle) in Feynman diagrams – or as the *absence* of some gauge configurations. However, they are a necessary computational tool to preserve [unitarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarity_%28physics%29).

The exact form or formulation of ghosts is dependent on the particular [gauge](wikipedia://en/2000736) chosen, although the same physical results must be obtained with all gauges since the gauge one chooses to carry out calculations is an arbitrary choice. The [Feynman–'t Hooft gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_gauge) is usually the simplest gauge for this purpose, and is assumed for the rest of this article.

Consider for example non-Abelian gauge theory with

$$\int \mathcal{D}[A] \exp i \int \mathrm d^4 x \left ( - \frac{1}{4} F^a_{\mu \nu} F^{a \mu \nu } \right ).$$

The integral needs to be constrained via gauge-fixing via $G(A) = 0$ to integrate only over physically distinct configurations. Following Faddeev and Popov, this constraint can be applied by inserting

$$1 = \int \mathcal{D}[\alpha (x) ] \delta (G(A^{\alpha })) \mathrm{det} \frac{\delta G(A^{\alpha} )}{\delta \alpha }$$

into the integral. $A^{\alpha }$ denotes the gauge-fixed field.[^o7] The determinant is then expressed as a [Berezin integral](wikipedia://en/20041308). Indeed, for any square matrix $M$, one has the identity

$$\int \exp\left[-\theta^TM\eta\right] \,d\theta\,d\eta = \det M$$

where the integration variables $\theta,\eta$ are [Grassmann variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassmann_variable) (aka [supernumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernumber)): they anti-commute and square to zero. In the present case, one introduces a field of Grassmann variables, one for every point in space-time (corresponding to the determinant at that point in space-time, *i.e.* one for each fiber of the gauge-field fiber bundle.) Used in the above identity for the determinant, these fields become the Fadeev-Popov ghost fields.

Because Grassmann numbers anti-commute, they resemble the anti-commutation property of the [Pauli exclusion principle](wikipedia://en/24669), and thus are sometimes taken to be stand-ins for particles with spin 1/2. This identification is somewhat treacherous, as the correct construction for [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276) passes through the [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305), not the Grassmann algebra. The Clifford algebra has a natural [filtration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtration_%28abstract_algebra%29) inherited from the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628); this induces a [gradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra), the [associated graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_graded_algebra), which *is* naturally isomorphic to the Grassmann algebra. The details of this grading are presented at length in the article on [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305).

:::

## Spin–statistics relation violated {#Spin–statistics_relation_violated}

The Faddeev–Popov ghosts violate the [spin–statistics relation](wikipedia://en/352827), which is another reason why they are often regarded as "non-physical" particles.

For example, in [Yang–Mills theories](wikipedia://en/672202) (such as [quantum chromodynamics](wikipedia://en/25264)) the ghosts are [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [scalar fields](wikipedia://en/5173456) ([spin](wikipedia://en/19593829) 0), but they [anti-commute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutativity) (like [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermions)).

In general, [anti-commuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutativity) ghosts are associated with [bosonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) symmetries, while [commuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity) ghosts are associated with [fermionic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) symmetries.

## Gauge fields and associated ghost fields {#Gauge_fields_and_associated_ghost_fields}

Every gauge field has an associated ghost, and where the gauge field acquires a mass via the [Higgs mechanism](wikipedia://en/644550), the associated ghost field acquires the same mass (in the [Feynman–'t Hooft gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_gauge) only, not true for other gauges).

## Appearance in Feynman diagrams {#Appearance_in_Feynman_diagrams}

In [Feynman diagrams](wikipedia://en/11617), the ghosts appear as closed loops wholly composed of 3-vertices, attached to the rest of the diagram via a gauge particle at each 3-vertex. Their contribution to the [S-matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S_matrix) is exactly cancelled (in the [Feynman–'t Hooft gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_gauge)) by a contribution from a similar loop of gauge particles with only 3-vertex couplings or gauge attachments to the rest of the diagram. (A loop of gauge particles not wholly composed of 3-vertex couplings is not cancelled by ghosts.) The opposite sign of the contribution of the ghost and gauge loops is due to them having opposite fermionic/bosonic natures. (Closed fermion loops have an extra −1 associated with them; bosonic loops don't.)

## Ghost field Lagrangian {#Ghost_field_Lagrangian}

The Lagrangian for the ghost fields $c^a(x)\,$ in [Yang–Mills theories](wikipedia://en/672202) (where $a$ is an index in the adjoint representation of the [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group)) is given by

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ghost}}=\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}^{a}\partial^{\mu}c^{a}+gf^{abc}\left(\partial^{\mu}\bar{c}^{a}\right)A_{\mu}^{b}c^{c}\;.$$

The first term is a [kinetic term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_term) like for regular complex scalar fields, and the second term describes the interaction with the [gauge fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_field) as well as the [Higgs field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_field). Note that in *abelian* gauge theories (such as [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268)) the ghosts do not have any effect since the [structure constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_constant) $f^{abc} = 0$ vanish. Consequently, the ghost particles do not interact with abelian gauge fields.
