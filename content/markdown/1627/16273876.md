# Cox–Zucker machine

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cox–Zucker machine"}

In [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), the **Cox–Zucker machine** is an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) introduced by [David A. Cox](wikipedia://en/41248158) and [Steven Zucker](wikipedia://en/6743885) for studying [elliptic surfaces](wikipedia://en/3073462). It determines whether a given set of [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of an elliptic surface provides a basis, up to [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330), for the surface's [Mordell–Weil group](wikipedia://en/5841813), in the case where the base curve is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578). In this context, a section is a morphism from the base curve to the surface whose composition with the fibration is the identity; informally, it selects one point on each fiber.[^o2]

:::

The underlying method appeared in Cox and Zucker's 1979 article "Intersection numbers of sections of elliptic surfaces".[^o3] The name "Cox–Zucker machine" was later used by Charles Schwartz in 1984.[^o2]

## Name origin {#Name_origin}

The name is a near-[homophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophone) of the obscenity "cocksucker". In a 2021 memorial tribute to Zucker in *[Notices of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4977512)*, Cox recalled that, shortly after he and Zucker met as graduate students at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), they noticed the effect of their surnames in alphabetical order and joked that they should write a joint paper. Cox wrote: "A few weeks after we met, we realized that we had to write a joint paper because the combination of our last names, in the usual alphabetical order, is remarkably obscene."[^o5]
