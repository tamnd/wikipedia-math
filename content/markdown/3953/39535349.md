# Francis Raven

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Francis Raven"}

**Francis Raven** (April 26, 1914 – December 1983[^o6]) was an American [cryptologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptologist) and an early employee of the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939). He helped to crack many codes to assist the United States during World War II. He also helped the NSA's training program by creating two cryptology courses.

:::

## Early career {#Early_career}

He graduated from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1934 and joined the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy) as a [reserve officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROTC), where he was assigned to the Naval Security Group. His commission was reactivated in 1940 and he rejoined the NSG, working on Japanese problems with [Agnes Driscoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnes_Driscoll). After a brief period working on German ciphers, he returned to Japanese issues, and he was part of a group that systematically solved many of the low level codes starting in 1942. These were important as a source of [cribs](wikipedia://en/517019) used in working the [JN-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JN-25) fleet code. He also worked on the [JADE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JADE_%28cypher_machine%29) and [CORAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CORAL_%28cipher%29) machines, both of which were successfully cracked.

## After the war years {#After_the_war_years}

After the war, he stayed on as a civilian employee of the NSG, joining the [Armed Forces Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Security_Agency) in 1946 and moving on to NSA at its formation in 1952. From 1956 on, he held a series of executive posts within the agency, culminating in his appointment as Chief of the Office of NSA's training program where he played a major part in the development of two cryptology courses; these efforts won him several civilian awards.

## Retirement {#Retirement}

In retirement, he established a firm for genealogical research and he was active there until his death in 1983.
