# Dodecahedral number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dodecahedral number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **dodecahedral number** is a [figurate number](wikipedia://en/325260) that represents a [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407). The *n*th dodecahedral number is given by the formula

:::

$${n(3n-1)(3n-2) \over 2}={3n \choose 3}$$

The first such numbers are: [0](wikipedia://en/34513), [1](wikipedia://en/77591639), [20](wikipedia://en/208259), [84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/84_%28number%29), [220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/220_%28number%29), 455, 816, 1330, 2024, 2925, 4060, 5456, 7140, 9139, 11480, … .

## History {#History}

The first study of dodecahedral numbers appears to have been by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525), around 1630, in his *De solidorum elementis*. Prior to Descartes, figurate numbers had been studied by the ancient Greeks and by [Johann Faulhaber](wikipedia://en/9768822), but only for [polygonal numbers](wikipedia://en/213996), [pyramidal numbers](wikipedia://en/509081), and [cubes](wikipedia://en/451445). Descartes introduced the study of figurate numbers based on the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) and some [semiregular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/1548894); his work included the dodecahedral numbers. However, *De solidorum elementis* was lost, and not rediscovered until 1860. In the meantime, dodecahedral numbers had been studied again by other mathematicians, including [Friedrich Wilhelm Marpurg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_Marpurg) in 1774, [Georg Simon Klügel](wikipedia://en/19043679) in 1808, and [Sir Frederick Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Pollock,_1st_Baronet) in 1850.[^o3]

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Dodeca2024.jpg.* The (3n+1) tetrahedral number is also the (n+1) dodecahedral one.

The (3n+1)th [tetrahedral number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_number) is also the (n+1)th dodecahedral number. Illustrated is a geometrical rendering of this equality. When the triangular faces of the tetrahedron are divided into congruent trapezoids, the result is a dodecahedral graph; with the figurate number array for the (3n+1)th tetrahedral number each edge of the dodecahedral graph fall along (n+1) unit balls.

:::

## Generating Function {#Generating_Function}

The ordinary generating function of the Dodecahedral numbers is

$$\frac{x(1 + 16 x + 10 x^2)}{(-1 + x)^4}$$
