# Ward's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ward's conjecture"}

In mathematics, **Ward's conjecture** is the conjecture made by (txt, p. 451) that "many (and perhaps all?) of the [ordinary](wikipedia://en/32742753) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) that are regarded as being [integrable](wikipedia://en/2573213) or solvable may be obtained from the [self-dual](wikipedia://en/609737) [gauge](wikipedia://en/64324611) [field equations](wikipedia://en/1293340) (or its generalizations) by reduction".

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

(txt) explain how a variety of completely integrable equations such as the [Korteweg–De Vries equation](wikipedia://en/344116) (KdV) equation, the [Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation](wikipedia://en/4211219) (KP) equation, the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748), the [sine-Gordon equation](wikipedia://en/306645), the [Ernst equation](wikipedia://en/17254254) and the [Painlevé equations](wikipedia://en/2033003) all arise as reductions or other simplifications of the [self-dual Yang–Mills equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual_Yang–Mills_equations):

$$F= \star F$$

where $F$ is the [curvature](wikipedia://en/489440) of a [connection](wikipedia://en/706412) on an [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_%28topology%29) 4-dimensional [pseudo-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/287555), and $\star$ is the [Hodge star operator](wikipedia://en/310914).

They also obtain the equations of an integrable system known as the Euler–Arnold–Manakov top, a generalization of the [Euler top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_top), and they state that the [Kovalevsaya top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovalevsaya_top) is also a reduction of the self-dual Yang–Mills equations.

:::

## Penrose–Ward transform {#Penrose–Ward_transform}

Via the [Penrose–Ward transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose–Ward_transform) these solutions give the [holomorphic vector bundles](wikipedia://en/4794668) often seen in the context of algebraic [integrable systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_systems).
