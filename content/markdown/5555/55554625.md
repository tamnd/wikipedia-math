# Domenico Guglielmini

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Domenico Guglielmini"}

*Image: Guglielmini, Domenico – Della natura de fiumi trattato fisico-matematico, 1739 - BEIC 124586.jpg.* Incipit della *Natura dei fiumi*

**Domenico Guglielmini** ([Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna), 27 September 1655 - [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), 27 July 1710) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist) and physician, active and successful mainly in Bologna and Padua.[^o3][^n1]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna) to a well-off family, he graduated in medicine in 1678 with [Marcello Malpighi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Malpighi) at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), at the same time he studied mathematics with [Geminiano Montanari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geminiano_Montanari) and became a member of the Academia della Traccia o dei Filosofi.[^o6]

His first mathematical writing topic was astronomy, but later he focused his studies on hydraulics. In 1686 he was named "Bologna General Water Administrator", an important role due to the large number of watercourses existing in the area and the frequent flooding that required surveillance. The experience gave inspiration for his well-known work "Della natura dei fiumi" which is considered a masterpiece of modern river hydraulics.[^o7]

Guglielmini was elected an associated member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1686 and a [fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1687.[^n1] He married Costanza Gioannetti and had three daughters and a son, Giuseppe Ferdinando, who became his biographer. In 1690 he was appointed professor of mathematics at the University of Bologna and in 1694 professor of [Hydrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrometry).

In 1698 he was invited by the prestigious [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) to teach mathematics, astronomy and medicine and to collaborate in the restoration of the fortifications of [Kotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotor) in [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia) (today [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro)).[^o9]

He died in 1709 after eight months of agony for a cerebral haemorrhage. He was buried in the church of St. Massimo in Padua and a sumptuous monument was erected in the [Basilica of Saint Anthony of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Anthony_of_Padua).
