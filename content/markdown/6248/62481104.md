# Casey Mann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Casey Mann"}

*Image: Heesch-5.svg.* A polygon with Heesch number 5, found by Casey Mann[^o7]

**Casey Mann** is an American mathematician, specializing in [discrete](wikipedia://en/386468) and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), in particular [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) and [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008). He is Professor of Mathematics at [University of Washington Bothell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_Bothell), and received the PhD at the [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas) in 2001.

:::

He is known for his 2015 discovery, with [Jennifer McLoud-Mann](wikipedia://en/57639660) and undergraduate student David Von Derau, of the 15th and last class of [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014) to [tile the plane](wikipedia://en/4850275).

Mann is also known for his work on [Heesch's problem](wikipedia://en/1561945), to which he contributed a polygon with *Heesch number* 5. This problem is closely related to the [einstein problem](wikipedia://en/43542926), of whether there exists a shape that can tessellate space, but only in a non-periodic way.

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Mann received his B.S. in mathematics at [East Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Central_University) in [Ada, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada,_Oklahoma), and completed his Ph.D. in 2001 from the [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas). His dissertation in [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), supervised by [Chaim Goodman-Strauss](wikipedia://en/56181508), was *Heesch's Problem and Other Tiling Problems*.

Upon completing his doctorate, Mann joined the [University of Texas at Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Tyler) faculty for eleven years.. He joined the faculty of University of Washington Bothell in 2013, where he is active in engaging undergraduate students in research.
