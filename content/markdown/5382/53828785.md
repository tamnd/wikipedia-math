# Starlike tree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Starlike tree"}

*Image: Starlike graph.svg.* A starlike tree

In the mathematical subdiscipline of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) is said to be **starlike** if it has exactly one vertex of [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) greater than 2. This high-degree vertex is the **root** (or **central vertex**), and a starlike tree can be seen as resulting from attaching to this central vertex at least three [linear graphs](wikipedia://en/4587190) (paths). Starlike trees are also referred to as **spider graphs**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

More formally, let $k \geq 3$ and $n_1, \ldots, n_k \geq 1$ be positive integers. The starlike tree $S(n_1, \ldots, n_k)$ is a tree $T$ with a central vertex $v$ of degree $k$ such that $T \setminus v \cong P_{n_1} \cup \cdots \cup P_{n_k}$, where $P_t$ denotes the [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) on $t$ vertices, and every neighbor of $v$ in $T$ has degree one or two. The total number of vertices in $S(n_1, \ldots, n_k)$ is $n_1 + \cdots + n_k + 1$. The simplest starlike tree is the [star graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_graph) $S_k = S(1, \ldots, 1)$ with $k$ branches of length one.[^o4]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

### Spectral properties {#Spectral_properties}

Two finite starlike trees are [isospectral](wikipedia://en/386031), i.e. their [graph Laplacians](wikipedia://en/1394358) have the same spectra, if and only if they are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577).[^o8] The graph Laplacian has always only one eigenvalue equal or greater than 4.[^o9]

### Spectral radius bounds {#Spectral_radius_bounds}

The [spectral radius](wikipedia://en/386169) of a starlike tree (the largest eigenvalue of its [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463)) can be bounded in terms of its maximum degree $\Delta$. For starlike trees $S(n_1, \ldots, n_k)$ with $k \geq 4$ and $n_1, \ldots, n_k \geq 2$, the spectral radius $\lambda_1$ satisfies:[^o4]

$$\frac{k-1}{\sqrt{k-2}} < \lambda_1(S(n_1, \ldots, n_k)) < \frac{k}{\sqrt{k-1}}$$

or equivalently, in terms of the maximum degree $\Delta = k$:

$$\frac{\Delta - 1}{\sqrt{\Delta - 2}} < \lambda_1 < \frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta - 1}}$$

These bounds show that the spectral radius of such starlike trees is asymptotically $\sqrt{\Delta}$ as the maximum degree grows large.

For specific cases:[^o4]

- If $k = 3$ and all branches have length 1, then $\lambda_1 = \sqrt{3}$
- If $k = 3$ and all branches have length 2, then $\lambda_1 = 2$
- If $k \geq 4$ and all branches have length 1 (i.e., the tree is a star $S_{k+1}$), then $\lambda_1 = \sqrt{k}$

### Eigenvalues in the interval (−2, 2) {#Eigenvalues_in_the_interval_(−2,_2)}

The eigenvalues of starlike trees have been characterized with respect to the interval $(-2, 2)$. A starlike tree $S(n_1, n_2, n_3)$ with three branches has all of its eigenvalues in the open interval $(-2, 2)$ if and only if it is isomorphic to one of the following:

- $S(1, 1, m)$ for any positive integer $m$
- $S(1, 2, 2)$, $S(1, 2, 3)$, or $S(1, 2, 4)$

For starlike trees with four or more branches $(k \geq 4)$, at least one eigenvalue lies outside the interval $(-2, 2)$.[^o4]

### Topological indices {#Topological_indices}

Vertex-degree-based topological indices are molecular descriptors defined as $TI(G) = \sum_{1 \leq i \leq j \leq n-1} m_{ij}(G) \varphi_{ij}$, where $m_{ij}(G)$ is the number of edges between vertices of degree $i$ and degree $j$, and the values $\varphi_{ij}$ determine the specific index. Examples include the [Randić index](wikipedia://en/24257203), [first Zagreb index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_index), [harmonic index](wikipedia://en/81771501), and [atom-bond connectivity index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom-bond_connectivity_index).[^o13]

For a starlike tree $X$ with $n$ vertices and central degree $k$, any such index satisfies $TI(X) = k_1 P_k + k Q_k + (n-1)\varphi_{22}$, where $k_1$ is the number of branches of length 1, $P_k = \varphi_{1k} + \varphi_{22} - \varphi_{12} - \varphi_{2k}$, and $Q_k = \varphi_{12} + \varphi_{2k} - 2\varphi_{22}$. This shows that the index value depends primarily on the number of unit-length branches.[^o13]

Among all starlike trees on $n$ vertices, the extremal values are typically attained by the star graph $S(1, 1, \ldots, 1)$ with $n-1$ branches and the tree $S(2, 2, n-5)$. For indices where $P_k \leq 0$ for all $k \geq 3$ (including the Randić, harmonic, sum-connectivity, geometric-arithmetic, and augmented Zagreb indices), the star graph attains the minimum and $S(2,2,n-5)$ attains the maximum. The reverse holds for indices where $P_k \geq 0$ (including the first Zagreb, Albertson, and atom-bond connectivity indices).[^o13]
