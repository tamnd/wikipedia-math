# Conjugate (square roots)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conjugate"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **conjugate** of an [expression](wikipedia://en/609125) of the form $a + b \sqrt d$ is $a - b \sqrt d,$ provided that $\sqrt d$ does not appear in $a$ and $b$. One says also that the two expressions are conjugate.[^o2]

:::

In particular, the two solutions of a [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175) are conjugate, as per the $\pm$ in the [quadratic formula](wikipedia://en/59217) $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac} }{2a}$.

[Complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation) is the special case where the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) is $i = \sqrt{-1},$ the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

As

$$(a + b \sqrt d)(a - b \sqrt d) = a^2 - b^2 d$$

and

$$(a + b \sqrt d) + (a - b \sqrt d) = 2a,$$

the sum and the product of conjugate expressions do not involve the square root anymore.

This property is used for removing a square root from a [denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominator), by multiplying the [numerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerator) and the denominator of a [fraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_%28mathematics%29) by the conjugate of the denominator (see [Rationalisation](wikipedia://en/16458571)).[^o6] An example of this usage is:

$$\frac{a + b \sqrt d}{x + y\sqrt d} = \frac{(a + b \sqrt d)(x - y \sqrt d)}{(x + y \sqrt d)(x - y \sqrt d)} = \frac{ax - dby + (xb - ay) \sqrt d}{x^2 - y^2 d}.$$

Hence:

$$\frac{1}{a + b \sqrt d} = \frac{a - b \sqrt d}{a^2 - db^2}.$$

A corollary property is that the subtraction:

$$(a+b\sqrt d) - (a-b\sqrt d)= 2b\sqrt d,$$

leaves only a term containing the root.

:::
