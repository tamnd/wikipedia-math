# Fritz Noether

*Image: NoetherFamily HeisigFamily 1930 MFO3130.jpg.* Left to right: Herrmann, **Fritz**, and Regine Noether, Lotte and Gottfried Heisig; c. 1930–1931 in the [Giant Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Mountains).

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Fritz Noether"}

**Fritz Alexander Ernst Noether** (; 7 October 1884 – 11 September 1941) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who emigrated from [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). He was later executed by the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD).[^o4]

:::

He was born to the mathematician [Max Noether](wikipedia://en/2482337) and was the younger brother of mathematician [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896).

## Biography {#Biography}

Fritz Noether's father Max Noether was professor of mathematics at the [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen). Starting in 1904, Fritz studied mathematics at the University of Erlangen and then at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), where he obtained his doctorate in 1909 with a dissertation about rolling movements of a sphere on surfaces of rotation, written under the direction of [Aurel Voss](wikipedia://en/56692263). He obtained his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1911 at the [Technische Hochschule Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_Karlsruhe).

He married in 1911 and had two children:[^o8] Herman D. Noether, born 1912 who became a chemist, and [Gottfried E. Noether](wikipedia://en/11071873), born 1915 who became an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and educator, and later wrote a brief biography of his father.[^o7]

Noether served in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), was wounded, and received the [Iron Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Cross). From 1922 to 1933 he was professor of mathematics at the *Technische Universität Breslau* (now [Wrocław University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Science_and_Technology)).[^o9]

Not allowed to work in [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) for being a [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew), he emigrated in 1934 to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), while his sister Emmy immigrated to the United States.[^o7] Fritz was appointed to a professorship at the [Tomsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk_State_University). His son Gottfried studied mathematics in [Tomsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk).

In November 1937, during the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge), he was arrested at his home in Tomsk by the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD). [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) wrote a letter on his behalf to Soviet foreign minister [Maxim Litvinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Litvinov), without success.[^o10] On 23 October 1938, Noether was sentenced to 25 years of imprisonment on charges of espionage and sabotage. He served time in various prisons.

As was revealed much later,[^o11] on 8 September 1941, less than three months after the [German invasion of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), the [Military Collegium of the USSR Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Collegium_of_the_USSR_Supreme_Court) sentenced Noether to death on the accusation of "[anti-Soviet propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Soviet_agitation)". He was shot in [Oryol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oryol) on 11 September 1941 during the [Medvedev Forest massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvedev_Forest_massacre). His burial place is unknown, but there is a memorial plaque in the [Gengenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gengenbach) Cemetery, Germany, at the site of his wife's grave.

On 22 Dec 1988, the Plenum of the [USSR Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Soviet_Union) ruled that Noether had been convicted on groundless charges and voided his sentence, thus fully [rehabilitating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehabilitation_%28Soviet%29) him.[^o11]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In 1909, Fritz Noether studied the concept of rigid bodies in [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) proposed by [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born). This resulted in the [Herglotz–Noether theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herglotz–Noether_theorem).

In 1921, he introduced the [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) now known as [Fredholm operators](wikipedia://en/279722) and the concept of the index of such an operator, giving an example of an operator whose [kernel](wikipedia://en/1072915) and [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) have different finite dimension and providing a formula for the difference of these dimensions using a complex [contour integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_integral).

In 1923, Fritz Noether presented a critique of [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg)'s dissertation. Heisenberg had analyzed the transition from [laminar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminar_flow) to [turbulent flow](wikipedia://en/154664) in fluids, and Noether claimed that the applied methods were not rigorous.[^o8][^o9]
