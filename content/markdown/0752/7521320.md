# Stable group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stable group"}

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a **stable group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that is stable in the sense of [stability theory](wikipedia://en/15828771). An important class of examples is provided by **groups of finite Morley rank** (see below).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A **group of finite Morley rank** is an abstract [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* such that the formula *x* = *x* has finite [Morley rank](wikipedia://en/1401941) for the model *G*. It follows from the definition that the [theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_%28logic%29) of a group of finite Morley rank is [ω-stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ω-stable); therefore groups of finite Morley rank are stable groups. Groups of finite Morley rank behave in certain ways like [finite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) objects. The striking similarities between groups of finite Morley rank and finite groups are an object of active research.
- All [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) have finite Morley rank, in fact rank 0.
- [Algebraic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_groups) over [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018) have finite Morley rank, equal to their [dimension](wikipedia://en/253251) as [algebraic sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_set).
- (Sela 2006) showed that [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735), and more generally [torsion-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group) [hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817), are stable. Free groups on more than one generator are not [superstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstable).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="The Cherlin–Zilber conjecture"}

## The Cherlin–Zilber conjecture {#The_Cherlin–Zilber_conjecture}

The **Cherlin–Zilber conjecture** (also called the **algebraicity conjecture**), due to Gregory (Cherlin 1979) and Boris (Zil'ber 1977), suggests that infinite (ω-stable) [simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_groups) are simple [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) over [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018). The conjecture would have followed from [Zilber](wikipedia://en/47992829)'s trichotomy conjecture. Cherlin posed the question for all ω-stable simple groups, but remarked that even the case of groups of finite Morley rank seemed hard.

Progress towards this conjecture has followed [Borovik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Borovik)’s program of transferring methods used in classification of [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_groups). One possible source of counterexamples is **bad groups**: [nonsoluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble_group) connected groups of finite Morley rank all of whose proper connected definable subgroups are [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569). (A group is called **connected** if it has no definable subgroups of finite index other than itself.)

A number of special cases of this conjecture have been proved; for example:

- Any connected group of Morley rank 1 is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974).
- Cherlin proved that a connected rank 2 group is solvable.
- Cherlin proved that a simple group of Morley rank 3 is either a bad group or isomorphic to PSL<sub>2</sub>(*K*) for some algebraically closed field *K* that *G* interprets.
- (txt) showed that an infinite group of finite Morley rank is either an algebraic group over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 2, or has finite 2-rank.
