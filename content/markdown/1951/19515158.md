# Higher-dimensional algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Higher-dimensional algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially ([higher](wikipedia://en/5274013)) [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), **higher-dimensional algebra** is the study of [categorified](wikipedia://en/1596969) structures. It has applications in nonabelian [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), and generalizes [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384).

:::

## Higher-dimensional categories {#Higher-dimensional_categories}

A first step towards defining higher dimensional algebras is the concept of [2-category](wikipedia://en/2740708) of [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013), followed by the more 'geometric' concept of double category.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

A higher level concept is thus defined as a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of categories, or super-category, which generalises to higher dimensions the notion of [category](wikipedia://en/53759) – regarded as any structure which is an interpretation of [Lawvere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawvere)'s axioms of the elementary theory of abstract categories (ETAC).[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9] Thus, a supercategory and also a [super-category](wikipedia://en/407039), can be regarded as natural extensions of the concepts of [meta-category](wikipedia://en/2145170),[^o10] [multicategory](wikipedia://en/2145170), and [multi-graph, *k*-partite graph](wikipedia://en/360581), or [colored graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colored_graph) (see a [color figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_figure), and also its definition in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401)).

Supercategories were first introduced in 1970,[^o11] and were subsequently developed for applications in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) (especially [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) and [topological quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/443757)) and [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology) or [mathematical biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology).[^o12]

Other pathways in higher-dimensional algebra involve: [bicategories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategory), homomorphisms of bicategories, [variable categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_category) (also known as indexed or [parametrized categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametrized_category)), [topoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topoi), effective descent, and [enriched](wikipedia://en/142622) and [internal categories](wikipedia://en/31813104).

## Double groupoids {#Double_groupoids}

In **higher-dimensional algebra** (HDA), a *double groupoid* is a generalisation of a one-dimensional [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) to two dimensions,[^o14] and the latter groupoid can be considered as a special case of a category with all invertible arrows, or [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891).

[Double groupoids](wikipedia://en/25981912) are often used to capture information about [geometrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrical) objects such as [higher-dimensional manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-dimensional_space) (or [*n*-dimensional manifolds](wikipedia://en/974084)). In general, an [*n*-dimensional manifold](wikipedia://en/974084) is a space that locally looks like an [*n*-dimensional Euclidean space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-dimensional_Euclidean_space), but whose global structure may be [non-Euclidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Euclidean).

Double groupoids were first introduced by [Ronald Brown](wikipedia://en/27079705) in *Double groupoids and crossed modules* (1976),[^o14] and were further developed towards applications in [nonabelian](wikipedia://en/4791400) [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] A related, 'dual' concept is that of a double [algebroid](wikipedia://en/310921), and the more general concept of [R-algebroid](wikipedia://en/25989751).

## Applications {#Applications}

### Theoretical physics {#Theoretical_physics}

In [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), there exist [quantum categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_category).[^o23][^o24][^o25] and [quantum double groupoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_double_groupoid).[^o25] One can consider quantum double groupoids to be [fundamental groupoids](wikipedia://en/11004) defined via a [2-functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-functor), which allows one to think about the physically interesting case of [quantum fundamental groupoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_fundamental_groupoid) (QFGs) in terms of the [bicategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategory) **Span(Groupoids)**, and then constructing 2-[Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932) and 2-[linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) for manifolds and [cobordisms](wikipedia://en/391832). At the next step, one obtains [cobordisms](wikipedia://en/391832) with corners via [natural transformations](wikipedia://en/45022) of such 2-functors. A claim was then made that, with the [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29), "the extended [TQFT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TQFT), or ETQFT, gives a theory equivalent to the [Ponzano–Regge model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponzano–Regge_model) of [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312)";[^o25] similarly, the [Turaev–Viro model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turaev–Viro_model) would be then obtained with [representations](wikipedia://en/6355948) of SU<sub>*q*</sub>(2). Therefore, one can describe the [state space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_space) of a gauge theory – or many kinds of [quantum field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theories) (QFTs) and local quantum physics, in terms of the [transformation groupoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_groupoid) given by symmetries, as for example in the case of a gauge theory, by the [gauge transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation) acting on states that are, in this case, connections. In the case of symmetries related to [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678), one would obtain structures that are representation categories of [quantum groupoids](wikipedia://en/55131254),[^o23] instead of the 2-[vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) that are representation categories of groupoids.
