# 26-fullerene graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="26-fullerene graph"}

In the mathematical field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **26-fullerene graph** is a [polyhedral graph](wikipedia://en/24656270) with *V* = 26 vertices and *E* = 39 edges. Its planar embedding has three hexagonal faces (including the one shown as the external face of the illustration) and twelve pentagonal faces. As a planar graph with only pentagonal and hexagonal faces, meeting in three faces per vertex, this graph is a [fullerene](wikipedia://en/10628). The existence of this fullerene has been known since at least 1968.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The 26-fullerene graph has $D_{3h}$ [prismatic symmetry](wikipedia://en/2922089), the same group of symmetries as the [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584). This symmetry group has 12 elements; it has six symmetries that arbitrarily permute the three hexagonal faces of the graph and preserve the [orientation](wikipedia://en/187446) of its planar embedding, and another six orientation-reversing symmetries.[^o8]

The number of fullerenes with a given even number of vertices grows quickly in the number of vertices; 26 is the largest number of vertices for which the fullerene structure is unique. The only two smaller fullerenes are the graph of the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) (a fullerene with 20 vertices) and the graph of the [truncated hexagonal trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/3521966) (a 24-vertex fullerene), which are the two types of cells in the [Weaire–Phelan structure](wikipedia://en/2795211).

The 26-fullerene graph has many [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031). One must remove at least five edges from the graph in order to obtain a subgraph that has exactly one perfect matching. This is a unique property of this graph among fullerenes in the sense that, for every other number of vertices of a fullerene, there exists at least one fullerene from which one can remove four edges to obtain a subgraph with a unique perfect matching.[^o7]

*Image: 26fullerene distance labels.svg.* 12-bit distance labels for the 26-fullerene graph, in [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263)

The vertices of the 26-fullerene graph can be labeled with sequences of 12 [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit), in such a way that distance in the graph equals half of the [Hamming distance](wikipedia://en/41227) between these [bitvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitvector). This can also be interpreted as an [isometric embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_embedding) from the graph into a 12-dimensional [taxicab geometry](wikipedia://en/408354). The 26-fullerene graph is one of only five fullerenes with such an embedding.[^o8]

:::

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

In 2009, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* published a puzzle involving [Hamiltonian paths](wikipedia://en/244437) in this graph, taking advantage of the correspondence between its 26 vertices and the 26 letters of the English alphabet.[^o10][^o11]
