# Kleiman's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kleiman's theorem"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Kleiman's theorem**, introduced by (Kleiman 1974), concerns [dimension](wikipedia://en/58058216) and smoothness of [scheme-theoretic intersection](wikipedia://en/52580781) after some perturbation of factors in the intersection.

:::

Precisely, it states:[^o3] given a connected [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) *G* [acting](wikipedia://en/56600253) transitively on an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *X* over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *k* and $V_i \to X, i = 1, 2$ [morphisms of varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_of_varieties), *G* contains a nonempty open subset such that for each *g* in the set,

1. either $gV_1 \times_X V_2$ is empty or has pure dimension $\dim V_1 + \dim V_2 - \dim X$, where $g V_1$ is $V_1 \to X \overset{g}\to X$,
1. (Kleiman–[Bertini theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertini_theorem)) If $V_i$ are smooth varieties and if the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of the base field *k* is zero, then $gV_1 \times_X V_2$ is smooth.

Statement 1 establishes a version of [Chow's moving lemma](wikipedia://en/38307346):[^o4] after some perturbation of cycles on *X*, their intersection has expected dimension.

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Sketch of proof"}

## Sketch of proof {#Sketch_of_proof}

We write $f_i$ for $V_i \to X$. Let $h: G \times V_1 \to X$ be the composition that is $(1_G, f_1): G \times V_1 \to G \times X$ followed by the [group action](wikipedia://en/56600253) $\sigma: G \times X \to X$.

Let $\Gamma = (G \times V_1) \times_X V_2$ be the [fiber product](wikipedia://en/56444110) of $h$ and $f_2: V_2 \to X$; its set of closed points is

$$\Gamma = \{ (g, v, w) | g \in G, v \in V_1, w \in V_2, g \cdot f_1(v) = f_2(w) \}.$$

We want to compute the dimension of $\Gamma$. Let $p: \Gamma \to V_1 \times V_2$ be the projection. It is surjective since $G$ acts transitively on *X*. Each fiber of *p* is a coset of stabilizers on *X* and so

$$\dim \Gamma = \dim V_1 + \dim V_2 + \dim G - \dim X.$$

Consider the [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) $q: \Gamma \to G$; the fiber of *q* over *g* is $g V_1 \times_X V_2$ and has the expected dimension unless empty. This completes the proof of Statement 1.

For Statement 2, since *G* acts transitively on *X* and the smooth locus of *X* is nonempty (by characteristic zero), *X* itself is smooth. Since *G* is smooth, each geometric fiber of *p* is smooth and thus $p_0 : \Gamma_0 := (G \times V_{1, \text{sm}}) \times_X V_{2, \text{sm}} \to V_{1, \text{sm}} \times V_{2, \text{sm}}$ is a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041). It follows that a general fiber of $q_0 : \Gamma_0 \to G$ is smooth by [generic smoothness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_smoothness). $\square$

:::
