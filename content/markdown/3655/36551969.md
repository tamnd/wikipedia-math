# Arithmetic number

*Image: Arithmetic number Cuisenaire rods 6.png.* Demonstration, with [Cuisenaire rods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisenaire_rods), of the arithmetic nature of the number 6

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **arithmetic number** is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) for which the [average](wikipedia://en/612) of its [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) is also an integer. For instance, 6 is an arithmetic number because the average of its divisors is

:::

$$\frac{1+2+3+6}{4}=3,$$

which is also an integer. However, 2 is not an arithmetic number because its only divisors are 1 and 2, and their average 3/2 is not an integer.

The first numbers in the [sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) of arithmetic numbers are

1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 27, 29, 30, 31, 33, 35, 37, 38, 39, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 49, ... .

## Density {#Density}

It is known that the [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) of such numbers is 1:[^o3] indeed, the proportion of numbers less than *X* which are not arithmetic is [asymptotically](wikipedia://en/641995)[^o4]

$$\exp\left( { -c \sqrt{\log\log X} } \,\right)$$

where *c* = 2$\sqrt{\log 2}$ + o(1).

A number *N* is arithmetic if the [number of divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_of_divisors) *d*(*N*) divides the [sum of divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum-of-divisors_function) σ(*N*). It is known that the [density](wikipedia://en/1819983) of integers *N* obeying the stronger condition that *d*(*N*)<sup>2</sup> divides σ(*N*) is 1/2.[^o3][^o4]
