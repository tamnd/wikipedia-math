# Donald W. Loveland

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Donald W. Loveland"}

**Donald W. Loveland** (born December 26, 1934)[^o3] is a professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) who specializes in [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence).[^o4] He is well known for the [Davis–Putnam–Logemann–Loveland algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis–Putnam–Logemann–Loveland_algorithm).[^o5]

:::

Loveland graduated from [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College) in 1956, received a master's degree from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1958 and a Ph.D. from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in 1964. He joined the Duke University Computer Science Department in 1973. He previously served as a faculty member in the Department of Mathematics at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) and [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University).[^o3][^o6][^o7]

He received the [Herbrand Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbrand_Award) for Distinguished Contributions to Automated Reasoning in 2001.[^o7] He is a Fellow of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) (2000),[^o8] a Fellow of the [Association of Artificial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Artificial_Intelligence) (1993),[^o9] and a [Fellow of the American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (2019).[^o10]
