# Michael Harris (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Harris"}

**Michael Howard Harris** (born 1954) is an American mathematician known for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is a professor of mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and professor emeritus of mathematics at [Université Paris Cité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Paris_Cité). In 2025, he was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Harris was born in [Kingsessing, Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsessing,_Philadelphia), Pennsylvania[^o29] and is of Jewish descent.[^o5] He received his B.A. in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1973.[^o6] He received his M.A. and Ph.D. in mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under the supervision of [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) in 1976 and 1977 respectively.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Harris was a faculty member at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) from 1977 to 1994.[^o6] In 1994, he became a professor of mathematics at [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) and the [Institut de mathématiques de Jussieu – Paris Rive Gauche](wikipedia://en/57642956), where he has been emeritus since 2021.[^o6][^o9] He became a professor of mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 2013.[^o6]

He was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1983 to 1984 and in the fall of 2011.[^o10] He has held visiting positions at various institutions, including [Bethlehem University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_University), the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494), the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute).[^o6]

His former doctoral students include [Laurent Fargues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Fargues) and [Gaëtan Chenevier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaëtan_Chenevier).[^o7]

He has organized or co-organized more than 20 conferences, workshops, and special programs in his field of number theory.[^o11]

## Work {#Work}

### Research {#Research}

Harris's research focuses on [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), [automorphic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_forms), [*L*-functions](wikipedia://en/245283), and [motives](wikipedia://en/430680).[^o28] He has developed the theory of [coherent cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_cohomology) of [Shimura varieties](wikipedia://en/10389861) and applied it to number theoretic problems on [special values of *L*-functions](wikipedia://en/25305882), [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_representations), and the [theta correspondence](wikipedia://en/2995611).[^o29] His later work focuses on geometric aspects of the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).[^o29]

In 2001, Harris and [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) proved the [local Langlands conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Langlands_conjecture) for [GL(*n*)](wikipedia://en/113564) over a *p*-adic [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) [^o14] The [Sato–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/597837) and its generalization to all [totally real fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) was proved by [Laurent Clozel](wikipedia://en/51408473), Harris, [Nicholas Shepherd-Barron](wikipedia://en/7681535), and [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) under mild assumptions in 2008,[^o15][^o16][^o17] and completed by [Thomas Barnet-Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Barnet-Lamb), [David Geraghty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Geraghty_%28mathematician%29), Harris, and Taylor in 2011.[^o18]

### *Mathematics without Apologies* {#Mathematics_without_Apologies}

Harris wrote the book *Mathematics without Apologies: Portrait of a Problematic Vocation*, published in 2015.[^o20][^o21][^o22][^o23]

### *Silicon Reckoner* {#Silicon_Reckoner}

Since 2021, Harris has written the newsletter *Silicon Reckoner* exploring questions and issues related to the mechanization of mathematics and [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence).[^o25]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Harris received the [Sophie Germain Prize](wikipedia://en/48485384) (2006),[^o6] the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) (joint with Richard Taylor, 2007),[^o29][^o27] the Grand Prix Scientifique de la [Fondation Simone et Cino del Duca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_and_Cino_Del_Duca_Foundation) (2009),[^o6] He is a two-time invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (2002, 2014).[^o6]

Harris was a [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) (1983–1985) and a member of the [Institut Universitaire de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Universitaire_de_France) (2001–2011)[^o6][^o29] He has been elected a [Member of the Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Academia_Europaea) (2016),[^o6] [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society) (2019),[^o6][^o28] Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (2019),[^o6] and Member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (2022).[^o29][^o6]
