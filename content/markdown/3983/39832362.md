# Adam Marcus (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adam Marcus"}

**Adam Wade Marcus** (born 1979) is an American mathematician. He held the Chair of Combinatorial Analysis in the Institute of Mathematics at the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) until February 2023.[^o8] The team of Marcus, [Daniel Spielman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Spielman) and [Nikhil Srivastava](wikipedia://en/46709675) was awarded the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya_Prize) in 2014 for their resolution of the [Kadison–Singer problem](wikipedia://en/48769660) and later the [Michael and Sheila Held Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_and_Sheila_Held_Prize) in 2021 for their solution to long-standing conjectures in the study of [Ramanujan graphs](wikipedia://en/849412).

:::

## History {#History}

Marcus grew up in [Marietta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta,_Georgia) and was a boarding student at the [Darlington School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington_School) in [Rome, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome,_Georgia). He attended the [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis) for his undergraduate degree, where he was a Compton Fellow.[^o10] He then completed his doctoral studies under the supervision of [Prasad Tetali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prasad_Tetali) at the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology). Following his graduation in 2008, he spent four years as a Gibbs Assistant Professor in Applied Mathematics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). In 2012, Marcus cofounded Crisply, an analytics company in Boston, Massachusetts, where he served as chief scientist until 2015. After leaving Crisply, Marcus spent five years as an assistant professor in the mathematics department and program in applied and computational mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) before moving to [EPFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPFL) in 2020. He is an alumnus of the [Hampshire College Summer Studies in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/242549).[^o11]

## Awards {#Awards}

During 2003–2004, Marcus was a [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) scholar in Hungary.[^o13] In 2008, he was awarded the inaugural [Dénes König Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dénes_König_Prize) in Discrete Mathematics from the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) for his work in solving the [Stanley–Wilf conjecture](wikipedia://en/7892663).[^o14] A team consisting of Marcus, [Daniel Spielman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Spielman), and [Nikhil Srivastava](wikipedia://en/46709675) was awarded the 2014 [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya_Prize) for their resolution of the [Kadison–Singer problem](wikipedia://en/48769660).[^o15] He was an invited speaker at the 2014 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Seoul, South Korea.[^o16] The team of Marcus, [Spielman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Spielman), and [Srivastava](wikipedia://en/46709675) was also awarded the 2021 [Michael and Sheila Held Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_and_Sheila_Held_Prize) for their work in resolving the [Kadison–Singer problem](wikipedia://en/48769660) and their solution to long-standing conjectures in the study of Ramanujan graphs.[^o17]
