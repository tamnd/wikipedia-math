# Kenneth Falconer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kenneth Falconer"}

**Kenneth John Falconer** CBE (born 25 January 1952) is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and in particular on [fractal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_geometry).[^o3] He is [Regius Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor_of_Mathematics) in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews).[^o4]

:::

## Research {#Research}

Falconer is known for his work on the mathematics of [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals) and in particular sets and measures arising from [iterated function systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterated_function_systems), especially [self-similar](wikipedia://en/28782) and self-affine sets. Closely related is his research on [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/14294) and other [fractal dimensions](wikipedia://en/285907). He formulated *[Falconer's conjecture](wikipedia://en/38368203)* on the dimension of distance sets and conceived the notion of a [digital sundial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_sundial).[^o3] In combinatorial geometry he established a lower bound of 5 for the [chromatic number of the plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadwiger-Nelson_problem) in the Lebesgue measurable case.

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Falconer was educated at [Kingston Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_Grammar_School), [Kingston upon Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_upon_Thames) and [Corpus Christi College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Cambridge). He graduated in 1974 and completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1979 under the supervision of [Hallard Croft](wikipedia://en/81629381).

He was a research fellow at [Corpus Christi College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Cambridge) from 1977 to 1980 before moving to [Bristol University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_University). He was appointed Professor of Pure Mathematics at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews) in 1993 and was head of the School of Mathematics and Statistics from 2001 to 2004. He served on the council of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) from 2000 to 2009 including as publications secretary from 2006 to 2009.[^o3]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Falconer was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1998.

In 2020, he was awarded the [Shephard Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shephard_Prize) of the London Mathematical Society.[^o9]

Falconer was appointed [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (CBE) in the [2024 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_New_Year_Honours) for services to mathematics.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Falconer was born 25 January 1952 at Bearsted Memorial Maternity Hospital outside [Hampton Court Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Court_Palace).[^o3]

His recreational interests include long-distance walking and hill walking. He was chair of the [Long Distance Walkers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Distance_Walkers_Association) from 2000 to 2003 and editor of their journal *Strider* from 1987 to 1992 and 2007–12.[^o12] In 2021, he was appointed a Vice President of the LDWA.[^o3][^o13] He has twice climbed all the [Munros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munro) as well as all the [Corbetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_mountains_and_hills_in_the_British_Isles).[^o14]
