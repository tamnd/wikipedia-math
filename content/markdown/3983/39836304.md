# Bitmessage

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bitmessage"}

**Bitmessage** is a [decentralized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralization), [encrypted](wikipedia://en/10294), [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer), trustless [communications protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_protocol) that can be used by one person to send encrypted messages to another person, or to multiple subscribers.

:::

Bitmessage was conceived by software developer Jonathan Warren, who based its design on the decentralized [digital currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_currency), [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265). The software was released in November 2012 under the [MIT license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_License).[^o3]

Bitmessage gained a reputation for being out of reach of warrantless [wiretapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiretapping) conducted by the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) (NSA), due to the decentralized nature of the protocol, and its encryption being difficult to crack. This prevents the accidental eavesdropping.[^o4] As a result, downloads of the Bitmessage program increased fivefold during June 2013, after news broke of classified email surveillance activities conducted by the NSA.[^o3]

It achieves anonymity and privacy by relying on the [blockchain](wikipedia://en/44065971) flooding propagation mechanism and asymmetric encryption algorithm.[^o4]

Bitmessage has also been mentioned as an experimental alternative to email by *[Popular Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Science)*[^o5] and [CNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNET).[^o6]

Some [ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware) programs instruct affected users to use Bitmessage to communicate with the attackers.[^o7]

PyBitmessage version 0.6.2 (March 1, 2017) had a [remote code execution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrary_code_execution) vulnerability. It was fixed in version 0.6.3 (February 13, 2018).[^o8][^o9]
