# Brian D. Ripley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brian D. Ripley"}

**Brian David Ripley** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (born 29 April 1952) is a British [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). From 1990, he was professor of [applied statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_statistics) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and also a [professorial fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorial_fellow) at [St Peter's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_College,_Oxford). He retired August 2014 due to ill health.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ripley has made contributions to the fields of [spatial statistics](wikipedia://en/9467349) and [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706). His work on [artificial neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network) in the 1990s helped to bring aspects of [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) and [data mining](wikipedia://en/42253) to the attention of statistical audiences.[^o5] He emphasised the value of [robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691) in his books *Pattern Recognition and Neural Networks* and *Modern Applied Statistics with S*.

Ripley helped develop the [S-PLUS](wikipedia://en/3830007) programming language[^o6][^o7] and its [open source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_source) derivative [R](wikipedia://en/376707).[^o8] He co-authored two books based on S, *S Programming* and *Modern Applied Statistics with S*.[^o6][^o7] Since mid-1997 he is a member of the "R Core Team"[^o9] and from 2000 to 2021 he was one of the most active committers to the R core.[^o10] The [package](wikipedia://en/65740838) MASS[^o11] is one of only fifteen "recommended packages"[^o12] for R (with June 2024 more than 20,900[^o13]).

He was educated at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he was awarded both the [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) (at the time awarded to the best graduate essay writer who had been undergraduate at Cambridge in that cohort) and the [Rollo Davidson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize). The university also awarded him the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110) in 1987 for an essay entitled *Statistical Inference for Spatial Processes*, later published as a book.[^o14] He served on the faculty of [Imperial College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College,_London) from 1976 until 1983, at which point he moved to the [University of Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strathclyde).[^o15]

In 2026, he was awarded the [Rousseeuw Prize for Statistics](wikipedia://en/72172766) alongside four other contributors to R. He told the BBC he would spend "some, maybe all" of his \$200k share of the prize on improving R.[^o16]
