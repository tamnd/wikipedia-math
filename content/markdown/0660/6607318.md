# Andrei Okounkov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Andrei Okounkov"}

**Andrei Yuryevich Okounkov** (Андре́й Ю́рьевич Окунько́в, *Andrej Okun'kov*, born July 26, 1969) is a Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works on [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and its applications to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function). He is currently a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and the academic supervisor of [HSE International Laboratory of Representation Theory and Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSE_Faculty_of_Mathematics).[^o3] In 2006, he received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) "for his contributions to bridging probability, representation theory and algebraic geometry."[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He graduated with a B.S. in mathematics, summa cum laude, from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1993 and received his doctorate, also at Moscow State, in 1995 under [Alexandre Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515) and Grigori Olshanski.[^o6] He is a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). He previously was a professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he was awarded a Packard Fellowship (2001), the European Mathematical Society Prize (2004), and the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) (2006); an assistant and associate professor at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he was awarded a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship); and an instructor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). He was going to rejoin the faculty at Berkeley in the summer of 2022, but decided to stay at Columbia, teaching a graduate class in the fall of 2023.

## Work {#Work}

He has worked on the representation theory of [infinite symmetric groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_symmetric_group), the statistics of [plane partitions](wikipedia://en/2933308), and the [quantum cohomology](wikipedia://en/7781359) of the [Hilbert scheme](wikipedia://en/6612581) of points in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). Much of his work on Hilbert schemes was joint with [Rahul Pandharipande](wikipedia://en/8950386).

Okounkov, along with Pandharipande, [Nikita Nekrasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Nekrasov), and Davesh Maulik, has formulated well-known conjectures relating the [Gromov–Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/2301291) and [Donaldson–Thomas invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson–Thomas_invariant) of [threefolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties).

In 2006, at the 25th [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain), he received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) "for his contributions to bridging probability, representation theory and algebraic geometry."[^o4] In 2016, he became a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o8] In 2026 he was awarded the [Nemmers Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1252473).[^o9]
