# Maximilian Herzberger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Maximilian Herzberger"}

**Maximilian Jacob Herzberger** (7 or 17 Mar 1899, [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), Germany — 9 Apr 1982, [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans), United States)[^o3][^o7] was a German-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), known for his development of the [superachromat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superachromat) lens.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Maximilian Herzberger was the son of Leopold Herzberger (born 7 Mar 1870, [Krefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krefeld) — died in [Rochester (NY)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_%28NY%29)) and Sonja/Sofia Behrendt/Berendt/Berends (22 Mar 1876, Petersburg (Germany) — 28 Jan 1945, [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence)); he had a sister Olga (24 Sep 1897, Berlin — 2 Aug 1922, Berlin). The family was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish).[^o5] He studied mathematics and physics at the [Berlin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_University), where [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) was one of his professors, and later became a friend and advisor.[^o14] In 1923, Herzberger finished his Ph.D. thesis *Ueber Systeme hyperkomplexer Grössen* under [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) and [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522) at the philosophical faculty.[^o6] In 1925, he married Edith Kaufmann (10 Oct 1901, [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) — 16 Feb 2001, [Carlsbad (California)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsbad_%28California%29) or [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans)); they had three children, born in Jena, viz. Ruth (born 1928), [Ursula Bellugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Bellugi) (1931), and Hans (6 Aug 1932, spouse of [Radhika Herzberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radhika_Herzberger)).[^o3][^o7] No later than Sep 1930, he was assistant of [Hans Boegehold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Boegehold),<sup>(de)</sup> the chief of calculation office at [Carl Zeiss Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Zeiss_AG).[^o9]

In 1934, the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis) deprived him from his professorship at [Jena University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena_University) and his contract with Zeiss. Leaving Germany with a "total of \$10 in my pockets".,[^o14] he went initially to the Netherlands, where he was hosted by the Dutch physicist [A.C.S. van Heel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bram_van_Heel).[^o8] After the [Nazi occupation of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_occupation_of_the_Netherlands), he emigrated with his family to [Rochester (NY)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_%28NY%29),[^o9] where he became head of [Eastman Kodak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastman_Kodak)'s optical research laboratories, arranged by Einstein.[^o14] In 1940, he and his family became U.S. citizens.[^o14] In 1945, he got the [Cressy Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cressy_Morrison) Award of the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o14]

In 1954 he finished the development of the [superachromat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superachromat) as the ultimately well-corrected lens for Kodak.[^o14][^o10] in 1959, he was part of the inaugural class of Fellows of the Optical Society of America.[^o11] In 1962, he was awarded the [Frederic Ives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Ives) Medal of the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Society_of_America).[^o12][^o13] In 1965, he retired from his position at Kodak, and helped building a graduate institute for optics in Switzerland,[^o14] until in 1968 he followed invitation of the [University of New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Orleans) to teach at their Physics Department.[^o14][^o9]

He held patents for an *"apochromatic telescope objective having three air spaced components"*,[^o15] and a *"superachromatic objective"*.[^o16] He died on 9 April 1982.[^o17]
