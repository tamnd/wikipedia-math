# William Feller

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Feller"}

**William "Vilim" Feller** (July 7, 1906 – January 14, 1970), born **Vilibald Srećko Feller**, was a Croatian–American mathematician specializing in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Feller was born in [Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb) to Ida Oemichen-Perc, a Croatian–Austrian Catholic, and [Eugen Viktor Feller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Viktor_Feller), son of a Polish–Jewish father (David Feller) and an Austrian mother (Elsa Holzer).[^o4]

Eugen Feller was a famous chemist and created *Elsa fluid* named after his mother. According to [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891), Eugen Feller's surname was a "Slavic tongue twister", which William changed at the age of twenty.[^o5] This claim appears to be false. His forename, Vilibald, was chosen by his Catholic mother for the saint day of his birthday.

## Career and later life {#Career_and_later_life}

Feller held a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) position at the University of [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel) beginning in 1928. Because he refused to sign a Nazi oath,[^o8] he fled the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) and went to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen), Denmark in 1933. He also lectured in Sweden ([Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) and [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund)).[^o9] As a refugee in Sweden, Feller reported being troubled by increasing fascism at the universities. He reported that the mathematician [Torsten Carleman](wikipedia://en/6063370) would offer his opinion that Jews and foreigners should be executed.[^o10]

After marrying a former student from Kiel, Clara Mary Nielsen, in 1938, he moved with her to the US in 1939. In that year he joined [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) as an associate professor; he became a US citizen in 1944. He moved to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1945 and to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1950. At Princeton, he became Eugene Higgins Professor of Mathematics. He remained there until his death on January 14, 1970.[^o24]

## Work {#Work}

The works of Feller are contained in 104 papers and two books on a variety of topics such as [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), theory of [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) in addition to his work in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) and probability.

Feller was one of the greatest [probabilists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_probabilists) of the twentieth century. He is remembered for his championing of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) as a branch of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) in Sweden and the United States. In the middle of the 20th century, probability theory was popular in France and Russia, while [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) was more popular in the United Kingdom and the United States, according to the Swedish statistician, [Harald Cramér](wikipedia://en/223129).[^o13] His two-volume textbook on probability theory and its applications was called "the most successful treatise on probability ever written" by [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891).[^o14] By stimulating his colleagues and students in Sweden and then in the United States, Feller helped establish research groups studying the analytic theory of probability. In his research, Feller contributed to the study of the relationship between [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876) and [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), where his theory of [generators](wikipedia://en/1644938#Infinitesimal_generator) of [one-parameter semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-parameter_semigroup) of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) gave rise to the theory of "Feller operators".

Numerous topics relating to probability are named after him, including [Feller processes](wikipedia://en/6891551), [Feller's explosion test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feller's_explosion_test), [Feller–Brown movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feller–Brown_movement), and the [Lindeberg–Feller theorem](wikipedia://en/39406). Feller made fundamental contributions to [renewal theory](wikipedia://en/1945387), [Tauberian theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauberian_theorems), [random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks), [diffusion processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_processes), and the [law of the iterated logarithm](wikipedia://en/496558). Feller was among those early editors who launched the journal *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)*.

- *An Introduction to Probability Theory and its Applications, Volume II, 2nd edition* (1971)

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1949, Feller was named a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o19] He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1958, the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1960, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1966.[^o20][^o21][^o22] Feller won the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) in 1969.[^o23] He was president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o24]
