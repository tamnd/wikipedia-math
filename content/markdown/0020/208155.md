# 12 (number)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="12"}

**12** (**twelve**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [11](wikipedia://en/208156) and preceding [13](wikipedia://en/37231).

:::

Twelve is the 3rd [superior highly composite number](wikipedia://en/1759451), the 3rd [colossally abundant number](wikipedia://en/1759217), the 5th [highly composite number](wikipedia://en/208732), and is divisible by the numbers from [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29) to [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_%28number%29), and [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29), a comparatively large number of divisors.

It is central to many systems of [timekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time), including the [Western calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar) and units of time of day, and frequently appears in the world's [major religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups).

## Name {#Name}

Twelve is the largest number with a [single-syllable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monosyllable) name in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language). Early [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) numbers have been theorized to have been non-[decimal](wikipedia://en/8214): evidence includes the unusual phrasing of [eleven](wikipedia://en/208156) and twelve, the [former use of "hundred"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_hundred) to refer to groups of [120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/120_%28number%29), and the presence of glosses such as "tentywise" or "ten-count" in medieval texts showing that writers could not presume their readers would normally understand them that way.[^o4][^o5][^o6] Such uses gradually disappeared with the introduction of [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786) during the [12th-century Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th-century_Renaissance).

Derived from [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English), *twelf* and *tuelf* are first attested in the 10th-century [Lindisfarne Gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindisfarne_Gospels)' [Book of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_John).Specially, a passage referring to [Judas Iscariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_Iscariot) as "one of the [twelve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Apostles)" (*an of ðæm tuelfum*).[^o7][^o9] It has cognates in every [Germanic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_language) (e.g. German zwölf), whose [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language) ancestor has been [reconstructed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_reconstruction) as *twaliƀi...*, from *twa* ("[two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29)") and suffix *\-lif-* or *\-liƀ-* of uncertain meaning.[^o9] It is sometimes compared with the [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language) *dvýlika*, although *\-lika* is used as the suffix for all numbers from 11 to 19 (analogous to "-teen").[^o9] Every other [Indo-European language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language) instead uses a form of "two"+"[ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29)", such as the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *duōdecim*.[^o9] The usual [ordinal form](wikipedia://en/26547932) is "twelfth" but "dozenth" or "duodecimal" (from the Latin word) is also used in some contexts, particularly [base-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base-12) numeration. Similarly, a group of twelve things is usually a "[dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dozen)" but may also be referred to as a "dodecad" or "duodecad". The adjective referring to a group of twelve is "duodecuple".

As with eleven,[^o8] the earliest forms of twelve are often considered to be connected with Proto-Germanic *liƀan* or *liƀan* ("to leave"), with the implicit meaning that "two is left" after having already counted to ten.[^o9] The Lithuanian suffix is also considered to share a similar development.[^o9] The suffix *\-lif-* has also been connected with reconstructions of the Proto-Germanic for ten.[^o8][^o10]

As mentioned above, 12 has its own name in Germanic languages such as English ([dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dozen)), Dutch (dozijn), German (Dutzend), and Swedish (dussin), all derived from Old French *dozaine*. It is a compound number in many other languages, e.g. Italian *dodici* (but in Spanish and Portuguese, 16, and in French, 17 is the first compound number), Japanese 十二 *jūni*.

### Written representation {#Written_representation}

In prose writing, *twelve*, being the last single-syllable numeral, is sometimes taken as the last number to be written as a word, and [13](wikipedia://en/37231) the first to be written using digits. This is not a binding rule, and in English language tradition, it is sometimes recommended to spell out numbers up to and including either *nine*, *ten* or *twelve*, or even *ninety-nine* or *one hundred*. Another system spells out all numbers written in one or two words (*sixteen*, *twenty-seven*, *fifteen thousand*, but *372* or *15,001*).[^o12] In [German orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_orthography), there used to be the widely followed (but unofficial) rule of spelling out numbers up to twelve (*zwölf*). The [Duden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duden) (the German standard dictionary) mentions this rule as outdated.

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

12 is the smallest [abundant number](wikipedia://en/321831) because the sum of the [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisors) of 12, 16, is greater than 12. It is also the 3rd [colossally abundant number](wikipedia://en/1759217).[^o16] 12 is a [semiperfect number](wikipedia://en/321918) because some of its proper divisors; 1, 2, 3, and 6; sum to 12.

12 is a [highly composite number](wikipedia://en/208732) because it has 6 divisors, more than any number less than 12. It is the 3rd [superior highly composite number](wikipedia://en/1759451).[^o19] 12 is a [refactorable number](wikipedia://en/5482655) because it has 6 divisors and 12 is a multiple of 6. It is the smallest of two known [sublime numbers](wikipedia://en/6679463), numbers that have a [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670) of divisors whose sum is also perfect.[^o21]

12 is a [Pell number](wikipedia://en/1355482) because 17/12⁠⁠ is the closest approximation of the square root of 2 of any fraction with a denominator less than or equal to 12.

There are twelve [Jacobian elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_elliptic_function) and twelve cubic [distance-transitive graphs](wikipedia://en/7768222).[^o23][^o24]

:::

### Shapes {#Shapes}

A twelve-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) is a [dodecagon](wikipedia://en/508486). In its regular form, it is the largest polygon that can [uniformly](wikipedia://en/1548669#Archimedean,_uniform_or_semiregular_tilings) tile the plane alongside other regular polygons, as with the [truncated hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864030) or the [truncated trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864170).[^o26]

A regular [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) has twelve [pentagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon) faces. Regular [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285) and [octahedrons](wikipedia://en/80177635) both have 12 edges, while regular [icosahedrons](wikipedia://en/43856084) have 12 vertices.

The cubic close packing and hexagonal close packing, which are the two densest possible [sphere packings](wikipedia://en/368621) in three-dimensional space (the [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311), proved by [Thomas Hales](wikipedia://en/2388356)), both have each sphere touching twelve other spheres. Twelve is also the [kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555) in three dimensions.

There are twelve [complex apeirotopes](wikipedia://en/8581934#Regular_complex_5-apeirotopes_and_higher) in dimensions five and higher, which include [van Oss polytopes](wikipedia://en/8581934#van_Oss_polygon) in the form of complex $n$-[orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex).[^o27] There are also twelve [paracompact](wikipedia://en/6821728#Regular_and_uniform_hyperbolic_honeycombs) hyperbolic [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) of [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) in five-dimensional space.

[Bring's curve](wikipedia://en/35477794) is a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) four, with a domain that is a regular hyperbolic 20-sided [icosagon](wikipedia://en/445032).[^o28] By the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Bonnet_theorem), the area of this [fundamental polygon](wikipedia://en/1668732) is equal to $12\pi$.

### Functions {#Functions}

Twelve is the smallest weight for which a [cusp form](wikipedia://en/610165) exists. This cusp form is the discriminant $\Delta(q)$ whose Fourier coefficients are given by the [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan) $\tau$-function and which is (up to a constant multiplier) the 24th power of the [Dedekind eta function](wikipedia://en/447020):

$$\Delta(\tau)=(2\pi)^{12}\eta^{24}(\tau)$$

This fact is related to a constellation of interesting appearances of the number twelve in mathematics ranging from the fact that the [abelianization](wikipedia://en/8847#Abelianization) of [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) $\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathrm {Z})$ has twelve elements, to the value of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) at $-1$ being $-\tfrac {1}{12}$, which stems from the [Ramanujan summation](wikipedia://en/9052595)

$$1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + \cdots=-\frac{1}{12} \quad (\mathfrak{R})$$

Although the series is [divergent](wikipedia://en/876428), methods such as Ramanujan summation can assign finite values to divergent series.

#### In other bases {#In_other_bases}

The [duodecimal](wikipedia://en/8400) system (12<sub>10</sub> \[twelve\] = 10<sub>12</sub>), which is the use of 12 as a division factor for many [ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_weights_and_measures) and [medieval weights and measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_weights_and_measures), including [hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hour), probably originates from [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia).

## Religion {#Religion}

The number twelve carries religious, mythological and magical [symbolism](wikipedia://en/37673); since antiquity, the number has generally represented perfection, entirety, or cosmic order.[^n1]

### Judaism and Christianity {#Judaism_and_Christianity}

section

The number 12 is notable within the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible), and in Christianity:

[Ishmael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishmael)—the first-born son of [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham)—has 12 sons/princes ([Genesis 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chayei_Sarah):16), and [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob) also has 12 sons, who are the progenitors of the [Twelve Tribes of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Tribes_of_Israel).[^o38] This is reflected in Christian tradition, notably in the [twelve Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Apostles). When [Judas Iscariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_Iscariot) is disgraced, a meeting is held ([Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles)) to add [Saint Matthias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Matthias) to complete the number twelve once more.

The Old Testament contains [Twelve Minor Prophets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Minor_Prophets).

The [Book of Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation) contains much numerical symbolism, and many of the numbers mentioned have 12 as a divisor. mentions a woman—interpreted as the [people of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites), the [Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church) and the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary)—wearing a crown of twelve stars (representing each of the twelve tribes of Israel). Furthermore, there are 12,000 people sealed from each of the twelve tribes of Israel (the [Tribe of Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Dan) is omitted while [Manasseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Joseph) is mentioned), making a total of [144,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/144000_%28number%29) (which is the square of 12 multiplied by a thousand).

- According to the New Testament, Jesus had [twelve Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Apostles).
- The "[Twelve Days of Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Night_%28holiday%29)" count the interval between [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas) and [Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_%28feast%29).
- Eastern Orthodoxy observes twelve [Great Feasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Feasts).

### Astrology {#Astrology}

## Timekeeping {#Timekeeping}

- The [lunar year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_year) is 12 [lunar months](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_months). Adding 11 or 12 days completes the [solar year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_year).[^o47]
- Most [calendar systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar) – solar or lunar – have twelve months in a [year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year).
- The Chinese use a 12-year cycle for time-reckoning called [Earthly Branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthly_Branches).
- There are twelve [hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hour) in a half day, numbered one to twelve for both the *ante meridiem* (a.m.) and the *post meridiem* (p.m.). 12:00 p.m. is midday or [noon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noon), and 12:00 a.m. is [midnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight).
- The basic units of time (60 seconds, 60 minutes, 24 hours) are evenly divisible by twelve into smaller units.

### Music {#Music}

#### Music theory {#Music_theory}

- Twelve is the number of [pitch classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_class) in an [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave), not counting the duplicated (octave) pitch. Also, the total number of [major keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_key), (not counting [enharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enharmonic) equivalents) and the total number of [minor keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_key) (also not counting equivalents). This applies only to twelve tone [equal temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_temperament), the most common tuning used today in western influenced music.
- The twelfth is the [interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_%28music%29) of an octave and a fifth. Instruments such as the [clarinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarinet) which behave as a stopped cylindrical pipe [overblow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overblowing) at the twelfth.
- The [twelve-tone technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-tone_technique) (also dodecaphony) is a method of [musical composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_composition) devised by [Arnold Schoenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schoenberg). Music using the technique is called twelve-tone music.
- The [twelve-bar blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-bar_blues) is one of the most prominent chord progressions in popular music.

### Art theory {#Art_theory}

- There are twelve basic [hues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hue) in the [color wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_wheel): three primary colors (red, yellow, blue), three secondary colors (orange, green, purple) and six tertiary colors (names for these vary, but are intermediates between the primaries and secondaries).

- There are 12 troy ounces in a [troy pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_weight) (used for precious metals).
- Twelve of something is called a [dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dozen).
- In the [former British currency system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/£sd), there were twelve [pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_pence) in a [shilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilling).[^o57]
- In English, twelve is the number of greatest magnitude that has just one [syllable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syllable). *Image: Animated analog SVG clock.svg.* The numerical range on the analog clock ends at 12.
- 12 is the last number featured on the analogue clock, and also the starting point of the transition from A.M. to P.M. hours or vice versa.
- There are twelve months within a year, with the last one being [December](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December).
- 12 [inches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inches) are in a [foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_%28length%29).
- 12 is [slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slang) for [Police officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_officers) because of the 10-12 [Police radio code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_code).
