## Detailed list {#Detailed_list}

To help compare different orders of magnitude, the following list describes various lengths between $1.6 \times 10^{-35}$ metres and $10^{10^{10^{122}}}$metres. Metres are used in these tables to provide a common reference point, but metric prefixes above "k" are not commonly used with metres. So for example, 1.21 Gm would more commonly be written as 1.21 million km or (in [scientific notation](wikipedia://en/59715)) 1.21 × 10<sup>6</sup> km. Interplanetary distances are also commonly measured in [astronomical units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit). Distances on the interstellar or larger scale are typically measured in [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year) or [parsecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsec).

The *[decimetre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimetre)* ([SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) symbol: *dm*) is a [unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement) of [length](wikipedia://en/18542) in the [metric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system) equal to 10<sup>−1</sup> [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) ($\tfrac{1}{10}$ m = 0.1 m). To help compare different [orders of magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_magnitude), this section lists lengths between 10 [centimetres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centimetre) and 100 centimetres (10<sup>−1</sup> metre and 1 metre).

### Conversions {#Conversions}

10 centimetres (abbreviated to 10 cm) is equal to:

- 1 [decimetre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimetre) (dm), a term not in common use (1 [L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litre) = 1 dm<sup>3</sup>.)
- 100 [millimetres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millimetre)
- \3.9 [inches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch)
- a side of a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) of area [\0.01 m<sup>2</sup>](wikipedia://en/203673)
- the edge of a [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_%28geometry%29) with a volume of [1 × 10<sup>\-3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_magnitude_%28one_cubic_millimetre_to_one_cubic_metre%29) (1 L)

A length of *100 kilometres* (about 62 miles), as a rough amount, is relatively common in measurements on Earth and for some astronomical objects. It is the altitude at which the [FAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fédération_Aéronautique_Internationale) defines [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) to begin.

To help compare [orders of magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_magnitude), this section lists [lengths](wikipedia://en/18542) between 100 and 1,000 [kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometre) (10<sup>5</sup> and 10<sup>6</sup> [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre)).

## 1 megametre {#1_megametre}

*Image: 1e6m comparison Mars Mercury Moon Pluto Haumea - no transparency.png.* Small planets, the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) and [dwarf planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_planet) in the Solar System have diameters from one to ten million metres. Top row: [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars) (left), [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_%28planet%29) (right); bottom row: [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) (left), [Pluto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto) (center), and [Haumea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haumea_%28dwarf_planet%29) (right), to scale.

The *megametre* ([SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) symbol: *Mm*) is a [unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement) of [length](wikipedia://en/18542) in the [metric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system) equal to 1 [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) (10<sup>6</sup> m). To help compare different [orders of magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_magnitude), this section lists [lengths](wikipedia://en/18542) starting at 10<sup>6</sup> [m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) (1 Mm or 1,000 [km](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometre)).

### Conversions {#Conversions}

1 megametre is equal to:

- 1000 km
- × 10<sup>6</sup> m (one million metres)
- approximately 621.37 [miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile)

The *ronnametre* ([SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) symbol: *Rm*) is a [unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement) of [length](wikipedia://en/18542) in the [metric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system) equal to 10<sup>27</sup> [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre).[^o349]

To help compare different [orders of magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_magnitude), this section lists [distances](wikipedia://en/39378) starting at 1 Rm (10<sup>27</sup> [m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) or 105.7 billion [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year)). At this scale, expansion of the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe) becomes significant. Distance of these objects are derived from their measured [redshifts](wikipedia://en/26262), which depend on the [cosmological](wikipedia://en/5378) models used.

- \>1 Rm – >105.7 billion light-years – size of universe beyond the [cosmic light horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable_universe), depending on its curvature; if the curvature is zero (i.e. the universe is spatially flat), the value can be [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824#Cosmology) (see [Shape of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe)) as previously mentioned.
- \2.764 Rm - 292.2 billion light-years – circumference of the observable universe, as it is in the shape of a sphere.
- \221.617 Rm - 23.425 trillion light-years - circumference of the observable universe, as it is in the shape of a [hypersphere](wikipedia://en/39792).

## 1 quettametre {#1_quettametre}

The *quettametre* ([SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) symbol: *Qm*) is a [unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement) of [length](wikipedia://en/18542) in the [metric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system) equal to 10<sup>30</sup> [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre).[^o349]

## Upper limits {#Upper_limits}

- ≈10<sup>10<sup>10<sup>122</sup></sup></sup>[light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year) – the possible size of the universe after [cosmological inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_inflation).[^o359]
- ≈∞ light-years — theoretical size of the [multiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse) if it exists.
