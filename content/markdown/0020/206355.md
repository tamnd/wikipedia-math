# John Veitch (poet)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Veitch"}

*Image: John Veitch2.jpg.*

*Image: John Veitch.jpg.* John Veitch

**John Veitch** (24 October 1829 – 3 September 1894), [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet) and [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian): John was born in [Peebles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peebles); the only son of [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War) veteran James Veitch and his wife, Nancy Ritchie; a woman, steeped in the folk traditions of the Borders; he was educated at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).[^o2]

:::

He was assistant lecturer successively to [Sir William Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Hamilton,_Bart) and [Alexander Campbell Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Campbell_Fraser) (1856–60). In 1860 he was appointed to the chair of [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447) at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), and in 1864 to the [corresponding chair at the University of Glasgow](wikipedia://en/4870432).[^o2]

In [St. Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrews), he lived at 8 Playfair Terrace.[^o3]

In [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), he lectured to working men and women and between 1877 and 1883 put on courses for the [Glasgow Association for the Higher Education of Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Association_for_the_Higher_Education_of_Women). He founded the [Scottish Mountaineering Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Mountaineering_Club) in 1892. He deplored the damaging environmental impacts of industrialisation and campaigned to save old buildings.[^o4]

In philosophy an [intuitionist](wikipedia://en/19513), he was dismissive of [idealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealism) arguments. He is remembered chiefly for his work on the literature and antiquities of the [Scottish Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Borders). See *Memoir* by his niece, Mary RL Bryce (1896).[^o2]

## Publications {#Publications}

- Translations of [Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525)' *Discours de la méthode* (1850) and *Méditationes* (1852)
- An edition of Sir William Hamilton's lectures with memoir (1869, in collaboration with [HL Mansel](wikipedia://en/206369))
- *The Tweed and other Poems* (1875)[^o6]
- *Lucretius and the Atomic Theory* (1875)
- *The History and Poetry of the Scottish Border* (1877; ed. 1893)
- *Hamilton* (1882)
- *The River Tweed from its Source to the Sea* (1884)
- *Institutes of Logic* (1885)
- *The Feeling for Nature in Scottish Poetry* (1887)
- *Knowing and Being* (1889)
- *Merlin* (1889)
- *Dualism and Monism* (1895)
- *Border Essays* (1896)
