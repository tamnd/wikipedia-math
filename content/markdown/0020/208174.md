# 8

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="8"}

**8** (**eight**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [7](wikipedia://en/208159) and preceding [9](wikipedia://en/173457).

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

English *eight*, from [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) eahta*, æhta*, [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic) *\*ahto* is a direct continuation of the [hypothesized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language#Development_of_the_hypothesis) [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_numerals) *\*oḱtṓ(w)-*, and as such cognate with Greek ὀκτώ and Latin octo-, both of which stems are reflected by the English prefix oct(o)-, as in the ordinal adjective *octaval* or *octavary*, the distributive adjective is *[octonary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octonary)*. The adjective *octuple* (Latin octu-plus) may also be used as a noun, meaning "a set of eight items"; the diminutive *[octuplet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octuplet)* is mostly used to refer to eight siblings delivered in one birth.

The [Semitic numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_numerals) is based on a root *\*θmn-*, whence Akkadian *smn-*, Arabic *ṯmn-*, Hebrew *šmn-* etc. The [Chinese numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_numeral), written 八 ([Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Mandarin): *bā*; [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_language): *baat*), is from [Old Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Chinese) *\*priāt-*, ultimately from Sino-Tibetan *b-r-gyat* or *b-g-ryat* which also yielded Tibetan *brgyat*.

It has been argued that, as the [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) 7 is the highest number of items that can universally be [cognitively processed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magical_Number_Seven,_Plus_or_Minus_Two) as a single set, the etymology of the numeral *eight* might be the first to be considered composite, either as "twice four" or as "two short of ten", or similar. The [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) words for "eight" are from a [Proto-Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Turkic) stem *\*sekiz*, which has been suggested as originating as a negation of *eki* "two", as in "without two fingers" (i.e., "two short of ten; two fingers are not being held up");[^o4] this same principle is found in [Uralic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uralic_languages) *\*kakteksa*, which conveys a meaning of "two before (ten)". The Proto-Indo-European reconstruction *\*oḱtṓ(w)-* itself has been argued as representing an old dual, which would correspond to an original meaning of "twice four". Proponents of this "quaternary hypothesis" adduce the numeral *9*, which might be built on the stem *new-*, meaning "new" (indicating the beginning of a "new set of numerals" after having counted to eight).[^o5]

## Evolution of the Arabic digit {#Evolution_of_the_Arabic_digit}

*Image: Evo8glyph.svg.* Evolution of the numeral 8 from the [Brahmi numerals](wikipedia://en/1903587) to the [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786)

The modern digit 8, like all modern [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786) other than zero, originates with the [Brahmi numerals](wikipedia://en/1903587). The [Brahmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script) digit for *eight* by the 1st century was written in one stroke as a curve └┐ looking like an uppercase H with the bottom half of the left line and the upper half of the right line removed. However, the digit for eight used in India in the early centuries of the Common Era developed considerable graphic variation, and in some cases took the shape of a single wedge, which was adopted into the Perso-Arabic tradition as ٨ (and also gave rise to the later Devanagari form ८); the alternative curved glyph also existed as a variant in Perso-Arabic tradition, where it came to look similar to our digit 5.

The digits as used in [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) by the 10th century were a distinctive western variant of the glyphs used in the Arabic-speaking world, known as *ghubār* numerals (*ghubār* translating to "[sand table](wikipedia://en/8965926)"). In these digits, the line of the *5*-like glyph used in Indian manuscripts for eight came to be formed in ghubār as a closed loop, which was the *8*-shape that became adopted into European use in the 10th century.[^o7]

Just as in most modern [typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface), in typefaces with [text figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_figures) the character for the digit 8 usually has an [ascender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascender_%28typography%29), as, for example, in

The [infinity symbol](wikipedia://en/1605389) ∞, described as a "sideways figure eight", is unrelated to the digit 8 in origin; it is first used (in the mathematical meaning "infinity") in the 17th century, and it may be derived from the [Roman numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral) for "one thousand" CIƆ, or alternatively from the final Greek letter, [ω](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ω).

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

8 is a [composite number](wikipedia://en/82289) and the first number which is neither [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) nor [semiprime](wikipedia://en/339542). By [Mihăilescu's Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_conjecture), it is the only nonzero [perfect power](wikipedia://en/4023591) that is one less than another perfect power. 8 is the first proper [Leyland number](wikipedia://en/4618420) of the form $x^{y} + y^{x}$, where in its case $x$ and $y$ both equal 2. 8 is a [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) and the only nontrivial Fibonacci number that is a [perfect cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_cube).[^o10] [Sphenic numbers](wikipedia://en/245203) always have exactly eight divisors.[^o11] 8 is the base of the [octal](wikipedia://en/22330) number system.[^o12]

### Geometry {#Geometry}

A [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with eight sides is an [octagon](wikipedia://en/314575).[^o14] A regular octagon can fill a [plane-vertex](wikipedia://en/1548669#Plane-vertex_tilings) with a regular [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) and a regular [icositetragon](wikipedia://en/24082273), as well as [tessellate](wikipedia://en/321671) two-dimensional space alongside squares in the [truncated square tiling](wikipedia://en/2864244). This tiling is one of eight [Archimedean tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_tiling) that are semi-regular, or made of more than one type of regular [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621), and the only tiling that can admit a regular octagon.[^o15] The [Ammann–Beenker tiling](wikipedia://en/19292825) is a nonperiodic tesselation of [prototiles](wikipedia://en/39372) that feature prominent octagonal *silver* eightfold symmetry, that is the two-dimensional [orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052) of the four-dimensional [8-8 duoprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-8_duoprism).[^o16]

An [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) is a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299) with eight [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) as [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). is the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) to the cube and one of eight [convex deltahedra](wikipedia://en/493995).[^o17][^o18] The [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula), or *eight-pointed star*, is the only [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) with [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805). It has eight triangular faces alongside eight vertices that forms a cubic [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537), composed of two self-dual [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/5242521) that makes it the simplest of five [regular compounds](wikipedia://en/8606809). The [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), on the other hand, is a [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) cube or rectified octahedron, and one of only two convex [quasiregular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/8810651). It contains eight equilateral triangular faces, whose first [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) is the [cube-octahedron compound](wikipedia://en/8010874).[^o19][^o20]

### Vector spaces {#Vector_spaces}

The [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) are a [hypercomplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercomplex_numbers) [normed division algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_division_algebra) that are an extension of the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). They are a [double cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_covering_group) of [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) SO(8). The [special unitary group](wikipedia://en/173997) SO(3) has an eight-dimensional [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) whose colors are ascribed [gauge symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_symmetry_%28mathematics%29) that represent the [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28mathematics%29) of the eight [gluons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluon) in the [Standard Model](wikipedia://en/47641). [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305) display a periodicity of 8.[^o22]

### Group theory {#Group_theory}

The [Lie group](wikipedia://en/1691490) [**E<sub>8</sub>**](wikipedia://en/647994) has [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) 8, and is one of 5 exceptional Lie groups.[^o24][^o25] The order of the smallest [non-abelian group](wikipedia://en/4791400) whose subgroups are all normal is 8.

The [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099) describes the eightfold periodicity of the homotopy groups of the direct limit of the orthogonal groups O(n). This has many manifestations in mathematics, including the representations of Clifford algebras.

## In science {#In_science}

### Physics {#Physics}

In nuclear physics, the second [magic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_number_%28physics%29).[^o32]

### Chemistry {#Chemistry}

The number eight plays a central role in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), particularly in the context of the [octet rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octet_rule) (also known as the eight-electron rule). According to this theory, atoms of the main group elements from the second period of the [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table) onwards strive to have a maximum of eight outer electrons ([valence electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_electron)) in [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule) in order to achieve a stable [noble gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_gas) configuration. This rule applies in particular to the elements [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon), [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen), [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) and [fluorine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorine), as these often form compounds in which they achieve eight valence electrons. The [atomic number](wikipedia://en/673) eight is also significant in chemistry, as it represents the element oxygen, which is in the eighth position in the periodic table.

## In culture {#In_culture}

### Currency {#Currency}

- Sailors and civilians alike from the 1500s onward referred to evenly divided parts of the [Spanish dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_dollar) as "pieces of eight", or "bits".

### In religion, folk belief and divination {#In_religion,_folk_belief_and_divination}

#### Buddhism {#Buddhism}

*Image: Dharma Wheel.svg.* In Buddhism, the 8-spoked [Dharmacakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmacakra) represents the [Noble Eightfold Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Eightfold_Path).

In general, "eight" seems to be an auspicious number for Buddhists. The [Dharmacakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmacakra), a [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism) symbol, has eight spokes.[^o40] The Buddha's principal teaching—the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths)—ramifies as the [Noble Eightfold Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Eightfold_Path) and the Buddha emphasizes the importance of the eight attainments or jhanas.

#### Islam {#Islam}

*Image: Rub\_El\_Hizb.svg.* The [octagram](wikipedia://en/2667613) *[Rub el Hizb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub_el_Hizb)*

- The [octagram](wikipedia://en/2667613) *[Rub el Hizb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub_el_Hizb)* is often used in Islamic symbology.

#### As a lucky number {#As_a_lucky_number}

- The number eight is considered to be a [lucky number in Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbers_in_Chinese_culture) and other Asian cultures.[^o43] Eight (八; [accounting](wikipedia://en/5781#Numeral_characters) 捌; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin) *bā*) is considered a [lucky number in Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbers_in_Chinese_culture#Eight) because it sounds like the word meaning to generate wealth (發(T) 发(S); [Pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *fā*). Property with the number 8 may be valued greatly by Chinese. For example, a Hong Kong [number plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plate) with the number 8 was sold for \$640,000.[^o44] The opening ceremony of the [Summer Olympics in Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) started at 8 seconds and 8 minutes past 8 p.m. (local time) on 8 August 2008.[^o45]
- In Pythagorean [numerology](wikipedia://en/21137) the number 8 represents victory, prosperity and overcoming.
- Eight is also considered a lucky number in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan), but the reason is different from that in Chinese culture.[^o46] Eight gives an idea of growing prosperous, because the letter (八) broadens gradually.
- The Japanese thought of eight as a holy number in the ancient times. The reason is less well-understood, but it is thought that it is related to the fact they used eight to express large numbers vaguely such as manyfold (literally, eightfold and twentyfold), many clouds (literally, eight clouds), millions and millions of Gods (literally, eight millions of Gods), etc. It is also guessed that the ancient Japanese gave importance to pairs, so some researchers guess twice as four, which is also guessed to be a holy number in those times because it indicates the world (north, south, east, and west) might be considered a very holy number.
- In [numerology](wikipedia://en/21137), 8 is the number of building, and in some theories, also the number of destruction.

#### In astrology {#In_astrology}

- In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), 8 was the number of "unmoving" stars in the sky, and symbolized the perfection of incoming planetary energy.

### In sports and other games {#In_sports_and_other_games}

*Image: 8-Ball.jpg.* An 8-ball in pool

- In [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football), the number 8 has historically been the number of the Central Midfielder.
- In [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball):
  - The [center fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_fielder) is designated as number 8 for scorekeeping purposes.
- In [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league):
  - Most competitions (though not the [Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_League), which uses static squad numbering) use a position-based player numbering system in which one of the two starting props wears the number 8.
- In the [2008 Games of the XXIX Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) held in [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing), the official opening was on 08/08/08 at 8:08:08 p.m. [CST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Standard_Time).

### In literature {#In_literature}

- In [Terry Pratchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pratchett)'s *[Discworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discworld)* series, eight is a magical number[^o50] and is considered taboo. Eight is not safe to be said by wizards on the [Discworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discworld_%28world%29) and is the number of Bel-Shamharoth. Also, there are eight days in a Disc week and eight colours in a Disc spectrum, the eighth one being [octarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octarine).

### In slang {#In_slang}

- An "eighth" is a common measurement of [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_%28drug%29), meaning an eighth of an [ounce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ounce). It is also a common unit of sale for [psilocybin mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psilocybin_mushrooms).[^o52]
- In [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia) and [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela), "volverse un ocho" (meaning to tie oneself in a figure 8) refers to getting in trouble or contradicting oneself.
- In China, "8" is used in chat speak as a term for parting. This is due to the closeness in pronunciation of "8" (bā) and the English word "bye".

### Other uses {#Other_uses}

- A figure 8 is the common name of a [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) [shape](wikipedia://en/169191), often used in the context of sports, such as skating.[^o54] [Figure-eight turns of a rope or cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-eight_knot) around a cleat, pin, or bitt are used to belay something.[^o55]
