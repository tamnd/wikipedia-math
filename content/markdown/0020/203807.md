# Liouville–Neumann series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Liouville–Neumann series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Liouville–Neumann series** is a function series that results from applying the [resolvent formalism](wikipedia://en/3378540) to solve [Fredholm integral equations](wikipedia://en/363377) in [Fredholm theory](wikipedia://en/4355120).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Liouville–Neumann series is defined as

$$\phi\left(x\right) = \sum^\infty_{n=0} \lambda^n \phi_n \left(x\right)$$

which, provided that $\lambda$ is small enough so that the series converges, is the unique [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) solution of the [Fredholm integral equation](wikipedia://en/363377) of the second kind,

If the *n*th iterated [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28integral_operator%29) is defined as *n*−1 nested integrals of *n* operator kernels $K$,

$$K_n\left(x,z\right) = \int\int\cdots\int K\left(x,y_1\right)K\left(y_1,y_2\right) \cdots K\left(y_{n-1}, z\right) dy_1 dy_2 \cdots dy_{n-1}$$

then

$$\phi_n\left(x\right) = \int K_n\left(x,z\right)f\left(z\right)dz$$

with

$$\phi_0\left(x\right) = f\left(x\right)~,$$

so *K*<sub>0</sub> may be taken to be $\delta(x-z)$, the kernel of the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator).

The [resolvent](wikipedia://en/3378540), also called the "solution kernel" for the integral operator, is then given by a generalization of the [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630#Generalizations_beyond_real_and_complex_values),

$$R\left(x, z;\lambda\right) = \sum^\infty_{n=0} \lambda^n K_{n} \left(x, z\right),$$

where *K*<sub>0</sub> is again $\delta(x-z)$.

The solution of the integral equation thus becomes simply

$$\phi\left(x\right) = \int R\left( x, z;\lambda\right) f\left(z\right)dz.$$

Similar methods may be used to solve the [Volterra integral equations](wikipedia://en/2091691).
