# Vigesimal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vigesimal"}

*Image: Maya.svg.* The [Maya numerals](wikipedia://en/19823) are an example of a base-20 numeral system.[^o2]

A **vigesimal** ( ) or **base-20** (**base-score**) numeral system is based on [twenty](wikipedia://en/208259) (in the same way in which the [decimal numeral system](wikipedia://en/8214) is based on [ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29)). *Vigesimal* is derived from the Latin adjective la, meaning 'twentieth'.

:::

## Places {#Places}

In a vigesimal [place](wikipedia://en/280582) system, twenty individual numerals (or digit symbols) are used, ten more than in the decimal system. One modern method of finding the extra needed symbols is to write [ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29) as the letter A, or 10 , where the <sub>20</sub> means [base](wikipedia://en/701207) 20, to write [nineteen](wikipedia://en/208292) as 19, and the numbers between with the corresponding letters of the alphabet. This is similar to the common [computer-science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) practice of writing [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263) numerals over 9 with the letters "A–F". Another less common method skips over the letter "I", in order to avoid confusion between I<sub>20</sub> as [eighteen](wikipedia://en/208294) and [one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29), so that the number eighteen is written as J<sub>20</sub>, and nineteen is written as K<sub>20</sub>. The number twenty is written as 20.

According to this notation:

40 is equivalent to [forty](wikipedia://en/341671) in decimal = (2 × 20<sup>1</sup>) + (0 × 20<sup>0</sup>)

260 is equivalent to [two hundred and sixty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/260_%28number%29) in decimal = (13 × 20<sup>1</sup>) + (0 × 20<sup>0</sup>)

400 is equivalent to [four hundred](wikipedia://en/449606) in decimal = (1 × 20<sup>2</sup>) + (0 × 20<sup>1</sup>) + (0 × 20<sup>0</sup>).

In the rest of this article below, numbers are expressed in decimal notation, unless specified otherwise. For example, **10** means [ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29), **20** means [twenty](wikipedia://en/208259). Numbers in vigesimal notation use the convention that I means eighteen and J means nineteen.

## Fractions {#Fractions}

As 20 is divisible by two and five and is adjacent to 21, the product of three and seven, thus covering the first four prime numbers, many vigesimal fractions have simple representations, whether terminating or recurring (although thirds are more complicated than in decimal, repeating two digits instead of one). In decimal, dividing by three twice (ninths) only gives one digit periods ($\tfrac{1}{9}$ = 0.1111.... for instance) because 9 is the number below ten. 21, however, the number adjacent to 20 that is divisible by 3, is not divisible by 9. Ninths in vigesimal have six-digit periods. As 20 has the same prime factors as 10 (two and five), a fraction will terminate in decimal [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it terminates in vigesimal.

## Cyclic numbers {#Cyclic_numbers}

The prime factorization of twenty is 2<sup>2</sup> × 5, so it is not a [perfect power](wikipedia://en/4023591). However, its squarefree part, 5, is congruent to 1 (mod 4). Thus, according to [Artin's conjecture on primitive roots](wikipedia://en/2085185), vigesimal has infinitely many [cyclic](wikipedia://en/1672489) primes, but the fraction of primes that are cyclic is not necessarily ~37.395%. An UnrealScript program that computes the lengths of recurring periods of various fractions in a given set of bases found that, of the first 15,456 primes, ~39.344% are cyclic in vigesimal.

## Use {#Use}

### Quinary-vigesimal {#Quinary-vigesimal}

Many cultures that use a vigesimal system count in fives to twenty, then count twenties similarly. Such a system is referred to as *quinary-vigesimal* by linguists. Examples include [Maya](wikipedia://en/19823) and [Inuit-Yupik-Unangan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskimo–Aleut_languages).[^o19][^o20][^n1]

### Africa {#Africa}

Vigesimal systems are common in Africa, for example in [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_numerals).[^o23] While the Yoruba number system may be regarded as a vigesimal system, it is complex. [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_language), [Khana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khana_language), and [Krongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krongo_language) also have full vigesimal systems, while [Jola-Fonyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jola-Fonyi_language), [Gola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gola_language), and [Maasina Fulfulde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasina_Fulfulde) use a mixed or hybrid vigesimal-decimal system (i.e. vigesimal up to 100, decimal after). [Supyire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supyire_language#Number_system) is unique in using a base 80 system with subbases of 20, 10, and 5, but does also have a word for 400.[^o24]

### Americas {#Americas}

- In [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), vigesimal is occasionally used in phrases like "Four [score](wikipedia://en/208259) and seven years ago ..." in Lincoln's [Gettysburg address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_address) (see below).
- Twenty is a base in the [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization) and [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec) number systems. The Maya use the following names for the powers of twenty: kal (20), bak (20<sup>2</sup> = 400), pic (20<sup>3</sup> = 8,000), calab (20<sup>4</sup> = 160,000), kinchil (20<sup>5</sup> = 3,200,000) and alau (20<sup>6</sup> = 64,000,000). Vigesimal systems predated the Maya in the area, possibly going back to the Olmec, and were commonly used in other Central American languages as well. See [Maya numerals](wikipedia://en/19823) and [Maya calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_calendar), [Nahuatl language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl_language).
- The [Caddoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddoan_languages) language family in central North America traditionally used quinary-vigesimal numbers, as did some tribes along the Pacific coast of North America such as the [Haida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haida_language).[^o20]
- The [Inuit-Yupik-Unangan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskimo–Aleut_languages), including [Greenlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_language), [Iñupiaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iñupiaq_language#Numerals), [Nunivak Cupʼig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunivak_Cupʼig_language), and [Yupʼik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Alaskan_Yupʼik), among others, all have base-20 number systems with sub-base 5. In 1994, Inuit students in [Kaktovik, Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaktovik,_Alaska), came up with the base-20 [Kaktovik numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaktovik_numerals) to better represent their language. Before this invention led to a revival, the Inuit numerals had been falling out of use.[^o26] The Kaktovik numerals are:

### Asia {#Asia}

- [Dzongkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzongkha_numerals), the national language of [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), has a full vigesimal system, with numerals for the powers of 20, 400, 8,000 and 160,000.
- [Atong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atong_language_%28Sino-Tibetan%29), a language spoken in the South Garo Hills of Meghalaya state, Northeast India, and adjacent areas in Bangladesh, has a full vigesimal system that is nowadays considered archaic.[^o29]
- In [Santali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santali_language), a [Munda language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munda_languages) of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India), "fifty" is expressed by the phrase *bār isī gäl*, literally "two twenty ten."[^o30] Likewise, in [Didei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gataq_language), another Munda language spoken in India, complex numerals are decimal to 19 and decimal-vigesimal to 399.[^o31]
- The [Burushaski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burushaski) number system is base-20. For example, 20 altar, 40 alto-altar (2 times 20), 60 iski-altar (3 times 20) etc.
- In [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia), the [Ainu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainu_language) also uses a counting system that is based around the number 20. "hotnep" is 20, "wanpe etu hotnep" (ten more until two twenties) is [30](wikipedia://en/341668), "tu hotnep" (two twenties) is 40, "ashikne hotnep" (five twenties) is [100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_%28number%29). Subtraction is also heavily used, e.g. "shinepesanpe" (one more until ten) is 9.
- The [Chukchi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_language) has a vigesimal numeral system.[^o32]

### Oceania {#Oceania}

There is some evidence of base-20 usage in the [Māori language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Māori_language) of New Zealand with the suffix *hoko-* (i.e. *hoko-whitu*, *hoko-tahi*).

### Caucasus {#Caucasus}

- Twenty (otsi, ოცი) is used as a base number in [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language) for numbers 30 to 99. For example, [40](wikipedia://en/341671) (ormotsi, ორმოცი) literally means two-times-twenty, whereas [80](wikipedia://en/343338) (otkhmotsi, ოთხმოცი), means four-times-twenty. On the other hand, [31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_%28number%29) (otsdatertmeti, ოცდათერთმეტი) literally means, *twenty-and-eleven*. [67](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/67_%28number%29) (samotsdashvidi, სამოცდაშვიდი) is said as, "three-twenty-and-seven".
- Twenty (tq’a, ткъа, ტყა) is used as a base number in the [Nakh languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakh_languages) ([Chechen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen_language), [Ingush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingush_language), and [Batsbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bats_language)).

### Europe {#Europe}

In several European languages like [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language), [20](wikipedia://en/208259) is used as a base, at least with respect to the linguistic structure of the names of certain numbers (though a thoroughgoing consistent vigesimal system, based on the powers 20, 400, 8000 etc., is not generally used). The [Celtic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) use a base 20 counting system, and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) having extensive influence from [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaulish) also uses a base 20 system. Most variations of French in the modern day uses a hybrid system, with more numbers in [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec) and [Wallonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallonia) being base 10. In other regions like [French Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Switzerland) base 10 is exclusively used.[^o37]

- Twenty (vingt) is used as a base number in the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) names of numbers from 70 to 99, except in the French of [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda), the [Aosta Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aosta_Valley) and the [Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands). For example, quatre-vingts, the French word for "[80](wikipedia://en/343338)", literally means "four-twenties"; *soixante-dix*, the word for "[70](wikipedia://en/343334)", is literally "sixty-ten"; soixante-quinze ("[75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75_%28number%29)") is literally "sixty-fifteen"; quatre-vingt-sept ("[87](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/87_%28number%29)") is literally "four-twenties-seven"; quatre-vingt-dix ("[90](wikipedia://en/343340)") is literally "four-twenties-ten"; and quatre-vingt-seize ("[96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/96_%28number%29)") is literally "four-twenties-sixteen". However, in the French of Belgium, Switzerland, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Rwanda, the Aosta Valley, and the Channel Islands, the numbers 70 and 90 generally have the names septante and nonante. Therefore, the year 1996 is mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-seize in Parisian French, but it is mille neuf cent nonante-six in Belgian French. In Switzerland, "80" can be quatre-vingts (Geneva, Neuchâtel, Jura) or huitante (Vaud, Valais, Fribourg).
- Twenty (tyve) is used as a base number in the [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language) names of tens from 50 to 90. For example, tres (short for tresindstyve) means 3 times 20, i.e. [60](wikipedia://en/343325). However, Danish numerals are not vigesimal since it is only the names of some of the tens that are etymologically formed in a vigesimal way. In contrast with e.g. French quatre-vingt-seize, the units only go from zero to nine between each ten which is a defining trait of a decimal system. For details, see [Danish numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language#Numerals).
- Twenty (ugent) is used as a base number in the [Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breton_language) names of numbers from 40 to 49 and from 60 to 99. For example, daou-ugent means 2 times 20, i.e. [40](wikipedia://en/341671), and triwec'h ha pevar-ugent (literally "three-six and four-twenty") means 3×6 + 4×20, i.e. 98. However, 30 is tregont and not \*dek ha ugent ("ten and twenty"), and 50 is hanter-kant ("half-hundred").
- Twenty (ugain) is used as a base number in [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) for numbers from 20 to 99 (e.g. 50 is deg a deugain, "ten and twoscore"), although since the 1940s a decimal counting system is often used for cardinal numbers. However, the vigesimal system exclusively is used for ordinal numbers, and is still required in telling the time, money, and with weights and measures. Deugain means "two twenties" i.e. 40, trigain means 'three twenties' i.e. 60, etc. dau ar bymtheg a deugain means 57 (two on fifteen and forty). As with Breton, 50 can also be expressed as hanner cant ("half hundred"). Prior to its withdrawal from circulation, papur chweugain (note of sixscore) was the nickname for the ten-shilling (120 pence) note, as 120 (old) pence was equal to half a pound sterling. the term chweugain continues to be used to mean 50 pence in modern Welsh, and phrases like pisin chweugain ('50p piece') are also not uncommon.
- Twenty (fichead) is traditionally used as a base number in [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic), with deich ar fhichead or fichead 's a deich being 30 (ten over twenty, or twenty and ten), dà fhichead 40 (two twenties), dà fhichead 's a deich 50 (two twenty and ten) / leth-cheud 50 (half a hundred), trì fichead 60 (three twenties) and so on up to naoidh fichead 180 (nine twenties). Nowadays a decimal system is taught in schools, but the vigesimal system is still used by many, particularly older speakers.
- Twenty (feed) is traditionally used as a base number in [Manx Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manx_Gaelic), with jeih as feed being 30 (ten and twenty), daeed 40 (two twenties), jeih as daeed 50 (ten and two twenties), tree feed 60 (three twenty) and so on. A decimal system also exists, using the following tens: jeih (ten), feed (twenty), treead (thirty), daeed (forty), queigad (fifty), sheyad (sixty), shiagtad (seventy), hoghtad (eighty) and nuyad (ninety).
- Twenty (njëzet) is used as a base number in [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language). The word for 40 (dyzet) means "two times 20". The [Arbëreshë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbëreshë_people) in Italy may use trizetë for 60. Formerly, katërzetë was also used for 80. Today [Cham Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cham_Albanians) in Greece use all zet numbers. Basically, 20 means 1 zet, 40 means 2 zet, 60 means 3 zet and 80 means 4 zet. Albanian is the only language in the Balkans which has retained elements of the vigesimal numeral system side by side with decimal system. The existence of the two systems in Albanian reflect the contribution of Pre-Indo-European people of the Balkans to the formation of the Paleo-Balkan Indo-European tribes and their language.[^o38]
- Twenty (hogei) is used as a base number in [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) for numbers up to 100 (ehun). The words for 40 (berrogei), 60 (hirurogei) and 80 (laurogei) mean "two-score", "three-score" and "four-score", respectively. For example, the number 75 is called hirurogeita hamabost, lit. "three-score-and ten-five". The Basque nationalist [Sabino Arana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabino_Arana) proposed a vigesimal digit system to match the spoken language,[^o39] and, as an alternative, a reform of the spoken language to make it decimal,[^o40] but both are mostly forgotten.[^o41]
- Twenty (dwisti or dwujsti) is used as a base number in the [Resian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resian_dialect) trïkrat dwisti (3×20), 70 by trïkrat dwisti nu dësat (3×20 + 10), 80 by štirikrat dwisti (4×20) and 90 by štirikrat dwisti nu dësat (4×20 + 10).[^o42][^o43]
- In the [£sd currency system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/£sd) (used in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) pre-1971), there were 20 [shillings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilling) (worth 12 pence each) to the [pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_Sterling). Under the decimal system introduced in 1971 (1 pound equals 100 new pence instead of 240 pence in the old system), the shilling coins still in circulation were re-valued at 5 pence (no more were minted and the shilling coin was demonetised in 1990).
- In the imperial weight system there are twenty [hundredweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundredweight) in a [ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton).
- In [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), the name of the [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) 20 is most commonly phrased with the word 'twenty'. Counting by the score has been used historically; for example, the famous opening of the [Gettysburg Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Address), "Four score and seven years ago...", refers to the signing of the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_%28United_States%29) in 1776, 87 years earlier. In the [King James Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authorized_King_James_Version), the term *score* is used over 130 times, though a single score is always expressed as "twenty". *Score* is still occasionally used to denote groups of 20 analogously to the use of *[dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dozen)* to quantify groups of 12.
- Other languages have terms similar to *score*, such as [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_%28language%29) and [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_%28language%29) no.
- Even in regions where greater aspects of the [Brythonic Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittonic_languages) languages may be less apparent in modern dialect, sheep enumeration systems that are vigesimal are recalled to the present day. See [Yan Tan Tethera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Tan_Tethera).

### Software applications {#Software_applications}

[Open Location Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Location_Code) uses a word-safe version of base 20 for its [geocodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocoding). The characters in this alphabet were chosen to avoid accidentally forming words. The developers scored all possible sets of 20 letters in 30 different languages for likelihood of forming words, and chose a set that formed as few recognizable words as possible.[^o46] The alphabet is also intended to reduce typographical errors by avoiding visually similar digits, and is case-insensitive.

### Related observations {#Related_observations}

- Among [multiples](wikipedia://en/3912709) of [10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29), 20 is described in a special way in some languages. For example, the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language) words treinta ([30](wikipedia://en/341668)) and cuarenta (40) consist of "tre(3)+inta (10 times)", "cuar(4)+enta (10 times)", but the word veinte ([20](wikipedia://en/208259)) is not currently connected to any word meaning "two" (although historically it is[^o49]). Similarly, in [Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages) such as Arabic and Hebrew, the numbers 30, 40 ... 90 are expressed by morphologically plural forms of the words for the numbers 3, 4 ... 9, but the number 20 is expressed by a morphologically plural form of the word for 10. The Japanese language has a special word (hatachi) for 20 years (of age), and for the 20th day of the month (hatsuka).
- In some languages (e.g. English, [Slavic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) and German), the names of the two-digit numbers from [11](wikipedia://en/208156) to [19](wikipedia://en/208292) consist of one word, but the names of the two-digit numbers from [21](wikipedia://en/209248) on consist of two words. So for example, the English words eleven ([11](wikipedia://en/208156)), twelve ([12](wikipedia://en/208155)), thirteen ([13](wikipedia://en/37231)) etc., as opposed to *twenty*-one ([21](wikipedia://en/209248)), *twenty*-two ([22](wikipedia://en/362193)), *twenty*-three ([23](wikipedia://en/362203)), etc. In French, this is true up to 16. In a number of other languages (such as [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language)), the names of the numbers from 11 to 19 contain two words, but one of these words is a special "teen" form, which is different from the ordinary form of the word for the number 10, and it may in fact be only found in these names of the numbers 11–19.
- [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese)[^o50] and [Wu Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chinese) frequently use the single unit 廿 (Cantonese *yàh*, [Shanghainese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghainese) *nyae* or *ne*, Mandarin *niàn*) for twenty, in addition to the fully decimal 二十 (Cantonese *yìh sàhp*, Shanghainese *el sah*, Mandarin *èr shí*) which literally means "two ten". Equivalents exist for 30 and 40 (卅 and 卌 respectively: Mandarin *sà* and *xì*), but these are more seldom used. This is a historic remnant of a vigesimal system.
- Although Khmer numerals have represented a [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) [positional notation](wikipedia://en/437052) system since at least the 7th century, [Old Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Khmer), or Angkorian Khmer, also possessed separate symbols for the numbers 10, 20, and 100. Each multiple of 20 or 100 would require an additional stroke over the character, so the number 47 was constructed using the 20 symbol with an additional upper stroke, followed by the symbol for number 7. This suggests that spoken Angkorian Khmer used a vigesimal system.
- [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language) uses the term ยี่สิบ (*yi sip*) for 20. Other multiples of ten consist of the base number, followed by the word for ten, e.g. สามสิบ (*sam sip*), lit. three ten, for thirty. The *yi* of *yi sip* is different from the number two in other positions, which is สอง (*song*). Nevertheless, *yi sip* is a loan word from Chinese.
- [Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_language) similarly forms multiples of ten by putting the base number in front of the word ten, so ສາມສິບ (*sam sip*), litt. three ten, for thirty. The exception is twenty, for which the word ຊາວ (*xao*) is used. (ซาว *sao* is also used in the North-Eastern and Northern dialects of Thai, but not in standard Thai.)
- The [Kharosthi numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharosthi#Numerals) system behaves like a partial vigesimal system.
