# Émile Clapeyron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Émile Clapeyron"}

**Benoît Paul Émile Clapeyron** (; 26 January 1799 – 28 January 1864) was a French [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), one of the founders of [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952). He is known for developing the [ideal gas law](wikipedia://en/59881).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), Clapeyron studied at the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique), graduating in 1818.[^o4] He also studied at [École des mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_des_mines). In 1820 he and [Gabriel Lamé](wikipedia://en/52145) went to [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) to teach and work at the school of public works there. He returned to Paris only after the [Revolution of July 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_July_1830), supervising the construction of the first [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) lines connecting Paris to [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles,_Yvelines) and [Paris to Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris–Saint-Germain-en-Laye_railway).[^o4] The half brothers [Stéphane Mony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stéphane_Mony) and [Eugène Flachat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugène_Flachat) collaborated in this project, which was financed by Adolphe d'Eichthal<sup>(fr)</sup>, [Rothschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mayer_de_Rothschild), Auguste Thurneyssen, Sanson Davillier and the [Péreire brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Péreire_brothers).[^o5] Clapeyron took his [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine) designs to England in 1836 to find a manufacturer and engaged [Sharp, Roberts and Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp,_Roberts_and_Co.)[^o4]

From 1844 to 1859 Clapeyron was a professor at [École des Ponts et Chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_des_Ponts_et_Chaussées).[^o4]

Clapeyron married Mélanie Bazaine, daughter of [Pierre-Dominique Bazaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Dominique_Bazaine) (mathematician and [ingénieur des ponts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingénieur_des_ponts)), and older sister of [Pierre-Dominique (Adolphe) Bazaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Dominique_%28Adolphe%29_Bazaine) (railway engineer) and [François Achille Bazaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Achille_Bazaine) (Marshal of France).

## Work {#Work}

In 1834, he made his first contribution to the creation of modern thermodynamics by publishing a report entitled *Mémoire sur la puissance motrice de la chaleur* (*Memoir on the Motive Power of Heat*), in which he developed the work of the physicist [Nicolas Léonard Sadi Carnot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Léonard_Sadi_Carnot), deceased two years before. Though Carnot had developed a compelling analysis of a generalised [heat engine](wikipedia://en/13654), he had employed the clumsy and already unfashionable [caloric theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caloric_theory).

Clapeyron, in his memoire, presented Carnot's work in a more accessible and analytic graphical form, showing the [Carnot cycle](wikipedia://en/5994167) as a closed [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) on an [indicator diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indicator_diagram), a [chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chart) of [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) against [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) (named in his honor **Clapeyron's graph**). Clapeyron's analysis of Carnot was more broadly disseminated in 1843 when [Johann Poggendorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Poggendorff) translated it into German.[^o7]

In 1842 Clapeyron published his findings on the "optimal position for the piston at which the various [valves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_valve_%28steam_engine%29) should be opened or closed."[^o4][^o8] In 1843, Clapeyron further developed the idea of a [reversible process](wikipedia://en/416681), already suggested by Carnot and made a definitive statement of *Carnot's principle*, what is now known as the [second law of thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/133017).

These foundations enabled him to make substantive extensions of [Clausius'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Clausius) work, including the formula, now known as the [Clausius–Clapeyron relation](wikipedia://en/1780425), which characterises the [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423) between two [phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28matter%29) of [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter). He further considered questions of phase transitions in what later became known as [Stefan problems](wikipedia://en/679186).

Clapeyron also worked on the characterisation of [perfect gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_gas), the equilibrium of homogeneous [solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid), and calculations of the [statics](wikipedia://en/28767) of continuous [beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_%28structure%29), notably the [theorem of three moments](wikipedia://en/23409732)[^o9] (**Clapeyron's [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977)**).
