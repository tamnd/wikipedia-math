# 11 (number)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="11"}

**11** (**eleven**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [10](wikipedia://en/208151) and preceding [12](wikipedia://en/208155). It is the smallest number whose name in English has three syllables.

:::

## Name {#Name}

"Eleven" derives from the [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) *ęndleofon*, which is first attested in [Bede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bede)'s late 9th-century *[Ecclesiastical History of the English People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_History_of_the_English_People)*.[^o6] It has cognates in every [Germanic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_language) (for example, German elf), whose [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language) ancestor has been [reconstructed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_reconstruction) as \*ainalifa-,[^o4] from the prefix \*aina- (adjectival "[one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29)") and suffix \*-lifa-, of uncertain meaning.[^o6] It is sometimes compared with the [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language) *vienúolika*, though *\-lika* is used as the suffix for all numbers from 11 to 19.[^o6]

The [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) form has closer cognates in [Old Frisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Frisian), [Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Saxon), and [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse), whose ancestor has been reconstructed as \*ainlifun. This was formerly thought to be derived from Proto-Germanic \*tehun ("[ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29)");[^o6][^o5] it is now sometimes connected with \*leikʷ- or \*leip- ("left; remaining"), with the implicit meaning that "one is left" after counting to ten.[^o6]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

11 is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), and a [super-prime](wikipedia://en/13398615). 11 forms a [twin prime](wikipedia://en/41997) pair with [13](wikipedia://en/37231), and [sexy prime pairs](wikipedia://en/343116) with both 5 and 17. 11 is also the first prime exponent that does not yield a [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) because 2<sup>11</sup> − 1 is not prime:

2<sup>11</sup> − 1 = 2047 = 23 × 89.

(5, 11) is a pair of [brown numbers](wikipedia://en/11301521) because 5! + 1 = 11<sup>2</sup>. Only three such pairs of numbers are known. Rows in [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) can be seen as representation of [powers](wikipedia://en/99491) of 11.[^o10]

### Geometry {#Geometry}

*Image: Fotothek df tg 0004812 Geometrie ^ Architektur ^ Festungsbau ^ Vermessung.jpg.* Copper engraving of a [hendecagon](wikipedia://en/614840), by Anton Ernst Burkhard von Birckenstein (1698)

An 11-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) is called a [hendecagon](wikipedia://en/614840), or *undecagon*. A regular hendecagon is the polygon with the fewest number of sides that is not able to be [constructed](wikipedia://en/61229) with a straightedge, compass, and [angle trisector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_trisector).[^o12]

The [Mathieu group](wikipedia://en/380207) $\mathrm{M}_{11}$ is the smallest of twenty-six [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806). It has [order](wikipedia://en/493527) $7920 =2^{4}\cdot3^{2}\cdot5\cdot11 = 8\cdot9\cdot10\cdot11$, with 11 as its largest prime factor. $\mathrm{M}_{11}$ is the [maximal subgroup](wikipedia://en/1472649) Mathieu group $\mathrm{M}_{12}$, where 11 is also its largest prime factor.

## Music {#Music}

The interval of an [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave) plus a fourth is an [11th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_%28interval%29). A complete 11th chord has almost every note of a [diatonic scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatonic_scale).

In [just intonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_intonation) and [microtonal music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtonality), the number 11 or the ratio 11/8 denotes an interval between that of a perfect fifth and of a [tritone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritone); eleven eighths the frequency of the fundamental tone, or the [eleventh harmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_harmonic). It is an unstable interval and appears mostly in contemporary and experimental music.

## Cultural references {#Cultural_references}

### Film {#Film}

In the [mockumentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mockumentary) film "[This Is Spinal Tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Spinal_Tap)", the idiomatic phrase [up to eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_to_eleven) is coined to allude to going beyond typical limits, in this case guitar amplifier volume levels.

### "Eleventh hour" {#"Eleventh_hour"}

Being one hour before 12:00, the *eleventh hour* means the last possible moment to take care of something, and often implies a situation of urgent danger or emergency (see [Doomsday clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doomsday_clock)). "The eleventh hour" is a phrase in the [Parable of the Workers in the Vineyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parable_of_the_Workers_in_the_Vineyard) in the Bible.

### Eleventh Night {#Eleventh_Night}

In [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) communities in [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland), bonfires are lit to mark the eve of Protestant [William III of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_III_of_England)'s victory over the [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) [James II of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_England) at the [Battle of the Boyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Boyne).

## Languages {#Languages}

While 11 has its own name in Germanic languages such as English, German, or Swedish, and some Latin-based languages such as Spanish, Portuguese, and French, it is the first compound number in many other languages: Chinese 十一 shí yī, Korean 열하나 yeol hana or 십일 ship il.

In Basque, hamaika, "11", also [means "a lot"](wikipedia://en/538913).[^o23]

## Religion {#Religion}

The number 11 (alongside its multiples 22 and 33) are master numbers in [numerology](wikipedia://en/21137), especially in [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age).[^o25]
