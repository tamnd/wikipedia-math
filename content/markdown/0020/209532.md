# QED manifesto

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="QED manifesto"}

The **QED manifesto** was a proposal for a computer-based database of all [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) knowledge, strictly formalized and with all proofs having been [checked automatically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_proof_checking). ([Q.E.D.](wikipedia://en/25228) means quod erat demonstrandum in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), meaning "which was to be demonstrated.")

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The idea for the project arose in 1993, mainly under the impetus of [Robert Boyer](wikipedia://en/4384637). The goals of the project, tentatively named *QED project* or *project QED*, were outlined in the QED manifesto, a document first published in 1994, with input from several researchers.[^o3] Explicit authorship was deliberately avoided. A dedicated mailing list was created, and two scientific conferences on QED took place, the first one in 1994 at [Argonne National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratories) and the second in 1995 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) organized by the [Mizar](wikipedia://en/209074) group.[^o4]

The project seems to have dissolved by 1996, never having produced more than discussions and plans. In a 2007 paper, Freek Wiedijk identifies two reasons for the failure of the project.[^o5] In order of importance:

- Very few people are working on formalization of mathematics. There is no compelling application for fully mechanized mathematics.
- [Formalized mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formalized_mathematics) does not yet resemble real, traditional mathematics. This is partly due to the complexity of mathematical notation, and partly to the limitations of existing [theorem provers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_theorem_prover) and [proof assistants](wikipedia://en/1258607); the paper finds that the major contenders, [Mizar](wikipedia://en/209074), [HOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOL_theorem_prover_family), and [Rocq](wikipedia://en/581974), have serious shortcomings in their abilities to express mathematics.

Nonetheless, QED-style projects are regularly proposed. The [Mizar](wikipedia://en/209074) Mathematical Library formalizes a large portion of undergraduate mathematics, and was considered the largest such library in 2007.[^o6] Similar projects include the [Metamath](wikipedia://en/6023946) proof database and the mathlib library written in [Lean](wikipedia://en/62889984).[^o7]

In 2014 the Twenty years of the QED Manifesto[^o8] workshop was organized as part of the [Vienna Summer of Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Summer_of_Logic).
