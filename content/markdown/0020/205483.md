# Jean-Pierre Serre

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Pierre Serre"}

**Jean-Pierre Serre** (; born 15 September 1926) is a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who has made contributions to [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1954 and the inaugural [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2003.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Personal life {#Personal_life}

Born in [Bages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bages,_Pyrénées-Orientales), [Pyrénées-Orientales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrénées-Orientales), to pharmacist parents, Serre was educated at the Lycée de Nîmes. Then he studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) from 1945 to 1948.[^o5] He was awarded his doctorate from the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in 1951. From 1948 to 1954 he held positions at the [Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_la_Recherche_Scientifique) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). In 1956 he was elected professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France), a position he held until his retirement in 1994.

His wife, Professor [Josiane Heulot-Serre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiane_Serre), was a chemist; she also was the director of the Ecole Normale Supérieure de Jeunes Filles. Their daughter is the former French diplomat, historian and writer [Claudine Monteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudine_Monteil). The French mathematician [Denis Serre](wikipedia://en/45107336) is his nephew. He practices skiing, table tennis, and rock climbing (in [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau)).

### Career {#Career}

From a very young age, Serre was an outstanding figure in the school of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536),[^o7] working on [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) and then [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), where he introduced [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) theory and [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) techniques. Serre's thesis concerned the [Leray–Serre spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3085825) associated to a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826). Together with Cartan, Serre established the technique of using [Eilenberg–MacLane spaces](wikipedia://en/1073230) for computing [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697), which at that time was one of the major problems in topology.

In his speech at the Fields Medal award ceremony in 1954, [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) gave high praise to Serre, and also made the point that the award was for the first time awarded to a non-analyst. Serre subsequently changed his research focus.

#### Algebraic geometry {#Algebraic_geometry}

In the 1950s and 1960s, a fruitful collaboration between Serre and the two-years-younger [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) led to important foundational work, much of it motivated by the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705). Two major foundational papers by Serre were *Faisceaux algébriques cohérents* (FAC, 1955),[^o9] on [coherent cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_cohomology), and *Géométrie algébrique et géométrie analytique* ([GAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAGA), 1956).[^o10]

Even at an early stage in his work Serre had perceived a need to construct more general and refined [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) theories to tackle the Weil conjectures. The problem was that the cohomology of a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) could not capture as much topology as [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology) with integer coefficients. Amongst Serre's early candidate theories of 1954–55 was one based on [Witt vector](wikipedia://en/5573710) coefficients.

Around 1958, Serre suggested that isotrivial principal bundles on algebraic varieties—those that become trivial after pullback by a finite [étale map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_map)—are important. This acted as one important source of inspiration for Grothendieck to develop [étale topology](wikipedia://en/5707590) and the corresponding theory of [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231).[^o11] These tools, developed in full by Grothendieck and collaborators in [Séminaire de géométrie algébrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique) (SGA) 4 and SGA 5, provided the tools for the eventual proof of the Weil conjectures by [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544).

#### Other work {#Other_work}

*Image: SERRE Jean-Pierre.jpg.* Serre

From 1959 onward Serre's interests turned towards [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), in particular [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module) and [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000).

Amongst his most original contributions were: his "[Conjecture II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_conjecture_II_%28algebra%29)" (still open) on Galois cohomology; his use of [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on [trees](wikipedia://en/18673708) (with [Hyman Bass](wikipedia://en/7085910)); the Borel–Serre compactification; results on the number of points of curves over finite fields; [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) in [ℓ-adic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic_cohomology) and the proof that these representations have often a "large" image; the concept of [p-adic modular form](wikipedia://en/37776049); and the [Serre conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_conjecture_%28number_theory%29) (now a theorem) on mod-*p* representations that made [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) a connected part of mainstream [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177).

In his paper FAC,[^o9] Serre asked whether a finitely generated [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) over a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) is [free](wikipedia://en/326454). This question led to a great deal of activity in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), and was finally answered in the affirmative by [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) and [Andrei Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820) independently in 1976. This result is now known as the [Quillen–Suslin theorem](wikipedia://en/539562).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

At the age of 27 in 1954, Serre was (and still is) the youngest person ever to have been awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859). He went on to win the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize) in 1985, the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) in 1995, the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2000, and was the first recipient of the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2003. He has been awarded other prizes, such as the Gold Medal of the [French National Scientific Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Scientific_Research_Centre) (Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique, CNRS).

He is a foreign member of several scientific Academies (US, Norway, Sweden, Russia, the Royal Society, [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (1978),[^o14] [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences),[^o15] [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o16] the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society)[^o17]) and has received many honorary degrees (from Cambridge, Oxford, Harvard, Oslo and others). In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o18]

Serre has been awarded the highest honors in France as [Grand Cross of the Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour) (Grand Croix de la Légion d'Honneur) and [Grand Cross of the Legion of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_national_du_Mérite) (Grand Croix de l'Ordre National du Mérite).
