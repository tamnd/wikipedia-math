# 7

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="7"}

**7** (**seven**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [6](wikipedia://en/208171) and preceding [8](wikipedia://en/208174). It is the only [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) preceding a [cube](wikipedia://en/451445).

:::

As an early prime number in the series of [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integers), the number seven has symbolic associations in [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythology), [superstition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstition) and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy). The seven [classical planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_planet) resulted in seven being the number of days in a week.[^o3] 7 is often considered [lucky](wikipedia://en/192727) in [Western culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) and is often seen as highly symbolic.

## Evolution of the Arabic digit {#Evolution_of_the_Arabic_digit}

*Image: SevenGlyph.svg.*

For early [Brahmi numerals](wikipedia://en/1903587), 7 was written more or less in one stroke as a curve that looks like an uppercase $\langle J\rangle$ vertically inverted (ᒉ). The western Arab peoples' main contribution was to make the longer line diagonal rather than straight, though they showed some tendencies to making the digit more rectilinear. The eastern Arab peoples developed the digit from a form that looked something like 6 to one that looked like an uppercase V. Both modern Arab forms influenced the European form, a two-stroke form consisting of a horizontal upper stroke joined at its right to a stroke going down to the bottom left corner, a line that is slightly curved in some font variants. As is the case with the European digit, the [Cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cham_script#Numerals) and [Khmer digit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_script#Numerals) for 7 also evolved to look like their digit 1, though in a different way, so they were also concerned with making their 7 more different. For the Khmer this often involved adding a horizontal line to the top of the digit.[^o6] This is analogous to the horizontal stroke through the middle that is sometimes used in [handwriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handwriting) in the Western world but which is almost never used in [computer fonts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_fonts). This horizontal stroke is, however, important to distinguish the glyph for seven from the glyph for [one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29) in writing that uses a long upstroke in the glyph for 1. In some Greek dialects of the early 12th century the longer line diagonal was drawn in a rather semicircular transverse line.

*Image: Digital77.svg.*

On [seven-segment displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven-segment_display), 7 is the digit with the most common graphic variation (1, 6 and 9 also have variant glyphs). Most devices use three line segments, but devices made by some Japanese companies such as [Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation) and [Casio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casio), as well as in the Koreas and Taiwan, 7 is written with four line segments because in those countries, 7 is written with a "hook" on the left, as ① in the following illustration. Further segments can give further variation. For example, [Schindler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schindler_Group) elevators in the United States and Canada installed or modernized from the late 1990s onwards usually use a 16 segment display and show the digit 7 in a manner more similar to that of handwriting.

*Image: sevens.svg.*

While the shape of the character for the digit 7 has an [ascender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascender_%28typography%29) in most modern [typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface), in typefaces with [text figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_figures) the character usually has a [descender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descender), as, for example, in

Most people in Continental Europe,[^o7] Indonesia, and some in Britain, Ireland, Israel, Canada, and Latin America, write 7 with a line through the middle (7), sometimes with the top line crooked. The line through the middle is useful to clearly differentiate that digit from the digit *one*, as they can appear similar when written in certain styles of handwriting. This form is used in official handwriting rules for [primary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_school) in Russia, Ukraine, Bulgaria, Poland, other Slavic countries,[^o8] France,[^o9] Italy, Belgium, the Netherlands, Finland,[^o10] Romania, Germany, Greece,[^o11] and Hungary.

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

Seven, the fourth prime number, is not only a [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) (since $2^3 - 1 = 7$) but also a [double Mersenne prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Mersenne_prime) since the exponent, 3, is itself a Mersenne prime.[^o13] It is also a [Newman–Shanks–Williams prime](wikipedia://en/323705),[^o14] a [Woodall prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodall_prime),[^o15] a [factorial prime](wikipedia://en/409293),[^o16] a [Harshad number](wikipedia://en/641307), a [lucky prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_prime),[^o17] a [happy number](wikipedia://en/542994) (happy prime),[^o18] a [safe prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_prime) (the only Mersenne safe prime), a [sexy prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexy_prime), a [Leyland number of the second kind](wikipedia://en/4618420#Leyland_number_of_the_second_kind) and [Leyland prime of the second kind](wikipedia://en/4618420#Leyland_number_of_the_second_kind) ($2^5-5^2$), and the fourth [Heegner number](wikipedia://en/522147).[^o21] Seven is the lowest natural number that cannot be represented as the sum of the squares of three integers.

A seven-sided shape is a [heptagon](wikipedia://en/365349).[^o22] The [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) *n*-gons for *n* ⩽ 6 can be constructed by [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge) alone, which makes the heptagon the first regular polygon that cannot be directly constructed with these simple tools.[^o23]

7 is the only number *D* for which the equation 2${}^{n}$ − *D* = *x*${}^{2}$ has more than two solutions for *n* and *x* [natural](wikipedia://en/21474). In particular, the equation 2${}^{n}$ − 7 = *x*${}^{2}$ is known as the [Ramanujan–Nagell equation](wikipedia://en/2412819). 7 is one of seven numbers in the positive [definite quadratic](wikipedia://en/251478) [integer matrix](wikipedia://en/4500787) representative of all [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) numbers: {1, 3, 5, 7, 11, 15, 33}.[^o24]

There are 7 [frieze groups](wikipedia://en/375503) in two dimensions, consisting of [symmetries](wikipedia://en/28186) of the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) whose group of [translations](wikipedia://en/204682) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the group of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).[^o26] These are related to the [17](wikipedia://en/208288) [wallpaper groups](wikipedia://en/386214) whose transformations and [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) repeat two-dimensional patterns in the plane.[^o27]

A [heptagon](wikipedia://en/365349) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is unable to generate [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635) alongside other polygons, like the regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014). However, it is one of fourteen polygons that can fill a [plane-vertex tiling](wikipedia://en/1548669#Plane-vertex_tilings), in its case only alongside a regular [triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) and a 42-sided polygon (\3.7.42).[^o29][^o30] Otherwise, for any regular *n*-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621), the maximum number of intersecting diagonals (other than through its center) is at most 7.[^o31]

In two dimensions, there are precisely seven [7-uniform](wikipedia://en/1548669#k-uniform_tilings) *Krotenheerdt* tilings, with no other such *k*-uniform tilings for *k* > 7, and it is also the only *k* for which the count of *Krotenheerdt* tilings agrees with *k*.[^o33]

The [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404), the smallest possible [finite projective plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_projective_plane), has 7 points and 7 lines arranged such that every line contains 3 points and 3 lines cross every point.[^o34] This is related to other appearances of the number seven in relation to [exceptional objects](wikipedia://en/5280515), like the fact that the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) contain seven distinct square roots of −1, [seven-dimensional vectors](wikipedia://en/2368531) have a [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092), and the number of [equiangular lines](wikipedia://en/2706034) possible in seven-dimensional space is anomalously large.[^o35][^o36][^o37]

*Image: Dice Distribution (bar).svg.* Graph of the probability distribution of the sum of two six-sided dice

The lowest known dimension for an [exotic sphere](wikipedia://en/1114931) is the seventh dimension.[^o38]

In [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742), 7 is the highest dimension for non-simplex [hypercompact *Vinberg polytopes*](wikipedia://en/5358554#Hypercompact_Coxeter_groups_%28Vinberg_polytopes%29) of rank *n + 4* mirrors, where there is one unique figure with eleven [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647). On the other hand, such figures with rank *n + 3* mirrors exist in dimensions 4, 5, 6 and 8; *not* in 7.[^o40]

There are seven fundamental types of [catastrophes](wikipedia://en/434288).[^o41]

When rolling two standard six-sided [dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice), seven has a 1 in 6 probability of being rolled, the greatest of any number.[^o42] The opposite sides of a standard six-sided die always add to 7.

The [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234) are seven problems in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) that were stated by the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) in 2000.[^o43] Currently, six of the problems remain [unsolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsolved_problems_in_mathematics).[^o44]

#### Decimal calculations {#Decimal_calculations}

999,999 divided by 7 is exactly 142,857. Therefore, when a [vulgar fraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulgar_fraction) with 7 in the [denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominator) is converted to a [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) expansion, the result has the same six-[digit](wikipedia://en/280582) repeating sequence after the decimal point, but the sequence can start with any of those six digits.[^o50] In [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) representation, the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) of 7 repeats six [digits](wikipedia://en/280582) (as 0.$\overline{142857}$),[^o51] whose sum when [cycling](wikipedia://en/1672489#Relation_to_repeating_decimals) back to [1](wikipedia://en/77591639) is equal to 28.

## In science {#In_science}

### In psychology {#In_psychology}

- In Western culture, seven is consistently listed as people's favorite number[^o55][^o56]
- When guessing numbers 1–10, the number 7 is most likely to be picked[^o57]

## Classical antiquity {#Classical_antiquity}

The [Pythagoreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagoreans) invested particular numbers with unique spiritual properties. The number seven was considered to be particularly interesting because it consisted of the union of the physical (number [4](wikipedia://en/208161)) with the spiritual (number [3](wikipedia://en/208157)).[^o59] In Pythagorean [numerology](wikipedia://en/21137) the number 7 means spirituality.

## Culture {#Culture}

The number seven had mystical and religious significance in Mesopotamian culture by the 22nd century BCE at the latest. This was likely because in the Sumerian [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) number system, dividing by seven was the first division which resulted in infinitely [repeating fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repeating_fraction).[^o61]
