# Symmetric relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symmetric relation"}

A **symmetric relation** is a type of [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931). A [homogeneous relation](wikipedia://en/12987179) $R$ on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $X$ is symmetric if:

:::

for all $a$ and $b$ in $X$, $a R b$ if and only if $b R a$,

where the notation $a R b$ means that the [ordered pair](wikipedia://en/22362) $(a, b)$ is in the relation $R$.

An example is the relation "is equal to", because if *a* = *b* is true then *b* = *a* is also true. If *R*<sup>T</sup> represents the [converse](wikipedia://en/3941020) of *R*, then *R* is symmetric if and only if *R* = *R*<sup>T</sup>.[^o2]

Symmetry, along with [reflexivity](wikipedia://en/200458) and [transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463), are the three defining properties of an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Relationship to asymmetric and antisymmetric relations {#Relationship_to_asymmetric_and_antisymmetric_relations}

*Image: Symmetric-and-or-antisymmetric.svg.* Symmetric and antisymmetric relations

By definition, a nonempty relation cannot be both symmetric and [asymmetric](wikipedia://en/1229368) (where if *a* is related to *b*, then *b* cannot be related to *a* (in the same way)). However, a relation can be neither symmetric nor asymmetric, which is the case for "is less than or equal to" and "preys on").

Symmetric and [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176) (where the only way *a* can be related to *b* and *b* be related to *a* is if *a* = *b*) are actually independent of each other, as these examples show.

*Mathematical examples*

::: {#o8 .table cue=table name="Mathematical examples"}

|  | **Symmetric** | **Not symmetric** |
|---|---|---|
| **Antisymmetric** | [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) | [divides](wikipedia://en/49492), less than or equal to |
| **Not antisymmetric** | [congruence](wikipedia://en/48167) in [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087) | [//](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_division) (integer division), most nontrivial [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) |

:::

*Non-mathematical examples*

::: {#o9 .table cue=table name="Non-mathematical examples"}

|  | **Symmetric** | **Not symmetric** |
|---|---|---|
| **Antisymmetric** | is the same person as, and is married | is the plural of |
| **Not antisymmetric** | is a full biological sibling of | preys on |

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- A symmetric and [transitive relation](wikipedia://en/200463) is always [quasireflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasireflexive_relation).
- One way to count the symmetric relations on *n* elements, that in their binary matrix representation the upper right triangle determines the relation fully, and it can be arbitrary given, thus there are as many symmetric relations as *n* × *n* binary upper triangle matrices, 2<sup>*n*(*n*+1)/2</sup>.

:::
