# F-space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="F-space"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), an **F-space** is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $X$ over the [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) numbers together with a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) $d : X \times X \to \mathbb{R}$ such that

:::

1. Scalar multiplication in $X$ is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122#Continuous_functions_between_metric_spaces) with respect to $d$ and the standard metric on $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}.$
1. Addition in $X$ is continuous with respect to $d.$
1. The metric is [translation-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation-invariant_metric); that is, $d(x + a, y + a) = d(x, y)$ for all $x, y, a \in X.$
1. The metric space $(X, d)$ is [complete](wikipedia://en/7832).

The operation $x \mapsto \|x\| := d(0, x)$ is called an **F-norm**, although in general an F-norm is not required to be homogeneous. By [translation-invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_invariance), the metric is recoverable from the F-norm. Thus, a real or complex F-space is equivalently a real or complex vector space equipped with a complete F-norm.

Some authors use the term [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) rather than F-space, but usually the term "Fréchet space" is reserved for [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) F-spaces. Some other authors use the term "F-space" as a synonym of "Fréchet space", by which they mean a locally convex complete metrizable [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752). The metric may or may not necessarily be part of the structure on an F-space; many authors only require that such a space be [metrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078) in a manner that satisfies the above properties.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

All [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) and [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801) are F-spaces. In particular, a Banach space is an F-space with an additional requirement that $d(a x, 0) = |a| d(x, 0).$[^o4]

The [L<sup>p</sup> spaces](wikipedia://en/45194) can be made into F-spaces for all $p \geq 0$ and for $p \geq 1$ they can be made into locally convex and thus Fréchet spaces and even Banach spaces.

### Example 1 {#Example_1}

$L^{\frac{1}{2}}[0,\, 1]$ is an F-space. It admits no continuous seminorms and no continuous linear functionals — it has trivial [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988).

### Example 2 {#Example_2}

Let $W_p(\mathbb{D})$ be the space of all complex valued [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448)

$$f(z) = \sum_{n \geq 0} a_n z^n$$

on the unit disc $\mathbb{D}$ such that

$$\sum_n \left|a_n\right|^p < \infty$$

then for $0 < p < 1,$ $W_p(\mathbb{D})$ are F-spaces under the [p-norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-norm):

$$\|f\|_p = \sum_n \left|a_n\right|^p \qquad (0 < p < 1).$$

In fact, $W_p$ is a [quasi-Banach algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-Banach_algebra). Moreover, for any $\zeta$ with $|\zeta| \leq 1$ the map $f \mapsto f(\zeta)$ is a bounded linear (multiplicative functional) on $W_p(\mathbb{D}).$

:::

## Sufficient conditions {#Sufficient_conditions}

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem (Klee (1952))"}

**Theorem[^n1][^o9] (Klee (1952)).**

Let $d$ be any[^o11] metric on a vector space $X$ such that the topology $\tau$ induced by $d$ on $X$ makes $(X, \tau)$ into a topological vector space. If $(X, d)$ is a complete metric space then $(X, \tau)$ is a [complete topological vector space](wikipedia://en/53695856).

:::

## Related properties {#Related_properties}

The [open mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/17395276) implies that if $\tau \text{ and } \tau_2$ are topologies on $X$ that make both $(X, \tau)$ and $\left(X, \tau_2\right)$ into [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856) [metrizable topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/64278078) (for example, Banach or [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801)) and if one topology is [finer or coarser](wikipedia://en/393069) than the other then they must be equal (that is, if $\tau \subseteq \tau_2 \text{ or } \tau_2 \subseteq \tau \text{ then } \tau = \tau_2$).[^n2]

- A linear [almost continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_continuous) map into an F-space whose graph is closed is continuous.[^n3]
- A linear [almost open](wikipedia://en/65738568) map into an F-space whose graph is closed is necessarily an [open map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_map).[^n3]
- A linear continuous [almost open](wikipedia://en/65738568) map from an F-space is necessarily an [open map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_map).[^n4]
- A linear continuous almost open map from an F-space whose image is of the [second category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_category) in the codomain is necessarily a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjection) [open map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_map).[^n3]
