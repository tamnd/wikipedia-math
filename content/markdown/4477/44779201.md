# Nine-point conic

*Image: Nine point conic.svg.* blue solid #a17123 solid #188f61 If $P$ were inside triangle $\triangle ABC$, the nine-point conic would be an ellipse.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nine-point conic"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **nine-point conic** of a [complete quadrangle](wikipedia://en/2605272) is a [conic](wikipedia://en/19008673) that passes through the three diagonal points and the six [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of sides of the complete quadrangle.

:::

The nine-point conic was described by [Maxime Bôcher](wikipedia://en/3180285) in 1892.[^o2] The better-known [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243) is an instance of Bôcher's conic. The [nine-point hyperbola](wikipedia://en/18388859) is another instance.

Bôcher used the four points of the complete quadrangle as three vertices of a triangle with one independent point:

Given a triangle $\triangle ABC$ and a point $P$ in its plane, a conic can be drawn through the following nine points:

the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the sides of $\triangle ABC$,

the midpoints of the lines joining $P$ to the vertices, and

the points where these last named lines cut the sides of the triangle.

The conic is an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) if $P$ lies in the interior of $\triangle ABC$ or in one of the regions of the plane separated from the interior by two sides of the triangle, otherwise the conic is a [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052). Bôcher notes that when $P$ is the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244), one obtains the nine-point circle, and when $P$ is on the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of $\triangle ABC$, then the conic is an equilateral hyperbola.

In 1912 Maud Minthorn showed that the nine-point conic is the locus of the center of a conic through four given points.[^o3]

The nine-point conic with respect to a line $l$ is the conic through the six harmonic conjugates of the intersection of the sides of the complete quadrangle with $l$.
