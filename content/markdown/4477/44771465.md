# David G. Cantor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David G. Cantor"}

**David Geoffrey Cantor** (April 12, 1935 – November 19, 2012) was an American mathematician, specializing in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). The [Cantor–Zassenhaus algorithm](wikipedia://en/6059689) for factoring polynomials is named after him; he and [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825) published it in 1981.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Cantor was born on April 12, 1935.[^o4] He completed his undergraduate studies at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), graduating in 1956, and earned his doctorate from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA) in 1960, where he was supervised by [Basil Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_Gordon) and [Ernst G. Straus](wikipedia://en/6920898).[^o5] He became an assistant professor at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1962, moved back to UCLA in 1964, and retired in 1991. After his retirement, he worked at the Center for Communications Research in [La Jolla, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla).[^o5]

Cantor specialized in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).[^o5] The [Cantor–Zassenhaus algorithm](wikipedia://en/6059689) for factoring polynomials is named after him; he and [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825) published it in 1981.[^o7] He received the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Postdoctoral Fellowship in 1960 and a [Sloan Foundation Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 1968.[^o5] In 2012, he became one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8] At the time of his death, he had been a member of the American Mathematical Society for 54 years.[^o4]

Cantor lived in [San Diego, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California). He died on November 19, 2012, at the age of 77.[^o5][^o4]
