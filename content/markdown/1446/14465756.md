# Otomar Hájek

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Otomar Hájek"}

**Otomar Hájek** (December 31, 1930 – December 18, 2016)[^o2] was a [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his contributions to [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039).

:::

He was born in [Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade) in [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia), moving with his family to [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) in 1935, to the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands) in 1939 and via [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria) and southern France to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) in 1940 where they lived until 1945 when they returned to Prague. After high school in 1949 he studied mathematics at [Charles University in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University_in_Prague), resulting in a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1963 with his thesis *Dynamical Systems in the Plane*. At the same place he joined the mathematics and physics faculty in 1965, before moving in 1968 to [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland) and [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University), where he worked until 1995 as a professor, becoming an [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) in 1996.[^o3]

In the mid-1970s, Hájek also received von Humboldt award at [TH Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt_University_of_Technology), Fachbereich Mathematik.[^o4][^o5]
