# Julian Keilson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Julian Keilson"}

**Julian Keilson** (November 19, 1924 – March 8, 1999 in [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York)) was an American mathematician.[^o2][^o3][^o4] He was known for his work in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). His work in [survival analysis](wikipedia://en/419259) is relevant to many fields, e.g., medical research, parts supply, asset depreciation, rental pricing, etc.[^o5]

:::

He got his [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.) in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) from [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College), and [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc.) and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). His Ph.D. thesis advisor was the Nobel Prize–winning professor of Physics, [Julian Schwinger](wikipedia://en/269980). Next he worked at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) Lincoln Laboratories and [GTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTE) Laboratories before joining the faculty at [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester) (1966–96) where he started the Applied Statistics department in the business school. He also taught at [MIT Sloan School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Sloan_School_of_Management) (1986–92).
