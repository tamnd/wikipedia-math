# Adequate equivalence relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adequate equivalence relation"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **adequate equivalence relation** is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on [algebraic cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_cycles) of smooth [projective varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_varieties) used to obtain a well-working theory of such cycles, and in particular, well-defined [intersection products](wikipedia://en/3148602). [Pierre Samuel](wikipedia://en/2604194) formalized the concept of an adequate equivalence relation in 1958.[^o2] Since then it has become central to theory of motives. For every adequate equivalence relation, one may define the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [pure motives](wikipedia://en/430680) with respect to that relation.

:::

Possible (and useful) adequate equivalence relations include *rational*, *algebraic*, *homological* and *numerical equivalence*. They are called "adequate" because dividing out by the equivalence relation is [functorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functorial), i.e. push-forward (with change of codimension) and pull-back of cycles is well-defined. Codimension 1 cycles modulo rational equivalence form the classical [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055) modulo linear equivalence. All cycles modulo rational equivalence form the [Chow ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_ring).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *Z*<sup>\*</sup>(*X*) := **Z**\[*X*\] be the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) on the algebraic cycles of *X*. Then an adequate equivalence relation is a family of [equivalence relations](wikipedia://en/9259), *~<sub>X</sub>* on *Z*<sup>\*</sup>(*X*), one for each smooth projective variety *X*, satisfying the following three conditions:

1. (Linearity) The equivalence relation is compatible with addition of cycles.
1. ([Moving lemma](wikipedia://en/38307346)) If $\alpha, \beta \in Z^{*}(X)$ are cycles on *X*, then there exists a cycle $\alpha' \in Z^{*}(X)$ such that $\alpha$ *~<sub>X</sub>* $\alpha'$ and $\alpha'$ intersects $\beta$ properly.
1. (Push-forwards) Let $\alpha \in Z^{*}(X)$ and $\beta \in Z^{*}(X \times Y)$ be cycles such that $\beta$ intersects $\alpha \times Y$ properly. If $\alpha$ *~<sub>X</sub>* 0, then $(\pi_Y)_{*}(\beta \cdot (\alpha \times Y))$ *~<sub>Y</sub>* 0, where $\pi_Y : X \times Y \to Y$ is the projection.

The push-forward cycle in the last axiom is often denoted

$$\beta(\alpha) := (\pi_Y)_{*}(\beta \cdot (\alpha \times Y))$$

If $\beta$ is the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427), then this reduces to the push-forward of the function. The generalizations of functions from *X* to *Y* to cycles on *X × Y* are known as [correspondences](wikipedia://en/57389807). The last axiom allows us to push forward cycles by a correspondence.

:::

## Examples of equivalence relations {#Examples_of_equivalence_relations}

The most common equivalence relations, listed from strongest to weakest, are gathered in the following table.

::: {#o6 .table cue=table}

|  | definition | remarks |
|---|---|---|
| rational equivalence | *Z ~*<sub>rat</sub>*Z'* if there is a cycle *V* on *X* × [**P**<sup>1</sup>](wikipedia://en/398578) [flat](wikipedia://en/542520) over **P**<sup>1</sup>, such that \[*V* ∩ *X* × {0}\] − \[*V* ∩ *X* × {∞}\] = \[*Z*\] − \[*Z'*\]. | the finest adequate equivalence relation (Lemma 3.2.2.1 in Yves André's book[^o7]) "∩" denotes intersection in the cycle-theoretic sense (i.e. with multiplicities) and \[*.*\] denotes the cycle associated to a subscheme. see also [Chow ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_ring) |
| algebraic equivalence | *Z ~*<sub>alg</sub> *Z* if there is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) *C* and a cycle *V* on *X* × *C* flat over *C*, such that \[*V* ∩ *X* × {*c*}\] − \[*V* ∩ *X* × {*d*}\] = \[*Z*\] − \[*Z'*\] for two points *c* and *d* on the curve. | Strictly stronger than homological equivalence, as measured by the [Griffiths group](wikipedia://en/44750391). See also [Néron–Severi group](wikipedia://en/1873706). |
| smash-nilpotence equivalence | *Z ~*<sub>sn</sub> *Z* if *Z* − *Z* is smash-nilpotent on *X*, that is, if $(Z - Z')^{\otimes n}$ *~*<sub>rat</sub> 0 on *X*<sup>*n*</sup> for *n* >> 0. | introduced by Voevodsky in 1995.[^o8] |
| homological equivalence | for a given [Weil cohomology](wikipedia://en/2960213) *H*, *Z ~*<sub>hom</sub> *Z* if the image of the cycles under the cycle class map agrees | depends a priori of the choice of *H*, not assuming the [standard conjecture](wikipedia://en/10470079) *D* |
| numerical equivalence | *Z ~*<sub>num</sub> *Z* if deg(*Z* ∩ *T*) = deg(*Z* ∩ *T*), where *T* is any cycle such that dim*T* = codim*Z* (The intersection is a linear combination of points and we add the intersection multiplicities at each point to get the degree.) | the coarsest equivalence relation (Exercise 3.2.7.2 in Yves André's book[^o9]) |

:::
