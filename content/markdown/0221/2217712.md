# Solenoid (mathematics)

*This page discusses a class of topological groups. For the wrapped loop of wire, see [Solenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenoid).*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Solenoid"}

*Image: Smale-Williams Solenoid Large.png.* The Smale-Williams solenoid.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **solenoid** is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) (i.e. a [continuum](wikipedia://en/24758520)) that may be obtained as the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of an inverse system of [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315) and [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564)

:::

$$f_i: S_{i+1} \to S_i \quad \forall i \ge 0$$

where each $S_i$ is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) and *f*<sub>*i*</sub> is the map that uniformly wraps the circle $S_{i+1}$ for $n_{i+1}$ times ($n_{i+1} \geq 2$) around the circle $S_i$.[^o2] This construction can be carried out geometrically in the three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>3</sup>. A solenoid is a one-dimensional homogeneous [indecomposable continuum](wikipedia://en/3261244) that has the structure of an [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) compact [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315).

Solenoids were first introduced by [Vietoris](wikipedia://en/105043) for the $n_i = 2$ case,[^o3] and by [van Dantzig](wikipedia://en/528853) the $n_i = n$ case, where $n\geq 2$ is fixed.[^o4] Such a solenoid arises as a one-dimensional **expanding attractor**, or **Smale–Williams attractor**, and forms an important example in the theory of [hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_dynamics) [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

### Geometric construction and the Smale–Williams attractor {#Geometric_construction_and_the_Smale–Williams_attractor}

*Image: Smale-Williams Solenoid.png.* A solid torus wrapped twice around inside another solid torus in **R**<sup>3</sup>

*Image: Solenoid.gif.* The first six steps in the construction of the Smale-Williams attractor.

Each solenoid may be constructed as the intersection of a nested system of embedded solid tori in **R**<sup>3</sup>.

Fix a sequence of natural numbers {*n*<sub>*i*</sub>}, *n*<sub>*i*</sub> ≥ 2. Let *T*<sub>0</sub> = *S*<sup>1</sup> × *D* be a [solid torus](wikipedia://en/1251473). For each *i* ≥ 0, choose a solid torus *T*<sub>*i*+1</sub> that is wrapped longitudinally *n*<sub>*i*</sub> times inside the solid torus *T*<sub>*i*</sub>. Then their intersection

$$\Lambda=\bigcap_{i\ge 0}T_i$$

is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the solenoid constructed as the inverse limit of the system of circles with the maps determined by the sequence {*n*<sub>*i*</sub>}.

Here is a variant of this construction isolated by [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294) as an example of an **expanding attractor** in the theory of smooth dynamical systems. Denote the angular coordinate on the circle *S*<sup>1</sup> by *t* (it is defined mod 2π) and consider the complex coordinate *z* on the two-dimensional [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) *D*. Let *f* be the map of the solid torus *T* = *S*<sup>1</sup> × *D* into itself given by the explicit formula

$$f(t,z) = \left(2t, \tfrac{1}{4}z + \tfrac{1}{2}e^{it}\right).$$

This map is a smooth [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) of *T* into itself that preserves the [foliation](wikipedia://en/371227) by meridional disks (the constants 1/2 and 1/4 are somewhat arbitrary, but it is essential that 1/4 \< 1/2 and 1/4 + 1/2 \< 1). If *T* is imagined as a rubber tube, the map *f* stretches it in the longitudinal direction, contracts each meridional disk, and wraps the deformed tube twice inside *T* with twisting, but without self-intersections. The [hyperbolic set](wikipedia://en/2658073) *Λ* of the discrete dynamical system (*T*, *f*) is the intersection of the sequence of nested solid tori described above, where *T*<sub>*i*</sub> is the image of *T* under the *i*th iteration of the map *f*. This set is a one-dimensional (in the sense of [topological dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_dimension)) [attractor](wikipedia://en/191094), and the dynamics of *f* on *Λ* has the following interesting properties:

- meridional disks are the [stable manifolds](wikipedia://en/5466649), each of which intersects *Λ* over a [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172)
- [periodic points](wikipedia://en/4941621) of *f* are [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_subset) in *Λ*
- the map *f* is [topologically transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_transitive) on *Λ*

General theory of solenoids and expanding attractors, not necessarily one-dimensional, was developed by R. F. Williams and involves a projective system of infinitely many copies of a compact [branched manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branched_manifold) in place of the circle, together with an expanding self-[immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953).

### Construction in toroidal coordinates {#Construction_in_toroidal_coordinates}

In the [toroidal coordinates](wikipedia://en/12700262) with radius $R$, the solenoid can be parametrized by $t\in \mathbb{R}$ as

$$\zeta = 2\pi t, \quad re^{i\theta} = \sum_{k=1}^\infty r_k e^{2\pi i \omega_k t}$$

where

$$\omega_k = \frac{1}{n_1 \cdots n_k}, \quad r_k = R \delta_1 \cdots \delta_k$$

Here, $\delta_k$ are adjustable shape-parameters, with constraint $0 < \delta < 1 - \frac{1}{1 + \sin \frac{\pi}{n_k}}$. In particular, $\delta = \frac{1}{2n_k}$ works.

Let $S\subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be the solenoid constructed this way, then the topology of the solenoid is just the subset topology induced by the [Euclidean topology](wikipedia://en/1482085) on $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Since the parametrization is bijective, we can pullback the topology on $S$ to $\mathbb{R}$, which makes $\mathbb{R}$ itself the solenoid. This allows us to construct the inverse limit maps explicitly:

$$g_k: \mathbb{R} \to S_k, \quad g_k(t) = (r, \theta, \zeta)\text{ in toroidal coordinates, where } \zeta = 2\pi t, \quad re^{i\theta} = \sum_{k=1}^k r_k e^{2\pi i \omega_k t}$$

### Construction by symbolic dynamics {#Construction_by_symbolic_dynamics}

Viewed as a set, the solenoid is just a Cantor-continuum of circles, wired together in a particular way. This suggests to us the construction by [symbolic dynamics](wikipedia://en/1986263), where we start with a circle as a "racetrack", and append an "odometer" to keep track of which circle we are on.

Define $S = S^1 \times \prod_{k=1}^\infty \mathbb{Z}_{n_k}$ as the solenoid. Next, define addition on the odometer $\mathbb{Z} \times \prod_{k=1}^\infty \mathbb{Z}_{n_k} \to \prod_{k=1}^\infty \mathbb{Z}_{n_k}$, in the same way as [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423). Next, define addition on the solenoid $+: \mathbb{R} \times S \to S$ by

$$r + (\theta, n) = ((r + \theta \mod 1), \lfloor r + \theta \rfloor + n)$$

The topology on the solenoid is generated by the basis containing the subsets $S' \times Z'_{(m_1, ..., m_k)}$, where $S'$ is any open interval in $S^1$, and $Z'_{(m_1, ..., m_k)}$ is the set of all elements of $\prod_{k=1}^\infty \mathbb{Z}_{n_k}$ starting with the initial segment $(m_1, ..., m_k)$.

## Pathological properties {#Pathological_properties}

Solenoids are [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [metrizable spaces](wikipedia://en/19738) that are [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), but not [locally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_connected) or [path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected). This is reflected in their [pathological](wikipedia://en/372742) behavior with respect to various [homology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theories), in contrast with the standard properties of homology for [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686). In [Čech homology](wikipedia://en/856614), one can construct a non-exact [long homology sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence) using a solenoid. In [Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steenrod)-style homology theories,[^o11] the 0th homology group of a solenoid may have a fairly complicated structure, even though a solenoid is a connected space.
