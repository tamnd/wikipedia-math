# Icositrigon

Regular polygon stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Icositrigon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **icositrigon** (or **icosikaitrigon**) or 23-gon is a 23-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621). The icositrigon has the distinction of being the smallest regular polygon that is not [neusis constructible](wikipedia://en/3752339).

:::

## Regular icositrigon {#Regular_icositrigon}

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) icositrigon* is represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {23}.

A regular icositrigon has [internal angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) of $\frac{3780}{23}$ degrees, with an area of $A = \frac{23}{4}a^2 \cot \frac{\pi}{23} = 23r^2 \tan \frac{\pi}{23} \simeq 41.8344\,a^2,$ where $a$ is side length and $r$ is the inradius, or [apothem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothem).

The regular icositrigon is not [constructible](wikipedia://en/523430) with a [compass and straightedge](wikipedia://en/61229) or [angle trisection](wikipedia://en/91111), on account of the [number 23](wikipedia://en/362203) being neither a [Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime) nor [Pierpont prime](wikipedia://en/2595060#Polygon_construction). In addition, the regular icositrigon is the [smallest regular polygon that is not constructible even with neusis](wikipedia://en/3752339#Use_of_the_neusis).

Concerning the nonconstructability of the regular icositrigon, A. Baragar (2002) showed it is not possible to construct a regular 23-gon using only a compass and twice-notched straightedge by demonstrating that every point constructible with said method lies in a tower of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) over $\mathbb{Q}$ such that $\mathbb{Q} = K_0 \subset K_1 \subset \dots \subset K_n = K$, being a sequence of nested fields in which the degree of the extension at each step is 2, 3, 5, or 6.

Suppose $\alpha$ in $\mathbb{C}$ is constructible using a compass and twice-notched straightedge. Then $\alpha$ belongs to a field $K$ that lies in a tower of fields $\mathbb{Q} = K_0 \subset K_1 \subset \dots \subset K_n = K$ for which the index $[K_j: K_{j - 1}]$ at each step is 2, 3, 5, or 6. In particular, if $N = [K : \mathbb{Q}]$, then the only primes dividing $N$ are 2, 3, and 5. (Theorem 5.1)

If we can construct the regular p-gon, then we can construct $\zeta_p = e^\frac{2\pi i}{p}$, which is the root of an [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) of degree $p - 1$. By Theorem 5.1, $\zeta_p$ lies in a field $K$ of degree $N$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, where the only primes that divide $N$ are 2, 3, and 5. But $\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_p]$ is a subfield of $K$, so $p - 1$ divides $N$. In particular, for $p = 23$, $N$ must be divisible by 11, and for $p = 29$, *N* must be divisible by 7.[^o4]

This result establishes, considering prime-power regular polygons less than the 100-gon, that it is impossible to construct the 23-, 29-, 43-, 47-, 49-, 53-, 59-, 67-, 71-, 79-, 83-, and 89-gons with neusis. But it is not strong enough to decide the cases of the [11-](wikipedia://en/614840), 25-, 31-, 41-, and 61-gons. Elliot Benjamin and Chip Snyder discovered in 2014 that the regular hendecagon (11-gon) is neusis constructible; the remaining cases are still open.[^o5]

An icositrigon is not [origami constructible](wikipedia://en/232840#Constructions) either, because 23 is not a Pierpont prime, nor a [power of two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_two) or [three](wikipedia://en/14214617).[^o6] It can be constructed using the [quadratrix of Hippias](wikipedia://en/40429150), [Archimedean spiral](wikipedia://en/61559), and other [auxiliary curves](wikipedia://en/91111#With_an_auxiliary_curve); yet this is true for all regular polygons.[^o7]
