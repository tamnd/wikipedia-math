# Gaussian noise

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaussian noise"}

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) theory, **Gaussian noise**, named after [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), is a kind of [signal noise](wikipedia://en/53675110) that has a [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (pdf) equal to that of the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) (which is also known as the Gaussian distribution).[^o9][^o6] In other words, the values that the noise can take are Gaussian-distributed. A special case is *white Gaussian noise*, in which the values at any pair of time points are [identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iid) and [statistically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistically_independent) (and hence [uncorrelated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncorrelated)).

:::

The probability density function $p$ of a Gaussian random variable $z$ is given by:

$$p(z) = \frac 1 {\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{(z-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} }$$

where—in the case of digital imaging—$z$ represents the grey level, $\mu$ the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) grey value and $\sigma$ its [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590).[^o8]

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In [communication channel](wikipedia://en/156700) testing and modelling, Gaussian noise is used as additive [white noise](wikipedia://en/46182) to generate [additive white Gaussian noise](wikipedia://en/202097).

In [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications) and [computer networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_networking), communication channels can be affected by [wideband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wideband) Gaussian noise coming from many natural sources, such as the thermal vibrations of atoms in conductors (referred to as thermal noise or [Johnson–Nyquist noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson–Nyquist_noise)), [shot noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_noise), [black-body radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-body_radiation) from the earth and other warm objects, and from celestial sources such as the Sun.

In [digital images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_image) sources of Gaussian noise arise during acquisition e.g. [sensor noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_noise) caused by poor illumination and/or high temperature, and/or transmission e.g. [electronic circuit noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_noise_level).[^o8] In [digital image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_image_processing) Gaussian noise can be reduced using a [spatial filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_filter), though when smoothing an image, an undesirable outcome may result in the blurring of fine-scaled image edges and details because they also correspond to blocked high frequencies. Conventional spatial filtering techniques for [noise removal](wikipedia://en/476836) include: mean ([convolution](wikipedia://en/7519)) filtering, [median filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_filter) and [Gaussian smoothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_smoothing).[^o9][^o7]
