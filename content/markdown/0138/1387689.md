# Table of Lie groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Table of Lie groups"}

This article gives a table of some common [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and their associated [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).

:::

The following are noted: the [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) properties of the group ([dimension](wikipedia://en/8398); [connectedness](wikipedia://en/6233); [compactness](wikipedia://en/6042); the nature of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004); and whether or not they are [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected)) as well as on their algebraic properties ([abelian](wikipedia://en/2974); [simple](wikipedia://en/292831); [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group)).

For more examples of Lie groups and other related topics see the [list of simple Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_simple_Lie_groups); the [Bianchi classification](wikipedia://en/7811800) of groups of up to three dimensions; see [classification of low-dimensional real Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/59500886) for up to four dimensions; and the [list of Lie group topics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lie_group_topics).

## Real Lie groups and their algebras {#Real_Lie_groups_and_their_algebras}

Column legend

- **Cpt**: Is this group *G* [compact](wikipedia://en/6042)? (Yes or No)
- **$\pi_0$**: Gives the [group of components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_components) of *G*. The order of the component group gives the number of [connected components](wikipedia://en/6233). The group is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) if and only if the component group is [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059) (denoted by 0).
- **$\pi_1$**: Gives the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of *G* whenever *G* is connected. The group is [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) if and only if the fundamental group is [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059) (denoted by 0).
- **UC**: If *G* is not simply connected, gives the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of *G*.

## Complex Lie groups and their algebras {#Complex_Lie_groups_and_their_algebras}

Note that a "complex Lie group" is defined as a complex analytic manifold that is also a group whose multiplication and inversion are each given by a holomorphic map. The dimensions in the table below are dimensions over **C**. Note that every complex Lie group/algebra can also be viewed as a real Lie group/algebra of twice the dimension.

The Lie algebra of affine transformations of dimension two, in fact, exist for any field. An instance has already been listed in the first table for real Lie algebras.
