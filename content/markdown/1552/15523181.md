# Riemannian Penrose inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riemannian Penrose inequality"}

In mathematical [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), the **Penrose inequality**, first conjectured by Sir [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193), estimates the mass of a [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) in terms of the total area of its [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_holes) and is a generalization of the [positive mass theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_mass_theorem). The **Riemannian Penrose inequality** is an important special case. Specifically, if (*M*, *g*) is an [asymptotically flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotically_flat) Riemannian [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) with nonnegative [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) and [ADM mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADM_mass) *m*, and *A* is the area of the outermost [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) (possibly with multiple [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29)), then the Riemannian Penrose inequality asserts

:::

$$m \geq \sqrt{\frac{A}{16\pi}}.$$

This is purely a geometrical fact, and it corresponds to the case of a complete three-dimensional, [space-like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-like), [totally geodesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_geodesic) [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of a (3 + 1)-dimensional spacetime. Such a submanifold is often called a time-symmetric initial data set for a spacetime. The condition of (*M*, *g*) having nonnegative scalar curvature is equivalent to the spacetime obeying the [dominant energy condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_energy_condition).

This inequality was first proved by [Gerhard Huisken](wikipedia://en/39508202) and [Tom Ilmanen](wikipedia://en/76365326) in 1997 in the case where *A* is the area of the largest component of the outermost minimal surface. Their proof relied on the machinery of weakly defined [inverse mean curvature flow](wikipedia://en/15710792), which they developed. In 1999, [Hubert Bray](wikipedia://en/47433672) gave the first complete proof of the above inequality using a conformal [flow](wikipedia://en/14617515) of metrics. Both of the papers were published in 2001.

## Physical motivation {#Physical_motivation}

The original physical argument that led Penrose to conjecture such an inequality invoked the [Hawking area theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking_area_theorem) and the [cosmic censorship hypothesis](wikipedia://en/7609).

## Case of equality {#Case_of_equality}

Both the Bray and Huisken–Ilmanen proofs of the Riemannian Penrose inequality state that under the hypotheses, if

$$m = \sqrt{\frac{A}{16\pi}},$$

then the manifold in question is isometric to a slice of the [Schwarzschild spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzschild_spacetime) outside its outermost minimal surface, which is a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) of [Schwarzschild radius](wikipedia://en/206115).

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Penrose conjecture"}

## Penrose conjecture {#Penrose_conjecture}

More generally, Penrose conjectured that an inequality as above should hold for spacelike submanifolds of spacetimes that are not necessarily time-symmetric. In this case, nonnegative scalar curvature is replaced with the [dominant energy condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_energy_condition), and one possibility is to replace the minimal surface condition with an [apparent horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_horizon) condition. Proving such an inequality remains an open problem in general relativity, called the **Penrose conjecture.**

:::
