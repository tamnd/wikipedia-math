# Dévissage

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dévissage"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **dévissage** is a technique introduced by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) for proving statements about [coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/603780) on [Noetherian schemes](wikipedia://en/2783891). Dévissage is an adaptation of a certain kind of [Noetherian induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noetherian_induction). It has many applications, including the proof of [generic flatness](wikipedia://en/26374518) and the proof that higher [direct images](wikipedia://en/3022744) of coherent sheaves under proper [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphisms) are coherent.

:::

Laurent Gruson and [Michel Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928) extended this concept to the relative situation, that is, to the situation where the scheme under consideration is not necessarily noetherian, but instead admits a finitely presented morphism to another scheme. They did this by defining an object called a relative dévissage which is well-suited to certain kinds of inductive arguments. They used this technique to give a new criterion for a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) to be [flat](wikipedia://en/492616). As a consequence, they were able to simplify and generalize the results of EGA IV 11 on [descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent_%28category_theory%29) of flatness.[^o2]

The word *dévissage* is French for *unscrewing*.

## Grothendieck's dévissage theorem {#Grothendieck's_dévissage_theorem}

Let *X* be a noetherian scheme. Let **C** be a collection of objects of the category of coherent *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-modules which contains the zero sheaf and which has the property that, for any short exact sequence $0 \to A' \to A \to A'' \to 0$ of coherent sheaves, if two of *A*, *A*′, and *A*′′ are in **C**, then so is the third. Let *X*′ be a closed subspace of the underlying [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) of *X*. Suppose that for every irreducible closed subset *Y* of *X*′, there exists a coherent sheaf *G* in **C** whose fiber at the [generic point](wikipedia://en/3043978) *y* of *Y* is a one-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) *k*(*y*). Then every coherent *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-module whose support is contained in *X*′ is contained in **C**.[^o5]

In the particular case that *X*′ = *X*, the theorem says that **C** is the category of coherent *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-modules. This is the setting in which the theorem is most often applied, but the statement above makes it possible to prove the theorem by noetherian induction.

A variation on the theorem is that if every direct factor of an object in **C** is again in **C**, then the condition that the fiber of *G* at *x* be one-dimensional can be replaced by the condition that the fiber is non-zero.[^o6]

:::

## Gruson and Raynaud's relative dévissages {#Gruson_and_Raynaud's_relative_dévissages}

Suppose that f : *X* → *S* is a finitely presented morphism of affine schemes, *s* is a point of *S*, and *M* is a finite type *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-module. If *n* is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474), then Gruson and Raynaud define an ***S*-dévissage in dimension *n*** to consist of:

1. A closed finitely presented subscheme *X*′ of *X* containing the closed subscheme defined by the annihilator of *M* and such that the dimension of *X*′ ∩ f<sup>−1</sup>(*s*) is less than or equal to *n*.
1. A scheme *T* and a factorization *X*′ → *T* → *S* of the restriction of *f* to *X*′ such that *X*′ → *T* is a [finite morphism](wikipedia://en/2139357) and *T* → *S* is a smooth affine morphism with geometrically integral fibers of dimension *n*. Denote the generic point of *T* ×<sub>*S*</sub> *k*(*s*) by τ and the pushforward of *M* to *T* by *N*.
1. A free finite type *O*<sub>*T*</sub>-module *L* and a homomorphism α : *L* → *N* such that α ⊗ *k*(τ) is bijective.

If *n*<sub>1</sub>, *n*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *n*<sub>*r*</sub> is a strictly decreasing sequence of natural numbers, then an *S*-dévissage in dimensions *n*<sub>1</sub>, *n*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *n*<sub>*r*</sub> is defined recursively as:

1. An *S*-dévissage in dimension *n*<sub>1</sub>. Denote the [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) of α by *P*<sub>1</sub>.
1. An *S*-dévissage in dimensions *n*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *n*<sub>*r*</sub> of *P*<sub>1</sub>.

The dévissage is said to lie between dimensions *n*<sub>1</sub> and *n*<sub>*r*</sub>. *r* is called the **length** of the dévissage. The last step of the recursion consists of a dévissage in dimension *n*<sub>*r*</sub> which includes a morphism α<sub>*r*</sub> : *L*<sub>*r*</sub> → *N*<sub>*r*</sub>. Denote the cokernel of this morphism by *P*<sub>*r*</sub>. The dévissage is called **total** if *P*<sub>*r*</sub> is zero.[^o8]

Gruson and Raynaud prove in wide generality that locally, dévissages always exist. Specifically, let *f* : (*X*, *x*) → (*S*, *s*) be a finitely presented morphism of pointed schemes and *M* be an *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-module of finite type whose fiber at *x* is non-zero. Set *n* equal to the dimension of *M* ⊗ *k*(*s*) and *r* to the codepth of *M* at *s*, that is, to *n* − depth(*M* ⊗ *k*(*s*)).[^o9] Then there exist affine étale neighborhoods *X*′ of *x* and *S*′ of *s*, together with points *x*′ and *s*′ lifting *x* and *s*, such that the residue field extensions *k*(*x*) → *k*(*x*′) and *k*(*s*) → *k*(*s*′) are trivial, the map *X*′ → *S* factors through *S*′, this factorization sends *x*′ to *s*′, and that the pullback of *M* to *X*′ admits a total *S*′-dévissage at *x*′ in dimensions between *n* and *n* − *r*.
