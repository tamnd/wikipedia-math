# Huguette Delavault

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Huguette Delavault"}

**Huguette Delavault** (15 January 1924 – 2 April 2003) was a French mathematician, specialising in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).[^o3][^o4][^o5] She co-founded [Femmes et Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femmes_et_Sciences).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hugette Delavault was born on 15 January 1924, in [Andilly, Charente-Maritime.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andilly,_Charente-Maritime)[^o4][^o5] Her parents were both teachers.[^o3] She studied at a school for teachers, the École normale d'instituteurs in [La Rochelle, France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rochelle,_France), from 1940 to 1943, and then became a student at the École normale supérieure de Fontenay-aux-Roses from 1946 to 1949.[^o3][^o4][^o5] After interrupting her studies for health reasons,[^o5] in 1952 she passed her [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in mathematics.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Delavault became a researcher at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) (CNRS) from 1952 to 1958, while earning a doctorate in mathematics in 1957 from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) under the supervision of [Henri Villat](wikipedia://en/32126284); her dissertation applied the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) and [Hankel transform](wikipedia://en/1452960) to the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) and [Maxwell's equations](wikipedia://en/19737), using [cylindrical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_coordinates).[^o3][^o5]

She became a researcher at the [University of Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rennes) in 1958, was promoted to professor in 1962.[^o3] The university's mathematics department was unusual in 1960 because it had a relatively high percentage of women. There were fifteen professors and three of them were women. Delavault was one of them and the other two were [Marie Charpentier](wikipedia://en/55849176) and [Paulette Libermann](wikipedia://en/46726637). Charpentier taught mechanics and Libermann was known for differential geometry.[^o9]

In 1970, Delavault became a professor at the [École nationale supérieure d'ingénieurs de Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_nationale_supérieure_d'ingénieurs_de_Caen).[^o3][^o5] She retired in 1984.[^o3]

Delavault died on 2 April 2003.[^o5]

## Activism and service {#Activism_and_service}

From 1976 onward Delavault was a prominent activist for feminist causes, including popularising science and mathematics among women and providing equal opportunities for them both in the academy and in the public sector.[^o3][^o4]

She served as deputy director of the École normale supérieure de Fontenay-aux-Roses from 1976 to 1980, and was twice president of l'Association française des femmes diplômées des universités (the French association for university women).[^o3]

Delavault co-founded [Femmes et Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femmes_et_Sciences) with [Françoise Cyrot-Lackmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Cyrot-Lackmann), [Claudine Hermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudine_Hermann), [Françoise Gaspard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Gaspard) and [Colette Kreder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colette_Kreder) in 2000.[^o11]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Delavault became an officer in the [Order of Academic Palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Palmes_Académiques) in 1967. In 1971 she became a chevalier (knight) in the [French National Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Order_of_Merit_%28France%29), and in 1995 a chevalier in the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

There is a walkway on the University of Toulouse's campus which is named in her honour her.[^o13]
