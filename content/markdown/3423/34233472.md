# Minimal ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minimal ideal"}

In the branch of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a **minimal right ideal** of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is a non-[zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_ideal) right [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) which contains no other non-zero right ideal. Likewise, a **minimal left ideal** is a non-zero left ideal of *R* containing no other non-zero left ideals of *R*, and a **minimal ideal** of *R* is a non-zero ideal containing no other non-zero two-sided ideal of *R* (Isaacs 2009, p. 190).

:::

In other words, minimal right ideals are [minimal elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_element) of the [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) (poset) of non-zero right ideals of *R* ordered by [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29). The reader is cautioned that outside of this context, some posets of ideals may admit the zero ideal, and so the zero ideal could potentially be a minimal element in that poset. This is the case for the poset of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) of a ring, which may include the zero ideal as a [minimal prime ideal](wikipedia://en/16143832).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The definition of a minimal right ideal *N* of a ring *R* is equivalent to the following conditions:

- *N* is non-zero and if *K* is a right ideal of *R* with {0} ⊆ *K* ⊆ *N*, then either *K* = {0} or *K* = *N*.
- *N* is a [simple](wikipedia://en/29430) right *R*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410).

Minimal ideals are the [dual notion](wikipedia://en/609737) to [maximal ideals](wikipedia://en/48164).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Many standard facts on minimal ideals can be found in standard texts such as (Anderson Fuller 1992), (Isaacs 2009), (Lam 2001), and (Lam 1999).

- In a [ring with unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_with_unity), [maximal](wikipedia://en/48164) right ideals always exist. In contrast, minimal right, left, or two-sided ideals in a ring with unity need not exist.
- The right [socle of a ring](wikipedia://en/3571350#Socle_of_a_module) $\mathrm{soc}(R_R)$ is an important structure defined in terms of the minimal right ideals of *R*.
- Rings for which every right ideal contains a minimal right ideal are exactly the rings with an essential right socle.
- Any right [Artinian ring](wikipedia://en/489273) or right [Kasch ring](wikipedia://en/34212423) has a minimal right ideal.
- [Domains](wikipedia://en/487627) that are not [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067) have no minimal right ideals.
- In rings with unity, minimal right ideals are necessarily [principal right ideals](wikipedia://en/145126), because for any nonzero *x* in a minimal right ideal *N*, the set *xR* is a nonzero right ideal of *R* inside *N*, and so *xR* = *N*.
- **Brauer's lemma:** Any minimal right ideal *N* in a ring *R* satisfies *N*<sup>2</sup> = {0} or *N* = *eR* for some [idempotent element](wikipedia://en/361940) *e* of *R* (Lam 2001, p. 162).
- If *N*<sub>1</sub> and *N*<sub>2</sub> are non-isomorphic minimal right ideals of *R*, then the product *N*<sub>1</sub>*N*<sub>2</sub> equals {0}.
- If *N*<sub>1</sub> and *N*<sub>2</sub> are distinct minimal ideals of a ring *R*, then *N*<sub>1</sub>*N*<sub>2</sub> = {0}.
- A [simple ring](wikipedia://en/443387) with a minimal right ideal is a [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring).
- In a [semiprime ring](wikipedia://en/7739701), there exists a minimal right ideal if and only if there exists a minimal left ideal (Lam 2001, p. 174).

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

A non-zero [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) *N* of a right module *M* is called a **minimal submodule** if it contains no other non-zero submodules of *M*. Equivalently, *N* is a non-zero submodule of *M* which is a [simple module](wikipedia://en/29430). This can also be extended to [bimodules](wikipedia://en/492612) by calling a non-zero sub-bimodule *N* a **minimal sub-bimodule** of *M* if *N* contains no other non-zero sub-bimodules.

If the module *M* is taken to be the right *R*-module *R*<sub>*R*</sub>, then the minimal submodules are exactly the minimal right ideals of *R*. Likewise, the minimal left ideals of *R* are precisely the minimal submodules of the left module <sub>*R*</sub>*R*. In the case of two-sided ideals, we see that the minimal ideals of *R* are exactly the minimal sub-bimodules of the bimodule <sub>*R*</sub>*R*<sub>*R*</sub>.

Just as with rings, there is no guarantee that minimal submodules exist in a module. Minimal submodules can be used to define the [socle of a module](wikipedia://en/3571350#Socle_of_a_module).

:::
