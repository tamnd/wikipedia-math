# Non-Hausdorff manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-Hausdorff manifold"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), it is a usual axiom of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) to be a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637). In [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), this axiom is relaxed, and one studies **non-Hausdorff manifolds**: spaces [locally homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_homeomorphic) to [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), but not necessarily Hausdorff.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Bug-eyed line"}

### Line with two origins {#Line_with_two_origins}

The most familiar non-Hausdorff manifold is the **line with two origins**,[^n1] or **bug-eyed line**. This is the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) of two copies of the real line, $\mathbb{R} \times \{a\}$ and $\mathbb{R} \times \{b\}$ (with $a \neq b$), obtained by identifying points $(x,a)$ and $(x,b)$ whenever $x \neq 0.$

An equivalent description of the space is to take the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) $\mathbb{R}$ and replace the origin $0$ with two origins $0_a$ and $0_b.$ The subspace $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$ retains its usual Euclidean topology. And a [local base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_base) of open neighborhoods at each origin $0_i$ is formed by the sets $(U\setminus\{0\})\cup\{0_i\}$ with $U$ an open neighborhood of $0$ in $\mathbb{R}.$

For each origin $0_i$ the subspace obtained from $\mathbb{R}$ by replacing $0$ with $0_i$ is an open neighborhood of $0_i$ homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}.$[^n1] Since every point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to the Euclidean line, the space is [locally Euclidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_Euclidean). In particular, it is [locally Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_Hausdorff), in the sense that each point has a Hausdorff neighborhood. But the space is not Hausdorff, as every neighborhood of $0_a$ intersects every neighbourhood of $0_b.$ It is however a [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721).

The space is [second countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_countable).

The space exhibits several phenomena that do not happen in Hausdorff spaces:

- The space is [path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected) but not [arc connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_connected). In particular, to get a path from one origin to the other one can first move left from $0_a$ to $-1$ within the line through the first origin, and then move back to the right from $-1$ to $0_b$ within the line through the second origin. But it is impossible to join the two origins with an arc, which is an injective path; intuitively, if one moves first to the left, one has to eventually backtrack and move back to the right.
- The intersection of two [compact sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) need not be compact. For example, the sets $[-1,0)\cup\{0_a\}$ and $[-1,0)\cup\{0_b\}$ are compact, but their intersection $[-1,0)$ is not.
- The space is [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) in the sense that every point has a local base of compact neighborhoods. But the line through one origin does not contain a closed neighborhood of that origin, as any neighborhood of one origin contains the other origin in its closure. So the space is not a [regular space](wikipedia://en/53058), and even though every point has at least one closed compact neighborhood, the origin points do not admit a local base of closed compact neighborhoods.

The space does not have the homotopy type of a [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex), or of any Hausdorff space.[^n2]

:::

### Line with many origins {#Line_with_many_origins}

The **line with many origins**[^n3] is similar to the line with two origins, but with an arbitrary number of origins. It is constructed by taking an arbitrary set $S$ with the discrete topology and taking the quotient space of $\mathbb{R}\times S$ that identifies points $(x,\alpha)$ and $(x,\beta)$ whenever $x\ne 0.$ Equivalently, it can be obtained from $\mathbb{R}$ by replacing the origin $0$ with many origins $0_\alpha,$ one for each $\alpha\in S.$ The neighborhoods of each origin are described as in the two origin case.

If there are infinitely many origins, the space illustrates that the closure of a compact set need not be compact in general. For example, the closure of the compact set $A=[-1,0)\cup\{0_\alpha\}\cup(0,1]$ is the set $A\cup\{0_\beta:\beta\in S\}$ obtained by adding all the origins to $A$, and that closure is not compact. From being locally Euclidean, such a space is [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) in the sense that every point has a local base of compact neighborhoods. But the origin points do not have any closed compact neighborhood.

### Branching line {#Branching_line}

Similar to the line with two origins is the **branching line**.

This is the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) of two copies of the real line

$$\mathbb{R} \times \{a\} \quad \text{ and } \quad \mathbb{R} \times \{b\}$$

with the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259)

$$(x, a) \sim (x, b) \quad \text{ if } \; x < 0.$$

This space has a single point for each negative real number $r$ and two points $x_a, x_b$ for every non-negative number: it has a "fork" at zero.

### Etale space {#Etale_space}

The [etale space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etale_space) of a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466), such as the sheaf of continuous real functions over a manifold, is a manifold that is often non-Hausdorff. (The etale space is Hausdorff if it is a sheaf of functions with some sort of [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) property.)[^o13]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Because non-Hausdorff manifolds are [locally homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_homeomorphic) to [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), they are [locally metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_metrizable_space) (but not [metrizable](wikipedia://en/19738) in general) and [locally Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/5075766) (but not [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) in general).

:::
