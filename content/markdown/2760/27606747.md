# Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences"}

***Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences*** is a 1966 textbook by [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Mary L. Boas](wikipedia://en/27606754) intended to develop skills in mathematical problem-solving needed for junior to senior-graduate courses in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry). The book provides a comprehensive survey of [analytic techniques](wikipedia://en/173416) and provides careful statements of important theorems while omitting most detailed proofs. Each section contains a large number of problems, with selected answers. [Numerical computational approaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_physics) using computers are outside the scope of the book.

:::

The book, now in its third edition, was still widely used in university classrooms as of 1999[^o3] and is frequently cited in other textbooks and scientific papers.
