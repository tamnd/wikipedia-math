# Mary L. Boas

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mary L. Boas"}

**Mary Layne Boas** (March 10, 1917 – February 17, 2010) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) professor[^o3] best known as the author of *[Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences](wikipedia://en/27606747)*[^o8] (1966), an undergraduate textbook that was still widely used in college classrooms as of 1999.[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

She received a bachelor's degree (1938) and a master's degree (1940) in mathematics at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), and a Ph.D. (1948) in physics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o6] She taught physics at [DePaul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePaul_University) in Chicago for thirty years, retiring in 1987 to return to Washington.[^o6] Prior to her time at DePaul University, she served as an instructor in the mathematics department at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In 2005, at the age of 88, Boas published the third edition of her textbook.[^o8] She established the Mary L. Boas Endowed Scholarship at the University of Washington in 2008 to recognize outstanding academic achievements by female students in physics.[^o9][^o10]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Mary Boas was married to mathematician [Ralph P. Boas Jr.](wikipedia://en/3088231) Her son, [Harold P. Boas](wikipedia://en/11754242), is also a noted mathematician. She died on February 17, 2010, at her home near [Seattle, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle,_Washington).
