# Ida Craven Merriam

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ida Craven Merriam"}

**Ida Craven Merriam** (1904–1997)[^o2] was an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who became "one of the seminal figures in the early administration of the [Social Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_%28United_States%29) program",[^o3] helping to found the nonprofit National Academy of Social Insurance.[^o10][^o4]

:::

Ida Craven, a native of [Philadelphia, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania), was born on November 6, 1904.[^o5] She graduated [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) in English and history from [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) in 1925,[^o3][^o9][^o4] where she was president of the Wellesley Forum.[^o6] She studied economics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), and then earned a Ph.D. in 1928 from the [Brookings Graduate School of Economics and Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o3][^o9][^o4] While working towards her doctorate at Brookings, she taught a summer economics class at the [Bryn Mawr Summer School for Women Workers in Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_Summer_School_for_Women_Workers_in_Industry).[^o7] After completing her studies, she worked for the *Encyclopedia of Social Sciences* and as an assistant professor at the [Connecticut College for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_College), and married Mylon Merriam in 1933.[^o9]

She left her position at Connecticut College in 1936 to work in the research and statistics bureau of the Social Security Administration,[^o3][^o8] soon after the creation of the SSA in 1935,[^o4] She became the head of that unit[^o3] and Assistant Commissioner of the Office of Research and Statistics in 1955.[^o9] As Assistant Commissioner, she published special reports on topics including housing, benefit levels, health insurance, disability, widows, work-life balance, prospects for future retirees, and related social welfare systems in Europe. Her unit also set the first [poverty thresholds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_threshold) for the US.[^o10] She retired in 1972.[^o3]

In 1965, she was elected as a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) "for outstanding sustained accomplishments and contributions through statistical research to social insurance and social welfare."[^o11] The [United States Civil Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Civil_Service_Commission) gave Merriam a Federal Woman's Award in 1966, and she also won the Distinguished Service Award of the [Department of Health, Education and Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health,_Education_and_Welfare).[^o9][^o4]

## Death {#Death}

Ida Craven Merriam died on April 8, 1997, in [Cheverly, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheverly,_Maryland), near the Collington Episcopal Life Care Center in [Mitchellville, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchellville,_Maryland), where she had been an inpatient for three years before her death.[^o9]
