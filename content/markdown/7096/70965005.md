# Appeal to the law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Appeal to the law"}

An **appeal to the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law)** (***argumentum ad legem*** in Latin) is an informal [fallacy](wikipedia://en/53986) in which someone tries to encourage or defend an action based on its legality, or condemn it as morally reprehensible, purely because it is [illegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violation_of_law).[^o2] This line of reasoning is faulty because although the law of the land is important, it does not necessarily match up with the [morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality) or [sensibility](wikipedia://en/21478754) of an action.[^o3][^o4][^o5] In reality, many statutorily forbidden acts are *[malum prohibitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malum_prohibitum)* rather than *[malum in se](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malum_in_se)*.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- "So what if I cheated on my husband? It's not like being unfaithful is a crime!"
- "Filling your shoes with river water is cheap, simple *and* legal in all 50 states."
- "I think that routine circumcision is unethical, but my friend keeps arguing that because it's legal it must be ethical"
