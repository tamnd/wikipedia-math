In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset)'s definition a **semiregular polytope** is usually taken to be a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) that is [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581560) and has all its [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) being [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693). [E.L. Elte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.L._Elte) compiled a [longer list in 1912](wikipedia://en/27806942#Elte's_semiregular_polytopes_of_the_first_kind) as *The Semiregular Polytopes of the Hyperspaces* which included a wider definition.

:::

## Gosset's list {#Gosset's_list}

In [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853) and below, the terms *semiregular polytope* and *[uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392)* have identical meanings, because all uniform [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621) must be [regular](wikipedia://en/333306). However, since not all [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra) are [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra), the number of semiregular polytopes in dimensions higher than three is much smaller than the number of uniform polytopes in the same number of dimensions.

The three convex semiregular [4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/24979) are the [rectified 5-cell](wikipedia://en/2720430), [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756) and [rectified 600-cell](wikipedia://en/2720121). The only semiregular polytopes in higher dimensions are the [*k*<sub>21</sub> polytopes](wikipedia://en/12210742), where the rectified 5-cell is the special case of *k* = 0. These were all listed by Gosset, but a proof of the completeness of this list was not published until the work of (Makarov 1988) for four dimensions, and (Blind Blind 1991) for higher dimensions.

## Euclidean honeycombs {#Euclidean_honeycombs}

*Image: HC P1-P3.png.* The [tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3735260) in Euclidean 3-space has alternating tetrahedral and octahedral cells.

Semiregular polytopes can be extended to semiregular [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654). The semiregular Euclidean honeycombs are the [tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3735260) (3D), [gyrated alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_alternated_cubic_honeycomb) (3D) and the [5<sub>21</sub> honeycomb](wikipedia://en/26842937) (8D).

Gosset [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961):

1. [Tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3735260) or [alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternated_cubic_honeycomb) (Simple tetroctahedric check), ↔ (Also [quasiregular polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiregular_polytope))
1. [Gyrated alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_alternated_cubic_honeycomb) (Complex tetroctahedric check),

Semiregular E-honeycomb:

- [5<sub>21</sub> honeycomb](wikipedia://en/26842937) (9-ic check) (8D Euclidean honeycomb),

(Gosset 1900) additionally allowed Euclidean honeycombs as facets of higher-dimensional Euclidean honeycombs, giving the following additional figures:

1. Hypercubic honeycomb prism, named by Gosset as the (*n* – 1)-ic semi-check (analogous to a single rank or file of a chessboard)
1. [Alternated hexagonal slab honeycomb](wikipedia://en/2961#Frieze_forms) (tetroctahedric semi-check),

## Hyperbolic honeycombs {#Hyperbolic_honeycombs}

*Image: H3 4333-0100 center ultrawide.png.* The [hyperbolic tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/42747613) has tetrahedral and two types of octahedral cells.
