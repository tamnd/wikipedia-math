# McKay conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="McKay conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **McKay conjecture** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of equality between two numbers: the number of irreducible [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) of degree not divisible by a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ for a given finite group and the same number for the [normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) in that group of a [Sylow $p$-subgroup](wikipedia://en/53993).

:::

It is named after the Canadian mathematician [John McKay](wikipedia://en/4583624), who originally stated a limited version of it as a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) in 1971, for the special case of $p=2$ and [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600). The conjecture was later generalized by other mathematicians to a more general conjecture for any prime value of $p$ and more general groups.

In 2023, a [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the general conjecture was announced by [Britta Späth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britta_Späth) and [Marc Cabanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Cabanes).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose $p$ is a prime number, $G$ is a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707), and $P \leq G$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup. Define

$$\textrm{Irr}_{p'}(G) := \{\chi \in \textrm{Irr}(G) : p \nmid \chi(1) \}$$

where $\textrm{Irr}(G)$ denotes the set of complex [irreducible characters](wikipedia://en/691741#Definitions) of the group $G$. The McKay conjecture claims the equality

$$|\textrm{Irr}_{p'}(G)| = |\textrm{Irr}_{p'}(N_G(P))|$$

where $N_G(P)$ is the normalizer of $P$ in $G$.

In other words, for any finite group $G$, the number of its [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) complex [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) whose dimension is not divisible by $p$ equals that number for the normalizer of any of its Sylow $p$-subgroups. (Here we count [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) representations as the same.)

:::

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Alperin-McKay conjecture"}

## History {#History}

In McKay's original papers on the subject,[^o7][^o8] the statement was given for the prime $p=2$ and simple groups, but examples of computations of $|\textrm{Irr}_{p'}(G)|$ for odd primes or [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) are mentioned. [Marty Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Isaacs) also checked the conjecture for the prime 2 and [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012) $G$.[^o9] The first appearance of the conjecture for arbitrary primes is in a paper by [Jon L. Alperin](wikipedia://en/25403965) giving also a version in [block theory](wikipedia://en/1110685), now called the **Alperin–McKay conjecture**.[^o10]

:::

::: {#o12 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

In 2007, [Martin Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Isaacs), [Gunter Malle](wikipedia://en/58001049) and [Gabriel Navarro](wikipedia://en/78201740) showed that the McKay conjecture reduces to the checking of a so-called **inductive McKay condition** for each [finite simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group).[^o13][^o14] This opens the door to a proof of the conjecture by using the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).

The Isaacs−Malle−Navarro paper was also an inspiration for similar reductions for Alperin weight conjecture (named after [Jonathan Lazare Alperin](wikipedia://en/25403965)), its block version, the Alperin−McKay conjecture, and Dade's conjecture (named after [Everett C. Dade](wikipedia://en/34698353)).

The McKay conjecture for the prime 2 was proven by Britta Späth and [Gunter Malle](wikipedia://en/58001049) in 2016.[^o15]

An important step in proving the inductive McKay condition for all simple groups is to determine the [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) $\textrm{Aut}(G)$ on the set $\textrm{Irr}(G)$ for each finite [quasisimple group](wikipedia://en/1642729) $G$. The solution was given by Späth[^o16] in the form of an $\textrm{Aut}(G)$-equivariant [Jordan decomposition of characters](wikipedia://en/10574794#Jordan_decomposition) for finite quasisimple groups of Lie type.

A proof of the McKay conjecture for all primes and all finite groups was announced by Britta Späth and Marc Cabanes in October 2023 in various conferences, a manuscript being available later in 2024.[^o17]
