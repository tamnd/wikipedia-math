# Cartan–Dieudonné theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cartan–Dieudonné theorem"}

Cartan's theorem (disambiguation)|Cartan's theorem In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cartan–Dieudonné theorem**, named after [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) and [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084), establishes that every [orthogonal transformation](wikipedia://en/1382381) in an *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) symmetric bilinear space can be described as the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of at most *n* [reflections](wikipedia://en/295829).

:::

The notion of a symmetric bilinear space is a generalization of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) whose structure is defined by a [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) (which need not be [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_bilinear_form), so is not necessarily an [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) – for instance, a [pseudo-Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/10962546) is also a symmetric bilinear space). The orthogonal transformations in the space are those [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/18102#Endomorphisms_and_automorphisms) which preserve the value of the bilinear form between every pair of vectors; in Euclidean space, this corresponds to preserving distances and [angles](wikipedia://en/1196). These orthogonal transformations form a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) under composition, called the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954).

For example, in the two-dimensional [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697) plane, every orthogonal transformation is either a reflection across a [line](wikipedia://en/946975) through the origin or a [rotation](wikipedia://en/410009) about the origin (which can be written as the composition of two reflections). Any arbitrary composition of such rotations and reflections can be rewritten as a composition of no more than 2 reflections. Similarly, in three-dimensional Euclidean space, every orthogonal transformation can be described as a single reflection, a rotation (2 reflections), or an [improper rotation](wikipedia://en/244324) (3 reflections). In four dimensions, [double rotations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_rotation) are added that represent 4 reflections.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Let (*V*, *b*) be an *n*-dimensional, [non-degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_form) symmetric bilinear space over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) with [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) not equal to 2. Then, every element of the orthogonal group O(*V*, *b*) is a composition of at most *n* reflections.

:::
