# Einar Steingrímsson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Einar Steingrímsson"}

Einar **Einar Steingrímsson** (born 20 July 1955[^o3]) is an Icelandic mathematician whose research lies in [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533), especially the study of [permutation patterns](wikipedia://en/24360911) and [permutation statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_statistics). He is a research professor (emeritus) in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at the [University of Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strathclyde).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Einar grew up in Reykjavík and left secondary school early at age eighteen (the normal graduation age at the time was twenty), and trained as a ship builder at Slippstöðin in [Akureyri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akureyri).[^o4] After completing qualifying examinations independently, he enrolled at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). He graduated there as a BA and MA in mathematics in 1987, and as PhD at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1992 under the supervision of [Richard P. Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397); his dissertation was titled *Permutation Statistics of Indexed and Poset Permutations*.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Einar moved to [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg), Sweden, in 1990, while still a graduate student at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and, in 1992, Einar was hired at [Chalmers University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalmers_University_of_Technology) as part of the joint mathematics institute with the [University of Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Gothenburg).[^o8] In 2004 he became a professor of mathematics at [Reykjavik University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reykjavik_University). From 2010 to 2021 he was a professor at the [University of Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strathclyde) in Glasgow, Scotland.[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

Einar's research focuses on permutation statistics, pattern avoidance, and related enumerative problems.

- In a 1994 paper, he studied permutation statistics defined on colored permutations (then called indexed permutations), relating them to classical statistics such as descents and inversions.[^o10]
- In a 2000 paper joint with Eric Babson, he introduced vincular permutation patterns (then called "generalized patterns"); that paper also classified Mahonian statistics expressible via these patterns.[^o11]
- New Euler–Mahonian bi-statistics on permutations and words were described in a joint work with R. J. Clarke and Jiang Zeng, extending earlier Eulerian–Mahonian results.[^o12]
- Connections between permutation tableaux and pattern avoidance were examined in his joint work with [Lauren K. Williams](wikipedia://en/43551379), linking tableaux combinatorics to steady-state distributions in the asymmetric simple exclusion process.[^o13]
- His joint work with Anders Claesson and Vít Jelínek established that the [growth rate](wikipedia://en/7892663#Allowable_growth_rates) of the class of 1324-avoiding permutations is at most 16, and presented a still-open conjecture that if true would lower this bound to $e^{\pi\sqrt{2/3}}$, approximately 13.00195.[^o14]
