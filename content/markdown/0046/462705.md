# Central angle

*Image: Sector central angle arc.svg.* Angle $\angle AOB$ is a central angle.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Central angle"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **central angle** is an [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) whose apex (vertex) is the center $O$ of a circle and whose legs (sides) are [radii](wikipedia://en/1780815) intersecting the circle in two distinct points $A$ and $B$. Central angles are [subtended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtend) by an [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) between those two points, and the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) is the central angle of a circle of radius one (measured in [radians](wikipedia://en/26003)).[^o2] The central angle is also known as the arc's [angular distance](wikipedia://en/3720362). The arc length spanned by a central angle on a sphere is called *[spherical distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_distance)*.

:::

The size of a central angle $\theta$ is $0^{\circ} < \theta < 360^{\circ}$ or $0 < \theta < 2\pi$ (radians). When defining or drawing a central angle, in addition to specifying the points $A$ and $B$, one must specify whether the angle being defined is the convex angle (\<180°) or the reflex angle (>180°). Equivalently, one must specify whether the movement from point $A$ to point $B$ is clockwise or counterclockwise.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulas"}

## Formulas {#Formulas}

If the intersection points $A$ and $B$ of the legs of the angle with the circle form a [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007), then $\theta = 180^{\circ}$ is a [straight angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_angle). (In radians, $\theta = \pi$.)

Let $L$ be the **minor arc** of the circle between points $A$ and $B$, and let $R$ be the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the circle.[^o5]

*Image: Angle central convex.svg.* Convex central angle, subtended by minor arc $L$.

If the central angle $\theta$ is subtended by $L$, then

$$0^{\circ} < \theta < 180^{\circ} \, , \quad \theta = \left( {\frac{180L}{\pi R}} \right) ^{\circ}=\frac{L}{R}.$$

::: {#o6 .proof cue=math-proof name="Proof (for degrees)"}

**Proof (for degrees).**

The [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of a circle with radius $R$ is $2\pi R$, and the minor arc $L$ is the $\tfrac{\theta}{360^{\circ}}$ proportional part of the whole circumference (see [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29)). So:

$$L=\frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}} \cdot 2 \pi R \quad \Rightarrow \quad \theta = \left( {\frac{180L}{\pi R}} \right) ^{\circ}.$$

:::

*Image: Angle central reflex.svg.* Reflex central angle, *not* subtended by $L$.

::: {#o7 .proof cue=math-proof name="Proof (for radians)"}

**Proof (for radians).**

The [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of a circle with radius $R$ is $2\pi R$, and the minor arc $L$ is the $\tfrac{\theta}{2\pi}$ proportional part of the whole circumference (see [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29)). So

$$L=\frac{\theta}{2 \pi} \cdot 2 \pi R \quad \Rightarrow \quad \theta = \frac{L}{R}.$$

:::

If the central angle $\theta$ is **not** subtended by the minor arc $L$, then $\theta$ is a reflex angle and

$$180^{\circ} < \theta < 360^{\circ} \, , \quad \theta = \left( 360 - \frac{180L}{\pi R} \right) ^{\circ}=2\pi-\frac{L}{R}.$$

If a tangent at $A$ and a tangent at $B$ intersect at the exterior point $P$, then denoting the center as $O$, the angles $\angle BOA$ (convex) and $\angle BPA$ are [supplementary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplementary_angles) (sum to 180°).

:::

## Central angle of a regular polygon {#Central_angle_of_a_regular_polygon}

A [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) with $n$ sides has a [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262) upon which all its vertices lie, and the center of the circle is also the center of the polygon. The central angle of the regular polygon is formed at the center by the circumradii to two adjacent vertices. The measure of this angle is $2\pi/n.$
