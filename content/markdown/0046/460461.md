# Nest algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nest algebra"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **nest algebras** are a class of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987) that generalise the [upper-triangular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper-triangular_matrix) algebras to a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) context. They were introduced by (txt).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Nest algebras are formally defined as the algebra of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) leaving [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) each [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) contained in a [subspace nest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_nest), that is, a set of subspaces which is [totally ordered](wikipedia://en/30330) by [inclusion](wikipedia://en/27631) and is also a [complete lattice](wikipedia://en/44777). Since the [orthogonal projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) corresponding to the subspaces in a nest [commute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_operation), nests are commutative subspace lattices. This makes them one of the simplest examples of [commutative subspace lattice algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_subspace_lattice_algebra).

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

By way of an example, let us apply this definition to recover the finite-dimensional upper-triangular matrices. Let us work in the $n$-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $\mathbb{C}^n$, and let $e_1,e_2,\dots,e_n$ be the [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005). For $j=0,1,2,\dots,n$, let $S_j$ be the $j$-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{C}^n$ [spanned](wikipedia://en/56353) by the first $j$ basis vectors $e_1,\dots,e_j$. Let

$$N=\{ (0)=S_0, S_1, S_2, \dots, S_{n-1}, S_n=\mathbb{C}^n \};$$

then *N* is a subspace nest, and the corresponding nest algebra of *n* × *n* complex matrices *M* leaving each subspace in *N* invariant – that is, satisfying $MS\subseteq S$ for each *S* in *N* – is precisely the set of upper-triangular matrices.

If we omit one or more of the subspaces *S<sub>j</sub>* from *N* then the corresponding nest algebra consists of block upper-triangular matrices.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Nest algebras are among the simplest examples of non-[self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) operator algebras. Furthermore, they are [closed](wikipedia://en/293802) in the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698) and [hyperreflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperreflexive) with distance constant 1.

:::
