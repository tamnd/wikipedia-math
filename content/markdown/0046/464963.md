# One half

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="One half"}

**One half** is the [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) of [2](wikipedia://en/64516). It is an [irreducible fraction](wikipedia://en/14822) with a numerator of 1 and a denominator of 2. It often appears in [mathematical equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_equation), [recipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recipe) and [measurements](wikipedia://en/19022).

:::

## As a word {#As_a_word}

One half is one of the few fractions which are commonly expressed in natural [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) by [suppletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppletion) rather than regular derivation: the word for "half" in various languages is etymologically unrelated to their word for "two". In [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), for example, other small fractions, such as "sixth" and "eleventh", are derived from the corresponding number word.

A *half* can also be said to be one part of something divided into two equal parts. It is acceptable to write one half as a [hyphenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen) word, *one-half*.

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

One half is the [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) that lies midway between 0 and 1 on the [number line](wikipedia://en/287188). [Multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) by one half is equivalent to [division by two](wikipedia://en/80754), or "halving"; conversely, division by one half is equivalent to multiplication by two, or "doubling".

*Image: Eye of Horus square.png.* A [square](wikipedia://en/659939) of side length [one](wikipedia://en/77591639), here dissected into [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048) whose [areas](wikipedia://en/1209) are successive [powers](wikipedia://en/99491) of one half

A number raised to the [power](wikipedia://en/99491) of one half is equal to its [square root](wikipedia://en/29208). This follows from the fact that when multiplying powers, the exponents add. So, $a^{1/2}$ times itself is $a^{1/2 + 1/2}$ which is $a^1$, which equals $a$.

The area of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is one half its [base](wikipedia://en/30654#Area) and its height, also known as its [altitude](wikipedia://en/161241).[^o5]

*Image: ModularGroup-FundamentalDomain.svg.* [Fundamental region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_region) of the modular *[j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988)* in the **[upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826)** (shaded gray), with [modular discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_discriminant) $|\tau| \ge 1$ and $-\tfrac{1}{2} < \mathfrak{R}(\tau) \le \tfrac{1}{2}$, where $-\tfrac{1}{2} < \mathfrak{R}(\tau) < 0 \Rightarrow |\tau| > 1$

The [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) evaluated at one half is the square root of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601).[^o6]

It has two different [decimal representations](wikipedia://en/3293594) in [base ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_ten), the familiar $0.5$ and the [recurring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurring_decimal) $0.4\overline{9}$, with a similar pair of expansions in any even [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_of_computation); while in odd bases, one half has no [terminating](wikipedia://en/13612447) representation.

The [Bernoulli number](wikipedia://en/4964) $B_{1}$ has the value $\pm \tfrac {1}{2}$ (its sign depending on competing conventions).[^o7][^o8]

The [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) is the conjecture that every nontrivial [complex root](wikipedia://en/5826) of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) has a real part equal to $\tfrac {1}{2}$.[^o9]

## Computer characters {#Computer_characters}

The "one-half" symbol has its own [code point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) as a [precomposed character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precomposed_character) in the [Latin-1 Supplement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin-1_Supplement) block of [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode), rendering as ½.

The reduced size of this symbol may make it illegible to readers with relatively mild [visual impairment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_impairment); consequently the decomposed forms $\tfrac{1}{2}$ or $\tfrac{1}{2}$ may be more appropriate.
