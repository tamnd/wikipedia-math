# Ideal norm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ideal norm"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), the **norm of an ideal** is a generalization of a [norm](wikipedia://en/450555) of an element in the [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634). It is particularly important in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) since it measures the size of an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of a complicated [number ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_ring) in terms of an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in a less complicated [ring](wikipedia://en/48404). When the less complicated number ring is taken to be the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613), **Z**, then the norm of a nonzero ideal *I* of a number ring *R* is simply the size of the finite [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) *R*/*I*.

:::

## Relative norm {#Relative_norm}

Let *A* be a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) with [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) *K* and [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) of *B* in a finite [separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340) *L* of *K*. (this implies that *B* is also a Dedekind domain.) Let $\mathcal{I}_A$ and $\mathcal{I}_B$ be the [ideal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_group) of *A* and *B*, respectively (i.e., the sets of nonzero [fractional ideals](wikipedia://en/460700).) Following the technique developed by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), the **norm map**

$$N_{B/A}\colon \mathcal{I}_B \to \mathcal{I}_A$$

is the unique [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) that satisfies

$$N_{B/A}(\mathfrak q) = \mathfrak{p}^{[B/\mathfrak q : A/\mathfrak p]}$$

for all nonzero [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) $\mathfrak q$ of *B*, where $\mathfrak p = \mathfrak q\cap A$ is the [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) of *A* lying below $\mathfrak q$.

Alternatively, for any $\mathfrak b\in\mathcal{I}_B$ one can equivalently define $N_{B/A}(\mathfrak{b})$ to be the [fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) of *A* generated by the set $\{ N_{L/K}(x) | x \in \mathfrak{b} \}$ of [field norms](wikipedia://en/450555) of elements of *B*.[^o3]

For $\mathfrak a \in \mathcal{I}_A$, one has $N_{B/A}(\mathfrak a B) = \mathfrak a^n$, where $n = [L : K]$.

The ideal norm of a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) is thus compatible with the field norm of an element:

$N_{B/A}(xB) = N_{L/K}(x)A.$[^o4]

Let $L/K$ be a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) with [rings of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) $\mathcal{O}_K\subset \mathcal{O}_L$.

Then the preceding applies with $A = \mathcal{O}_K, B = \mathcal{O}_L$, and for any $\mathfrak b\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{O}_L}$ we have

$$N_{\mathcal{O}_L/\mathcal{O}_K}(\mathfrak b)= K \cap\prod_{\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)} \sigma (\mathfrak b),$$

which is an element of $\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{O}_K}$.

The notation $N_{\mathcal{O}_L/\mathcal{O}_K}$ is sometimes shortened to $N_{L/K}$, an [abuse of notation](wikipedia://en/1698066) that is compatible with also writing $N_{L/K}$ for the field norm, as noted above.

In the case $K=\mathbb{Q}$, it is reasonable to use positive [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) as the range for $N_{\mathcal{O}_L/\mathbb{Z}}\,$ since $\mathbb{Z}$ has trivial [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) and [unit group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_group) $\{\pm 1\}$, thus each nonzero [fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) of $\mathbb{Z}$ is generated by a uniquely determined positive [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024). Under this convention the relative norm from $L$ down to $K=\mathbb{Q}$ coincides with the [absolute norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_norm) defined below.

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Absolute norm"}

## Absolute norm {#Absolute_norm}

Let $L$ be a [number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) with [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) $\mathcal{O}_L$, and $\mathfrak a$ a nonzero (integral) [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of $\mathcal{O}_L$.

The absolute norm of $\mathfrak a$ is

$$N(\mathfrak a) :=\left [ \mathcal{O}_L: \mathfrak a\right ]=\left|\mathcal{O}_L/\mathfrak a\right|.\,$$

By convention, the norm of the zero ideal is taken to be zero.

If $\mathfrak a=(a)$ is a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126), then

$N(\mathfrak a)=\left|N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\right|$.[^o7]

The norm is [completely multiplicative](wikipedia://en/1055940): if $\mathfrak a$ and $\mathfrak b$ are ideals of $\mathcal{O}_L$, then

$N(\mathfrak a\cdot\mathfrak b)=N(\mathfrak a)N(\mathfrak b)$.[^o7]

Thus the absolute norm extends uniquely to a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396)

$$N\colon\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{O}_L}\to\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times,$$

defined for all nonzero [fractional ideals](wikipedia://en/460700) of $\mathcal{O}_L$.

The norm of an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $\mathfrak a$ can be used to give an upper bound on the field norm of the smallest nonzero element it contains:

there always exists a nonzero $a\in\mathfrak a$ for which

$$\left|N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\right|\leq \left ( \frac{2}{\pi}\right )^s \sqrt{\left|\Delta_L\right|}N(\mathfrak a),$$

where

- $\Delta_L$ is the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) of $L$ and
- $s$ is the number of pairs of (non-real) complex [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) of $L$ into $\mathbb{C}$ (the number of complex places of $L$).[^o8]
