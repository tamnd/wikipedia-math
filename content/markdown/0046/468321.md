# Nagell–Lutz theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nagell–Lutz theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Nagell–Lutz theorem** is a result in the [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/9109) of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225), which describes [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330) points on elliptic curves over the integers. It is named for [Trygve Nagell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Nagell) and [Élisabeth Lutz](wikipedia://en/6738820).

:::

## Definition of the terms {#Definition_of_the_terms}

Suppose that the equation

$$y^2 = x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c$$

defines a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) [cubic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_curve) *E* with integer [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) *a*, *b*, *c*, and let *D* be the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) of the cubic [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) on the right side:

$$D = -4a^3c + a^2b^2 + 18abc - 4b^3 - 27c^2.$$

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

If $P = (x,y)$ is a [rational point](wikipedia://en/2960200) of finite [order](wikipedia://en/19447#order_of_an_element) on *E*, for the [elliptic curve group law](wikipedia://en/10225#The_group_law), then:

1. *x* and *y* are integers;
1. either $y = 0$, in which case *P* has order two, or else *y* divides *D*, which immediately implies that $y^2$ divides *D*.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The Nagell–Lutz theorem generalizes to arbitrary number fields and more general cubic equations.[^o7] For curves over the rationals, the generalization says that, for a nonsingular cubic curve whose Weierstrass form

$$y^2 +a_1 x y + a_3 y = x^3 + a_2 x^2 + a_4 x + a_6$$

has integer coefficients, any rational point $P = (x,y)$ of finite order must have integer coordinates, or else have order 2 and coordinates of the form $x=m/4$, $y=n/8$, for *m* and *n* integers.

:::

## History {#History}

The result is named for its two independent discoverers, the Norwegian [Trygve Nagell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Nagell) (1895–1988) who published it in 1935, and [Élisabeth Lutz](wikipedia://en/6738820) (1937).
