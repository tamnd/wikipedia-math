# Stefan Burr

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Stefan Burr"}

**Stefan Andrus Burr** (born 1940) is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), specializing in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), particularly [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175). He is a retired professor of [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at [The City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_City_College_of_New_York).

:::

## Career {#Career}

He did his undergraduate studies at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), graduating with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in Mathematics with Highest Honors in 1961 June.[^o5] He was a member of their team for the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) on 1958 November 22, where the team received an honorable mention;[^o6] 1959 November 21, where he individually received an honorable mention;[^o7] and 1960 December 3, where the team won first prize and he additionally received an honorable mention.[^o8] As a member of the class of 1961, he received the [Dorothea Klumpke Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothea_Klumpke) Prize in Mathematics in 1960.[^o9] As a student there, he joined the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 1960 April/May.[^o10]

He graduated from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1963[^o11] and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1968,[^o12][^o13] both in Mathematics. His PhD thesis, *An Elementary Solution of the [Waring-Goldbach Problem](wikipedia://en/26497499)* (1968 or 1969)[^o14] was advised by [Bernard Morris Dwork](wikipedia://en/1678313).[^o15]

While working on his PhD at Princeton, he had a job as a Scientific Programmer for [Lockheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Corporation) MSC (c. 1965).[^o16][^o17] Afterwards, starting c. 1966, he had a job as a member of the technical staff at [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs),[^o18][^o19][^o20][^o21] then moved to [American Telephone & Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Corporation) (AT&T) [Long Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33_Thomas_Street) c. 1975, where he worked as a Staff [Supervisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervisor) until c. 1978.[^o17][^o21][^o22][^o23]

Afterwards, c. 1979, he became a member of the faculty of [City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York), part of the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) (CUNY) system, in the Department of Computer Science (and perhaps briefly in the Department of Mathematics[^o24]).[^o17][^o25][^o26] In 1982, he spent 3 months from May to August visiting the [University of Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Coimbra) in [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal) as a [Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program), where he lectured and researched computer science.[^o27][^o28][^o29][^o30] He may have spent some time at [Lehman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_College) c. 1996.[^o31][^o32] He retired after teaching in the Fall 2006 semester.[^o33][^o34][^o35]

He became Mathematics [Vice-Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chair) of the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences) in January 1988,[^o36][^o37] became Mathematics [Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairperson) in 1990,[^o38][^o39] and retired from the position in June 1992.[^o40][^o41] He became a managing editor of the *[Journal of Graph Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Graph_Theory)* in 1991,[^o42][^o43] and retired from the position c. 1997.[^o44][^o45]

Many of his publications involve problems from the field of [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175). He has published 27 papers with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284).[^o46] The [Burr–Erdős conjecture](wikipedia://en/3086101), published as a conjecture by Burr and Erdős in 1975, solved only in 2015, states that [sparse graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_graph) have [linearly growing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_growth) [Ramsey numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number).
