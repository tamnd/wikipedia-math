# Karine Chemla

*Image: Karine Chemla (cropped).jpg.* Chemla at [Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) in 2009

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karine Chemla"}

**Karine Chemla** (born 8 February 1957) is a French [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and [sinologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinology) who works as a director of research at the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (CNRS).[^o7] She is also a senior fellow at the [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) [Institute for the Study of the Ancient World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_the_Ancient_World).[^o2] She was elected a Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 2019.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Chemla studied at [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) and the [École normale supérieure de jeunes filles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_jeunes_filles), earning an [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in mathematics in 1978 and a [diploma of advanced studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Advanced_Studies) in 1979.[^o5] At this time, her work was in pure mathematics. However, in 1980, influenced by the work of [Ilya Prigogine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Prigogine), she won a Singer–Polignac scholarship to travel to China and study the history of Chinese mathematics.[^o4] Returning to France, she earned her Ph.D. in the history of mathematics from [Paris 13 University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_13_University) in 1982, and began working for CNRS at that time.[^o5]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Chemla's research interests include [Chinese mathematics](wikipedia://en/1524543), 19th century French [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and the theory of the history of mathematics.[^o7]

With Guo Shuchun, Chemla published in 2004 a critical edition and translation into French of *[The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207)*.[^o4][^o8][^o9][^o10] She is also the co-editor, with Cécile Michel, of *Mathematics, Administrative and Economic Activities in Ancient Worlds* (Springer, 2020).[^o11]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Chemla was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1998.[^o13][^o14] She became a member of the [Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina) in 2004,[^o15] of the [International Academy of the History of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_the_History_of_Science) in 2005,[^o5] and of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2013.[^o16] In 2013–2014 she was the holder of the Sarton Chair of History of Science at [Ghent University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent_University).[^o17] She served as president of the European Society for the History of Science 2014–2016.[^o18] She is the 2020 winner of the Otto Neugebauer Prize[^o19] and the 2021 winner of the [LMS-BSHM Hirst Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMS-BSHM_Hirst_Prize).
