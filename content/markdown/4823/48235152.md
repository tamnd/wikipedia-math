# Known-key distinguishing attack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Known-key distinguishing attack"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), a **known-key distinguishing attack** is an [attack model](wikipedia://en/3201589) against [symmetric ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_cipher), whereby an attacker who knows the [key](wikipedia://en/53039) can find a structural property in cipher, where the transformation from plaintext to ciphertext is not random. There is no common formal definition for what such a transformation may be. The **chosen-key distinguishing attack** is strongly related, where the attacker can choose a key to introduce such transformations.[^o2]

:::

These attacks do not directly compromise the confidentiality of ciphers, because in a classical scenario, the key is unknown to the attacker. Known-/chosen-key distinguishing attacks apply in the "open key model" instead.[^o2] They are known to be applicable in some situations where [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594) are converted to [hash functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hash_function), leading to practical [collision attacks](wikipedia://en/969624) against the hash.[^o3]

Known-key distinguishing attacks were first introduced in 2007 by [Lars Knudsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Ramkilde_Knudsen) and [Vincent Rijmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Rijmen)[^o2] in a paper that proposed such an attack against 7 out of 10 rounds of the [AES](wikipedia://en/1260) cipher and another attack against a generalized [Feistel cipher](wikipedia://en/352709). Their attack finds plaintext/ciphertext pairs for a cipher with a known key, where the input and output have *s* least significant bits set to zero, in less than 2<sup>*s*</sup> time (where *s* is fewer than half the [block size](wikipedia://en/294065)).[^o4]

These attacks have also been applied to reduced-round [Threefish](wikipedia://en/20019277) ([Skein](wikipedia://en/20007592))[^o5][^o6] and [Phelix](wikipedia://en/883596).[^o7]
