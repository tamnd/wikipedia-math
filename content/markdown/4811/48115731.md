# Pia Nalli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pia Nalli"}

**Pia Maria Nalli** (10 February 1886 – 27 September 1964) was an Italian mathematician known for her work on the [summability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summability_theory) of [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038), on [Morera's theorem](wikipedia://en/407047) for analytic functions of several variables and for finding the solution to the [Fredholm integral equation](wikipedia://en/363377) of the third kind for the first time.[^o5] Her research interests ranged from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [tensor analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_analysis); she was a speaker at the 1928 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).

:::

She is also remembered for her struggles against discrimination against women in the Italian university hiring system.[^o6] A street in Rome is named after her.

## Life and academic career {#Life_and_academic_career}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Nalli was born on February 10, 1886, in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), to a middle-class family with seven children.[^o9] She studied at the [University of Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Palermo), where she obtained a [laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) in 1910 under the supervision of Giuseppe Bagnera, with a thesis concerning [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and in the same year joined the [Circolo Matematico di Palermo](wikipedia://en/32146789).[^o9]

After finishing her studies, Nalli assisted Bagnera in Palermo in 1911 and then began working as a school teacher. She completed a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) thesis in 1914 on the theory of [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532), and continued to work on [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) and [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) for the next several years.[^o9]

### Academic career {#Academic_career}

Nalli served as assistant to Giuseppe Bagnera at the University of Palermo from 1 April 1911 to 16 November 1911. She then taught at a number of secondary schools, first in the girls' school at Avellino, then in Trapani, and from 16 November 1912 in the girls' technical school in Palermo. During this time Nalli continued her research, completing her thesis "*Esposizione e confronto critico delle diverse definizioni proposte per l'integrale definito di una funzione limitata o no*",[^o4] a study of the theory of the integral based on recent work on the subject by [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161), [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514) [Charles de la Vallée Poussin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_la_Vallée_Poussin), [Giuseppe Vitali](wikipedia://en/634990) and [Arnaud Denjoy](wikipedia://en/1485520).[^o9]

In 1921, Nalli became extraordinary professor at the [University of Cagliari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cagliari). She had been ranked second to [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009) in the competition for the position, possibly in part because she was female, but Picone chose to stay at the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania) and become head of mathematics there, so the Cagliari position fell to Nalli. In 1923, she was listed first in a search for a position at the [University of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia), but not offered the position. Finally, after similar mistreatment from several other universities, she moved to the University of Catania as a full professor in 1927. At around this time, perhaps encouraged by [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), she switched her research focus from [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) to [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus).[^o9]

She was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1928.[^o12]

### Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Nalli died on 27 September 1964, in Catania. A street in Rome, the Via Pia Nalli, is named after her.[^o9]

## Selected works {#Selected_works}

Pia Nalli published 61 mathematical works, including the monograph (Nalli 1914) and a textbook.[^o17] Her "Selected works" (Nalli 1976) include this monograph plus eleven articles on topics mainly belonging to [functional](wikipedia://en/11180) and mathematical analysis: the following list includes also her doctoral thesis (Nalli 1911) and other works on [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus)

- Nalli, Pia. (1911). "Riduzione di un fascio di curve piane di genere uno, corrispondente a sé stesso in una trasformazione birazionale involutoria del piano". *Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo*. 31 (1). p. 92–108. doi:10.1007/BF03018796.
- Nalli, Pia. (1914). "Esposizione e confronto critico delle diverse definizioni proposte per l'integrale definito di una funzione limitata o no". Stabilimento tipografico Virzì. p. 163.
- Nalli, Pia. (1922). "Sulle operazioni funzionali lineari". *Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo*. 46 (1). p. 49–90. doi:10.1007/BF03018259.
- Nalli, Pia. (1928). "Sulla metrica superficiale di una varietà". *Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo*. 52 (1). p. 260–264. doi:10.1007/BF03016619.
- Nalli, Pia. (1937). "Trasporti rigidi di vettori nelle varietà metriche". *Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo*. 61 (2). p. 314–338. doi:10.1007/BF03014120. zbl:0020.26002.
- Nalli, Pia. (1976). "Opere scelte". Litografia D. Cislaghi per conto dell'Unione Matematica Italiana e dell'Università di Catania. p. 364. zbl:1308.01004.
