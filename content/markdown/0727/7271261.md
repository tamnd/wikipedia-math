# Statistical semantics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical semantics"}

In [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), **statistical semantics** applies the methods of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) to the problem of determining the meaning of words or phrases, ideally through [unsupervised learning](wikipedia://en/233497), to a degree of precision at least sufficient for the purpose of [information retrieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_retrieval).

:::

## History {#History}

The term *statistical semantics* was first used by [Warren Weaver](wikipedia://en/473287) in his well-known paper on [machine translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_translation).[^o3] He argued that [word-sense disambiguation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-sense_disambiguation) for machine translation should be based on the [co-occurrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-occurrence) frequency of the context words near a given target word. The underlying assumption that "a word is characterized by the company it keeps" was advocated by [J. R. Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._Firth).[^o4] This assumption is known in [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) as the [distributional hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributional_hypothesis).[^o5] Emile Delavenay defined *statistical semantics* as the "statistical study of the meanings of words and their frequency and order of recurrence".[^o6] "[Furnas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Furnas) et al. 1983" is frequently cited as a foundational contribution to statistical semantics.[^o7] An early success in the field was [latent semantic analysis](wikipedia://en/689427).

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Research in statistical semantics has resulted in a wide variety of algorithms that use the distributional hypothesis to discover many aspects of [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107), by applying statistical techniques to [large corpora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_corpus):

- Measuring the [similarity in word meanings](wikipedia://en/1473135)[^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13]
- Measuring the similarity in word relations [^o14]
- Modeling [similarity-based generalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similarity-based_generalization)[^o15]
- Discovering words with a given relation[^o16]
- Classifying relations between words[^o17]
- [Extracting keywords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyword_extraction) from documents[^o18][^o19]
- Measuring the cohesiveness of text[^o20]
- [Discovering the different senses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-sense_induction) of words[^o21]
- Distinguishing the different senses of words[^o22]
- Subcognitive aspects of words[^o23]
- Distinguishing praise from criticism[^o24]

:::

## Related fields {#Related_fields}

Statistical semantics focuses on the meanings of common words and the relations between common words, unlike [text mining](wikipedia://en/318439), which tends to focus on whole documents, document collections, or named entities (names of people, places, and organizations). Statistical semantics is a subfield of [computational semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_semantics), which is in turn a subfield of [computational linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics) and [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing).

Many of the applications of statistical semantics (listed above) can also be addressed by [lexicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicon)-based algorithms, instead of the [corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_corpus)-based algorithms of statistical semantics. One advantage of corpus-based algorithms is that they are typically not as labour-intensive as lexicon-based algorithms. Another advantage is that they are usually easier to adapt to new languages or noisier new text types from e.g. social media than lexicon-based algorithms are.[^o26] However, the best performance on an application is often achieved by combining the two approaches.[^o27]
