# Gluon field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gluon field"}

In [theoretical](wikipedia://en/19594028) [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics), the **gluon field** is a [four-vector](wikipedia://en/226424) field characterizing the propagation of [gluons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluon) in the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction) between [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark). It plays the same role in [quantum chromodynamics](wikipedia://en/25264) as the [electromagnetic four-potential](wikipedia://en/731884) in [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268) – the gluon field constructs the [gluon field strength tensor](wikipedia://en/38184895).

:::

Throughout this article, Latin indices take values 1, 2, ..., 8 for the eight gluon [color charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge), while Greek indices take values 0 for timelike components and 1, 2, 3 for spacelike components of four-dimensional vectors and tensors in [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758). Throughout all equations, the [summation convention](wikipedia://en/195407) is used on all color and tensor indices, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

## Introduction {#Introduction}

Gluons can have eight [colour charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colour_charge) so there are eight fields, in contrast to photons which are neutral and so there is only one photon field.

The gluon fields for each color charge each have a "timelike" component analogous to the [electric potential](wikipedia://en/59615), and three "spacelike" components analogous to the [magnetic vector potential](wikipedia://en/1191067). Using similar symbols:[^o3]

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}^n(\mathbf{r}, t) = [ \underbrace{\mathcal{A}^n_0(\mathbf{r}, t)}_{\text{timelike}} , \underbrace{\mathcal{A}^n_1(\mathbf{r}, t), \mathcal{A}^n_2(\mathbf{r}, t), \mathcal{A}^n_3(\mathbf{r}, t)}_{\text{spacelike}} ] = [\phi^n (\mathbf{r}, t), \mathbf{A}^n (\mathbf{r}, t)]$$

where $n = 1, 2, ... 8$ are not [exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent) but enumerate the eight gluon color charges, and all components depend on the [position vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Position_vector) $\mathbf{r}$ of the gluon and time *t*. Each $\mathcal{A}^a_\alpha$ is a scalar field, for some component of spacetime and gluon color charge.

The [Gell-Mann matrices](wikipedia://en/563694) $\lambda^{a}$ are eight 3 × 3 matrices which form matrix [representations](wikipedia://en/19378200) of the [*SU*(3) group](wikipedia://en/173997). They are also [generators](wikipedia://en/99945) of the SU(3) group, in the context of quantum mechanics and field theory; a generator can be viewed as an [operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28physics%29) corresponding to a [symmetry transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_transformation) (see [symmetry in quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/39580830)). These matrices play an important role in QCD as QCD is a [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) of the SU(3) [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) obtained by taking the color charge to define a local symmetry: each Gell-Mann matrix corresponds to a particular gluon color charge, which in turn can be used to define [color charge operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge_operator). Generators of a group can also form a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) for a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), so the overall gluon field is a "[superposition](wikipedia://en/1201321)" of all the color fields. In terms of the Gell-Mann matrices (divided by 2 for convenience),

$$t_a = \frac{\lambda_a}{2}\,,$$

the components of the gluon field are represented by 3 × 3 matrices, given by:

$$\mathcal{A}_{\alpha} = t_a \mathcal{A}^a_\alpha \equiv t_1 \mathcal{A}^1_\alpha + t_2 \mathcal{A}^2_\alpha + \cdots + t_8 \mathcal{A}^8_\alpha$$

or collecting these into a vector of four 3 × 3 matrices:

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{r}, t) = [\mathcal{A}_0(\mathbf{r}, t),\mathcal{A}_1(\mathbf{r}, t),\mathcal{A}_2(\mathbf{r}, t),\mathcal{A}_3(\mathbf{r}, t)]$$

the gluon field is:

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}} = t_a \boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}^a \,.$$

## Gauge covariant derivative in QCD {#Gauge_covariant_derivative_in_QCD}

Below the definitions (and most of the notation) follow K. Yagi, T. Hatsuda, Y. Miake[^o5] and Greiner, Schäfer.[^o6]

The gauge [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) $D_{\mu}$ is required to transform quark fields in [manifest covariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifest_covariance); the [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) that form the [four-gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-gradient) $\partial_{\mu}$ alone are not enough. The components which act on the color triplet quark fields are given by:

$$D_\mu =\partial_\mu \pm ig_s t_a \mathcal{A}^a_\mu\,,$$

wherein $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358), and

$$g_s = \sqrt{4\pi \alpha_s}$$

is the [dimensionless](wikipedia://en/8267) [coupling constant for QCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_constant#QCD_and_asymptotic_freedom), and $\alpha_s$ is the [strong coupling constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_constant). Different authors choose different signs. The [partial derivative](wikipedia://en/52565) term includes a 3 × 3 [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718), conventionally not written for simplicity.

The [quark fields in triplet representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge#Quark_and_gluon_fields_and_color_charges) are written as [column vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_vector):

$$\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\ \psi_{2}\\ \psi_{3} \end{pmatrix},\overline{\psi}=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\psi}^*_{1}\\ \overline{\psi}^*_{2}\\ \overline{\psi}^*_{3} \end{pmatrix}$$

The quark field $\psi$ belongs to the [fundamental representation](wikipedia://en/649743) (**3**) and the [antiquark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiparticle) field $\overline{\psi}$ belongs to the [complex conjugate representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_conjugate) (**3<sup>\*</sup>**), [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) is denoted by $*$ (not overbar).

## Gauge transformations {#Gauge_transformations}

The [gauge transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation) of each gluon field $\mathcal{A}^n_\alpha$ which leaves the gluon field strength tensor unchanged is;[^o6]

$$\mathcal{A}^n_\alpha\rightarrow e^{i\bar{\theta}(\mathbf{r},t)} \left(\mathcal{A}^n_\alpha + \frac{i}{g_s}\partial_\alpha\right)e^{-i\bar{\theta}(\mathbf{r},t)}$$

where

$$\bar{\theta}(\mathbf{r},t) = t_n \theta^n(\mathbf{r},t)\,,$$

is a 3 × 3 matrix constructed from the $t^{n}$ matrices above and $\theta^{n} = \theta^{n}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ are eight [gauge functions](wikipedia://en/23912155) dependent on spatial position $\mathbf{r}$ and time *t*. [Matrix exponentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_exponentiation) is used in the transformation. The gauge covariant derivative transforms similarly. The functions $\theta^{n}$ here are similar to the gauge function $\chi(\mathbf{r}, t)$ when changing the [electromagnetic four-potential](wikipedia://en/731884) $A$, in spacetime components:

$$A'_\alpha (\mathbf{r},t) = A_\alpha (\mathbf{r},t) - \partial_\alpha \chi (\mathbf{r},t) \,$$

leaving the electromagnetic tensor $F$ invariant.

The quark fields are invariant under the [gauge transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation);[^o6]

$$\psi(\mathbf{r},t) \rightarrow e^{ig\bar{\theta}(\mathbf{r},t)}\psi(\mathbf{r},t)$$
