# Filtration (probability theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Filtration"}

In the [theory of stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), a subdiscipline of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **filtrations** are [totally ordered](wikipedia://en/30330) collections of subsets that are used to model the information that is available at a given point and therefore play an important role in the formalization of random (stochastic) processes.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal A, P)$ be a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) and let $I$ be an [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) with a [total order](wikipedia://en/30330) $\leq$ (often $\mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{R}^+$, or a subset of $\mathbb R^+$).

For every $i \in I$ let $\mathcal F_i$ be a [sub-*σ*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra#Sub_σ-algebras) of $\mathcal A$. Then

$$\mathbb F:= (\mathcal F_i)_{i \in I}$$

is called a filtration, if $\mathcal F_k \subseteq \mathcal F_\ell$ for all $k \leq \ell$. So filtrations are families of *σ*-algebras that are ordered non-decreasingly.[^o15] If $\mathbb F$ is a filtration, then $(\Omega, \mathcal A, \mathbb F, P)$ is called a **filtered probability space**.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let $(X_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ be a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) on the probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal A, P)$. Let $\sigma(X_k \mid k \leq n)$ denote the [*σ*-algebra generated by the random variables](wikipedia://en/29586#σ-algebra_generated_by_random_variable_or_vector) $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$. Then

$$\mathcal F_n:=\sigma(X_k \mid k \leq n)$$

is a *σ*-algebra and $\mathbb F= (\mathcal F_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is a filtration.

$\mathbb F$ really is a filtration, since by definition all $\mathcal F_n$ are *σ*-algebras and

$$\sigma(X_k \mid k \leq n) \subseteq \sigma(X_k \mid k \leq n+1).$$

This is known as the [natural filtration](wikipedia://en/5864648) of $\mathcal A$ with respect to $(X_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$.

:::

## Types of filtrations {#Types_of_filtrations}

### Right-continuous filtration {#Right-continuous_filtration}

If $\mathbb F= (\mathcal F_i)_{i \in I}$ is a filtration, then the corresponding **right-continuous filtration** is defined as[^o14]

$$\mathbb F^+:= (\mathcal F_i^+)_{i \in I},$$

with

$$\mathcal F_i^+:= \bigcap_{z > i} \mathcal F_z.$$

The filtration $\mathbb F$ itself is called right-continuous if $\mathbb F^+ = \mathbb F$.[^o16]

### Complete filtration {#Complete_filtration}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal F, P)$ be a probability space, and let

$$\mathcal N_P:= \{A \subseteq \Omega \mid A \subseteq B \text{ for some } B \in \mathcal F \text{ with } P(B)=0 \}$$

be the set of all sets that are contained within a $P$-[null set](wikipedia://en/21520).

A filtration $\mathbb F= (\mathcal F_i)_{i \in I}$ is called a **complete filtration**, if every $\mathcal F_i$ contains $\mathcal N_P$. This implies $(\Omega, \mathcal F_i, P)$ is a [complete measure space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_measure_space) for every $i \in I.$ (The converse is not necessarily true.)

::: {#o11 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Usual conditions"}

### Augmented filtration {#Augmented_filtration}

A filtration is called an **augmented filtration** if it is complete and right continuous. For every filtration $\mathbb F$ there exists a smallest augmented filtration $\tilde {\mathbb F}$ refining $\mathbb F$.

If a filtration is an augmented filtration, it is said to satisfy the **usual hypotheses** or the **usual conditions**.[^o16]
