# Cyclic surgery theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cyclic surgery theorem"}

In [three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-manifold) [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of mathematics, the **cyclic surgery theorem** states that, for a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446), [irreducible](wikipedia://en/1001293) three-manifold *M* whose boundary is a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) *T*, if *M* is not a [Seifert-fibered space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert-fibered_space) and *r,s* are slopes on *T* such that their [Dehn fillings](wikipedia://en/1240699) have cyclic [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004), then the distance between *r* and *s* (the minimal number of times that two simple closed curves in *T* representing *r* and *s* must intersect) is at most 1. Consequently, there are at most three Dehn fillings of *M* with cyclic fundamental group. The theorem appeared in a 1987 paper written by [Marc Culler](wikipedia://en/29258614), [Cameron Gordon](wikipedia://en/4695115), [John Luecke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Luecke_%28mathematician%29) and [Peter Shalen](wikipedia://en/3520910).[^o2]

:::
