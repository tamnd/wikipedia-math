# Carlitz–Wan conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carlitz–Wan conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Carlitz–Wan conjecture** classifies the possible [degrees](wikipedia://en/5930652) of exceptional polynomials over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) *F*<sub>*q*</sub> of *q* elements. A [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) *f*(*x*) in *F*<sub>*q*</sub>\[*x*\] of degree *d* is called exceptional over *F*<sub>*q*</sub> if every [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) factor (differing from *x* − *y*) or (*f*(*x*) − *f*(*y*))/(*x* − *y*)) over *F*<sub>*q*</sub> becomes reducible over the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of *F*<sub>*q*</sub>. If *q* > *d*<sup>4</sup>, then *f*(*x*) is exceptional [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *f*(*x*) is a [permutation polynomial](wikipedia://en/15641015) over *F*<sub>*q*</sub>.

:::

The Carlitz–Wan conjecture states that there are no exceptional polynomials of degree *d* over *F*<sub>*q*</sub> if gcd(*d*, *q* − 1) > 1.

In the special case that *q* is [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) and *d* is [even](wikipedia://en/143135), this [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) was proposed by [Leonard Carlitz](wikipedia://en/10356778) (1966) and [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by Fried, Guralnick, and Saxl (1993).[^o2] The general form of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture was proposed by [Daqing Wan](wikipedia://en/22098796) (1993)[^o3] and later proved by [Hendrik Lenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lenstra) (1995).[^o4]
