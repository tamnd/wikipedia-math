# Gustave Juvet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gustave Juvet"}

**Gustave Juvet** (born 25 September 1896, [La Côte-aux-Fées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Côte-aux-Fées), [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Neuchâtel) – 2 April 1936, [Valais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valais)) was a Swiss mathematician.[^o2] He made contributions to [relativity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativity_theory), [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology). He authored the first comprehensive French treatise on [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus) in 1922, and his work on [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305) and [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276) between 1930 and 1935 anticipated unified geometric approaches to physics that would later become influential in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Juvet received his *[licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_%28degree%29)* in mathematical sciences from the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel) in 1917 and then the same degree from the Sorbonne in 1919. He taught astronomy and geodesy from 1920 to 1928 at the University of Neuchâtel. In 1928 he became a professor at the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne), where he retained his academic position until his unexpected death from a heart attack in 1936. In 1926 he received his doctorate from the *Faculté des sciences de Paris*.[^o4]

Juvet was a person of very wide interests; in mathematics, he can be characterized as a mathematical physicist interested in relativity theory, quantum mechanics, and cosmology. He had translated Hermann Weyl's *Raum, Zeit, Materie* (1st edition 1918) into French already in 1922 (with R. Leroy) and was a prolific author who had mastered the Levi-Civita tensor calculus fairly early in his career (in Paris).[^o5]

He was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1928 in Bologna and in 1932 in Zürich. For the two years 1932 and 1933 he was the President of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society).

In adolescence, while attending Neuchâtel's gymnasium, he was a close friend of [Jean Piaget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget) (who became a famous psychologist) and [Rolin Wavre](wikipedia://en/50515882) (who became a prominent mathematician).[^o6] Juvet married in 1925.

In 1922, Juvet published *Introduction au calcul tensoriel et au calcul différentiel absolu*, the first comprehensive French treatise on [tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus) and [absolute differential calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_differential_calculus), establishing his reputation in the early development of relativity studies in France. From 1927 to 1928, in collaboration with [Ferdinand Gonseth](wikipedia://en/54012910), he advanced [five-dimensional](wikipedia://en/2851410) extensions of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) and [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism)—applying [Kaluza–Klein theory](wikipedia://en/17070) to the metric structure of [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) and to the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874). Between 1930 and 1935, he authored seminal papers on [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305) (including *Opérateurs de Dirac et équations de Maxwell* and works on [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276)), anticipating unified geometric approaches to physics later known as [geometrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrodynamics). His analyses of [axiomatic geometry](wikipedia://en/21317755) and structural methods in [modern physical theories](wikipedia://en/19594028) were cited by [Gaston Bachelard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Bachelard) and [Albert Lautman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lautman), reflecting Juvet's influence on the philosophical foundations of twentieth-century science.[^o28]
