# Density matrix embedding theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Density matrix embedding theory"}

The **density matrix embedding theory (DMET)** is a [numerical](wikipedia://en/21506) technique to solve strongly correlated electronic structure problems. By mapping the system to a fragment plus its entangled quantum bath, the local [electron correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_correlation) effects on the fragment can be accurately modeled by a [post-Hartree–Fock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Hartree–Fock) solver. This method has shown high-quality results in 1D- and 2D- [Hubbard models](wikipedia://en/1702398),[^o2] and in chemical model systems incorporating the fully interacting electronic Hamiltonian, including long-range interactions. [^o3]

:::

The basis of DMET is the [Schmidt decomposition](wikipedia://en/5582812) for quantum states, which shows that a given quantum many-body state, with macroscopically many degrees of freedom, K, can be represented exactly by an **Impurity** model consisting of 2N degrees of freedom for N\<\<K. Using an existing approximation (here called the effective lattice model) to the many-body state (for example in the mean-field approximation where correlations are neglected), DMET relates this effective lattice model to the impurity model by a one-body local potential, U. This potential is then optimised by requiring that the **[Density matrix](wikipedia://en/62844)** of the impurity model and effective lattice model projected onto the impurity cluster match. When this matching is determined self-consistently, U thus derived in principle exactly models the correlations of the system (since the mapping from the full Hamiltonian to the impurity Hamiltonian is exact).
