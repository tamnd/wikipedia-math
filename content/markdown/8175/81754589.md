# George Yevick

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Yevick"}

**George Yevick** (May 8, 1922 – October 6, 2011) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), inventor, and a [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) at [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Institute_of_Technology). His work on the [many-body problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-body_problem) yielded a foundation of classical fluid [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), the [Percus–Yevick approximation](wikipedia://en/10159772). He co-invented the first [e-reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-reader) with Adnan Wally in 1972. He is also known for his contributions to [David Bohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bohm)'s work in [quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics).

:::

Yevick was defeated in a run for the Democratic Party primary election for U.S. Congress in New Jersey in 1964.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Yevick was born in [Berwick, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick,_Pennsylvania).[^o5] He was of Jewish descent.[^o6] During the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States), George's [Scout leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_leader) lent him money to attend the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT), where he earned a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in 1943 and a [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) in 1947 under the advisement of [Victor Weisskopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Weisskopf)[^o7] at the [MIT Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory).[^o8][^o5] While at MIT, he met his wife [Miriam Yevick](wikipedia://en/81754592) (Lipschutz),[^o5] a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who had escaped from [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp) in 1940 during the [Nazi occupation of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_occupation_of_Belgium).[^o9] Their son, [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Yevick) and granddaughter, [Hannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Yevick), are also physicists.[^o10]

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

Yevick was appointed to the faculty at [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Institute_of_Technology) in 1947.[^o12] He spent his entire career there as a professor of Physics and Engineering Physics,[^o13] and he became a [Emeritus Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_Professor) in his retirement.[^o14] They awarded him with an [honorary degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree), a [Master of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Engineering), in 1958.[^o15]

In 1958, with [Jerome K. Percus](wikipedia://en/70331841), George co-authored a foundational study on classical fluid [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).[^o16] This work was later named the [Percus–Yevick approximation](wikipedia://en/10159772) (PY). The approximation helped established a widely used family of liquid-state theories despite relying on uncontrolled approximations.[^o17] With the [hypernetted-chain equation](wikipedia://en/15398902), the PY is a foundational closure approximation for computing [radial distribution functions](wikipedia://en/4538599) and related thermodynamic properties of dense fluids.[^o18]

Yevick and his wife corresponded frequently with [David Bohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bohm) while he was at [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo). They are most known for contributing to and circulating Bohm's [causal interpretation of quantum theory](wikipedia://en/54717).[^o19] Bohm also asked for Yevick's thoughts on his theory of [luminiferous aether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminiferous_aether) as a unification of physics using [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_%28physics%29) in relation to [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) and [vacuum energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_energy). From Yevick's wife, Bohm requested a [mathematical proof](wikipedia://en/82285) to support a statistical mechanics argument that rare exceptions are negligible, such that typical behavior could be treated as physically reliable.[^o20][^o19] Yevick's work was later used in [holographic](wikipedia://en/14286) theories of [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) developed by [David Finkelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Finkelstein).[^o21]

### Inventions {#Inventions}

Yevick developed more than 40 [patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent).[^o5][^o23] In 1972, Yevick, with Adnan Wally, co-invented the first [e-reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-reader), which had an [LCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCD) that was resistant to washout. It was publicized on the front page of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* and predicted to revolutionize the publishing industry.[^o24][^o25] However, the invention happened in parallel with the onset of the development of [ebooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebook) by [Michael S. Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_S._Hart)[^o26] and personal computing devices, overshadowing the work and preventing its commercialization.[^o24]

## Politics and advocacy {#Politics_and_advocacy}

Yevick advocated for establishing a science and technology museum in New York City based on an educational model, featuring interactive exhibits and modern audiovisual interpretation to demonstrate basic scientific principles to the public.[^o28]

A resident of [Leonia, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonia,_New_Jersey), Yevick ran as a [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_%28United_States%29) in the [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_election) of the [1964 United States House of Representatives elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections) for a seat for [New Jersey's 9th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey's_9th_congressional_district).[^o29] He was defeated by [Henry Helstoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Helstoski).[^o30] His platform was centered around [civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights).[^o31] He was the elected chair of the New Jersey Democratic Council,[^o32][^o33] an [anti-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-war_movement) political group of [Reform Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Democrat).[^o34]
