# Dugundji extension theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dugundji extension theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Dugundji extension theorem** is a theorem in [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) due to American mathematician [James Dugundji](wikipedia://en/36501408). It is directly related to the [Tietze–Urysohn extension theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tietze–Urysohn_extension_theorem) — about extending [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on [normal spaces](wikipedia://en/48629) — of which it is, in a sense, a generalization.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $X$ be a [metrizable space](wikipedia://en/19738), $A$ a closed subset of X, and $L$ a [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844). Then:

- Every continuous map $f : A \to L$ admits a continuous extension $F : X \to L$ such that the image $F(X)$ is contained in the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of $f(A).$[^o4]

or, equivalently:

- Every continuous map from $A$ into a [convex subset](wikipedia://en/6292) $K$ of $L$ admits a continuous extension from $X$ into $K.$[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Comparison with the Tietze–Urysohn extension theorem"}

## Comparison with the Tietze–Urysohn extension theorem {#Comparison_with_the_Tietze–Urysohn_extension_theorem}

The first version of the Tietze extension theorem corresponds to the special case of the above theorem where the target space L is the real line ℝ. Urysohn generalized this to replacing the domain being a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) by an arbitrary normal space. The Dugundji extension theorem is a transverse generalization, replacing the target ℝ by an arbitrary locally convex space. There is another generalization of the Tietze theorem assuming that the domain X is [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) and the target L is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989).

:::

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Fix some metric $d$ on $X.$ Consider the open cover of $X - A$ that consists of the [open balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_ball) $B(x, d(x, A)/2)$ for $x \in X - A.$ Since every metric space is [paracompact](wikipedia://en/48631), there exists a locally finite open cover $\{ U_i \}_{i \in I}$ of $X - A$ such that each $U_i$ is contained in one of those balls. Choose a partition of unity $\varphi_i$ subordinate to this cover. For each $i$, pick a point $a_i \in A$ satisfying

$$d(x_i, a_i) \le 2 d(x_i, A),$$

which is possible since for each $\epsilon > 0$, there is an $a \in A$ with $d(x, a) \le d(x, A) + \epsilon$. Define the extension $F$ on $X$ by:

$$F(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if }x\in A,\\\sum_i \varphi_i(x)f(a_i)&\text{else.}\end{cases}$$

The map $F$ is clearly continuous on $X - A$. We shall then show it is continuous at each point $a$ in $A$ as well. For each $x$ in $U_i$, we have: $2 d(x, x_i) \le d(x_i, A)$ or

$$d(x, x_i) + 2 d(x_i, A) \le 5 d(x_i, A) - 5 d(x, x_i).$$

Thus, we have:

$$d(x, a_i) \le 5 d(x, A)$$

and then

$$d(a, a_i) \le 6 d(a, x).$$

Now, let a convex neighborhood *C* of $F(a)$ be given. Then, since $f$ is continuous, there is some $\delta > 0$ such that $f(B(a, \delta) \cap A) \subset C$. Then we have $F(B(a, \delta/6)) \subset C$ by the above inequality, completing the proof of the continuity. $\square$

:::
