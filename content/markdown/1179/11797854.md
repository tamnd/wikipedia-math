# Double complex

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double complex"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [Homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), a **double complex** is a generalization of a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) where instead of having a $\mathbb{Z}$-grading, the objects in the bicomplex have a $\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$-grading. The most general definition of a double complex, or a bicomplex, is given with objects in an [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854) $\mathcal{A}$. A bicomplex[^o2] is a sequence of objects $C_{p,q} \in \text{Ob}(\mathcal{A})$ with two differentials, the **horizontal differential**

> $$d^h: C_{p,q} \to C_{p+1,q}$$

and the **vertical differential**

> $$d^v:C_{p,q} \to C_{p,q+1}$$

which have the compatibility relation

> $$d_h\circ d_v = d_v\circ d_h$$

Hence a double complex is a [commutative diagram](wikipedia://en/138484) of the form

> $$\begin{matrix} & & \vdots & & \vdots & & \\ & & \uparrow & & \uparrow & & \\ \cdots & \to & C_{p,q+1} & \to & C_{p+1,q+1} & \to & \cdots \\ & & \uparrow & & \uparrow & & \\ \cdots & \to & C_{p,q} & \to & C_{p+1,q} & \to & \cdots \\ & & \uparrow & & \uparrow & & \\ & & \vdots & & \vdots & & \\ \end{matrix}$$

where the rows and columns form chain complexes.

:::

Some authors[^o3] instead require that the squares anticommute. That is

> $$d_h\circ d_v + d_v\circ d_h = 0.$$

This eases the definition of [Total Complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Complexes). By setting $f_{p,q} = (-1)^p d^v_{p,q} \colon C_{p,q} \to C_{p,q-1}$, we can switch between having commutativity and anticommutativity. If the commutative definition is used, this alternating sign will have to show up in the definition of Total Complexes.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

There are many natural examples of bicomplexes that come up in nature. In particular, for a [Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923), there is a bicomplex associated to it[^o6]<sup>pg 7-8</sup> which can be used to construct its [de-Rham complex](wikipedia://en/221530).

Another common example of bicomplexes are in [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), where on an [almost complex manifold](wikipedia://en/514258) $X$ there's a bicomplex of differential forms $\Omega^{p,q}(X)$ whose components are linear or anti-linear. For example, if $z_1,z_2$ are the complex coordinates of $\mathbb{C}^2$ and $\overline{z}_1,\overline{z}_2$ are the complex conjugate of these coordinates, a $(1,1)$-form is of the form

> $$f_{a,b}dz_a\wedge d\overline{z}_b$$

:::
