# Fourier algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fourier algebra"}

**Fourier** and related [algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) occur naturally in the [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) of [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [groups](wikipedia://en/19447). They play an important role in the [duality theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_theory) of these groups. The Fourier–Stieltjes algebra and the Fourier–Stieltjes transform on the Fourier algebra of a locally compact group were introduced by [Pierre Eymard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Eymard) in 1964.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Informal {#Informal}

Let $G$ be a locally compact abelian group, and $\hat{G}$ the [dual group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_dual) of $G$. Then $L_1(\hat{\mathit{G}})$ is the space of all functions on $\hat{G}$ which are integrable with respect to the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on $\hat{G}$, and it has a [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665) structure where the product of two functions is [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519). We define $A(G)$ to be the set of Fourier transforms of functions in $L_1(\hat{\mathit{G}})$, and it is a closed sub-algebra of $CB(G)$, the space of bounded continuous complex-valued functions on $G$ with pointwise multiplication. We call $A(G)$ the Fourier algebra of $G$.

Similarly, we write $M(\hat{\mathit{G}})$ for the measure algebra on $\hat{G}$, meaning the convolution algebra of all finite regular [Borel measures](wikipedia://en/4322) on $\hat{G}$. We define $B(G)$ to be the set of Fourier-Stieltjes transforms of measures in $M(\hat{\mathit{G}})$. It is a closed sub-algebra of $CB(G)$, the space of bounded continuous complex-valued functions on $G$ with pointwise multiplication. We call $B(G)$ the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra of $G$. Equivalently, $B(G)$ can be defined as the linear span of the set $P(G)$ of continuous [positive-definite functions](wikipedia://en/649616) on $G$.

Since $L_1(\hat{\mathit{G}})$ is naturally included in $M(\hat{\mathit{G}})$, and since the Fourier-Stieltjes transform of an $L_1(\hat{\mathit{G}})$ function is just the Fourier transform of that function, we have that $A(G) \subset B(G)$. In fact, $A(G)$ is a closed ideal in $B(G)$.

### Formal {#Formal}

While non-abelian groups don't have dual groups, we may still define the Fourier algebras for any locally compact group in terms of [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788).[^o7] Let $G$ be a locally compact group. For any unitary representation $\pi$ of $G$ on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $H_\pi$, and any $\xi,\eta\in H_\pi$, we denote by $\pi_{\xi,\eta}$ the complex-valued function on $G$ defined by $\pi_{\xi,\eta}(g)=\langle \pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle$. The Fourier-Stieltjes algebra $B(G)$ is then defined as the algebra of all complex functions on $G$ that arise as matrix coefficients $\pi_{\xi,\eta}$ for some unitary representation $\pi$ of $G$ and some $\xi,\eta\in H_\pi$. $B(G)$ forms an algebra with pointwise addition and multiplication, as for any unitary representations $\pi$ and $\rho$ of $G$, $\pi_{\xi_1,\eta_1}+\rho_{\xi_2,\eta_2}=(\pi\oplus\rho)_{\xi_1\oplus\xi_2,\eta_1\oplus\eta_2}$ and $\pi_{\xi_1,\eta_1}\rho_{\xi_2,\eta_2}=(\pi\otimes\rho)_{\xi_1\otimes\xi_2,\eta_1\otimes\eta_2}$. We define the norm in $B(G)$ to be given by $\| u\|=\text{inf} \{ \| \xi\| \|\eta\| : u(g)=\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle \text{ for some representation } \pi \}$. This norm makes $B(G)$ a [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665). We define the Fourier algebra $A(G)$ to be the closed subalgebra spanned by the matrix coefficients of the left [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011) of $G$ on $L^2(G)$.

### Abelian case {#Abelian_case}

Let $B(\mathit{G})$ be a Fourier–Stieltjes algebra and $A(\mathit{G})$ be a Fourier algebra such that the locally compact group $\mathit{G}$ is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974). Let $M(\widehat{\mathit{G}})$ be the measure algebra of finite measures on $\widehat{G}$ and let $L_1(\widehat{\mathit{G}})$ be the [convolution algebra](wikipedia://en/381010#The_convolution_algebra_L1.28G.29) of [integrable](wikipedia://en/40645#Haar_integral) [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) on $\widehat{G}$, where $\widehat{\mathit{G}}$ is the character group of the Abelian group $\mathit{G}$.

The Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a finite measure $\mu$ on $\widehat{\mathit{G}}$ is the function $\widehat{\mu}$ on $\mathit{G}$ defined by

$$\widehat{\mu}(x) = \int_{\widehat{G}} \overline{X(x)} \, d \mu(X), \quad x \in G$$

The space $B(\mathit{G})$ of these functions is an algebra under pointwise multiplication is isomorphic to the measure algebra $M(\widehat{\mathit{G}})$. Restricted to $L_1(\widehat{\mathit{G}})$, viewed as a subspace of $M(\widehat{\mathit{G}})$, the Fourier–Stieltjes transform is the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) on $L_1(\widehat{\mathit{G}})$ and its image is, by definition, the Fourier algebra $A(\mathit{G})$. The generalized [Bochner theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochner_theorem) states that a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) on $\mathit{G}$ is equal, [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688), to the Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a non-negative finite measure on $\widehat{G}$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is positive definite. Thus, $B(\mathit{G})$ can be defined as the [linear span](wikipedia://en/56353) of the set of continuous positive-definite functions on $\mathit{G}$. This definition is still valid when $\mathit{G}$ is not Abelian.

## Results {#Results}

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Helson–Kahane–Katznelson–Rudin theorem"}

### Helson–Kahane–Katznelson–Rudin theorem {#Helson–Kahane–Katznelson–Rudin_theorem}

Let $A(G)$ be the Fourier algebra of a compact group $G$. Building upon the work of [Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185), [Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462), [Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643), and [Beurling](wikipedia://en/286530), in 1959 [Helson](wikipedia://en/35258187), [Kahane](wikipedia://en/6949961), [Katznelson](wikipedia://en/5643045), and [Rudin](wikipedia://en/5491766) proved that, when $G$ is compact and abelian, a function f defined on a closed convex subset of the plane operates in $A(G)$ if and only if $f$ is real analytic.[^o13] In 1969 [Dunkl](wikipedia://en/35216154) proved the result holds when $G$ is compact and contains an infinite abelian subgroup.

:::

### Relationship to group operator algebras {#Relationship_to_group_operator_algebras}

If $G$ is a locally compact group, we may define the associated [group algebras](wikipedia://en/381010) $C^*(G)$ to be the enveloping [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) of $L^1(G)$ and the [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) $VN(G)$ associated to the left regular representation $\lambda$ of $G$ on $L^2(G)$. Then $B(G)$ is the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) [dual](wikipedia://en/7988) of $C^*(G)$ and $VN(G)$ is the dual of $A(G)$. The pairing between $A(G)$ and $VN(G)$ is defined by, for $T\in VN(G)$ and $u\in A(G)$ defined $u(x)=\langle \lambda (x)\xi ,\eta\rangle$, by $\langle T,u\rangle=\langle T\xi,\eta\rangle$.

In the case where $G$ is abelian, we have $A(G)\cong L^1(\hat{G})$ and $VN(G)\cong L^\infty(\hat{G})$, so this reduces to the fact that $L^\infty(\hat{G})$ is the dual of $L^1(\hat{G})$. Likewise, we have $C^*(G)\cong C_0(\hat{G})$ (the algebra of continuous functions on $\hat{G}$ vanishing at infinity) and $B(G)\cong M(\hat{G})$, so that $B(G)$ is the dual of $C^*(G)$ reduces to the fact that $M(\hat{G})$ is the dual of $C_0(\hat{G})$.
