# MaxDDBS

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="MaxDDBS"}

The **Maximum Degree-and-Diameter-Bounded Subgraph problem (MaxDDBS)** is a problem in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a connected host graph $G$, an [upper bound](wikipedia://en/42693) for the [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) $\Delta$, and an upper bound for the [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) $D$, we look for the largest subgraph $S$ of $G$ with maximum degree at most $\Delta$ and diameter at most $D$.[^o4]

This problem is also referred to as the **Degree-Diameter Subgraph Problem**, as it contains the [degree diameter problem](wikipedia://en/19726161) as a special case (namely, by taking a sufficiently large [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) as a host graph). Despite being a natural generalization of the [Degree-Diameter Problem](wikipedia://en/19726161), MaxDDBS only began to be investigated in 2011, while research in the Degree-Diameter Problem has been active since the 1960s.[^o4]

There is also a weighted version of the problem (MaxWDDBS) where edges have positive integral weights, and the diameter is measured as the sum of weights along the shortest path.[^o4]

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Regarding its computational complexity, the problem is [NP-hard](wikipedia://en/54681), and not in [APX](wikipedia://en/2018925) (i.e. it cannot be approximated to within a constant factor in [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) time).[^o6] The problem remains NP-hard even when restricting to only one constraint (either degree or diameter).[^o4]

The Largest Degree-Bounded Subgraph Problem is NP-hard when the subgraph must be connected, while the Maximum Diameter-Bounded Subgraph becomes the [maximum clique problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_clique_problem) for $D = 1$, which was one of [Karp's 21 NP-complete problems](wikipedia://en/2012564).[^o4]

## Bounds and relationships {#Bounds_and_relationships}

The order of any graph with maximum degree $\Delta$ and diameter $D$ cannot exceed the [Moore bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_bound):[^o4]

$$M_{\Delta,D} = 1 + \Delta + \Delta(\Delta - 1) + \cdots + \Delta(\Delta - 1)^{D-1}$$

This bound also serves as a theoretical upper bound for MaxDDBS. If we denote by $N_{\Delta,D}$ the order of the largest graph with maximum degree $\Delta$ and diameter $D$, then for any solution $S$ of MaxDDBS with $n$ vertices:

$$n \leq N_{\Delta,D} \leq M_{\Delta,D}$$

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

MaxDDBS has diverse practical applications:[^o4]

- **Parallel and distributed computing**: Communication time is crucial in [parallel processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing). Identifying a subnetwork of bounded degree and diameter within a parallel architecture enables efficient computation.
- **Network security and botnets**: In [botnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botnet) analysis, attackers may select subnetworks with specific degree and diameter constraints to maximize damage while avoiding detection. Understanding MaxDDBS helps predict attacking network parameters and develop defensive measures.
- **Biological networks**: The problem has been applied to [protein interaction networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein–protein_interaction) to identify network cores. Bounding both degree and diameter (rather than diameter alone) can reveal richer interaction patterns.

:::

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

A greedy heuristic algorithm has been proposed for MaxWDDBS with a worst-case approximation ratio of $\frac{\min(n,N_{\Delta,D})}{\Delta+1}$, where $n$ is the number of vertices in the host graph.[^o4] The algorithm starts with a $\Delta$-star and grows the subgraph by adding edges incident to live vertices until no more edges can be added while maintaining the degree constraint.

For the diameter-bounded variant alone, an algorithm exists with approximation ratio $O(n^{1/2})$.[^o4]

Experimental studies on various host graphs show that the [greedy algorithm](wikipedia://en/89247) often performs significantly better than its theoretical worst-case bound suggests, such as on [antiprism graphs](wikipedia://en/45012210) or [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477) (Watts-Strogatz and Barabási-Albert models).[^o4]

:::

## Special cases in specific host graphs {#Special_cases_in_specific_host_graphs}

The problem has been studied for various host graph families, with bounds established for mesh networks, hypercubes, honeycomb networks, triangular networks, butterfly networks, Beneš networks, and oxide networks.[^o13]

### Mesh networks {#Mesh_networks}

When the host graph is a $k$ -dimensional [mesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesh_network), the problem relates to counting [lattice points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_point) in balls under the [$L^{1}$ metric](wikipedia://en/408354).[^o6]

For a mesh with $\Delta = 2k$, the largest subgraph contains as many vertices as lattice points in a closed ball of radius $D/2$.[^o6]

The number of lattice points $|B_k(p)|$ in a maximal ball of radius $p$ in $k$ dimensions is given by:[^o6]

- For even diameter $D = 2p$: These are the [Delannoy numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delannoy_numbers)
- For odd diameter $D = 2p + 1$: These form a [Riordan array](wikipedia://en/38457493) of coordination sequences

Specific constructions have been developed for:[^o6]

- 3D mesh with $\Delta = 4$:
  - Achieves $\frac{4p^3}{3} + 2p^2 - \frac{4p}{3} + 3$ vertices for $D = 2p$
- 2D mesh with $\Delta = 3$:
  - Achieves $2p^2 - 2p + 1$ vertices for $D = 2p$

These constructions are asymptotically optimal, with average degree approaching $\Delta$ as $p \to \infty$.

### Hypercube {#Hypercube}

For the $k$-dimensional [hypercube](wikipedia://en/6706815) $Q_k$, when $D \leq \Delta$, there exists a subcube $Q_\Delta$ containing a subgraph of order:

$$\Phi_\Delta(D) = \sum_{i=0}^{D} \binom{\Delta}{i}$$

This represents the volume of a [Hamming ball](wikipedia://en/79346579) of radius $D$.[^o4]
