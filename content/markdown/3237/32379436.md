# Charles W. Curtis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles W. Curtis"}

*Image: Curtis charles w.jpg.* Charles W. Curtis

**Charles Whittlesey Curtis** (October 13, 1926 – May 3, 2026) was an American mathematician and [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), known for his work in [finite group theory](wikipedia://en/41890#Finite_group_theory) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200). He was a professor of mathematics at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Curtis received a bachelor's degree from [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College) in 1948,[^o3] and his Ph.D. from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1951, under the supervision of [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson). He taught at the [University of Wisconsin&ndash;Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin&ndash;Madison) from 1954 to 1963.[^o5] Subsequently, he moved to the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon), where he was an [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor).[^o6]

While at Yale, on June 17, 1950 in [Cheshire, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire,_Connecticut), Curtis married his wife Elizabeth, a [kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten) teacher and childcare provider. At the time of their 50th anniversary in 2000, they had three grandchildren.[^o7]

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

Curtis died in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon), on May 3, 2026, at the age of 99.[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Curtis introduced [Curtis duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_duality), a [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) operation on the [characters](wikipedia://en/293418) of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615). His book with [Irving Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Reiner) (Curtis Reiner 1962) was the standard text on representation theory for many years.
