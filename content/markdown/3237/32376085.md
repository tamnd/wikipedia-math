# Consumer math

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Consumer math"}

**Consumer math** comprises practical [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) techniques used in [commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce) and everyday life. In the United States, consumer math is typically offered in [high schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school), some [elementary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_school), or in some colleges which grant [associate's degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate's_degrees).

:::

A U.S. consumer math course might include a review of [elementary arithmetic](wikipedia://en/1567386), including [fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractions), [decimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimals), and [percentages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percentages). Elementary [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) is often included as well, in the context of solving practical business problems. The practical applications typically include: changing money, [checking accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checking_accounts), [budgeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budgeting), [price discounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discounts_and_allowances), [markups and markdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markup_%28business%29), [payroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payroll) calculations, investing ([simple and compound interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_%28economics%29)),[^o2][^o3] [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxes), [consumer and business credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_%28finance%29), and [mortgages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgages).

The emphasis in these courses is on computational skills and their practical application, with practical application being predominant. For instance, while computational formulas are covered in the material on interest and mortgages, the use of prepared tables based on those formulas is also presented and emphasized.
