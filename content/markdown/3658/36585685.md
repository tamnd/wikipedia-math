# Pernicious number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pernicious number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **pernicious number** is a positive integer such that the [Hamming weight](wikipedia://en/1127884) of its [binary representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system) is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), that is, there is a prime number of 1s when it is written as a binary number.[^o2]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

No power of two is a pernicious number. This is trivially true, because powers of two in binary form are represented as a one followed by zeros. So each power of two has a Hamming weight of one, and [one is not considered to be a prime](wikipedia://en/23666#Primality_of_one).[^o5] On the other hand, every number of the form $2^n+1$ with $n>1$, including every [Fermat number](wikipedia://en/91127), is a pernicious number. This is because the sum of the digits in binary form is 2, which is a prime number.[^o5]

A [Mersenne number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number) $2^n-1$ has a binary representation consisting of $n$ ones, and is pernicious when $n$ is prime. Every [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) is a Mersenne number for prime $n$, and is therefore pernicious. By the [Euclid–Euler theorem](wikipedia://en/43350725), the even [perfect numbers](wikipedia://en/23670) take the form $2^{n-1}(2^n-1)$ for a Mersenne prime $2^n-1$; the binary representation of such a number consists of a prime number $n$ of ones, followed by $n-1$ zeros. Therefore, every even perfect number is pernicious.[^o8][^o9]
