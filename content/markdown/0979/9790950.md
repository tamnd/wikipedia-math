# Regular category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regular category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **regular category** is a category with [finite limits](wikipedia://en/39248) and [coequalizers](wikipedia://en/431940) of all pairs of morphisms called **kernel pairs**, satisfying certain *exactness* conditions. In that way, regular categories recapture many properties of [abelian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_categories), like the existence of *images*, without requiring additivity. At the same time, regular categories provide a foundation for the study of a fragment of [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983), known as regular logic.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A category *C* is called **regular** if it satisfies the following three properties:[^o4]

- *C* is [finitely complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_complete_category).
- If *f* : *X* → *Y* is a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) in *C*, and

*Image: Regular\_category\_1.png.*

is a [pullback](wikipedia://en/841689), then the coequalizer of *p*<sub>0</sub>, *p*<sub>1</sub> exists. The pair (*p*<sub>0</sub>, *p*<sub>1</sub>) is called the **kernel pair** of *f*. Being a pullback, the kernel pair is unique up to a unique [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828).

- If *f* : *X* → *Y* is a morphism in *C*, and

*Image: Regular\_category\_2.png.*

is a pullback, and if *f* is a [regular epimorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_epimorphism), then *g* is a regular epimorphism as well.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of regular categories include:

- **[Set](wikipedia://en/26829)**, the category of [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) and [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) between the sets
- More generally, every [elementary topos](wikipedia://en/815802)
- **[Grp](wikipedia://en/800092)**, the category of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) and [group homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396)
- The category of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) and [ring homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/26411)
- More generally, the category of models of any [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753)
- Every [bounded meet-semilattice](wikipedia://en/755647), with morphisms given by the order relation
- Every [abelian category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_categories)

The following categories are *not* regular:

- **[Top](wikipedia://en/435051)**, the category of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) and [continuous functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29)
- **[Cat](wikipedia://en/2072741)**, the category of [small categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category) and [functors](wikipedia://en/10987)

:::

## Epi-mono factorization {#Epi-mono_factorization}

In a regular category, the [regular epimorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_epimorphism) and the [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538) form a [factorization system](wikipedia://en/7149012). Every morphism *f:X→Y* can be factorized into a regular epimorphism *e:X→E* followed by a monomorphism *m:E→Y*, so that *f=me*. The factorization is unique in the sense that if *e':X→E'*is another regular epimorphism and *m':E'→Y* is another monomorphism such that *f=m'e**, then there exists an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/53759#Types_of_morphisms)*****h:E→E' *such that*he=e' *and*m'h=m*. The monomorphism*m*is called the****image**of*****f*.***

## Exact sequences and regular functors {#Exact_sequences_and_regular_functors}

In a regular category, a diagram of the form $R\rightrightarrows X\to Y$ is said to be an **exact sequence** if it is both a coequalizer and a kernel pair. The terminology is a generalization of [exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_sequences) in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410): in an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063), a diagram

$$R\;\overset r{\underset s\rightrightarrows}\; X\xrightarrow{f} Y$$

is exact in this sense if and only if $0\to R\xrightarrow{(r,s)}X\oplus X\xrightarrow{(f,-f)} Y\to 0$ is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) in the usual sense.

A functor between regular categories is called **regular**, if it preserves finite limits and coequalizers of kernel pairs. A functor is regular if and only if it preserves finite limits and exact sequences. For this reason, regular functors are sometimes called **exact functors**. Functors that preserve finite limits are often said to be **left exact**.

## Regular logic and regular categories {#Regular_logic_and_regular_categories}

Regular logic is the fragment of [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) that can express statements of the form

$$\forall x (\phi (x) \to \psi (x)),$$

where $\phi$ and $\psi$ are regular [formulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28mathematical_logic%29) i.e. formulae built up from [atomic formulae](wikipedia://en/4472066), the truth constant, binary [meets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_%28mathematics%29) (conjunction) and [existential quantification](wikipedia://en/91420). Such formulae can be interpreted in a regular category, and the interpretation is a model of a [sequent](wikipedia://en/252355) $\forall x (\phi (x) \to \psi (x))$, if the interpretation of $\phi$ factors through the interpretation of $\psi$.[^o12] This gives for each theory (set of sequents) *T* and for each regular category *C* a category **Mod**(*T*,C) of models of *T* in *C*. This construction gives a functor **Mod**(*T*,-):**RegCat**→**Cat** from the category **RegCat** of [small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category) regular categories and regular functors to small categories. It is an important result that for each theory *T* there is a regular category *R(T)*, such that for each regular category *C* there is an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051)

$$\mathbf{Mod}(T,C)\cong \mathbf{RegCat}(R(T),C),$$

which is natural in *C*. Here, *R(T)* is called the *classifying* category of the regular theory *T.* Up to equivalence any small regular category arises in this way as the classifying category of some regular theory.[^o12]

## Exact (effective) categories {#Exact_(effective)_categories}

The theory of [equivalence relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_relations) is a regular theory. An equivalence relation on an object $X$ of a regular category is a monomorphism into $X \times X$ that satisfies the interpretations of the conditions for reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity.

Every kernel pair $p_0, p_1: R \rightarrow X$ defines an equivalence relation $R \rightarrow X \times X$. Conversely, an equivalence relation is said to be **effective** if it arises as a kernel pair.[^o15] An equivalence relation is effective if and only if it has a coequalizer and it is the kernel pair of this.

A regular category is said to be **exact**, or **exact in the sense of [Barr](wikipedia://en/12296704)**, or **effective regular**, if every equivalence relation is effective.[^o16] (Note that the term "exact category" is also used differently, for the [exact categories in the sense of Quillen](wikipedia://en/10987985).)

### Examples of exact categories {#Examples_of_exact_categories}

- The [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) is exact in this sense, and so is any (elementary) [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464). Every equivalence relation has a coequalizer, which is found by taking [equivalence classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_classes).
- Every [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) is exact.
- Every category that is [monadic](wikipedia://en/355140) over the category of sets is exact.
- The category of [Stone spaces](wikipedia://en/380219) is regular, but not exact.
