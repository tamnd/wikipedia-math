# Joel Lee Brenner

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Joel Lee Brenner"}

**Joel Lee Brenner** (1912-8-2 – 1997) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_theory), [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890). He is known as the translator of several popular [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) texts. He was a teaching professor at some dozen colleges and universities and was a Senior Mathematician at [Stanford Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Research_Institute) from 1956 to 1968. He published over one hundred scholarly papers, 35 with coauthors, and wrote book reviews.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 1930 Brenner earned a B.A. degree with major in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). In graduate study there he was influenced by Hans Brinkmann, [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916), and [Marshall Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Stone). He was granted the Ph.D. in February 1936.[^o5] Brenner later described some of his [reminiscences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reminiscence) of his student days at Harvard and of the state of American mathematics in the 1930s in an article for [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly).[^o7]

In 1951 Brenner published his findings about matrices with [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) entries.[^o8] He developed the idea of a [characteristic root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_root) of a quaternion matrix (an eigenvalue) and shows that they must exist. He also shows that a quaternion matrix is unitarily-equivalent to a [triangular matrix](wikipedia://en/374222).

In 1956 he became a Senior Mathematician at [Stanford Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Research_Institute). Brenner, in collaboration with Donald W. Bushaw and S. Evanusa, assisted in the translation and revision of [Felix Gantmacher](wikipedia://en/27928415)'s *Applications of the Theory of Matrices* (1959).[^o9]

Brenner translated [Nikolaj Nikolaevič Krasovskii's](wikipedia://en/6631582) book *Stability of motion: applications of Lyapunov's second method to differential systems and equations with delay* (1963). He also translated and edited the book *Problems in differential equations* by [Aleksei Fedorovich Filippov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Fedorovich_Filippov).

Brenner translated *Problems in Higher Algebra*[^o10] by [D. K. Faddeev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._K._Faddeev) and I.S. Sominiski. The [exercises](wikipedia://en/35557313) in this book covered [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity), as well as some [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384).

In 1959 Brenner generalized propositions by [Alexander Ostrowski](wikipedia://en/5827091) and [G. B. Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._B._Price) on [minors](wikipedia://en/262107) of a [diagonally dominant matrix](wikipedia://en/5262748).[^o11] His work is credited with stimulating a reawakening of interest in the [permanent](wikipedia://en/215889) of a matrix.[^o12]

One of the challenges in linear algebra is to find the [eigenvalues and eigenvectors](wikipedia://en/2161429) of a square matrix of complex numbers. In 1931 [S. A. Gershgorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._A._Gershgorin) described geometric bounds on the eigenvectors in terms of the matrix elements. This result known as the [Gershgorin circle theorem](wikipedia://en/2470414) has been used as a basis for extension. In 1964 Brenner reported on *Theorems of Gersgorin Type*.[^o13] In 1967 at [University of Wisconsin—Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin—Madison), working in the Mathematics Research Center, he produced a technical report *New root-location theorems for partitioned matrices*.[^o14]

In 1968 Brenner, following [Alston Householder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alston_Householder), published "Gersgorin theorems by Householder’s proof".[^o15] In 1970 he published the survey article (21 references) "Gersgorin theorems, regularity theorems, and bounds for determinants of partitioned matrices".[^o16] The article was extended with "Some determinantal identities".[^o17]

In 1971 Brenner extended his geometry of the spectrum of a square complex matrix deeper into abstract algebra with his paper "Regularity theorems and Gersgorin theorems for matrices over rings with valuation".[^o18] He writes, "Theorems can be extended to non-commutative domains, in particular to [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) matrices. Secondly, the [ring of polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_polynomials) has a valuation ... a different type of regularity ..."

## Collaborations {#Collaborations}

Joel Lee Brenner was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1936.

Beasley relates that he

was a graduate student and \[Brenner\] was visiting the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) in 1966-67. Shortly after arriving at UBC, Joel circulated a memo to all the graduate students, informing them that he had several open problems in various areas of mathematics and would share them with willing students. Hoping to get a problem in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) that I might work into a thesis, I went to his office and inquired about the problems. He presented me the Van der Waerden conjecture, which he informed me would be quite difficult, and after defining the [permanent](wikipedia://en/215889) for me sent me off with several problems concerning the permanent function. His encouragement and enthusiasm persevered through several "proofs" of the [Van der Waerden conjecture](wikipedia://en/215889#Van_der_Waerden's_conjecture), and soon some of the less well-known problems had been solved. He would always tell me how a proposed attack would work and leave me to fight out the details. Those exchanges led to the publication of my first paper, and I became his thirteenth coauthor. By the time Joel had left UBC in the spring of 1967, I was firmly entrenched in matrix theory.[^o5]

In 1981 Brenner and [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon) collaborated to polish an idea due to [H. W. Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._W._Kuhn) for proving the [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414). In the solution by Eric S. Rosenthal to a problem in the *American Mathematical Monthly* posted by Harry D. Ruderman,[^o20] Kuhn's work from 1974 was cited. A query was made and prompted an article by Brenner and Lyndon.[^o21] The version of the fundamental theorem stated was as follows:

Let *P*(*z*) be a non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients. Then there is a positive number *S* > 0, depending only on *P*, with the following property:

for every δ > 0 there is a complex number *z* such that |*z*| ≤ *S* and |*P*(*z*)| \< δ .

Brenner ultimately acquired 35 coauthors in his publications.

### Alternating group {#Alternating_group}

Given an ordered set Ω with *n* elements, the [even permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_permutation) on it determine the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) A<sub>n</sub>. In 1960 Brenner proposed the following research problem in group theory:[^o23] For which A<sub>n</sub> does there exist an element *a*<sub>n</sub> such that every element *g* is similar to a [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) of *a*<sub>n</sub>? Brenner states that the property is true for 4 \< *n* \< 10; in symbols it may be expressed

$$\exists a_n \ \forall g \ \exists u \ \exists y \ (g = u^{-1}(a_n y a_n^{-1} y^{-1}) u) .$$

The alternating groups are [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600), and in 1971 Brenner began a series of articles titled "Covering theorems for finite simple groups". He was interested in the [cycle type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_type) of [cyclic permutations](wikipedia://en/449166), and when A<sub>n</sub> ⊂ *C C*, where *C* is a [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) of a certain type.[^o24][^o25][^o26]

In 1977 he posed the question, "What permutations in A<sub>n</sub> can be expressed as a product of permutations of periods k and l" ?[^o27]
