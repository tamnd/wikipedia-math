# Modal companion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modal companion"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), a **modal companion** of a [superintuitionistic](wikipedia://en/769022) (intermediate) logic *L* is a [normal](wikipedia://en/911820) [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) that interprets *L* by a certain canonical translation, described below. Modal companions share various properties of the original [intermediate logic](wikipedia://en/769022), which enables the study of intermediate logics using tools developed for modal logic.

:::

## Gödel–McKinsey–Tarski translation {#Gödel–McKinsey–Tarski_translation}

Let *A* be a [propositional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus) [intuitionistic](wikipedia://en/169262) formula. A modal formula *T*(*A*) is defined by induction on the complexity of *A*:[^n1]

$T(p)=\Box p,$ for any [propositional variable](wikipedia://en/1557562) $p$,

$$T(\bot)=\bot,$$

$$T(A\land B)=T(A)\land T(B),$$

$$T(A\lor B)=T(A)\lor T(B),$$

$$T(A\to B)=\Box(T(A)\to T(B)).$$

As negation is in intuitionistic logic defined by $A\to\bot$, we also have

$$T(\neg A)=\Box\neg T(A).$$

*T* is called the **Gödel translation** or **[Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736)–[McKinsey](wikipedia://en/31516391)–[Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) translation**. The translation is sometimes presented in slightly different ways: for example, one may insert $\Box$ before every subformula. All such variants are provably equivalent in [S4](wikipedia://en/911960).

## Modal companions {#Modal_companions}

For any normal modal logic *M* that extends **S4**, we define its **si-fragment** *ρM* as

$$\rho M=\{A\mid M\vdash T(A)\}.$$

The si-fragment of any [normal extension](wikipedia://en/929925) of **S4** is a superintuitionistic logic. A modal logic *M* is a **modal companion** of a superintuitionistic logic *L* if $L=\rho M$.

Every superintuitionistic logic has modal companions. The **smallest modal companion** of *L* is

$$\tau L=\mathbf{S4}\oplus\{T(A)\mid L\vdash A\},$$

where $\oplus$ denotes normal closure. It can be shown that every superintuitionistic logic also has a **largest modal companion**, which is denoted by *σL*. A modal logic *M* is a companion of *L* if and only if $\tau L\subseteq M\subseteq\sigma L$.

For example, **S4** itself is the smallest modal companion of intuitionistic logic (**IPC**). The largest modal companion of **IPC** is the [Grzegorczyk](wikipedia://en/24824350) logic **Grz**, axiomatized by the axiom

$$\Box(\Box(A\to\Box A)\to A)\to A$$

over **[K](wikipedia://en/911820)**. The smallest modal companion of classical logic (**CPC**) is [Lewis](wikipedia://en/433707)' **[S5](wikipedia://en/12708106)**, whereas its largest modal companion is the logic

$$\mathbf{Triv}=\mathbf K\oplus(A\leftrightarrow\Box A).$$

More examples:

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| *L* | *τL* | *σL* | other companions of *L* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IPC** | **S4** | **Grz** | **S4.1**, **Dum**, ... |
| **[KC](wikipedia://en/17637#Law_of_the_weak_excluded_middle)** | **S4.2** | **Grz.2** | **S4.1.2**, ... |
| **[LC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel–Dummett_logic)** | **S4.3** | **Grz.3** | **S4.1.3**, **S4.3Dum**, ... |
| **CPC** | **S5** | **Triv** | see below |

:::

## Blok–Esakia isomorphism {#Blok–Esakia_isomorphism}

The set of extensions of a superintuitionistic logic *L* ordered by inclusion forms a [complete lattice](wikipedia://en/44777), denoted Ext*L*. Similarly, the set of normal extensions of a modal logic *M* is a complete lattice NExt*M*. The companion operators *ρM*, *τL*, and *σL* can be considered as mappings between the lattices Ext**IPC** and NExt**S4**:

$$\rho\colon\mathrm{NExt}\,\mathbf{S4}\to\mathrm{Ext}\,\mathbf{IPC},$$

$$\tau,\sigma\colon\mathrm{Ext}\,\mathbf{IPC}\to\mathrm{NExt}\,\mathbf{S4}.$$

It is easy to see that all three are [monotone](wikipedia://en/48260), and $\rho\circ\tau=\rho\circ\sigma$ is the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) on Ext**IPC**. [L. Maksimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larisa_Maksimova) and [V. Rybakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_V._Rybakov) have shown that *ρ*, *τ*, and *σ* are actually [complete](wikipedia://en/44777#Morphisms_of_complete_lattices), join-complete and meet-complete lattice homomorphisms respectively. The cornerstone of the theory of modal companions is the **Blok–Esakia theorem**, proved independently by [Wim Blok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Blok) and [Leo Esakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Esakia). It states

*The mappings*ρ*and*σ*are mutually [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) lattice [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) of* Ext**IPC** *and* NExt**Grz**.

Accordingly, *σ* and the [restriction](wikipedia://en/185427#Restrictions_and_extensions) of *ρ* to NExt**Grz** are called the **Blok–Esakia isomorphism**. An important corollary to the Blok–Esakia theorem is a simple syntactic description of largest modal companions: for every superintuitionistic logic *L*,

$$\sigma L=\tau L\oplus\mathbf{Grz}.$$

:::

## Semantic description {#Semantic_description}

The Gödel translation has a frame-theoretic counterpart. Let $\mathbf F=\langle F,R,V\rangle$ be a [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463) and [reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458) modal [general frame](wikipedia://en/6933049). The [preorder](wikipedia://en/23582) *R* induces the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259)

$$x\sim y \iff x\,R\,y \land y\,R\,x$$

on *F*, which identifies points belonging to the same cluster. Let $\langle\rho F,\le\rangle=\langle F,R\rangle/{\sim}$ be the induced [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_set) [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) (i.e., *ρF* is the set of [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of $\sim$), and put

$$\rho V=\{A/{\sim}\mid A\in V,A=\Box A\}.$$

Then $\rho\mathbf F=\langle\rho F,\le,\rho V\rangle$ is an intuitionistic general frame, called the **skeleton** of **F**. The point of the skeleton construction is that it preserves validity modulo Gödel translation: for any intuitionistic formula *A*,

*A* is valid in *ρ***F** if and only if *T*(*A*) is valid in **F**.

Therefore, the si-fragment of a modal logic *M* can be defined semantically: if *M* is complete with respect to a class *C* of transitive reflexive general frames, then *ρM* is complete with respect to the class $\{\rho\mathbf F;\,\mathbf F\in C\}$.

The largest modal companions also have a semantic description. For any intuitionistic general frame $\mathbf F=\langle F,\le,V\rangle$, let *σV* be the closure of *V* under Boolean operations (binary [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) and [complement](wikipedia://en/54347)). It can be shown that *σV* is closed under $\Box$, thus $\sigma\mathbf F=\langle F,\le,\sigma V\rangle$ is a general modal frame. The skeleton of *σ***F** is isomorphic to **F**. If *L* is a superintuitionistic logic complete with respect to a class *C* of general frames, then its largest modal companion *σL* is complete with respect to $\{\sigma\mathbf F;\,\mathbf F\in C\}$.

The skeleton of a [Kripke frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kripke_frame) is itself a Kripke frame. On the other hand, *σ***F** is never a Kripke frame if **F** is a Kripke frame of infinite depth.

## Preservation theorems {#Preservation_theorems}

The value of modal companions and the Blok–Esakia theorem as a tool for investigation of intermediate logics comes from the fact that many interesting properties of logics are preserved by some or all of the mappings *ρ*, *σ*, and *τ*. For example,

- [decidability](wikipedia://en/913118) is preserved by *ρ*, *τ*, and *σ*,
- [finite model property](wikipedia://en/911960#Finite_model_property) is preserved by *ρ*, *τ*, and *σ*,
- [tabularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabular_logic) is preserved by *ρ* and *σ*,
- [Kripke completeness](wikipedia://en/911960#Correspondence_and_completeness) is preserved by *ρ* and *τ*,
- [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) definability on Kripke frames is preserved by *ρ* and *τ*.

:::

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

Every intermediate logic *L* has an [infinite](wikipedia://en/142679) number of modal companions, and moreover, the set $\rho^{-1}(L)$ of modal companions of *L* contains an [infinite descending chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_descending_chain). For example, $\rho^{-1}(\mathbf{CPC})$ consists of **S5**, and the logics $L(C_n)$ for every positive integer *n*, where $C_n$ is the *n*-element cluster. The set of modal companions of any *L* is either [countable](wikipedia://en/6026), or it has the [cardinality of the continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901). Rybakov has shown that the lattice Ext*L* can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in $\rho^{-1}(L)$; in particular, a logic has a continuum of modal companions if it has a continuum of extensions (this holds, for instance, for all intermediate logics below **KC**). It is unknown whether the converse is also true.

The Gödel translation can be applied to [rules](wikipedia://en/252311) as well as formulas: the translation of a rule

$$R=\frac{A_1,\dots,A_n}{B}$$

is the rule

$$T(R)=\frac{T(A_1),\dots,T(A_n)}{T(B)}.$$

A rule *R* is [admissible](wikipedia://en/1151794) in a logic *L* if the set of theorems of *L* is closed under *R*. It is easy to see that *R* is admissible in a superintuitionistic logic *L* whenever *T*(*R*) is admissible in a modal companion of *L*. The converse is not true in general, but it holds for the largest modal companion of *L*.
