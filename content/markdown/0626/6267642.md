# Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma"}

The **Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma** is a result in [system analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_analysis) and [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) which states: Given a number $\gamma > 0$, two n-vectors B, C and an n x n [Hurwitz matrix](wikipedia://en/78291990) A, if the pair $(A,B)$ is completely [controllable](wikipedia://en/100638), then a symmetric matrix P and a vector Q satisfying

:::

$$A^T P + P A = -Q Q^T$$

$$P B-C = \sqrt{\gamma}Q$$

exist if and only if

$$\gamma+2 Re[C^T (j\omega I-A)^{-1}B]\ge 0$$

Moreover, the set $\{x: x^T P x = 0\}$ is the unobservable subspace for the pair $(C,A)$.

The lemma can be seen as a generalization of the [Lyapunov equation](wikipedia://en/984752) in stability theory. It establishes a relation between a [linear matrix inequality](wikipedia://en/5410577) involving the [state space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_space) constructs A, B, C and a condition in the [frequency domain](wikipedia://en/370346).

The Kalman–Popov–Yakubovich lemma which was first formulated and proved in 1962 by [Vladimir Andreevich Yakubovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Andreevich_Yakubovich)[^o2] where it was stated that for the strict frequency inequality. The case of nonstrict frequency inequality was published in 1963 by [Rudolf E. Kálmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_E._Kálmán).[^o3] In that paper the relation to solvability of the Lur’e equations was also established. Both papers considered scalar-input systems. The constraint on the control dimensionality was removed in 1964 by Gantmakher and Yakubovich[^o4] and independently by [Vasile Mihai Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasile_M._Popov).[^o5] Extensive reviews of the topic can be found in [^o6] and in Chapter 3 of.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Multivariable Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma"}

## Multivariable Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma {#Multivariable_Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov_lemma}

Given $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}, B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}, M = M^T \in \mathbb{R}^{(n+m) \times (n+m)}$ with $\det(j\omega I - A) \ne 0$ for all $\omega \in \mathbb{R}$ and $(A, B)$ controllable, the following are equivalent:

for all $\omega \in \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$

$\left[\begin{matrix} (j\omega I - A)^{-1}B \\ I \end{matrix}\right]^* M \left[\begin{matrix} (j\omega I - A)^{-1}B \\ I \end{matrix}\right] \le 0$ there exists a matrix $P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ such that $P = P^T$ and

$$M + \left[\begin{matrix} A^T P + PA & PB \\ B^T P & 0 \end{matrix}\right] \le 0.$$

The corresponding equivalence for strict inequalities holds even if $(A, B)$ is not controllable. [^o10]
