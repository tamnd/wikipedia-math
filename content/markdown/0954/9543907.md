# James Massey

*Image: ETH-BIB-Massey, James L. (1934-2013)-Portr 16742.tif.* James Massey

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Massey"}

**James Lee Massey** (February 11, 1934 – June 16, 2013)[^o2] was an American [information theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theorist) and cryptographer, Professor [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of Digital Technology at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich). His notable work includes the application of the [Berlekamp–Massey algorithm](wikipedia://en/514832) to [linear codes](wikipedia://en/1866743), the design of the [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594) [IDEA](wikipedia://en/15076) (with [Xuejia Lai](wikipedia://en/10016061), based on the [Lai-Massey scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai-Massey_scheme)) and [SAFER](wikipedia://en/630497), and the [Massey-Omura cryptosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey-Omura_cryptosystem) (with [Jim K. Omura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_K._Omura)).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Massey was born in [Wauseon, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wauseon,_Ohio). As a child, after the death of his father in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio), he moved with his mother and brother to [Mendota, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendota,_Illinois). At age 14, his family moved to [Ottawa, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa,_Illinois). After graduating from [St. Bede Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Bede_Academy), he entered the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame). He received a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) from Notre Dame in 1956 and was granted an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Fellowship. After three years of military service, he began graduate studies in 1959 at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), where he concentrated on [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) and was awarded a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1962, with [John Wozencraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wozencraft) as his advisor. He returned to Notre Dame, where he taught electrical engineering until 1977, publishing significant research in [convolutional codes](wikipedia://en/40962), [frame synchronization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_synchronization) techniques and feedback-assisted communication. After a brief period at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), Massey accepted a position at ETH Zurich in 1980. He remained there until his retirement in 1998.

Massey died of [colon cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_cancer) on June 16, 2013 in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark).[^o2]
