# George Szekeres

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Szekeres"}

**George Szekeres** (; 29 May 1911 – 28 August 2005), born **Szekeres György**, was a Hungarian–Australian mathematician. After migrating to Australia after World War II, he worked first for the [University of Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Adelaide), and then the [University of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales). He is known for his co-authoring a paper with fellow Hungarian mathematician [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) known as the "[Happy Ending problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Ending_problem)", and married mathematician [Esther Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Klein).

:::

## Early years and education {#Early_years_and_education}

Szekeres György was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), on 29 May 1911.[^o8][^o4]

He received his degree in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) at the [Royal Joseph University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Joseph_University) (later Technical University of Budapest).[^o8] He graduated with a degree in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) in 1933, having studied only one mathematical course, in [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176).[^o4]

The so-called "[Happy Ending problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Ending_problem)" is an example of how mathematics pervaded Szekeres's life.[^o5] During 1933, George and several other students met frequently, often at the *[Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_%28statue%29)* statue in [City Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Park_%28Budapest%29),[^o15] to discuss mathematics. At one of these meetings, [Esther Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Klein) proposed the problem:[^o5] "Given five points in the plane in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736), prove that four of them form a [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278)." After allowing George, [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), and the other students to scratch their heads for some time, Esther explained her proof.[^o5] Subsequently, George and Paul wrote a paper (1935) that generalises this result;[^o6] it is regarded as one of the foundational works in the field of [combinatorial geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_geometry). Erdős dubbed the original problem the "Happy Ending" problem because it resulted in the marriage of George and Esther.[^o15]

## Career {#Career}

Szekeres worked for six years in Budapest as an [analytical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_chemist), and during this time married [Esther Klein](wikipedia://en/2575958) in 1937. Being [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish), the family had to escape from the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) persecution so Szekeres took a job as a leather chemist in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai), China. There they lived through World War II, the Japanese occupation and the beginnings of the Communist revolution.[^o8][^o4]

In 1948, Szekeres was offered and accepted a position as lecturer at the [University of Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Adelaide), Australia,[^o9] and the family (with their son, born in China), moving to [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide), [South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Australia), in June 1948. Esther also taught at the university.[^o4]

In 1964, the family moved to [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney), where Szekeres took a position as professor of mathematics at the [University of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales) (UNSW), and taught there until his retirement in 1976, when he became emeritus professor. He continued to publish papers after his retirement.[^o4][^o9]

In 1978 he published a joint paper with Erdős entitled "Some number theoretic problems on [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficients)". His 1985 paper "Distribution of labelled trees by diameter" analysed the graphs described by the title and, among many other results, found the expected value of the diameter of a random labelled tree on *nn* vertices.[^o4] Also in 1985 he showed his continuing interest in using computers in mathematical investigations with "Computer examination of the two-dimensional simultaneous approximation constant".[^o4]

He worked closely with many prominent mathematicians throughout his life, including Erdős, his wife Esther, [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998), [Béla Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956), [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694), [Alf van der Poorten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_van_der_Poorten), [Miklós Laczkovich](wikipedia://en/6021299), and [John Coates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Coates_%28mathematician%29). He worked on many problems and published many papers, especially in the field of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) in particular.[^o4] Many mathematical concepts bear his name, including [Szekeres snark](wikipedia://en/4776914), [Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates](wikipedia://en/2089093), [Szekeres–Wilf number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szekeres–Wilf_number), and the [Erdős–Szekeres theorem](wikipedia://en/3115543).

## Other activities {#Other_activities}

Szekeres was a founder member of the [Australian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4579582) in August 1956, and served as its president from 1972 to 1974.[^o4]

He devised problems for secondary school [mathematical olympiads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_olympiad) ran by UNSW, and for a yearly undergraduate competition ran by the [Sydney University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_University) Mathematics Society.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Szkeres married Esther Klein on 13 June 1937.[^o4]

When they first moved to Australia after the war, the Szekeres shared an apartment in [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide) with mathematician [Márta Svéd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Márta_Svéd), an old school friend of Esther, and her husband [George Svéd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Svéd), for three years. Esther and George had two children.[^o8][^o15] After moving to Sydney, they bought a house in [Turramurra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turramurra), which they had obtained at a low price owing to it having belonged to a person who had died in suspicious circumstances in the [Bogle–Chandler case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogle–Chandler_case).[^o4]

Szekeres loved art and music, particularly [chamber music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_music). He was an accomplished musician, playing [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin) and [viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola). He played in the North Sydney Symphony Orchestra and the [Ku-ring-gai Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku-ring-gai_Philharmonic_Orchestra), and was founding treasurer of the latter until 2000. He also loved walking and even in his 80s enjoyed long walks with his daughter Judith.[^o4]

After returning to Adelaide in 2004, George and Esther died within an hour of each other on 28 August 2005[^o8][^o15] in Wynwood Nursing Home[^o4] in [Norwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwood,_South_Australia) (now closed).[^o16]

### In fiction {#In_fiction}

The Svéds' granddaughter, author [Miriam Sved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Sved),[^o18][^o19][^o20] wrote the historical novel *A Universe of Sufficient Size*, published in 2019.[^o21] It tells the story of a group of Jewish mathematicians in Hungary who flee the country, which she based on stories she had heard from Márta Svéd. While the story is fictional, several characters are loosely based on people in Svéd's circle, in particular Esther and George Szekeres, as well as Paul Erdős.[^o15]
