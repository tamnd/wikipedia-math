# Arthur Herbert Copeland

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Herbert Copeland"}

**Arthur Herbert Copeland** (June 22, 1898 [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York) – July 6, 1970) was an American mathematician. He graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1926 and taught at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) and the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). His main interest was in the foundations of [probability](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o4]

:::

He worked with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) on the [Copeland-Erdős constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copeland-Erdős_constant). His son, Arthur Herbert Copeland, Jr. (1926-2019), was also a mathematician.[^o6]

Copeland published a paper about [pairwise voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pairwise_voting), which was very similar to the work of [Ramon Llull](wikipedia://en/69677) and [Marquis de Condorcet](wikipedia://en/37749). The system he described became known as "[Copeland's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copeland's_method)".
