# Cathetus

*Image: Triangle Sides.svg.* A right-angled triangle where *c*<sub>1</sub> and *c*<sub>2</sub> are the catheti and *h* is the hypotenuse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cathetus"}

In a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420), a **cathetus** (originally from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) κάθετος, "perpendicular"; plural: **catheti**), commonly known as a **leg**, is either of the sides that are adjacent to the [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956). It is occasionally called a "side about the right angle". The side opposite the right angle is the [hypotenuse](wikipedia://en/251366). In the context of the hypotenuse, the catheti are sometimes referred to simply as "the other two sides".

:::

If the catheti of a right triangle have equal lengths, the triangle is [isosceles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosceles). If they have different lengths, a distinction can be made between the minor (shorter) and major (longer) cathetus. The [ratio](wikipedia://en/87837) of the lengths of the catheti defines the [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) tangent and cotangent of the [acute angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_angle) in the triangle: the ratio $c_1/c_2$ is the tangent of the acute angle adjacent to $c_2$ and is also the cotangent of the acute angle adjacent to $c_1$.

In a right triangle, the length of a cathetus is the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of the length of the adjacent segment cut by the [altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_%28geometry%29) to the hypotenuse and the length of the whole hypotenuse.

By the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034), the sum of the squares of the lengths of the catheti is equal to the square of the length of the hypotenuse.

In architecture, the term **cathetus** has been used for the eye of the [volute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volute). It was so termed because its position is determined, in an [Ionic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_order) (or voluted) [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_%28architecture%29), by a line let down from the point in which the volute generates.
