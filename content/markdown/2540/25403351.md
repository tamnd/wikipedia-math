# Chang's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chang's conjecture"}

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a branch of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **Chang's conjecture**, attributed to [Chen Chung Chang](wikipedia://en/25413983) by (Vaught 1963, 309), states that every model of type (ω<sub>2</sub>,ω<sub>1</sub>) for a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_language) has an [elementary submodel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_submodel) of type (ω<sub>1</sub>,ω). A model is of type (*α*,*β*) if it is of cardinality *α* and a unary relation is represented by a subset of cardinality *β*. The usual notation is $(\omega_2,\omega_1)\twoheadrightarrow(\omega_1,\omega)$.

:::

The [axiom of constructibility](wikipedia://en/696472) implies that Chang's conjecture fails. [Silver](wikipedia://en/21626512) proved the consistency of Chang's conjecture from the consistency of an ω<sub>1</sub>-[Erdős cardinal](wikipedia://en/326540). Hans-Dieter Donder showed a weak version of the reverse implication: if Chang's conjecture is not only consistent but actually holds, then ω<sub>2</sub> is ω<sub>1</sub>-Erdős in [K](wikipedia://en/3781904).

More generally, Chang's conjecture for two pairs (*α*,*β*), (*γ*,*δ*) of cardinals is the claim that every model of type (*α*,*β*) for a countable language has an elementary submodel of type (*γ*,*δ*). The consistency of $(\omega_3,\omega_2)\twoheadrightarrow(\omega_2,\omega_1)$ was shown by [Laver](wikipedia://en/22280145) from the consistency of a [huge cardinal](wikipedia://en/248117).
