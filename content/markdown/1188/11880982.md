# Material nonimplication

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Material nonimplication"}

**Material nonimplication** or **abjunction** (la) is a term referring to a logic operation used in generic circuits and [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/54476844).[^o3] It is the [negation](wikipedia://en/161019) of [material implication](wikipedia://en/658808). That is to say that for any two [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094) $P$ and $Q$, the material nonimplication from $P$ to $Q$ is true [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the negation of the material implication from $P$ to $Q$ is true. This is more naturally stated as that the material nonimplication from $P$ to $Q$ is true only if $P$ is true and $Q$ is false.

:::

It may be written using logical notation as $P \nrightarrow Q$, $P \not \supset Q$, or "L*pq*" (in [Bocheński notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocheński_notation)), and is logically equivalent to $\neg (P \rightarrow Q)$, and $P \land \neg Q$.

## Properties {#Properties}

**falsehood-preserving**: The interpretation under which all variables are assigned a [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) of "false" produces a truth value of "false" as a result of material nonimplication.

:::

## Symbol {#Symbol}

The symbol for material nonimplication is simply a crossed-out material implication symbol. Its Unicode symbol is [219B<sub>16</sub>](wikipedia://en/4912832#Unicode) (8603 decimal): ↛.

Bitwise operation: P & ~Q. This is usually called "bit clear" (BIC) or "and not" (ANDN).

Logical operation: P && !Q.
