# Harold P. Boas

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Harold P. Boas"}

**Harold P. Boas** (born June 26, 1954) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), professor emeritus of [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University), where he was *Professor and Presidential Professor for Teaching Excellence* in the department of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).[^o13]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Boas was born in [Evanston, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois), United States. He is the youngest of three children of two noted mathematicians, [Ralph P. Boas, Jr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_P._Boas,_Jr) and [Mary L. Boas](wikipedia://en/27606754).[^o4]

## Education {#Education}

He received his A.B. and S.M. degrees in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1976[^o11] and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1980 under the direction of [Norberto Kerzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norberto_Kerzman).

## Career {#Career}

Boas was a [J. F. Ritt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ritt) Assistant Professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (1980–1984) before moving to [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University) as an assistant professor, where he advanced to the rank of associate professor in 1987 and full professor in 1992.[^o11] He was given the university title of *Presidential Professor for Teaching Excellence* in 2012 and the System title of *Regents Professor* in 2014.[^o13]

He has held visiting positions at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).

## Mathematical works and service {#Mathematical_works_and_service}

Boas has published over thirty papers twelve of which he coauthored with his colleague [Emil J. Straube](wikipedia://en/58526201). Starting from late 80s until mid 90s, they had a spectacular progress in the study of global regularity of the [Bergman projection](wikipedia://en/3205927) and of the $\overline{\partial}$-Neumann problem. In particular, they have proved the global regularity in the sense of preservation of [Sobolev spaces](wikipedia://en/611964) on large class of [pseudoconvex domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoconvex_domain).[^o11] Boas also provided a counterexample to the Lu Qi-Keng Conjecture which was raised by Lu Qi-Keng in 1966 and asked whether it was true for every bounded domain that the [Bergman kernel](wikipedia://en/3205923) function had no zeroes.[^o9]

He has also published more than 200 short reviews for [zbMathOpen](wikipedia://en/1701961) and more than 550 reviews for [Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646) over [MathSciNet](wikipedia://en/2580683).

Boas was part of Russian Translation Project of the American Mathematical Society and translated more than 84 Russian research articles in English as well as a book by [Alexander M. Kytmanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_M._Kytmanov) from Russian into English.

He has served as the editor of the [Notices of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4977512) between 2011 and 2003, took part in editorial duties in several research journals.

## Awards {#Awards}

He was the winner of the 1995 [Stefan Bergman Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bergman_Prize) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11] In 2012, he became a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.

Boas is also known for his excellence in teaching and expository works, including *Reflections on the arbelos* (for which he won the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) in 2009).[^o12] He has twice won the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 2007 and 2017.[^o12][^o13]

He has revised and updated his father's books, such as the fourth edition of *[A Primer of Real Functions](wikipedia://en/56963959)* and the second edition of *Invitation to Complex Analysis*.
