# Category of matrices

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of matrices"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **category of matrices**, often denoted $\mathbf{Mat}$, is the [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) whose [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) are [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) are [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859), with composition given by [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280).[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Let $A$ be an $n\times m$ [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859), i.e. a matrix with $n$ rows and $m$ columns. Given a $p\times q$ matrix $B$, we can form the [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280) $BA$ or $B\circ A$ only when $q=n$, and in that case the resulting matrix is of dimension $p\times m$.

In other words, we can only multiply matrices $A$ and $B$ when the number of rows of $A$ matches the number of columns of $B$. One can keep track of this fact by declaring an $n\times m$ matrix to be of type $m\to n$, and similarly a $p\times q$ matrix to be of type $q\to p$. This way, when $q=n$ the two arrows have matching source and target, $m\to n\to p$, and can hence be composed to an arrow of type $m\to p$.

This is precisely captured by the mathematical concept of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759), where the arrows, or [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphisms), are the matrices, and they can be composed only when their domain and codomain are compatible (similar to what happens with [functions](wikipedia://en/185427)). In detail, the category $\mathbf{Mat}_\mathbb{R}$ is constructed as follows:

- It has [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers) as [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29);
- Given numbers $m$ and $n$, a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) $m\to n$ is an $n\times m$ matrix, i.e. a matrix with $n$ rows and $m$ columns;
- The [identity morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_morphism) at each object $n$ is given by the $n\times n$ [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718);
- The composition of morphisms $A:m\to n$ and $B:n\to p$ (i.e. of matrices $n\times m$ and $p\times n$) is given by [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280).

More generally, one can define the category $\mathbf{Mat}_\mathbb{F}$ of matrices over a fixed field $\mathbb{F}$, such as the one of [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers).

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The category of matrices $\mathbf{Mat}_\mathbb{R}$ is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of finite-dimensional real [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) and [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102). This is witnessed by the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) mapping the number $n$ to the vector space $\mathbb{R}^n$, and an $n\times m$ matrix to the corresponding linear map $\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^n$.[^o8][^o3] A possible interpretation of this fact is that, as mathematical theories, abstract finite-dimensional vector spaces and concrete matrices have the same expressive power.
- More generally, the category of matrices $\mathbf{Mat}_\mathbb{F}$ is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of finite-dimensional [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over the field $\mathbb{F}$ and $\mathbb{F}$-[linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102).[^o8]
- A [linear row operation](wikipedia://en/20556859#Row_operations) on a $n\times m$ matrix $A$ can be equivalently obtained by applying the same operation to the $n\times n$ [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718), and then multiplying the resulting $n\times n$ matrix with $A$. In particular, elementary row operations correspond to [elementary matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_matrices). This fact can be seen as an instance of the [Yoneda lemma](wikipedia://en/34359) for the category of matrices.[^o9][^o10]
- The [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) operation makes the category of matrices a [dagger category](wikipedia://en/7152070). The same can be said about the [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548) in the case of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).

:::

## Particular subcategories {#Particular_subcategories}

- For every fixed $n$, the morphisms $n\to n$ of $\mathbf{Mat}_\mathbb{R}$ are the $n\times n$ matrices, and form a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652), canonically isomorphic to the monoid of linear endomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}^n$. In particular, the [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) $n\times n$ matrices form a [group](wikipedia://en/19447). The same can be said for a generic field $\mathbb{F}$.
- A [stochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/217313) is a real matrix of nonnegative entries, such that the sum of each column is one. Stochastic matrices include the identity and are closed under composition, and so they form a [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) of $\mathbf{Mat}_\mathbb{R}$.[^o12]
