# Squire's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Squire's theorem"}

In [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), **Squire's theorem** states that of all the [perturbations](wikipedia://en/102140) that may be applied to a [shear flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_flow) (i.e. a [velocity field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity_field) of the form $\mathbf{U} = (U(z), 0, 0)$), the perturbations which are least stable are two-dimensional, i.e. of the form $\mathbf{u}' = (u'(x,z,t),0,w'(x,z,t))$, rather than the three-dimensional disturbances.[^o2] This applies to [incompressible flows](wikipedia://en/166084) which are governed by the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395). The theorem is named after [Herbert Squire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Squire), who proved the theorem in 1933.[^o3]

:::

Squire's theorem allows many simplifications to be made in [stability theory](wikipedia://en/5493795). If we want to decide whether a flow is unstable or not, it suffices to look at two-dimensional perturbations. These are governed by the [Orr–Sommerfeld equation](wikipedia://en/10106425) for [viscous](wikipedia://en/18963754) flow, and by [Rayleigh's equation](wikipedia://en/52478131) for inviscid flow.
