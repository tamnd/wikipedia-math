# Edward Jarvis (physician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Jarvis"}

**Edward Jarvis** (9 January 1803, [Concord, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_Massachusetts) – 31 October 1884, [Dorchester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorchester,_Massachusetts)) was an American physician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was descended from John Jarvis, a shipbuilder who emigrated from [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), to [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston) in 1661. He attended public school in Concord, and [Westford Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westford_Academy).[^o4] He graduated from [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1826. In 1827 he taught school in Concord, also studying with [Josiah Bartlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_Bartlett).[^o4] He graduated from the Boston Medical School in 1830, and practised in [Northfield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northfield,_Massachusetts), from 1830 to 1832. He practised in Concord until 1837. There he was influenced by statistician [Lemuel Shattuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuel_Shattuck).[^o4] After Concord, he practised in [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), from 1837 to 1842, and then in Dorchester, Massachusetts. He was elected a member of the [American Antiquarian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Antiquarian_Society) in 1854.[^o5]

Jarvis made a sanitary survey of [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts), by order of the government, and published a report (1855), and subsequently, by appointment of the [United States Secretary of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Interior), he tabulated the mortality statistics of the United States as reported in the census of 1860, his work constituting one half of the fourth volume of the reports of the [eighth census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Census_of_the_United_States). He was a member of numerous learned societies, and was president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) from 1852 until his death. In 1863, he was elected as a member to the American Philosophical Society.[^o6]

Additionally, Jarvis wrote a large number of reports on public health, mortality rates, education, insanity, and other subjects. In his 1871 essay entitled *Relation of Education to Insanity*, Jarvis argued that too much education was directly linked to increased rates of insanity.[^o7]
