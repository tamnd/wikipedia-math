# Truncated order-8 hexagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated order-8 hexagonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated order-8 hexagonal tiling** is a semiregular tiling of the hyperbolic plane. It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of t{6,8}.

:::

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

This tiling can also be constructed from \*664 symmetry, as t{(6,6,4)}.

*Image: H2\_tiling\_466-7.png.*

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

From a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) there are fourteen hyperbolic [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/18640974) that can be based from the regular order-6 octagonal tiling.

Drawing the tiles colored as red on the original faces, yellow at the original vertices, and blue along the original edges, there are 7 forms with full \[8,6\] symmetry, and 7 with subsymmetry.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

### Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The dual of the tiling represents the fundamental domains of (\*664) [orbifold](wikipedia://en/2915678) symmetry. From \[(6,6,4)\] (\*664) symmetry, there are 15 small index subgroup (11 unique) by mirror removal and alternation operators. Mirrors can be removed if its branch orders are all even, and cuts neighboring branch orders in half. Removing two mirrors leaves a half-order gyration point where the removed mirrors met. In these images fundamental domains are alternately colored black and white, and mirrors exist on the boundaries between colors. The symmetry can be doubled to [862 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/862_symmetry) by adding a bisecting mirror across the fundamental domains. The [subgroup index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroup_index)-8 group, \[(1<sup>\+</sup>,6,1<sup>\+</sup>,6,1<sup>\+</sup>,4)\] (332332) is the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) of \[(6,6,4)\].

A large subgroup is constructed \[(6,6,4<sup>\*</sup>)\], index 8, as (4\*33) with gyration points removed, becomes (\*3<sup>8</sup>), and another large subgroup is constructed \[(6,6<sup>\*</sup>,4)\], index 12, as (6\*32) with gyration points removed, becomes (\*(32)<sup>6</sup>).
