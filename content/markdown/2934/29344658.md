# Rauzy fractal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rauzy fractal"}

*Image: Rauzy fractal.png.* Rauzy fractal

In mathematics, the **Rauzy fractal** is a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) set associated with the Tribonacci [substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_substitution)

:::

$$s(1)=12,\ s(2)=13,\ s(3)=1 \,.$$

It was studied in 1981 by Gérard Rauzy,[^o2] with the idea of generalizing the dynamic properties of the [Fibonacci morphism](wikipedia://en/1702139). That fractal set can be generalized to other maps over a 3-letter alphabet, generating other fractal sets with interesting properties, such as periodic [tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessallation) of the plane and [self-similarity](wikipedia://en/28782) in three [homothetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation) parts.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Tribonacci word"}

### Tribonacci word {#Tribonacci_word}

The **infinite tribonacci word** is a [word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_%28mathematics%29) constructed by iteratively applying the *Tribonacci* or *Rauzy map* : $s(1)=12$, $s(2)=13$, $s(3)=1$.[^o7][^o8] It is an example of a [morphic word](wikipedia://en/37432570). Starting from 1, the Tribonacci words are:[^o9]

- $$t_0 = 1$$
- $$t_1 = 12$$
- $$t_2 = 1213$$
- $$t_3 = 1213121$$
- $$t_4 = 1213121121312$$

We can show that, for $n>2$, $t_n = t_{n-1}t_{n-2}t_{n-3}$; hence the name "[Tribonacci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_number)".

:::

### Fractal construction {#Fractal_construction}

*Image: Rauzy fractal construction.png.* Construction

Consider, now, the space $R^3$ with cartesian coordinates (x,y,z). The **Rauzy fractal** is constructed this way:[^o11]

\1) Interpret the sequence of letters of the infinite Tribonacci word as a sequence of unitary [vectors](wikipedia://en/23218956) of the space, with the following rules (1 = direction x, 2 = direction y, 3 = direction z).

\2) Then, build a "stair" by tracing the points reached by this sequence of vectors (see figure). For example, the first points are:

- $$1 \Rightarrow (1, 0, 0)$$
- $$2 \Rightarrow (1, 1, 0)$$
- $$1 \Rightarrow (2, 1, 0)$$
- $$3 \Rightarrow (2, 1, 1)$$
- $$1 \Rightarrow (3, 1, 1)$$

etc...Every point can be colored according to the corresponding letter, to stress the self-similarity property.

\3) Then, project those points on the contracting plane (plane orthogonal to the main direction of propagation of the points, none of those projected points escape to infinity).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- Can be [tiled](wikipedia://en/321671) by three copies of itself, with area reduced by factors $k$, $k^2$ and $k^3$ with $k$ solution of $k^3+k^2+k-1=0$: $k = \frac{1}{3}\left(-1-\frac{2}{\sqrt[3]{17+3 \sqrt{33}}}+\sqrt[3]{17+3 \sqrt{33}}\right) = 0.543689...$.
- Stable under exchanging pieces. We can obtain the same set by exchanging the place of the pieces.
- [Connected](wikipedia://en/6233) and simply connected. Has no hole.
- Tiles the plane periodically, by translation.
- The matrix of the Tribonacci map has $x^3 - x^2 - x -1$ as its [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268). Its eigenvalues are a real number $\beta = 1.839286...$, called the [Tribonacci constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_number), a [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number), and two complex conjugates $\alpha$ and $\bar \alpha$ with $\alpha \bar \alpha=1/\beta$.
- Its boundary is fractal, and the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) of this boundary equals 1.093364... , the solution of $2|\alpha|^{3s}+|\alpha|^{4s}=1$.[^o14]

:::

## Variants and generalization {#Variants_and_generalization}

For any unimodular substitution of Pisot type, which verifies a coincidence condition (apparently always verified), one can construct a similar set called "Rauzy fractal of the map". They all display [self-similarity](wikipedia://en/28782) and generate, for the examples below, a periodic tiling of the plane.
