# Panconnectivity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Panconnectivity"}

*Image: Panconnected graph animation.gif.* Each possible pair of vertices $s$ and $t$ have paths of length 1 through $n-1$, where $n$ is the number of vertices. Thus, the graph shown is panconnected.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **panconnected graph** is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) in which, for every two [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) $s$ and $t$, there exist [paths](wikipedia://en/638889) from $s$ to $t$ of every possible length from the distance $d(s,t)$ up to $n - 1$, where $n$ is the number of vertices in the graph. The concept of panconnectivity was introduced in 1975 by [Yousef Alavi](wikipedia://en/614947) and James E. Williamson.[^o2]

:::

Panconnected graphs are necessarily [pancyclic](wikipedia://en/28691490): if $uv$ is an [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29), then it belongs to a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) of every possible length, and therefore the graph contains a cycle of every possible length. Panconnected graphs are also a generalization of [Hamiltonian-connected graphs](wikipedia://en/244437) (graphs that have a Hamiltonian path connecting every pair of vertices).

Several classes of graphs are known to be panconnected:

- If $G$ has a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle), then the [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_of_a_graph) of $G$ (the graph on the same vertex set that has an edge between every two vertices whose distance in $G$ is at most two) is panconnected.[^o2]
- If $G$ is any [connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph), then the [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_of_a_graph) of $G$ (the graph on the same vertex set that has an edge between every two vertices whose distance in $G$ is at most three) is panconnected.[^o2]
- If every vertex in an $n$-vertex graph has degree at least $n/2 + 1$, then the graph is panconnected.[^o3]
- If an $n$-vertex graph has at least $(n - 1)(n - 2)/2 + 3$ edges, then the graph is panconnected.[^o3]

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

**Vertex-pancyclic graphs**: A graph of order $n$ is vertex-pancyclic if every vertex lies on cycles of every possible length from the graph's [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) up to $n$. While vertex-pancyclic graphs need not be panconnected, they share the property of having rich cycle structures.[^o5]

**Hamilton-connected graphs**: These are graphs where every pair of vertices is connected by a [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437). All panconnected graphs are Hamilton-connected, but the converse is not true. For example, the $L(n)$ graphs ([line graphs](wikipedia://en/675231) of certain [inclusion graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_graph)) are Hamilton-connected for $n \ge 4$ but not panconnected.[^o5]
