# Equiprojective polyhedra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equiprojective polyhedra"}

In mathematics, a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) is defined to be $k$-**equiprojective** if every [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of the polygon onto a plane, in a direction not parallel to a face of the polyhedron, forms a $k$-gon. For example, a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is 6-equiprojective: every projection not parallel to a face forms a [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733), More generally, every [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) over a convex $k$ is $(k+2)$-equiprojective. [Zonohedra](wikipedia://en/669402) are also equiprojective. Hasan and his colleagues later found more equiprojective polyhedra by truncating equally the tetrahedron and three other [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034).

:::

(Hasan Lubiw 2008) shows there is an $O(n \log n)$ time algorithm to determine whether a given polyhedron is equiprojective.
