# Robin Neumayer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robin Neumayer"}

*Image: Robin Neumayer Oberwolfach 2018.jpg.* Neumayer at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematisches_Forschungsinstitut_Oberwolfach) in 2018

**Robin Tonra Neumayer** is an American mathematician, the recipient of the 2024 [Sadosky Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadosky_Prize) in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). Her research connects the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), and [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474), and includes the study of geometric aspects of [Sobolev-type inequalities](wikipedia://en/4665038), [isoperimetric inequalities](wikipedia://en/326182), and the [Rayleigh–Faber–Krahn inequality](wikipedia://en/33651947). She is an assistant professor of mathematics at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Neumayer majored in mathematics, with a business minor, at the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina). She graduated [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude) in 2012. She continued her studies in mathematics at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin), where she received a master's degree in 2014 and completed her Ph.D. in 2017. Her doctoral dissertation, *Minimality and stability properties in Sobolev and isoperimetric inequalities*, was jointly supervised by [Alessio Figalli](wikipedia://en/38609892) and Francesco Maggi. In her dissertation, she also cites Maria Girardi of the University of South Carolina as a mentor who strongly influenced her choice to continue in mathematics.

After postdoctoral study at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), she joined [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) as a tenure-track Gregg Zeitlin Early Career Professor in 2021.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Neumayer is the 2024 recipient of the [Sadosky Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadosky_Prize) in analysis of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698), given "for outstanding contributions to calculus of variations, partial differential equations, and geometric analysis". She also received a [National Science Foundation CAREER Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation_CAREER_Award) in 2024.
