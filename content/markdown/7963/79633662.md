# Simons cone

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simons cone"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694), the **Simons cone** refers to a specific [minimal](wikipedia://en/276734) [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) in $\mathbb R^8$ that plays a crucial role in resolving [Bernstein's problem](wikipedia://en/30486183) in higher dimensions. It is named after American mathematician [Jim Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Simons cone is defined as the [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) given by the equation

$$S = \{x \in \mathbb R^8 | x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 + x_4^2 = x_5^2 + x_6^2 + x_7^2 + x_8^2 \} \subset \mathbb R^8.$$

This 7-dimensional [cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28mathematics%29) has the distinctive property that its [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) vanishes at every point except at the origin, where the cone has a [singularity](wikipedia://en/54240).[^o4][^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The classical Bernstein theorem states that any minimal graph in $\mathbb R^3$ must be a plane. This was extended to $\mathbb R^4$ by [Wendell Fleming](wikipedia://en/35262774) in 1962 and [Ennio De Giorgi](wikipedia://en/1752401) in 1965, and to dimensions up to $\mathbb R^5$ by [Frederick J. Almgren Jr.](wikipedia://en/12998276) in 1966 and to $\mathbb R^8$ by [Jim Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844) in 1968. The existence of the Simons cone as a minimizing cone in $\mathbb R^8$ demonstrated that the Bernstein theorem could not be extended to $\mathbb R^9$ and higher dimensions. Bombieri, De Giorgi, and [Enrico Giusti](wikipedia://en/32836383) proved in 1969 that the Simons cone is indeed area-minimizing, thus providing a negative answer to the Bernstein problem in higher dimensions.[^o4][^o5]

:::
