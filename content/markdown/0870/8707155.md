# Statistical shape analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical shape analysis"}

*Image: Face\_Recognition\_3252983.png.* An illustration of how a human face can be broken into shapes for facial recognition

**Statistical shape analysis** is an analysis of the [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) properties of some given set of [shapes](wikipedia://en/169191) by [statistical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical) methods. For instance, it could be used to quantify differences between male and female gorilla skull shapes, normal and [pathological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathology) bone shapes, leaf outlines with and without herbivory by insects, etc. Important aspects of shape analysis are to obtain a measure of distance between shapes, to estimate mean shapes from (possibly random) samples, to estimate shape variability within samples, to perform clustering and to test for differences between shapes.[^o2][^o3] One of the main methods used is [principal component analysis](wikipedia://en/76340) (PCA). Statistical shape analysis has applications in various fields, including [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging),[^o4] [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision), [computational anatomy](wikipedia://en/48520204), sensor measurement, and geographical profiling.[^o5]

:::

## Landmark-based techniques {#Landmark-based_techniques}

In the [point distribution model](wikipedia://en/12265304), a shape is determined by a finite set of coordinate points, known as [landmark points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmark_point). These landmark points often correspond to important identifiable features such as the corners of the eyes. Once the points are collected some form of [registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_registration) is undertaken. This can be a baseline methods used by [Fred Bookstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Bookstein) for [geometric morphometrics in anthropology](wikipedia://en/45580833). Or an approach like [Procrustes analysis](wikipedia://en/3680074) which finds an average shape.

[David George Kendall](wikipedia://en/4631370) investigated the statistical distribution of the shape of triangles, and represented each triangle by a point on a sphere. He used this distribution on the sphere to investigate [ley lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ley_lines) and whether three stones were more likely to be co-linear than might be expected.[^o7] Statistical distribution like the [Kent distribution](wikipedia://en/5624561) can be used to analyse the distribution of such spaces.

Alternatively, shapes can be represented by curves or surfaces representing their contours,[^o8] by the spatial region they occupy.[^o9]

## Shape deformations {#Shape_deformations}

Differences between shapes can be quantified by investigating [deformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_%28engineering%29) transforming one shape into another. In particular a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) preserves smoothness in the deformation. This was pioneered in [D'Arcy Thompson's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Arcy_Thompson) [On Growth and Form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Growth_and_Form) before the advent of computers.[^o11] Deformations can be interpreted as resulting from a [force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_%28physics%29) applied to the shape. Mathematically, a deformation is defined as a [mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapping_%28mathematics%29) from a shape *x* to a shape *y* by a transformation function $\Phi$, i.e., $y = \Phi(x)$.[^o12] Given a notion of size of deformations, the distance between two shapes can be defined as the size of the smallest deformation between these shapes.

**[Diffeomorphometry](wikipedia://en/48520204#Diffeomorphometry:_The_metric_space_of_shapes_and_forms)**[^o13] is the focus on comparison of shapes and forms with a metric structure based on diffeomorphisms, and is central to the field of [Computational anatomy](wikipedia://en/48520204).[^o14] Diffeomorphic registration,[^o15] introduced in the 90's, is now an important player with existing codes bases organized around ANTS,[^o16] DARTEL,[^o17] DEMONS,[^o18] [LDDMM](wikipedia://en/49418115),[^o19] StationaryLDDMM,[^o20] and FastLDDMM[^o21] are examples of actively used computational codes for constructing correspondences between coordinate systems based on sparse features and dense images. [Voxel-based morphometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voxel-based_morphometry) (VBM) is an important technology built on many of these principles. Methods based on diffeomorphic flows are also used. For example, deformations could be diffeomorphisms of the ambient space, resulting in the LDDMM ([Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping](wikipedia://en/49418115)) framework for shape comparison.[^o22]
