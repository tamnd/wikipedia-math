# Cornelius Lanczos

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cornelius Lanczos"}

**Cornelius (Cornel) Lanczos** (Lánczos Kornél, ; born as **Kornél Lőwy**, until 1906: *Löwy (Lőwy) Kornél*; February 2, 1893 – June 25, 1974) was a Hungarian, American, and later Irish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist). According to [György Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/György_Marx) he was one of [the Martians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Martians_%28scientists%29),[^o4] a group of Hungarian scientific luminaries who immigrated to the United States to escape [national socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism). He was remembered by his colleagues as an innovative scholar and an excellent educator.[^o7][^o23]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

He was born in [Székesfehérvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Székesfehérvár), [Fejér County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fejér_County_%28former%29), [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary)[^o6] to Károly Lőwy and Adél Hahn. He grew up in relative comfort and attended a Catholic Gymnasium (high school). Between 1911 and 1916, he studied at the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University), where one of his professors in physics was [Roland Eötvös](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loránd_Eötvös), whose skills as an experimental physicist impressed him.[^o7] In mathematics, his notable teacher was [Lipót Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357), then a young mathematician.[^o7] Lanczos graduated with a teacher's diploma in mathematics and physics. He worked an assistant of Károly Tangl at the Department of Experimental Physics at the Polytechnical University of Budapest from 1916 to 1921.[^o7]

In his doctoral dissertation titled *The Relation of Maxwell's Aether Equations to Functional Theory*,[^o8] Lanczos re-wrote [Maxwell's equations](wikipedia://en/19737) of electromagnetism in terms of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and applied a relativistic [variational principle](wikipedia://en/491097).[^o4] He sent a copy of his thesis to [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), who replied, "I studied your paper as far as my present overload allowed. I believe I may say this much: this does involve competent and original brainwork, on the basis of which a doctorate should be obtainable... I gladly accept the honorable dedication."[^o9] Lanczos maintained his contact with Einstein for another 35 years, until the latter's death.[^o4] In 1921, Lanczos completed his Ph.D. training at the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged) under the supervision of Rudolf Ortvay, a former student of [Arnold Sommerfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Sommerfeld).[^o4] While Ortvay was not distinguished as a researcher, he was an inspirational teacher who brought [modern physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_physics) to Hungary.[^o4][^o7]

## Career {#Career}

As a consequence of the restrictions from the new right-wing regime in Hungary, Lanczos moved to Germany in search of employment.[^o4] From 1921 to 1924, Lanczos served as a lecturer at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg).[^o6] In 1924 he discovered an [exact solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Stockum_dust) to the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540) of general relativity representing a cylindrically symmetric rigidly rotating configuration of [dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_solution) particles.[^o11] This was later rediscovered by [Willem Jacob van Stockum](wikipedia://en/2589644) in 1938.[^o12] It is one of the simplest known exact solutions in general relativity[^o13] and is regarded as an important example, in part because it exhibits [closed timelike curves](wikipedia://en/287091).[^o14]

Lanczos worked at the [University of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Frankfurt_am_Main) from 1924 to 1931,[^o6] delivering lectures for [Erwin Madelung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Madelung) as a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent).[^o7] He also briefly served as assistant to [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) in Berlin during the academic year 1928–29,[^o9]upon invitation by the latter.[^o6] It was [Leo Szilard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Szilard) who recommended him to Einstein.[^o4] Einstein wrote to Madelung, requesting a leave of absence for Lanczos, which was granted. Before leaving for Berlin, Lanczos wrote to Einstein that [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe) was being considered as his temporary replacement.[^o15] By the time he went to work with Einstein, Lanczos had already written multiple papers on relativity.[^o15] In Berlin, Lanczos examined the [motion of singularities](wikipedia://en/2037563)—meaning, particles—in curved spacetime as described by [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). Einstein had a high opinion of Lanczos for his mathematical skills. In this capacity, Lanczos replaced [Marcel Grossmann](wikipedia://en/1247037) as Einstein's collaborator, helping him with the difficult [mathematics of general relativity](wikipedia://en/1789646).[^o4] Although Einstein and Lanczos published no papers together, Einstein referred to the works of Lanczos in one of his subsequent articles on [distant parallelism](wikipedia://en/903686).[^o15] Another innovation of Lanczos in this context was [harmonic coordinates](wikipedia://en/7203375), which he introduced independently of [Théophile De Donder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Théophile_De_Donder). These were later used by [Yvonne Choquet-Bruhat](wikipedia://en/22081008) in her proof of the local existence and uniqueness of solutions to the Einstein field equations in vacuum with [initial values](wikipedia://en/498383) and in algorithms of [numerical relativity](wikipedia://en/1797708) that [simulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_physics) the spiral and merger of [binary systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_system) of [compact objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_object), which emit gravitational waves.[^o16]

Following the seminal publication of [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg) announcing the creation of his [matrix formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_mechanics) of quantum mechanics in 1925,[^o17] Lanczos wrote a paper demonstrating how the new theory could be expressed in terms of linear [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234).[^o18][^o4] However, at the time, this paper had little impact, in part because physicists were more used dealing with [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309).[^o22] [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger) published a series of papers detailing his own [undulatory version](wikipedia://en/59874) of quantum theory, which proved rather popular among physicists.[^o4] But Lanczos' paper made it clear that the two seemingly different formulations of quantum mechanics were in fact equivalent,[^o4] something Schrödinger himself later proved.[^o19] [Carl Eckart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Eckart) independently reached the same conclusion, based on the work of Lanczos.[^o20][^o21] This paper also helped [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) create his own formulation of quantum mechanics as a theory of [linear transformations](wikipedia://en/18102).[^o22] Lanczos's 1926 paper was the earliest continuum-theoretic formulation of quantum mechanics;[^o7] it was close to the notion of a [quantum field](wikipedia://en/25267).[^o22] Moreover, Lanczos was willing to accept the [probabilistic interpretation of the wave function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_interpretation).[^o23] In 1972, at an event organized by the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), Italy, [Bartel Leendert van der Waerden](wikipedia://en/91390) publicly recognized the significance of that paper, which correctly formulated the [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429) problem in terms of integration and even came close to introducing the [Dirac $\delta$-distribution](wikipedia://en/37021). But van der Waerden was unaware that Lanczos was in the audience until [Léon Rosenfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Léon_Rosenfeld) urged the latter to come to the stage.[^o7]

In 1927 Lanczos married Maria Rupp.[^o9]p. 41, 53 He moved to the United States in 1931.[^o7] Mindful of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), he turned his attention towards [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).[^o4] He began conducting research in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506),[^o7] and developed a number of concepts in service of early digital computers.[^o23] He served as a professor of mathematics and [aeronautical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering) at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) from 1931 to 1946.[^o6] Between 1927 and 1939, Lanczos split his life between two continents. His wife Maria Rupp, who had contracted tuberculosis, stayed with Lanczos' parents in Székesfehérvár year-around while Lanczos went to Purdue for half the year, teaching graduate students matrix mechanics and [tensor analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_analysis).[^o9]p. 41, 53 His lecture notes on quantum mechanics examined in detail its [mathematical formulation](wikipedia://en/20728), including topics in [function space](wikipedia://en/340630) and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).[^o4] At Purdue, he introduced an "experimental" curriculum for female students.[^o23]

In 1933 his son Elmar was born; Elmar came to [Lafayette, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette,_Indiana) with his father in August 1939, just before the Second World War broke out.[^o9]p. 41, 53 Maria died in 1938, the same year Lanczos became an American citizen. His father died the following year.[^o4] After the War, he left Purdue[^o7] and moved to [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle), working for the [Boeing Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing) and the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).[^o6] Between 1949 and 1952, Lanczos worked for the National Bureau of Standards (now the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology)) Institute for Numerical Analysis at the [University of California at Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA).[^o6] There, he participated in the [Mathematical Tables Project](wikipedia://en/488896).[^o7]

In 1942, Lanczos and [Gordon Charles Danielson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Charles_Danielson) developed a practical technique in [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), now known as the [fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512) (FFT).[^o24][^o7] But the significance of his discovery was not appreciated at the time, partly because there were no machines to execute this algorithm,[^o23] and today the FFT is credited to [J. W. Cooley](wikipedia://en/1565795) and [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974), who published the [Cooley–Tukey algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooley_Tukey_Algorithm) in 1965.[^o25] (As a matter of fact, similar claims can be made for several other mathematicians, including [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125).[^o25]) The FFT was implemented on a [digital computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer#Digital_computers) for the first time in 1966.[^o7]

Working in at the U.S. National Bureau of Standards in the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) after 1949, Lanczos developed a number of techniques for mathematical calculations using digital computers, such as the [Lanczos algorithm](wikipedia://en/2593852) for determining the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue,_eigenvector,_and_eigenspace) of large [Hermitian matrices](wikipedia://en/189682).[^o23]

In 1949, Lanczos showed that the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848), which plays a fundamental role in general relativity, can be obtained from a tensor potential now called the [Lanczos potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanczos_tensor).[^o26]

In 1952, Lanczos examined the utility of the [Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/184539) in [approximating](wikipedia://en/1092713) the solution of linear systems.[^o27]

During the [McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism) era, Lanczos came under suspicion for possible [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) links.[^o9] In 1955, he accepted an invitation from [Éamon de Valera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éamon_de_Valera), then the Prime Minister of Ireland, and moved to the School of Theoretical Physics at the [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies),[^o7] where his colleagues included Schrödinger and [John Lighton Synge](wikipedia://en/1252509).[^o7][^o28] Shortly after arriving he gave lectures on numerical methods, such as [a new approximation](wikipedia://en/1982776) for the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) he developed.[^o7] He remained there until his death in 1974.[^o29] He wrote many scientific papers and books during this period; he also became interested in some newly developed ideas in mathematical physics, notably [Schwartz distributions](wikipedia://en/51955) and [Sobolev spaces](wikipedia://en/611964).[^o7] Despite being a victim of Joseph McCarthy, Lanczos still referred to the United States as his "dream land" on conversations with those he knew.[^o7]

*Image: Gedenktafel, Szent István Platz 3, 2022 Székesfehérvár.jpg.* A commemorative plaque at the childhood home of Lanczos. The inscription reads: "Kornél Lánczos spent the years of his youth in this house from 1893 to 1911, an excellent practitioner of mathematics and physics, a world-renowned scientist of quantum mechanics and relativity."

In 1960, he won the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) for a paper explaining how to decompose an arbitrary rectangular matrix into three, the middle of which is [diagonal](wikipedia://en/174080) and the other two [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/105620). This technique is now recognized as [singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207), of use in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833).[^o7]

[Lanczos resampling](wikipedia://en/2338241) is based on a windowed [sinc function](wikipedia://en/610583) as a practical upsampling filter approximating the ideal sinc function, now widely used in video up-sampling for digital zoom applications and [image scaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_scaling). It was invented by Claude Duchon, who named it after Lanczos due to Duchon's use of the [sigma approximation](wikipedia://en/1455348) in constructing the filter, a technique created by Lanczos.[^o30]

During his career, he was invited to lecture of various topics of mathematical physics at many different institutions.[^o6] he maintained contact with his doctoral supervisor Ortvay before the War,[^o7] and occasionally returned to Budapest to lecture on various topics, such as the [Stark effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stark_effect) (1930) and [Hamilton's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton's_principle) and [canonical equations](wikipedia://en/198319) in classical mechanics (1933).[^o4] Throughout his life, Lanczos maintained his conviction that mathematics should not be separated from its history; he lectured on this topic with great enthusiasm.[^o23]

In 1974, he published a paper on the [vector potential](wikipedia://en/422936) in curved spacetime.[^o7] He died in Budapest that same year of a sudden [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction) during a summer visit. His collected works, six volumes in all, are held at North Carolina State University in collaboration with the Eötvös Physical Society in Budapest.[^o4] He was of sound mind mind up until the day he died, when he was working on the Fourier analysis of [random sequences](wikipedia://en/48256), a topic he was scheduled to lecture on in Dublin in July that year.[^o7]

## Publications {#Publications}

### Books {#Books}

- (1970). "The Variational Principles of Mechanics". University of Toronto Press. isbn:0-486-65067-7 Dedicated to Albert Einstein. This is a graduate text on [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics).[^o34] He published it shortly after moving to Los Angeles.[^o7] In the preface of the first edition (1949) it is described as a two-semester graduate course of three hours weekly. The second edition (1962) contains a new chapter on [relativistic mechanics](wikipedia://en/2021419) and the third (1966) has an appendix on [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159) for [cyclic coordinates](wikipedia://en/23371726#Cyclic_coordinates_and_conserved_momenta). In the fourth edition (1970), Lanczos discusses at length [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918) and makes further use of Noether's theorem.[^o35]
- (1956). "Applied Analysis". Prentice Hall Reprinted 2010 by Dover Publications. . An exposition of his investigations of ideas in the boundary between [classical](wikipedia://en/48396) and numerical analysis illustrated by worked examples, topics covered include large scale [linear systems](wikipedia://en/722503), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954), [numerical quadrature](wikipedia://en/170089) and [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) expansions.[^o37] The chapter on numerical quadrature was inspired by a number of problems posed by Schrödinger.[^o7]
- (1961). "Linear Differential Operators". Van Nostrand
- (1965). "Albert Einstein and the Cosmic World Order". Interscience Publishers Based on six lectures delivered at the University of Michigan in the spring of 1962.
- (1966). "Discourse on Fourier Series". Oliver & Boyd This book was written for advanced undergraduates and graduate students in mathematics, physics, and engineering. Lanczos clarifies the meanings of terms such as limit or [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652); and discusses the [Sturm–Liouville problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturm–Liouville_problems), the [Fejér mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fejér_mean), the [Gibbs phenomenon](wikipedia://en/373986), Fourier versus [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448), Fourier integrals, the Fourier transform, and the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610).[^o41][^o42] He goes into the historical development of the subject,[^o41] but does not cover [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration) or [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630).[^o42]
- *Numbers without End*, Edinburgh: Oliver & Boyd. 1968.
- (1970). "Judaism and Science". Leeds University Press. isbn:978-0-853-16021-2 Lectures given in honor of [Selig Brodetsky](wikipedia://en/4601432).
- (1970). "Space Through the Ages: The Evolution of Geometrical Ideas from Pythagoras to Hilbert and Einstein". Academic Press. isbn:9780124358508 Based on a series of lectures given to mathematicians, physicists, chemists, engineers, and philosophers at North Carolina State University in 1968, Lanczos overviews the [history of geometry](wikipedia://en/11953) from the time of the ancient Greeks up until the early twentieth century.[^o46][^o45] He elaborates upon [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), [tensor analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_analysis), and the abstract [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) of [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/20598932) and [Banach](wikipedia://en/3989), and briefly describes [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849).[^o45] He does not, however, discuss [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).[^o46]
- (1974). "The Einstein Decade (1905-1915)". Elek. isbn:978-0-236-17632-8 In this book, Lanczos made use of his fluency in the German language as his grasp of to mathematics and physics discuss in detail the [scientific publications of Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_scientific_publications_by_Albert_Einstein) during that time.[^o7]
- (1998). "Cornelius Lanczos: Collected Published Papers with Commentaries". North Carolina State University. isbn:0-929493-01-X
