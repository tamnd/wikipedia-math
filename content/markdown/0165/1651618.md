# Tibor Gallai

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tibor Gallai"}

**Tibor Gallai** (born Tibor Grünwald, 15 July 1912 – 2 January 1992) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He worked in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), especially in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), and was a lifelong friend and collaborator of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284). He was a student of [Dénes Kőnig](wikipedia://en/1327120) and an advisor of [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938). He was a corresponding member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) (1991).

:::

## His main results {#His_main_results}

The [Edmonds&ndash;Gallai decomposition theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonds&ndash;Gallai_decomposition_theorem), which was proved independently by Gallai and [Jack Edmonds](wikipedia://en/3625459), describes finite graphs from the point of view of matchings. Gallai also proved, with [Milgram](wikipedia://en/21029542), [Dilworth's theorem](wikipedia://en/749033) in 1947, but as they hesitated to publish the result, Dilworth independently discovered and published it.[^o4]

Gallai was the first to prove the higher-dimensional version of [van der Waerden's theorem](wikipedia://en/157178).

With [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) he gave a [necessary and sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_and_sufficient_condition) for a sequence to be the degree sequence of a graph, known as the [Erdős–Gallai theorem](wikipedia://en/30208106).
