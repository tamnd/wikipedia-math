# Induced subgraph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Induced subgraph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **induced subgraph** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is another graph, formed from a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) of the graph and *all* of the edges, from the original graph, connecting pairs of vertices in that subset.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Formally, let $G=(V,E)$ be any graph, and let $S\subseteq V$ be any subset of vertices of $G$. Then the induced subgraph $G[S]$ is the graph whose vertex set is $S$ and whose edge set consists of all of the edges in $E$ that have both endpoints in $S$.[^o4] That is, for any two vertices $u,v\in S$, $u$ and $v$ are adjacent in $G[S]$ if and only if they are adjacent in $G$. The same definition works for [undirected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph), [directed graphs](wikipedia://en/19721986), and even [multigraphs](wikipedia://en/1774970).

The induced subgraph $G[S]$ may also be called the subgraph induced in $G$ by $S$, or (if context makes the choice of $G$ unambiguous) the induced subgraph of $S$.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Important types of induced subgraphs include the following.

*Image: Snake in the box.svg.* The [snake-in-the-box](wikipedia://en/1850040) problem concerns the longest [induced paths](wikipedia://en/7714430) in [hypercube graphs](wikipedia://en/6706815)

- [Induced paths](wikipedia://en/7714430) are induced subgraphs that are [paths](wikipedia://en/638889). The [shortest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_path) between any two vertices in an unweighted graph is always an induced path, because any additional edges between pairs of vertices that could cause it to be not induced would also cause it to be not shortest. Conversely, in [distance-hereditary graphs](wikipedia://en/17315337), every induced path is a shortest path.[^o7]
- [Induced cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_cycle) are induced subgraphs that are [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609). The [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) of a graph is defined by the length of its shortest cycle, which is always an induced cycle. According to the [strong perfect graph theorem](wikipedia://en/744171), induced cycles and their [complements](wikipedia://en/1175649) play a critical role in the characterization of [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531).[^o8]
- [Cliques](wikipedia://en/524466) and [independent sets](wikipedia://en/524501) are induced subgraphs that are respectively [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) or [edgeless graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgeless_graph).
- [Induced matchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_matching) are induced subgraphs that are [matchings](wikipedia://en/581797).
- The [neighborhood](wikipedia://en/7309251) of a vertex is the induced subgraph of all vertices adjacent to it.

:::

::: {#o10 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computation"}

## Computation {#Computation}

The [induced subgraph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/6892618) is a form of the [subgraph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/450062) in which the goal is to test whether one graph can be found as an induced subgraph of another. Because it includes the [clique problem](wikipedia://en/249254) as a special case, it is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete).[^o11]
