# Niemeier lattice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Niemeier lattice"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Niemeier lattice** is one of the 24 [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_bilinear_form) even [unimodular lattices](wikipedia://en/1648605) of [rank](wikipedia://en/244541) 24, which were classified by (txt). (Venkov 1978) gave a simplified proof of the classification. (Witt 1941) mentions that he found more than 10 such lattices, but gives no further details. One example of a Niemeier lattice is the [Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919) found in 1967.

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

Niemeier lattices are usually labelled by the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109) of their [root lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_lattice). Each Niemeier lattice can be constructed from its root lattice (except for the Leech lattice which has no roots) by adjoining elements known as glue vectors, as detailed in §16.1 of (Conway Sloane 1998). The Dynkin diagrams associated with a Niemeier lattice have rank either 0 or 24, and all of their components have the same [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number). (The Coxeter number, at least in these cases, is the number of roots divided by the dimension.) There are exactly 24 Dynkin diagrams with these properties, and there turns out to be a unique Niemeier lattice for each of these Dynkin diagrams.

The complete list of Niemeier lattices is given in the following table. In the table,

*G*<sub>0</sub> is the order of the group generated by reflections

*G*<sub>1</sub> is the order of the group of automorphisms fixing all components of the Dynkin diagram

*G*<sub>2</sub> is the order of the group of automorphisms of permutations of components of the Dynkin diagram

*G*<sub>∞</sub> is the index of the root lattice in the Niemeier lattice, in other words, the order of the "glue code". It is the square root of the discriminant of the root lattice.

*G*<sub>0</sub>×*G*<sub>1</sub>×*G*<sub>2</sub> is the order of the automorphism group of the lattice

*G*<sub>∞</sub>×*G*<sub>1</sub>×*G*<sub>2</sub> is the order of the automorphism group of the corresponding deep hole.

## The neighborhood graph of the Niemeier lattices {#The_neighborhood_graph_of_the_Niemeier_lattices}

If *L* is an odd unimodular lattice of dimension 8*n* and *M* its sublattice of even vectors, then *M* is contained in exactly 3 unimodular lattices, one of which is *L* and the other two of which are even. (If *L* has a norm 1 vector then the two even lattices are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic).) The [**Kneser neighborhood graph**](wikipedia://en/2724154) in 8*n* dimensions has a point for each even lattice, and a line joining two points for each odd 8*n* dimensional lattice with no norm 1 vectors, where the vertices of each line are the two even lattices associated to the odd lattice. There may be several lines between the same pair of vertices, and there may be lines from a vertex to itself. Kneser proved that this graph is always connected. In 8 dimensions it has one point and no lines, in 16 dimensions it has two points joined by one line, and in 24 dimensions it is the following graph:

*Image: Niemeier Lattices Neighborhood graph.svg.*

Each point represents one of the 24 Niemeier lattices, and the lines joining them represent the 24 dimensional odd unimodular lattices with no norm 1 vectors. The number on the left is the Coxeter number of the Niemeier lattice. The red index number in the node indicates the row of the associated table above.

In 32 dimensions the neighborhood graph has more than a billion vertices.

## Properties {#Properties}

Some of the Niemeier lattices are related to [sporadic simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group). The Leech lattice is acted on by a [double cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_covering_group) of the [Conway group](wikipedia://en/345511), and the lattices A<sub>1</sub><sup>24</sup> and A<sub>2</sub><sup>12</sup> are acted on by the [Mathieu groups](wikipedia://en/380207) M<sub>24</sub> and M<sub>12</sub>.

The Niemeier lattices, other than the Leech lattice, correspond to the *deep holes* of the Leech lattice. This implies that the [affine Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_Dynkin_diagram) of the Niemeier lattices can be seen inside the Leech lattice, when two points of the Leech lattice are joined by no lines when they have distance $\sqrt 4$, by 1 line if they have distance $\sqrt 6$, and by a double line if they have distance $\sqrt 8$.

Niemeier lattices also correspond to the 24 orbits of primitive norm zero vectors *w* of the even unimodular Lorentzian lattice [II<sub>25,1</sub>](wikipedia://en/32486086), where the Niemeier lattice corresponding to *w* is *w*<sup>⊥</sup>/*w*.

:::
