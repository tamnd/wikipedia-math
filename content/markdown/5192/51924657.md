# Prince (cipher)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prince"}

**Prince** is a block cipher targeting low latency, unrolled hardware implementations. It is based on the so-called FX construction.[^o3] Its most notable feature is the **alpha reflection**: the decryption is the encryption with a related key which is very cheap to compute. Unlike most other "lightweight" ciphers, it has a small number of rounds and the layers constituting a round have low logic depth. As a result, fully unrolled implementation are able to reach much higher frequencies than [AES](wikipedia://en/1260) or [PRESENT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRESENT_%28cipher%29). According to the authors, for the same time constraints and technologies, PRINCE uses 6–7 times less area than PRESENT-80 and 14–15 times less area than AES-128.[^o4]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The block size is 64 bits and the key size is 128 bits. The key is split into two 64 bit keys $K_{0}$ and $K_{1}$. The input is XORed with $K_{0}$, then is processed by a core function using $K_{1}$. The output of the core function is xored by $K'_{0}$ to produce the final output ($K_{0}'$ is a value derived from $K_{0}$). The decryption is done by exchanging $K_{0}$ and $K'_{0}$ and by feeding the core function with $K_{1}$ xored with a constant denoted alpha.[^o6]

The core function contain 5 "forward" rounds, a middle round, and 5 "backward" rounds, for 11 rounds in total. The original paper mentions 12 rounds without explicitly depicting them; if the middle round is counted as two rounds (as it contains two nonlinear layers), then the total number of rounds is 12.

A forward round starts with a round constant XORed with $K_{1}$, then a nonlinear layer $S$, and finally a linear layer $M$. The "backward" rounds are exactly the inverse of the "forward" rounds except for the round constants.

The nonlinear layer is based on a single 4-bit [S-box](wikipedia://en/240991) which can be chosen among the affine-equivalent of 8 specified S-boxes.

The linear layer consists of multiplication by a 64x64 matrix $M'$ and a shift row similar to the one in [AES](wikipedia://en/1260) but operating on 4-bit nibbles rather than bytes.

$M'$ is constructed from 16x16 matrices $M_{0}$ and $M_{1}$ in such a way that the multiplication by $M'$ can be computed by four smaller multiplications, two using $M_{0}$ and two using $M_{1}$.

The middle round consists of the $S$ layer followed by $M'$ followed by the $S^{-1}$ layer.

## Cryptanalysis {#Cryptanalysis}

To encourage [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) of the Prince cipher, the organizations behind it created the "Prince challenge". <https://www.emsec.rub.de/research/research_startseite/prince-challenge/>

The paper "Security analysis of PRINCE"[^o9] presents several attacks on full and round reduced variants, in particular, an attack of complexity 2<sup>\125.1</sup> and a related key attack requiring 2<sup>33</sup> data.

A generic time–memory–data tradeoff for FX constructions has been published, with an application to Prince.[^o10] The paper argues that the FX construction is a fine solution to improve the security of a widely deployed cipher (like [DES-X](wikipedia://en/619801) did for [DES](wikipedia://en/7978)) but that it is a questionable choice for new designs. It presents a tweak to the Prince cipher to strengthen it against this particular kind of attack.

A [biclique cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/41940042) attack has been published on the full cipher. It is somewhat inline with the estimation of the designers since it reduces the key search space by 2<sup>\1.28</sup> (the original paper mentions a factor 2). [^o11]

The paper "Reflection Cryptanalysis of PRINCE-Like Ciphers" focuses on the alpha reflection and establishes choice criteria for the alpha constant. It shows that a poorly chosen alpha would lead to efficient attacks on the full cipher; but the value randomly chosen by the designers is not among the weak ones.[^o12]

Several meet-in-the-middle attacks have been published on round reduced versions.[^o13][^o14][^o15]

An attack in the multi-user setting can find the keys of 2 users among a set of 2<sup>32</sup> users in time 2<sup>65</sup>.[^o16]

An attack on 10 rounds with overall complexity of 118.56 bits has been published.[^o17]

An attack on 7 rounds with time complexity of 2<sup>57</sup> operations has been published.[^o18]

A differential fault attack has been published using 7 faulty cipher texts under random 4 bit nibble fault model.[^o19]

The paper "New approaches for round-reduced PRINCE cipher cryptanalysis"[^o20] presents boomerang attack and [known-plaintext attack](wikipedia://en/517019) on reduced round versions up to 6 rounds.

In 2015 few additional attacks have been published but are not freely available.[^o21][^o22]
