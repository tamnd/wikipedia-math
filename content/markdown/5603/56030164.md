# Chi-bounded

*Image: Chi bounded.svg.* A graph $G$ that can be 4-colored, whose largest [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) is size 4. Families this graph belongs to include the [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531), graphs of [boxicity](wikipedia://en/11167471) two, the [circle](wikipedia://en/1399873) and [polygon-circle graphs](wikipedia://en/28174525), and the [even-hole-free graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even-hole-free_graph) (specifically the **even-cycle-free graphs**).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chi-bounded"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **$\chi$-bounded** (using the Greek letter [chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi_%28letter%29)) family $\mathcal{F}$ of graphs is one for which there is some function $f$ such that, for every integer $t$ the graphs in $\mathcal{F}$ with $t=\omega(G)$ ([clique number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_number)) can be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) with at most $f(t)$ colors. The function $f(t)$ is called a **$\chi$-binding function** for $\mathcal{F}$. These concepts and their notations were formulated by [András Gyárfás](wikipedia://en/614969). The use of the Greek letter [chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi_%28letter%29) in the term $\chi$-bounded is based on the fact that the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of a graph $G$ is commonly denoted $\chi(G)$. An overview of the area can be found in a survey of Alex Scott and [Paul Seymour](wikipedia://en/1575708).

:::

## Nontriviality {#Nontriviality}

It is not true that the family of all graphs is $\chi$-bounded. As (Descartes 1947), (Zykov 1949) and (Mycielski 1955) showed, there exist [triangle-free graphs](wikipedia://en/7667996) of arbitrarily large chromatic number, so for these graphs it is not possible to define a finite value of $f(2)$. Thus, $\chi$-boundedness is a nontrivial concept, true for some graph families and false for others.

## Specific classes {#Specific_classes}

Every class of graphs of bounded [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) is (trivially) $\chi$-bounded, with $f(t)$ equal to the bound on the chromatic number. This includes, for instance, the [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), the [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431), and the graphs of bounded [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816). Complementarily, the graphs in which the [independence number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_number) is bounded are also $\chi$-bounded, as [Ramsey's theorem](wikipedia://en/184898) implies that they have large cliques.

[Vizing's theorem](wikipedia://en/5449464) can be interpreted as stating that the [line graphs](wikipedia://en/675231) are $\chi$-bounded, with $f(t)=t+1$. The [claw-free graphs](wikipedia://en/12415907) more generally are also $\chi$-bounded with $f(t)\le t^2$. This can be seen by using Ramsey's theorem to show that, in these graphs, a vertex with many neighbors must be part of a large clique. This bound is nearly tight in the worst case, but connected claw-free graphs that include three mutually-nonadjacent vertices have even smaller chromatic number, $f(t)=2t$.

Other $\chi$-bounded graph families include:

- The [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531), with $f(t)=t$
- The graphs of [boxicity](wikipedia://en/11167471) two, which is the [intersection graphs](wikipedia://en/7726759) of axis-parallel rectangles, with $f(t)\in O(t\log(t))$([big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578))
- The graphs of bounded [clique-width](wikipedia://en/16795502)
- The [intersection graphs](wikipedia://en/7726759) of scaled and translated copies of any compact convex shape in the plane
- The [polygon-circle graphs](wikipedia://en/28174525), with $f(t)=2^t$
- The [circle graphs](wikipedia://en/1399873), with $f(t)=2t\log_2t+2\log_2\log_2t+10t$ and (generalizing circle graphs) the "outerstring graphs", intersection graphs of bounded curves in the plane that all touch the unbounded face of the [arrangement](wikipedia://en/864149) of the curves
- The [outerstring graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outerstring_graph) is an intersection graph of curves that lie in a common half-plane and have one endpoint on the boundary of that half-plane
- The [intersection graphs](wikipedia://en/7726759) of curves that cross a fixed curve between 1 and $n \in \mathbb{N}$ times
- The [even-hole-free graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even-hole-free_graph) (or more precisely, the **even-cycle-free graphs**, which is free of holes of length 4), with $f(t)=2t$, as every such graph has a [bisimplicial vertex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisimplicial_vertex), a vertex whose neighborhood is the union of two cliques

However, although intersection graphs of convex shapes, circle graphs, and outerstring graphs are all special cases of [string graphs](wikipedia://en/16081202), the string graphs themselves are not $\chi$-bounded. They include as a special case the intersection graphs of [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860), which are also not $\chi$-bounded.

## Unsolved problems {#Unsolved_problems}

::: {#o9 .problem cue=unsolved}

Are all tree-free graph classes $\chi$-bounded?

:::

According to the [Gyárfás–Sumner conjecture](wikipedia://en/56030165), for every [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) $T$, the graphs that do not contain $T$ as an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) are $\chi$-bounded. For instance, this would include the case of claw-free graphs, as a claw is a special kind of tree. However, the conjecture is known to be true only for certain special trees, including [paths](wikipedia://en/4587190) and radius-two trees.

A $\chi$-bounded class of graphs is **polynomially $\chi$-bounded** if it has a $\chi$-binding function $f(t)$ that grows at most polynomially as a function of $t$. As every $n$-vertex graph $G$ contains an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) with cardinality at least $n/\chi(G)$, all polynomially $\chi$-bounded classes have the [Erdős–Hajnal property](wikipedia://en/42585591). Another problem on $\chi$-boundedness was posed by Louis Esperet, who asked whether every hereditary class of graphs that is $\chi$-bounded is also polynomially $\chi$-bounded. A strong counterexample to Esperet's conjecture was announced in 2022 by Briański, Davies, and Walczak, who proved that there exist $\chi$-bounded hereditary classes whose function $f(t)$ can be chosen arbitrarily as long as it grows more quickly than a certain cubic polynomial.

:::
