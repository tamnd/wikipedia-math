# Sander P. Zwegers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sander P. Zwegers"}

**Sander Pieter Zwegers** (born April 16, 1975) is a Dutch mathematician known for making a connection between [Maass forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maass_form) and [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717)'s [mock theta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mock_theta_function) in 2002.[^o2][^o3] He was born in [Oosterhout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oosterhout). After a period at the [Max-Planck Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck_Institute) in Bonn, he became an assistant professor at the [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin) in 2008. Since 2011, he has been professor of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne).

:::

## Research {#Research}

In 1976, the American mathematician George Andrews found what is nowadays known as the "[Lost Notebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Notebook)" of Ramanujan. It contains many remarkable results, including the mysterious [mock theta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mock_theta_functions). This notebook contains what many specialists regard as Ramanujan’s deepest work. It was Sander Zwegers who, as a PhD student, had groundbreaking ideas how to fit the mock theta functions into a broader context. His 2002 PhD thesis has led to numerous publications and international conferences.[^o5]

Zwegers' general area of interest is number theory. More specifically, he studies modular forms and variations thereof, such as Maass forms, mock modular forms, (indefinite) theta functions, and (Maass) Jacobi forms.
