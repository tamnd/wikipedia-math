# Kolmogorov's three-series theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kolmogorov's three-series theorem"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Kolmogorov's three-series theorem**, named after [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), gives a criterion for the [almost sure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_sure) [convergence](wikipedia://en/50723) of an [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) in terms of the convergence of three different series involving properties of their [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543). Kolmogorov's three-series theorem, combined with [Kronecker's lemma](wikipedia://en/3759614), can be used to give a relatively easy proof of the [strong law of large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_law_of_large_numbers#Strong_law).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $(X_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ be [independent random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables). The random series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty X_n$ [converges](wikipedia://en/50723) [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) in $\mathbb{R}$ if the following conditions hold for some $A > 0$, and only if the following conditions hold for any $A > 0$:

$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|X_n| \ge A)$ converges.; Let $Y_n = X_n\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_n|\le A\}}$. Then $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[Y_n]$, the series of [expected values](wikipedia://en/9653) of $Y_n$, converges.; $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{var}(Y_n)$ converges, where $Y_n$ is defined as in the second condition.

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

### Sufficiency of conditions ("if") {#Sufficiency_of_conditions_("if")}

Condition (i) and [Borel–Cantelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel–Cantelli) give that $X_n = Y_n$ for $n$ large, [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908). Hence $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_n$ converges if and only if $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_n$ converges. Conditions (ii)-(iii) and [Kolmogorov's two-series theorem](wikipedia://en/41362059) give the almost sure convergence of $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_n$.

### Necessity of conditions ("only if") {#Necessity_of_conditions_("only_if")}

Suppose that $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_n$ converges almost surely.

Without condition (i), by Borel–Cantelli there would exist some $A > 0$ such that $\{|X_n| \ge A\}$ for infinitely many $n$, almost surely. But then the series would diverge. Therefore, we must have condition (i).

We see that condition (iii) implies condition (ii): [Kolmogorov's two-series theorem](wikipedia://en/41362059) along with condition (i) applied to the case $A = 1$ gives the convergence of $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(Y_n - \mathbb{E}[Y_n])$. So given the convergence of $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_n$, we have $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[Y_n]$ converges, so condition (ii) is implied.

Thus, it only remains to demonstrate the necessity of condition (iii), and we will have obtained the full result. It is equivalent to check condition (iii) for the series $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Z_n = \textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(Y_n - Y'_n)$ where for each $n$, $Y_n$ and $Y'_n$ are [IID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IID)—that is, to employ the assumption that $\mathbb{E}[Y_n] = 0$, since $Z_n$ is a sequence of random variables bounded by 2, converging almost surely, and with $\mathrm{var}(Z_n) = 2\mathrm{var}(Y_n)$. So we wish to check that if $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Z_n$ converges, then $\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{var}(Z_n)$ converges as well. This is a special case of a more general result from [martingale theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martingale_theory) with summands equal to the increments of a [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536) sequence and the same conditions ($\mathbb{E}[Z_n] = 0$; the series of the [variances](wikipedia://en/32344) is converging; and the summands are [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258)).[^o9][^o10][^o11]

:::

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

As an illustration of the theorem, consider the example of the *[harmonic series with random signs](wikipedia://en/142488#Random_harmonic_series)*:

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \pm \frac{1}{n}.$$

Here, "$\pm$" means that each term $1/n$ is taken with a random sign that is either $1$ or $-1$ with respective probabilities $1/2,\ 1/2$, and all random signs are chosen independently. Let $X_n$ in the theorem denote a random variable that takes the values $1/n$ and $-1/n$ with equal probabilities. With $A=2$ the summands of the first two series are identically zero and var(Y<sub>n</sub>)=$n^{-2}$. The conditions of the theorem are then satisfied, so it follows that the harmonic series with random signs converges almost surely. On the other hand, the analogous series of (for example) square root reciprocals with random signs, namely

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},$$

*diverges* almost surely, since condition (3) in the theorem is not satisfied for any A. Note that this is different from the behavior of the analogous series with *alternating* signs, $\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n/\sqrt{n}$, which does converge.
