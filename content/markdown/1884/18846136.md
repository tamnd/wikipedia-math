# Cyclotomic character

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cyclotomic character"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **cyclotomic character** is a [character](wikipedia://en/293418#Multiplicative_character) of a [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) giving the Galois [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity). As a one-dimensional [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $R$, its [representation space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_space) is generally denoted by $R(1)$ (that is, it is a representation $\chi : G \to \operatorname{Aut}_{R}(R(1)) \approx \operatorname{GL}(1, R)$).

:::

## *p*-adic cyclotomic character {#p-adic_cyclotomic_character}

Fix $p$ a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), and let $G_\mathbf{Q}$ denote the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). The roots of unity

$$\mu_{p^n} = \left\{ \zeta \in \bar\mathbf{Q}^\times \mid \zeta^{p^n} = 1 \right\}$$

form a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order $p^n$, generated by any choice of a [primitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_root_of_unity) $p^{n}$th root of unity $\zeta_{p^{n}}$.

Since all of the primitive roots in $\mu_{p^n}$ are [Galois conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_conjugate), the Galois group $G_\mathbf{Q}$ acts on $\mu_{p^n}$ by automorphisms. After fixing a primitive root of unity $\zeta_{p^n}$ generating $\mu_{p^n}$, any element $\zeta\in\mu_{p^n}$ can be written as a power of $\zeta_{p^n}$, where the exponent is a unique element in $\mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z}$, which is a unit if $\zeta$ is also primitive. One can thus write, for $\sigma\in G_\mathbf{Q}$,

$$\sigma.\zeta := \sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^{a(\sigma, n)}$$

with $a(\sigma,n) \in (\mathbf{Z}/p^n \mathbf{Z})^\times$ independent of $\zeta$, but depending on both $\sigma$ and $p$. This defines a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) called the **mod $p^{n}$ cyclotomic character**:

$$\begin{aligned}{\chi_{p^n}}:G_{\mathbf{Q}} &\to (\mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z})^{\times} \\ \sigma &\mapsto a(\sigma, n), \end{aligned}$$

which is viewed as a [character](wikipedia://en/293418) since the action corresponds to a homomorphism $G_{\mathbf Q} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mu_{p^n}) \cong (\mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z})^\times \cong \mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z})$.

Fixing $p$ and $\sigma$ and varying $n$, the $a(\sigma, n)$ form a compatible system in the sense that they give an element of the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109)

$$\varprojlim_n (\mathbf{Z}/p^n\mathbf{Z})^\times \cong \mathbf{Z}_p^\times,$$

the units in the ring of [p-adic integers](wikipedia://en/51423). Thus the ${\chi_{p^n}}$ assemble to a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) called **$p$-adic cyclotomic character**:

$$\begin{aligned} \chi_p:G_{\mathbf Q} &\to \mathbf{Z}_p^\times \cong \mathrm{GL_1}(\mathbf{Z}_p) \\ \sigma &\mapsto (a(\sigma, n))_n \end{aligned}$$

encoding the action of $G_{\mathbf Q}$ on all $p$-power roots of unity $\mu_{p^n}$ simultaneously. In fact equipping $G_{\mathbf Q}$ with the [Krull topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krull_topology) and $\mathbf{Z}_p$ with the [$p$-adic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic) topology makes this a continuous representation of a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315).

## As a compatible system of $\ell$-adic representations {#As_a_compatible_system_of_-adic_representations}

By varying $\ell$ over all prime numbers, a [compatible system of ℓ-adic representations](wikipedia://en/21641504) is obtained from the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic characters (when considering compatible systems of representations, the standard terminology is to use the symbol $\ell$ to denote a prime instead of $p$). That is to say, $\chi = \{ \chi_{\ell} \}_{\ell}$ is a "family" of $\ell$-adic representations

$$\chi_\ell:G_\mathbf{Q}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_1(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)$$

satisfying certain compatibilities between different primes. In fact, the $\chi_{\ell}$ form a [strictly compatible system of ℓ-adic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_compatible_system_of_ℓ-adic_representations).

## Geometric realizations {#Geometric_realizations}

The $p$-adic cyclotomic character is the $p$-adic [Tate module](wikipedia://en/3094921) of the [multiplicative group scheme](wikipedia://en/504697) $\mathbf{G}_{m,\mathbf{Q}}$ over $\mathbf{Q}$. As such, its representation space can be viewed as the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of the groups of $p^{n}$th roots of unity in $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$.

In terms of [cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231), the $p$-adic cyclotomic character is the [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) of the first $p$-adic [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) group of $\mathbf{G}_{m}$. It can also be found in the étale cohomology of a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469), namely the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578): it is the dual of $H^{2}_{\text{é}t}(\mathbf{P}^{1} )$.

In terms of [motives](wikipedia://en/430680), the $p$-adic cyclotomic character is the $p$-adic realization of the **Tate motive** $\mathbf{Z}(1)$. As a [Grothendieck motive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_motive), the Tate motive is the dual of $H^{2}( \mathbf{P}^{1} )$.[^o6]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The $p$-adic cyclotomic character satisfies several nice properties.

- It is [unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unramified) at all primes $\ell \ne p$ (i.e. the [inertia subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertia_subgroup) at $\ell$ acts trivially).
- If $\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}$ is a [Frobenius element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element) for $\ell \ne p$, then $\chi_{p}(\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}) = \ell$.
- It is [crystalline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystalline_representation) at $p$.
