# Conductor of an abelian variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conductor of an abelian variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), the **conductor of an abelian variety** defined over a [local](wikipedia://en/145375) or [global field](wikipedia://en/288358) *F* is a measure of how "bad" the [bad reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_reduction) at some prime is. It is connected to the [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644) in the field generated by the [torsion points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_point#Abelian_varieties).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) *A* defined over a field *F* as above, with ring of integers *R*, consider the [Néron model](wikipedia://en/9190726) of *A*, which is a 'best possible' model of *A* defined over *R*. This model may be represented as a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) over

Spec(*R*)

(cf. [spectrum of a ring](wikipedia://en/37291)) for which the [generic fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_fibre) constructed by means of the morphism

Spec(*F*) → Spec(*R*)

gives back *A*. Let *A*<sup>0</sup> denote the open subgroup scheme of the Néron model whose fibres are the connected components. For a maximal ideal *P* of *R* with [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) *k*, *A*<sup>0</sup><sub>*k*</sub> is a group variety over *k*, hence an extension of an abelian variety by a linear group. This linear group is an extension of a torus by a [unipotent group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipotent_group). Let *u<sub>P</sub>* be the dimension of the unipotent group and *t<sub>P</sub>* the dimension of the torus. The order of the conductor at *P* is

$$f_P = 2u_P + t_P + \delta_P , \,$$

where $\delta_P\in\mathbb N$ is a measure of wild ramification. When *F* is a number field, the conductor ideal of *A* is given by

$$f= \prod_P P^{f_P}.$$

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- *A* has [good reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_reduction) at *P* if and only if $u_P=t_P=0$ (which implies $f_P=\delta_P= 0$).
- *A* has [semistable reduction](wikipedia://en/898483) if and only if $u_P=0$ (then again $\delta_P= 0$).
- If *A* acquires semistable reduction over a Galois extension of *F* of degree prime to *p*, the residue characteristic at *P*, then δ<sub>P</sub> = 0.
- If $p> 2d+1$, where *d* is the dimension of *A*, then $\delta_P=0$.
- If $p\le 2d+1$ and *F* is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of ramification degree $e(F/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, there is an upper bound expressed in terms of the function $L_p(n)$, which is defined as follows:

Write $n=\sum_{k\ge0}c_kp^k$ with $0\le c_k<p$ and set $L_p(n)=\sum_{k\ge0}kc_kp^k$. Then[^o6]

$$(*)\qquad f_P \le 2d + e(F/\mathbb{Q}_p) \left( p \left\lfloor \frac{2d}{p-1} \right\rfloor + (p-1)L_p\left( \left\lfloor \frac{2d}{p-1} \right\rfloor \right) \right).$$

Further, for every $d,p,e$ with $p\le 2d+1$ there is a field $F/\mathbb{Q}_p$ with $e(F/\mathbb{Q}_p)=e$ and an abelian variety $A/F$ of dimension $d$ so that $(*)$ is an equality.

:::
