# Winifred Sargent

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Winifred Sargent"}

**Winifred Lydia Caunden Sargent** (8 May 1905 – October 1979) was an English mathematician. She studied at [Newnham College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College,_Cambridge) and carried out research into [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration), [fractional integration and differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_integration_and_differentiation) and the properties of [BK-spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BK-spaces).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Sargent was born into a [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) family, daughter of Henry Sargent and Edith, his second wife, growing up in [Fritchley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritchley), [Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire). She attended [Ackworth School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ackworth_School), a private school for Quakers, from 1915 to 1919. She then won a scholarship to attend [The Mount School, York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mount_School,_York), another Quaker school, and later the [Herbert Strutt School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belper_School) in [Belper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belper).[^o4] In 1923, while there, she won a [Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby) scholarship, a State Scholarship, and a Mary Ewart scholarship to attend [Newnham College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College,_Cambridge) and study mathematics in 1924.

While at Newnham she won further awards: an [Arthur Hugh Clough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Hugh_Clough) Scholarship in 1927, a Mary Ewart Travelling Scholarship and a Goldsmiths Company Senior Studentship both in 1928. She graduated with a [First class degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_degree) and remained at Cambridge conducting research but was unsatisfied by her progress and left to teach mathematics at [Bolton High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolton).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Sargent's first publication was in 1929, *On Young's criteria for the convergence of Fourier series and their conjugates*, published in the *[Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society](wikipedia://en/24334026).*[^o6] In 1931 she was appointed an Assistant Lecturer at [Westfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_College) and became a member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in January 1932.[^o7] in 1936 she moved to [Royal Holloway, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway,_University_of_London), at the time both women's colleges. In 1939 she became a doctoral student of [Lancelot Bosanquet](wikipedia://en/35221526), but [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) broke out, preventing his formal supervision from continuing. In 1941 Sargent was promoted to lecturer at Royal Holloway, moving to [Bedford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_College_%28London%29) in 1948. She served on the [Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403) teaching committee from 1950 to 1954.[^o8] In 1954 she was awarded the degree of Sc.D. ([Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science)) by Cambridge and was given the title of [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29). While at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) she supervised Alan J. White in 1959.[^o9][^o10]

Bosanquet started a weekly seminar in mathematics in 1947, which Sargent attended without absence for twenty years until her retirement in 1967. She rarely presented at it, and did not attend mathematical conferences, despite being a compelling speaker.

## Mathematical results {#Mathematical_results}

Much of Sargent's mathematical research involved studying types of integral, building on work done on [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration) and the [Riemann integral](wikipedia://en/26390). She produced results relating to the [Perron](wikipedia://en/412708) and Denjoy integrals and [Cesàro summation](wikipedia://en/705600). Her final three papers consider [BK-spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BK-spaces) or Banach coordinate spaces, proving a number of interesting results.[^o12]

For example, her 1936 paper[^o13] proves a version of [Rolle's theorem](wikipedia://en/200305) for [Denjoy–Perron integrable](wikipedia://en/412708) functions using different techniques from the standard proofs:[^o14] as in much of Dr. Sargent's work, the arguments are pushed as far as they will go and counter examples given to show that the results are the best possible.

Her 1953 paper[^o15] established several important results on [summability kernels](wikipedia://en/42625595) and is referenced in two textbooks on [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).[^o16] Her papers in 1950 and 1957 contributed to [fractional integration and differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_integration_and_differentiation) theory.[^o17]

In her obituary, her work is described as being:[^o18]

marked by its exceptional lucidity, its exactness of expression and by the decisiveness of her results. She made important contributions to a field in which the complexity of the structure can only be revealed by subtle arguments.
