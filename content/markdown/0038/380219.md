# Stone space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stone space"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Stone space**, also known as a **profinite space**, **profinite set**, or **Boolean space**,[^o3] is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected) space. Stone spaces are named after [Marshall Harvey Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Harvey_Stone) who introduced and studied them in the 1930s in the course of his investigation of [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959), which culminated in [his representation theorem for Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/604111).

:::

## Equivalent conditions {#Equivalent_conditions}

The following conditions on the topological space $X$ are equivalent:[^o4][^o2]

- $X$ is a Stone space;
- $X$ is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to the [projective limit](wikipedia://en/15109) (in the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051)) of an inverse system of finite [discrete spaces](wikipedia://en/56061);
- $X$ is compact and [totally separated](wikipedia://en/6233#Disconnected_spaces);
- $X$ is compact, [T<sub>0</sub>](wikipedia://en/68121), and [zero-dimensional](wikipedia://en/517682) (in the sense of the [small inductive dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_inductive_dimension));
- $X$ is [coherent](wikipedia://en/544273) and Hausdorff.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Important examples of Stone spaces include finite [discrete spaces](wikipedia://en/56061), the [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172) and the space $\mathbb{Z}_p$ of [$p$-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integers), where $p$ is any [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). Generalizing these examples, any [product](wikipedia://en/24910) of arbitrarily many finite discrete spaces is a Stone space, and the topological space underlying any [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266) is a Stone space. The [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) of the natural numbers with the discrete topology, or indeed of any discrete space, is a Stone space.

:::

## Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras {#Stone's_representation_theorem_for_Boolean_algebras}

To every [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959) $B$ we can associate a Stone space $S(B)$ as follows: the elements of $S(B)$ are the [ultrafilters](wikipedia://en/31911) on $B,$ and the topology on $S(B),$ called the Stone topology, is generated by the sets of the form $\{ F \in S(B) : b \in F \},$ where $b \in B.$

[Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/604111) states that every Boolean algebra is isomorphic to the Boolean algebra of [clopen sets](wikipedia://en/233398) of the Stone space $S(B)$; and furthermore, every Stone space $X$ is homeomorphic to the Stone space belonging to the Boolean algebra of clopen sets of $X.$ These assignments are [functorial](wikipedia://en/10987), and we obtain a [category-theoretic duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_category) between the category of Boolean algebras (with homomorphisms as morphisms) and the category of Stone spaces (with continuous maps as morphisms).

Stone's theorem gave rise to a number of similar dualities, now collectively known as [Stone dualities](wikipedia://en/379619).

:::

## Condensed mathematics {#Condensed_mathematics}

The category of Stone spaces with continuous maps is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the [pro-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-category) of the [category of finite sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_finite_sets), which explains the term "profinite sets". The profinite sets are at the heart of the project of [condensed mathematics](wikipedia://en/68011960), which aims to replace topological spaces with "condensed sets", where a topological space *X* is replaced by the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) that takes a profinite set *S* to the set of continuous maps from *S* to *X*.[^o11]
