# Sergei Sobolev

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sergei Sobolev"}

Prof **Sergei Lvovich Sobolev**, [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (Серге́й Льво́вич Со́болев; 6 October 1908 – 3 January 1989) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564).

:::

Sobolev introduced notions that are now fundamental for several areas of mathematics. [Sobolev spaces](wikipedia://en/611964) can be defined by some growth conditions on the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247). They and their embedding theorems are an important subject in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). [Generalized functions](wikipedia://en/1580246) (later known as [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29)) were first introduced by Sobolev in 1935 for [weak solutions](wikipedia://en/3555416), and further developed by [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485). Sobolev abstracted the classical notion of [differentiation](wikipedia://en/7921), so expanding the range of application of the technique of Newton and Leibniz. The theory of distributions is considered now as the calculus of the modern epoch.[^o3]

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) as the son of Lev Aleksandrovich Sobolev, a lawyer, and his wife, Natalya Georgievna.[^o5] His city was renamed Petrograd in his youth and then Leningrad in 1924.

Sobolev studied mathematics at [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) and graduated in 1929, having studied under Professor [Nikolai Günther](wikipedia://en/41820318). After graduation, he worked with [Vladimir Smirnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Smirnov_%28mathematician%29), whom he considered as his second teacher. He worked in Leningrad from 1932 and at the [Steklov Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/984494) in Moscow from 1934. He headed the institute in evacuation to [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan) during [World War&nbsp;II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War&nbsp;II). He was a [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) professor of mathematics from 1935 to 1957 and also a deputy director of the [Institute for Atomic Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurchatov_Institute) from 1943 to 1957, where he participated in the [Soviet atomic bomb project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_atomic_bomb_project). In 1958, he led with [Nikolay Brusentsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Brusentsov) the development of the [ternary computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternary_computer) [Setun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setun).

In 1956, Sobolev joined a number of scientists in proposing a large-scale scientific and educational initiative for the Eastern parts of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), which resulted in the creation of the Siberian Division of the [Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^o6] He was the founder and first director of the Institute of Mathematics at [Akademgorodok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademgorodok) near [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk), which was later to bear his name, and played an important role in the establishment and development of [Novosibirsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk_State_University). In 1962, he called for a reform of the Soviet education system.[^o7]

He died in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow).[^o8]

## Family {#Family}

In 1930 he married Ariadna Dmitrievna.[^o5]

## Publications {#Publications}

In 1955 he co-wrote *[The Main Features of Cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Main_Features_of_Cybernetics)* with [Alexey Lyapunov](wikipedia://en/14655582) and [Anatoly Kitov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Kitov), which was published in *[Problems of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problems_of_Philosophy_%28magazine%29)*.
