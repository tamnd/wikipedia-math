# Jesse Douglas

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jesse Douglas"}

**Jesse Douglas** (July 3, 1897 – September 7, 1965) was an American mathematician and [Fields Medalist](wikipedia://en/10859) known for his general solution to [Plateau's problem](wikipedia://en/338129).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

He was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family[^o4] in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), the son of Sarah (née Kommel) and Louis Douglas. He attended [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) as an undergraduate, graduating with honors in Mathematics in 1916. He then moved to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) as a graduate student, obtaining a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics in 1920.[^o5]

Douglas was one of two winners of the first [Fields Medals](wikipedia://en/10859),[^o6] awarded in 1936. He was honored for solving, in 1930, the [problem of Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau_problem), which asks whether a minimal surface exists for a given boundary. The problem, open since 1760 when [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange) raised it, is part of the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and is also known as the *[soap bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_bubble) problem*. Douglas also made significant contributions to the [inverse problem of the calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/10452186). The [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) awarded him the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) in 1943.

Douglas worked at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o7] Later he became a full professor at the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) where he taught until his death. At the time CCNY only offered undergraduate degrees and he taught the advanced calculus course.
