# Arithmetic of abelian varieties

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic of abelian varieties"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **arithmetic of abelian varieties** is the study of the [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) of an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152), or a family of abelian varieties. It goes back to the studies of [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) on what are now recognized as [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225); and has become a very substantial area of [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) both in terms of results and conjectures. Most of these can be posed for an abelian variety *A* over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) *K*; or more generally (for [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358) or more general finitely-generated rings or fields).

:::

## Integer points on abelian varieties {#Integer_points_on_abelian_varieties}

There is some tension here between concepts: *integer point* belongs in a sense to [affine geometry](wikipedia://en/243890), while *abelian variety* is inherently defined in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849). The basic results, such as [Siegel's theorem on integral points](wikipedia://en/3989092), come from the theory of [diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558).

## Rational points on abelian varieties {#Rational_points_on_abelian_varieties}

The basic result, the [Mordell–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/23087727) in [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), says that *A*(*K*), the group of points on *A* over *K*, is a [finitely-generated abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_abelian_group). A great deal of information about its possible [torsion subgroups](wikipedia://en/144052) is known, at least when *A* is an elliptic curve. The question of the *rank* is thought to be bound up with [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) (see below).

The [torsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor) theory here leads to the [Selmer group](wikipedia://en/1970862) and [Tate–Shafarevich group](wikipedia://en/25711736), the latter (conjecturally finite) being difficult to study.

## Heights {#Heights}

The theory of [heights](wikipedia://en/3141855) plays a prominent role in the arithmetic of abelian varieties. For instance, the canonical [Néron–Tate height](wikipedia://en/9236652) is a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) with remarkable properties that appear in the statement of the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161).

## Reduction mod *p* {#Reduction_mod_p}

Reduction of an abelian variety *A* modulo a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) of (the integers of) *K* — say, a prime number *p* — to get an abelian variety *A<sub>p</sub>* over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), is possible for [almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) *p*. The 'bad' primes, for which the reduction [degenerates](wikipedia://en/232952) by acquiring [singular points](wikipedia://en/981915), are known to reveal very interesting information. As often happens in number theory, the 'bad' primes play a rather active role in the theory.

Here a refined theory of (in effect) a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to reduction mod *p* — the [Néron model](wikipedia://en/9190726) — cannot always be avoided. In the case of an elliptic curve there is an algorithm of [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) describing it.

## L-functions {#L-functions}

For abelian varieties such as A<sub>*p*</sub>, there is a definition of [local zeta-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_zeta-function) available. To get an L-function for A itself, one takes a suitable [Euler product](wikipedia://en/398540) of such local functions; to understand the finite number of factors for the 'bad' primes one has to refer to the [Tate module](wikipedia://en/3094921) of A, which is (dual to) the [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) group H<sup>1</sup>(A), and the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) action on it. In this way one gets a respectable definition of [Hasse–Weil L-function](wikipedia://en/1157842) for A. In general its properties, such as [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377), are still conjectural – the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture) (which was proven in 2001) was just a special case, so that's hardly surprising.

It is in terms of this L-function that the [conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjecture_of_Birch_and_Swinnerton-Dyer) is posed. It is just one particularly interesting aspect of the general theory about values of L-functions L(*s*) at integer values of *s*, and there is much empirical evidence supporting it.

## Complex multiplication {#Complex_multiplication}

Since the time of [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) (who knew of the *lemniscate function* case) the special role has been known of those abelian varieties $A$ with extra automorphisms, and more generally endomorphisms. In terms of the ring ${\rm End}(A)$, there is a definition of [abelian variety of CM-type](wikipedia://en/1138222) that singles out the richest class. These are special in their arithmetic. This is seen in their L-functions in rather favourable terms – the [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) required is all of the [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) type, rather than needing more general [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation). That reflects a good understanding of their Tate modules as [Galois modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module). It also makes them *harder* to deal with in terms of the conjectural [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) ([Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518) and [Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/3124804)). In those problems the special situation is more demanding than the general.

In the case of elliptic curves, the [Kronecker Jugendtraum](wikipedia://en/2164726) was the programme [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) proposed, to use elliptic curves of CM-type to do [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) explicitly for [imaginary quadratic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field) – in the way that [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) allow one to do this for the field of rational numbers. This generalises, but in some sense with loss of explicit information (as is typical of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables)).

## Manin–Mumford conjecture {#Manin–Mumford_conjecture}

The Manin–Mumford conjecture of [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086) and [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594), proved by [Michel Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928),[^o10][^o11] states that a curve *C* in its [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) *J* can only contain a finite number of points that are of finite order (a [torsion point](wikipedia://en/3039330)) in *J*, unless *C* = *J*. There are other more general versions, such as the [Bogomolov conjecture](wikipedia://en/18992933) which generalizes the statement to non-torsion points.

:::
