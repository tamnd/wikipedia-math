# Edward de Bono

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edward de Bono"}

**Edward Charles Francis Publius de Bono** (19 May 1933 – 10 June 2021)[^o3] was a [Maltese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_people) physician and commentator. He originated the term [lateral thinking](wikipedia://en/298235), and wrote many books on thinking, including *[Six Thinking Hats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Thinking_Hats)*.[^o4]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Edward Charles Francis Publius de Bono was born in [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta) on 19 May 1933.[^o6] He was the son of [Josephine Burns de Bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephine_Burns_de_Bono).

Educated at [St. Edward's College, Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Edward's_College,_Malta), he then gained a medical degree from the [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta). Following this, he proceeded as a [Rhodes Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholar) in 1955 to [Christ Church, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford),[^o7] where he gained an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxbridge_and_Dublin%29) in psychology and physiology. He represented Oxford in polo and set two canoeing records. He then gained a PhD degree in medicine from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

De Bono held faculty appointments at the universities of Oxford, Cambridge, London and Harvard.[^o10] He was a professor at the [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta), the [University of Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pretoria), the [University of Central England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Central_England) (now called [Birmingham City University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_University)) and [Dublin City University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_City_University). De Bono held the Da Vinci Professor of Thinking chair at the [University of Advancing Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Advancing_Technology) in Tempe, Arizona, US.[^o8] He was one of the 27 Ambassadors for the European Year of Creativity and Innovation 2009.[^o9]

The originator of the term 'Lateral Thinking', de Bono wrote 85 books with translations into 47 languages.[^o10] He taught his thinking methods to government agencies, corporate clients, organizations and individuals, privately or publicly in group sessions. He promoted the World Center for New Thinking (2004–2011), based in Malta, which applied Thinking Tools to solution and policy design on the geopolitical level. His book The Use of Lateral Thinking (1967) was described as one of the 12 most influential books since [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) by *[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times)*.[^o11]

In 1976, de Bono took part in a radio debate for the BBC with British philosopher [A. J. Ayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Ayer), on the subject of effective democracy.[^o12]

Starting on Wednesday 8 September 1982, the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) ran a series of 10 weekly programmes entitled *de Bono's Thinking Course*.[^o13] In the shows, he explained how thinking skills could be improved by attention and practice. The series was repeated the following year. A book with the same title accompanied the series. In May 1994, he gave a half-hour *[Opinions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinions_%28TV_series%29)* lecture televised on [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4) and subsequently published in *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* as "Thinking Hats On".[^o14] In 1995, he created a futuristic documentary film, *2040: Possibilities by Edward de Bono*, depicting a lecture to an audience of viewers released from a [cryogenic freeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryonics) for contemporary society in the year 2040.[^o10]

Convinced that a key way forward for humanity is a better language, he published *The Edward de Bono Code Book* in 2000. In this book, he proposed [a suite of new words based on numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasigraphy), where each number combination represents a useful idea or situation that currently does not have a single-word representation. For example, de Bono code 6/2 means "Give me my point of view and I will give you your point of view." Such a code might be used in situations where one or both of the two parties in a dispute are making insufficient effort to understand the other's perspective.[^o15]

### Parallel thinking {#Parallel_thinking}

**Parallel thinking** is a term coined by de Bono.[^o18][^o19] Parallel thinking is described as a constructive alternative to: "adversarial thinking"; [debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debate); and the approaches exemplified by Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle (whom de Bono refers to as the "Greek gang of three" (GG3)[^o20][^o21]). In general, parallel thinking is a further development of the well-known [lateral-thinking](wikipedia://en/298235) processes, focusing even more on explorations—looking for *what can be* rather than for *what is*.

Parallel thinking is defined as a thinking process where focus is split in specific directions. When done in a group it effectively avoids the consequences of the adversarial approach (as used in courts). In adversarial debate, the objective is to [prove](wikipedia://en/27952971) or [disprove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disprove) statements put forward by the parties (normally two). This is also known as the [dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813) approach. In Parallel Thinking, practitioners put forward as many statements as possible in several (preferably more than two) parallel tracks. This leads to *exploration* of a subject where all participants can contribute, in parallel, with knowledge, facts, feelings, etc. Crucial to the method is that the process is done in a disciplined manner, and that all participants play along and contribute *in parallel*. Thus each participant must stick to the specific track. [Six Thinking Hats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Thinking_Hats) is an example of its implementation.[^o19]

:::

## Other ideas {#Other_ideas}

De Bono invented the [L game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_game), which he introduced in his book *The Five-Day Course in Thinking*.

In 2000, de Bono advised a UK [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office) committee that the [Arab–Israeli conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab–Israeli_conflict) might be due, in part, to low levels of [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc) found in people who eat [unleavened bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unleavened_bread) (e.g. [pita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pita) [flatbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbread)). De Bono argued that [low zinc levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_deficiency) leads to heightened aggression. He suggested shipping out jars of [Marmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmite) to compensate.[^o23][^o24]

Edward de Bono argued that companies could raise money just as governments now do – by printing it. He put forward the idea of [private currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_currency) as a claim on products or services produced by the issuer.[^o25]

## Critiques {#Critiques}

In the *Handbook of Creativity*, [Robert J. Sternberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Sternberg) writes,

> Equally damaging to the scientific study of creativity, in our view, has been the takeover of the field, in the popular mind, by those who follow what might be referred to as a pragmatic approach. Those taking this approach have been concerned primarily with developing creativity, secondarily with understanding it, but almost not at all with testing the validity of their ideas about it. \[...\] Perhaps the foremost proponent of this approach is Edward De Bono, whose work on lateral thinking and other aspects of creativity has had what appears to be considerable commercial success.[^o27]

*Frameworks For Thinking* is an evaluation of 42 popular thinking frameworks conducted by a team of researchers. Regarding Edward de Bono they write,

> \[he\] is more interested in the usefulness of developing ideas than proving the reliability or efficacy of his approach. There is sparse research evidence to show that generalised improvements in thinking performance can be attributed to training in the use of CoRT \[Cognitive Research Trust\] or Thinking Hats tools. An early evaluation of CoRT reported significant benefits for Special Educational Needs (SEN) pupils... However, in a more recent study with Australian aboriginal children (Ritchie and Edwards, 1996), little evidence of generalisation was found other than in the area of creative thinking.[^o28]

Summarising de Bono's 1985 work in *Conflicts: A Better Way to Resolve Them*, M. Afzalur Rahim, [distinguished professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professors_in_the_United_States#Distinguished_%28research/teaching%29_professor) of management at [Western Kentucky University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Kentucky_University) with a particular focus on [conflict management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_management) in organizations, gives his view that, as pertains to Rahim's own field of research, "De Bono's approach to total elimination of [conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_%28process%29) is no different from the approaches of the classicists. This approach to dealing with conflict is completely out of tune with modern thinking and, therefore, unsatisfactory."[^o29]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1971 de Bono married Josephine Hall-White. They had two sons, Caspar and Charlie, and later divorced.[^o31] His will, published after his death, named two more children, another son, Francis Edward de Bono, also known as Edward de Bono, the son of Magdalena Szekely, and a daughter, Juliana Pars.[^o32]

De Bono regularly visited Australia and in 1995 acquired Little Green Island, a [private island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_island) of 24 ha located off of [Shoal Point, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal_Point,_Queensland), from businesswoman [Janet Holmes à Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Holmes_à_Court).[^o33] He subsequently constructed a retreat hosting corporate seminars and "think tank" events.[^o34][^o35] De Bono owned four private islands in total, also including Reklusia Cay in the Bahamas, [West Skeam Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Skeam_Island) in Ireland, and Tessera in Italy's [Venetian Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Lagoon). In a 2003 interview he observed "I just like islands, that's all", and compared owning an island to having "your own principality, territory and psychological space".[^o36]

De Bono was awarded honorary degrees from the [Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMIT_University),[^o37] and the [University of Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dundee).[^o38] Asteroid [2541 Edebono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2541_Edebono) discovered by [Luboš Kohoutek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luboš_Kohoutek) is named after him. Three years after De Bono died, a middle school in Handaq named the school after him, using the legacy of lateral thinking.[^o39]
