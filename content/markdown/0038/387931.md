# French curve

*Image: Krzywiki.jpg.* A set of the three most common French curves, also known as a [Burmester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Burmester) set. The bottom object is most commonly used for [hyperbolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolas); the smaller one above it is suited for [ellipses](wikipedia://en/9277). The large one is used mostly for [parabolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolas).[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="French curve"}

A **French curve** is a template usually made from [metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal), [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood) or [plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic) composed of many different curved segments. It is used in manual [drafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_drawing) and in [fashion design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_design) to draw smooth curves of varying radii. The curve is placed on the drawing material, and a [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil), [knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knife) or other implement is traced around its curves to produce the desired result. They were invented by the German mathematician [Ludwig Burmester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Burmester) and are also known as Burmester (curve) set.

:::

## Clothing design {#Clothing_design}

*Image: L-Zeichnen2.png.* A complete [Burmester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Burmester) set from the *[Lexikon der gesamten Technik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexikon_der_gesamten_Technik)* (1904)

French curves are used in [fashion design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_design) and [sewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing) alongside [hip curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_curves), [straight edges](wikipedia://en/147854) and [right-angle rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28tool%29). [Commercial clothing patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_%28sewing%29) can be personalized for fit by using French curves to draw neckline, sleeve, bust and waist variations.
