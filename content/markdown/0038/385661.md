# Inverse scattering problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inverse scattering problem"}

In mathematics and physics, the **inverse scattering problem** is the problem of determining characteristics of an object, based on data of how it [scatters](wikipedia://en/164483) incoming radiation or particles.[^n1] It is the [inverse problem](wikipedia://en/203956) to the **direct scattering problem**, which is to determine how radiation or particles are scattered based on the properties of the scatterer.

:::

[Soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) equations are a class of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) which can be studied and solved by a method called the [inverse scattering transform](wikipedia://en/4916481), which reduces the nonlinear PDEs to a linear inverse scattering problem. The [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748), the [Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation) and the [KP equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KP_equation) are examples of soliton equations. In one space dimension the inverse scattering problem is equivalent to a [Riemann-Hilbert problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann-Hilbert_problem).[^n2] Inverse scattering has been applied to many problems including [radiolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar), [echolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_location), [geophysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysical) survey, [nondestructive testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondestructive_testing), [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging), and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).[^n3][^n4]
