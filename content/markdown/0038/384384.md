# Solomon Lefschetz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Solomon Lefschetz"}

**Solomon Lefschetz** (Соломо́н Ле́фшец; 3 September 1884 – 5 October 1972) was a Russian-born American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who did fundamental work on [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), its applications to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and the theory of non-linear [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753).[^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), the son of Alexander Lefschetz and his wife Sarah or Vera Lifschitz, both [Russian-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-Jewish) traders who used to travel around Europe and the Middle East (since they held [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) passports).[^o7] Thereafter, the family moved to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) and he was educated there in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) at the [École Centrale Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Centrale_Paris) but emigrated to the US in 1905.

He was badly injured in an industrial accident in 1907, losing both hands.[^o9] He moved towards mathematics, receiving a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in algebraic geometry from [Clark University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_University) in Worcester, Massachusetts in 1911.[^o10] He then took positions at the [University of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln) and [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), moving to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1924, where he was soon given a permanent position. He remained there until 1953.

In the application of topology to algebraic geometry, he followed the work of [Charles Émile Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Émile_Picard), whom he had heard lecture in Paris at the [École Centrale Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Centrale_Paris). He proved theorems on the topology of [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) sections of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties), which provide a basic inductive tool (these are now seen as allied to [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560), though a [Lefschetz pencil](wikipedia://en/2783111) of hyperplane sections is a more subtle system than a Morse function because hyperplanes intersect each other). The [Picard–Lefschetz formula](wikipedia://en/34794974) in the theory of [vanishing cycles](wikipedia://en/5859204) is a basic tool relating the [degeneration](wikipedia://en/232952) of families of varieties with 'loss' of topology, to [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250). He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1920 in Strasbourg.[^o11] His book *L'analysis situs et la géométrie algébrique* from 1924, though opaque foundationally given the current technical state of [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory), was in the long term very influential (one could say that it was one of the sources for the eventual proof of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705), through [SGA 7](wikipedia://en/394508#SGA_7) also for the study of [Picard groups](wikipedia://en/1850134) of [Zariski surface](wikipedia://en/3096979)). In 1924 he was awarded the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) for his work in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1925 and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1929.[^o12][^o13]

The [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/582530), now a basic result of topology, was developed by him in papers from 1923 to 1927, initially for [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). Later, with the rise of [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) in the 1930s, he contributed to the [intersection number](wikipedia://en/384327) approach (that is, in cohomological terms, the ring structure) via the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) and duality on manifolds. His work on topology was summed up in his monograph *[Algebraic Topology](http://www.ams.org/online_bks/coll27/)* (1942). From 1944 he worked on [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309).

He was editor of the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* from 1928 to 1958. During this time, the *Annals* became an increasingly well-known and respected journal, and Lefschetz played an important role in this.[^o14]

In 1945 he travelled to Mexico for the first time, where he joined the Institute of Mathematics at the [National University of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Mexico) as a visiting professor. He visited frequently for long periods, and during 1953–1966 he spent most of his winters in Mexico City.[^o14] He played an important role in the foundation of mathematics in Mexico, and sent several students back to Princeton. His students included Emilio Lluis, [José Adem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Adem), [Samuel Gitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Gitler_Hammer), [Santiago López de Medrano](wikipedia://en/44779577), [Francisco Javier González-Acuña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Javier_González-Acuña) and Alberto Verjovsky.[^o3]

Lefschetz came out of retirement in 1958, because of the launch of [Sputnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik), to augment the mathematical component of [Glenn L. Martin Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_L._Martin_Company)'s [Research Institute for Advanced Studies](wikipedia://en/25718963) (RIAS) in Baltimore, Maryland. His team became the world's largest group of mathematicians devoted to research in [nonlinear differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_differential_equation).[^o15] The RIAS mathematics group stimulated the growth of nonlinear differential equations through conferences and publications. He left RIAS in 1964 to form the Lefschetz Center for Dynamical Systems at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), Providence, Rhode Island.[^o16]

- *L´Analysis situs et la géométrie algébrique*, Paris, Gauthier-Villars 1924[^o18]
- *Intersections and transformations of complexes and manifolds*, [Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937) vol. 28, 1926, pp. 1–49, [online](http://www.ams.org/journals/tran/1926-028-01/S0002-9947-1926-1501331-3/S0002-9947-1926-1501331-3.pdf); fixed-point theorem, published in vol. 29, 1927, pp. 429–462, [online](http://www.ams.org/journals/tran/1927-029-02/S0002-9947-1927-1501397-1/S0002-9947-1927-1501397-1.pdf).

- *Topology*, AMS 1930[^o20]
- *Algebraic Topology*, New York, American Mathematical Society 1942
- *Introduction to topology*, Princeton 1949
- with [Joseph P. LaSalle](wikipedia://en/47624922), *Stability by Liapunov's direct method with applications*, New York, Academic Press 1961[^o21]
- *Algebraic geometry*, Princeton 1953, 2nd edn., 1964
- *Differential equations: geometric theory*, Interscience, 1957,[^o22] 2nd edn., 1963
- *Stability of nonlinear control systems*, 1965
