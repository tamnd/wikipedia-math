# J. H. C. Whitehead

*Image: Whiteheadlink.png.* Whitehead link

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="J. H. C. Whitehead"}

**John Henry Constantine Whitehead** (11 November 1904 – 8 May 1960), known as "Henry", was a British mathematician and was one of the founders of [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831). He was born in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) (then known as Madras), in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India), and died in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), in 1960.

:::

## Life {#Life}

J. H. C. (Henry) Whitehead was the son of the Right Rev. [Henry Whitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Whitehead_%28bishop%29), [Bishop of Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Madras_of_the_Church_of_South_India), who had studied mathematics at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), and was the nephew of [Alfred North Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) and Isobel Duncan. He was brought up in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), went to [Eton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College) and read mathematics at [Balliol College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford). After a year working as a stockbroker, at Buckmaster & Moore, he started a PhD in 1929 at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). His thesis, titled *The representation of [projective spaces](wikipedia://en/242135)*, was written under the direction of [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen) in 1930. While in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), he also worked with [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384).

He became a fellow of Balliol in 1933. In 1934 he married the concert pianist Barbara Smyth, great-great-granddaughter of [Elizabeth Fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Fry) and a cousin of [Peter Pears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pears); they had two sons. In 1936, he co-founded [The Invariant Society](wikipedia://en/30248689), the student mathematics society at Oxford.[^o5]

During the Second World War he worked on [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) for submarine warfare. Later, he joined the codebreakers at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), and by 1945 was one of some fifteen mathematicians working in the "[Newmanry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newmanry)", a section headed by [Max Newman](wikipedia://en/19867) and responsible for breaking a German [teleprinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleprinter) cipher using machine methods.[^o6] Those methods included the [Colossus machines](wikipedia://en/6229), early digital electronic computers.[^o6]

From 1947 to 1960 he was the [Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professorships#Waynflete_Professors_of_Pure_Mathematics) at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford).[^o7]

He became president of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) (LMS) in 1953, a post he held until 1955.[^o8] The LMS established two prizes in memory of Whitehead. The first is the annually awarded, to multiple recipients, [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913); the second a biennially awarded [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize).[^o9]

[Joseph J. Rotman](wikipedia://en/51256431), in his book on algebraic topology, as a tribute to Whitehead's intellect, says, "There is a canard that every textbook of algebraic topology either ends with the definition of the Klein bottle or is a personal communication to J. H. C. Whitehead."[^o10]

Whitehead died from an asymptomatic heart attack during a visit to Princeton University in May 1960.[^o11]

In the late 1950s, Whitehead had approached [Robert Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Maxwell), then chairman of [Pergamon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon_Press), to start a new journal, [*Topology*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28journal%29), however Whitehead died before its first edition appeared in 1962.

## Work {#Work}

Whitehead's definition of [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) gave a setting for homotopy theory that became standard. He introduced the idea of [simple homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), which was later much developed in connection with [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500). The [Whitehead product](wikipedia://en/3129615) is an operation in homotopy theory. The [Whitehead problem](wikipedia://en/212619) on [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) was solved (as an [independence](wikipedia://en/1141208) proof) by [Saharon Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211).[^o13] His involvement with topology and the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798) led to the creation of the [Whitehead manifold](wikipedia://en/1192013). The definition of [crossed modules](wikipedia://en/1977281) is due to him. He also made important contributions in [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), particularly on [triangulations](wikipedia://en/1440976) and their associated [smooth structures](wikipedia://en/1542238).
