# Pontryagin class

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pontryagin class"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Pontryagin classes**, named after [Lev Pontryagin](wikipedia://en/582075), are certain [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530) of real vector bundles. The Pontryagin classes lie in [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) with degrees a multiple of four.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a real vector bundle $E$ over $M$, its $k$-th Pontryagin class $p_k(E)$ is defined as[^o4]

$$p_k(E) = p_k(E, \mathbb{Z}) = (-1)^k c_{2k}(E\otimes \mathbb{C}) \in H^{4k}(M, \mathbb{Z}),$$

where:

- $c_{2k}(E\otimes \mathbb{C})$ denotes the $2k$-th [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) of the [complexification](wikipedia://en/755604) $E\otimes \mathbb{C} = E\oplus iE$ of $E$,
- $H^{4k}(M, \mathbb{Z})$ is the $4k$-[cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) group of $M$ with [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) coefficients.

The rational Pontryagin class $p_k(E, \mathbb{Q})$ is defined to be the image of $p_k(E)$ in $H^{4k}(M, \mathbb{Q})$, the $4k$-cohomology group of $M$ with [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) coefficients.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The **total Pontryagin class**

$$p(E)=1+p_1(E)+p_2(E)+\cdots\in H^*(M,\mathbb{Z}),$$

is (modulo 2-torsion) multiplicative with respect to [Whitney sum](wikipedia://en/507354#W) of vector bundles, i.e.,[^o7]

$$2p(E\oplus F)=2p(E)\smile p(F)$$

for two vector bundles $E$ and $F$ over $M$. In terms of the individual Pontryagin classes $p_k$,

$$2p_1(E\oplus F)=2p_1(E)+2p_1(F),$$

$$2p_2(E\oplus F)=2p_2(E)+2p_1(E)\smile p_1(F)+2p_2(F)$$

and so on.

The vanishing of the Pontryagin classes and [Stiefel–Whitney classes](wikipedia://en/877761) of a vector bundle does not guarantee that the vector bundle is trivial. For example, up to [vector bundle isomorphism](wikipedia://en/276524#Vector_bundle_morphisms), there is a unique nontrivial rank 10 vector bundle $E_{10}$ over the [9-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). (The [clutching function](wikipedia://en/8536059) for $E_{10}$ arises from the [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/173954#Homotopy_groups) $\pi_8(\mathrm{O}(10)) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.) The Pontryagin classes and Stiefel-Whitney classes all vanish: the Pontryagin classes don't exist in degree 9, and the Stiefel–Whitney class $w_9$ of $E_{10}$ vanishes by the [Wu formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiefel-Whitney_class#Relations_over_the_Steenrod_algebra) $w_9 = w_1 w_8 + Sq^1(w_8)$. Moreover, this vector bundle is stably nontrivial, i.e. the [Whitney sum](wikipedia://en/507354#W) of $E_{10}$ with any trivial bundle remains nontrivial. (Hatcher 2009, 76)

Given a $2 k$-dimensional vector bundle $E$ we have

$$p_k(E)=e(E)\smile e(E),$$

where $e(E)$ denotes the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) of $E$, and $\smile$ denotes the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) of cohomology classes.

### Pontryagin classes and curvature {#Pontryagin_classes_and_curvature}

As was shown by [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905) and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) around 1948, the rational Pontryagin classes

$$p_k(E,\mathbf{Q})\in H^{4k}(M,\mathbf{Q})$$

can be presented as differential forms which depend polynomially on the [curvature form](wikipedia://en/489440) of a vector bundle. This [Chern–Weil theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern–Weil_theory) revealed a major connection between [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and global [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

For a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) $E$ over a $n$-dimensional [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) $M$ equipped with a [connection](wikipedia://en/706412), the total Pontryagin class is expressed as

$$p=\left[1-\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Omega ^2)}{8 \pi ^2}+\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Omega ^2)^2-2 {\rm Tr}(\Omega ^4)}{128 \pi ^4}-\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Omega ^2)^3-6 {\rm Tr}(\Omega ^2) {\rm Tr}(\Omega ^4)+8 {\rm Tr}(\Omega ^6)}{3072 \pi ^6}+\cdots\right]\in H^*_{dR}(M),$$

where $\Omega$ denotes the [curvature form](wikipedia://en/489440), and $H^*_{dR} (M)$ denotes the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) groups.

### Pontryagin classes of a manifold {#Pontryagin_classes_of_a_manifold}

The **Pontryagin classes of a smooth manifold** are defined to be the Pontryagin classes of its [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794).

[Novikov](wikipedia://en/628515) proved in 1966 that if two compact, oriented, smooth manifolds are [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) then their rational Pontryagin classes $p_k(M, \mathbf{Q})$ in $H^{4k}(M, \mathbf{Q})$ are the same. If the dimension is at least five, there are at most finitely many different smooth manifolds with given [homotopy type](wikipedia://en/212250#Homotopy_equivalence_of_spaces) and Pontryagin classes.[^o11]

### Pontryagin classes from Chern classes {#Pontryagin_classes_from_Chern_classes}

The Pontryagin classes of a complex vector bundle $\pi\colon E \to X$ are completely determined by its Chern classes. This follows from the fact that $E\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C} \cong E\oplus \bar{E}$, the Whitney sum formula, and properties of Chern classes of its complex conjugate bundle. That is, $c_i(\bar{E}) = (-1)^ic_i(E)$ and $c(E\oplus\bar{E}) = c(E)c(\bar{E})$. Then, given this relation, we can see

> $1 - p_1(E) + p_2(E) - \cdots + (-1)^np_n(E) = (1 + c_1(E) + \cdots + c_n(E)) \cdot (1 - c_1(E) + c_2(E) -\cdots + (-1)^nc_n(E))$[^o13].

For example, we can apply this formula to find the Pontryagin classes of a complex vector bundle on a curve and a surface. For a curve, we have

> $$(1-c_1(E))(1 + c_1(E)) = 1 + c_1(E)^2$$

so all of the Pontryagin classes of complex vector bundles are trivial.

In general, looking at first two terms of the product

> $$(1-c_1(E) + c_2(E) + \ldots + (-1)^n c_n(E))(1 + c_1(E) + c_2(E) +\ldots + c_n(E)) = 1 - c_1(E)^2 + 2c_2(E) + \ldots$$

we can see that $p_1(E) = c_1(E)^2 - 2c_2(E)$. In particular, for line bundles this simplifies further since $c_2(L) = 0$ by dimension reasons.

### Pontryagin classes on a quartic K3 surface {#Pontryagin_classes_on_a_quartic_K3_surface}

Recall that a quartic polynomial whose vanishing locus in $\mathbb{CP}^3$ is a smooth subvariety is a [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116). If we use the normal sequence

$$0 \to \mathcal{T}_X \to \mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{CP}^3}|_X \to \mathcal{O}(4) \to 0$$

we can find

$$\begin{aligned} c(\mathcal{T}_X) &= \frac{c(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{CP}^3}|_X)}{c(\mathcal{O}(4))} \\ &= \frac{(1+[H])^4}{(1+4[H])} \\ &= (1 + 4[H] + 6[H]^2)\cdot(1 - 4[H] + 16[H]^2) \\ &= 1 + 6[H]^2 \end{aligned}$$

showing $c_1(X) = 0$ and $c_2(X) = 6[H]^2$. Since $[H]^2$ corresponds to four points, due to Bézout's lemma, we have the second chern number as $24$. Since $p_1(X) = -2c_2(X)$ in this case, we have $p_1(X) = -48$. This number can be used to compute the third stable homotopy group of spheres.[^o15]

:::

## Pontryagin numbers {#Pontryagin_numbers}

**Pontryagin numbers** are certain [topological invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_invariant) of a smooth [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). Each Pontryagin number of a manifold $M$ vanishes if the dimension of $M$ is not divisible by 4. It is defined in terms of the Pontryagin classes of the [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) $M$ as follows:

Given a smooth $4 n$-dimensional manifold $M$ and a collection of natural numbers

$k_1, k_2, \ldots , k_m$ such that $k_1+k_2+\cdots +k_m =n$,

the Pontryagin number $P_{k_1,k_2,\dots,k_m}$ is defined by

$$P_{k_1,k_2,\dots, k_m}=p_{k_1}\smile p_{k_2}\smile \cdots\smile p_{k_m}([M])$$

where $p_k$ denotes the $k$-th Pontryagin class and $[M]$ the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of $M$.

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

1. Pontryagin numbers are oriented [cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) invariant; and together with [Stiefel-Whitney numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiefel-Whitney_number) they determine an oriented manifold's oriented cobordism class.
1. Pontryagin numbers of closed [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) (as well as Pontryagin classes) can be calculated as integrals of certain polynomials from the curvature tensor of a Riemannian manifold.
1. Invariants such as [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) and [$\hat A$-genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Â_genus) can be expressed through Pontryagin numbers. For the theorem describing the linear combination of Pontryagin numbers giving the signature see [Hirzebruch signature theorem](wikipedia://en/7192444).

:::
