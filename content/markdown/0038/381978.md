# Kiyoshi Oka

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kiyoshi Oka"}

**Kiyoshi Oka** was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who did fundamental work in the theory of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Oka was born in [Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka). He went to [Kyoto Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Imperial_University) in 1919, turning to mathematics in 1923 and graduating in 1924.

He was in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) for three years from 1929, returning to [Hiroshima University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_University). He published solutions to the first and second [Cousin problems](wikipedia://en/622966), and work on [domains of holomorphy](wikipedia://en/3236377), in the period 1936–1940. He received his [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) degree from Kyoto Imperial University in 1940. These were later taken up by [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) and his school, playing a basic role in the development of [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) theory.

The [Oka–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/66786706) is due to a work of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) in 1935 and Oka's work in 1937.[^o4]

Oka continued to work in the field, and proved [Oka's coherence theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oka's_coherence_theorem) in 1950. [Oka's lemma](wikipedia://en/34637995) is also named after him.

He was a professor at [Nara Women's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Women's_University) from 1949 to retirement at 1964. He received many honours in Japan.
