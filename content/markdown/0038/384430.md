# W. V. D. Hodge

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="W. V. D. Hodge"}

**Sir William Vallance Douglas Hodge** FRS[^o4] (; 17 June 1903 – 7 July 1975) was a British mathematician, specialising in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

His discovery of far-reaching [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) relations between [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625)—an area now called [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) and pertaining more generally to [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538)—has been a major influence on subsequent work in geometry.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hodge was born in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) in 1903, the younger son and second of three children of Jane (born 1875) and Archibald James Hodge (1869–1938) His father was a searcher of records in the property market and a partner in the firm of Douglas and Company and his mother was the daughter of a confectionery business owner William Vallance.[^o8][^o9][^o10] They lived at 1 Church Hill Place in the [Morningside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningside,_Edinburgh) district.[^o11]

*Image: 1 Church Hill Place, Edinburgh.jpg.* Hodge's childhood home at 1 Church Hill Place in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh)

He attended [George Watson's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watson's_College) and then the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), where he graduated with an MA degree in 1923. With help from [E. T. Whittaker](wikipedia://en/1077330), whose son [J. M. Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._M._Whittaker) was a college friend, he then enrolled as an affiliated student at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), in order to study the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos). At Cambridge, he was influenced by the geometer [H. F. Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._F._Baker). He was awarded a BA degree from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1925, an MA degree in 1930, and a Doctor of Science (ScD) degree in 1950.[^o12]

## Career {#Career}

In 1926, he took up a teaching position at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), and began work on the interface between the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424), particularly problems posed by [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716), and the topological methods of [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384). This made his reputation, but led to some initial scepticism on the part of Lefschetz. According to [Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698)'s memoir, Lefschetz and Hodge in 1931 had a meeting in [Max Newman](wikipedia://en/19867)'s rooms in Cambridge, to try to resolve issues. In the end Lefschetz was convinced.[^o4] In 1928 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were Sir [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [Ralph Allan Sampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Allan_Sampson), [Charles Glover Barkla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Glover_Barkla), and Sir [Charles Galton Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Galton_Darwin). He was awarded the Society's [Gunning Victoria Jubilee Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunning_Victoria_Jubilee_Prize) for the period 1964 to 1968.[^o14]

In 1930, Hodge was awarded a Research Fellowship at St John's College, Cambridge. He spent the year 1931–2 at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where Lefschetz was, visiting also [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). At this time he was also assimilating [de Rham's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham's_theorem), and defining the [Hodge star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_star) operation. It would allow him to define [harmonic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_form) and so refine the de Rham theory.

On his return to Cambridge, he was offered a University Lecturer position in 1933. He became the [Lowndean Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowndean_Professor) of Astronomy and Geometry at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), a position he held from 1936 to 1970. He was the first head of [DPMMS](wikipedia://en/3254271).

He was the Master of [Pembroke College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_College,_Cambridge) from 1958 to 1970, and vice-president of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) from 1959 to 1965. He was knighted in 1959. Amongst other honours, he received the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110) in 1937 and the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal) of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1974.

## Death {#Death}

He died in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) on 7 July 1975.

## Work {#Work}

The [Hodge index theorem](wikipedia://en/1242892) was a result on the [intersection number](wikipedia://en/384327) theory for curves on an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311): it determines the [signature](wikipedia://en/463225) of the corresponding [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478). This result was sought by the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424), but was proved by the topological methods of [Lefschetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz).

*The Theory and Applications of Harmonic Integrals*[^o17] summed up Hodge's development during the 1930s of his general theory. This starts with the existence for any [Kähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric) of a theory of [Laplacians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) – it applies to an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* (assumed [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers), [projective](wikipedia://en/320469) and [non-singular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsingular_variety)) because [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) itself carries such a metric. In [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) terms, a cohomology class of degree *k* is represented by a [*k*-form](wikipedia://en/221519) *α* on *V*(**C**). There is no unique representative; but by introducing the idea of *harmonic form* (Hodge still called them 'integrals'), which are solutions of [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941), one can get unique *α*. This has the important, immediate consequence of splitting up

*H*<sup>*k*</sup>(*V*(**C**), **C**)

into subspaces

*H*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup>

according to the number *p* of [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) differentials *dz<sup>i</sup>* wedged to make up *α* (the cotangent space being spanned by the *dz<sup>i</sup>* and their complex conjugates). The dimensions of the subspaces are the [Hodge numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_number).

This *Hodge decomposition* has become a fundamental tool. Not only do the dimensions h<sup>*p*,*q*</sup> refine the [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262), by breaking them into parts with identifiable geometric meaning; but the decomposition itself, as a varying 'flag' in a complex vector space, has a meaning in relation with [moduli problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_problem). In broad terms, Hodge theory contributes both to the discrete and the continuous classification of algebraic varieties.

Further developments by others led in particular to an idea of [mixed Hodge structure](wikipedia://en/64743403) on singular varieties, and to deep analogies with [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231).

## Hodge conjecture {#Hodge_conjecture}

The [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518) on the 'middle' spaces H<sup>*p*,*p*</sup> is still unsolved, in general. It is one of the seven [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234) set up by the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655).

:::

## Exposition {#Exposition}

Hodge also wrote, with [Daniel Pedoe](wikipedia://en/2073714), a three-volume work *Methods of Algebraic Geometry*, on classical algebraic geometry, with much concrete content – illustrating though what [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) called 'the debauch of indices' in its component notation. According to [Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), this was intended to update and replace [H. F. Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._F._Baker)'s *Principles of Geometry*.

## Family {#Family}

In 1929 he married Kathleen Anne Cameron.[^o22]
