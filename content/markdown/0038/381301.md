# Function field (scheme theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Function field"}

The **sheaf of rational functions** *K<sub>X</sub>* of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* is the generalization to [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) of the notion of [function field of an algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/2783843) in classical [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). In the case of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties), such a sheaf associates to each open set *U* the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of all [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) on that open set; in other words, *K<sub>X</sub>*(*U*) is the set of fractions of [regular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function) on *U*. Despite its name, *K<sub>X</sub>* does not always give a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) for a general scheme *X*.

:::

## Simple cases {#Simple_cases}

In the simplest cases, the definition of *K<sub>X</sub>* is straightforward. If *X* is an ([irreducible](wikipedia://en/2652476)) [affine algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_variety), and if *U* is an open subset of *X*, then *K<sub>X</sub>*(*U*) will be the [fraction field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_field) of the ring of regular functions on *U*. Because *X* is affine, the ring of regular functions on *U* will be a localization of the global sections of *X*, and consequently *K<sub>X</sub>* will be the [constant sheaf](wikipedia://en/2783581) whose value is the fraction field of the global sections of *X*.

If *X* is [integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#integral) but not affine, then any non-empty affine open set will be [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in *X*. This means there is not enough room for a regular function to do anything interesting outside of *U*, and consequently the behavior of the rational functions on *U* should determine the behavior of the rational functions on *X*. In fact, the fraction fields of the rings of regular functions on any affine open set will be the same, so we define, for any *U*, *K<sub>X</sub>*(*U*) to be the common fraction field of any ring of regular functions on any open affine subset of *X*. Alternatively, one can define the function field in this case to be the [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) of the [generic point](wikipedia://en/3043978).

## General case {#General_case}

The trouble starts when *X* is no longer integral. Then it is possible to have [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441) in the ring of regular functions, and consequently the fraction field no longer exists. The naive solution is to replace the fraction field by the [total quotient ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_quotient_ring), that is, to invert every element that is not a zero divisor. Unfortunately, in general, the total quotient ring does not produce a presheaf much less a sheaf. The well-known article of Kleiman, listed in the bibliography, gives such an example.

The correct solution is to proceed as follows:

For each open set *U*, let *S<sub>U</sub>* be the set of all elements in Γ(*U*, *O<sub>X</sub>*) that are not zero divisors in any stalk *O<sub>X,x</sub>*. Let *K<sub>X</sub><sup>pre</sup>* be the presheaf whose sections on *U* are [localizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) *S<sub>U</sub><sup>−1</sup>*Γ(*U*, *O<sub>X</sub>*) and whose restriction maps are induced from the restriction maps of *O<sub>X</sub>* by the universal property of localization. Then *K<sub>X</sub>* is the sheaf associated to the presheaf *K<sub>X</sub><sup>pre</sup>*.

## Further issues {#Further_issues}

Once *K<sub>X</sub>* is defined, it is possible to study properties of *X* which depend only on *K<sub>X</sub>*. This is the subject of [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733).

If *X* is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) over a field *k*, then over each open set *U* we have a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *K<sub>X</sub>*(*U*) of *k*. The dimension of *U* will be equal to the [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) of this field extension. All finite transcendence degree field extensions of *k* correspond to the rational function field of some variety.

In the particular case of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) *C*, that is, dimension 1, it follows that any two non-constant functions *F* and *G* on *C* satisfy a polynomial equation *P*(*F*,*G*) = 0.
