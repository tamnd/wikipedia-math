# Group algebra of a locally compact group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Group algebra of a locally compact group"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **group algebra** is any of various constructions to assign to a [locally compact group](wikipedia://en/1651204) an [operator algebra](wikipedia://en/455987) (or more generally a [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665)), such that representations of the algebra are related to representations of the group. As such, they are similar to the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) associated to a discrete group.

:::

## The algebra *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) of continuous functions with compact support {#The_algebra_Cc(G)_of_continuous_functions_with_compact_support}

If *G* is a [locally compact Hausdorff group](wikipedia://en/1651204), *G* carries an essentially unique left-invariant countably additive [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) *μ* called a [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645). Using the Haar measure, one can define a [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) operation on the space *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) of complex-valued continuous functions on *G* with [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support); *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) can then be given any of various [norms](wikipedia://en/990534) and the [completion](wikipedia://en/549445) will be a group algebra.

To define the convolution operation, let *f* and *g* be two functions in *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*). For *t* in *G*, define

$$[f * g](t) = \int_G f(s) g \left (s^{-1} t \right )\, d \mu(s).$$

The fact that $f * g$ is continuous is immediate from the [dominated convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/351853). Also

$$\operatorname{Support}(f * g) \subseteq \operatorname{Support}(f) \cdot \operatorname{Support}(g)$$

where the dot stands for the product in *G*. *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) also has a natural [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) defined by:

$$f^*(s) = \overline{f(s^{-1})} \, \Delta(s^{-1})$$

where Δ is the [modular function](wikipedia://en/40645#The_modular_function) on *G*. With this involution, it is a [\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/293426).

> **Theorem.** With the norm:
>
> $$\|f\|_1 := \int_G |f(s)| \, d\mu(s),$$
>
> *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) becomes an involutive [normed algebra](wikipedia://en/1991870) with an [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628).

The approximate identity can be indexed on a neighborhood basis of the identity consisting of compact sets. Indeed, if *V* is a compact neighborhood of the identity, let *f<sub>V</sub>* be a non-negative [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) supported in *V* such that

$$\int_V f_{V}(g)\, d \mu(g) =1.$$

Then {*f<sub>V</sub>*}<sub>*V*</sub> is an approximate identity. A group algebra has an identity, as opposed to just an approximate identity, [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the topology on the group is the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology).

Note that for discrete groups, *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) is the same thing as the complex group ring **C**\[*G*\].

The importance of the group algebra is that it captures the [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) theory of *G* as shown in the following

> **Theorem.** Let *G* be a locally compact group. If *U* is a strongly continuous unitary representation of *G* on a Hilbert space *H*, then
>
> $$\pi_U (f) = \int_G f(g) U(g)\, d \mu(g)$$
>
> is a non-degenerate bounded \*-representation of the normed algebra *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*). The map
>
> $$U \mapsto \pi_U$$
>
> is a bijection between the set of strongly continuous unitary representations of *G* and non-degenerate bounded \*-representations of *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*). This bijection respects unitary equivalence and [strong containment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_containment). In particular, $\pi$<sub>*U*</sub> is irreducible if and only if *U* is irreducible.

Non-degeneracy of a representation $\pi$ of *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) on a Hilbert space *H*<sub>$\pi$</sub> means that

$$\left \{\pi(f) \xi : f \in \operatorname{C}_c(G), \xi \in H_\pi \right \}$$

is dense in *H*<sub>$\pi$</sub>.

## The convolution algebra *L*<sup>1</sup>(*G*) {#The_convolution_algebra_L1(G)}

It is a standard theorem of [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) that the completion of *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) in the *L*<sup>1</sup>(*G*) norm is isomorphic to the space [*L*<sup>1</sup>(*G*)](wikipedia://en/45194) of equivalence classes of functions which are integrable with respect to the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645), where, as usual, two functions are regarded as equivalent if and only if they differ only on a set of Haar measure zero.

> **Theorem.** *L*<sup>1</sup>(*G*) is a [Banach \*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_*-algebra) with the convolution product and involution defined above and with the *L*<sup>1</sup> norm. *L*<sup>1</sup>(*G*) also has a bounded approximate identity.

## The group C\*-algebra *C\**(*G*) {#The_group_C*-algebra_C*(G)}

Let **C**\[*G*\] be the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) of a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) *G*.

For a locally compact group *G*, the group [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) *C\**(*G*) of *G* is defined to be the C\*-enveloping algebra of *L*<sup>1</sup>(*G*), i.e. the completion of *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) with respect to the largest C\*-norm:

$$\|f\|_{C^*} := \sup_\pi \|\pi(f)\|,$$

where $\pi$ ranges over all non-degenerate \*-representations of *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) on Hilbert spaces. When *G* is discrete, it follows from the triangle inequality that, for any such $\pi$, one has:

$$\|\pi (f)\| \leq \| f \|_1,$$

hence the norm is well-defined.

It follows from the definition that, when G is a discrete group, *C\**(*G*) has the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245): any \*-homomorphism from **C**\[*G*\] to some **B**(*H*) (the C\*-algebra of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on some [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H*) factors through the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516):

$$\mathbf{C}[G] \hookrightarrow C^*_{\max}(G).$$

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Reduced C*-algebra"}

### The reduced group C\*-algebra *C<sub>r</sub>\**(*G*) {#The_reduced_group_C*-algebra_Cr*(G)}

The reduced group C\*-algebra *C<sub>r</sub>\**(*G*) is the completion of *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) with respect to the norm

$$\|f\|_{C^*_r} := \sup \left \{ \|f*g\|_2: \|g\|_2 = 1 \right \},$$

where

$$\|f\|_2 = \sqrt{\int_G |f|^2 \, d\mu}$$

is the *L*<sup>2</sup> norm. Since the completion of *C<sub>c</sub>*(*G*) with regard to the *L*<sup>2</sup> norm is a Hilbert space, the *C<sub>r</sub>\** norm is the norm of the bounded operator acting on *L*<sup>2</sup>(*G*) by convolution with *f* and thus a C\*-norm.

Equivalently, *C<sub>r</sub>\**(*G*) is the C\*-algebra generated by the image of the left [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011) on *ℓ*<sup>2</sup>(*G*).

In general, *C<sub>r</sub>\**(*G*) is a quotient of *C\**(*G*). The reduced group C\*-algebra is isomorphic to the non-reduced group C\*-algebra defined above if and only if *G* is [amenable](wikipedia://en/657430).

:::

## von Neumann algebras associated to groups {#von_Neumann_algebras_associated_to_groups}

The group von Neumann algebra *W\**(*G*) of *G* is the [enveloping von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/15374396) of *C\**(*G*).

For a discrete group *G*, we can consider the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) ℓ<sup>2</sup>(*G*) for which *G* is an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063). Since *G* operates on ℓ<sup>2</sup>(*G*) by permuting the basis vectors, we can identify the complex group ring **C**\[*G*\] with a subalgebra of the algebra of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on ℓ<sup>2</sup>(*G*). The weak closure of this subalgebra, *NG*, is a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185).

The center of *NG* can be described in terms of those elements of *G* whose [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) is finite. In particular, if the identity element of *G* is the only group element with that property (that is, *G* has the [infinite conjugacy class property](wikipedia://en/973790)), the center of *NG* consists only of complex multiples of the identity.

*NG* is isomorphic to the [hyperfinite type II<sub>1</sub> factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperfinite_type_II-1_factor) if and only if *G* is [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable), [amenable](wikipedia://en/657430), and has the infinite conjugacy class property.
