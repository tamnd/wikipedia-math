# Trivial topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trivial topology"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) with the **trivial topology** is one where the only [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) are the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) and the entire space. Such spaces are commonly called **indiscrete**, **anti-discrete**, **concrete** or **codiscrete**. Intuitively, this has the consequence that all points of the space are "lumped together" and cannot be [distinguished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_distinguishable) by topological means. Every indiscrete space can be viewed as a [pseudometric space](wikipedia://en/68503) in which the [distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) between any two points is zero.

:::

## Details {#Details}

The trivial topology is the topology with the least possible number of [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358), namely the empty set and the entire space, since the definition of a topology requires these two sets to be open. Despite its simplicity, a space *X* with more than one element and the trivial topology lacks a key desirable property: it is not a [T<sub>0</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space).

Other properties of an indiscrete space *X*—many of which are quite unusual—include:

- The only [closed sets](wikipedia://en/47279) are the empty set and *X*.
- The only possible [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) of *X* is {*X*}.
- If *X* has more than one point, then since it is not [T<sub>0</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space), it does not satisfy any of the higher [T axioms](wikipedia://en/40117302) either. In particular, it is not a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637). Not being Hausdorff, *X* is not an [order topology](wikipedia://en/213650), nor is it [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable).
- *X* is, however, [regular](wikipedia://en/53058), [completely regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_regular), [normal](wikipedia://en/48629), and [completely normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_normal_space); all in a rather vacuous way though, since the only closed sets are ∅ and *X*.
- *X* is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and therefore [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact), [Lindelöf](wikipedia://en/310480), and [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact).
- Every [function](wikipedia://en/185427) whose [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) is a topological space and [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) *X* is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29).
- *X* is [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected) and so [connected](wikipedia://en/6233).
- *X* is [second-countable](wikipedia://en/901459), and therefore is [first-countable](wikipedia://en/904940), [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) and [Lindelöf](wikipedia://en/310480).
- All [subspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) of *X* have the trivial topology.
- All [quotient spaces](wikipedia://en/237213) of *X* have the trivial topology
- Arbitrary [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_space) of trivial topological spaces, with either the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910) or [box topology](wikipedia://en/954333), have the trivial topology.
- All [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) in *X* [converge](wikipedia://en/26551602) to every point of *X*. In particular, every sequence has a convergent subsequence (the whole sequence or any other subsequence), thus *X* is [sequentially compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequentially_compact).
- The [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of every set except *X* is empty.
- The [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of every non-empty subset of *X* is *X*. Put another way: every non-empty subset of *X* is [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594), a property that characterizes trivial topological spaces.
  - As a result of this, the closure of every open subset *U* of *X* is either ∅ (if *U* = ∅) or *X* (otherwise). In particular, the closure of every open subset of *X* is again an open set, and therefore *X* is [extremally disconnected](wikipedia://en/5783949).
- If *S* is any subset of *X* with more than one element, then all elements of *X* are [limit points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point) of *S*. If *S* is a [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798), then every point of *X* \\ *S* is still a limit point of *S*.
- *X* is a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550).
- Two topological spaces carrying the trivial topology are [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) they have the same [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174).

In some sense the opposite of the trivial topology is the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology), in which every subset is open.

The trivial topology belongs to a [uniform space](wikipedia://en/32339) in which the whole cartesian product *X* × *X* is the only [entourage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entourage_%28topology%29).

Let **Top** be the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) with continuous maps and **Set** be the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) with functions. If *G* : **Top** → **Set** is the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) that assigns to each topological space its underlying set (the so-called [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783)), and *H* : **Set** → **Top** is the functor that puts the trivial topology on a given set, then *H* (the so-called [cofree functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofree_functor)) is [right adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991) to *G*. (The so-called [free functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_functor) *F* : **Set** → **Top** that puts the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology) on a given set is [left adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991) to *G*.)[^o3][^o4]
