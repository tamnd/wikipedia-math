# Adrian Mathias

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Adrian Mathias"}

**Adrian Richard David Mathias** (born 12 February 1944) is a British mathematician working in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553). The [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) notion [Mathias forcing](wikipedia://en/15550368#Mathias_forcing) is named for him.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Mathias was educated at [Shrewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_School) and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he read mathematics and graduated in 1965. After graduation, he moved to [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) where he studied with [Ronald Jensen](wikipedia://en/3785996), visiting [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin), and [Monash University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University) during that period.

In 1969, he returned to Cambridge as a research fellow at [Peterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse) and was admitted to the Ph.D. at Cambridge University in 1970. From 1969 to 1990, Mathias was a fellow of Peterhouse; during this period, he was the editor of the [Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society](wikipedia://en/24334026) from 1972 to 1974, spent one academic year (1978/79) as *Hochschulassistent* to Jensen in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) and another year (1989/90) at the [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California). After leaving Peterhouse in 1990, Mathias had visiting positions in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), at the [Mathematisches Forschungsinstitut Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematisches_Forschungsinstitut_Oberwolfach), at the [CRM](wikipedia://en/21193525) in [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona), and in [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_de_los_Andes_%28Colombia%29), before becoming Professor at the [Université de la Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_la_Réunion). He retired from his professorship in 2012 and was admitted to the higher degree of [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 2015.[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

Mathias became mathematically active soon after the introduction of [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) by [Paul Cohen](wikipedia://en/22994), and Kanamori[^o6] credits his survey of forcing that was eventually published as *Surrealist landscape with figures*[^o7] as being a "vital source" on forcing in its early days.

His paper *Happy families*,[^o8] extending his 1968 Cambridge thesis, proves important properties of the forcing now known as [Mathias forcing](wikipedia://en/15550368#Mathias_forcing). In the same paper he shows that no (infinite) [maximal almost disjoint family](wikipedia://en/448267) can be [analytic](wikipedia://en/634261).

Mathias also used forcing to separate two weak forms of the [Axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), showing that the *ordering principle*, which states that any set can be [linearly ordered](wikipedia://en/30330), does not imply the [Boolean Prime Ideal Theorem](wikipedia://en/314919).[^o9]

His more recent work on forcing includes the study of the theory PROVI of *provident sets*, a minimalist axiom system that still allows the forcing construction to proceed.[^o10]

Mathias is also known for his writings around sociological aspects of logic. These include *The ignorance of Bourbaki* and *Hilbert, Bourbaki and the scorning of logic*, in which Mathias criticises [Bourbaki's](wikipedia://en/167394) approach to logic; in *A Term of Length 4,523,659,424,929* he shows that the number in the title is the number of symbols required for Bourbaki's definition of the number [1](wikipedia://en/77591639). Mathias has also considered claims that standard [ZFC](wikipedia://en/152214) is stronger than necessary for "mainstream" mathematics; his paper *What is Mac Lane missing?* on this topic appeared alongside [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574)'s response *Is Mathias an ontologist?*. Mathias also conducted a detailed study of the strength of a weakened system suggested by Mac Lane.[^o11]
