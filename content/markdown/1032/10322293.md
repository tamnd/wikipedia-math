# David Abrahams (mathematician)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="David Abrahams"}

**Ian David Abrahams** (born 15 January 1958) is an English mathematician and held the [Beyer Professor of Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyer_Chair_of_Applied_Mathematics) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester), 2008–2016. From 2014 to 2016 he was Director of the International Centre for Mathematical Sciences in Edinburgh and in October 2016 he succeeded [John Toland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Toland_%28mathematician%29) as Director of the [Isaac Newton Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/736837), and [N M Rothschild and Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_M_Rothschild_and_Sons) Professor of Mathematics, in Cambridge. He was President 2007–2009,[^o4] of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139).[^o5] In 2017 he was awarded the [IMA](wikipedia://en/697139)/[LMS](wikipedia://en/591097) [David Crighton Medal](wikipedia://en/61378347) for services to mathematics.[^o6] He was awarded a CBE in the 2026 King's Birthday Honours for services to the mathematical sciences.[^o7]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Born in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester), Abrahams was the son of Harry Abrahams and of Leila Abrahams.[^o5]

He completed his [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in [aeronautical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_engineering) in 1979 and PhD (and [DIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploma_of_Imperial_College)) in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in 1982, both at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London). There he won two scholarships and the Finsbury Medal for top undergraduate. For his PhD he was supervised by Frank Leppington for a thesis entitled *The scattering of sound by finite thin elastic plates and cavities*.[^o9]

In the same year, he moved to Manchester on a 1-year contract. This was the beginning of a collaboration with [Gerald Russell Wickham](wikipedia://en/83622302). First, they developed some general techniques for solving matrix [Wiener–Hopf](wikipedia://en/4363034) problems and this gave the solution to a basic problem of [diffraction](wikipedia://en/8603) theory, namely, scattering by two parallel, semi-infinite, staggered plates. Motivated by the problems of [austenitic steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austenite) [welds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welding), they went on to develop a theory for wave propagation in certain inhomogeneous anisotropic solids. They also gave asymptotic solutions for scattering by small defects in an elastic half-space making use of a certain expansion of the half-space [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001).

More recently Abrahams has found aspects of the [Wiener-Hopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener-Hopf) technique that impinge on [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics) and [probability](wikipedia://en/23542). This has led to developments, for example in relating Wiener-Hopf factorisation to [Spitzer's identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitzer's_identity) and other important results within probability theory.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 2004, Abrahams married Penelope Lawrence Warwick with whom he has one daughter and two step sons.[^o5]

Abrahams's leisure interests include motorcycling and he owns a 1977 [Triumph Bonneville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumph_Bonneville) T140V, as well as a 1000cc [Moto Guzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moto_Guzzi) .
