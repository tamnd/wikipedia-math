# Hyperhomology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperhomology"}

In [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), the **hyperhomology** or **hypercohomology** ($\mathbb{H}_*(-), \mathbb{H}^*(-)$) is a generalization of (co)homology functors which takes as input not objects in an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) $\mathcal{A}$ but instead [chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404) of objects, so objects in $\text{Ch}(\mathcal{A})$. It is a sort of cross between the derived functor cohomology of an object and the homology of a chain complex since hypercohomology corresponds to the derived [global sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_sections_functor) functor $\mathbf{R}^*\Gamma(-)$.

:::

Hyperhomology is no longer used much: since about 1970 it has been largely replaced by the roughly equivalent concept of a [derived functor](wikipedia://en/433875) between [derived categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_categories).

## Motivation {#Motivation}

One of the motivations for hypercohomology comes from the fact that there is no obvious generalization of cohomological long exact sequences associated to short exact sequences

> $$0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$$

i.e. there is an associated long [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)

> $$0 \to H^0(M') \to H^0(M) \to H^0(M'')\to H^1(M') \to \cdots$$

It turns out that hypercohomology gives techniques for constructing a similar cohomological associated long exact sequence from an arbitrary long exact sequence since its inputs are given by chain complexes instead of just objects from an abelian category.

> $$0 \to M_1 \to M_2\to \cdots \to M_k \to 0$$

We can turn this chain complex into a distinguished triangle (using the language of [triangulated categories](wikipedia://en/1679700) on a derived category)

> $$M_1 \to [M_2 \to \cdots \to M_{k-1}] \to M_k[-k+3] \xrightarrow{+1}$$

which we denote by

> $$\mathcal{M}'_\bullet \to \mathcal{M}_\bullet \to \mathcal{M}''_\bullet \xrightarrow{+1}$$

Then, taking derived global sections $\mathbf{R}^*\Gamma(-)$ gives a long exact sequence, which is a long exact sequence of hypercohomology groups.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

We give the definition for hypercohomology as this is more common. As usual, hypercohomology and hyperhomology are essentially the same: one converts from one to the other by dualizing, i.e. by changing the direction of all arrows, replacing injective objects with [projective](wikipedia://en/3017382) ones, and so on.

Suppose that *A* is an abelian category with [enough injectives](wikipedia://en/473779#Enough_injectives) and *F* a [left exact functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_exact_functor) to another abelian category *B*. If *C* is a complex of objects of *A* bounded on the left, the **hypercohomology**

**H**<sup>*i*</sup>(*C*)

of *C* (for an integer *i*) is calculated as follows:

1. Take a [quasi-isomorphism](wikipedia://en/3653039) *Φ* : *C* → *I*, here *I* is a complex of injective elements of *A*.
1. The hypercohomology **H**<sup>*i*</sup>(*C*) of *C* is then the cohomology *H*<sup>*i*</sup>(*F*(*I*)) of the complex *F*(*I*).

The hypercohomology of *C* is independent of the choice of the quasi-isomorphism, up to unique isomorphisms.

The hypercohomology can also be defined using [derived categories](wikipedia://en/985897): the hypercohomology of *C* is just the cohomology of *RF*(*C*) considered as an element of the derived category of *B*.

For complexes that vanish for negative indices, the hypercohomology can be defined as the derived functors of **H**<sup>0</sup> = *FH*<sup>0</sup> = *H*<sup>0</sup>*F*.

:::

## The hypercohomology spectral sequences {#The_hypercohomology_spectral_sequences}

There are two hypercohomology [spectral sequences](wikipedia://en/803894); one with *E*<sub>2</sub> term

$$R^iF(H^j(C))$$

and the other with *E*<sub>1</sub> term

$$R^jF(C^i)$$

and *E*<sub>2</sub> term

$$H^i(R^jF(C))$$

both converging to the hypercohomology

$$H^{i+j}(RF(C)),$$

where *R*<sup>*j*</sup>*F* is a [right derived functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_derived_functor) of *F*.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

### Applications {#Applications}

One application of hypercohomology spectral sequences are in the study of [gerbes](wikipedia://en/822043). Recall that rank n vector bundles on a space $X$ can be classified as the [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614) group $H^1(X,\underline{GL}_n)$. The main idea behind gerbes is to extend this idea cohomologically, so instead of taking $H^1(X,\textbf{R}^0F)$ for some functor $F$, we instead consider the cohomology group $H^1(X,\textbf{R}^1F)$, so it classifies objects which are glued by objects in the original classifying group. A closely related subject which studies gerbes and hypercohomology is [Deligne cohomology](wikipedia://en/31615715).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

For a [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *X* over a field *k*, the second spectral sequence from above gives the [Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/43662331) for [algebraic de Rham cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_de_Rham_cohomology): $E_1^{p,q}=H^q(X,\Omega_X^p)\Rightarrow \mathbf{H}^{p+q}(X,\Omega_X^{\bullet})=:H_{DR}^{p+q}(X/k)$.; Another example comes from the [holomorphic log complex](wikipedia://en/1052096) on a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742).[^o11] Let *X* be a complex [algebraic manifold](wikipedia://en/4499397) and $j: X\hookrightarrow Y$ a good compactification. This means that *Y* is a compact algebraic manifold and $D = Y-X$ is a divisor on $Y$ with [simple normal crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_normal_crossing). The natural inclusion of complexes of sheaves $\Omega^{\bullet}_Y(\log D)\rightarrow j_*\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}$ turns out to be a quasi-isomorphism and induces an isomorphism $H^k(X;\mathbb{C})\rightarrow \mathbf{H}^k(Y, \Omega^{\bullet}_Y(\log D))$.

:::
