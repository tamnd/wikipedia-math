# Differential equations of addition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential equations of addition"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **differential equations of addition** (DEA) are one of the most basic equations related to [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536) that mix additions over two different groups (e.g. addition modulo 2<sup>32</sup> and addition over GF(2)) and where input and output differences are expressed as XORs.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

**Differential equations of addition** (DEA) are of the following form:

$$(x+y)\oplus((x\oplus a)+(y\oplus b))=c$$

where $x$ and $y$ are $n$-bit **unknown** variables and $a$, $b$ and $c$ are **known** variables. The symbols $+$ and $\oplus$ denote *addition modulo* $2^n$ and *bitwise exclusive-or* respectively. The above equation is denoted by $(a, b, c)$.

Let a set

$$S=\{(a_i, b_i, c_i)|i < k\}$$

for integer $i$ denote a system of $k(n)$ **DEA** where $k(n)$ is a polynomial in $n$. It has been proved that the satisfiability of an arbitrary set of DEA is in the [complexity class P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity_class_P) when a brute force search requires an [exponential time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_time).

In 2013, some properties of a special form of DEA were reported by Chengqing Li et al., where $a=0$ and $y$ is assumed known. Essentially, the special DEA can be represented as $(x \dotplus \alpha) \oplus (x\dotplus \beta)=c$. Based on the found properties, an algorithm for deriving $x$ was proposed and analyzed.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Solution to an arbitrary set of DEA (either in batch and or in adaptive query model) was due to [Souradyuti Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souradyuti_Paul) and [Bart Preneel](wikipedia://en/1520490). The solution techniques have been used to attack the stream cipher [Helix](wikipedia://en/883596).

:::
