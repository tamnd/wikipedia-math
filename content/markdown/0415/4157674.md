# Great snub dodecicosidodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great snub dodecicosidodecahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great snub dodecicosidodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great snub dodecicosidodecahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great snub dodecicosidodecahedron** (or **great snub dodekicosidodecahedron**) is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron), indexed as U<sub>64</sub>. It has 104 faces (80 [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) and 24 [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544)), 180 edges, and 60 vertices.[^o2] It has [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) . It has the unusual feature that its 24 pentagram faces occur in 12 [coplanar](wikipedia://en/1508434) pairs.

:::

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

Let the point $p$ be given by

$$p= \begin{pmatrix} \phi^{-\frac12} \\ 0 \\ \phi^{-1} \end{pmatrix},$$

where $\phi$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). Let the matrix $M$ be given by

$$M= \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & -\phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) \\ \phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) & -1/2 \\ 1/(2\phi) & 1/2 & \phi/2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$M$ is the rotation around the axis $(1, 0, \phi)$ by an angle of $2\pi/5$, counterclockwise. Let the linear transformations $T_0, \ldots, T_{11}$ be the transformations which send a point $(x, y, z)$ to the [even permutations](wikipedia://en/49727) of $(\pm x, \pm y, \pm z)$ with an even number of minus signs. The transformations $T_i$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521). The transformations $T_i M^j$ $(i = 0,\ldots, 11$, $j = 0,\ldots, 4)$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). Then the 60 points $T_i M^j p$ are the vertices of a great snub dodecicosidodecahedron. The edge length equals $\sqrt2$, the circumradius equals $1$, and the midradius equals $\frac12\sqrt2$.

For a great snub dodecicosidodecahedron whose edge length is 1, the circumradius is

$$R = \frac12\sqrt2.$$

Its midradius is

$$r=\frac12.$$

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

It shares its vertices and edges, as well as 20 of its triangular faces and all its pentagrammic faces, with the [great dirhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2910030), (although the latter has 60 edges not contained in the great snub dodecicosidodecahedron). It shares its other 60 triangular faces (and its pentagrammic faces again) with the [great disnub dirhombidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4145225), alongside all its vertices as well.

The convex hull is a non-uniform [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486) with rectangles of length ratio 1:$\sqrt\phi$ (which can be decomposed into two [Kepler triangles](wikipedia://en/13938136) joined at the hypotenuse).

The edges and triangular faces also occur in the [compound of twenty octahedra](wikipedia://en/14737027). In addition, 20 of the triangular faces occur in one [enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer) of the [compound of twenty tetrahemihexahedra](wikipedia://en/14736821), and the other 60 triangular faces occur in the other enantiomer.
