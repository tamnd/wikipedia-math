# Cubohemioctahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cubohemioctahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Cubohemioctahedron.stl.* 3D model of a cubohemioctahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **cubohemioctahedron** is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron), indexed as U<sub>15</sub>. It has 10 faces (6 [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) and 4 regular [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733)), 24 edges and 12 vertices.[^o2] Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is a [crossed quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/5817627).

:::

It is given [Wythoff symbol](wikipedia://en/3691548) **$\tfrac{4}{3}$ 4 | 3**, although that is a double-covering of this figure.

A nonconvex polyhedron has intersecting faces which do not represent new edges or faces. In the picture vertices are marked by golden spheres, and edges by silver cylinders.

It is a [hemipolyhedron](wikipedia://en/24084383) with 4 [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) faces passing through the model center. The hexagons intersect each other and so only triangular portions of each are visible.

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

It shares the [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) and [edge arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_arrangement) with the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) (having the square faces in common), and with the [octahemioctahedron](wikipedia://en/4157168) (having the hexagonal faces in common).

### Hexahemioctacron {#Hexahemioctacron}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table The **hexahemioctacron** is the dual of the cubohemioctahedron, and is one of nine [dual hemipolyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_hemipolyhedra). It appears visually indistinct from the [octahemioctacron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahemioctacron).

Since the cubohemioctahedron has four hexagonal [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) passing through the model center, thus it is [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952), and can be seen as having four [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at infinity.

In [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685)'s *Dual Models*, they are represented with intersecting infinite [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) passing through the model center, cut off at a certain point that is convenient for the maker.
