# Great snub icosidodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great snub icosidodecahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great snub icosidodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great snub icosidodecahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great snub icosidodecahedron** is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron), indexed as U<sub>57</sub>. It has 92 faces (80 [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) and 12 [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544)), 150 edges, and 60 vertices.[^o2] It can be represented by a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) sr{$\tfrac{5}{2}$,3}, and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) .

:::

This polyhedron is the [snub](wikipedia://en/9428917) member of a family that includes the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898), the [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878) and the [great icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157391).

In the book *[Polyhedron Models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedron_Models)* by [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685), the polyhedron is misnamed *[great inverted snub icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157679)*, and vice versa.

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

Let $\xi\approx 0.3990206456527105$ be the positive zero of the polynomial $x^3+2x^2-\phi^{-2}$, where $\phi$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). Let the point $p$ be given by

$$p= \begin{pmatrix} \xi \\ \phi^{-2}-\phi^{-2}\xi \\ -\phi^{-3}+\phi^{-1}\xi+2\phi^{-1}\xi^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Let the matrix $M$ be given by

$$M= \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & -\phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) \\ \phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) & -1/2 \\ 1/(2\phi) & 1/2 & \phi/2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$M$ is the rotation around the axis $(1, 0, \phi)$ by an angle of $2\pi/5$, counterclockwise. Let the linear transformations $T_0, \ldots, T_{11}$ be the transformations which send a point $(x, y, z)$ to the [even permutations](wikipedia://en/49727) of $(\pm x, \pm y, \pm z)$ with an even number of minus signs. The transformations $T_i$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521). The transformations $T_i M^j$ $(i = 0,\ldots, 11$, $j = 0,\ldots, 4)$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). Then the 60 points $T_i M^j p$ are the vertices of a great snub icosahedron. The edge length equals $2\xi\sqrt{1-\xi}$, the circumradius equals $\xi\sqrt{2-\xi}$, and the midradius equals $\xi$.

For a great snub icosidodecahedron whose edge length is 1, the circumradius is

$$R = \frac12\sqrt{\frac{2-\xi}{1-\xi}} \approx 0.8160806747999234$$

Its midradius is

$$r=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\xi}} \approx 0.6449710596467862$$

The four positive real roots of the [sextic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextic) in $R^{2}$,

$$4096R^{12} - 27648R^{10} + 47104R^8 - 35776R^6 + 13872R^4 - 2696R^2 + 209 = 0$$

are, in order, the circumradii of the [great retrosnub icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157697) (U<sub>74</sub>), great snub icosidodecahedron (U<sub>57</sub>), [great inverted snub icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157679) (U<sub>69</sub>) and [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137) (U<sub>29</sub>).

### Great pentagonal hexecontahedron {#Great_pentagonal_hexecontahedron}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great pentagonal hexecontahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great pentagonal hexecontahedron

The **great pentagonal hexecontahedron** (or **great petaloid ditriacontahedron**) is a nonconvex [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) and [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) to the uniform *great snub icosidodecahedron*. It has 60 intersecting irregular pentagonal faces, 120 edges, and 92 vertices.

:::

## Proportions {#Proportions}

Denote the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) by $\phi$. Let $\xi\approx -0.199\,510\,322\,83$ be the negative zero of the polynomial $P = 8x^3-8x^2+\phi^{-2}$. Then each pentagonal face has four equal angles of $\arccos(\xi)\approx 101.508\,325\,512\,64^{\circ}$ and one angle of $\arccos(-\phi^{-1}+\phi^{-2}\xi)\approx 133.966\,697\,949\,42^{\circ}$. Each face has three long and two short edges. The ratio $l$ between the lengths of the long and the short edges is given by

$$l = \frac{2-4\xi^2}{1-2\xi}\approx 1.315\,765\,089\,00.$$

The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) equals $\arccos(\xi/(\xi+1))\approx 104.432\,268\,611\,86^{\circ}$. Part of each face lies inside the solid, hence is invisible in solid models. The other two zeroes of the polynomial $P$ play a similar role in the description of the [great inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_inverted_pentagonal_hexecontahedron) and the [great pentagrammic hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/24368316).
