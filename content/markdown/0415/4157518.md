# Truncated dodecadodecahedron

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Truncated dodecadodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a truncated dodecadodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated dodecadodecahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated dodecadodecahedron** (or **stellatruncated dodecadodecahedron**) is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron), indexed as U<sub>59</sub>. It is given a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $t_{0,1,2}\{\tfrac{5}{3},5\}.$ It has 54 faces (30 [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), 12 [decagons](wikipedia://en/327393), and 12 [decagrams](wikipedia://en/19546742)), 180 edges, and 120 vertices.[^o2] The central region of the polyhedron is connected to the exterior via 20 small triangular holes.

:::

The name *truncated dodecadodecahedron* is somewhat misleading: truncation of the [dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157379) would produce rectangular faces rather than squares, and the pentagram faces of the dodecadodecahedron would turn into truncated pentagrams rather than decagrams. However, it is the quasitruncation of the dodecadodecahedron, as defined by (Coxeter Longuet-Higgins Miller 1954).[^o4] For this reason, it is also known as the **quasitruncated dodecadodecahedron**.[^o5] Coxeter et al. credit its discovery to a paper published in 1881 by Austrian mathematician Johann Pitsch.[^o6]

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

[Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of a truncated dodecadodecahedron are all the triples of numbers obtained by circular shifts and sign changes from the following points (where $\varphi = \tfrac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)):

$$\begin{array}{lcr} \Bigl(1,& 1,& 3\Bigr), \\ \Bigl(\frac{1}{\varphi},& \frac{1}{\varphi^2},& 2\varphi\Bigr), \\ \Bigl(\varphi,& \frac{2}{\varphi},& \varphi^2\Bigr), \\ \Bigl(\varphi^2,& \frac{1}{\varphi^2},& 2\Bigr), \\ \Bigl(\sqrt{5},& 1,& \sqrt{5}\Bigr). \end{array}$$

Each of these five points has eight possible sign patterns and three possible circular shifts, giving a total of 120 different points.

## As a Cayley graph {#As_a_Cayley_graph}

The truncated dodecadodecahedron forms a [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) for the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on five elements, as generated by two group members: one that swaps the first two elements of a five-tuple, and one that performs a [circular shift](wikipedia://en/1917276) operation on the last four elements. That is, the 120 vertices of the polyhedron may be placed in one-to-one correspondence with the 5! [permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutations) on five elements, in such a way that the three neighbors of each vertex are the three permutations formed from it by swapping the first two elements or circularly shifting (in either direction) the last four elements.[^o9]

### Medial disdyakis triacontahedron {#Medial_disdyakis_triacontahedron}

The [medial disdyakis triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/24369094) is a nonconvex [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). It is the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of the [uniform](wikipedia://en/8464397) truncated dodecadodecahedron.
