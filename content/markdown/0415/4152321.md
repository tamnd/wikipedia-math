# Reliability theory of aging and longevity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reliability theory of aging and longevity"}

The **reliability theory of aging** is an attempt to apply the principles of [reliability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliability_theory) to create a [mathematical model](wikipedia://en/20590) of [senescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senescence).[^o2] The theory was published in Russian by Leonid A. Gavrilov and Natalia S. Gavrilova as *Biologiia prodolzhitelʹnosti zhizni* in 1986, and in English translation as *The Biology of Life Span: A Quantitative Approach* in 1991.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

One of the models suggested in the book is based on an analogy with the reliability theory. The underlying [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) is based on the previously suggested premise that humans are born in a highly defective state. This is then made worse by environmental and mutational damage; exceptionally high [redundancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redundancy_%28engineering%29) due to the extremely high number of low-reliable components (e.g., [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28biology%29)) allows the organism to survive for a while.[^o7][^o8]

The theory suggests an explanation of two aging phenomena for higher organisms: the [Gompertz law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gompertz_law) of exponential increase in mortality rates with age and the "late-life mortality plateau" (mortality deceleration compared to the Gompertz law at higher ages).[^o8]

The book criticizes a number of hypotheses known at the time, discusses drawbacks of the hypotheses put forth by the authors themselves, and concludes that regardless of the suggested mathematical models, the underlying biological mechanisms remain unknown.[^o9][^o10]
