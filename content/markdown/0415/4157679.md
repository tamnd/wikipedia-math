# Great inverted snub icosidodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great inverted snub icosidodecahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great inverted snub icosidodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great inverted snub icosidodecahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great inverted snub icosidodecahedron** (or **great vertisnub icosidodecahedron**) is a [uniform star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8464397), indexed as U<sub>69</sub>. It is given a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\operatorname{sr}\{\tfrac{5}{3},3\},$ and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) . In the book *[Polyhedron Models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedron_Models)* by [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685), the polyhedron is misnamed *[great snub icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157558)*, and vice versa.

:::

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

Let $\xi\approx -0.5055605785332548$ be the largest (least negative) negative zero of the polynomial $x^3+2x^2-\phi^{-2}$, where $\phi$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). Let the point $p$ be given by

$$p= \begin{pmatrix} \xi \\ \phi^{-2}-\phi^{-2}\xi \\ -\phi^{-3}+\phi^{-1}\xi+2\phi^{-1}\xi^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Let the matrix $M$ be given by

$$M= \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & -\phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) \\ \phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) & -1/2 \\ 1/(2\phi) & 1/2 & \phi/2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$M$ is the rotation around the axis $(1, 0, \phi)$ by an angle of $2\pi/5$, counterclockwise. Let the linear transformations $T_0, \ldots, T_{11}$ be the transformations which send a point $(x, y, z)$ to the [even permutations](wikipedia://en/49727) of $(\pm x, \pm y, \pm z)$ with an even number of minus signs. The transformations $T_i$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521). The transformations $T_i M^j$ $(i = 0,\ldots, 11$, $j = 0,\ldots, 4)$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). Then the 60 points $T_i M^j p$ are the vertices of a great snub icosahedron. The edge length equals $-2\xi\sqrt{1-\xi}$, the circumradius equals $-\xi\sqrt{2-\xi}$, and the midradius equals $-\xi$.

For a great snub icosidodecahedron whose edge length is 1, the circumradius is

$$R = \frac12\sqrt{\frac{2-\xi}{1-\xi}} \approx 0.6450202372957795$$

Its midradius is

$$r=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\xi}} \approx 0.4074936889340787$$

The four positive real roots of the [sextic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextic) in $R^{2}$,

$$4096R^{12} - 27648R^{10} + 47104R^8 - 35776R^6 + 13872R^4 - 2696R^2 + 209 = 0$$

are the circumradii of the [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137) (U<sub>29</sub>), [great snub icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157558) (U<sub>57</sub>), great inverted snub icosidodecahedron (U<sub>69</sub>), and [great retrosnub icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157697) (U<sub>74</sub>).

### Great inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron {#Great_inverted_pentagonal_hexecontahedron}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron

The **great inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron** (or **petaloidal trisicosahedron**) is a nonconvex [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). It is composed of 60 [concave](wikipedia://en/605156) pentagonal faces, 150 edges and 92 vertices.

It is the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of the [uniform](wikipedia://en/8464397) great inverted snub icosidodecahedron.

:::

## Proportions {#Proportions}

Denote the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) by $\phi$. Let $\xi\approx 0.252\,780\,289\,27$ be the smallest positive zero of the polynomial $P = 8x^3-8x^2+\phi^{-2}$. Then each pentagonal face has four equal angles of $\arccos(\xi)\approx 75.357\,903\,417\,42^{\circ}$ and one angle of $360^{\circ}-\arccos(-\phi^{-1}+\phi^{-2}\xi)\approx 238.568\,386\,330\,33^{\circ}$. Each face has three long and two short edges. The ratio $l$ between the lengths of the long and the short edges is given by

$$l = \frac{2-4\xi^2}{1-2\xi}\approx 3.528\,053\,034\,81.$$

The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) equals $\arccos(\xi/(\xi+1))\approx 78.359\,199\,060\,62^{\circ}$. Part of each face lies inside the solid, hence is invisible in solid models. The other two zeroes of the polynomial $P$ play a similar role in the description of the [great pentagonal hexecontahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_pentagonal_hexecontahedron) and the [great pentagrammic hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/24368316).
