# Octahemioctahedron

*Image: Octahemioctahedron.stl.* 3D model of an octahemioctahedron

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Octahemioctahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **octahemioctahedron** or **octatetrahedron**[^n1] is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron), indexed as $U_{3}$. It contains twelve faces (eight [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) and four [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733)), twenty-four edges, and twelve vertices. Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is an [antiparallelogram](wikipedia://en/5817627). Since its hexagonal faces pass through its center, it is a [hemipolyhedron](wikipedia://en/24084383).

:::

## Construction and properties {#Construction_and_properties}

An octahemioctahedron can be constructed from four diagonals of a cube that bisect the interior into four hexagons, and the edges form the structure of a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280). The four hexagonal planes form a polyhedral surface when eight triangles are added. Thus, the resulting polyhedron has 12 faces, 24 edges, and 12 vertices. If six squares replace the triangular faces, the resulting polyhedron becomes a [cubohemioctahedron](wikipedia://en/4156879). The octahemioctahedron is a [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223), with the [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) being an [antiparallelogram](wikipedia://en/5817627).

It is the only hemipolyhedron that is [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable), and the only uniform polyhedron with an [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of zero, a topological [torus](wikipedia://en/74800).[^n1]

The octahemioctahedron belongs to a family of concave [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) "of the second sort".

## Octahemioctacron {#Octahemioctacron}

*Image: Hexahemioctacron.png.* The dual of an octahemioctahedron

The dual of the octahemioctahedron is the **octahemioctacron**, with its four [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at infinity. Since the hemipolyhedra have faces passing through the center, the dual figures have corresponding [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at infinity; properly, on the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) at infinity. (Wenninger 2003) stated that they are represented with intersecting [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), each extending in both directions to the same vertex at infinity, in order to maintain symmetry. In practice, the model prisms are cut off at a certain point that is convenient for the maker. Wenninger suggested these figures are members of a new class of [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) figures, called *stellation to infinity*. However, Wenninger also suggested that strictly speaking, they are not polyhedra because their construction does not conform to the usual definitions.
