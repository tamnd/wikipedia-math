# Great dodecahemicosahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great dodecahemicosahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great dodecahemicosahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great dodecahemicosahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great dodecahemicosahedron** (or **small dodecahemiicosahedron**) is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron), indexed as U<sub>65</sub>. It has 22 faces (12 [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014) and 10 [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733)), 60 edges, and 30 vertices.[^o2] Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is a [crossed quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278#More_quadrilaterals).

:::

It is a [hemipolyhedron](wikipedia://en/24084383) with ten hexagonal faces passing through the model center.

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

Its [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) is the [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023). It also shares its [edge arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_arrangement) with the [dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157379) (having the pentagonal faces in common), and with the [small dodecahemicosahedron](wikipedia://en/4157599) (having the hexagonal faces in common).

### Great dodecahemicosacron {#Great_dodecahemicosacron}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table The **great dodecahemicosacron** is the dual of the great dodecahemicosahedron, and is one of nine [dual hemipolyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_hemipolyhedra). It appears visually indistinct from the [small dodecahemicosacron](wikipedia://en/24087323).

Since the hemipolyhedra have [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) passing through the center, the [dual figures](wikipedia://en/8815) have corresponding [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at infinity; properly, on the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) at infinity.[^o8] In [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685)'s *Dual Models*, they are represented with intersecting [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), each extending in both directions to the same vertex at infinity, in order to maintain symmetry. In practice, the model prisms are cut off at a certain point that is convenient for the maker. Wenninger suggested these figures are members of a new class of [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) figures, called *stellation to infinity*. However, he also suggested that strictly speaking, they are not polyhedra because their construction does not conform to the usual definitions.

The great dodecahemicosahedron can be seen as having ten [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at infinity.
