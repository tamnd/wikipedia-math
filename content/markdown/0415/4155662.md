# Smoothing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smoothing"}

*Image: Exemple\_de\_lissage\_exponentiel\_simple.png.* Simple exponential smoothing example. Raw data: mean daily temperatures at the Paris-Montsouris weather station (France) from 1960/01/01 to 1960/02/29. Smoothed data with alpha factor = 0.1.

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing), to **smooth** a [data set](wikipedia://en/8495) is to create an approximating [function](wikipedia://en/185427) that attempts to capture important [patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern) in the data, while leaving out [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise) or other fine-scale structures/rapid phenomena. In smoothing, the data points of a signal are modified so individual points higher than the adjacent points (presumably because of noise) are reduced, and points that are lower than the adjacent points are increased, leading to a smoother signal.

:::

Reducing noise by smoothing may aid in data analysis in two notable ways:

1. Help uncover more meaningful information from the underlying data, such as trends.[^o2]
1. Provide analyses that are both flexible and robust.[^o3]

Many different [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) are used in smoothing, most commonly [binning](wikipedia://en/18614570), [kernels](wikipedia://en/16307234), and [local weighted regression](wikipedia://en/4146592).[^o2]

## Compared to curve fitting {#Compared_to_curve_fitting}

Smoothing may be distinguished from the related and partially overlapping concept of [curve fitting](wikipedia://en/555425) in the following ways:

- curve fitting often involves the use of an explicit function form for the result, whereas the immediate results from smoothing are the "smoothed" values with no later use made of a functional form if there is one;
- the aim of smoothing is to give a general idea of relatively slow changes of value with little attention paid to the close matching of data values, while curve fitting concentrates on achieving as close a match as possible.
- smoothing methods often have an associated tuning parameter which is used to control the extent of smoothing. Curve fitting will adjust any number of parameters of the function to obtain the 'best' fit.

## Linear smoothers {#Linear_smoothers}

In the case that the smoothed values can be written as a [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) of the observed values, the smoothing operation is known as a **linear smoother**; the matrix representing the transformation is known as a **smoother matrix** or [hat matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_matrix).

The operation of applying such a matrix transformation is called [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519). Thus the matrix is also called convolution matrix or a [convolution kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolution_kernel). In the case of simple series of data points (rather than a multi-dimensional image), the convolution kernel is a one-dimensional [vector](wikipedia://en/23218956).

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

One of the most common algorithms is the "[moving average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_average_%28finance%29)", often used to try to capture important trends in repeated [statistical surveys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_survey). In [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing) and [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision), smoothing ideas are used in [scale space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_space) representations. The simplest smoothing algorithm is the "rectangular" or "unweighted sliding-average smooth". This method replaces each point in the signal with the average of "m" adjacent points, where "m" is a positive integer called the "smooth width". Usually m is an odd number. The *triangular smooth* is like the *rectangular smooth* except that it implements a weighted smoothing function.[^o8]
