# Inverted snub dodecadodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inverted snub dodecadodecahedron"}

Uniform polyhedron stat table

*Image: Inverted snub dodecadodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of an inverted snub dodecadodecahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **inverted snub dodecadodecahedron** (or **vertisnub dodecadodecahedron**) is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron), indexed as U<sub>60</sub>.[^o2] It is given a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\operatorname{sr}\{5/3,5\}.$

:::

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

Let $\xi\approx 2.109759446579943$ be the largest real zero of the polynomial $P=2x^4-5x^3+3x+1$. Denote by $\phi$ the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). Let the point $p$ be given by

$$p= \begin{pmatrix} \phi^{-2}\xi^2-\phi^{-2}\xi+\phi^{-1}\\ -\phi^{2}\xi^2+\phi^{2}\xi+\phi\\ \xi^2+\xi \end{pmatrix}.$$

Let the matrix $M$ be given by

$$M= \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & -\phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) \\ \phi/2 & 1/(2\phi) & -1/2 \\ 1/(2\phi) & 1/2 & \phi/2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$M$ is the rotation around the axis $(1, 0, \phi)$ by an angle of $2\pi/5$, counterclockwise. Let the linear transformations $T_0, \ldots, T_{11}$ be the transformations which send a point $(x, y, z)$ to the [even permutations](wikipedia://en/49727) of $(\pm x, \pm y, \pm z)$ with an even number of minus signs. The transformations $T_i$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521). The transformations $T_i M^j$ $(i = 0,\ldots, 11$, $j = 0,\ldots, 4)$ constitute the group of rotational symmetries of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). Then the 60 points $T_i M^j p$ are the vertices of a snub dodecadodecahedron. The edge length equals $2(\xi+1)\sqrt{\xi^2-\xi}$, the circumradius equals $(\xi+1)\sqrt{2\xi^2-\xi}$, and the midradius equals $\xi^2+\xi$.

For a great snub icosidodecahedron whose edge length is 1, the circumradius is

$$R = \frac12\sqrt{\frac{2\xi-1}{\xi-1}} \approx 0.8516302281174128$$

Its midradius is

$$r=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\xi}{\xi-1}} \approx 0.6894012223976083$$

The other real root of P plays a similar role in the description of the [snub dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157575).

### Medial inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron {#Medial_inverted_pentagonal_hexecontahedron}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table

*Image: Medial inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron.stl.* 3D model of a medial inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron

The **medial inverted pentagonal hexecontahedron** (or **midly petaloid ditriacontahedron**) is a nonconvex [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). It is the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of the [uniform](wikipedia://en/8464397) inverted snub dodecadodecahedron. Its faces are irregular nonconvex pentagons, with one very acute angle.

:::

## Proportions {#Proportions}

Denote the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) by $\phi$, and let $\xi\approx -0.236\,993\,843\,45$ be the largest (least negative) real zero of the polynomial $P=8x^4-12x^3+5x+1$. Then each face has three equal angles of $\arccos(\xi)\approx 103.709\,182\,219\,53^{\circ}$, one of $\arccos(\phi^2\xi+\phi)\approx 3.990\,130\,423\,41^{\circ}$ and one of $360^{\circ}-\arccos(\phi^{-2}\xi-\phi^{-1})\approx 224.882\,322\,917\,99^{\circ}$. Each face has one medium length edge, two short and two long ones. If the medium length is $2$, then the short edges have length

$$1-\sqrt{\frac{1-\xi}{\phi^3-\xi}}\approx 0.474\,126\,460\,54,$$

and the long edges have length

$$1+\sqrt{\frac{1-\xi}{\phi^{-3}-\xi}} \approx 37.551\,879\,448\,54.$$

The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) equals $\arccos(\xi/(\xi+1))\approx 108.095\,719\,352\,34^{\circ}$. The other real zero of the polynomial $P$ plays a similar role for the [medial pentagonal hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/24161196).
