# Bozenna Pasik-Duncan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bozenna Pasik-Duncan"}

**Bozenna Janina Pasik-Duncan** (born 1947) is a Polish-American mathematician who works as a professor of mathematics at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas).

:::

## Research {#Research}

Pasik-Duncan's research concerns [stochastic control](wikipedia://en/17058216) and its applications in communications, economics, and health science. She is also interested in [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471), particularly for [women in STEM fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_STEM_fields).[^o4][^o8]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Pasik-Duncan attended high school in [Radom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radom).[^o6] She earned a master's degree in mathematics from the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in 1970.[^o4] She completed a Ph.D. at the [Warsaw School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_School_of_Economics) in 1978, and earned a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) there in 1986.[^o4]

She moved to the University of Kansas mathematics department in 1984,[^o4] joining there her husband Tyrone Duncan (also a University of Kansas mathematician).[^o8]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

She was a recipient of the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE)'s Third Millennium Medal in 2000, and became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the IEEE in 2001.[^o4][^o11] She was the 2004 [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer),[^o10] and the 2004 winner of the [Louise Hay Award](wikipedia://en/51270001) for Contributions to Mathematics Education of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o11] She was named a Fellow of the [International Federation of Automatic Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Automatic_Control) in 2014.[^o12] Pasik-Duncan was selected as a Fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the Class of 2021 "*for her decades of contributions: as a founder and sustainer of the Women in Control Committee of the IEEE Control Systems Society; as the chair of IFAC’s Task Force on Diversity and Inclusion; and via other programs and activities to support and encourage women and girls in mathematics and engineering*".[^o13]
