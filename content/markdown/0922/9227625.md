# Ed Pegg Jr.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ed Pegg Jr."}

**Edward Taylor Pegg Jr.** (born December 7, 1963) is an expert on mathematical [puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle) and is a self-described [recreational](wikipedia://en/26446) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He wrote an online puzzle column called Ed Pegg Jr.'s *Math Games* for the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) during the years 2003–2007. His puzzles have also been used by [Will Shortz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Shortz) on the puzzle segment of [NPR's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Radio) *[Weekend Edition Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekend_Edition)*. He was a fan of [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) and regularly participated in [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner) conferences. In 2009, he teamed up with [Tom M. Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Malin_Rodgers) and [Alan Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Schoen) to edit two Gardner tribute books.[^o3]

:::

Pegg received a master's degree in mathematics from the [University of Colorado at Colorado Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Colorado_Springs),[^o4] writing his thesis on the subject of fair dice.[^o5] In 2000, he left [NORAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORAD) to join [Wolfram Research](wikipedia://en/594109), where he collaborated on *[A New Kind of Science](wikipedia://en/93070)* (NKS). In 2004, he started assisting [Eric W. Weisstein](wikipedia://en/836189) at Wolfram [MathWorld](wikipedia://en/374235).[^o6] He has made contributions to several hundred MathWorld articles.[^o7] He was one of the chief consultants for *[Numb3rs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numb3rs)*.
