# Quadratic growth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quadratic growth"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) or [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) is said to exhibit **quadratic growth** when its values are [proportional](wikipedia://en/81863) to the [square](wikipedia://en/659942) of the function argument or sequence position. "Quadratic growth" often means more generally "quadratic growth in the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602)", as the argument or sequence position goes to infinity – in [big Theta notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Theta_notation), $f(x)=\Theta(x^2)$.[^o2] This can be defined both continuously (for a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued function of a real variable) or discretely (for a sequence of real numbers, i.e., real-valued function of an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) or [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) variable).

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of quadratic growth include:

- Any [quadratic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_polynomial).
- Certain [integer sequences](wikipedia://en/51421) such as the [triangular numbers](wikipedia://en/213915). The $n$th triangular number has value $n(n+1)/2$, approximately $n^2/2$.

For a real function of a real variable, quadratic growth is equivalent to the [second derivative](wikipedia://en/3224219) being constant (i.e., the [third derivative](wikipedia://en/9092737) being zero), and thus functions with quadratic growth are exactly the quadratic polynomials, as these are the [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28linear_operator%29) of the third derivative operator $D^3$. Similarly, for a sequence (a real function of an integer or natural number variable), quadratic growth is equivalent to the second [finite difference](wikipedia://en/39147) being constant (the third finite difference being zero),[^o5] and thus a sequence with quadratic growth is also a quadratic polynomial. Indeed, an integer-valued sequence with quadratic growth is a polynomial in the zeroth, first, and second [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) with integer values. The coefficients can be determined by taking the [Taylor polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_polynomial) (if continuous) or [Newton polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_operator#Newton.27s_series) (if discrete).

Algorithmic examples include:

- The amount of time taken in the worst case by certain [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775), such as [insertion sort](wikipedia://en/15205), as a function of the input length.[^o6]
- The numbers of live cells in space-filling [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342) patterns such as the [breeder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeder_%28cellular_automaton%29), as a function of the number of time steps for which the pattern is simulated.[^o7]
- [Metcalfe's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metcalfe's_law) stating that the value of a communications network grows quadratically as a function of its number of users.[^o8]
