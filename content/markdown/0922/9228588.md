# Florimond de Beaune

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Florimond de Beaune"}

**Florimond de Beaune** (7 October 1601, [Blois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blois) – 18 August 1652, Blois) was a French jurist[^o7] and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and an early follower of [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525).[^o3] R. Taton calls him "a typical example of the erudite amateurs" active in 17th-century science.[^o7]

:::

In a 1638 letter to Descartes, de Beaune posed a problem that can be reformulated in modern notation as solving the [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309)

$$\frac{\operatorname{d}y}{\operatorname{d}x}=\frac{\alpha}{y-x}$$

now seen as the first example of the inverse tangent method of deducing properties of a curve from its [tangents](wikipedia://en/31482).[^o4][^o5]

His *Tractatus de limitibus aequationum* was reprinted in England in 1807;[^o6] in it, he finds [upper and lower bounds](wikipedia://en/42693) for the solutions to [quadratic equations](wikipedia://en/25175) and [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787), as simple functions of the coefficients of these equations.[^o3] His *Doctrine de l'angle solide* and *Inventaire de sa bibliothèque* were also reprinted, in Paris in 1975.[^o7] Another of his writings was *Notae breves*, the introduction to a 1649 edition of Descartes' *[La Géométrie](wikipedia://en/239981)*.[^o8]
