# Nu-transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nu-transform"}

In the theory of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), a **ν-transform** is an operation that transforms a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) or a [point process](wikipedia://en/2147685) into a different point process. Intuitively the ν-transform randomly relocates the points of the point process, with the type of relocation being dependent on the position of each point.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### For measures {#For_measures}

Let $\delta_x$ denote the [Dirac measure](wikipedia://en/1019002) on the point $x$ and let $\mu$ be a simple point measure on $S$. This means that

$$\mu= \sum_k \delta_{s_k}$$

for distinct $s_k \in S$ and $\mu(B)< \infty$ for every bounded set $B$ in $S$. Further, let $\nu$ be a [Markov kernel](wikipedia://en/21180014) from $S$ to $T$.

Let $\tau_k$ be independent random elements with distribution $\nu_{s_k}=\nu(s_k,\cdot)$. Then the point process

$$\zeta = \sum_{k} \delta_{\tau_k}$$

is called the ν-transform of the measure $\mu$ if it is locally finite, meaning that $\zeta(B) < \infty$ for every bounded set $B$[^o11]

### For point processes {#For_point_processes}

For a [point process](wikipedia://en/2147685) $\xi$, a second point process $\zeta$ is called a $\nu$-transform of $\xi$ if, conditional on $\{ \xi=\mu\}$, the point process $\zeta$ is a $\nu$-transform of $\mu$.[^o11]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Stability {#Stability}

If $\zeta$ is a [Cox process](wikipedia://en/6196078) directed by the random measure $\xi$, then the $\nu$-transform of $\zeta$ is again a Cox-process, directed by the random measure $\xi \cdot \nu$ (see [Transition kernel#Composition of kernels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_kernel#Composition_of_kernels))[^o12]

Therefore, the $\nu$-transform of a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) with intensity measure $\mu$ is a [Cox process](wikipedia://en/6196078) directed by a [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846) with distribution $\mu \cdot \nu$.

### Laplace transform {#Laplace_transform}

It $\zeta$ is a $\nu$-transform of $\xi$, then the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of $\zeta$ is given by

$$\mathcal L_{\zeta}(f)= \exp \left( \int \log \left[ \int \exp(-f(t)) \mu_s(\mathrm dt)\right] \xi(\mathrm ds)\right)$$

for all bounded, positive and measurable functions $f$.[^o11]

:::
