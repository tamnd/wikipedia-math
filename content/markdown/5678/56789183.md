# Nikolai Georgievich Makarov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nikolai Georgievich Makarov"}

**Nikolai Georgievich Makarov** (Николай Георгиевич Макаров; born January 1955) is a Russian-American mathematician. He is known for his work in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and its applications to [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). He is currently the Richard Merkin Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech), where he has been teaching since 1991.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Makarov belongs to the Leningrad school of geometric function theory. He graduated from the [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) with a bachelor's degree in 1982. He received his Ph.D. (Candidate of Science) from the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) in 1986 under [Nikolai Nikolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Nikolski) with thesis *Metric properties of harmonic measure* (title translated from Russian). He was an academic at the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) in Leningrad. Since 1991 he has been a professor at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech).

In 1986 he was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).[^o5] In 1986 he was awarded the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) for solving difficult problems involving the boundary behavior of the conformal mapping of a disk onto a domain with a [Jordan curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_curve) boundary using stochastic methods. In 2020, he was awarded the [Rolf Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Schock_Prize), "for his significant contributions to [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and its applications to mathematical physics".[^o6]

His doctoral students include the [Fields medallist](wikipedia://en/10859) [Stanislav Smirnov](wikipedia://en/22752379), Alexei Poltoratski and Dapeng Zhan.

## Research {#Research}

Makarov works in complex analysis and related fields (potential theory, harmonic analysis, spectral theory) as well as on various applications to [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518), [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices) and mathematical [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774).

Makarov's most well-known result concerns the theory of [harmonic measure](wikipedia://en/12141074) in the complex plane. Makarov's theorem states that: Let Ω be a simply connected domain in the complex plane. Suppose that ∂Ω (the boundary of Ω) is a Jordan curve. Then the harmonic measure on ∂Ω has [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) 1.[^o9][^o10]

Makarov has also studied [diffusion-limited aggregation](wikipedia://en/1312515) which describes crystal growth in two dimensions with [Lennart Carleson](wikipedia://en/1669541) and Beurling-Malliavin theory with his former student Alexei Poltoratski. He has studied the thermodynamic formalism for iterations of the rational functions with another of his former students [Stanislav Smirnov](wikipedia://en/22752379), Fields medallist. He has studied the [universality](wikipedia://en/1752072) laws and field convergence in normal [random matrix](wikipedia://en/1648765) ensembles.

His most recent research concerns the mathematical [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774) and its relation to [Schramm–Loewner evolution](wikipedia://en/6706108) theory.
