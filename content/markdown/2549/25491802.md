# Herbert Busemann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herbert Busemann"}

*Image: Fenchel Alexandrov Busemann Jessen.jpg.* [Werner Fenchel](wikipedia://en/4784468), [Aleksandr Danilovich Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Danilovich_Aleksandrov), Herbert Busemann, [Børge Jessen](wikipedia://en/20754801) (1954).

**Herbert Busemann** (12 May 1905 – 3 February 1994) was a [German-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [convex](wikipedia://en/4165882) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He is the author of [Busemann's theorem](wikipedia://en/11329384) in [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) and [geometric tomography](wikipedia://en/385523). He was a member of the [Royal Danish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters) and a winner of the [Lobachevsky Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky_Medal) (1985), the first American mathematician to receive it.[^o2] He was also a [Fulbright scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_scholar) in [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand) in 1952.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Herbert Busemann was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) to a well-to-do family. His father, Alfred Busemann, was a director of [Krupp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krupp), where Busemann also worked for several years. He studied at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) (LMU), the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), and [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome). He defended his dissertation at [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1931, where his advisor was [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238). He remained in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) as an assistant until 1933, when he escaped [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) (he had a [Jewish grandfather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws)).[^o5] He worked at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) until 1936, when he left to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). There, he got married in 1939 and naturalized in 1943. He had temporary positions at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), [Illinois Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology), [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College), and eventually became a professor in 1947 at [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California).[^o6] He advanced to a [distinguished professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor) in 1964, and continued working at USC until his retirement in 1970. Over the course of his work at USC, he supervised over 10 Ph.D. students.

He is the author of six monographs, two of which were translated into Russian. He received [Lobachevsky Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky_Medal) in 1985 for his book *The geometry of geodesics*.[^o7]

Busemann was also an active mathematical citizen. At different times, he was the president of the [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California) chapter of [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), and a member of the council of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

Busemann was also an accomplished linguist; he was able to read and speak in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), and [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language). He could also read [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language), [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) and [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language).[^o9] He translated a number of papers and monograph, most notably from [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), a rare language at the time. He was also an accomplished artist and had several public exhibitions of his [Hard-edge paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard-edge_painting)\].[^o10] He died in [Santa Ynez, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ynez,_California) on February 3, 1994, at the age of 88.[^o11]

Busemann's Selected papers are now available in two volumes (908 and 842 pages), with introductory biographical material and commentaries on his work, and published by edited by Athanase Papadopoulos, Springer Verlag, 2018.
