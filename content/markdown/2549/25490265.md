# Nigel Smart (cryptographer)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nigel Smart"}

**Nigel Smart** is a professor at [COSIC](wikipedia://en/8116603) at the [Katholieke Universiteit Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven) and Chief Cryptographer at Zama.[^o3] He is a cryptographer with interests in the theory of cryptography and its application in practice.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Smart received a [BSc degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSc_degree) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [University of Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Reading) in 1989.[^o7]

He then obtained his [PhD degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD_degree) from the [University of Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kent) at [Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury) in 1992; his thesis was titled *The Computer Solutions of Diophantine Equations*.

## Career {#Career}

Smart proceeded to work as a [research fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_fellow) at the University of Kent, the [Erasmus University Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_University_Rotterdam), and [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University) until 1995. From 1995 to 1997, he was a lecturer in mathematics at the University of Kent, and then spent three years in industry at [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) from 1997 to 2000. From 2000 to 2017 he was at the University of Bristol, where he founded the cryptology research group. From 2018 he has been based in the COSIC group at the Katholieke Universiteit Leuven, where he is now a part-time professor.

Smart held a [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) Wolfson Merit Award (2008–2013), and two [ERC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) Advanced Grant (2011–2016 and 2016-2021). He was a director of the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043) (2012–2014), and was elected vice president for the period 2014-2016.[^o9] In 2016 he was named as a Fellow of the IACR.[^o10] In 2026 he was awarded the [RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Award_for_Excellence_in_Mathematics).

## Research {#Research}

Prof. Smart is known for his work in [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography), especially work on the [ECDLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECDLP).[^o12][^o13][^o14] He has also worked on [pairing-based cryptography](wikipedia://en/8605953) contributing a number of algorithms such as the [SK-KEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK-KEM)[^o15] and the Ate-pairing[^o16]

As well as elliptic curve cryptography, his work has been instrumental in the effort to make secure multiparty computation practical. A few of his works in this direction include.[^o17][^o18][^o19]

His work with Gentry and Halevi on performing the first large calculation using Fully Homomorphic Encryption[^o20] won the [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) *[Pat Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Goldberg) Best Paper Award* for 2012.[^o21]

In addition to his three years at [HP Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_Laboratories), Smart was a founder of the startup [Identum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identum) specialising in pairing based cryptography and identity based encryption. This was bought by [Trend Micro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trend_Micro) in 2008.[^o22] In 2013 he formed, with [Yehuda Lindell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehuda_Lindell), Unbound Security (formally called Dyadic Security), a company focusing on deploying distributed cryptographic solutions based on multi-party computations. Unbound Security was bought by Coinbase in 2021.[^o23]

He is also the co-founder, along with [Kenny Paterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Paterson), of the [Real World Crypto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_World_Crypto) conference series.[^o24]
