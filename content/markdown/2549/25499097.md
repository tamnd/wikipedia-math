# N-body units

::: {#o1 .table cue=table}

| Quantity | Expression |
|---|---|
| Unit of [length](wikipedia://en/18542) (*R*) | $\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{M^2} \sum_{i,j \ne i}^{N} \frac{m_i m_j}{\left\| \vec{r_j}-\vec{r_i} \right\| }$ |
| Unit of [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) (*M*) | $M = \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i$ |

:::

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="N-body units"}

***N*-body units** are a completely self-contained [system of units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_of_measurement) used for [*N*-body simulations](wikipedia://en/4917686) of self-gravitating systems in astrophysics. In this system, the base physical units are chosen so that the total mass, *M*, the [gravitational constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_constant), *G*, and the [virial radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virial_radius), *R*, are normalized. The underlying assumption is that the system of *N* objects (stars) satisfies the [virial theorem](wikipedia://en/32664). The consequence of standard *N*-body units is that the velocity dispersion of the system, *v*, is $\scriptstyle \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}$ and that the dynamical or crossing time, *t*, is $\scriptstyle 2\sqrt{2}$. The use of standard *N*-body units was advocated by [Michel Hénon](wikipedia://en/958653) in 1971.[^o5] Early adopters of this system of units included H. Cohn in 1979[^o6] and D. Heggie and R. Mathieu in 1986.[^o7] At the conference [MODEST14](http://www.astro.uni-bonn.de/~sambaran/DS2014/) in 2014, D. Heggie proposed that the community abandon the name "*N*-body units" and replace it with the name "Hénon units" to commemorate the originator.[^o8]

:::
