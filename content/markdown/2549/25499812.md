# Ieke Moerdijk

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ieke Moerdijk"}

**Izak** "**Ieke**" **Moerdijk** (; born 23 January 1958) is a Dutch mathematician, currently working at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University), who in 2012 won the [Spinoza Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinoza_Prize).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Moerdijk studied mathematics, philosophy and general linguistics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam). He obtained his PhD *cum laude* in 1985 at the same institution. His thesis was entitled *Topics in intuitionism and topos theory* and was written under the supervision of [Anne Sjerp Troelstra](wikipedia://en/15067195).[^o5][^o6]

After that, he worked as [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). From 1988 to 2011 he was professor at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University). After working at the Mathematical Institute of the [Radboud University Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen) for a few years, he returned to [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) in 2016.[^o7]

In 2000 Moerdijk was an invited speaker to the 3rd [European Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13505846).[^o8] He was elected member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2006[^o9] and of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2014.[^o10]

Moerdijk received the 2011 [Descartes-Huygens prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes-Huygens_Prize) for his contribution to French–Dutch scientific collaborations from the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_sciences) in Paris.[^o11][^o12] In 2012 he received the [Spinoza prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinoza_Prize) from the [Netherlands Organisation for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Organisation_for_Scientific_Research).[^o3][^o13][^o14]

## Research {#Research}

Moerdijk's research interests lie in the fields of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), [algebraic](wikipedia://en/38801) and [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), and their applications to [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).

Moerdijk is seen, together with [André Joyal](wikipedia://en/9157736), as one of the founders of algebraic set theory.[^o16][^o17] In 1992 he wrote, together with [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574), a book on [topos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topos_theory) that became the standard reference on the subject: *Sheaves in geometry and logic. A first introduction to topos theory*.[^o18] In 1995 he made pioneering contributions to [constructive non-standard analysis](wikipedia://en/25454823), of which he is one of the founders.[^o19]

Moerdijk's research has also covered topics in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625); in particular, he wrote in 2003 an influential monograph on [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227) and [Lie groupoids](wikipedia://en/310923).[^o20] Recently Moerdijk pursues, among other topics, research on the theory of [operads](wikipedia://en/3126358), on the logic structure of [quantum information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information_theory), and on [dendroidal sets.](wikipedia://en/29199687)

Moerdijk has written more than a hundred publications and is the author of several influential books. He supervised 19 PhD students as of 2021.[^o6]
