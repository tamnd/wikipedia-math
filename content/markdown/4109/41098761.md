# Benno Kerry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benno Kerry"}

**Benno Kerry** (; né **Kohn**; 11 December 1858 – 20 May 1889) was an Austrian philosopher.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Kerry was born as Benno Kohn[^o5] in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866). He studied under [Ernst Laas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Laas) and [Otto Liebmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Liebmann) at the [University of Strassburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strassburg) and from 1877/78 under [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano) at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna). In 1881 he obtained his doctorate with the dissertation *Untersuchungen über das Causalproblem auf dem Boden einer Kritik der einschlägigen Lehren J. St. Mills* ("Investigations concerning the problem of causality on the basis of a critique of the relevant doctrines of [John Stuart Mill](wikipedia://en/15626)"). In Vienna, as part of the [School of Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Brentano) he befriended [Alois Höfler](wikipedia://en/68541147).[^o6]

In 1885 he obtained his habilitation as *Privatdozent* in Strasburg with *Grundzüge einer Theorie der mathematischen und nicht-mathematischen Grenzbegriffe. Ein Beitrag zur Erkenntnistheorie* ("Foundations of a theory of mathematical and non-mathematical limit concepts. A contribution to epistemology") and became the assistant of the [neo-Kantian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Kantianism) [Wilhelm Windelband](wikipedia://en/1183183).

Kerry was influenced by [Bernard Bolzano](wikipedia://en/302185)[^o7] and became an important conduit of his work. He was among the first students of Brentano (with [Meinong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius_Meinong) and [Höfler](wikipedia://en/68541147)) to distinguish clearly between concept and object.

Kerry died on 20 May 1889[^o8] as a result of an ear infection.[^o9]

Kerry was [double first cousins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin) with Fritz Kerry, who was the father of [Richard Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kerry) and grandfather of [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry).[^o10]

## Influence {#Influence}

Kerry exercised an influence not just within the circle of Brentano, especially on [Alois Höfler](wikipedia://en/68541147) (for the concept "psychical labor"), [Edmund Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl) (in the *[Philosophy of Arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6704759)*),[^o6] and [Kazimierz Twardowski](wikipedia://en/1022980),[^o12] but also on [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416).[^o17] In fact, Frege conceived his paper "[Concept and Object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_and_Object)" as a reply to Kerry's criticisms. Furthermore he was in close contact with [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216) and it is thanks to his review of the *Mannigfaltigkeitslehre* that [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) came to know of the work of Cantor.

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Kerry's paradox"}

### Kerry and Frege {#Kerry_and_Frege}

Kerry (in particular in his fourth article) criticized Frege for having confused concept and object in his *[Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Grundlagen_der_Arithmetik)* and in *[Begriffsschrift](wikipedia://en/900125)*.[^o15] Frege responded to a number of aspects of this critique in 1892 with the paper "Begriff und Gegenstand" ("Concept and Object"). The controversy became known as the "concept *horse* problem"[^o16] or "Kerry's paradox."[^o17]

:::
