# Bers slice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bers slice"}

*Image: Bers-Einbettung.png.* Computer generated image of the bers embedding of a punctured torus’ 2-dimensional Teichmüller space.

In the mathematical theory of [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652), **Bers slices** and **Maskit slices**, named after [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972) and [Bernard Maskit](wikipedia://en/45153921), are certain slices through the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of Kleinian groups.

:::

## Bers slices {#Bers_slices}

For a [quasi-Fuchsian group](wikipedia://en/30960521), the limit set is a Jordan curve whose complement has two components. The quotient of each of these components by the groups is a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181), so we get a map from marked quasi-Fuchsian groups to pairs of Riemann surfaces, and hence to a product of two copies of [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661). A Bers slice is a subset of the moduli space of quasi-Fuchsian groups for which one of the two components of this map is a [constant function](wikipedia://en/320861) to a single point in its copy of Teichmüller space.

The Bers slice gives an embedding of Teichmüller space into the moduli space of quasi-Fuchsian groups, called the **Bers embedding**, and the closure of its image is a [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) of Teichmüller space called the **Bers compactification**.

## Maskit slices {#Maskit_slices}

A Maskit slice is similar to a Bers slice, except that the group is no longer quasi-Fuchsian, and instead of fixing a point in Teichmüller space one fixes a point in the boundary of Teichmüller space.

The **Maskit boundary** is a fractal in the Maskit slice separating discrete groups from more chaotic groups.
