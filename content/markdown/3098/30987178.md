# Vasily Seseman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Vasily Seseman"}

**Vasily Seseman** (Vosylius Sezemanas, Василий Эмильевич Сеземан); 1884–1963) was a [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts) philosopher and professor of [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_people), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_people), [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_people) and [Baltic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_German) descent.[^o3][^o4][^o6] A representative of [neo-Kantianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Kantianism),[^o3] Seseman is mostly remembered for his role in fostering philosophy in newly independent Lithuania and developing [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language) philosophical vocabulary (most remarkable are his translations of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308) into Lithuanian and contributions to Lithuanian [encyclopedias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia)). A close associate of [Viktor Zhirmunsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Zhirmunsky) and [Lev Karsavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Karsavin), as a prisoner of [Gulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag) he was also an informal philosophy tutor and supporter of [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) writer [Bidia Dandaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidia_Dandaron).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Wilhelm Sesemann was born on the 11 June 1884 \[O.S. 30 May\] in Vyborg, Great Duchy of Finland (present-day, [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia)) to Ida Maria Sesemann (née Baekmann) and Emil Hermann Sesemann.[^o6] Seseman's father, born in Vyborg, was a [doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) of German descent, while his mother was from the [Governorate of Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_of_Livonia) (present-day, [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia) and [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia)) and of [Baltic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_German) descent .[^o6] Through his sister Ida Maria Parland (née Sesemann), Seseman was the uncle of [Henry Parland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Parland), [Oscar Parland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Parland), [Ralf Parland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralf_Parland) and Herman Parland.[^o7]

Growing up in between Vyborg and [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg), Seseman attended the Lutheran school (Katharinenschule) in St Petersburg. As he grew up, he adopted a more Russian identity, changing Wilhelm to Wassilij (Vasily) and embracing [Russian Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Christianity).

After two years of medical studies he turned to philosophy, fervently studying classical authors under [Nikolay Lossky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Lossky) and classical languages under [Tadeusz Stefan Zieliński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Stefan_Zieliński) at [University of St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Petersburg_University). In 1909-1911 the university sent him to Germany to prepare him for a teaching career. In Berlin and Marburg, he took courses in philosophy, psychology, and pedagogics under [Hermann Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Cohen), [Paul Natorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Natorp), [Ernst Cassirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Cassirer), [Hermann Alexander Diels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Alexander_Diels), and [Heinrich Wölfflin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Wölfflin). In Germany he also met [José Ortega y Gasset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Ortega_y_Gasset) who made a great impression on him, and re-established a lifelong friendship with [Nicolai Hartmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolai_Hartmann) who in St Petersburg had influenced Vasily's decision to switch from medicine to philosophy.

Upon his return to St. Petersburg, Seseman taught philosophy and classical languages until [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), when he enlisted as a volunteer in the Russian army.

From 1915 to 1917 he taught philosophy as a [privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at the University of St. Petersburg, and from 1918 to 1919 at the Viatka Pedagogical Institute. He received a docentship in Saratov, where he worked (together with Viktor Zhirmunsky) until 1921.

Later Seseman, as a Finnish citizen, emigrated to Finland and then to Berlin, where he finally found a teaching position at the Russian Institute.

In 1923 Seseman was invited to become a professor at [Kaunas University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas_University) in Lithuania. When Vilnius was [regained by Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet–Lithuanian_Mutual_Assistance_Treaty#In_Vilnius_Region), he moved there and worked at the [University of Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University) until the Nazis closed it down in 1943. He worked as a [German language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) teacher during the [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Lithuania_by_Nazi_Germany#German_occupation), and led a philosophy course in the Jewish ghetto.

According to his stepdaughter's interview, while living in poverty, at his place in central Vilnius he also hid from the Nazis a Jewish girl (who later disappeared), and supplied ghetto Jews with false documents allowing emigration. He made a narrow escape from being burnt alive for being a supporter of the Jews while the Nazi troops were abandoning the city for the Soviet troops. His efforts on behalf of the Jews were posthumously recognized by the Lithuanian government awarding him a medal.

He spent 1945-1950 teaching at the University of Vilnius again, but then was arrested by the Soviet authorities, accused of “anti-Soviet activities” and “relations with Zionist organizations” and sentenced to 15 years of labor camps. In Siberia he met a [Buddhist tantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_tantra) practitioner [Bidia Dandaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidia_Dandaron) who learned a lot from Seseman, as a result embracing Kantian ideas and developing his own synthesis of Tibetan Buddhist and European philosophical thought in his writings. Their friendship continued after they were released.

In 1956 Seseman was released, in 1958 [rehabilitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_rehabilitation) and resumed his professorship at Vilnius where he taught for the rest of his life.

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

Seseman liked to call his philosophy “gnoseological idealism”, trying to revive [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) through a re-evaluation of the metaphysical tradition of [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology). His other philosophical aim was to overcome the dichotomies between the subjective-psychological and the objective-realistic in the [theory of knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_knowledge) and metaphysics in general.

Seseman's concern for formal questions in linguistics and aesthetics may make him a precursor of modern [semiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotics).
