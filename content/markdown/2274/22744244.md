# Ron Aharoni

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ron Aharoni"}

**Ron Aharoni** (רון אהרוני; born 1952) is an Israeli [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working in finite and infinite [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). Aharoni is a professor at the [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology), where he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics in 1979. With [Nash-Williams](wikipedia://en/16550517) and [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) he generalized [Hall's marriage theorem](wikipedia://en/186969) by obtaining the right transfinite conditions for infinite [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431). He subsequently proved the appropriate versions of the [Kőnig theorem](wikipedia://en/5465118) and the [Menger theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menger_theorem) for [infinite graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_graph) (the latter with [Eli Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Berger)). In 2000 he proved, building on joint work with P. Haxell, a topological version of Hall's theorem. This was used to prove the first open case (that of 3-uniform hypergraphs) of a famous conjecture by Ryser: in a 3-partite hypergraph the ratio between the covering number and the matching number is at most 2.

:::

Aharoni is the author of several nonspecialist books; the most successful is *[Arithmetic for Parents](wikipedia://en/22225493)*, a book helping parents and elementary school teachers in teaching basic mathematics. He also wrote a book on the connections between *Mathematics, poetry and beauty* and on philosophy, *The Cat That is not There*. His book, "Man detaches meaning", is on a mechanism common to jokes and poetry. His last to date book is [Circularity: A Common Secret to Paradoxes, Scientific Revolutions and Humor](http://www.worldscientific.com/worldscibooks/10.1142/9805),[^o3] which binds together mathematics, philosophy and the secrets of humor.
