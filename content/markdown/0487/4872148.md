# Theodor Estermann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theodor Estermann"}

**Theodor Estermann** (5 February 1902 – 29 November 1991) was a German-born British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working in the field of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513). The [Estermann measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estermann_measure), a measure of the [central symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_symmetry) of a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), is named after him.[^o2]

:::

He was born in [Neubrandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubrandenburg), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), "to keen [Zionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionists) who named him in honour of [Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl)."[^o3] His doctorate, completed in 1925, was supervised by [Hans Rademacher](wikipedia://en/1864916). He spent most of his career at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), eventually as a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor). [Heini Halberstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heini_Halberstam), [Klaus Roth](wikipedia://en/787827) and [Robert Charles Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Charles_Vaughan_%28mathematician%29) were Ph.D. students of his.

Though Estermann left Germany in 1929, before the [Nazis seized power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1933) in 1933, some historians count him among the early emigrants who fled Nazi Germany.[^o4][^o5]

The physicist [Immanuel Estermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Estermann) was the brother of Theodor Estermann.
