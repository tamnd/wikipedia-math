# Leslie Earl Simon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leslie Earl Simon"}

**Leslie Earl Simon** (August 11, 1900 – October 28, 1983) was an American military officer and scientist, a major general in the [U.S. Army's Ordnance Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Ordnance_Corps), and director of the [Ballistic Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_Research_Laboratory) at the [Aberdeen Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Proving_Ground) military facility in Maryland.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Leslie Earl Simon was born in [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee) on August 11, 1900.[^o4][^o5] He graduated from the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) at [West Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point,_New_York) in 1924, eventually attaining the rank of [major general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_general_%28United_States%29).[^o4][^o6] From 1938 to 1941 he served as assistant director of the Ballistic Research Laboratory and from 1941 until 1949 as director, supervising development of the country's first supersonic wind tunnel, the [world’s first electronic digital computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENIAC), and the [Army's missile test range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range) at White Sands, New Mexico. He received the [Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Service_Medal_%28U.S._Army%29) in 1956.[^o6] After retiring from the army Simon served for 6 years as a vice-president of the Carborundum Company.

He died in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida) on October 28, 1983, and was buried at [Arlington National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_National_Cemetery).[^o4][^o7][^o8]

## Writing {#Writing}

*An Engineer's Manual of Statistical Methods* was an introduction to the quality control methods pioneered by [Walter A. Shewhart](wikipedia://en/433679): it was written for ordnance officers and drew upon Simon's knowledge of munitions production. The book appeared as the United States entered World War II and statisticians were mobilising for the war effort. Simon became a respected figure in the statistics community. In 1945 he was made a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/51140705), in 1948 he was the first recipient of the [Shewhart Medal](wikipedia://en/831964) and in 1966 he received the [Wilks Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/15966180). The citation for the latter read as follows: "To Major General Leslie E. Simon for his pioneering contributions to Quality Control, Sampling Inspection, Reliability and Army Design of Experiments, and for his timely promotion of statistical activities which have benefited not only the Army but our government and country as well."

Simon was one of the Allied scientists sent to Europe at the end of World War II to investigate German weaponry and *German Research* drew on the experience to discuss various secret weapons, with an emphasis on airplanes, rocketry and the Germans' research methodology. It was first published in 1947. The book has become a collector's item in Europe since [Hergé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hergé) featured it in the storyline of *[The Adventures of Tintin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tintin)* comic *[The Calculus Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Calculus_Affair)*, published in 1956, where it appears on page 23. The book is available, nevertheless, in various versions. Hergé [censored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship) the [swastika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swastika), when he inserted the book.

## Selected works {#Selected_works}

- *An Engineer's Manual of Statistical Methods,* 1941, John Wiley and Sons,
- *German Research in World War II: An Analysis of the Conduct of Research*, 1947, John Wiley.
- *German Research* was also published under the same title in the UK in 1948 by Chapman & Hall, then republished in 1970 as *Secret Weapons of the Third Reich: German Research in World War II* by Paladin Press .
