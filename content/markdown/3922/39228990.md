# Riesz rearrangement inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riesz rearrangement inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Riesz rearrangement inequality**, sometimes called **Riesz–Sobolev** inequality, states that any three non-negative functions $f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^+$, $g : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^+$ and $h : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^+$ satisfy the inequality

:::

$$\iint_{\mathbb{R}^n\times \mathbb{R}^n} f(x) g(x-y) h(y) \, dx\,dy \le \iint_{\mathbb{R}^n\times \mathbb{R}^n} f^*(x) g^*(x-y) h^*(y) \, dx\,dy,$$

where $f^* : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^+$, $g^* : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^+$ and $h^* : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^+$ are the [symmetric decreasing rearrangements](wikipedia://en/35918024) of the functions $f$, $g$ and $h$ respectively.

## History {#History}

The inequality was first proved by [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126) in 1930,[^o3] and independently reproved by S.L.Sobolev in 1938. Brascamp, [Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Lieb) and Luttinger have shown that it can be generalized to arbitrarily (but finitely) many functions acting on arbitrarily many variables.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Riesz rearrangement inequality can be used to prove the [Pólya–Szegő inequality](wikipedia://en/24701090).

:::

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

### One-dimensional case {#One-dimensional_case}

In the one-dimensional case, the inequality is first proved when the functions $f$, $g$ and $h$ are [characteristic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_function) of a finite unions of intervals. Then the inequality can be extended to characteristic functions of measurable sets, to measurable functions taking a finite number of values and finally to nonnegative measurable functions.[^o10]

### Higher-dimensional case {#Higher-dimensional_case}

In order to pass from the one-dimensional case to the higher-dimensional case, the spherical rearrangement is approximated by Steiner symmetrization for which the one-dimensional argument applies directly by Fubini's theorem.[^o12]

:::

## Equality cases {#Equality_cases}

In the case where any one of the three functions is a strictly symmetric-decreasing function, equality holds only when the other two functions are equal, up to translation, to their symmetric-decreasing rearrangements.[^o14]
