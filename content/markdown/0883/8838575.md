# Nicholas Kratzer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nicholas Kratzer"}

**Nicholas Kratzer** (1487? – 1550), also known as Nicolaus Kratzer and Nicholas Crutcher, was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), astronomer, and [horologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horologist). Much of Kratzer's professional life was spent in England, where he was appointed as astronomer to King [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) in 1486 or 1487,[^o4] Kratzer was the son of a saw-smith, and was educated at the Universities of [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) (B.A. 1509) and [Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_of_Halle-Wittenberg). He came to England in 1516 and established himself as part of the artistic and scientific circle around Sir [Thomas More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_More).[^o5] Kratzer tutored More's children in mathematics and astronomy and More introduced him at court in much the same way as he had their mutual friend [Hans Holbein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Holbein_the_Younger), who produced [a portrait of Kratzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Nicolaus_Kratzer). In the same manner as Holbein, Kratzer's talents obtained him a court position as astronomer and clock maker to the king.

*Image: Fowler Corpus Christi Kratzer's dial.jpg.* Sundial by Kratzer, formerly in the garden of [Corpus Christi College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Oxford).[^o8]

Kratzer was the designer of a painted ceiling featuring the zodiac and the seven planets which was installed in a temporary banqueting house erected at [Greenwich Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Palace) in March 1527. According to accounts kept by Richard Gibson, an officer of the revels, "Master Nikolas" worked with the painter "Master Hans", Hans Holbein, and was paid 4 shillings a day.[^o6]

Kratzer also collaborated with Holbein on producing maps, and in return the artist produced a portrait of Kratzer in 1528 that now hangs in the [Louvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louvre); it depicts the craftsman surrounded by the tools of his trade, and with an unfinished polyhedral [sundial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundial). His close relationship with Holbein and More also may be observed in his annotations of Holbein's draft for his portrait of the More family.[^o7] Kratzer identifies the various family members and their ages for the benefit of More's friend, the theologian [Erasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desiderius_Erasmus).

Kratzer (under the name Nich. Cratcherus) is recorded as a reader of [Corpus Christi College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Oxford), where he is presumed to have taught mathematics.[^o8] He designed at least two fixed sundials in Oxford, both of which are now lost. One was in [St Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Church_of_St_Mary_the_Virgin) churchyard,[^o9] the other in the garden of [Corpus Christi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Oxford).[^o8] He also designed a portable dial for Cardinal Wolsey, which is now in the [History of Science Museum, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Science_Museum,_Oxford).[^o10] Another dial attributed to Kratzer was found at [Acton Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acton_Court), near Bristol, in the 1980s.[^o11][^o12]

Peter Drinkwater has presented a critical evaluation of the sundials attributed to Kratzer, in particular the one in the Holbein portrait.[^o13] He comments that "Kratzer triumphed, not through genius or creativity, but through having learned what others had discovered and invented, and by being the first to apply that learning in England". [John North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_North_%28historian%29) concurs: "Kratzer doubtless had nothing new to offer of a fundamental kind. Many of his dials were unusual, but his favorite polyhedral dial was perhaps more useful as a repository of verses \[...\] than for actually announcing the time with any accuracy". Nonetheless, Kratzer brought useful German skills and knowledge to the English court and was well-regarded over a period of nearly 30 years.[^o5]
