# Subspace theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subspace theorem"}

In mathematics, the **subspace theorem** says that points of small [height](wikipedia://en/3141855) in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) lie in a finite number of [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862). It is a result obtained by (txt).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The subspace theorem states that if *L*<sub>1</sub>,...,*L*<sub>*n*</sub> are [linearly independent](wikipedia://en/101863) [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear) [forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_form) in *n* variables with [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1158) coefficients and if ε>0 is any given real number, then the non-zero integer points *x* with

$$|L_1(x)\cdots L_n(x)|<|x|^{-\epsilon}$$

lie in a finite number of [proper subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) of **Q**<sup>*n*</sup>.

A quantitative form of the theorem, which determines the number of subspaces containing all solutions, was also obtained by Schmidt, and the theorem was generalised by (Schlickewei 1977) to allow more general [absolute values](wikipedia://en/5042667) on [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The theorem may be used to obtain results on [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) such as [Siegel's theorem on integral points](wikipedia://en/3989092) and solution of the [S-unit equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-unit_equation).[^o8]

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="A corollary on Diophantine approximation"}

### A corollary on Diophantine approximation {#A_corollary_on_Diophantine_approximation}

The following corollary to the subspace theorem is often itself referred to as the *subspace theorem*. If *a*<sub>1</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*n*</sub> are algebraic such that 1,*a*<sub>1</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*n*</sub> are linearly independent over **Q** and ε>0 is any given real number, then there are only finitely many rational *n*-tuples (*x*<sub>1</sub>/y,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>/y) with

$$|a_i-x_i/y|<y^{-(1+1/n+\epsilon)},\quad i=1,\ldots,n.$$

The specialization *n* = 1 gives the [Thue–Siegel–Roth theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thue–Siegel–Roth_theorem). One may also note that the exponent 1+1/*n*+ε is best possible by [Dirichlet's theorem on diophantine approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet's_theorem_on_diophantine_approximation).
