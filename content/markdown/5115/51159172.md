# One-relator group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="One-relator group"}

In the mathematical subject of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **one-relator group** is a group given by a [group presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_presentation) with a single defining relation. One-relator groups play an important role in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) by providing many explicit examples of finitely presented groups.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A one-relator group is a group *G* that admits a group presentation of the form

::: {#o4 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$G=\langle X\mid r=1\, \rangle \tag{1}$$

:::

where *X* is a set (in general possibly infinite), and where $r\in F(X)$ is a freely and cyclically reduced word.

If *Y* is the set of all letters $x\in X$ that appear in *r* and $X'=X\setminus Y$ then

$$G=\langle Y\mid r=1\, \rangle \ast F(X').$$

For that reason *X* in ((1)) is usually assumed to be finite where one-relator groups are discussed, in which case ((1)) can be rewritten more explicitly as

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$G=\langle x_1,\dots, x_n\mid r=1\, \rangle, \tag{2}$$

:::

where $X=\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ for some integer $n\ge 1.$

:::

## Freiheitssatz {#Freiheitssatz}

Let *G* be a one-relator group given by presentation ((1)) above. Recall that *r* is a freely and cyclically reduced word in *F*(*X*). Let $y\in X$ be a letter such that $y$ or $y^{-1}$ appears in *r*. Let $X_1\subseteq X\setminus \{y\}$. The subgroup $H=\langle X_1\rangle\le G$ is called a Magnus subgroup of *G*.

A famous 1930 theorem of [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666),[^o7] known as [Freiheitssatz](wikipedia://en/12756944), states that in this situation *H* is [freely generated by $X_1$](wikipedia://en/59735), that is, $H=F(X_1)$. See also[^o8][^o9] for other proofs.

## Properties of one-relator groups {#Properties_of_one-relator_groups}

Here we assume that a one-relator group *G* is given by presentation ((2)) with a finite generating set $X=\{x_1,\dots, x_n\}$ and a nontrivial freely and cyclically reduced defining relation $1\ne r\in F(X)$.

- A one-relator group *G* is [torsion-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group) if and only if $r\in F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is not a proper power.
- Every one-relator group *G* is [virtually](wikipedia://en/10377971) torsion-free, that is, admits a torsion-free [subgroup of finite index](wikipedia://en/230581).[^o11]
- Every one-relator group is coherent. [^o12] That is, every finitely generated subgroup is finitely presented.
- A one-relator presentation is diagrammatically aspherical.[^o13]
- If $r\in F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is not a proper power then the [presentation complex](wikipedia://en/12821736) *P* for presentation ((2)) is a finite [Eilenberg–MacLane complex](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(G,1)$.[^o14]
- If $r\in F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is not a proper power then a one-relator group *G* has [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) $\le 2$.
- A one-relator group $G$ is [free](wikipedia://en/59735) if and only if $r\in F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is a [primitive element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_element_%28free_group%29) or the empty word; in the case of a primitive word $G$ is free of rank $n - 1$, in the case of the empty word it is free of rank $n$.[^o15]
- Suppose the element $r\in F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is of minimal length under the action of $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$, and suppose that for every $i=1,\dots,n$ either $x_i$ or $x_i^{-1}$ occurs in *r*. Then the group *G* is [freely indecomposable](wikipedia://en/360036).[^o16]
- If $r\in F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is not a proper power then a one-relator group *G* is [locally indicable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_indicable_group), that is, every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup of *G* admits a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) onto $\mathbb Z$.[^o17]
- Every one-relator group *G* has algorithmically decidable [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563).[^o18]
- If *G* is a one-relator group and $H\le G$ is a Magnus subgroup then the [subgroup membership problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroup_membership_problem) for *H* in *G* is decidable.[^o18]
- It is unknown if one-relator groups have solvable [conjugacy problem](wikipedia://en/4942384).
- It is unknown if the [isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/18054026) is decidable for the class of one-relator groups.
- A one-relator group *G* given by presentation ((2)) has [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) *n* (that is, it cannot be generated by fewer than *n* elements) unless $r\in F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is a primitive element.[^o19]
- Let *G* be a one-relator group given by presentation ((2)). If $n\ge 3$ then the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) of *G* is trivial, $Z(G)=\{1\}$. If $n=2$ and *G* is non-abelian with non-trivial center, then the center of *G* is [infinite cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic).[^o20]
- Let $r,s\in F(X)$ where $X=\{x_1,\dots, x_n\}$. Let $N_1=\langle\langle r\rangle\rangle_{F(X)}$ and $N_2=\langle\langle s\rangle\rangle_{F(X)}$ be the [normal closures](wikipedia://en/153099) of *r* and *s* in *F*(*X*) accordingly. Then $N_1=N_2$ if and only if $r$ is [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176) to $s$ or $s^{-1}$ in *F*(*X*).[^o21][^o22]
- There exists a finitely generated one-relator group that is not [Hopfian](wikipedia://en/11486001) and therefore not [residually finite](wikipedia://en/5780875), for example the [Baumslag–Solitar group](wikipedia://en/11485892) $B(2,3)=\langle a,b\mid b^{-1}a^2b=a^3\rangle$.[^o23]
- Let *G* be a one-relator group given by presentation ((2)). Then *G* satisfies the following version of the [Tits alternative](wikipedia://en/12704641). If *G* is torsion-free then every subgroup of *G* either contains a free group of rank 2 or is [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012). If *G* has nontrivial torsion, then every subgroup of *G* either contains a free group of rank 2, or is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327), or is [infinite dihedral](wikipedia://en/4296050).[^o24]
- Let *G* be a one-relator group given by presentation ((2)). Then the normal subgroup $N=\langle\langle r\rangle\rangle_{F(X)}\le F(X)$ admits a free basis of the form $\{u_i^{-1}ru_i\mid i\in I\}$ for some family of elements $\{u_i\in F(X)\mid i\in I\}$.[^o25]

## One-relator groups with torsion {#One-relator_groups_with_torsion}

Suppose a one-relator group *G* given by presentation ((2)) where $r=s^m$ where $m\ge 2$ and where $1\ne s\in F(X)$ is not a proper power (and thus *s* is also freely and cyclically reduced). Then the following hold:

- The element *s* has [order](wikipedia://en/493527) *m* in *G*, and every element of finite order in *G* is conjugate to a power of *s*.[^o27]
- Every finite subgroup of *G* is [conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_subgroup) to a subgroup of $\langle s\rangle$ in *G*. Moreover, the subgroup of *G* generated by all torsion elements is a free product of a family of conjugates of $\langle s\rangle$ in *G*.[^o11]
- *G* admits a torsion-free [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of finite index.[^o11]
- Newman's "spelling theorem"[^o28][^o29] Let $1\ne w\in F(X)$ be a freely reduced word such that $w=1$ in *G*. Then *w* contains a subword *v* such that *v* is also a subword of $r$ or $r^{-1}$ of length $|v|=1+(m-1)|s|$. Since $m\ge 2$ that means that $|v|>|r|/2$ and presentation ((2)) of *G* is a [Dehn presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehn's_algorithm).
- *G* has virtual cohomological dimension $\le 2$.[^o30]
- *G* is a [word-hyperbolic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group).[^o31]
- *G* has decidable [conjugacy problem](wikipedia://en/4942384).[^o28]
- *G* is [coherent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_group), that is every finitely generated subgroup of *G* is finitely presentable.[^o32]
- The [isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/18054026) is decidable for finitely generated one-relator groups with torsion, by virtue of their hyperbolicity.[^o33]
- *G* is [residually finite](wikipedia://en/5780875).[^o34]
- $G$ is virtually [free-by-cyclic](wikipedia://en/10377468), i.e. $G$ has a subgroup $H$ of finite-index such that there is a free normal subgroup $F \triangleleft H$ with cyclic quotient $F/H$.[^o35]

::: {#o37 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Magnus-Moldavansky method"}

## Magnus–Moldavansky method {#Magnus–Moldavansky_method}

Starting with the work of Magnus in the 1930s, most general results about one-relator groups are proved by induction on the length |*r*| of the defining relator *r*. The presentation below follows Section 6 of Chapter II of Lyndon and Schupp[^o38] and Section 4.4 of Magnus, Karrass and Solitar[^o39] for Magnus' original approach and Section 5 of Chapter IV of Lyndon and Schupp[^o40] for the Moldavansky's HNN-extension version of that approach.[^o41]

Let *G* be a one-relator group given by presentation ((1)) with a finite generating set *X*. Assume also that every generator from *X* actually occurs in *r*.

One can usually assume that $\#X\ge 2$ (since otherwise *G* is cyclic and whatever statement is being proved about *G* is usually obvious).

The main case to consider when some generator, say *t*, from *X* occurs in *r* with exponent sum 0 on *t*. Say $X=\{t, a, b,\dots, z\}$ in this case. For every generator $x\in X\setminus \{t\}$ one denotes $x_i=t^{-i}xt^i$ where $i\in \mathbb Z$. Then *r* can be rewritten as a word $r_0$ in these new generators $X_{\infty}= \{(a_i)_i, (b_i)_i, \dots, (z_i)_i\}$ with $|r_0|<|r|$.

For example, if $r=t^{-2}bt a t^3b^{-2}a^2t^{-1}at^{-1}$ then $r_0=b_2a_1b_{-2}^{-2}a_{-2}^2a_{-1}$.

Let $X_0$ be the alphabet consisting of the portion of $X_{\infty}$ given by all $x_i$ with $m(x)\le i\le M(x)$ where $m(x), M(x)$ are the minimum and the maximum subscripts with which $x_i^{\pm 1}$ occurs in $r_0$.

Magnus observed that the subgroup $L=\langle X_0\rangle \le G$ is itself a one-relator group with the one-relator presentation $L=\langle X_0\mid r_0=1\rangle$. Note that since $|r_0|<|r|$, one can usually apply the inductive hypothesis to $L$ when proving a particular statement about *G*.

Moreover, if $X_i=t^{-i}X_0t^i$ for $i\in \mathbb Z$ then $L_i=\langle X_i\rangle=\langle X_i| r_i=1\rangle$ is also a one-relator group, where $r_i$ is obtained from $r_0$ by shifting all subscripts by $i$. Then the normal closure $N=\langle \langle X_0\rangle\rangle_G$ of $X_0$ in *G* is

$$N=\left\langle \bigcup_{i\in \mathbb Z} L_i \right\rangle.$$

Magnus' original approach exploited the fact that *N* is actually an iterated [amalgamated product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamated_product) of the groups $L_i$, amalgamated along suitably chosen Magnus free subgroups. His proof of [Freiheitssatz](wikipedia://en/12756944) and of the solution of the word problem for one-relator groups was based on this approach.

Later Moldavansky simplified the framework and noted that in this case *G* itself is an [HNN-extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNN-extension) of *L* with associated subgroups being Magnus free subgroups of *L*.

If for every generator from $X_0$ its minimum and maximum subscripts in $r_0$ are equal then $G=L\ast \langle t\rangle$ and the inductive step is usually easy to handle in this case.

Suppose then that some generator from $X_0$ occurs in $r_0$ with at least two distinct subscripts. We put $Y_-$ to be the set of all generators from $X_0$ with non-maximal subscripts and we put $Y_+$ to be the set of all generators from $X_0$ with non-maximal subscripts. (Hence every generator from $Y_-$ and from $Y_-$ occurs in $r_0$ with a non-unique subscript.) Then $H_-=\langle Y_-\rangle$ and $H_+=\langle Y_+\rangle$ are free Magnus subgroups of *L* and $t^{-1}H_- t=H_+$. Moldavansky observed that in this situation

$$G=\langle L, t\mid t^{-1}H_- t=H_+\rangle$$

is an HNN-extension of *L*. This fact often allows proving something about *G* using the inductive hypothesis about the one-relator group *L* via the use of normal form methods and structural algebraic properties for the HNN-extension *G*.

The general case, both in Magnus' original setting and in Moldavansky's simplification of it, requires treating the situation where no generator from *X* occurs with exponent sum 0 in *r*. Suppose that distinct letters $x,y\in X$ occur in *r* with nonzero exponents $\alpha, \beta$ accordingly. Consider a homomorphism $f:F(X)\to F(X)$ given by $f(x)=xy^{-\beta}, f(y)=y^\alpha$ and fixing the other generators from *X*. Then for $r'=f(r)\in F(X)$ the exponent sum on *y* is equal to 0. The map *f* induces a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $\phi: G\to G'=\langle X\mid r'=1\rangle$ that turns out to be an embedding. The one-relator group *G**can then be treated using Moldavansky's approach. When $G'$ splits as an HNN-extension of a one-relator group*****L*, the defining relator $r_0$ of*L*still turns out to be shorter than*r*, allowing for inductive arguments to proceed. Magnus' original approach used a similar version of an embedding trick for dealing with this case.***

:::

## Two-generator one-relator groups {#Two-generator_one-relator_groups}

It turns out that many two-generator one-relator groups split as [semidirect products](wikipedia://en/48246) $G=F_m\rtimes\mathbb Z$. This fact was observed by [Ken Brown](wikipedia://en/41104429) when analyzing the BNS-invariant of one-relator groups using the Magnus-Moldavansky method.

Namely, let *G* be a one-relator group given by presentation ((2)) with $n=2$ and let $\phi:G\to \mathbb Z$ be an epimorphism. One can then change a free basis of $F(X)$ to a basis $t,a$ such that $\phi(t)=1,\phi(a)=0$ and rewrite the presentation of *G* in this generators as

$$G=\langle a,t\mid r=1\rangle$$

where $1\ne r=r(a,t)\in F(a,t)$ is a freely and cyclically reduced word.

Since $\phi(r)=0, \phi(t)=1$, the exponent sum on *t* in *r* is equal to 0. Again putting $a_i=t^{-i}at^i$, we can rewrite *r* as a word $r_0$ in $(a_i)_{i\in \mathbb Z}.$ Let $m,M$ be the minimum and the maximum subscripts of the generators occurring in $r_0$. Brown showed[^o43] that $\ker(\phi)$ is finitely generated [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $m<M$ and both $a_m$ and $a_{M}$ occur exactly once in $r_0$, and moreover, in that case the group $\ker(\phi)$ is free. Therefore if $\phi:G\to \mathbb Z$ is an epimorphism with a finitely generated kernel, then *G* splits as $G=F_m\rtimes \mathbb Z$ where $F_m=\ker(\phi)$ is a finite rank [free group](wikipedia://en/59735).

Later Dunfield and Thurston proved[^o44] that if a one-relator two-generator group $G=\langle x_1,x_2\mid r=1\rangle$ is chosen "at random" (that is, a cyclically reduced word *r* of length *n* in $F(x_1,x_2)$ is chosen uniformly at random) then the probability $p_n$ that a homomorphism from *G* onto $\mathbb Z$ with a finitely generated kernel exists satisfies

$$0.0006<p_n<0.975$$

for all sufficiently large *n*. Moreover, their experimental data indicates that the limiting value for $p_n$ is close to $0.94$.

## Examples of one-relator groups {#Examples_of_one-relator_groups}

- [Free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) $\mathbb Z\times \mathbb Z=\langle a, b \mid a^{-1}b^{-1}ab=1\rangle$
- [Baumslag–Solitar group](wikipedia://en/11485892) $B(m, n)=\langle a,b\mid b^{-1} a^m b= a^n\rangle$ where $m,n\ne 0$.
- [Torus knot](wikipedia://en/1165182) group $G=\langle a, b\mid a^p=b^q\rangle$ where $p,q\ge 1$ are [coprime integers](wikipedia://en/6556).
- [Baumslag–Gersten group](wikipedia://en/28923195) $G=\langle a,t \mid a^{a^t}=a^2\rangle =\langle a, t \mid (t^{-1}a^{-1}t) a (t^{-1} at)=a^2 \rangle$
- Oriented [surface group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_group) $G=\langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_n, b_n\mid [a_1,b_1]\dots [a_n,b_n]=1\rangle$ where $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ and where $n\ge 1$.
- Non-oriented surface group $G=\langle a_1,\dots, a_n\mid a_1^2\cdots a_n^2=1\rangle$, where $n\ge 1$.

## Generalizations and open problems {#Generalizations_and_open_problems}

- If *A* and *B* are two groups, and $r\in A\ast B$ is an element in their [free product](wikipedia://en/360036), one can consider a **one-relator product** $G=A\ast B/\langle\langle r\rangle\rangle=\langle A, B\mid r=1\rangle$.
- The so-called Kervaire conjecture, also known as Kervaire–Laudenbach conjecture, asks if it is true that if *A* is a nontrivial group and $B=\langle t\rangle$ is infinite cyclic then for every $r\in A\ast B$ the one-relator product $G=\langle A, t\mid r=1\rangle$ is nontrivial.[^o47]
- Klyachko proved the Kervaire conjecture for the case where *A* is torsion-free.[^o48]
- A conjecture attributed to Gersten[^o31] says that a finitely generated one-relator group is word-hyperbolic if and only if it contains no Baumslag–Solitar subgroups.
