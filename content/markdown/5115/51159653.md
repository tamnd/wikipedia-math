# Fibonacci word fractal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fibonacci word fractal"}

The **Fibonacci word fractal** is a [fractal curve](wikipedia://en/4972605) defined on the plane from the [Fibonacci word](wikipedia://en/1702139).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Fibonacci fractal first iterations.png.* The first iterations

*Image: Fibonacci curve L-system.png.* L-system representation[^o4]

This curve is built iteratively by applying the Odd–Even Drawing rule to the [Fibonacci word](wikipedia://en/1702139) 0100101001001...:

For each digit at position *k*:

1. If the digit is 0:
   - Draw a line segment then turn 90° to the left if *k* is [even](wikipedia://en/143135)
   - Draw a line segment then turn 90° to the right if *k* is [odd](wikipedia://en/143135)
1. If the digit is 1:
   - Draw a line segment and stay straight

To a Fibonacci word of length $F_n$ (the *n*<sup>th</sup> [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number)) is associated a curve $\mathcal{F}_n$ made of $F_n$ segments. The curve displays three different aspects whether *n* is in the form 3*k*, 3*k* + 1, or 3*k* + 2.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: FWF whites quares.png.* The Fibonacci numbers in the Fibonacci word fractal.

Some of the Fibonacci word fractal's properties include:[^o7][^o8]

- The curve $\mathcal{F_n}$ contains $F_n$ segments, $F_{n-1}$ right angles and $F_{n-2}$ flat angles.
- The curve never self-intersects and does not contain double points. At the limit, it contains an infinity of points asymptotically close.
- The curve presents self-similarities at all scales. The reduction ratio is $1+\sqrt{2}$. This number, also called the [silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526), is present in a great number of properties listed below.
- The number of self-similarities at level *n* is a [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) \\ −1. (more precisely: $F_{3n+3}-1$).
- The curve encloses an infinity of square structures of decreasing sizes in a ratio $1+\sqrt{2}$ (see figure). The number of those square structures is a Fibonacci number.
- The curve $\mathcal{F}_n$can also be constructed in different ways (see gallery below):
  - [Iterated function system](wikipedia://en/505225) of 4 and 1 homothety of ratio $1/(1+\sqrt2)$ and $1/(1+\sqrt2)^2$
  - By joining the curves $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$ and $\mathcal{F}_{n-2}$
  - [Lindenmayer system](wikipedia://en/46565)
  - By an iterated construction of 8 square patterns around each square pattern.
  - By an iterated construction of [octagons](wikipedia://en/314575)
- The [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) of the Fibonacci word fractal is $3\,\frac{\log\varphi}{\log(1+\sqrt 2)}\approx 1.6379$, with $\varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).
- Generalizing to an angle $\alpha$ between 0 and $\pi/2$, its Hausdorff dimension is $3\,\frac{\log\varphi}{\log(1+a+\sqrt{(1+a)^2+1})}$, with $a=\cos\alpha$.
- The Hausdorff dimension of its frontier is $\frac{\log 3}{{\log(1+\sqrt 2})}\approx 1.2465$.
- Exchanging the roles of "0" and "1" in the Fibonacci word, or in the drawing rule yields a similar curve, but oriented 45°.
- From the Fibonacci word, one can define the «dense Fibonacci word», on an alphabet of 3 letters: 102210221102110211022102211021102110221022102211021... . The usage, on this word, of a more simple drawing rule, defines an infinite set of variants of the curve, among which:
  - a "diagonal variant"
  - a "svastika variant"
  - a "compact variant"
- It is [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that the Fibonacci word fractal appears for every [sturmian word](wikipedia://en/1701956) for which the slope, written in [continued fraction expansion](wikipedia://en/739199), ends with an infinite sequence of "1"s.

:::

## The Fibonacci tile {#The_Fibonacci_tile}

*Image: Tiling Fibonacci word fractal.png.* Imperfect tiling by the Fibonacci tile. The area of the central square tends to zero.

The juxtaposition of four $F_{3k}$ curves allows the construction of a closed curve enclosing a surface whose [area](wikipedia://en/1209) is not null. This curve is called a "Fibonacci tile".

- The Fibonacci tile almost tiles the plane. The juxtaposition of 4 tiles (see illustration) leaves at the center a free square whose area tends to zero as *k* tends to infinity. At the limit, the infinite Fibonacci tile tiles the plane.
- If the tile is enclosed in a square of side 1, then its area tends to $2-\sqrt{2} = 0.5857$.

*Image: Tiling by Fibonacci tiles.jpg.* Perfect tiling by the Fibonacci snowflake

### Fibonacci snowflake {#Fibonacci_snowflake}

*Image: Fibonacci snowflakes 2 1, 2, 3, and 4.svg.* Fibonacci snowflakes for *i* = 2 for *n* = 1 through 4: $\sideset{}{_1^\left [ 2 \right ] \quad}\prod$, $\sideset{}{_2^\left [ 2 \right ] \quad}\prod$, $\sideset{}{_3^\left [ 2 \right ] \quad}\prod$, $\sideset{}{_4^\left [ 2 \right ] \quad}\prod$[^o13]

The **Fibonacci snowflake** is a Fibonacci tile defined by:[^o14]

- $q_n = q_{n-1}q_{n-2}$ if $n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$
- $q_n = q_{n-1}\overline{q}_{n-2}$ otherwise.

with $q_0=\epsilon$ and $q_1=R$, $L =$ "turn left" and $R =$ "turn right", and $\overline{R} = L$.

Several remarkable properties:[^o14][^o15]

- It is the Fibonacci tile associated to the "diagonal variant" previously defined.
- It tiles the plane at any order.
- It tiles the plane by translation in two different ways.
- its [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) at order *n* equals $4F(3n+1)$, where $F(n)$ is the *n*<sup>th</sup> [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number).
- its area at order *n* follows the successive indexes of odd row of the [Pell sequence](wikipedia://en/1355482) (defined by $P(n)=2P(n-1)+P(n-2)$).

:::
