# Formally smooth map

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formally smooth map"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $f:A\to B$ is called **formally smooth** (from [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Formellement lisse*) if it satisfies the following infinitesimal [lifting](wikipedia://en/10504570) property:

:::

Suppose *B* is given the structure of an *A*-algebra via the map *f*. Given a [commutative](wikipedia://en/245990) *A*-algebra, *C*, and a [nilpotent ideal](wikipedia://en/6389159) $N\subseteq C$, any *A*-algebra homomorphism $B\to C/N$ may be lifted to an *A*-algebra map $B \to C$. If moreover any such lifting is unique, then *f* is said to be **[formally étale](wikipedia://en/30285123)**.

Formally smooth maps were defined by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) in *[Éléments de géométrie algébrique](wikipedia://en/471387)* IV.

For finitely presented morphisms, formal smoothness is equivalent to [usual notion of smoothness](wikipedia://en/7837041).

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Smooth morphisms {#Smooth_morphisms}

All smooth morphisms $f:X\to S$ are equivalent to morphisms locally of finite presentation which are formally smooth. Hence formal smoothness is a slight generalization of smooth morphisms.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Non-example"}

### Non-example {#Non-example}

One method for detecting formal smoothness of a scheme is using [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) lifting criterion. For example, using the truncation morphism $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^3) \to k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ the infinitesimal lifting criterion can be described using the commutative square

> $$\begin{matrix} X & \leftarrow & \text{Spec}\left(\frac{k[\varepsilon]}{(\varepsilon^2)}\right) \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ S & \leftarrow & \text{Spec}\left(\frac{k[\varepsilon]}{(\varepsilon^3)}\right) \end{matrix}$$

where $X,S \in Sch/S$. For example, if

> $X = \text{Spec}\left( \frac{k[x,y]}{(xy)} \right)$ and $Y = \text{Spec}(k)$

then consider the [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) at the origin $(0,0) \in X(k)$ given by the ring morphism

> $$\frac{k[x,y]}{(xy)} \to \frac{k[\varepsilon]}{(\varepsilon^2)}$$

sending

> $$\begin{aligned} x &\mapsto \varepsilon \\ y &\mapsto \varepsilon \end{aligned}$$

Note because $xy \mapsto \varepsilon^2 = 0$, this is a valid morphism of commutative rings. Then, since a lifting of this morphism to

> $$\text{Spec}\left(\frac{k[\varepsilon]}{(\varepsilon^3)}\right) \to X$$

is of the form

> $$\begin{aligned} x &\mapsto \varepsilon + a\varepsilon^2 \\ y &\mapsto \varepsilon + b\varepsilon^2 \end{aligned}$$

and $xy \mapsto \varepsilon^2 + (a+b)\varepsilon^3= \varepsilon^2$, there cannot be an infinitesimal lift since this is non-zero, hence $X \in Sch/k$ is not formally smooth. This also proves this morphism is not smooth from the equivalence between formally smooth morphisms locally of finite presentation and smooth morphisms.
