# Rupert G. Miller

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rupert G. Miller"}

**Rupert Griel Miller, Jr.** (January 31, 1933 – March 15, 1986) was an American statistician known for his work on [multiple comparison](wikipedia://en/9444220)[^o3] and [jackknife resampling](wikipedia://en/9158134).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Miller was born in [Lancaster, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Pennsylvania) and attended the [Hill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_School). He entered [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1950, where he was a member of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) and the university's rowing team.[^o7] He graduated from Princeton with a BSc in mathematics in 1954 and moved to [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) to continue his study. He received his PhD in statistics at Stanford under [Samuel Karlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Karlin) in 1958. Subsequently, he taught at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) briefly before joining the statistics faculty at Stanford University in 1959 as an assistant professor. Miller was promoted to associate professor in 1962 and full professor in 1967. Between 1967 and 1972, he served as an associate editor for the [Journal of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/16179313), and from 1977 to 1979, he held the editor position for the [Annals of Statistics](wikipedia://en/22160768). His students include [Bradley Efron](wikipedia://en/7250868) and [Nancy Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Reid),[^o8] [Gabrielle Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrielle_Kelly), among others. His entire collection of papers and communications are archived by Stanford University.[^o9]

Miller became a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1968, and the following year, he became a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988). He was the editor-in-chief of the journal [Annals of Statistics](wikipedia://en/22160768) between 1977 and 1979.[^o10]

Miller's wife was Barbara J. Bonesteele Miller, an education analyst and a Stanford University alumna.[^o11] In 1983, Miller was diagnosed with a rare form of [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma) and died three years later in 1986 at [Stanford, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California).[^o5]
