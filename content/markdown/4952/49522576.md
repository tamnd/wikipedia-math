# Whittle likelihood

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whittle likelihood"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Whittle likelihood** is an approximation to the [likelihood function](wikipedia://en/44968) of a stationary Gaussian [time series](wikipedia://en/406624). It is named after the mathematician and statistician [Peter Whittle](wikipedia://en/2600214), who introduced it in his PhD thesis in 1951.[^o2] It is commonly used in [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis) and [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) for parameter estimation and signal detection.

:::

## Context {#Context}

In a [stationary Gaussian time series model](wikipedia://en/302944), the [likelihood function](wikipedia://en/44968) is (as usual in Gaussian models) a function of the associated mean and covariance parameters. With a large number ($N$) of observations, the ($N \times N$) covariance matrix may become very large, making computations very costly in practice. However, due to stationarity, the covariance matrix has a rather simple structure, and by using an approximation, computations may be simplified considerably (from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N\log(N))$).[^o4] The idea effectively boils down to assuming a [heteroscedastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroscedasticity) zero-mean Gaussian model in [Fourier domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_domain); the model formulation is based on the time series' [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811) and its [power spectral density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectral_density).[^o5][^o6][^o7]

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_N$ be a stationary Gaussian time series with (*one-sided*) power spectral density $S_1(f)$, where $N$ is even and samples are taken at constant sampling intervals $\Delta_t$. Let $\tilde{X}_1,\ldots,\tilde{X}_{N/2+1}$ be the (complex-valued) [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811) (DFT) of the time series. Then for the Whittle likelihood one effectively assumes independent zero-mean [Gaussian distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution) for all $\tilde{X}_j$ with variances for the real and imaginary parts given by

$$\operatorname{Var}\left(\operatorname{Re}(\tilde{X}_j)\right) = \operatorname{Var} \left( \operatorname{Im} (\tilde{X}_j)\right) = S_1(f_j)$$

where $f_j=\frac j {N \, \Delta_t}$ is the $j$th Fourier frequency. This approximate model immediately leads to the (logarithmic) likelihood function

$$\log\left(P(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\right) \propto -\sum_j \left( \log\left( S_1(f_j) \right) + \frac{|\tilde{x}_j|^2}{\frac N {2\,\Delta_t} S_1(f_j)} \right)$$

where $|\cdot|$ denotes the absolute value with the [periodogram](wikipedia://en/485457) $|\tilde{x}_j|^2=\left(\operatorname{Re}(\tilde{x}_j)\right)^2 + \left( \operatorname{Im} (\tilde{x}_j)\right)^2$.[^o5][^o6][^o10]

:::

## Special case of a known noise spectrum {#Special_case_of_a_known_noise_spectrum}

In case the noise spectrum is assumed a-priori *known*, and noise properties are not to be inferred from the data, the likelihood function may be simplified further by ignoring constant terms, leading to the sum-of-squares expression

$$\log\left(P(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\right) \;\propto\; -\sum_j\frac{|\tilde{x}_j|^2}{\frac N {2 \, \Delta_t} S_1(f_j)}$$

This expression also is the basis for the common [matched filter](wikipedia://en/1135311).

## Accuracy of approximation {#Accuracy_of_approximation}

The Whittle likelihood in general is only an approximation, it is only exact if the spectrum is constant, i.e., in the trivial case of [white noise](wikipedia://en/46182). The [efficiency](wikipedia://en/27250866) of the Whittle approximation always depends on the particular circumstances.[^o13] [^o14]

Note that due to [linearity](wikipedia://en/91591) of the Fourier transform, Gaussianity in Fourier domain implies Gaussianity in time domain and vice versa. What makes the Whittle likelihood only approximately accurate is related to the [sampling theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_theorem)—the effect of Fourier-transforming only a *finite* number of data points, which also manifests itself as [spectral leakage](wikipedia://en/545288) in related problems (and which may be ameliorated using the same methods, namely, [windowing](wikipedia://en/244097)). In the present case, the implicit periodicity assumption implies correlation between the first and last samples ($x_1$ and $x_N$), which are effectively treated as "neighbouring" samples (like $x_1$ and $x_2$).

::: {#o16 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

### Parameter estimation {#Parameter_estimation}

Whittle's likelihood is commonly used to estimate signal parameters for signals that are buried in non-white noise. The [noise spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectral_density) then may be assumed known,[^o18] or it may be inferred along with the signal parameters.[^o6][^o10]

### Signal detection {#Signal_detection}

Signal detection is commonly performed with the [matched filter](wikipedia://en/1135311), which is based on the Whittle likelihood for the case of a *known* noise power spectral density.[^o20][^o21] The matched filter effectively does a [maximum-likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum-likelihood) fit of the signal to the noisy data and uses the resulting [likelihood ratio](wikipedia://en/45035) as the detection statistic.[^o22]

The matched filter may be generalized to an analogous procedure based on a [Student-t distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student-t_distribution) by also considering uncertainty (e.g. [estimation](wikipedia://en/13070117) uncertainty) in the noise spectrum. On the technical side, the [EM algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EM_algorithm) may be utilized here, effectively leading to repeated or iterative matched-filtering.[^o22]

### Spectrum estimation {#Spectrum_estimation}

The Whittle likelihood is also applicable for estimation of the [noise spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectral_density), either alone or in conjunction with signal parameters.[^o24][^o25]
