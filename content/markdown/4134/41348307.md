# Cardy formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cardy formula"}

In physics, the **Cardy formula** gives the entropy of a [two-dimensional conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913) (CFT). In recent years, this formula has been especially useful in the calculation of the entropy of [BTZ black holes](wikipedia://en/17143970) and in checking the [AdS/CFT correspondence](wikipedia://en/644443) and the [holographic principle](wikipedia://en/14286).

:::

Using results by [J. L. Cardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cardy),[^o2] the following entropy formula can be derived:

$$S=2\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{c}{6}\bigl(L_0-\tfrac{c}{24}\bigr)}.$$

Here $c$ is the [central charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_charge), $L_0 = ER$ is the product of the total energy and radius of the system, and the shift of $c/24$ is related to the [Casimir effect](wikipedia://en/7555). These data emerge from the [Virasoro algebra](wikipedia://en/348029) of this CFT. The proof of the above formula relies on [modular invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_invariance) of a Euclidean CFT on the torus.

The Cardy formula is usually understood as counting the number of states of energy $\Delta = L_0 + \bar{L}_0$ of a CFT quantized on a circle. To be precise, the [microcanonical](wikipedia://en/1136447) entropy (that is to say, the logarithm of the number of states in a shell of width $\delta \lesssim 1$) is given by

$$S_\delta(\Delta) = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{c\Delta}{3}} + O(\ln \Delta)$$

in the limit $\Delta \to \infty$. This formula can be turned into a rigorous bound.[^o3]

In 2000, [E. Verlinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Verlinde) extended this to certain strongly-coupled (n+1)-dimensional CFTs.[^o4] The resulting **Cardy–Verlinde formula** was obtained by studying a radiation-dominated universe with the [Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric](wikipedia://en/343031)

$$ds^2=-dt^2+R^2(t)\Omega^2_n$$

where R is the radius of a n-dimensional sphere at time t. The radiation is represented by a (n+1)-dimensional CFT. The entropy of that CFT is then given by the formula

$$S=\frac{2\pi R}{n}\sqrt{E_c(2E-E_c)},$$

where E<sub>c</sub> is the Casimir effect, and E the total energy. The above reduced formula gives the maximal entropy

$$S\le S_{max}=\frac{2\pi RE}{n},$$

when E<sub>c</sub>=E, which is the [Bekenstein bound](wikipedia://en/1482061). The Cardy–Verlinde formula was later shown by Kutasov and Larsen[^o5] to be invalid for weakly interacting CFTs. In fact, since the entropy of higher dimensional (meaning n>1) CFTs is dependent on exactly marginal couplings, it is believed that a Cardy formula for the entropy is not achievable when n>1.
