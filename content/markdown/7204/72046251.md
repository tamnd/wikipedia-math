# Soddy line

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Soddy line"}

The **Soddy line** of a triangle is the line that goes through the centers of the two [Soddy circles](wikipedia://en/74395167) of that triangle.

:::

The Soddy line intersects the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) in the [de Longchamps point](wikipedia://en/14593776) and the [Gergonne line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_line) in the *Fletcher point*. It is also perpendicular to the Gergonne line and together all three lines form the [Euler-Gergonne-Soddy triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler-Gergonne-Soddy_triangle). The [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point) and the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) of the triangle are located on the Soddy line as well.

The line is named after Nobel laureate [Frederick Soddy](wikipedia://en/11778), who published a proof of a special case of [Descartes' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes'_theorem) about tangent circles as a poem in [Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29) in 1936.
