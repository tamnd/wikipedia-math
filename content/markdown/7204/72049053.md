# TUM School of Computation, Information and Technology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="TUM School of Computation, Information and Technology"}

The **TUM School of Computation, Information and Technology** (CIT) is a school of the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), established in 2022 by the merger of three former departments. As of 2022, it is structured into the Department of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the Department of [Computer Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_engineering), the Department of [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), and the Department of [Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering).

:::

## Department of Mathematics {#Department_of_Mathematics}

The Department of Mathematics (MATH) is located at the [Garching campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUM_Campus_Garching).

### History {#History}

[Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) was taught from the beginning at the *Polytechnische Schule in München* and the later *Technische Hochschule München*. [Otto Hesse](wikipedia://en/740995) was the department's first professor for [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), [analytical geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) and [analytical mechanics](wikipedia://en/454450). Over the years, several institutes for mathematics were formed.

In 1974, the Institute of Geometry was merged with the Institute of Mathematics to form the Department of Mathematics, and [informatics](wikipedia://en/23997153), which had been part of the Institute of Mathematics, became a separate department.[^o5]

## Department of Computer Science {#Department_of_Computer_Science}

The Department of Computer Science (CS) is located at the [Garching campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUM_Campus_Garching).

### History {#History}

The first courses in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at the Technical University of Munich were offered in 1967 at the Department of Mathematics, when [Friedrich L. Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_L._Bauer) introduced a two-semester lecture titled *Information Processing*. In 1968, [Klaus Samelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Samelson) started offering a second lecture cycle titled *Introduction to Informatics*.[^o10] By 1992, the computer science department had separated from the Department of Mathematics to form an independent Department of Informatics.[^o10]

In 2002, the department relocated from its old campus in the Munich city center to the new building on the [Garching campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUM_Campus_Garching).[^o10]

In 2017, the Department celebrated *50 Years of Informatics Munich* with a series of lectures and ceremonies, together with [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich) and the [University of the Bundeswehr Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Bundeswehr_Munich).[^o10]

### Notable people {#Notable_people}

Seven faculty members of the Department of Informatics have been awarded the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize), one of the highest endowed research prizes in Germany with a maximum of €2.5 million per award:

- 2020 – [Thomas Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Neumann)
- 2016 – [Daniel Cremers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Cremers)
- 2008 – [Susanne Albers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Albers)
- 1997 – [Ernst Mayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mayr_%28computer_scientist%29)
- 1995 – [Gerd Hirzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_Hirzinger)
- 1994 – [Manfred Broy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Broy)
- 1991 – Karl-Heinz Hoffmann (mathematician)

[Friedrich L. Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_L._Bauer) was awarded the 1988 [IEEE Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Computer_Society) [Computer Pioneer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Pioneer_Award) for inventing the [stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_%28abstract_data_type%29) data structure. [Gerd Hirzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_Hirzinger) was awarded the 2005 [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) Robotics and Automation Society Pioneer Award. Hans-Arno Jacobsen and [Burkhard Rost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkhard_Rost) were awarded the [Alexander von Humboldt Professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Professorship) in 2011 and 2008, respectively. [Rudolf Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Bayer) was known for inventing the [B-tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-tree) and [Red–black tree](wikipedia://en/26397).

## Department of Electrical Engineering {#Department_of_Electrical_Engineering}

The Department of Electrical Engineering (EE) is partly located at the [Munich campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUM_Campus_Munich) but expected to move entirely to Garching.

### History {#History}

*Image: TUM-N2 Muenchen-02.jpg.* Electrical Engineering in the city center

The first lectures in the field of [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity) at the *Polytechnische Schule München* were given as early as 1876 by the physicist [Wilhelm von Bezold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Bezold). Over the years, as the field of [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) became increasingly important, a separate department for electrical engineering emerged within the [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) department. In 1967, the department was renamed the Faculty of Mechanical and Electrical Engineering, and six electrical engineering departments were permanently established.

In April 1974, the formal establishment of the new TUM Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering took place. While still located in the [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) campus, a new building is currently in construction on the [Garching campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUM_Campus_Garching) and the department is expected to move by 2025.[^o16]

## Building {#Building}

*Image: MI-Gebäude der TU München Magistrale2.JPG.* Interior of the faculty building for the Departments of Mathematics and Computer Science

The Department of Computer Science shares a building with the [Department of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUM_Department_of_Mathematics).

In the building, two massive [parabolic](wikipedia://en/23231) [slides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playground_slide) run from the fourth floor to the ground floor. Their shape corresponds to the equation $z=y=hx^2/d^2$ and is supposed to represent the "connection of science and art".[^o23]

## Rankings {#Rankings}

The Department of Computer Science has been consistently rated the top [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) department in Germany by major rankings.[^o26][^o27][^o28] Globally, it ranks No. 29 (QS),[^o26] No. 10 (THE),[^o27] and within No. 51-75 (ARWU).[^o28] In the 2020 national [CHE University Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHE_University_Ranking), the department is among the top rated departments for [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [business informatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_informatics), being rated in the top group for the majority of criteria.[^o36]

The Department of Mathematics has been rated as one of the top [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) departments in Germany, ranking 43rd in the world and 2nd in Germany (after the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn)) in the [QS World University Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QS_World_University_Rankings), and within No. 51-75 in the [Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities).[^o30][^o29] In Statistics & Operational Research, QS ranks TUM first in Germany and 28th in the world.[^o31]

The Departments of Electrical and Computer Engineering are leading in Germany.[^o32][^o33] In Electrical & Electronic Engineering, TUM is rated 18th worldwide by [QS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QS_World_University_Rankings) and 22nd by [ARWU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities). In engineering as a whole, TUM is ranked 20th globally and 1st nationally in the [Times Higher Education World University Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Higher_Education_World_University_Rankings).[^o34]
