# Lars Lyberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lars Lyberg"}

**Lars Lyberg** (1 December 1944 – 9 March 2021)[^o2] was a Swedish statistician, head of [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) at [Statistics Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Sweden), the founding editor of the *[Journal of Official Statistics](wikipedia://en/19899707)*, and a Fellow of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) and [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988). His research focused on [data quality](wikipedia://en/1609808), [total survey error](wikipedia://en/32257005), and research in the multinational, multiregional, and multicultural contexts.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Lyberg received his PhD in statistics from the [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) where he also taught in 2003–2009 and 2010–2015.[^o4][^o5]

He began his career at [Statistics Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Sweden) in 1966 and later directed its research and development department until his retirement in 2010. During his tenure, he represented Sweden in the development of [ISO Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organization_for_Standardization) 20252 on Market, Opinion and Social Research.[^o2]

In 1985, he founded the Journal of Official Statistics and served as editor-in-chief for 25 years.[^o6]

He published numerous articles, books, conference volumes, and task force reports, most notably: *Total Survey Error: Past, Present, and Future,*[^o7] *Telephone Survey Methodology*,[^o8] *Measurement Errors in Surveys*,[^o9] *Survey Measurement and Process Quality*,[^o10] *Introduction to Survey Quality*,[^o11] *Survey Methods in Multicultural, Multinational, and Multiregional Contexts*,[^o12] *Survey Error in Practice,*[^o13] *AAPOR/WAPOR Task Force Report on Comparative Surveys*,[^o14][^o15] *and Big Data Meets Survey Science,*[^o16] and posthumously the *Handbook of Computational Social Science*[^o17] and *Improving the Measurement of Poverty and Social Exclusion in Europe: Reducing non-sampling errors*.[^o18]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He was married to Lilli Japec, a senior statistician at Statistics Sweden, and had two adopted sons from his first marriage.[^o2]

Known by his nickname "Lasse", he enjoyed American baseball, Southern cuisine, and vacationing in Florida.[^o6] He was also a [football (soccer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) fan and coach.[^o20] At the 20th anniversary of the Journal of Official Statistics, [Edith de Leeuw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_de_Leeuw) and approximately 50 statisticians presented him with a spoof titled the "Journal of Obnoxious Statistics (JOBS)".[^o21]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

Lyberg was an elected member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) and a Fellow of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664).[^o2] In 1993, he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/51140705).[^o23]

In 2012, the International Association of Survey Statisticians (IASS) recognized him with the Waksberg Award for his contributions in the area of survey quality.[^o24] In 2013, he was awarded the [Helen Dinerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Dinerman) Award from the [World Association for Public Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Association_for_Public_Opinion_Research). The [American Association for Public Opinion Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_Public_Opinion_Research) honored him in 2018 with the Award for Exceptionally Distinguished Achievement.[^o25] In recognition of his achievements in statistics for societal benefit, the Swedish Statistical Association named him 2019 statistician of the year.[^o26]

A virtual memorial was held at the 2021 [Joint Statistical Meeting](wikipedia://en/15571662) where Lyberg was remembered for his scholarship, humility, and sense of humor.[^o6] Since 2021, Demoskop has held The Lyberg Conference annually in Sweden with a YouTube live stream, covering topics concerning data collection, quality, and innovations.[^o27]
