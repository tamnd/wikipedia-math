# Decentralized finance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decentralized finance"}

**Decentralized finance** (often stylized as **DeFi**) provides [financial instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_instrument) and services through [smart contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_contract) on a programmable, permissionless [blockchain](wikipedia://en/44065971). This approach reduces the need for [intermediaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediary) such as [brokerages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokerage), [exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_%28organized_market%29), or [banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank).[^o2] DeFi platforms enable users to lend or borrow funds, speculate on asset price movements using [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_%28finance%29), trade [cryptocurrencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrencies), insure against risks, and earn [interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_%28economics%29) in savings-like accounts.[^o3] The DeFi ecosystem is built on a layered architecture and highly composable building blocks.[^o8] While some applications offer high [interest rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate),[^o3] they carry high risks.[^o4] Coding errors and hacks are a common challenge in DeFi.[^o61][^o3] DeFi protocols exhibit varying degrees of decentralization, with truly decentralized protocols potentially acting as neutral infrastructure, while false decentralization leaves protocols open to manipulation and fraud or to being regulated as [financial intermediaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_intermediary).[^o55]

:::

A core principle of DeFi is its accessibility. By operating on public blockchains, DeFi applications (commonly referred to as "dApps") enable users from across the globe to access financial services without the need for a centralized authority or compliance with traditional [Know Your Customer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_your_customer) (KYC) and [Anti-Money Laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Money_Laundering) (AML) regulations.[^o5]

DeFi also introduces programmability and composability to finance. Developers can create interoperable financial products by combining different DeFi protocols like building blocks, a concept often referred to as "money Legos".[^o6]

## History {#History}

*Image: The DeFi Stack Figure.jpg.* Multi-layered Architecture of the DeFi Stack

[Decentralized exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_exchange) (abbreviated DEXs) are alternative payment ecosystems that use new [protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_network_protocol) for financial transactions. They emerged within decentralized finance (DeFi),[^o8] a sector of [blockchain technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain_technology) and [fintech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fintech).[^o9]

Centralized exchanges (CEXs), DEXs and DEX aggregators are all built on a multi-layered DeFi architecture, with each layer serving a well-defined purpose.[^o8] (See Figure: *Multi-layered Architecture of the DeFi Stack*).

While they share common components of the first four layers, such as *the Settlement layer*, *Asset layer*, *Protocol layer* and *Application layer*, DEX aggregators have an additional component or *Aggregator layer*, which allows them to connect and interact with other DEXs via smart contracts.

The [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum) blockchain popularized [smart contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_contract), which are the basis of DeFi, in 2017. Other blockchains have since implemented smart contracts. Alternative Layer 1 protocols, such as Radix, have been developed utilizing sharded consensus mechanisms to attempt to resolve scalability issues inherent in earlier blockchains.[^o10]

, MakerDAO was a prominent lending DeFi platform based on a [stablecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stablecoin) that was established in 2017.[^o11][^o12] It allowed users to borrow [DAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_%28cryptocurrency%29), a token pegged to the [US dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar). Through a set of smart contracts that govern the loan, repayment, and liquidation processes, MakerDAO aimed to maintain the stable value of DAI in a [decentralized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralization) and autonomous manner.[^o13][^o14] In September 2024, MakerDAO rebranded as Sky, and its stablecoin DAI was renamed USDS.

In June 2020, Compound Finance, a decentralized finance protocol enabling users to lend or borrow cryptocurrency assets and which provides typical [interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_%28economics%29) payments to lenders, started rewarding lenders and borrowers with a [cryptocurrency](wikipedia://en/36662188) called Comp. This token, which is used for running Compound, can also be traded on [cryptocurrency exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_exchange). Other platforms followed suit, leading to stacked investment opportunities known as "yield farming" or "liquidity mining", where speculators shift cryptocurrency assets between pools in a platform and between platforms to maximize their total [yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yield_%28finance%29), which includes not only [interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_%28economics%29) and fees but also the value of additional tokens received as rewards.[^o15]

In July 2020, *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* described decentralized finance techniques and the risks involved.[^o15] In September 2020, [Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News) said that DeFi made up two-thirds of the [cryptocurrency](wikipedia://en/36662188) market in terms of price changes and that DeFi collateral levels had reached \$9 billion.[^o16] Ethereum saw a rise in developers during 2020 due to the increased interest in DeFi.[^o17]

DeFi has attracted [venture capitalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital) such as [Andreessen Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreessen_Horowitz)[^o4] and [Michael Novogratz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Novogratz).[^o18]

*The Economist* regarded the future of digital finance in 2022 as a "three-way fight" between: [Big Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Tech), such as [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook) with its digital wallet; "big rich countries" that have been testing their own digital currencies; and software developers "building all sorts of applications" to decentralize finance. Handling the risks presented by crypto-assets already valued at \$2.5 trillion was a particular challenge for US regulators.[^o19]

The evolution of Decentralized Finance (DeFi) began with Bitcoin in 2009 and matured with Ethereum’s 2015 launch, which introduced smart contracts and enabled decentralized applications. By 2021, DeFi protocols such as MakerDAO, Compound, [Aave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aave), and [Uniswap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniswap) had driven total value locked (TVL) to over \$180 billion.[^o20] The 2022 crypto market crash, which followed events like the collapse of Terra/LUNA and [FTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTX), exposed critical vulnerabilities in the DeFi space and triggered a downturn. In 2023, DeFi began recovering. A key trend was the tokenization of real-world assets (RWA) such as treasury bills, real estate, and carbon credits. Platforms like MakerDAO and Centrifuge enabled institutional investors to access on-chain yield tied to off-chain assets.[^o21] Layer 2 scaling solutions such as Arbitrum and [Optimism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimism_%28blockchain%29) also gained traction, reducing transaction fees and increasing throughput on top of Ethereum.[^o6] This period also marked the rise of intent-based DeFi, allowing users to specify desired outcomes, with protocols handling transaction logic (Bankless, 2023). In 2024, regulatory developments shaped DeFi's evolution. The European Union’s MiCA framework ([Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markets_in_Crypto-Assets)) came into effect, impacting stablecoin issuers and custodial services.[^o22][^o23] In response, many DeFi projects began adopting hybrid models, combining decentralized architecture with off-chain compliance layers to navigate legal requirements. Meanwhile, interoperability improved with the deployment of cross-chain bridges like Wormhole V2 and [Chainlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chainlink_%28blockchain_oracle%29) CCIP, enabling liquidity to flow between Ethereum, [Solana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solana_%28blockchain_platform%29), [Avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalanche_%28blockchain_platform%29), and other ecosystems.

## Key characteristics {#Key_characteristics}

DeFi uses [decentralized applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_application), also known as DApps, that perform financial functions on [distributed ledgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_ledger) called blockchains, a technology that was made popular by [bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265).[^o3] Transactions are directly made between participants, mediated by smart contract programs, rather than through a centralized intermediary.[^o4] These smart contracts, or DeFi protocols, typically run using [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_source) software.[^o26]

DApps are typically accessed through a browser extension or application. For example, [MetaMask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetaMask) allows users to interact with Ethereum through a digital wallet.[^o27][^o28] Many of these DApps can be linked to create complex financial services.[^o3] Examples include lending protocols, in which [stablecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stablecoin) holders can lend assets to a liquidity pool, and allow others to borrow those digital assets by depositing their own collateral.[^o29] The protocol is designed to automatically adjusts interest rates based on the demand for the asset.[^o4] Some DApps source external (off-chain) data, such as the price of an asset, through [blockchain oracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain_oracle).[^o30]

The [Aave protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aave) popularized "flash loans", which are uncollateralized loans of an arbitrary amount that are taken out and paid back within a single blockchain transaction.[^o31] Exploits of DeFi platforms have used flash loans to manipulate cryptocurrency spot prices.[^o32]

Another DeFi protocol is [Uniswap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniswap), which is a [decentralized exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_exchange) (DEX) set up to trade tokens issued on Ethereum. Rather than using a centralized exchange to fill orders, Uniswap pays users to form liquidity pools in exchange for a percentage of the fees collected from traders swapping tokens in and out of the liquidity pools. Because no centralized party runs Uniswap and any development team can use the open-source software, there is no entity to check the identities of the people using the platform and meet [KYC/AML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_your_customer) regulations. As of 2020, it was not clear what position regulators would take on the legality of such platforms;[^o33] subsequent regulatory developments are summarised in the Regulation section.

## Decentralized exchanges {#Decentralized_exchanges}

Decentralized exchanges (DEX) are a type of [cryptocurrency exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_exchange), which allow for either direct [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer), or Automated Market Maker (AMM) liquidity pool cryptocurrency transactions to take place without the need for an intermediary. The lack of an intermediary differentiates them from centralized exchanges (CEX).[^o8]

In transactions made through decentralized exchanges, the typical third party entities which would normally oversee the security and transfer of [assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset) (e.g. [banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank), [stockbrokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockbroker), online [payment gateways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_gateways), government [institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution), etc.) are substituted by a [blockchain](wikipedia://en/44065971) or [distributed ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_ledger). Some common methods of operation include the use of [smart contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_contracts) or [order book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_book) relaying –although numerous other variations are possible, with differing degrees of [decentralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralization).[^o36][^o37]

### Advantages {#Advantages}

Because traders on a decentralized exchange often do not need to transfer their assets to the exchange before executing a trade, decentralized exchanges reduce the risk of theft from [hacking of exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_and_security),[^o39] but liquidity providers do need to transfer tokens to the decentralized exchange. Decentralized exchanges are also more anonymous than exchanges that implement [know your customer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_your_customer) (KYC) requirements.[^o40][^o41]

As of 2018, there were signs that decentralized exchanges had been suffering from low trading volumes and reduced [market liquidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_liquidity).[^o42] The 0x project, a protocol for building decentralized exchanges with interchangeable liquidity, attempted to solve this issue.[^o43]

As of mid‑2025, weekly trading volume on decentralized exchanges (DEXs) averaged around \$18.6 billion, with more than 9.7 million unique wallets interacting with DeFi protocols.[^o44]

### Disadvantages {#Disadvantages}

Due to a lack of [KYC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_your_customer) processes, and no way to revert a transaction, users are at a loss if they are ever hacked for their passwords or private keys.[^o46] Liquidity providers staking in DeFi protocols may also suffer an impermanent loss when the relative value of the deposited token pair has shifted by the time the deposit is withdrawn.[^o47][^o48][^o49]

Although liquidity pool DEX are the most widely used, they may have some drawbacks. The most common problems of liquidity pool DEXes are [market price impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_impact), [slippage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slippage_%28finance%29), and [front running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_running).

Price impact arises from the design of automated market makers: the larger the trade, the greater its effect on the pool's prevailing price. For example, if the [constant product AMM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant_function_market_maker) is in use, every deal must keep the product xy = k constant, where x and y are quantities of two cryptocurrencies (or tokens) in the pool. Price impact is non-linear, so the larger is the input amount Δx, the lower is the final ratio y / x that gives an exchange price. The problem is mostly significant for relatively large deals versus the liquidity pool size.[^o50]

Front running is a special type of attack in public blockchains when some participant (usually a miner) seeing an upcoming trading transaction puts his own transaction ahead (playing with a transaction fee for example), making the initial transaction less profitable or even reverted. To provide some protection against front running attacks, many DeFi exchanges offer a slippage tolerance option for end-users. This setting allows users to set a limit on the worst acceptable execution price relative to the price observed at the time of transaction signing; if the executed price would breach that limit, the transaction reverts.[^o50]

Decentralized exchanges are particularly exposed to [cyberattacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberattack), and DEX-related incidents account for a major share of crypto-exchange losses.[^o51][^o52] In 2025, at least 20 incidents were reported, resulting in aggregate losses of \$535 million.[^o53]

### Degrees of decentralization {#Degrees_of_decentralization}

Decentralized Finance protocols exhibit varying degrees of decentralization, which largely depend on the architecture of their underlying smart contracts and external dependencies. When the protocol's smart contracts are deployed statically, such that the protocol's logic cannot be altered and no access-restricted functions are present, they may potentially function as neutral infrastructure. Conversely, if the smart contracts are upgradeable or permit the modification of key parameters, or if key functions are restricted to a specific set of users, this neutrality can be compromised.[^o55][^o55]

A decentralized exchange can still have centralized components, whereby some control of the exchange is still in the hands of a central authority. The governance of a DeFi platform, typically as part of a [Decentralized Autonomous Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_autonomous_organization), is done through tokens that grant voting rights and are distributed amongst participants. However, the majority of these tokens are often held by few individuals and are rarely used to vote.[^o56]

In July 2018, the decentralized exchange [Bancor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bancor) was reportedly hacked and suffered a loss of \$23.5 million in assets before freezing funds.[^o57] In a Twitter tweet, [Charlie Lee](wikipedia://en/57371211), the creator of [Litecoin](wikipedia://en/37395085), spoke out and claimed an exchange cannot be decentralized if it can lose or freeze customer funds.[^o58]

Operators of decentralized exchanges can face legal consequences from government regulators. One example is the founder of EtherDelta, who in November 2018 settled charges with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) over operating an unregistered securities exchange.[^o59]

## Errors and hacking {#Errors_and_hacking}

Coding errors and hacks are common in DeFi.[^o61][^o3] Blockchain transactions are irreversible, which means that an incorrect or fraudulent DeFi transaction cannot be corrected easily if at all.

The person or entity behind a DeFi protocol may be unknown and may disappear with investors' money.[^o26] Investor Michael Novogratz has described some DeFi protocols as "[Ponzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponzi_scheme)-like".[^o18]

DeFi has been compared to the [initial coin offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_coin_offering) craze of 2017, part of a [cryptocurrency bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_bubble). Inexperienced investors are at particular risk of losing money because of the sophistication required to interact with DeFi platforms and the lack of any intermediary with customer support.[^o61][^o62] On the other hand, as the code for DeFi smart contracts is generally [open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) that can be copied to set up competing platforms, experienced users and user-created bots might create instabilities as funds shift between platforms which share the same code.[^o26] In addition, DeFi platforms might inadvertently provide incentives for cryptocurrency miners to destabilize the system.[^o63]

Illicit actors, including cyber criminals, fraudsters and terrorist organisations, are exploiting the technical complexity of DeFi and the absence of [AML/CFT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti–money_laundering) checks to launder criminal proceeds or raise funds and crypto-assets to finance criminal activity. They use DeFi to obscure the movement of funds through various techniques and services.[^o64] In 2021, half of cryptocurrency crime was related to DeFi. This rise has been attributed to a combination of developer incompetence and non-existent or poorly enforced regulations.[^o65][^o66][^o67] Theft from DeFi can come from either external hackers stealing from vulnerable projects, or "rug pulls",[^o68] where the developers and influencers promote a project and then take the money, as a form of [pump-and-dump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump-and-dump).[^o67]

## Regulation {#Regulation}

In October 2021, the [FATF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force) included DeFi in the guidance for crypto service providers, making the authority's aim to regulate this type of asset. They are expecting each individual country to determine if individuals involved in DeFi can be considered a virtual asset provider and be subjected to the FATF's guidelines.[^o70][^o71]
