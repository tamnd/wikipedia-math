# William Edge (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Edge"}

**William Leonard Edge** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (8 November 1904 – 27 September 1997) was a British mathematician most known for his work in [finite geometry](wikipedia://en/267061). Students knew him as **WLE**.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Stockport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockport) to schoolteacher parents (his father William Henry Edge being a headmaster), Edge attended Stockport Grammar School before winning a place at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1923 with an entrance scholarship, later graduating MA DSc. In 1928 Trinity College made him a Research Fellow and he was also an Allen Scholar.

William Edge was a [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) student of [H. F. Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._F._Baker) at Cambridge. Edge's dissertation extended [Luigi Cremona](wikipedia://en/1846002)’s 1868 delineation of the [quadric](wikipedia://en/145570) [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158) in projective 3-space **RP**<sup>3</sup>. Edge made a "systematic classification of the quintic and sextic ruled surfaces of three-dimensional projective space."[^o3][^o4][^o5]

In 1932 [E. T. Whittaker](wikipedia://en/1077330) invited Edge to lecture at [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). An anachronism, Edge never drove a motor car and disdained the mass-media of radio and television; he was distressed by the decline of school geometry.[^o3] In 1949 he became Reader, and professor in 1969.

In the 1950s Edge began to explore [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over [Galois fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_field) as an entry to [finite geometry](wikipedia://en/267061). Points and lines of finite [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) arise as lines and planes in these spaces, and the projectivities of these spaces provide representation of some [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707). For example, in 1954 he described the space *S* over GF(3): 40 points, 13 in each plane and 4 on each line. In *S* he described a 16-point quadric with two [reguli](wikipedia://en/52079701) of four lines each.[^o6] He also extended work of Moore, Jordan and Dickson on the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) A<sub>8</sub> as represented by the [projective special linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) PSL(4,2).[^o7] The next year he parametrized the lines of the space S over GF(3) in analogy to the [Klein quadric](wikipedia://en/2933091) description of lines in **RP**<sup>3</sup>.[^o8] Edge's student [James Hirschfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_William_Peter_Hirschfeld) has advanced the science of finite geometry also.

In 1934 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were Sir [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [Herbert Westren Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Westren_Turnbull), [Edward Thomas Copson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Thomas_Copson) and [David Gibb](wikipedia://en/50969533). He won the Society's Keith Prize for 1943–45.[^o9]

Edge retired in 1975. A lifelong bachelor and devout Roman Catholic, Edge spent his final years in the care of the [Sisters of Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_Nazareth) House in [Bonnyrigg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnyrigg), just south of Edinburgh, and died there on 27 September 1997.[^o3]

Since 2013, every year the School of Mathematics of the University of Edinburgh celebrates the EDGE Days, an annual one-week workshop in algebraic geometry named after Edge.[^o10]
