# Kitaev chain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kitaev chain"}

In [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics), the **Kitaev chain** or **Kitaev–Majorana chain** is a simplified model for a [topological superconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_superconductor). It models a one dimensional lattice featuring [Majorana bound states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorana_fermion#Majorana_bound_states). The Kitaev chain has been used as a [toy model](wikipedia://en/701142) of semiconductor nanowires for the development of [topological quantum computers](wikipedia://en/4496518).[^o2][^o3] The model was first proposed by [Alexei Kitaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Kitaev) in 2000.[^o4]

:::

## Description {#Description}

### Hamiltonian {#Hamiltonian}

The [tight binding](wikipedia://en/3094328) [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381) of a Kitaev chain considers a one dimensional lattice with *N* site and spinless particles at zero temperature, subjected to nearest neighbour hopping interactions, given in [second quantization](wikipedia://en/418156) formalism as[^o7]

$$H=-\mu\sum_{j=1}^N \left(c_j^\dagger c_j-\frac12\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left[-t\left(c_{j+1}^\dagger c_j+c_j^\dagger c_{j+1}\right)+|\Delta|\left(c_{j+1}^\dagger c_j^\dagger+c_j c_{j+1}\right)\right]$$

There$\mu$ is the [chemical potential](wikipedia://en/218628), $c_j^\dagger,c_j$ are [creation and annihilation operators](wikipedia://en/701991), $t\geq 0$ the energy needed for a particle to hop from one location of the lattice to another, $\Delta=|\Delta|e^{i\theta}$ is the induced superconducting gap (p-wave pairing) and $\theta$ is the coherent superconducting phase. This Hamiltonian has particle-hole symmetry, as well as [time reversal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_reversal_symmetry).[^o8]

The Hamiltonian can be rewritten using Majorana operators, given by[^o7]

$$\left\{ \begin{matrix} \gamma^{\rm A}_j=c_j^\dagger+c_j\\ \gamma^{\rm B}_j=i(c_j^\dagger-c_j) \end{matrix} \right.,$$

which can be thought as the real and imaginary parts of the annihilation operator $c_j=\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\rm A}_j+i\gamma^{\rm B}_j)$. These Majorana operators are [Hermitian operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_operator), and [anticommutate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutator),

$$\{\gamma^\alpha_j,\gamma^\beta_k\}=2 \delta_{jk}\delta_{\alpha \beta}.$$

Using these operators the Hamiltonian can be rewritten as[^o7]

$$H=-\frac{i\mu}{2}\sum_{j=1}^N \gamma^{\rm B}_j \gamma^{\rm A}_j+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\omega_+\gamma^{\rm B}_j\gamma^{\rm A}_{j+1}+\omega_-\gamma^{\rm B}_{j+1}\gamma^{\rm A}_j\right)$$

where $\omega_\pm=|\Delta|\pm t$.

### Trivial phase {#Trivial_phase}

In the limit $t=|\Delta|\to 0$, we obtain the following Hamiltonian

$$H=-\frac{i\mu}{2}\sum_{j=1}^N \gamma^{\rm B}_j \gamma^{\rm A}_j$$

where the Majorana operators are coupled on the same site. This condition is considered a topologically trivial phase.[^o8]

### Non-trivial phase {#Non-trivial_phase}

In the limit $\mu\to 0$ and $|\Delta|\to t$, we obtain the following Hamiltonian

$$H_{\rm M}=it\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\gamma^{\rm B}_j\gamma^{\rm A}_{j+1}$$

where every Majorana operator is coupled to a Majorana operator of a different kind in the next site. By assigning a new fermion operator $\tilde{c}_j=\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\rm B}_j+i\gamma^{\rm A}_{j+1})$, the Hamiltonian is diagonalized, as

$$H_{\rm M}=2t\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\tilde{c}^\dagger_j \tilde{c}_j+\frac{1}{2}\right)$$

which describes a new set of *N*-1 [Bogoliubov quasiparticles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogoliubov_quasiparticle) with energy *t*. The missing mode given by $\tilde{c}_{\rm M}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\rm B}_N+i\gamma^{\rm A}_{1})$ couples the Majorana operators from the two endpoints of the chain, as this mode does not appear in the Hamiltonian, it requires zero energy. This mode is called a Majorana zero mode and is highly delocalized. As the presence of this mode does not change the total energy, the ground state is two-fold degenerate.[^o7] This condition is a topological superconducting non-trivial phase.[^o8]

The existence of the Majorana zero mode is [topologically protected from small perturbation due to symmetry considerations](wikipedia://en/36464530). For the Kitaev chain the Majorana zero mode persist as long as $\mu<2t$ and the superconducting gap is finite.[^o11] The robustness of these modes makes it a candidate for [qubits](wikipedia://en/25284) as a basis for [topological quantum computer](wikipedia://en/4496518).[^o12]

## Bulk case {#Bulk_case}

Using Bogoliubov-de Gennes formalism it can be shown that for the bulk case (infinite number of sites), that the energy yields[^o11]

$$E(k)=\pm\sqrt{(2t\cos k+\mu)^2+4|\Delta|^2\sin^2k},$$

and it is gapped, except for the case $\mu=2t$ and wave vector $k=0$. For the bulk case there are no zero modes. However a [topological invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_invariant) exists given by

$$Q=\mathrm{sign}\left\{\mathrm{pf}[iH(k=0)]\mathrm{pf}[iH(k=\pi)]\right\},$$

where $\mathrm{pf}[x]$ is the [Pfaffian](wikipedia://en/530060) operation. For $\mu>2t$, the invariant is strictly $Q=1$ and for $\mu<2t$, $Q=-1$ corresponding to the trivial and non-trivial phases from the finite chain, respectively. This relation between the topological invariant from the bulk case and the existence of Majorana zero modes in the finite case is called a bulk-edge correspondence.[^o11]

## Experimental efforts {#Experimental_efforts}

One possible realization of Kitaev chains is using semiconductor nanowires with strong [spin–orbit interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin–orbit_interaction) to break spin-degeneracy,[^o15] like [indium antimonide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indium_antimonide) or [indium arsenide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indium_arsenide).[^o16] A magnetic field can be applied to induce [Zeeman coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeman_effect) to spin polarize the wire and break [Kramers degeneracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramers_degeneracy).[^o15] The superconducting gap can be induced using [Andreev reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreev_reflection), by putting the wire in the proximity to a superconductor.[^o15][^o16] Realizations using 3D topological insulators have also been proposed.[^o16]

There is no single definitive way to test for Majorana zero modes. One proposal to experimentally observe these modes is using [scanning tunneling microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanning_tunneling_microscope).[^o16] A zero bias peak in the conductance could be the signature of a topological phase.[^o16] [Josephson effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect) between two wires in superconducting phase could also help to demonstrate these modes.[^o16]

In 2023 QuTech team from [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology) reported the realization of a poor man's Majorana, a Kitaev chain with two or three sites that produces a Majorana bound state that is not [topologically protected](wikipedia://en/36464530) and therefore only stable for a very small range of parameters.[^o2][^o3] It was obtained in a Kitaev chain consisting of two [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dot) in a superconducting [nanowire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanowire) strongly coupled by normal [tunneling](wikipedia://en/643769) and [Andreev tunneling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreev_tunneling) with the state arising when the rate of both processes match.[^o2][^o3] Some researches have raised concerns, suggesting that an alternative mechanism to that of Majorana bound states might explain the data obtained.[^o3][^o12]

In 2024, the first experiment in an optomechanical network was conducted to create a [bosonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) analogue of a Kitaev chain.[^o17]
