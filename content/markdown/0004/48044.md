# Transcendental argument for the existence of God

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transcendental argument for the existence of God"}

The **Transcendental Argument for the existence of God (TAG)** is a [transcendental](wikipedia://en/8569867) [argument](wikipedia://en/13018310) that attempts to prove the [existence of God](wikipedia://en/621169).

:::

Several distinct arguments are supposed transcendental arguments for God:

\1) A version was formulated by [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant) in his 1763 work *[The Only Possible Argument in Support of a Demonstration of the Existence of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Only_Possible_Argument_in_Support_of_a_Demonstration_of_the_Existence_of_God)* and the *[Critique of Practical Reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critique_of_Practical_Reason).* For Kant, [morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_from_morality) is best explained by the existence of God.

\2) [C. S. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._S._Lewis)'s [argument from reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_from_reason) is also a kind of transcendental argument.

\3) The term is also used by [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) [presuppositional apologetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presuppositional_apologetics) and the likes of [Cornelius Van Til](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Van_Til) and [Greg Bahnsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Bahnsen), which attempt to appeal to God as the [necessary condition](wikipedia://en/169319#Necessity) for the [possibility](wikipedia://en/1547308) of [experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience) and [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge).[^o2][^o3]

## Presuppositional apologetics {#Presuppositional_apologetics}

Presuppositional apologetics attempts to provide an alternative to [thomistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomistic) and [evidentialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidentialist) arguments.

### Criticism {#Criticism}

Bálint Békefi has argued that TAG, as an ambitious transcendental argument, is susceptible to the [Stroudian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Stroud) objection that how things must appear is not necessarily how things actually are. He also argues that the two strategies available to apologists for responding to Stroud are unsuccessful, and thus it is unlikely that TAG can overcome the Stroudian objection.[^o6]

[Internet Infidels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Infidels) co-founder Jeffery Jay Lowder has argued that the presuppositional apologetics' version of TAG is fatally flawed for numerous reasons.[^o7] First, Bahnsen fails to defend the *necessity* of Christianity instead of the mere *sufficiency* for the rational justification of the [laws of logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_Logic), the [laws of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformity_of_nature), and the [laws of morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality). In other words, such reasoning [affirms the consequent](wikipedia://en/675). Second, Bahnsen conflates "[atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism)" with "[materialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materialism)" and has really presented an argument against materialism, not an argument for Christianity. Third, Bahnsen believed that the laws of logic, laws of science, and laws of morality are [abstract objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_objects), but Christianity arguably underdetermines the relationship between God and abstract objects. Some Christian philosophers, such as [Peter van Inwagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_van_Inwagen), affirm [Platonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism) and the compatibility of God and abstract objects. But other Christian philosophers argue that Platonism is incompatible with [divine aseity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aseity). [William Lane Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lane_Craig) urges Christian philosophers to consider anti-realist theories of abstract objects.[^o8]

### Ash'ari {#Ash'ari}

Medieval [Ash'ari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash'ari) Islamic theologians formulated a type of transcendental argument based on the notion that morality, logic, etc. cannot be fully understood apart from revelation and thereby, belief in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) and the Islamic truth claims were necessary in order to interpret the external world. For [al-Ashari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ashari) and others, it does not make sense to argue against religion using a priori assumptions about morality or scientific probabilities when these can only be understood in light of divine revelation.[^o10]
