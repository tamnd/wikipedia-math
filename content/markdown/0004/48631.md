# Paracompact space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paracompact space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **paracompact space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) in which every [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) has an open [refinement](wikipedia://en/317552#Refinement) that is [locally finite](wikipedia://en/11611098). These spaces were introduced by (Dieudonné 1944). Every [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042) is paracompact.[^n1] Every paracompact [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) is [normal](wikipedia://en/48629), and a Hausdorff space is paracompact if[^n2] and only if it admits [partitions of unity](wikipedia://en/48636) subordinate to any open cover. Sometimes paracompact spaces are defined so as to always be Hausdorff.

:::

Every [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) of a paracompact space is paracompact. While compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are always closed, this is not true for paracompact subsets. A space such that every subspace of it is a paracompact space is called **hereditarily paracompact**. This is equivalent to requiring that every [open](wikipedia://en/39358) subspace be paracompact.

The notion of paracompact space is also studied in [pointless topology](wikipedia://en/24764), where it is more well-behaved. For example, the [product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_%28topology%29) of any number of paracompact [locales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locale_%28mathematics%29) is a paracompact locale, but the product of two paracompact spaces may not be paracompact.[^o5][^n3] Compare this to [Tychonoff's theorem](wikipedia://en/82739), which states that the [product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_%28topology%29) of any collection of compact topological spaces is compact. However, the product of a paracompact space and a compact space is always paracompact.

Every [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) is paracompact. A topological space is [metrizable](wikipedia://en/19738) if and only if it is a paracompact and [locally metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_metrizable_space) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637).

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A *[cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_%28set_theory%29)* of a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $X$ is a collection of [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of $X$ whose [union](wikipedia://en/47949) contains $X$. In symbols, if $U = \{U_\alpha : \alpha \in A\}$ is an [indexed family](wikipedia://en/23624339) of subsets of $X$, then $U$ is a cover of $X$ if

$$X \subseteq \bigcup_{\alpha \in A}U_{\alpha}.$$

A cover of a topological space $X$ is *[open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover)* if all its members are [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358). A *refinement* of a cover of a space $X$ is a new cover of the same space such that every set in the new cover is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of some set in the old cover. In symbols, the cover $V = \{V_\beta : \beta \in B\}$ is a refinement of the cover $U = \{U_\alpha : \alpha \in A\}$ if and only if, [for every](wikipedia://en/76174) $V_\beta$ in $V$, [there exists some](wikipedia://en/91420) $U_\alpha$ in $U$ such that $V_\beta \subseteq U_\alpha$.

An open cover of a space $X$ is *locally finite* if every point of the space has a [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28topology%29) that intersects only [finitely](wikipedia://en/11742) many sets in the cover. In symbols, $U = \{U_\alpha : \alpha \in A\}$ is locally finite if and only if, for any $x$ in $X$, there exists some neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ such that the set

$$\left\{ \alpha \in A : U_{\alpha} \cap V \neq \varnothing \right\}$$

is finite. A topological space $X$ is now said to be **paracompact** if every open cover has a locally finite open refinement.

This definition extends verbatim to locales, with the exception of locally finite: an open cover $U$ of $X$ is locally finite iff the set of opens $V$ that intersect only finitely many opens in $U$ also form a cover of $X$. Note that an open cover on a topological space is locally finite iff its a locally finite cover of the underlying locale.

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042) is paracompact.
- Every [regular](wikipedia://en/53058) [Lindelöf space](wikipedia://en/310480) is paracompact, by [Michael's theorem](wikipedia://en/78587932) in the Hausdorff case.[^o11] In particular, every [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [second-countable space](wikipedia://en/901459) is paracompact.
- The [Sorgenfrey line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorgenfrey_line) is paracompact, even though it is neither compact, locally compact, second countable, nor metrizable.
- Every [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) is paracompact.[^o12]
- (**Theorem of [A. H. Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._H._Stone)**) Every [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) is paracompact.[^o13] Early proofs were somewhat involved, but an elementary one was found by [M. E. Rudin](wikipedia://en/2976742).[^o14] Existing proofs of this require the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) for the non-[separable](wikipedia://en/27855) case. It has been shown that [ZF](wikipedia://en/152214) theory is not sufficient to prove it, even after the weaker [axiom of dependent choice](wikipedia://en/441950) is added.[^o15]
- A Hausdorff space admitting an [exhaustion by compact sets](wikipedia://en/3521238) is paracompact.

Some examples of spaces that are not paracompact include:

- The most famous counterexample is the [long line](wikipedia://en/338403), which is a nonparacompact [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193). (The long line is locally compact, but not second countable.)
- Another counterexample is a [product](wikipedia://en/24910) of [uncountably](wikipedia://en/32248) many copies of an [infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_%28cardinality%29) [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061). Any infinite set carrying the [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200) is not paracompact; in fact it is not even [metacompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacompact).
- The [Prüfer manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prüfer_manifold) *P* is a non-paracompact surface. (It is easy to find an uncountable open cover of *P* with no refinement of any kind.)
- The [bagpipe theorem](wikipedia://en/43365664) shows that there are 2<sup>ℵ<sub>1</sub></sup> topological [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of non-paracompact surfaces.
- The [Sorgenfrey plane](wikipedia://en/682853) is not paracompact despite being a product of two paracompact spaces.

## Properties {#Properties}

Paracompactness is weakly hereditary, i.e. every closed subspace of a paracompact space is paracompact. This can be extended to [F-sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-sigma_set) subspaces as well.[^n4]

- ([Michael's theorem](wikipedia://en/78587932)) A [regular space](wikipedia://en/53058) is paracompact if every open cover admits a locally finite refinement, not necessarily open. In particular, every regular [Lindelöf space](wikipedia://en/310480) is paracompact.
- (**Smirnov metrization theorem**) A topological space is metrizable if and only if it is paracompact, Hausdorff, and locally metrizable.
- [Michael selection theorem](wikipedia://en/17970233) states that [lower semicontinuous](wikipedia://en/57824) multifunctions from *X* into nonempty closed convex subsets of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) admit continuous selection iff *X* is paracompact.

Although a product of paracompact spaces need not be paracompact, the following are true:

- The product of a paracompact space and a [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042) is paracompact.
- The product of a [metacompact space](wikipedia://en/5074872) and a compact space is metacompact.

Both these results can be proved by the [tube lemma](wikipedia://en/3614395) which is used in the proof that a product of *finitely many* compact spaces is compact.

:::

## Paracompact Hausdorff spaces {#Paracompact_Hausdorff_spaces}

Paracompact spaces are sometimes required to also be [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) to extend their properties.

- (**Theorem of [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084)**) Every paracompact Hausdorff space is [normal](wikipedia://en/48629).
- Every paracompact Hausdorff space is a [shrinking space](wikipedia://en/5075898), that is, every open cover of a paracompact Hausdorff space has a shrinking: another open cover indexed by the same set such that the closure of every set in the new cover lies inside the corresponding set in the old cover.
- On paracompact Hausdorff spaces, [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) and [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614) are equal.[^o20]

### Partitions of unity {#Partitions_of_unity}

The most important feature of paracompact [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) is that they admit [partitions of unity](wikipedia://en/48636) subordinate to any open cover. This means the following: if *X* is a paracompact Hausdorff space with a given open cover, then there exists a collection of [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) functions on *X* with values in the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) \[0, 1\] such that:

- for every function *f*: *X* → **R** from the collection, there is an open set *U* from the cover such that the [support](wikipedia://en/381013) of *f* is contained in *U*;
- for every point *x* in *X*, there is a neighborhood *V* of *x* such that all but finitely many of the functions in the collection are identically 0 in *V* and the sum of the nonzero functions is identically 1 in *V*.

In fact, a T<sub>1</sub> space is Hausdorff and paracompact if and only if it admits partitions of unity subordinate to any open cover (see below). This property is sometimes used to define paracompact spaces (at least in the Hausdorff case).

Partitions of unity are useful because they often allow one to extend local constructions to the whole space. For instance, the integral of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on paracompact [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) is first defined locally (where the manifold looks like [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) and the integral is well known), and this definition is then extended to the whole space via a partition of unity.

## Relationship with compactness {#Relationship_with_compactness}

There is a similarity between the definitions of [compactness](wikipedia://en/6042) and paracompactness: For paracompactness, "subcover" is replaced by "open refinement" and "finite" by is replaced by "locally finite". Both of these changes are significant: if we take the definition of paracompact and change "open refinement" back to "subcover", or "locally finite" back to "finite", we end up with the compact spaces in both cases.

Paracompactness has little to do with the notion of compactness, but rather more to do with breaking up topological space entities into manageable pieces.

### Comparison of properties with compactness {#Comparison_of_properties_with_compactness}

Paracompactness is similar to compactness in the following respects:

- Every closed subset of a paracompact space is paracompact.
- Every paracompact [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) is [normal](wikipedia://en/48629).[^n4]

It is different in these respects:

- A paracompact subset of a Hausdorff space need not be closed. In fact, for metric spaces, all subsets are paracompact.
- A product of paracompact spaces need not be paracompact. The [square of the real line **R** in the lower limit topology](wikipedia://en/682853) is a classical example for this.

## Variations {#Variations}

There are several variations of the notion of paracompactness. To define them, we first need to extend the list of terms above:

A topological space is:

- **[metacompact](wikipedia://en/5074872)** if every open cover has an open point-finite refinement.
- **[orthocompact](wikipedia://en/5074937)** if every open cover has an open refinement such that the intersection of all the open sets about any point in this refinement is open.
- **fully normal** if every open cover has an open [star refinement](wikipedia://en/982000), and **fully T<sub>4</sub>** if it is fully normal and [T<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/148721) (see [separation axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_axioms)).

The adverb "**countably**" can be added to any of the adjectives "paracompact", "metacompact", and "fully normal" to make the requirement apply only to [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) open covers.

Every paracompact space is metacompact, and every metacompact space is orthocompact.

### Definition of relevant terms for the variations {#Definition_of_relevant_terms_for_the_variations}

- Given a cover and a point, the *star* of the point in the cover is the union of all the sets in the cover that contain the point. In symbols, the star of *x* in **U** = {*U*<sub>α</sub> : α in *A*} is

$$\mathbf{U}^{*}(x) := \bigcup_{U_{\alpha} \ni x}U_{\alpha}.$$

The notation for the star is not standardised in the literature, and this is just one possibility.

- A *[star refinement](wikipedia://en/982000)* of a cover of a space *X* is a cover of the same space such that, given any point in the space, the star of the point in the new cover is a subset of some set in the old cover. In symbols, **V** is a star refinement of **U** = {*U*<sub>α</sub> : α in *A*} if for any *x* in *X*, there exists a *U*<sub>α</sub> in *U* such that **V**<sup>\*</sup>(*x*) is contained in *U*<sub>α</sub>.
- A cover of a space *X* is *[point-finite](wikipedia://en/9856577)* (or *point finite*) if every point of the space belongs to only finitely many sets in the cover. In symbols, **U** is point finite if for any *x* in *X*, the set $\left\{\alpha \in A : x \in U_{\alpha} \right\}$ is finite.

As the names imply, a fully normal space is [normal](wikipedia://en/48629) and a fully T<sub>4</sub> space is T<sub>4</sub>. Every fully T<sub>4</sub> space is paracompact. In fact, for Hausdorff spaces, paracompactness and full normality are equivalent. Thus, a fully T<sub>4</sub> space is the same thing as a paracompact Hausdorff space.

Without the Hausdorff property, paracompact spaces are not necessarily fully normal. Any compact space that is not regular provides an example.

A historical note: fully normal spaces were defined before paracompact spaces, in 1940, by [John W. Tukey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Tukey).[^o33] The proof that all metrizable spaces are fully normal is easy. When it was proved by A.H. Stone that for Hausdorff spaces full normality and paracompactness are equivalent, he implicitly proved that all metrizable spaces are paracompact. Later [Ernest Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Michael) gave a direct proof of the latter fact and [M.E. Rudin](wikipedia://en/2976742) gave another, elementary, proof.
