# Korn

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Korn"}

**Korn** (stylized as **KoЯn**) is an American [nu metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_metal) band from [Bakersfield, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakersfield,_California), originally formed in 1993 by [James "Munky" Shaffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Shaffer), [Reginald "Fieldy" Arvizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Arvizu), and [David Silveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Silveria), who were members of the band [L.A.P.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A.P.D._%28band%29) Their current lineup features Shaffer (guitar), [Brian "Head" Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Welch) (guitar), [Jonathan Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Davis) (vocals), and [Ray Luzier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Luzier) (drums), the last of whom replaced Silveria in 2007. The band is notable for pioneering and popularizing the nu metal genre.

:::

Korn made a [demo tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_%28music%29), *[Neidermayer's Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neidermayer's_Mind)*, in 1993, which was distributed free to record companies and on request to members of the public. Their debut album *[Korn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korn_%28album%29)* was released in 1994, followed by their commercial breakthrough, *[Life Is Peachy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Is_Peachy)*, in 1996. The band first experienced mainstream success with *[Follow the Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follow_the_Leader_%28Korn_album%29)* (1998) and *[Issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issues_%28Korn_album%29)* (1999), both of which debuted at number one on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200). The band's mainstream success continued with *[Untouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untouchables_%28album%29)* (2002), *[Take a Look in the Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_a_Look_in_the_Mirror)* (2003), and *[See You on the Other Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_You_on_the_Other_Side_%28Korn_album%29)* (2005).

A [compilation album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compilation_album), *[Greatest Hits Vol. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits,_Vol._1_%28Korn_album%29)*, was released in 2004, spanning a decade of singles and concluding the band's recording contract with [Immortal Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortal_Records) and [Epic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Records). They signed to [Virgin Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Records), releasing *[See You on the Other Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_You_on_the_Other_Side_%28Korn_album%29)* in 2005, and an [untitled album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untitled_Korn_album) in 2007. The band's other recent albums, *[Korn III: Remember Who You Are](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korn_III:_Remember_Who_You_Are)* (2010) and *[The Path of Totality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Path_of_Totality)* (2011), were released via [Roadrunner Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadrunner_Records), with *[The Paradigm Shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Paradigm_Shift)* (2013) being released via [Prospect Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_Park_%28production_company%29) and [Caroline Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Records). *[The Serenity of Suffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Serenity_of_Suffering)* (2016) saw their return to Roadrunner Records, through which *[The Nothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nothing_%28Korn_album%29)* was released on September 13, 2019. Their latest album, *[Requiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_%28Korn_album%29)*, was released via [Loma Vista Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loma_Vista_Recordings) on February 4, 2022.

As of 2021, Korn had sold more than 40 million records worldwide. Several of their releases have been certified gold, platinum or multi-platinum by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA). Fourteen of the band's official releases have peaked in the top ten of the *Billboard* 200, eight of which have peaked in the top five. Korn has earned two [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award) out of eight nominations and two [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards) out of 11 nominations. Several of their music videos received substantial play on [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV)'s *[Total Request Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Request_Live)*, and were among the first to be retired on the show, including "[Got the Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Got_the_Life)" and "[Freak on a Leash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freak_on_a_Leash)".

## History {#History}

### Early years and formation (1989–1993) {#Early_years_and_formation_(1989–1993)}

*Image: Korn Logo Black PNG.png.* Korn's original logo designed by Jonathan Davis

Before Korn was formed, three of the original members of the band were associated with the band [L.A.P.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A.P.D._%28band%29)–[James Shaffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Shaffer), [Reginald Arvizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Arvizu), and [David Silveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Silveria).[^o7][^n1][^n2] The group originally consisted of Shaffer, Arvizu, and lead vocalist Richard Morrill; Silveria joined when he was 16.[^n1] When the band moved from [Bakersfield, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakersfield,_California)[^o11][^o12] to Los Angeles, Silveria dropped out of high school and Shaffer stayed in Bakersfield.[^n3] When Shaffer reunited with the band, they found a manager and released an [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play) entitled *Love and Peace Dude* in 1989 through [Triple X Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_X_Records).[^n4][^n5] L.A.P.D. released their sole full-length studio album *[Who's Laughing Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who's_Laughing_Now_%28album%29)* on May 3, 1991.[^o16] After the album's release, Morrill departed from the ensemble. The remaining trio were also briefly known as Creep, recording a demo with a singer named Corey before enlisting [Brian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Welch) and [Jonathan Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Davis) to form the band that went on to become Korn.[^n6]

When thinking of a band name, someone suggested "corn", but the band rejected that name, but Shaffer later had the idea to spell the name with a "K" instead of a "C", and a backwards "R", so the band's name would appear as "KoЯn".[^n6] The idea of using a backwards "R" came from the logo of toy retailer [Toys R Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toys_R_Us), for which many of the band members had previously worked.[^n6] Silveria explained the logo, designed by Davis, "the music makes the name, because Korn's a dumb name. But once we get established, it makes the name cool."[^n7]

Korn rented a studio from Jeff Creath called Underground Chicken Sound, in [Huntington Beach, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Beach,_California). While they were recording there, a crowd had been [loitering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loitering) outside the studio.[^n8] The band began playing a prelude to a later song "[Clown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clown_%28Korn_song%29)", attracting a larger crowd. Arvizu said the crowd gathered because it sounded so "different."[^n9] Korn started performing at gigs in the summer of 1993, with members saying that touring was a "pain-in-the-ass." While in Huntington Beach, the band was spotted by [Immortal Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortal_Records) [A&R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A&R) employee Paul Pontius. Pontius would describe Korn's sound as "the new genre of rock." In 1993, Korn released their first [demo album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_album), *[Neidermayer's Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neidermayer's_Mind),* which had very limited printing. It was not well received by critics or the public.[^n7] It was released to [record companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_company) and to people who filled out a flyer given out at gigs they played for free with [Biohazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biohazard_%28band%29) and [House of Pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Pain).[^n7] With this demo, Korn pioneered the nu metal sound, riffs, and rhythm.[^n10][^o27]

### *Korn*, *Life Is Peachy* and recognition (1994–1997) {#Korn,_Life_Is_Peachy_and_recognition_(1994–1997)}

By May 1994, Korn began recording their [self-titled debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korn_%28album%29) with [Ross Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Robinson).[^o29] Recording was finished by the end of the following month,[^n9] and on October 11, the band's debut album was released through [Immortal Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortal_Records) (an Epic imprint label);[^o31] it peaked at number one on the [Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_charts) [Heatseekers Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Heatseekers) chart[^o32] and would eventually reach number 72 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) in February 1996.[^o340] The album received positive reviews by critics, and is said to have established the [new wave of metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_metal).[^n11][^n12] As well as sparking the nu metal genre, it also started record producer [Ross Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Robinson)'s music career[^n11] and influenced bands such as [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_%28band%29), [Coal Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_Chamber), and [Limp Bizkit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limp_Bizkit).[^n11][^n12]

After the band finished recording the album, they began touring with Biohazard and House of Pain. Their record company gave them enough money for their own tour bus. Korn's first gig was in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta).[^n13] About halfway through the tour, the bus that Korn's record company gave them stopped working, and the band had to find a new one.[^n14] Their first tour was not very successful in promoting the album.[^n15]

Korn went on tour with [hardcore punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_punk) band [Sick of It All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sick_of_It_All) in January 1995.[^n16] The band embarked on their first [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) tour, including at [LA2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria_2) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) and L'Arapaho in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^n17] Later that year, Korn was chosen alongside [Deftones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deftones) as direct support for [Ozzy Osbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozzy_Osbourne).[^o43] Korn's self-titled album went [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIAA_certification) in the midst of the tour[^o43] and was eventually certified two-times platinum by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA).[^o339] Aside from touring, Korn released four singles. "[Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_%28Korn_song%29)" was released on August 1, 1994, and "[Shoots and Ladders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoots_and_Ladders_%28song%29)" was released on October 31, 1995. The latter received a [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award) nomination in 1997 for [Best Metal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Metal_Performance).[^o44] "[Need To](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need_To_%28Korn_song%29)" was also released in 1995, on April 8. The fourth and final single, "[Clown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clown_%28Korn_song%29)", was released on February 2, 1996. "Blind" was the only single to chart, peaking at number 15 on the Canadian *[RPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPM_%28magazine%29)* Alternative 30.[^o45]

After the success of their debut, Korn decided to enter the studio again for a second album.[^n15] By then, the band had created a large [fan base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_%28person%29), having played between 200 and 250 shows,[^o47] and the expectations for their follow-up album were high.[^n15][^o346] They went back into the studio in early April 1996 at Indigo Ranch Studios, [Malibu, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California).[^n15] ...We went in really fresh, and we wanted to get it done quickly to capture that energy. So it was probably about 60% knowing what I was going to play and 40% just playing whatever came to mind at that moment... It ended up really good, and it has a kind of energy I probably wouldn't have gotten if I'd worked everything out before hand.

*[Life Is Peachy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Is_Peachy)* was released October 15, 1996[^o51] and despite minimal radio airplay and television attention, the album debuted at number three on the *Billboard* 200,[^o340][^n15][^n18] and peaked at number one in New Zealand.[^o54] The album sold 106,000 copies in its first week.[^o55] [Jon Pareles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Pareles) from *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* said that the band was "mad at everybody, including themselves." The album was certified double platinum in the United States, platinum in Australia, and gold in Canada.[^o339]

The first single, "[No Place to Hide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Place_to_Hide_%28song%29)", spawned a Grammy nomination for Best Metal Performance.[^o58] "[A.D.I.D.A.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.D.I.D.A.S._%28Korn_song%29)" was released as the second single on March 4, 1997. It became the band's first charting single on *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_%28magazine%29)*, peaking at number 13 on the [Bubbling Under Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubbling_Under_Hot_100_Singles) chart.[^o59] The third single, "[Good God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_God_%28Korn_song%29)", was released on July 14, 1997.[^o60] A promotional disc was released in 1997 to promote both the band and the Life Is Peachy Tour featuring [Incubus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubus_%28band%29) and [the Urge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Urge) and included three live tracks.[^o61]

*Image: Korn London 1997.jpg.* Arvizu *(left)* and Silveria *(right)* at the [Brixton Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixton_Academy) in London during the Life Is Peachy Tour, February 24, 1997

Korn gained more popularity after co-headlining the [Lollapalooza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lollapalooza) summer tour festival in 1997 with [Tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_%28band%29). However, Korn was forced to stop touring after Shaffer was diagnosed with [viral meningitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningitis#Viral_meningitis).[^o62] The band remained relatively quiet during the end of the year, resting and setting new goals.[^n19] The band then founded their own label, Elementree Records, to sign and introduce new bands to their fans.[^n20] The label was run by Davis who first signed [Orgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orgy_%28band%29).[^o65] Silveria influenced Davis to sign them.[^n21]

Prior to the release of 1998's *Follow the Leader*, Gretchen Plewes, a [Zeeland, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeland,_Michigan) high school assistant principal, said in an interview for a Michigan newspaper that Korn's music is "indecent, vulgar, obscene and intends to be insulting" after giving a student named Eric VanHoven a one-day suspension for wearing a shirt with the Korn logo.[^o67] FM station [WKLQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTNR_%28FM%29) was filmed giving away hundreds of free Korn T-shirts, donated by the band outside the school. [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_County,_Michigan) County Police were called by the school, but they ended up helping handing out shirts.[^n22][^o69] Korn filed a [cease and desist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cease_and_desist) order against Plewes and the school district for their comments. They also threatened a multimillion-dollar lawsuit, but dropped both actions due to the band members' personal lives.[^n23] The period from 1993 to 1997 was embodied by wearing apparel that featured the [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) clothing brand.[^n24]

### *Follow the Leader* and *Issues* (1998–2001) {#Follow_the_Leader_and_Issues_(1998–2001)}

In 1998, Korn signed a publishing contract with [Warner Chappell Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Chappell_Music).[^o73] While Korn was waiting for a full partnership with Adidas (which had previously sponsored [Run-DMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run-DMC)), the sportwear brand refused to sign a contract with Korn. In a contradictory argument, Adidas told them: "We do sports, not music". In 1998, [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_%28brand%29) seized the opportunity and offered a \$500,000 deal to Korn, including their music featured in [Kevin Kerslake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Kerslake)-directed advertisements. Korn accepted the contract; Davis said, "That's more than Adidas ever did for us! It wasn't a sell-out thing. It was about respect."[^o74][^n25]

Prior to the release of the band's third album, Korn produced a weekly online TV show called *KornTV,*[^n26][^o77] which documented the making of the record and featured special guests such as [porn star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornographic_film_actor) [Ron Jeremy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Jeremy), [Limp Bizkit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limp_Bizkit), and [311](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/311_%28band%29).[^o77] The project also gave fans (nicknamed "nibletz") the chance to call in and ask the band questions, an approach that represented one of the first times a band utilized the Internet in such a way.[^o79] Korn released their third album *[Follow the Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follow_the_Leader_%28Korn_album%29)* on August 18, 1998,[^o78] which featured a number of guest vocalists such as [Ice Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Cube); [Pharcyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pharcyde) member [Tre Hardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slimkid3); [Fred Durst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Durst) of Limp Bizkit; and actor [Cheech Marin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheech_Marin) on the hidden track "[Earache My Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earache_My_Eye)" (written by Marin himself).[^o79]

Korn launched a political campaign-styled tour to promote *Follow the Leader**s release,[^n27][^o83] which took the group on a chartered jet all over North America.[^n27] Band members talked to fans and answered questions during special "fan conferences" organized at every stop along the tour and signed autographs. Jim Rose of the [Jim Rose Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Rose_Circus) hosted the entire "Kampaign" tour.[^n27][^n28]***

The album was considered a complete success by the band, debuting at [number one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_one) on the *Billboard* 200 with at least 268,000 copies sold in its first week of release[^n29][^o88] and, among other singles, spawning two of their biggest singles: "[Got the Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Got_the_Life)" and "[Freak on a Leash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freak_on_a_Leash)".[^n27] They both exposed Korn to a wider mainstream audience, with the music videos being mainstays on MTV's *[Total Request Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Request_Live)*. "Got the Life" was the show's very first "retired" video,[^n30][^n31][^o92] with "Freak on a Leash" also reaching retirement several months later.[^n30][^o94][^o95] In September 1998, Korn signed with a second music publisher, [Zomba Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomba_Group_of_Companies), through negotiations of the band's then-manager [Jeff Kwatinetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Kwatinetz) (the former [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer) of [The Firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Firm,_Inc.)), along with co-founders [Peter Katsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Katsis) and [Michael Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Green_%28agent%29).[^o73]

"Freak on a Leash" won a Grammy for [Best Music Video, Short Form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Short_Form_Music_Video), and received a nomination for [Best Hard Rock Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Hard_Rock_Performance).[^o96] The video also earned nine [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards) nominations for Video of the Year; Best Rock Video; Breakthrough Video; Best Direction; Best Special Effects; Best Art Direction; Best Cinematography; Best Editing; and Viewer's Choice.[^o97] It eventually won two awards for Best Rock Video and for Best Editing.[^n30][^o99][^o100] "Freak on a Leash" failed to enter the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100), although it did manage to peak at number six on the Bubbling Under Hot 100 chart.[^o59] *Follow the Leader* is considered by members of Korn to be the band's most commercially–successful album,[^n30][^n32] being certified five-times platinum by the RIAA[^o339] and having sold almost 10 million copies worldwide.[^n33]

The band's fourth album, *[Issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issues_%28Korn_album%29)*, produced by [Brendan O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_O'Brien_%28music_producer%29), was released on November 16, 1999,[^o104] featuring cover art designed by Alfredo Carlos, who won an MTV contest held for the fans.[^o105] *Issues* was released during a week of many highly anticipated records. It debuted at number one on the *Billboard* 200 with at least 573,000 copies sold,[^o106] keeping [Dr. Dre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Dre)'s second album *[2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_%28Dr._Dre_album%29)* and *[All the Way... A Decade of Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Way..._A_Decade_of_Song)* by [Céline Dion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Céline_Dion) from hitting number one.[^o106]

To celebrate the album's release, the band performed the record in its entirety in front of a live audience at New York's historic [Apollo Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Theater) and broadcast the concert simultaneously across many radio stations.[^o107][^o108] This performance marked the first performance by a rock band since [Buddy Holly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Holly) in the late 1950s.[^o109] This special event featured the [New York Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Police_Department) marching [drum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion_instrument) and [bagpipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagpipes) band conducted by [Richard Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gibbs), as well as a group of backup singers to enhance the more melodic choruses Davis used on the album.[^o109] A snippet of "[Falling Away from Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_Away_from_Me)" was featured on [RealVideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RealVideo) with a brief [interpretive dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretive_dance) by bassist [Reginald Arvizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Arvizu),[^o110] and also featured on their official website as an [MP3 file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MP3), although its release was against the advice of its attorneys and the corporate establishment.[^o111] The album was also promoted by the band's highly successful Sick and Twisted Tour.

A little before Korn's album *Issues* was released, the band appeared on an episode of *[South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)* titled "[Korn's Groovy Pirate Ghost Mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korn's_Groovy_Pirate_Ghost_Mystery)", in which the first single from *Issues*, "Falling Away from Me", was premiered.[^o112][^o113] The single became Korn's first entry on the *Billboard* Hot 100, reaching number 99.[^o114] "[Make Me Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_Me_Bad)" was released as the album's second single in February 2000, peaking at number 14 on the Bubbling Under Hot 100 chart.[^o59] A third single, "[Somebody Someone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somebody_Someone)", followed with more moderate success.

Music videos were filmed for all three singles, with long-time friend Fred Durst directing "Falling Away from Me" and Martin Weisz directing a concept video for "Make Me Bad", as well as a performance-based video for "Somebody Someone", which featured [CGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery) effects. Every video was a staple on *Total Request Live*, two of which made it to retirement.[^o94] *Issues* was described by [Stephen Thomas Erlewine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Thomas_Erlewine) to be less hip-hop-oriented than *Follow the Leader*.[^o104] It was certified three-times Platinum,[^o339] following up the success of *Follow the Leader*. In 2001, the brand [PONY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pony_International) reappeared in the clothing industry and initiated a partnership with Korn.[^n25]

### *Untouchables*, *Take a Look in the Mirror* and Welch's departure (2002–2005) {#Untouchables,_Take_a_Look_in_the_Mirror_and_Welch's_departure_(2002–2005)}

On June 11, 2002,[^o118] Korn re-emerged into the media with their fifth album *[Untouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untouchables_%28album%29)*. It debuted at number two on the *Billboard* 200 with 434,000 in sales.[^o119] The band has blamed [music piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_piracy) for the drop in sales, as an unmastered version of the album had leaked three months prior to its official release date.[^o120][^o121] On April 2, 2002, the [Opie and Anthony Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opie_and_Anthony_Show) began airing songs from *Untouchables*.[^o121] After playing a few songs, the broadcasters received a cease-and-desist letter from [Sony Music Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Music_Entertainment). Opie and Anthony said "The reason for the premature premiere was to infuriate a rival New York station, which disallows their in-studio guests to appear on 'The Opie and Anthony Show.'"[^o121] The release of this album was preceded by a show at the Hammerstein Ballroom in New York a day prior to the album's release broadcast digitally throughout movie theatres in the United States.[^o122]

The album contained experiments and styles never previously attempted by Korn. [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic) related: "The band is far more experimental this time out, delivering [Helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmet_%28band%29)-like ringing guitars that melt and morph into each other, a mix of [Metallica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallica)-esque blastbeats and tight funk drumming from the constantly improving David Silveria, and memorable riffs that take the shape of dark sound structures and offer more than just a collection of chords."[^o123] The first two music videos from *Untouchables*, "Here to Stay" and "[Thoughtless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoughtless_%28Korn_song%29)", were directed by the [Hughes Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Brothers).[^o124] "Here to Stay" earned Korn a Grammy Award for Best Metal Performance.[^o125] "Here to Stay" peaked at number 72 on the *Billboard* Hot 100.[^o114] During an interview with Wall of Sound, Munky revealed the album was the band's most expensive to make, but it produced several of his favourite songs, including "Thoughtless" and "Here To Stay".[^o126]

Due to the album *Untouchables* leaking onto the Internet, *[Take a Look in the Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_a_Look_in_the_Mirror)* was released on Friday November 21, 2003– four days earlier than its original release date.[^o127] The album therefore received a weekend's worth of sales, which resulted in a poor showing on the *Billboard* 200.[^o128] During its first full week, *Take a Look in the Mirror* soared ten places from number 19 to number 9, increasing the album's total sales to 179,000.[^o128]

It is the first album self-produced by Korn. The band explained that they wanted fans to hear the music as it should be.[^o127] The album presented different styles and themes compared to previous albums. Lead vocalist Jonathan Davis related: "The whole album is about love, hate and my hate of people and just losing my mind. The previous albums I did, I think the last three, I was coming from a place of hurt. And I just finally got to the point where I'm done hurting and I'm just pissed off about it now. It's turned back to just sheer hate and anger. And it definitely comes across on the album." [MTV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_News) said that Davis convinced his fans that they "will be shocked, particularly with the album's second track, 'Break Some Off,' which he called 'brutal'."[^o129]

Korn released the single "[Did My Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Did_My_Time)" on July 22, 2003,[^o130] which was used to promote the film *[Lara Croft: Tomb Raider - The Cradle of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_Croft:_Tomb_Raider_-_The_Cradle_of_Life)* but did not appear on its soundtrack due to unspecified legal issues.[^o131] The single debuted and peaked at number 38 on the *Billboard* Hot 100, becoming Korn's first and only top 40 hit in the United States.[^o114] "Did My Time" gave Korn another Grammy nomination in the Best Metal Performance category.[^o132]

Korn released their greatest hits album, *[Greatest Hits Vol. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits,_Volume_1_%28Korn_album%29)*, on October 5, 2004.[^o133] The album debuted at number four on *Billboard*, selling more than 129,000 copies.[^o134] This album assembles choice tracks from six Korn studio albums released between 1994 and 2003. The first single was a cover of the song "[Word Up!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_Up!_%28song%29)" originally composed by [Cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_%28band%29).[^o135] The single peaked at number 23 on the Bubbling Under Hot 100 chart.[^o59] Special editions of *Greatest Hits Vol. 1* included a DVD titled *Korn: Live at CBGB,* featuring seven select songs from their show of November 24, 2003 at [CBGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBGB).[^o136]

In early 2005, Brian Welch announced that he would be quitting the band. In front of a crowd of 10,000 in three services at Valley Bible Fellowship in Bakersfield, California, Welch said "I was addicted to methamphetamines and tried everything ... rehab, stuff on the Internet, but nothing helped me kick it. I was trying on my own to quit and couldn't do it. I wanted to die. No one knew what I was going through. I could not quit. Church was my last shot. I would sit in church high \[on drugs\]. I would wonder why people would go up to the front after the service. But one day it was for me. I said \[to God\], 'Show me how to quit.'"[^o137][^o138]

In a 2013 interview with *Rolling Stone*, Head described his final moments in the band as very tense: "the last year I was in the band, we were gonna kick out the bass player, Fieldy, and this guy's girlfriend couldn't be on this side of the stage because there were fights with another wife in the band. And obviously the drugs – it's no secret I was into the drugs, so crazy stuff, like having to finish our blow right before we got to the border because they were gonna come check to see if we had anything."[^o139] Following his departure from Korn, Welch released two autobiographies, a solo album, and formed a band named Love and Death, which released a debut album in 2013.[^o140][^o141]

### *See You on the Other Side* and Silveria's departure (2005–2007) {#See_You_on_the_Other_Side_and_Silveria's_departure_(2005–2007)}

*Image: Kornmaa2006.jpg.* Korn at the [MTV Asia Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Asia_Awards_2006) in [Bangkok, Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok), 2006

Upon completing their record deal with [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_BMG), Korn partnered with [EMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMI) and signed to [Virgin Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Records). As part of this innovative arrangement, Virgin paid Korn \$25 million upfront in exchange for a share in the profits of their next two studio albums, including tours and merchandising. Virgin also received a 30 percent stake in the band's licensing, ticket sales, and other revenue sources.[^o143][^o144]

The band's first album for Virgin Records, *[See You on the Other Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_You_on_the_Other_Side_%28Korn_album%29)*, was released on December 6, 2005,[^o146] and debuted at number three on the *Billboard* 200,[^o340] scanning close to 221,000 copies.[^o147] The album managed to stay in the top half of the *Billboard* 200 for 34 consecutive weeks.[^o340] The first single from the album, "[Twisted Transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_Transistor)", was accompanied by a music video directed by [Dave Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Meyers_%28director%29) in which hip-hop artists [Xzibit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xzibit), [Lil Jon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil_Jon), [Snoop Dogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snoop_Dogg), and [David Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Banner) portray Korn.[^o148] "Twisted Transistor" peaked at number 64 on the *Billboard* Hot 100.[^o114] The second single, "[Coming Undone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming_Undone)", was released in February 2006, peaking at number 79 on the *Billboard* Hot 100.[^o114] The music video was directed by [Little X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_X).[^o149] *See You on the Other Side* was certified Platinum in the United States,[^o339] and by mid-2007, the album had sold over 2.2 million copies worldwide.[^o150]

Korn held a press conference at the [Hollywood Forever Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Forever_Cemetery) on January 13, 2006, announcing the *See You on the Other Side* Tour.[^o151] [10 Years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_Years_%28band%29) and [Mudvayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudvayne) were selected to open all dates of the tour, which kicked off in their hometown of Bakersfield on what Mayor Harvey Hall officially declared as "Korn Day", February 24.[^o152] The resurrection of their Family Values Tour was announced on April 18, 2006, which featured co-headliners [Deftones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deftones), [Stone Sour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Sour), [Flyleaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyleaf_%28band%29), and the Japanese metal group [Dir En Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir_En_Grey) on the main stage.[^o153] Korn and [Evanescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanescence) co-headlined the 2007 edition, with [Atreyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atreyu_%28band%29), Flyleaf, [Hellyeah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellyeah), and [Trivium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivium_%28band%29) rounding out the main stage.[^o154]

While promoting *See You on the Other Side* in Europe, Jonathan Davis was diagnosed with [idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiopathic_thrombocytopenic_purpura), a blood platelet disorder that hospitalized him for the weekend and prevented him from performing at the renowned [Download Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Download_Festival).[^o155] Despite the illness, the band still performed with guest singers, including [Corey Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Taylor) of [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_%28band%29) and [Stone Sour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Sour) fame; [Trivium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivium_%28band%29)'s [Matt Heafy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Heafy); [Skindred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skindred)'s [Benji Webbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benji_Webbe); and [Avenged Sevenfold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenged_Sevenfold)'s [M. Shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Shadows). This led to Korn canceling the rest of their European bill for 2006,[^o156] including the [Hellfest Summer Open Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellfest_Summer_Open_Air). It was originally unknown to the public what his ailment was, but the singer revealed in a letter to fans that he was "dangerously low on blood platelets and at a high risk of death from a hemorrhage if the problem was not treated".[^o157]

In early December 2006 it was announced that founding drummer David Silveria would be taking an indefinite "temporary hiatus" from the band.[^o158][^o159] Korn then performed at the MTV studios in [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square) on December 9, 2006, for the *[MTV Unplugged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Unplugged)* series, which was broadcast on February 23, 2007, through MTV.com and on March 2, 2007, across North American, South American, European, and Asian MTV stations.[^o160] Korn played a 14-song [acoustic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_music) set, complete with guest appearances by [the Cure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cure) and [Amy Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Lee) of Evanescence.[^o161] The performance was eventually cut down to 11 songs for the album, two of which did not air on MTV. Sales of nearly 51,000 brought *[MTV Unplugged: Korn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Unplugged:_Korn)* to number nine in its first week out.[^o162]

### *The Path of Totality*, *The Paradigm Shift* and Welch's return (2011–2015) {#The_Path_of_Totality,_The_Paradigm_Shift_and_Welch's_return_(2011–2015)}

*Image: Korn-Live-Metaltown 2011.jpg.* Korn performing live at the [Metaltown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaltown) Festival, [Gothenburg, Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg) in June 2011

Korn's tenth studio album, *[The Path of Totality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Path_of_Totality)*, was released on December 6, 2011.[^o189] It was the second and final studio album for Roadrunner Records. The album features contributions from [Skrillex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skrillex); [12th Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Planet_%28musician%29); [Excision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excision_%28musician%29); Downlink; [Kill the Noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Stanczak); [Noisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noisia); and various other [EDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music) artists.[^o190] *The Path of Totality* debuted and peaked at number ten on the *Billboard* 200 with 55,000 copies scanned.[^o340][^o191] "[Get Up!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Up!_%28Korn_song%29)" was premiered as the lead single via *[Spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_%28magazine%29)* in April 2011.[^o192] The single managed to peak at number eight on the Bubbling Under Hot 100 chart.[^o59] It eventually became their first single to be certified gold in the United States.[^o339] The second single, "[Narcissistic Cannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcissistic_Cannibal)", was released in October 2011,[^o171] with its music video being directed and produced by [Alexander Bulkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Bulkley) of [ShadowMachine Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShadowMachine_Films).[^o193]

After a stint of festival appearances, Korn promoted *The Path of Totality* during [a headlining tour of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Path_of_Totality_Tour). Korn split their show into three sections. The band kicked off by playing tracks from their first two albums, including the B-side "Proud", which was originally included on the soundtrack to the 1997 film *[I Know What You Did Last Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_What_You_Did_Last_Summer)*.[^o329][^o194] They followed with a different stage set up, playing several of their new songs. The set list ended with hit singles and an encore. Other appearances came from [Datsik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsik_%28musician%29), Downlink, and Dope D.O.D. A special album release performance filmed and recorded at the [Hollywood Palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Palladium) was issued in various formats through [Shout! Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout!_Factory) in September 2012.[^o195] *The Path of Totality* won Album of the Year at the 2012 *[Revolver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolver_%28magazine%29)* Golden Gods Awards. This was Korn's first victory at the Golden Gods Awards, a ceremony that celebrates the best in hard rock and heavy metal music.[^o196] Korn was also inducted into the [Kerrang!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerrang!) Hall of Fame during the 2011 [Kerrang! Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerrang!_Awards).[^o197] On May 5, 2012, guitarist Brian "Head" Welch joined Korn on stage at the [Carolina Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Rebellion) festival to play "Blind" for the first time since his departure.[^o198] As recalled in the documentary DVD *Reconciliation*, Welch's appearance was a spur-of-the-moment decision: Welch initially visited the festival because of his daughter, who was a fan of bands performing. Welch joined [Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_%28band%29) on stage for a song, and would eventually be persuaded to join his old bandmates in Korn for a [meet and greet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_and_greet).[^o199] Upon Korn preparing for the show, the guitar tech prepared an extra guitar and amplifier for Welch, "just in case".[^o199]

On July 18, 2012, Jonathan Davis told [Billboard.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_%28magazine%29) that Korn was getting ready to start recording their eleventh studio album that would be released independently.[^o200] It was also announced that their next album would not contain any dubstep influences like *[The Path of Totality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Path_of_Totality)*.[^o201] Guitarist [James "Munky" Shaffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_"Munky"_Shaffer) stated in an interview with the *[Phoenix New Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_New_Times)* that the new album would be heavier, with more aggressive "in-your-face" guitars and vocals. He went on to say that he would be using new recording techniques in the studio to give his riffs a fresh approach.[^o202]

In November 2012, Mudvayne bassist [Ryan Martinie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Martinie) toured Korn as a temporary replacement for Reginald Arvizu, who stayed at home during his wife's pregnancy.[^o203] That month also saw the confirmation of Welch's return to the band.[^o204] His comeback was initially thought to be temporary as he was only scheduled for two shows in June 2013 at [Rock Am Ring and Rock Im Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_am_Ring_and_Rock_im_Park),[^o205] but [Ray Luzier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Luzier) announced at the January 25 [Sabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabian_Cymbals) show during NAMM that Welch would be working the whole tour.[^o206] In January 2013, [techno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno) artist Beta Traxx acknowledged he was working on a new Korn song for their upcoming album, which he commented would sound "like the past and the future at the same time." Luzier affirmed that the album would be produced by Don Gilmore.[^o207] The BK Entertainment Group updated their clients list on February 12 and attested that Korn had signed to their management's independent label - Prospect Park Productions.[^o2][^o3] On June 1, [Caroline Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Records_%28U.S.%29) added Korn to their roster confirming that the band have been signed on to a contract with the label; they are partnered with Korn's management Prospect Park.[^o208] On February 18, 2013, Korn posted a photo showing Head as part of the line up, which escalated rumors that he was in the process to rejoin Korn permanently. This was confirmed by Head himself in May, when it was also confirmed that he had been recording as part of the band for their upcoming album.[^o209]

Fieldy has said of the album "Right now, I will tell you this ... we've done something we've never done before. Normally if we do a new Korn record, we'll normally put 12 or 13 songs and that's how many we make. This time we've made 20-plus songs, and we'll put the best of what we have on there, whatever the numbers end up being. Korn's eleventh studio album, *[The Paradigm Shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Paradigm_Shift)*, was released on October 8, 2013.[^o213][^o214] Their first single, ["Never Never"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Never_%28Korn_song%29) was set to be released August 12, 2013.[^o215] The band recorded an episode of [Guitar Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_Center) Sessions for [DirecTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DirecTV) on September 11, 2013, which is scheduled to premiere November 1, 2013 on DirecTV Audience Channel.[^o216][^o217] Korn will also be the latest act to be inducted into the world-famous Hollywood [Rockwalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwalk) on October 8, 2013.[^o218]

### *The Serenity of Suffering* and *The Nothing* (2015–2020) {#The_Serenity_of_Suffering_and_The_Nothing_(2015–2020)}

Korn soon began the process of writing new songs for a twelfth studio album. Guitarist Head described the music on the new album as "heavier than anyone's heard us in a long time."[^o224] Their twelfth studio album *[The Serenity of Suffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Serenity_of_Suffering)* was released on October 21, 2016. On July 14, 2016, guitarist [James "Munky" Shaffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Shaffer) revealed to *[Metal Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Hammer)* that [Corey Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Taylor) of [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_%28band%29)/[Stone Sour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Sour) would make a guest appearance on the new album.[^o225] He also revealed [Nick Raskulinecz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Raskulinecz) as the album's producer.[^o226] On July 16, 2016, Korn premiered a new song, "Rotting in Vain", at [Chicago Open Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Open_Air) Festival.[^o227]

In late 2016, [Zac Baird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zac_Baird) left the live band after 10 years.[^o228] Jules Venturini completed the tour before [Davey Oberlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davey_Oberlin) was brought into the band.[^o229][^o230] It was later announced that Fieldy would be missing Korn's South American tour and [Robert Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Trujillo)'s then-12-year-old son [Tye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tye_Trujillo) was brought in to perform with the band for the tour's duration.[^o231][^o232] In March 2018, Korn embarked on a Japanese tour with support from [Deadly Apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadly_Apples), including a stop at [Vans Warped Tour Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vans_Warped_Tour).[^o233][^o234] On May 15, 2018, Korn announced the dates of special shows in order to mark the 20th anniversary of their album *Follow the Leader*.[^o235] In order to mark the album's 20th anniversary, the band partnered with the clothing company Pleasures to release clothing [merchandise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_%28business%29) dedicated to the album.[^o236] In August 2018, Luzier reported via [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram) that Korn has begun the process of tracking drums for the upcoming thirteenth album.[^o237] In a December 2018 interview to *Revolver Magazine*, Welch revealed that the upcoming album was scheduled for release in fall 2019, with [Nick Raskulinecz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Raskulinecz) resuming production duties. He also stated some of the tracks had been co-written with [John Feldmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Feldmann) and that the band had tracked demos with [Travis Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Barker) on drums.[^o238][^o239]

On June 25, 2019, the band revealed that their latest album, *[The Nothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nothing_%28Korn_album%29)*, would be released on September 13, 2019, and unveiled its first single: "[You'll Never Find Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You'll_Never_Find_Me_%28Korn_song%29)".[^o240] On August 2, the band released a brand new song from *The Nothing* called "[Cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_%28Korn_song%29)".[^o241][^o242] On August 22, Korn announced their new album by playing a concert in multiplayer game AdventureQuest 3D, complete with in-game merchandise, a virtual meet-and-greet, and performing a new song on a digital stage.[^o243] In July 2020, Korn released a cover of "[The Devil Went Down to Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Went_Down_to_Georgia)" by [Charlie Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Daniels), who had died earlier that year. The cover was recorded during sessions for *The Nothing* and featured rapper [Yelawolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelawolf) and proceeds were donated to non-profit *Awakening Youth*.[^o244][^o245] During an interview with *Wall of Sound*, [Munky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Shaffer) spoke about COVID ruining their plans to tour *The Nothing*, stating "It was kind of a fucking bummer, I gotta be honest man. I was so excited \[with\] that album. We put the artwork together, we designed the stages, we got everything ready to roll out, merch and all; you know everything a band does before they go on the road and promote an album."[^o126]

### *Requiem*, Fieldy's departure and upcoming fifteenth studio album (2021–present) {#Requiem,_Fieldy's_departure_and_upcoming_fifteenth_studio_album_(2021–present)}

In his first online interview of 2021, [Brian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Welch) revealed that the band had plans for 2021 but was coy about what they were, hinting it had something to do with touring in a powerful way.[^o247] On April 24, 2021, Korn released a one-off, online streaming event entitled *Monumental* that was filmed on the top of a parking structure at the *Stranger Things: A Drive-Into Experience* set in Los Angeles.[^o248]

On May 12, the band announced a 28-date summer U.S. tour with [Staind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staind) as the supporting act. The 2021 tour came after Korn was forced to cancel their planned 2020 run with [Faith No More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_No_More) due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).[^o249] On June 21, the band announced that their bassist Arvizu would be taking a hiatus and would not be performing with the band on their upcoming summer tour.[^o250] Ra Díaz of [Suicidal Tendencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicidal_Tendencies) replaced Arvizu on the tour.[^o251] In May 2025, Fieldy clarified his current status with the band, stating that he does not anticipate returning to the band anytime soon.[^o252] In April 2026, Fieldy revealed that not taking a COVID vaccination meant that he would not join the band on the 2021 tour, which then led to his permanent departure. He considers himself "retired" from Korn but has not ruled out a return.

The 2021 tour was marred by several separate instances where members contracted [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19): first, Jonathan Davis tested positive in August, causing several dates to be postponed.[^o254] Then in September, Munky was forced to sit out several dates, being replaced by [Love and Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_and_Death_%28band%29) guitarist JR Bareis while he recovered.[^o255] Lastly, in October Ray Luzier also tested positive, missing the next three shows while [Fever 333](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever_333) drummer Aric Improta stepped in.[^o256]

On April 23, the band confirmed that they had begun work on a fourteenth studio album, utilizing the [period of lockdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns) due to COVID-19 to begin writing.[^o257][^o258] The album was said to be fully written as of April 2021.[^o259] A new single was released November 11 entitled "[Start the Healing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Start_the_Healing)", with the music video debuting in a [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube) premiere. On January 13, 2022, the band released the second single from the album entitled "Forgotten".[^o260] The album *[Requiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_%28Korn_album%29)* was released on February 4, 2022.[^o261] On April 21, 2022, the band released a music video for the song, "Worst Is on Its Way".[^o262]

Shortly prior to the release of *Requiem*, Davis revealed in an interview with Music Feeds that the band were already in the process of working on a follow-up studio album.[^o263] Welch later stated in a September 2023 interview that a release of new material in 2024 was likely: "Yeah, \[there will be\] new music coming out next year. I don't know. We're just kind of in hiding right now, just doing our own thing."[^o264] Welch reiterated in late March 2024 that work was still underway, speaking positively regarding the content recorded thus far.[^o265] In early December, he stated that while the majority of the album had been written, there were no plans to release anything in the immediate future.[^o266] Shaffer reiterated once more in 2025 that recording is still underway.[^o267] Welch, in a February 2026 interview, clarified that while work on the album is still ongoing and there were plans to reconvene for exchange of new material, the band as a whole instead have decided to focus on touring-related endeavours due to already having a substantial catalogue of released works from the preceding decades.[^o268] Immediately following this, a since-deleted post depicted Shaffer with Chris Lord-Alge strongly implying that the mixing process was underway.[^o269] Shaffer stated in May that around 40 candidate songs had been written.[^o270] Mark Bennett, Luzier's drum tech, revealed in September that the studio drum performances for the upcoming album had been fully recorded.[^o271] As of March 2025, the elapsed time since the release of *Requiem* has become the longest thus far with no studio album release, exceeding the 1,109-day gap from *The Paradigm Shift* to *The Serenity of Suffering*.

On April 23, 2026, the group released a single, "Reward the Scars", which became their first song release in four years. The track appeared on the soundtrack for the *[Diablo IV: Lord of Hatred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diablo_IV:_Lord_of_Hatred)* expansion pack.[^o272]

## Artistry {#Artistry}

### Style and instrumentation {#Style_and_instrumentation}

Korn is identified as the pioneers of nu metal music, a form of [alternative metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_metal) music which began in the mid-1990s.[^n10][^o277] Less common labels applied at some point include [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music),[^o278] [rap rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap_rock),[^o279] [rap metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap_metal),[^o280][^o285] [funk metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_metal),[^o281][^o282] [groove metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_metal)[^o283] and [hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock).[^o284] The band is characterized by a style that incorporates [downtuned guitars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtuned_guitar), a groove-heavy rhythm section, haunted atmospheric production, and dark lyrics.[^o285] Their lyrics focus on pain and personal alienation rather than traditional heavy metal themes.[^n34][^o287]

Historically, when *Life Is Peachy* was released, the band's music was not yet labeled "nu metal". Thus, in November 1996, Korn was described as "one of the key players in an American metal revival" alongside [Deftones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deftones), "two bands that share the same roots".[^o288] In the music community, there have been disagreements whether the band should be considered as "heavy metal". In 2015, lead singer Jonathan Davis commented, "Yeah, we're heavy and downtuned, but metal, to me, is like [Judas Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_Priest) and [Iron Maiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Maiden), that's metal man. I always thought of us as a [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk) band."[^o289] Additionally, in an interview with [Metal Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Hammer), Davis said that he had hated the "nu metal" tag. Korn is widely credited as the originators of the nu metal scene. The genre was suddenly inundated with imitators of their sound.[^o290] [Brian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Welch) told *Metal Hammer*: "We never gave that tag to ourselves, it was whoever made it up, you know? When you're in a band, you want to call the shots, you want to be in charge of your branding, and it was someone else who branded us that. And so we were like, 'No, that's not cool.' So we didn't like it." Davis changed his stance on the nu metal tag later: "If we invented nu-metal then fuck yeah, cool. It's pretty cool to say we helped invent some kind of movement, that's pretty insane. The last big movement was us. Other bands helped along the way, but we spearheaded that whole thing. \[...\] When we first started we were like, 'We're not a metal band, y'all' then they'd go 'Oh, then you're nu-metal!' Well fuck you!"[^o291]

Bassist Reginald Arvizu plays his instrument using both the techniques of [fingerstyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerstyle_guitar) and [slapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slapping_%28music%29). Jonathan Davis was said by author Doug Small to be "the eye of the storm around which the music of Korn rages."[^n35] Small described the band as "a basket-case full of contradictions."[^n35]

### Lyrical themes {#Lyrical_themes}

Many of Korn's first works are based on early experiences. The song "[Daddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daddy_%28Korn_song%29)" was described by lead singer [Jonathan Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Davis): "When I was a kid, I was being abused by somebody else and I went to my parents and told them about it, and they thought I was lying and joking around. They never did shit about it. They didn't believe it was happening to their son.... I don't really like to talk about that song. This is as much as I've ever talked about it..."[^n12][^o296][^n36] "Kill You" was written about Davis's experiences as a child with his stepmother.[^n37] *Follow the Leader* marked the first album where the majority of the lyrics did not have origins relating to early occurrences, with songs like "Justin" and "Pretty" written about incidents occurring during adulthood.[^n38]

### Influences {#Influences}

A few years before establishing Korn, James "Munky" Shaffer; Reginald "Fieldy" Arvizu; and David Silveria (who were three members of the [funk metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_metal) band [L.A.P.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A.P.D._%28band%29)), alongside friend Brian "Head" Welch, all had the common influences of [Red Hot Chili Peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Chili_Peppers) and especially the [Faith No More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_No_More) album *[The Real Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Real_Thing_%28Faith_No_More_album%29)*.[^o301][^o314] Likewise, Davis was greatly inspired by the adventurousness of *The Real Thing* upon its 1989 release: "It showed everybody you could do heavy music and not be 'metal'. It was something completely different."[^o302]

In the *Guitar World* section "The Album that Changed My Life" of January 1997, Welch listed *The Real Thing* by Faith No More as the album that changed his life, while Arvizu named both the Red Hot Chili Peppers' *[Freaky Styley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freaky_Styley)* and Faith No More's *[Introduce Yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduce_Yourself)*.[^o303] Fieldy's biggest bass influences were [Flea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flea_%28musician%29) from Red Hot Chili Peppers and [Les Claypool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Claypool) from [Primus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primus_%28band%29). He was also greatly inspired by [Billy Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Gould)'s chemistry with his Faith No More bandmates and [Stanley Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Clarke).[^o304] In 2015, asked about what record has stuck with him through the years, Munky first named 1992's *[Angel Dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Dust_%28Faith_No_More_album%29)* by Faith No More, followed by *[Evil Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Empire_%28album%29)* by Rage Against the Machine and *[The Fragile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fragile)* by [Nine Inch Nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails).[^o305] For his part, David Silveria named [Mike Bordin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Bordin) of Faith No More one of his biggest influences.[^o306]

Munky later acknowledged the song structures and instrumentations of Faith No More in inspiring Korn's future songwriting: "Faith No More steered us in the direction where we ultimately ended up."[^o307] They became "fanatics" of Faith No More singer [Mike Patton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Patton), researched about him, and traveled to the band's hometown of San Francisco in order to watch them play. In this way, they discovered Patton's [avant-garde metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde_music) band [Mr. Bungle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Bungle), whose [1991 self-titled album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Bungle_%28album%29) had a long-standing impact on Korn's experimentation, dissonance, and eccentric stylings.[^o314][^o307] A notable example of Bungle's influence has been what Korn dubbed the "Mr. Bungle chord" (a flat fifth chord or "[tritone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritone)"), which became a band trademark throughout their discography.[^o314][^o308] Many reviewers point out stark similarities between the vocals of Davis and several techniques employed by Patton, such as those from *Angel Dust*.[^o309][^o310][^o311][^o312][^o313] Davis' rhythmic and aggressive singing was also based on [toasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toasting_%28Jamaican_music%29), a [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae)/[dancehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancehall) vocal style that emphasizes rhythm and embellishments.[^o314]

Besides the projects of Patton, Korn had a wide range of influences that included [groove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_%28music%29)-oriented metal acts such as [Sepultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepultura), [Prong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prong_%28band%29), and [Biohazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biohazard_%28band%29), in addition to the dynamics and live performances of [Rage Against the Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rage_Against_the_Machine) and its frontman [Zack de la Rocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_de_la_Rocha). Head later explained that they tried to mix the weird elements of Bungle with catchy choruses and melodies, alongside the songwriting and grooves from those metal bands.[^o314] On the other hand, Fieldy discards any influence [classic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_rock) groups like [The Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones) and [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin) have had on Korn; "Nobody in the band ever listened to that stuff."[^o315] However, Jonathan Davis acknowledged in 2022 that the song that first made him interested in rock music was Led Zeppelin's "[Whole Lotta Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Lotta_Love)" (1969).[^o316]

Guitarist Munky and Head described their admiration for [Steve Vai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Vai)'s ability to "make the guitar talk," saying it inspired how they approached tone and expression. Head specifically pointed out that Munky's fascination with Vai's 7‑string work led him to get one, and eventually Head followed suit. That low-register sound helped define Korn's signature groove, a musical shift they credit directly to Vai.[^o317]

Much of Korn's work has also been inspired by [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop), as suggested in the band's song "[All in the Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_in_the_Family_%28song%29)" and the [cover song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_song) of Ice Cube's "[Wicked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicked_%28Ice_Cube_song%29#Ko%D0%AFn_version)".[^n39][^n38][^o320] The band imitated the [samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_%28music%29) used by [Cypress Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress_Hill), with Davis going as far to say "If there's no Cypress Hill there would be no Korn."[^o321] The members of Korn are also influenced by [Alice in Chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_Chains),[^o322] [Pantera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantera),[^o323] [N.W.A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N.W.A),[^o325][^o307] [Bauhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus_%28band%29),[^o324] [Geto Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geto_Boys),[^o325] [Van Halen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Halen),[^o307] [The Pharcyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pharcyde),[^o307] [Nine Inch Nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails),[^o307] [Deftones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deftones),[^o307] and [John Zorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Zorn).[^o301][^o326]

## In other media {#In_other_media}

They have contributed music to the soundtracks for movies such as *[The Crow: City of Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crow:_City_of_Angels)*,[^o328] *[I Know What You Did Last Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_What_You_Did_Last_Summer)*,[^o329] *[Spawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spawn_%281997_film%29)*,[^o330] *[End of Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_Days_%28film%29)*,[^o331] and *[Lara Croft: Tomb Raider – The Cradle of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_Croft:_Tomb_Raider_–_The_Cradle_of_Life)*.[^o332] The music of Korn has also been featured on various television series, including *[South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)* (which [they starred as guests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korn's_Groovy_Pirate_Ghost_Mystery)),[^o333] *[Beavis and Butt-head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beavis_and_Butt-head)*,[^o334] and *[Daria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria)*.[^o335]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The band has sold over 40 million records worldwide as of 2021.[^o337][^o338] Seven of their official releases are certified platinum by the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA); two are certified double platinum; one is certified triple platinum; one is certified five times platinum; and two are certified Gold.[^o339] Fourteen of their records have reached the top 10 of the *Billboard* 200, with eight reaching the top five;[^o340] they are tied with [Van Halen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Halen) for the most top 10 records as a rock or metal artist.[^o341] The band has also won two Grammy Awards from eight nominations.[^o342][^o343]

In the book *Nu-metal: The Next Generation of Rock & Punk*, Korn was marked as the third biggest nu metal band in the world.[^n40] Greg Kennelty of *[Metal Injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Injection)* expressed the opinion that the band "practically invented" the genre, and stated that "their fingerprints are all over [modern metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_metal)."[^o345] Korn created a fan-base described by Doug Small and [Eaton Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eaton_Corporation) as extremely loyal.[^o346] Lauryn Schaffner of *Loudwire* referred to Korn as one of the "Big Four" of 1990s heavy metal, along with [Sepultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepultura), [Pantera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantera) and [Tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_%28band%29).[^o347] Staff of the same publication also listed them as the 37th-best metal band of all time in a 2016 article.[^o348]

Korn's debut album has been recognized for its influence on heavy music. Bands such as [Coal Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_Chamber) and Limp Bizkit were inspired by the album's "churning rage, emphasizing similar grooves and song structures" and "the sound's hip-hop elements."[^n12] [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_%28band%29), [Machine Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_Head_%28band%29), and [Sepultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepultura) were also inspired by Korn's debut album.[^n12] The album launched the career of [record producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_producer) [Ross Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Robinson),[^n11] who later produced albums such as *[Three Dollar Bill, Yall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Dollar_Bill,_Yall)* by Limp Bizkit, Sepultura's *[Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_%28Sepultura_album%29)* and Slipknot's first two albums.[^o352][^o353][^o354][^o355] Of their third album, Richard S. He of *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_%28magazine%29)* wrote: "More than any other album, *Follow the Leader* opened the floodgates for countless nu-metal acts to infiltrate the mainstream."[^o356] [Amy Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Lee) of [Evanescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanescence) also cited Korn as an influence growing up.[^o357][^o358] [Mucc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucc) guitarist [Miya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miya_%28musician%29) said that Davis' intense personal expressions in Korn influenced him to do the same in his own music.[^o359]

Korn's music videos received significant play on *Total Request Live*, and alongside Limp Bizkit, the band was credited with pitting the nu metal genre against pop acts such as [Backstreet Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backstreet_Boys), [NSYNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSYNC) and [Britney Spears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britney_Spears).[^n41][^o360] The band's videos had often peaked at the third spot of the show's chart, which was dubbed "the Korn spot".[^n41] Four of their videos had retired from the show.[^n41] They have also earned two Video Music Awards from 11 nominations.[^o361]
