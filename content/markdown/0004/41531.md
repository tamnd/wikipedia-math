# Stanisław Ulam

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stanisław Ulam"}

**Stanisław Marcin Ulam** (Polish: s; 13 April 1909 – 13 May 1984) was a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people)-American mathematician who made important contributions in advancing the understanding of nuclear physics and computer science. He participated in the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project), originated the [Teller–Ulam design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Teller–Ulam_design) of [thermonuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_weapon), recognised the concept of the [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342), invented the [Monte Carlo method of computation](wikipedia://en/56098), and championed [nuclear pulse propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_pulse_propulsion). In [pure](wikipedia://en/457210) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), he proved a number of theorems and proposed several conjectures.

:::

Born into a wealthy [Polish Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Jewish) family in [Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemberg), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), Ulam studied mathematics at the [Lwów Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Polytechnic#Second_Polish_Republic), where he earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1933 under the supervision of [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) and [Włodzimierz Stożek](wikipedia://en/1821218). In 1935, [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), whom Ulam had met in Warsaw, invited him to come to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), for a few months. From 1936 to 1939, he spent summers in Poland and academic years at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts), where he worked to establish important results regarding [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986). On 20 August 1939, he sailed for the United States for the last time with his 17-year-old brother [Adam Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Ulam). He became an assistant professor at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1940, and a United States citizen in 1941.

In October 1943, he received an invitation from [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe) to join the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project) at the secret [Los Alamos Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_Laboratory) in New Mexico. There, he worked on the [hydrodynamic](wikipedia://en/11034) calculations to predict the behavior of the [explosive lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_lens) that were needed by an [implosion-type weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design#Implosion-type_weapon). He was assigned to [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller)'s group, where he worked on [Teller's "Super" bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teller's_"Super"_bomb) for Teller and [Enrico Fermi](wikipedia://en/10264). After the war he left to become an associate professor at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California), but returned to Los Alamos in 1946 to work on [thermonuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design). With the aid of a cadre of female "[computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_computers#Wartime_computing_and_the_invention_of_electronic_computing)" he found that Teller's "Super" design was unworkable. In January 1951, Ulam and Teller came up with the [Teller–Ulam design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teller–Ulam_design), which became the basis for all thermonuclear weapons.

Ulam considered the problem of [nuclear propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_propulsion) of rockets, which was pursued by [Project Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Rover), and proposed, as an alternative to Rover's [nuclear thermal rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_thermal_rocket), to harness small nuclear explosions for propulsion, which became [Project Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Orion_%28nuclear_propulsion%29). With Fermi, [John Pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pasta), and [Mary Tsingou](wikipedia://en/37784250), Ulam studied the [Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou problem](wikipedia://en/2864857), which became the inspiration for the field of nonlinear science. He is probably best known for realizing that electronic computers made it practical to apply statistical methods to [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) without known [solutions](wikipedia://en/537048), and as computers have developed, the [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098) has become a common and standard approach to many problems.

## Poland {#Poland}

Stanisław Marcin Ulam was born in [Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv#Habsburg_Empire), [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_%28Eastern_Europe%29), on 13 April 1909.[^o50][^o103][^o4] At this time, Galicia was in the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria) of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), which was known to Poles as the [Austrian partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_partition). In 1918, it became part of the newly restored Poland, the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic), and the city took its Polish name again, [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv#Interwar_period).[^o116]

The Ulams were a wealthy [Polish Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Jewish) family of bankers, industrialists, and other professionals. Ulam's immediate family was "well-to-do but hardly rich".[^o44] His father, Józef Ulam, was born in Lwów and was a lawyer,[^o116] and his mother, Anna (née Auerbach), was born in [Stryj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryi).[^o61] His uncle, Michał Ulam, was an architect, building contractor, and lumber industrialist.[^o93] From 1916 until 1918, Józef's family lived temporarily in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866).[^o130] After they returned, Lwów became the epicenter of the [Polish–Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Ukrainian_War), during which the city experienced [a Ukrainian siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lemberg_%281918%29).[^o116]

*Image: Lwow - Kawiarnia Szkocka.jpg.* The [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651)'s building in [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv), Ukraine, now houses the Szkocka Restaurant & Bar (named for the original Scottish Café).

In 1919, Ulam entered Lwów Gymnasium Nr. VII, from which he graduated in 1927.[^o42] He then studied mathematics at the Lwów Polytechnic Institute. Under the supervision of [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075), he received his [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree in 1932, and became a [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science#Poland) in 1933.[^o130][^o5] At the age of 20, in 1929, he published his first paper *Concerning Functions of Sets* in the journal *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)*.[^o5] From 1931 until 1935, he traveled to and studied in [Wilno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilno) (Vilnius), Vienna, [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich), [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), and [Cambridge, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he met [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](wikipedia://en/145319).[^o117]

Along with [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162), [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425), [Włodzimierz Stożek](wikipedia://en/1821218), Kuratowski, and others, Ulam was a member of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328). Its founders were [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735) and [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), who were professors at the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv#Jan_Kazimierz_University_%281919–39%29). Mathematicians of this "school" met for long hours at the [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651), where the problems they discussed were collected in the [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115), a thick notebook provided by Banach's wife. Ulam was a major contributor to the book. Of the 193 problems recorded between 1935 and 1941, he contributed 40 problems as a single author, another 11 with Banach and Mazur, and an additional 15 with others. In 1957, he received from Steinhaus a copy of the book, which had survived the war, and translated it into English.[^o47] In 1981, Ulam's friend R. Daniel Mauldin published an expanded and annotated version.[^o90]

## Move to the United States {#Move_to_the_United_States}

In 1935, [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), whom Ulam had met in Warsaw, invited him to come to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), for a few months. In December of that year, Ulam sailed to the US. At Princeton, he went to lectures and seminars, where he heard [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen), [James Alexander](wikipedia://en/9848523), and [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736). During a tea party at von Neumann's house, he encountered [G. D. Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._D._Birkhoff), who suggested that he apply for a position with the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows).[^o130] Following up on Birkhoff's suggestion, Ulam spent summers in Poland and academic years at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) from 1936 to 1939, where he worked with [John C. Oxtoby](wikipedia://en/47435441) to establish results regarding [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986). These appeared in *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* in 1941.[^o42][^o106]

On 20 August 1939, in [Gdynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdynia), Józef Ulam, along with his brother Szymon, put his two sons, Stanislaw and 17-year-old [Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Ulam), on a ship headed for the US.[^o130] Eleven days later, the [Germans invaded Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland#German_invasion). Within two months, the Germans completed their [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Poland_%281939–1945%29) of western Poland, and the Soviets [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland#Soviet_invasion) and occupied eastern Poland. Within two years, Józef Ulam and the rest of his family, including Stanislaw's sister Stefania Ulam, were victims of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust), [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735) was in hiding, [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) was lecturing at the [underground university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Poland_during_World_War_II) in Warsaw, [Włodzimierz Stożek](wikipedia://en/1821218) and his two sons had been killed in the [massacre of Lwów professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_Lwów_professors), and the last problem had been recorded in the [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115). [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) survived the Nazi occupation by feeding [lice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lice) at [Rudolf Weigl's typhus research institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Weigl). In 1963, [Adam Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Ulam), who had become an eminent [kremlinologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlinology) at Harvard,[^o40] received a letter from George Volsky,[^o131] who hid in Józef Ulam's house after deserting from the Polish army. This reminiscence gave a chilling account of Lwów's chaotic scenes in late 1939.[^o94] In later life Ulam described himself as "an agnostic. Sometimes I muse deeply on the forces that are for me invisible. When I am almost close to the idea of God, I feel immediately estranged by the horrors of this world, which he seems to tolerate".[^o104] In 1940, after being recommended by Birkhoff, Ulam became an assistant professor at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). Here, he became a [United States citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_citizen) in 1941.[^o130] That year, he married [Françoise Aron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Aron_Ulam).[^o42] She had been a French exchange student at [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College), whom he met in Cambridge. They had one daughter, Claire. In [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin), Ulam met his friend and colleague C. J. Everett, with whom he collaborated on a number of papers.[^o118]

## Manhattan Project {#Manhattan_Project}

*Image: Ulam-stanislaw m.jpg.* Ulam's ID badge photo from [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory)

In early 1943, Ulam asked von Neumann to find him a war job. In October, he received an invitation to join an unidentified project near [Santa Fe, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe,_New_Mexico).[^o130] The letter was signed by [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe), who had been appointed as leader of the theoretical division of [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory) by [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer), its scientific director.[^o119] Knowing nothing of the area, he borrowed a New Mexico guide book. On the checkout card, he found the names of his Wisconsin colleagues, [Joan Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Hinton), [David Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_H._Frisch), and [Joseph McKibben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Laws_McKibben), all of whom had mysteriously disappeared.[^o130] This was Ulam's introduction to the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project), which was the US's wartime effort to create the atomic bomb.[^o89]

### Hydrodynamical calculations of implosion {#Hydrodynamical_calculations_of_implosion}

A few weeks after Ulam reached [Los Alamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos,_New_Mexico) in February 1944, the project experienced a crisis. In April, [Emilio Segrè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_G._Segrè) discovered that [plutonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium) made in [reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactors#Early_reactors) would not work in a [gun-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun-type_fission_weapon) plutonium weapon like the "[Thin Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thin_Man_nuclear_bomb)", which was being developed in parallel with a uranium weapon, the "[Little Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Boy)" that was dropped on [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki#Hiroshima). This problem threatened to waste an enormous investment in new reactors at the [Hanford site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanford_site) and to make slow uranium [isotope separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_separation) the only way to prepare [fissile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fissile) material suitable for use in bombs. To respond, Oppenheimer implemented, in August, a sweeping reorganization of the laboratory to focus on development of an [implosion-type weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design#Implosion-type_weapon) and appointed [George Kistiakowsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Kistiakowsky) head of the implosion department. He was a professor at Harvard and an expert on precise use of explosives.[^o77]

The basic concept of [implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implosion_%28mechanical_process%29) is to use chemical explosives to crush a chunk of fissile material into a [critical mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_mass), where [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron) multiplication leads to a [nuclear chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction), releasing a large amount of energy. Cylindrical implosive configurations had been studied by [Seth Neddermeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Neddermeyer), but von Neumann, who had experience with [shaped charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaped_charge) used in [armor-piercing ammunition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-explosive_anti-tank), was a [vocal advocate of spherical implosion](wikipedia://en/15942#Manhattan_Project) driven by [explosive lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_lens). He realized that the symmetry and speed with which implosion compressed the plutonium were critical issues,[^o77] and enlisted Ulam to help design lens configurations that would provide nearly spherical implosion. Within an implosion, because of enormous pressures and high temperatures, solid materials behave much like fluids. This meant that [hydrodynamical](wikipedia://en/11034) calculations were needed to predict and minimize asymmetries that would spoil a nuclear detonation. Of these calculations, Ulam said:The hydrodynamical problem was simply stated, but very difficult to calculate – not only in detail, but even in order of magnitude. In this discussion, I stressed pure pragmatism and the necessity to get a heuristic survey of the problem by simple-minded brute force, rather than by massive numerical work.[^o130]

Nevertheless, with the primitive facilities available at the time, Ulam and von Neumann did carry out numerical computations that led to a satisfactory design. This motivated their advocacy of a powerful computational capability at Los Alamos, which began during the war years,[^o55] continued through the cold war, and still exists.[^o56] [Otto Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Frisch) remembered Ulam as "a brilliant Polish topologist with a charming French wife. At once he told me that he was a pure mathematician who had sunk so low that his latest paper actually contained numbers with decimal points!"[^o64]

### Statistics of branching and multiplicative processes {#Statistics_of_branching_and_multiplicative_processes}

Even the inherent statistical fluctuations of [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron) multiplication within a [chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_reaction) have implications with regard to implosion speed and symmetry. In November 1944, [David Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hawkins_%28philosopher%29)[^o71] and Ulam addressed this problem in a report entitled "Theory of Multiplicative Processes".[^o78] This report, which invokes [probability-generating functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability-generating_function), is also an early entry in the extensive literature on statistics of [branching](wikipedia://en/377202) and multiplicative processes. In 1948, its scope was extended by Ulam and Everett.[^o102]

Early in the Manhattan project, [Enrico Fermi](wikipedia://en/10264)'s attention was focused on the use of reactors to produce plutonium. In September 1944, he arrived at Los Alamos, shortly after breathing life into the [first Hanford reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project#Hanford_reactors), which had been [poisoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_poison) by a [xenon isotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon-135).[^o74] Soon after Fermi's arrival, [Teller's "Super" bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teller's_"Super"_bomb) group, of which Ulam was a part, was transferred to a new division headed by Fermi.[^o120] Fermi and Ulam formed a relationship that became very fruitful after the war.[^o121]

## Post-war Los Alamos {#Post-war_Los_Alamos}

In September 1945, Ulam left Los Alamos to become an associate professor at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California). In January 1946, he suffered an acute attack of [encephalitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encephalitis), which put his life in danger, but which was alleviated by emergency brain surgery. During his recuperation, many friends visited, including [Nicholas Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Metropolis) from Los Alamos and the famous mathematician [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284),[^o60] who remarked: "Stan, you are just like before."[^o130] This was encouraging, because Ulam was concerned about the state of his mental faculties, for he had lost the ability to speak during the crisis. Another friend, [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891), asserted in a 1987 article that the attack changed Ulam's personality: afterwards, he turned from rigorous pure mathematics to more speculative conjectures concerning the application of mathematics to physics and [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology); Rota also cites Ulam's former collaborator Paul Stein as noting that Ulam was sloppier in his clothing afterwards, and John Oxtoby as noting that Ulam before the encephalitis could work for hours on end doing calculations, while when Rota worked with him, was reluctant to solve even a quadratic equation.[^o111] This assertion was not accepted by [Françoise Aron Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Aron_Ulam).[^o65]

By late April 1946, Ulam had recovered enough to attend a secret conference at Los Alamos to discuss [thermonuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design). Those in attendance included Ulam, von Neumann, Metropolis, Teller, [Stan Frankel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Frankel), and others. Throughout his participation in the Manhattan Project, Teller's efforts had been directed toward the development of a "super" weapon based on [nuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion), rather than toward development of a practical fission bomb. After extensive discussion, the participants reached a consensus that his ideas were worthy of further exploration. A few weeks later, Ulam received an offer of a position at Los Alamos from Metropolis and [Robert D. Richtmyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Richtmyer), the new head of its theoretical division, at a higher salary, and the Ulams returned to Los Alamos.[^o122]

### Monte Carlo method {#Monte_Carlo_method}

*Image: STAN ULAM HOLDING THE FERMIAC.jpg.* Stan Ulam holding the [FERMIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FERMIAC)

Late in the war, under the sponsorship of von Neumann, Frankel and Metropolis began to carry out calculations on the first general-purpose electronic computer, the [ENIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENIAC) at the Aberdeen Proving Ground in Maryland. Shortly after returning to Los Alamos, Ulam participated in a review of results from these calculations.[^o92] Earlier, while playing [solitaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitaire) during his recovery from surgery, Ulam had thought about playing hundreds of games to estimate statistically the probability of a successful outcome.[^o59] With ENIAC in mind, he realized that the availability of computers made such statistical methods very practical. John von Neumann immediately saw the significance of this insight. In March 1947 he proposed a statistical approach to the problem of neutron diffusion in fissionable material.[^o84] Because Ulam had often mentioned his uncle, Michał Ulam, "who just had to go to Monte Carlo" to gamble, Metropolis dubbed the statistical approach "The [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098)".[^o92] Metropolis and Ulam published the first unclassified paper on the Monte Carlo method in 1949.[^o99]

Fermi, learning of Ulam's breakthrough, devised an [analog computer](wikipedia://en/2428) known as the [Monte Carlo trolley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_trolley), later dubbed the [FERMIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FERMIAC). The device performed a mechanical simulation of random diffusion of neutrons. As computers improved in speed and programmability, these methods became more useful. In particular, many Monte Carlo calculations carried out on modern [massively parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massively_parallel) [supercomputers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer) are [embarrassingly parallel](wikipedia://en/1738712) applications, whose results can be very accurate.[^o56]

### Teller–Ulam design {#Teller–Ulam_design}

On 29 August 1949, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) tested its first fission bomb, the [RDS-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS-1). Created under the supervision of [Lavrentiy Beria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavrentiy_Beria), who sought to duplicate the US effort, this weapon was nearly identical to [Fat Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Man), for its design was based on information provided by spies [Klaus Fuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Fuchs), [Theodore Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Hall), and [David Greenglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Greenglass). In response, on 31 January 1950, [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) announced a crash program to develop a fusion bomb.[^o58]

To advocate an aggressive development program, [Ernest Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Lawrence) and [Luis Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Walter_Alvarez) came to Los Alamos, where they conferred with [Norris Bradbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norris_Bradbury), the laboratory director, and with [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow), [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller), and Ulam. Soon, these three became members of a short-lived committee appointed by Bradbury to study the problem, with Teller as chairman.[^o130] At this time, research on the use of a fission weapon to create a [fusion reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion) had been ongoing since 1942, but the design was still essentially the one originally proposed by Teller. His concept was to put [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium) and/or [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium) in close proximity to a fission bomb, with the hope that the heat and intense flux of neutrons released when the bomb exploded would ignite a self-sustaining [fusion reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion). Reactions of these [isotopes of hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotopes_of_hydrogen) are of interest because the energy per unit mass of fuel released by their fusion is much larger than that from fission of heavy nuclei.[^o108]

*Image: IvyMike2.jpg.* [Ivy Mike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Mike), the first full test of the Teller–Ulam design (a [staged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design#Two-stage_thermonuclear_weapons) fusion bomb), with a [yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_equivalent) of 10.4 megatons on 1 November 1952

Because the results of calculations based on Teller's concept were discouraging, many scientists believed it [could not lead to a successful weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Teller–Ulam_design#Teller's_"Super"), while others had moral and economic grounds for not proceeding. Consequently, several senior people of the Manhattan Project opposed development, including Bethe and Oppenheimer.[^o75] To clarify the situation, Ulam and von Neumann resolved to do new calculations to determine whether Teller's approach was feasible. To carry out these studies, von Neumann decided to use electronic computers: ENIAC at Aberdeen, a new computer, [MANIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MANIAC_I), at Princeton, and its twin, which was under construction at Los Alamos. Ulam enlisted Everett to follow a completely different approach, one guided by physical intuition. [Françoise Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Aron_Ulam) was one of[^o123] a cadre of women "[computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_computers#Wartime_computing_and_the_invention_of_electronic_computing)" who carried out laborious and extensive computations of thermonuclear scenarios on [mechanical calculators](wikipedia://en/432897), supplemented and confirmed by Everett's [slide rule](wikipedia://en/28743). Ulam and Fermi collaborated on further analysis of these scenarios. The results showed that, in workable configurations, a thermonuclear reaction would not ignite, and if ignited, it would not be self-sustaining. Ulam had used his expertise in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) to analyze the chain reaction in deuterium, which was much more complicated than the ones in uranium and plutonium, and he concluded that no self-sustaining chain reaction would take place at the (low) densities that Teller was considering.[^o68] In late 1950, these conclusions were confirmed by von Neumann's results.[^o65][^o109]

In January 1951, Ulam had another idea: to channel the mechanical shock of a nuclear explosion so as to compress the fusion fuel. On the recommendation of his wife,[^o65] Ulam discussed this idea with Bradbury and Mark before he told Teller about it.[^o91] Almost immediately, Teller saw its merit, but noted that soft [X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-rays) from the fission bomb would compress the thermonuclear fuel more strongly than mechanical shock and suggested ways to enhance this effect. On 9 March 1951, Teller and Ulam submitted a joint report describing these innovations.[^o85] A few weeks later, Teller suggested placing a [fissile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fissile) rod or cylinder at the center of the fusion fuel. The detonation of this "spark plug"[^o86] would help to initiate and enhance the fusion reaction. The design based on these ideas, called staged radiation implosion, has become the standard way to build thermonuclear weapons. It is often described as the "[Teller–Ulam design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teller–Ulam_design)".[^o110]

*Image: Ivy Mike Sausage device.jpg.* The *Sausage* device of [Mike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Mike) nuclear test (yield 10.4 Mt) on [Enewetak Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enewetak_Atoll). The test was part of the [Operation Ivy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ivy). The Sausage was the first true H-Bomb ever tested, meaning the first [thermonuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_weapon) device built upon the [Teller-Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teller–Ulam_design) principles of staged radiation implosion.

In September 1951, after a series of differences with Bradbury and other scientists, Teller resigned from Los Alamos, and returned to the University of Chicago.[^o76] At about the same time, Ulam went on leave as a visiting professor at Harvard for a semester.[^o124] Although Teller and Ulam submitted a joint report on their design[^o85] and jointly applied for a patent on it,[^o89] they soon became involved in a dispute over who deserved credit.[^o91] After the war, Bethe returned to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), but he was deeply involved in the development of thermonuclear weapons as a consultant. In 1954, he wrote an article on the history of the H-bomb,[^o45] which presents his opinion that both men contributed very significantly to the breakthrough. This balanced view is shared by others who were involved, including Mark and Fermi, but Teller persistently attempted to downplay Ulam's role.[^o113] "After the H-bomb was made," Bethe recalled, "reporters started to call Teller the father of the H-bomb. For the sake of history, I think it is more precise to say that Ulam is the father, because he provided the seed, and Teller is the mother, because he remained with the child. As for me, I guess I am the midwife."[^o112]

With the basic fusion reactions confirmed, and with a feasible design in hand, there was nothing to prevent Los Alamos from testing a thermonuclear device. On 1 November 1952, the first thermonuclear explosion occurred when [Ivy Mike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Mike) was detonated on [Enewetak Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enewetak_Atoll), within the US [Pacific Proving Grounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Proving_Grounds). This device, which used liquid deuterium as its fusion fuel, was immense and utterly unusable as a weapon. Nevertheless, its success validated the Teller–Ulam design, and stimulated intensive development of practical weapons.[^o124]

### Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou problem {#Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou_problem}

When Ulam returned to Los Alamos, his attention turned away from weapon design and toward the use of computers to investigate problems in physics and mathematics. With [John Pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pasta), who helped Metropolis to bring MANIAC on line in March 1952, he explored these ideas in a report "Heuristic Studies in Problems of Mathematical Physics on High Speed Computing Machines", which was submitted on 9 June 1953. It treated several problems that cannot be addressed within the framework of traditional analytic methods: billowing of fluids, rotational motion in gravitating systems, magnetic lines of force, and hydrodynamic instabilities.[^o79]

Soon, Pasta and Ulam became experienced with electronic computation on MANIAC, and by this time, Enrico Fermi had settled into a routine of spending academic years at the University of Chicago and summers at Los Alamos. During these summer visits, Pasta, Ulam, and [Mary Tsingou](wikipedia://en/37784250), a programmer in the MANIAC group, joined him to study a variation of the classic problem of a string of masses held together by springs that exert forces linearly proportional to their displacement from equilibrium.[^o14] Fermi proposed to add to this force a nonlinear component, which could be chosen to be proportional to either the square or cube of the displacement, or to a more complicated "broken linear" function. This addition is the key element of the [Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou problem](wikipedia://en/2864857), which is often designated by the abbreviation FPUT.[^o80][^o62]

A classical spring system can be described in terms of vibrational modes, which are analogous to the harmonics that occur on a stretched violin string. If the system starts in a particular mode, vibrations in other modes do not develop. With the nonlinear component, Fermi expected energy in one mode to transfer gradually to other modes, and eventually, to be distributed equally among all modes. This is roughly what began to happen shortly after the system was initialized with all its energy in the lowest mode, but much later, essentially all the energy periodically reappeared in the lowest mode.[^o62] This behavior is very different from the expected [equipartition of energy](wikipedia://en/516133). It remained mysterious until 1965, when [Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_David_Kruskal) and [Zabusky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Zabusky) showed that, after appropriate mathematical transformations, the system can be described by the [Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation), which is the prototype of nonlinear [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) that have [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) solutions. This means that FPUT behavior can be understood in terms of solitons.[^o49]

### Nuclear propulsion {#Nuclear_propulsion}

*Image: NASA-project-orion-artist.jpg.* An artist's conception of the NASA reference design for the Project Orion spacecraft powered by nuclear propulsion

Starting in 1955, Ulam and [Frederick Reines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Reines) considered [nuclear propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_propulsion) of aircraft and rockets.[^o88] This is an attractive possibility, because the nuclear energy per unit mass of fuel is a million times greater than that available from chemicals. From 1955 to 1972, their ideas were pursued during [Project Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Rover), which explored the use of nuclear reactors to power rockets.[^o125] In response to a question by Senator [John O. Pastore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_O._Pastore) at a congressional committee hearing on "Outer Space Propulsion by Nuclear Energy", on January 22, 1958, Ulam replied that "the future as a whole of mankind is to some extent involved inexorably now with going outside the globe."[^o73]

Ulam and C. J. Everett also proposed, in contrast to [Rover's continuous heating of rocket exhaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_thermal_rocket), to harness small nuclear explosions for propulsion.[^o87] [Project Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Orion_%28nuclear_propulsion%29) was a study of this idea. It began in 1958 and ended in 1965, after the [Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_Nuclear_Test_Ban_Treaty) of 1963 banned nuclear weapons tests in the atmosphere and in space.[^o105] Work on this project was spearheaded by physicist [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397), who commented on the decision to end Orion in his article, "Death of a Project".[^o57]

Bradbury appointed Ulam and [John H. Manley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Manley) as research advisors to the laboratory director in 1957. These newly created positions were on the same administrative level as division leaders, and Ulam held his until he retired from Los Alamos. In this capacity, he was able to influence and guide programs in many divisions: theoretical, physics, chemistry, metallurgy, weapons, health, Rover, and others.[^o125]

In addition to these activities, Ulam continued to publish technical reports and research papers. One of these introduced the [Fermi–Ulam model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi–Ulam_model), an extension of Fermi's theory of [the acceleration of cosmic rays](wikipedia://en/3035156).[^o67] Another, with Paul Stein and [Mary Tsingou](wikipedia://en/37784250), titled "Quadratic Transformations", was an early investigation of [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295) and is considered the first published use of the phrase "[chaotic behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaotic_behavior)".[^o39][^o81]

## Return to academia {#Return_to_academia}

*Image: Ulam 1.png.* When the positive integers are arrayed along the [Ulam spiral](wikipedia://en/67378), prime numbers, represented by dots, tend to collect along diagonal lines.

During his years at Los Alamos, Ulam was a visiting professor at Harvard from 1951 to 1952, [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) from 1956 to 1957, the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), in 1963, and the [University of Colorado at Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder) from 1961 to 1962 and 1965 to 1967. In 1967, the last of these positions became permanent, when Ulam was appointed Professor and Chairman of the Department of Mathematics at the University of Colorado. He kept a residence in Santa Fe, which made it convenient to spend summers at Los Alamos as a consultant.[^o41] He was an elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o17][^o18][^o19]

In Colorado, where he rejoined his friends Gamow, Richtmyer, and Hawkins, Ulam's research interests turned toward [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology). In 1968, recognizing this emphasis, the [University of Colorado School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_School_of_Medicine) appointed Ulam as Professor of Biomathematics, and he held this position until his death. With his Los Alamos colleague Robert Schrandt he published a report, "Some Elementary Attempts at Numerical Modeling of Problems Concerning Rates of Evolutionary Processes", which applied his earlier ideas on branching processes to evolution.[^o82] Another, report, with William Beyer, [Temple F. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_F._Smith), and M. L. Stein, titled "Metrics in Biology", introduced new ideas about numerical taxonomy and evolutionary distances.[^o83]

When he retired from Colorado in 1975, Ulam began to spend winter semesters at the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida), where he was a graduate research professor. In 1976, he was awarded the Commander's Cross with the Star of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) by the [Polish government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_government-in-exile) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London).[^o20] Except for [sabbaticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical) at the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis) from 1982 to 1983, and at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University) from 1980 to 1984,[^o41] this pattern of spending summers in Colorado and Los Alamos and winters in Florida continued until Ulam died of an apparent heart attack in Santa Fe on 13 May 1984.[^o103] [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) noted that "he died suddenly of heart failure, without fear or pain, while he could still prove and conjecture."[^o60] In 1987, [Françoise Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Aron_Ulam) deposited his papers with the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) Library in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia).[^o107] She continued to live in Santa Fe until she died in 2011, at the age of 93. Both Françoise and her husband were buried with her family in [Montparnasse Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montparnasse_Cemetery) in Paris.[^o66][^o72]

## Coining of the term "singularity" {#Coining_of_the_term_"singularity"}

Ulam reported in 1958 an earlier discussion with [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) "centered on the [accelerating progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_change) of technology and changes in human life, which gives the appearance of approaching some essential [singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity) in the history of the race beyond which human affairs, as we know them, could not continue".[^o22]

## Impact and legacy {#Impact_and_legacy}

Ulam participated in the creation of a [hydrogen bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bomb) as part of the [Los Alamos Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_Laboratory) nuclear project. From the publication of his first paper as a student in 1929 until his death, Ulam was constantly writing on mathematics. The list of Ulam's publications includes more than 150 papers.[^o42] Topics represented by a significant number of papers are: [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) (including [measurable cardinals](wikipedia://en/248102) and abstract [measures](wikipedia://en/19873)), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [transformation theory](wikipedia://en/1904373), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [projective algebra](wikipedia://en/1021753), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).[^o126]

Notable results of this work are:

- [Borsuk–Ulam theorem](wikipedia://en/4064)
- [Mazur–Ulam theorem](wikipedia://en/3794727)
- [Kuratowski–Ulam theorem](wikipedia://en/45249719)
- [Hyers–Ulam–Rassias stability](wikipedia://en/30656533)
- [Lucky number](wikipedia://en/208183)
- [Ulam spiral](wikipedia://en/67378)
- [Ulam conjecture](wikipedia://en/255594) (in graph theory)
- [Ulam's packing conjecture](wikipedia://en/43452583)
- [Ulam's game](wikipedia://en/32177562)
- [Ulam matrix](wikipedia://en/43209954)
- [Ulam numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulam_numbers)
- [Ulam–Warburton automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulam–Warburton_automaton)

Ulam played pivotal role in the development of thermonuclear weapons. According to Françoise Ulam: "Stan would reassure me that, barring accidents, the H-bomb rendered nuclear war impossible."[^o65] In 1980, Ulam and his wife appeared in the television documentary *[The Day After Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Day_After_Trinity)*.

*Image: LuckySieve.gif.* An animation demonstrating the lucky number sieve. The numbers in red are lucky numbers.

The [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098) has become a ubiquitous and standard approach to computation, and the method has been applied to a vast number of scientific problems.[^o95] In addition to problems in physics and mathematics, the method has been applied to [finance](wikipedia://en/1358940), social science,[^o101] [environmental risk assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_assessment),[^o100] linguistics,[^o97] radiation therapy,[^o98] and sports.[^o96]

The [Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou problem](wikipedia://en/2864857) is credited not only as "the birth of experimental mathematics",[^o62] but also as inspiration for the vast field of Nonlinear Science. In his [Lilienfeld Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilienfeld_Prize) lecture, [David K. Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_K._Campbell) noted this relationship and described how FPUT gave rise to ideas in [chaos](wikipedia://en/6295), [solitons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitons), and [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/990632).[^o48] In 1980, [Donald Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Kerr), laboratory director at Los Alamos, with the strong support of Ulam and [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425),[^o51] founded the Center for Nonlinear Studies (CNLS).[^o53] In 1985, CNLS initiated the *Stanislaw M. Ulam Distinguished Scholar* program, which provides an annual award that enables a noted scientist to spend a year carrying out research at Los Alamos.[^o54]

The fiftieth anniversary of the original FPUT paper was the subject of the March 2005 issue of the journal Chaos,[^o63] and the topic of the 25th Annual International Conference of CNLS.[^o52] The [University of Southern Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_Mississippi) and the University of Florida supported the *Ulam Quarterly*,[^o127] which was active from 1992 to 1996, and which was one of the first online mathematical journals.[^o128] Florida's Department of Mathematics has sponsored, since 1998, the annual *Ulam Colloquium Lecture*,[^o114] and in March 2009, the *Ulam Centennial Conference*.[^o115]

Ulam's work on non-[Euclidean distance metrics](wikipedia://en/53932) in the context of molecular biology made a significant contribution to [sequence analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_analysis)[^o69] and his contributions in theoretical biology are considered watersheds in the development of [cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata#History) theory, [population biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_biology), [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706), and biometrics generally (David Sankoff, however, challenged conclusions of Walter by writing that Ulam had only modest influence on early development of sequence alignment methods.[^o70]). Colleagues noted that some of his greatest contributions were in clearly identifying problems to be solved and general techniques for solving them.[^o46]

In 1987, Los Alamos issued a special issue of its *Science* publication, which summarized his accomplishments,[^o129] and which appeared, in 1989, as the book *From Cardinals to Chaos*. Similarly, in 1990, the University of California Press issued a compilation of mathematical reports by Ulam and his Los Alamos collaborators: *Analogies Between Analogies*.[^o43] During his career, Ulam was awarded honorary degrees by the Universities of [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico), [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin), and [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh).[^o130]

The programming language [Stan](wikipedia://en/42243853) is named after Ulam.[^o25]

In 2021, German film director Thorsten Klein made a [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventures_of_a_Mathematician) of Ulam's autobiography, *Adventures of a Mathematician*.[^o26]

In 2019, Polish entomologist Marcin Kamiński introduced the generic name *Ulamus* to honor Ulam's work.[^o27][^o28] *Ulamus* belongs to the molluscan family *Pteriidae*.[^o29]
