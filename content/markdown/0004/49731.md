# Alonzo Church

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alonzo Church"}

**Alonzo Church** (June 14, 1903 – August 11, 1995) was an American [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [logician](wikipedia://en/356213), and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) who made major contributions to [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and the foundations of [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).[^o4] He is best known for the [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203), the [Church–Turing thesis](wikipedia://en/6854), proving the unsolvability of the *[Entscheidungsproblem](wikipedia://en/9672)* ("decision problem"), the [Frege–Church ontology](wikipedia://en/19542399), and the [Church–Rosser theorem](wikipedia://en/150035). Alongside his doctoral student [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), Church is considered one of the founders of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Alonzo Church was born on June 14, 1903, in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), where his father, Samuel Robbins Church, was a [justice of the peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_of_the_peace)[^o8] and the judge of the [Municipal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_court) for the District of Columbia. He was the grandson of Alonzo Webster Church (1829-1909), [United States Senate Librarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Librarian) from 1881 to 1901, and great-grandson of [Alonzo Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonzo_Church_%28college_president%29), a professor of Mathematics and Astronomy and 6th President of the University of Georgia.[^o9] As a young boy, Church was partially blinded by an air gun accident.[^o10] The family later moved to Virginia after his father lost his position at the university because of failing eyesight. With help from his uncle, also named Alonzo Church, the son attended the private Ridgefield School for Boys in [Ridgefield, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridgefield,_Connecticut).[^o11] After graduating from Ridgefield in 1920, Church attended Princeton University, where he was an exceptional student. He published his first paper on [Lorentz transformations](wikipedia://en/18404)[^o12] in 1924 and graduated the same year with a degree in mathematics. He stayed at Princeton for graduate work, earning a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics in three years under [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen).

He married Mary Julia Kuczinski in 1925. The couple had three children: Alonzo Jr. (1929), Mary Ann (1933), and Mildred (1938).

After receiving his Ph.D., he taught briefly as an instructor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o13] He received a two-year [National Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council) that enabled him to attend [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1927–1928, and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) and [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) the following year.

He taught philosophy and mathematics at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) for nearly four decades, from 1929 to 1967. He held the Flint Professorship of Philosophy and Mathematics at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), 1967–1990.[^o14] He was a Plenary Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1962 in Stockholm.[^o15]

He received honorary Doctor of Science degrees from [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University) in 1969,[^o16] Princeton University in 1985,[^o17] and the [University at Buffalo, The State University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo,_The_State_University_of_New_York) in 1990 in connection with an international symposium in his honor organized by [John Corcoran](wikipedia://en/23686234).[^o18]

He was elected a Corresponding Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy) (FBA) in 1966,[^o19] to the [American Academy of the Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1967, to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1978.[^o20]

Church was a lifelong member of the [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian) church.[^o21] He died on August 11, 1995, at the age of 92.[^o22] He is buried in [Princeton Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Cemetery).[^o23]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Church is known for the following accomplishments:

- His proof that the [Entscheidungsproblem](wikipedia://en/9672), which asks for a [decision procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_procedure) to determine the truth of arbitrary propositions in a first-order mathematical [theory](wikipedia://en/3512103), is [undecidable](wikipedia://en/15631055). This is known as [Church's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church's_theorem).[^o25]
- His invention of the [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203).
- His use of the lambda calculus to prove that [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) is undecidable.[^o14]
- His articulation of what has come to be known as the [Church–Turing thesis](wikipedia://en/6854).
- Being a founding editor of the *[Journal of Symbolic Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Symbolic_Logic)*, editing its reviews section for 43 years from 1936 until 1979.
- His authorship of a prominent textbook in the field of mathematical logic, *Introduction to Mathematical Logic*.[^o26]
- The [Church–Rosser theorem](wikipedia://en/150035)

The lambda calculus emerged in his 1936 paper showing the unsolvability of the Entscheidungsproblem. This result preceded Alan Turing's work on the [halting problem](wikipedia://en/21391870), which also demonstrated the existence of a problem unsolvable by mechanical means. Upon hearing of Church's work, Turing enrolled at Princeton later that year under Church for a Ph.D.[^o27] Church and Turing then showed that the lambda calculus and the [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) used in Turing's halting problem were equivalent in capabilities, and subsequently demonstrated a variety of alternative "mechanical processes for computation." This resulted in the Church–Turing thesis.

The efforts for automatically generating a controller implementation from specifications originates from his ideas.[^o28]

The lambda calculus influenced the design of [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29) and [functional programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_programming) languages in general. The [Church encoding](wikipedia://en/2989409) is named in his honor.

In his honor the Alonzo Church Award for Outstanding Contributions to Logic and Computation was established in 2015 by the Association for Computing Machinery Special Interest Group for Logic and Computation ([ACM SIGLOG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_SIGLOG)), the [European Association for Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Association_for_Theoretical_Computer_Science) (EATCS), the European Association for Computer Science Logic ([EACSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EACSL)), and the [Kurt Gödel Society](wikipedia://en/2129012) (KGS). The award is for an outstanding contribution to the field published within the past 25 years and must not yet have received recognition via another major award, such as the [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award), the [Paris Kanellakis Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Award), or the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize).[^o29][^o30]

Church also made contributions to the [theory of random sequences](wikipedia://en/4257548).[^o31]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Church's elaboration of a methodology involving the logistic method, his philosophical criticisms of nominalism and his defense of realism, his argumentation leading to conclusions about the theory of meaning, and the detailed construction of the Fregean and [Russellian](wikipedia://en/4163) [intensional logics](wikipedia://en/333365), are more than sufficient to place him high up among the most important philosophers of this century. Church is also known for the [Frege-Church ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frege-Church_ontology), which he created based on the philosophical ideas of [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416). He is credited with formulating the Slingshot Argument, which suggests that sentential references must be truth-values, rather than propositions.

## Influence {#Influence}

Over the course of his academic career, Church oversaw 31 doctoral students.[^o14] Many of them have led distinguished careers in mathematics, computer science, and other academic subjects, including [Peter B. Andrews](wikipedia://en/4132316), [George A. Barnard](wikipedia://en/2792579), [David Berlinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Berlinski), [William W. Boone](wikipedia://en/34399699), [Martin Davis](wikipedia://en/953041), [Alfred L. Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_L._Foster), [Leon Henkin](wikipedia://en/356165), [John G. Kemeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_George_Kemeny), [Stephen C. Kleene](wikipedia://en/28805), [Simon B. Kochen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_B._Kochen), [Maurice L'Abbé](wikipedia://en/49321993), [Gary R. Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_R._Mar), [Michael O. Rabin](wikipedia://en/298404), [Nicholas Rescher](wikipedia://en/576633), [Hartley Rogers, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartley_Rogers,_Jr.), [J. Barkley Rosser](wikipedia://en/679224), [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875), [Raymond Smullyan](wikipedia://en/56517) and [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208).[^o35]

In addition to those he directly supervised, Church also had a large influence on other mathematicians and computer scientists. [Haskell Curry](wikipedia://en/42182), who expanded on Church's ideas with the concept of [currying](wikipedia://en/6600), stated that one of his textbooks, Introduction to Mathematical Logic (first published in 1944), was "written with the meticulous precision which characterizes the author's work generally."[^o36]
