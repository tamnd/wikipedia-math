# Menelaus of Alexandria

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Menelaus of Alexandria"}

**Menelaus of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria)** (; Μενέλαος ὁ Ἀλεξανδρεύς, *Menelaos ho Alexandreus*; 100 CE) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks)[^o2] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), the first to recognize [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on a curved surface as natural analogs of straight lines.

:::

## Life and works {#Life_and_works}

*Image: Menelaus - Sphaericorum libri tres, 1758 - 4920.jpg.* *Sphaericorum libri tres*

Although very little is known about Menelaus's life, it is supposed that he lived in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), where he probably moved after having spent his youth in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria). He was called *Menelaus of Alexandria* by both [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300) and [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus), and a conversation of his with Lucius, held in Rome, is recorded by [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch).

[Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979) (2nd century [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era)) also mentions, in his work *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)* (VII.3), two astronomical observations made by Menelaus in Rome in January of the year 98. These were [occultations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occultation) of the stars [Spica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spica) and [Beta Scorpii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Scorpii) by the moon, a few nights apart. Ptolemy used these observations to confirm [precession of the equinoxes](wikipedia://en/72576), a phenomenon that had been [discovered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_precession) by [Hipparchus](wikipedia://en/13600) in the 2nd century [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era).

In the 10th-century *[Kitāb al-Fihrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fihrist)* \[*The Book Catalogue*\] by [Ibn al-Nadīm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_al-Nadim), six books by Menelaus are mentioned: the *Book of Spherical Propositions* (*Sphaerica*), *On the Knowledge of the Weights and Distribution of Different Bodies*, The *Elements of Geometry* in three books, and *The Book on the Triangle*.[^o4] Only the first of these, *Sphaerica*, survives today in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) translation. Composed of three books, it deals with the geometry of the sphere and its application in astronomical measurements and calculations. The book introduces the concept of [spherical triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle) (figures formed of three great circle arcs, which he named "trilaterals") and proves [Menelaus' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus'_theorem) on collinearity of points on the edges of a triangle (which may have been previously known) and its analog for spherical triangles. It was later translated by the sixteenth century astronomer and mathematician [Francesco Maurolico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maurolico).

The lunar crater [Menelaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus_%28crater%29) is named after him.

## Bibliography {#Bibliography}

The titles of a few books by Menelaus have been preserved:

- *On the calculation of the chords in a circle*, composed of six books
- *Elements of geometry*, composed of three books, later edited by [Thabit ibn Qurra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabit_ibn_Qurra)
- *On the knowledge of the weights and distributions of different bodies*
- He may also have written a [star catalogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_catalogue).
