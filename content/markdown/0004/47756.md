# List of small groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of small groups"}

The following list in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) contains the [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) of small [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [group isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397).

:::

## Counts {#Counts}

For *n* = 1, 2, … the number of nonisomorphic groups of order *n* is

1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 5, 2, 2, 1, 5, 1, 2, 1, 14, 1, 5, 1, 5, ...

For labeled groups, see .

## Glossary {#Glossary}

Each group is named by Small Groups Library as G<sub>*o*</sub><sup>*i*</sup>, where *o* is the order of the group, and *i* is the index used to label the group within that order.

Common group names:

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cyclic group"}

- Z<sub>*n*</sub>: the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *n* (the notation C<sub>*n*</sub> is also used; it is isomorphic to the [additive group](wikipedia://en/332229) of **Z**/*n***Z**)

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dihedral group"}

- Dih<sub>*n*</sub>: the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of order 2*n* (often the notation D<sub>*n*</sub> or D<sub>2*n*</sub> is used)

:::

- D<sub>*n*</sub>: the dihedral group of order 2*n*, the same as Dih<sub>*n*</sub> (notation used in section List of small non-abelian groups)

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Klein four-group"}

- K<sub>4</sub>: the [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928) of order 4, isomorphic to Z<sub>2</sub> × Z<sub>2</sub> and Dih<sub>2</sub>

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Symmetric group"}

- S<sub>*n*</sub>: the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of degree *n*, containing the [*n*!](wikipedia://en/10606) [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of *n* elements

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alternating group"}

- A<sub>*n*</sub>: the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) of degree *n*, containing the [even permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_permutation) of *n* elements, of order 1 for *n* = 0, 1, and order *n*!/2 otherwise

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dicyclic group"}

- Dic<sub>*n*</sub> or Q<sub>4*n*</sub>: the [dicyclic group](wikipedia://en/154963) of order 4*n*

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Quaternion group"}

- Q<sub>8</sub>: the [quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130) of order 8, also Dic<sub>2</sub>

:::

The notations Z<sub>*n*</sub> and Dih<sub>*n*</sub> have the advantage that [point groups in three dimensions](wikipedia://en/2694525) C<sub>*n*</sub> and D<sub>*n*</sub> do not have the same notation. There are more [isometry groups](wikipedia://en/1101694) than these two, of the same abstract group type.

The notation *G* × *H* denotes the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of the two groups; *G*<sup>*n*</sup> denotes the direct product of a group with itself *n* times. *G* ⋊ *H* denotes a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) where *H* [acts](wikipedia://en/12781) on *G*; this may also depend on the choice of action of *H* on *G*.

[Abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) and [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600) are noted. (For groups of order *n* \< 60, the simple groups are precisely the cyclic groups Z<sub>*n*</sub>, for [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *n*.)

The [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) in the [cycle graphs](wikipedia://en/1681010) is represented by the black circle. The lowest order for which the cycle graph does not uniquely represent a group is order 16.

In the lists of [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011), the trivial group and the group itself are not listed. Where there are several isomorphic subgroups, the number of such subgroups is indicated in parentheses.

[Angle brackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_brackets) \<relations> show the [presentation of a group](wikipedia://en/99494).

## List of small abelian groups {#List_of_small_abelian_groups}

The finite abelian groups are either cyclic groups, or direct products thereof; see [Abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974). The numbers of nonisomorphic abelian groups of orders *n* = 1, 2, ... are

1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1, 1, 3, 2, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1, 1, 5, 1, 2, 1, 2, ...

## Classifying groups of small order {#Classifying_groups_of_small_order}

Small groups of [prime power](wikipedia://en/1630483) order *p*<sup>*n*</sup> are given as follows:

- Order *p*: The only group is cyclic.
- Order *p*<sup>2</sup>: There are just two groups, both abelian.

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="the Heisenberg group modulo p"}

- Order *p*<sup>3</sup>: There are three abelian groups, and two non-abelian groups. One of the non-abelian groups is the semidirect product of a normal cyclic subgroup of order *p*<sup>2</sup> by a cyclic group of order *p*. The other is the quaternion group for *p* = 2 and [the Heisenberg group modulo *p*](wikipedia://en/448518#Heisenberg_group_modulo_an_odd_prime_p) for *p* > 2.

:::

- Order *p*<sup>4</sup>: The classification is complicated, and gets much harder as the exponent of *p* increases.

Most groups of small order have a Sylow *p* subgroup *P* with a [normal *p*-complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_p-complement) *N* for some prime *p* dividing the order, so can be classified in terms of the possible primes *p*, *p*-groups *P*, groups *N*, and actions of *P* on *N*. In some sense this reduces the classification of these groups to the classification of *p*-groups. Some of the small groups that do not have a normal *p*-complement include:

- Order 24: The symmetric group S<sub>4</sub>
- Order 48: The binary octahedral group and the product S<sub>4</sub> × Z<sub>2</sub>
- Order 60: The alternating group A<sub>5</sub>.

The smallest order for which it is *not* known how many nonisomorphic groups there are is 2048 = 2<sup>11</sup>.[^o29]

## Small Groups Library {#Small_Groups_Library}

The [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_computer_algebra_system) [computer algebra system](wikipedia://en/54427) contains a [package](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Package_manager) called the "Small Groups library," which provides access to descriptions of small order groups. The groups are listed [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397). At present, the library contains the following groups:[^o31]

::: {#o32 .entry cue=list-entry name="2-groups"}

- those of order at most 2000[^o33] except for order 1024 (423 groups in the library; the ones of order 1024 had to be skipped, as there are additional 49 nonisomorphic [2-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) of order 1024[^o34]);

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cubefree"}

- those of [cubefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubefree) order at most 50000 (395 703 groups);

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Squarefree"}

- those of [squarefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree) order;

:::

- those of order *p*<sup>*n*</sup> for *n* at most 6 and *p* prime;
- those of order *p*<sup>7</sup> for *p* = 3, 5, 7, 11 (907 489 groups);
- those of order *pq*<sup>*n*</sup> where *q*<sup>*n*</sup> divides 2<sup>8</sup>, 3<sup>6</sup>, 5<sup>5</sup> or 7<sup>4</sup> and *p* is an arbitrary prime which differs from *q*;
- those whose orders factorise into at most 3 primes (not necessarily distinct).

It contains explicit descriptions of the available groups in computer readable format.

The smallest order for which the Small Groups library does not have information is 1024.
