# Catalan's constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Catalan's constant"}

*Image: Catalan constant area.png.* Catalan constant as area under the curve of arctan(*x*)/*x*

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Catalan's constant** $G$ is the alternating sum of the reciprocals of the odd [square numbers](wikipedia://en/213919):

$$G = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n + 1)^2} = \frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{3^2} + \frac{1}{5^2} - \frac{1}{7^2} + \frac{1}{9^2} - \cdots.$$

Its numerical value[^o3] is approximately

:::

$$G = 0.915965594177219015054603514932384110774...,$$

and it is also equal to $\beta(2)$, where $\beta$ is the [Dirichlet beta function](wikipedia://en/2216061).

Catalan's constant was named after [Eugène Charles Catalan](wikipedia://en/639553), who found quickly-converging series for its calculation and published a memoir on it in 1865.[^o4][^o5]

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

In [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774), Catalan's constant is 1/4 of the volume of an [ideal](wikipedia://en/64419430) hyperbolic [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), and therefore 1/4 of the [hyperbolic volume](wikipedia://en/8158628) of the complement of the [Whitehead link](wikipedia://en/1966668).[^o8] It is 1/8 of the volume of the complement of the [Borromean rings](wikipedia://en/873118).[^o9]

In [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), it arises in connection with counting [domino tilings](wikipedia://en/10565476),[^o10] [spanning trees](wikipedia://en/455770),[^o11] and [Hamiltonian cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) of [grid graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_graph).[^o12]

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), Catalan's constant appears in a conjectured formula for the asymptotic number of primes of the form $n^2+1$ according to [Hardy and Littlewood's Conjecture F](wikipedia://en/67378#Hardy_and_Littlewood's_Conjecture_F). However, it is an unsolved problem (one of [Landau's problems](wikipedia://en/5785677)) whether there are even infinitely many primes of this form.[^o13]

Catalan's constant also appears in the calculation of the [mass distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_distribution) of [spiral galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_galaxy).[^o14][^o15]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

::: {#o18 .problem cue=unsolved}

Is Catalan's constant irrational? If so, is it transcendental?

:::

It is not known whether $G$ is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689), let alone [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325).[^o19] $G$ has been called "arguably the most basic constant whose irrationality and transcendence (though strongly suspected) remain unproven".[^o20]

However, partial results exist. It is known that infinitely many of the numbers *β*(2*n*) are irrational, where *β*(*s*) is the [Dirichlet beta function](wikipedia://en/2216061).[^o21] In particular, at least one of *β*(2), *β*(4), *β*(6), *β*(8), *β*(10) and *β*(12) must be irrational, where *β*(2) is Catalan's constant.[^o22] These results by [Wadim Zudilin](wikipedia://en/4311292) and [Tanguy Rivoal](wikipedia://en/78396540) are related to similar ones given for the [odd zeta constants](wikipedia://en/2334098#Odd_positive_integers) *ζ*(2*n* + 1).

Catalan's constant is known to be an [algebraic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_%28algebraic_geometry%29), which follows from some of the double integrals given below.

:::

## Series representations {#Series_representations}

Catalan's constant appears in the evaluation of several rational series including:[^o24]

$$\frac{\pi^2}{16}+\frac G2 = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(4n+1)^2}.$$

$$\frac{\pi^2}{16}-\frac G2 = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(4n+3)^2}.$$

The following two formulas involve quickly converging series, and are thus appropriate for numerical computation:

$$\begin{aligned} G & = 3 \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{4n}} \left(-\frac{1}{2(8n+2)^2}+\frac{1}{2^2(8n+3)^2}-\frac{1}{2^3(8n+5)^2}+\frac{1}{2^3(8n+6)^2}-\frac{1}{2^4(8n+7)^2}+\frac{1}{2(8n+1)^2}\right) \\ & \qquad -2 \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{12n}} \left(\frac{1}{2^4(8n+2)^2}+\frac{1}{2^6(8n+3)^2}-\frac{1}{2^9(8n+5)^2}-\frac{1}{2^{10} (8n+6)^2}-\frac{1}{2^{12} (8n+7)^2}+\frac{1}{2^3(8n+1)^2}\right) \end{aligned}$$

and

$$G = \frac{\pi}{8}\log\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right) + \frac{3}{8}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(2n+1)^2 \binom{2n}{n}}.$$

The theoretical foundations for such series are given by Broadhurst, for the first formula,[^o25] and Ramanujan, for the second formula.[^o26] The algorithms for fast evaluation of the Catalan constant were constructed by E. Karatsuba.[^o27][^o28] Using these series, calculating Catalan's constant is now about as fast as calculating [Apéry's constant](wikipedia://en/2216711), $\zeta(3)$.[^o29]

Other quickly converging series, due to [Guillera](wikipedia://en/80673725) and Pilehrood and employed by the [y-cruncher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-cruncher) software, include:[^o29]

$$G = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty }\frac{(-8)^{k}(3k+2)}{(2k+1)^{3}{\binom{2k}{k}}^{3}}$$

$$G = \frac{1}{64}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty }\frac{256^{k}(580k^2-184k+15)}{k^3(2k-1)\binom{6k}{3k}\binom{6k}{4k}\binom{4k}{2k}}$$

$$G = -\frac{1}{1024}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty }\frac{(-4096)^k(45136k^4-57184k^3+21240k^2-3160k+165)}{k^3(2k-1)^3}\left( \frac{(2k)!^6(3k)!^3}{k!^3(6k)!^3} \right)$$

All of these series have [time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944) $O(n\log(n)^3)$.[^o29]

## Integral identities {#Integral_identities}

As Seán Stewart writes: "There is a rich and seemingly endless source of definite integrals that can be equated to or expressed in terms of Catalan's constant."[^o31] Some of these expressions include:

$$\begin{aligned} G &= -\frac{1}{\pi i} \int_0^{\pi/2} \ln\ln\tan x \ln\tan x \,dx, \\ G &= \iint_{[0,1]^2} \frac{1}{1 + x^2 y^2} \,dx\,dy, \\ G &= \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \frac{1}{1 - x^2 - y^2} \,dy\,dx, \\ G &= \int_1^\infty \frac{\ln t}{1 + t^2} \,dt, \\ G &= -\int_0^1 \frac{\ln t}{1 + t^2} \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{t}{\sin t} \,dt, \\ G &= \int_0^{\pi/4} \ln\cot t \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\pi/2} \ln(\sec t + \tan t) \,dt, \\ G &= \int_0^1 \frac{\arccos t}{\sqrt{1 + t^2}} \,dt, \\ G &= \int_0^1 \frac{\operatorname{arcsinh} t}{\sqrt{1 - t^2}} \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{1}{2} \int_0^\infty \frac{\operatorname{arctan} t}{t \sqrt{1 + t^2}} \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{1}{2} \int_0^1 \frac{\operatorname{arctanh} t}{\sqrt{1 - t^2}} \,dt, \\ G &= \int_0^\infty \operatorname{arccot} e^t \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{1}{4} \int_0^{\pi^2/4} \csc \sqrt{t} \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{1}{16} \left(\pi^2 + 4 \int_1^\infty \operatorname{arccsc}^2 t \,dt\right), \\ G &= \frac{1}{2} \int_0^\infty \frac{t}{\cosh t} \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{\pi}{2} \int_1^\infty \frac{(t^4 - 6t^2 + 1) \ln\ln t}{(1 + t^2)^3} \,dt, \\ G &= \frac{1}{2} \int_0^\infty \frac{\arcsin(\sin t)}{t} \,dt, \\ G &= 1 + \lim_{\alpha \to 1^-} \left\{ \int_0^\alpha \frac{(1 + 6t^2 + t^4) \arctan t}{t(1 - t^2)^2} \,dt + 2\operatorname{artanh}\alpha - \frac{\pi\alpha}{1 - \alpha^2} \right\}, \\ G &= 1 - \frac18 \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{x \sin(2xy/\pi)}{(x^2 + \pi^2) \cosh x \sinh y} \,dx\,dy, \\ G &= \int_0^\infty \int_0^\infty \frac{\sqrt[4]{x} \left(\sqrt{x} \sqrt{y} - 1\right)}{(x + 1)^2 \sqrt[4]{y} (y + 1)^2 \log (x y)} \,dx\,dy, \end{aligned}$$

where the last three formulas are related to [Malmsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Johan_Malmsten)'s integrals.[^o32]

If $K(k)$ is the [complete elliptic integral of the first kind](wikipedia://en/9960#Complete_elliptic_integral_of_the_first_kind), as a function of the elliptic modulus $k$, then

$$G = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^1 \mathrm{K}(k)\,dk.$$

If $E(k)$ is the [complete elliptic integral of the second kind](wikipedia://en/9960#Complete_elliptic_integral_of_the_second_kind), as a function of the elliptic modulus $k$, then

$$G = -\frac{1}{2} + \int_0^1 \mathrm{E}(k)\,dk.$$

With the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) $\Gamma(x + 1) = x!$, Catalan's constant is

$$\begin{aligned} G &= \frac{\pi}{4} \int_0^1 \Gamma\left(1 + \frac{x}{2}\right) \Gamma\left(1 - \frac{x}{2}\right) \,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi}{2} \int_0^{1/2} \Gamma(1 + y) \Gamma(1 - y) \,dy. \end{aligned}$$

The integral

$$G = \operatorname{Ti}_2(1) = \int_0^1 \frac{\arctan t}{t} \,dt$$

is a known [special function](wikipedia://en/23709110), called the [inverse tangent integral](wikipedia://en/3800787), and was extensively studied by [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717).

## Relation to special functions {#Relation_to_special_functions}

$G$ appears in values of the second [polygamma function](wikipedia://en/406932), also called the [trigamma function](wikipedia://en/2036750), at fractional arguments:[^o24]

$$\begin{aligned} \psi_1 \left(\tfrac14\right) &= \pi^2 + 8G, \\ \psi_1 \left(\tfrac34\right) &= \pi^2 - 8G. \end{aligned}$$

[Simon Plouffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Plouffe) gives an infinite collection of identities between the trigamma function, $\pi$<sup>2</sup> and Catalan's constant; these are expressible as paths on a graph (see #External links below).

Catalan's constant occurs frequently in relation to the [Clausen function](wikipedia://en/406899), the [inverse tangent integral](wikipedia://en/3800787), the [inverse sine integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_sine_integral), the [Barnes $G$-function](wikipedia://en/2060183), as well as integrals and series summable in terms of the aforementioned functions.

As a particular example, by first expressing the [inverse tangent integral](wikipedia://en/3800787) in its closed form – in terms of Clausen functions – and then expressing those Clausen functions in terms of the Barnes $G$-function, the following expression is obtained (see [Clausen function](wikipedia://en/406899) for more):

$$G = 4\pi \log\frac{G\left(\frac{3}{8}\right) G\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)} {G\left(\frac{1}{8}\right) G\left(\frac{5}{8}\right)} + 4\pi \log\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)} {\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)} + \frac{\pi}{2} \log\frac{1 + \sqrt{2}}{2\left(2 - \sqrt{2}\right)}.$$

If one defines the **[Lerch transcendent](wikipedia://en/1342978)** $\Phi(z, s, \alpha)$ by

$$\Phi(z, s, \alpha) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{z^n}{(n + \alpha)^s},$$

then

$$G = \frac{1}{4} \Phi\left(-1, 2, \tfrac{1}{2}\right).$$

This comes directly from the fact that $\beta(s) = 2^{-s} \Phi\left(-1, s, \tfrac{1}{2}\right)$ and $\beta(2) = G$, where $\beta(s)$ is the [Dirichlet beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_beta) function

## Continued fraction {#Continued_fraction}

$G$ can be expressed in the following form:[^o35]

$$G = \cfrac{1}{1 + \cfrac{1^4}{8 + \cfrac{3^4}{16 + \cfrac{5^4}{24 + \cfrac{7^4}{32 + \cfrac{9^4}{40 + \ddots}}}}}}.$$

The [simple continued fraction](wikipedia://en/46802) is given by

$$G = \cfrac{1}{1 + \cfrac{1}{10 + \cfrac{1}{1 + \cfrac{1}{8 + \cfrac{1}{1 + \cfrac{1}{88 + \ddots}}}}}}.$$

This continued fraction would have infinite terms if and only if $G$ is irrational, which is still unresolved.

The following continued fraction representation gives (asymptotically) 2.08 new correct decimal places per cycle:[^o37]

$$G = \frac{\frac{13}{2}}{Z_0}, \quad Z_k = a(k) + \frac{b(k)}{Z_{k+1}}$$

with

$$\begin{aligned} a(k) &= 3520k^6 + 5632k^5 + 2064k^4 - 384k^3 - 156k^2 + 16k + 7, \\ b(k) &= (2k + 1)^4 (2k + 2)^4 (20k^2 - 8k + 1) (20k^2 + 72k + 65). \end{aligned}$$
