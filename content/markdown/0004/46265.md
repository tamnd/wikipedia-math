# Atbash

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atbash"}

*Image: Atbash.png.* The Hebrew alphabet, run through Atbash.

**Atbash** (אתבש; also transliterated **Atbaš**) is a monoalphabetic [substitution cipher](wikipedia://en/48356) originally used to [encrypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encrypt) the [Hebrew alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_alphabet). It can be modified for use with any known [writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system) with a standard [collating order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collation).

:::

## Encryption {#Encryption}

The Atbash cipher is a particular type of [monoalphabetic cipher](wikipedia://en/48356) formed by taking the [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet) (or [abjad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad), [syllabary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syllabary), etc.) and mapping it to its reverse, so that the first letter becomes the last letter, the second letter becomes the second to last letter, and so on. For example, the [ISO basic Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_basic_Latin_alphabet) would work like this: Plain

Because there is only one way to perform this (which means there is only one possibility), the Atbash cipher provides no [communications security](wikipedia://en/40922), as it lacks any sort of [key](wikipedia://en/53039). If multiple [collating orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collation) are available, which one was used in encryption can be used as a key, but this does not provide significantly more security, considering that only a few letters can give away which one was used.

## History {#History}

The name derives from the first, last, second, and second to last Hebrew letters ([Aleph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleph)–[Taw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taw)–[Bet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bet_%28letter%29)–[Shin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_%28letter%29)).

The Atbash cipher for the modern [Hebrew alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_alphabet) would be: ◢→ By shifting the correlation one space to the left or the right, one may derive a variant **Batgash** (named for Bet–Taw–[Gimel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimel)–Shin) or **Ashbar** (for Aleph–Shin–Bet–[Reish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resh)). Either alternative mapping leaves one letter unsubstituted; respectively Aleph and Taw.

### In the Bible {#In_the_Bible}

Several [biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical) words are described by commentators[^o5] as being examples of Atbash:[^o6][^o7][^o8]

- [Jeremiah 25:26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremiah_25:26) – "The king of *[Sheshach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheshach)* shall drink after them" – Sheshach meaning [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon) in Atbash (בבל *bbl* → ששך *ššk*).
- [Jeremiah 51:1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremiah_51:1) – "Behold, I will raise up against Babylon, and against the inhabitants of *Lev-kamai*, a destroying wind." – Lev-kamai meaning [Chaldeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaldea) (כשדים *kśdym* → לבקמי *lbqmy*).
- [Jeremiah 51:41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremiah_51:41) – "How has *Sheshach* been captured! and the praise of the whole earth taken! How has Babylon become a curse among the nations!" – Sheshach meaning Babylon (בבל *bbl* → ששך *ššk*).

Regarding a potential Atbash switch of a single letter:

- Exodus - "Any place *I* will mention My name" (אזכיר) → "Any place *you* will mention My name" (תזכיר) (a → t), according to [Yom Tov Asevilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Tov_Asevilli)[^o9]

## Relationship to the affine cipher {#Relationship_to_the_affine_cipher}

The Atbash cipher can be seen as a special case of the [affine cipher](wikipedia://en/450714).

Under the standard affine convention, an alphabet of *m* letters is mapped to the numbers 0, 1, ... , *m* − 1. (The Hebrew alphabet has *m* = 22, and the standard Latin alphabet has *m* = 26). The Atbash cipher may then be enciphered and deciphered using the encryption function for an affine cipher by setting *a* = *b* = (*m* − 1):

$$\mathrm{E}(x) = \mathrm{D}(x) = \big((m - 1) x + (m - 1)\big) \bmod m.$$

This may be simplified to

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{E}(x) &= (m - 1)(x + 1) \bmod m \\ &= -(x + 1) \bmod m. \end{aligned}$$

If, instead, the *m* letters of the alphabet are mapped to 1, 2, ..., *m*, then the encryption and decryption function for the Atbash cipher becomes

$$\mathrm{E}(x) = (-x \bmod m) + 1.$$
