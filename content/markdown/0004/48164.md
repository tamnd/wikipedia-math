# Maximal ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maximal ideal"}

*Image: Dedekind.jpeg.* Richard Dedekind, one of the founders of ring theory

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a **maximal ideal** is a [two-sided ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-sided_ideal) that is [maximal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) (with respect to [set inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_inclusion)) amongst all *proper* ideals.[^o2][^o3] In other words, *I* is a maximal ideal of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* if there are no other two-sided ideals contained between *I* and *R*.

:::

Maximal ideals are important because the [quotients of rings](wikipedia://en/184635) by maximal ideals are [simple rings](wikipedia://en/443387), and in the special case of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) they are also [fields](wikipedia://en/10603). The set of maximal ideals of a commutative ring *R* is known as the **maximal spectrum** of *R* and is variously denoted m-Spec *R*, Specm *R*, MaxSpec *R*, Max *R*, or Spm *R*. When equipped with the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744), it is the subspace of the [closed points](wikipedia://en/33086408) of the [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum) Spec *R*, the set of the [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) equipped with Zariski topology.

In the theory of [noncommutative rings](wikipedia://en/24062516) a **maximal right ideal** is defined analogously as being a maximal element in the [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) of proper right ideals, and similarly, a **maximal left ideal** is defined to be a maximal element of the poset of proper left ideals. Since a one-sided maximal ideal *A* is not necessarily two-sided, the quotient *R*/*A* is not necessarily a ring, but it is a [simple module](wikipedia://en/29430) over *R*. If *R* has a unique maximal right ideal, then *R* is known as a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391), and the maximal right ideal is also the unique maximal left and unique maximal two-sided ideal of the ring, and is in fact the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) J(*R*).

It is possible for a ring to have a unique maximal two-sided ideal and yet lack unique maximal one-sided ideals: for example, in the ring of 2 by 2 [square matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_matrices) over a field, the [zero ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_ideal) is a maximal two-sided ideal, but there are many maximal right ideals.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

There are other equivalent ways of expressing the definition of maximal one-sided and maximal two-sided ideals. Given a ring *R* and a proper ideal *I* of *R* (that is *I* ≠ *R*), *I* is a maximal ideal of *R* if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

- There exists no other proper ideal *J* of *R* so that *I* ⊊ *J*.
- For any ideal *J* with *I* ⊆ *J*, either *J* = *I* or *J* = *R*.
- The quotient ring *R*/*I* is a simple ring.

There is an analogous list for one-sided ideals, for which only the right-hand versions will be given. For a right ideal *A* of a ring *R*, the following conditions are equivalent to *A* being a maximal right ideal of *R*:

- There exists no other proper right ideal *B* of *R* so that *A* ⊊ *B*.
- For any right ideal *B* with *A* ⊆ *B*, either *B* = *A* or *B* = *R*.
- The quotient module *R*/*A* is a simple right *R*-module.

Maximal right/left/two-sided ideals are the [dual notion](wikipedia://en/609737) to that of [minimal ideals](wikipedia://en/34233472).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- If **F** is a field, then the only maximal ideal is {0}.
- In the ring **Z** of integers, the maximal ideals are the [principal ideals](wikipedia://en/145126) generated by a prime number.
- More generally, all nonzero [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) are maximal in a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039).
- The ideal $(2, x)$ is a maximal ideal in ring $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. Generally, the maximal ideals of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ are of the form $(p, f(x))$ where $p$ is a prime number and $f(x)$ is a polynomial in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ which is irreducible modulo $p$.
- Every prime ideal is a maximal ideal in a [Boolean ring](wikipedia://en/54356), i.e., a ring consisting of only idempotent elements. In fact, every prime ideal is maximal in a commutative ring $R$ whenever there exists an integer $n > 1$ such that $x^n = x$ for any $x \in R$.
- The maximal ideals of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $\mathbb{C}[x]$ are principal ideals generated by $x-c$ for some $c\in \mathbb{C}$.
- More generally, the maximal ideals of the polynomial ring $K[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *K* are the ideals of the form $(x_1-a_1,...,x_n-a_n)$. This result is known as the weak [Nullstellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullstellensatz).
- Even more generally, let *K* be an algebraically closed field, $V\subseteq K^n$ be an [affine algebraic set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_set), and $\mathrm{I}(V)$ be the ideal of polynomials in $K[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ that vanish on *V*. Then the maximal ideals of the [coordinate ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) $K[V]=K[x_1,...,x_n]/\mathrm{I}(V)$ are of the form $\mathfrak{m}_a=(\overline{x_1}-a_1,...,\overline{x_n}-a_n)$, where $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in V$, and $\overline{x_i}$ are the images of $x_i$ under the natural projection map $K[x_1,\dots,x_n]\to K[V]$. Therefore, there is a one-to-one correspondence $a\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{m}_a$ between points $a\in V$ and maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}_a\in \mathrm{Spm}(K[V])$.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- An important ideal of the ring called the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) can be defined using maximal right (or maximal left) ideals.
- If *R* is a unital commutative ring with an ideal *m*, then *k* = *R*/*m* is a field if and only if *m* is a maximal ideal. In that case, *R*/*m* is known as the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680). This fact can fail in non-unital rings. For example, $4\mathbb{Z}$ is a maximal ideal in $2\mathbb{Z}$, but $2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is not a field.
- If *L* is a maximal left ideal, then *R*/*L* is a simple left *R*-module. Conversely in rings with unity, any simple left *R*-module arises this way. Incidentally this shows that a collection of representatives of simple left *R*-modules is actually a set since it can be put into correspondence with part of the set of maximal left ideals of *R*.
- **[Krull's theorem](wikipedia://en/4128358)** (1929): Every nonzero unital ring has a maximal ideal. The result is also true if "ideal" is replaced with "right ideal" or "left ideal". More generally, it is true that every nonzero [finitely generated module](wikipedia://en/326430) has a maximal submodule. Suppose *I* is an ideal which is not *R* (respectively, *A* is a right ideal which is not *R*). Then *R*/*I* is a ring with unity (respectively, *R*/*A* is a finitely generated module), and so the above theorems can be applied to the quotient to conclude that there is a maximal ideal (respectively, maximal right ideal) of *R* containing *I* (respectively, *A*).
- Krull's theorem can fail for rings without unity. A [radical ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_ring), i.e. a ring in which the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) is the entire ring, has no simple modules and hence has no maximal right or left ideals. See [regular ideals](wikipedia://en/24159446) for possible ways to circumvent this problem.
- In a commutative ring with unity, every maximal ideal is a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928). The converse is not always true: for example, in any nonfield [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) the zero ideal is a prime ideal which is not maximal. Commutative rings in which prime ideals are maximal are known as [zero-dimensional rings](wikipedia://en/61346#Dimension), where the dimension used is the [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079).
- If *k* is a field, the preimage of a maximal ideal of a [finitely generated *k*-algebra](wikipedia://en/11985661) under a *k*-algebra homomorphism is a maximal ideal. However, the preimage of a maximal ideal of a unital commutative ring under a ring homomorphism is not necessarily maximal. For example, let $f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}$ be the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) and $\mathfrak{n}=(0)$ in $\mathbb{Q}$. Then $\mathfrak{n}$ is maximal in $\mathbb{Q}$ but $f^{-1}(\mathfrak{n})=(0)$ is not maximal in $\mathbb{Z}$.
- A maximal ideal of a noncommutative ring might not be prime in the commutative sense. For example, let $M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{Z})$ be the ring of all $n\times n$ matrices over $\mathbb{Z}$. This ring has a maximal ideal $M_{n\times n}(p\mathbb{Z})$ for any prime $p$, but this is not a prime ideal since (in the case $n=2$)$A=\text{diag}(1,p)$ and $B=\text{diag}(p,1)$ are not in $M_{n\times n}(p\mathbb{Z})$, but $AB=pI_2\in M_{n\times n}(p\mathbb{Z})$. However, maximal ideals of noncommutative rings *are* prime in the generalized sense below.

:::

## Generalization {#Generalization}

For an *R*-module *A*, a **maximal submodule** *M* of *A* is a submodule *M* ≠ *A* satisfying the property that for any other submodule *N*, *M* ⊆ *N* ⊆ *A* implies *N* = *M* or *N* = *A*. Equivalently, *M* is a maximal submodule [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the quotient module *A*/*M* is a [simple module](wikipedia://en/29430). The maximal right ideals of a ring *R* are exactly the maximal submodules of the module *R*<sub>*R*</sub>.

Unlike rings with unity, a nonzero module does not necessarily have maximal submodules. However, as noted above, *finitely generated* nonzero modules have maximal submodules, and also [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488) have maximal submodules.

As with rings, one can define the [radical of a module](wikipedia://en/9379691) using maximal submodules. Furthermore, maximal ideals can be generalized by defining a **maximal sub-bimodule** *M* of a [bimodule](wikipedia://en/492612) *B* to be a proper sub-bimodule of *M* which is contained in no other proper sub-bimodule of *M*. The maximal ideals of *R* are then exactly the maximal sub-bimodules of the bimodule <sub>*R*</sub>*R*<sub>*R*</sub>.

:::
