# Julian Sochocki

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Julian Sochocki"}

**Julian Karol Sochocki** (Julian Karol Sochocki; Юлиан Васильевич Сохоцкий; February 2, 1842 – December 14, 1927) was a Polish-Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902)[^o3] and a professor at [Saint Petersburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University). He is known for his work in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). His name is sometimes transliterated from Russian in several different ways (e.g. Sokhotski or Sochotski).[^o3]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Sochocki was born on 2 February 1842 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland), Russian Empire, to a Polish family of Bazyli Sochocki and Józefa (Lewandowska),[^o5] where he attended state [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29). In 1860, he registered at the Physico-Mathematical Department of [St Petersburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University). His study there was interrupted for the period 1860–1865 because of his involvement with Polish patriotic movement: he had to return to Warsaw to escape prosecution.[^o3]

In 1866, he graduated from the Department of Physics and Mathematics at the [University of Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University). In 1868, he received his master's degree. In 1873, Sochocki obtained his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) based on his thesis entitled *On Certain Integrals and Functions Used in Series Expansions* (in Russian).[^o6]

His master's dissertation, practically the first text in Russian mathematical literature on [Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Louis_Cauchy) method of [residues](wikipedia://en/73390), was published in 1868. The dissertation itself contains many original grasps, which have been also ascribed to other mathematicians. His doctoral thesis contains the famous [Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem](wikipedia://en/12237167).

From 1868 Sochotcki lectured at the St Petersburg university, first as the "privat-docent", from 1882 as an ordinary professor, and from 1893 as a merited professor. In 1894, he was elected corresponding member of the [Polish Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning).[^o7]

His notable students included [Władysław Natanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Władysław_Natanson), Andrzej Pszenicki, Leon Staniewicz, and Wiktor Staniewicz.[^o5]

Sochocki died on December 14, 1927, in a nursing home in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad).

Sochocki is mainly remembered for the [Casorati&ndash;Sokhotski&ndash;Weierstrass theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casorati&ndash;Sokhotski&ndash;Weierstrass_theorem) and for the [Sokhotski&ndash;Plemelj theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokhotski&ndash;Plemelj_theorem).

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- *Теорiя интегральныхъ вычетовъ с нѣкоторыми приложенiями* (*A Theory of Integral Residues with Some Applications*) (1868)
- *Объ определенныхъ интегралахъ и функцiяхъ употребляемыхъ при разложенiяхъ въ ряды* (*On Definite Integrals and Functions Used in Series Expansions*) (1873)
- *О суммахъ Гаусса и о законе взаимности символа Лежандра* (*On Gauss Sums and the Reciprocity Law of the Legendre Symbol*) (1877)
- *Высшая алгебра* (*Higher Algebra*) (St. Petersburg, 1882)
- *Теорiя чиселъ* (*Number Theory*) (St. Petersburg, 1888)
- *Начало общего наибольшего делителя въ применении к теорiи делимости алгебраическихъ чиселъ* (*The Principle of the Greatest Common Divisor Applied to Divisibility Theory of Algebraic Numbers*) (1893), 25.0300.01, 25.0297.04
