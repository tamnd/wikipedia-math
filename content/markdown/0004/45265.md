# De Bruijn–Newman constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De Bruijn–Newman constant"}

The **de Bruijn–Newman constant**, denoted by **$\Lambda$** and named after [Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn](wikipedia://en/3166399) and [Charles Michael Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_M._Newman), is a [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) defined via the [zeros](wikipedia://en/264210) of a certain [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $H(\lambda,z)$, where $\lambda$ is a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) parameter and $z$ is a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) variable. More precisely,

:::

$$H(\lambda, z):=\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{\lambda u^{2}} \Phi(u) \cos (z u) \, du,$$

where $\Phi$ is the [super-exponentially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-exponential_function) decaying function

$$\Phi(u) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2 e^{5u} ) e^{-\pi n^2 e^{4u}}$$

and $\Lambda$ is the unique real number with the property that $H$ has only real zeros [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $\lambda\geq \Lambda$.

The constant is closely connected to the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125). Indeed, the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the statement that $\Lambda\leq 0$.[^o2] Brad Rodgers and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that $\Lambda\geq 0$, so the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to $\Lambda=0$.[^o3] A simplified proof of the Rodgers–Tao result was later given by Alexander Dobner.[^o4]

## History {#History}

De Bruijn showed in 1950 that $H$ has only real zeros if $\lambda\geq 1/2$, and moreover, that if $H$ has only real zeros for some $\lambda$, $H$ also has only real zeros if $\lambda$ is replaced by any larger value.[^o6] Newman proved in 1976 the existence of a constant $\Lambda$ for which the "if and only if" claim holds; and this then implies that $\Lambda$ is unique. Newman also conjectured that $\Lambda\geq 0$,[^o7] which was proven forty years later, by Brad Rodgers and Terence Tao in 2018.

## Heat-flow interpretation {#Heat-flow_interpretation}

The family $H_\lambda$ may be viewed as a deformation of the [Riemann xi function](wikipedia://en/5396799) under a [heat-type equation](wikipedia://en/166415). At $\lambda=0$, the function $H_0$ is essentially the Riemann xi function, written as an even [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247). Varying $\lambda$ multiplies the Fourier-side kernel by $e^{\lambda u^2}$. Differentiating under the integral sign gives

$${\partial H_\lambda\over\partial \lambda}=-{\partial^2 H_\lambda\over\partial z^2},$$

so that increasing $\lambda$ evolves $H_\lambda$ by the backward heat equation in the variable $z$.[^o18][^o3]

In this interpretation, the de Bruijn–Newman constant is the transition time at which the deformation changes from having non-real zeros to having only real zeros. De Bruijn's theorem says that once all zeros have become real, they remain real for all later values of the heat-flow parameter. Thus $\Lambda$ measures the stability of the real-zero property under this deformation: the Riemann hypothesis is the assertion that the undeformed function $H_0$ already lies on the real-zero side of the transition, while Newman's conjecture asserts that it lies exactly at the boundary rather than safely inside it.[^o6][^o3]

## Proofs of Newman's conjecture {#Proofs_of_Newman's_conjecture}

Newman's conjecture is the assertion that $\Lambda\geq 0$. The proof of this lower bound by Brad Rodgers and Terence Tao proceeds by contradiction. Assuming $\Lambda<0$, they analyze the motion of the zeros of $H_t$ under the backwards heat-flow deformation. Their analysis forces increasingly rigid control of the zeros in the range $\Lambda<t\leq 0$. In particular, they prove that it implies that the zeros of $H_0$ would have to be locally close to equally spaced. They then derive a contradiction with known results on the local distribution of zeros of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), such as estimates related to [Montgomery's pair correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_correlation_conjecture) work.[^o3]

A different proof was later given by [Alexander Dobner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dobner). Dobner's method avoids the zero-dynamics and zeta-zero gap estimates used by Rodgers and Tao. In the case of the Riemann xi function, the argument shows that for every $t<0$, the deformed function $\xi_t$ can be approximated by a [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258)

$$\zeta_t(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp\!\left({t\over4}\log^2 n\right)n^{-s},$$

whose zeros imply the existence of zeros of $\xi_t$ off the critical line, equivalently non-real zeros of the corresponding $H_t$, where the relationship is given by

$$H_t(z)={1\over 8}\,\xi_t\!\left({1+iz\over 2}\right).$$

Dobner's proof also gives a generalized form of Newman's conjecture for [$L$-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) in the extended [Selberg class](wikipedia://en/2200454).[^o11]

:::

## Upper bounds {#Upper_bounds}

De Bruijn's upper bound of $\Lambda\le 1/2$ was not improved until 2008, when Ki, Kim and Lee proved $\Lambda< 1/2$, making the [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) strict.[^o13]

In December 2018, the 15th [Polymath project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath_project) improved the bound to $\Lambda\leq 0.22$.[^o14][^o15][^o16] A manuscript of the Polymath work was submitted to arXiv in late April 2019,[^o17] and was published in the journal Research In the Mathematical Sciences in August 2019.[^o18]

This bound was further slightly improved in April 2020 by Platt and [Trudgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Trudgian) to $\Lambda\leq 0.2$.[^o19]
