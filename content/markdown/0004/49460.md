# Boole's syllogistic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boole's syllogistic"}

*Image: Square of opposition, set diagrams.svg.* Square of oppositionIn the [Venn diagrams](wikipedia://en/61701) black areas are [empty](wikipedia://en/9566) and red areas are nonempty.The faded arrows and faded red areas apply in traditional logic.

**[Boolean logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_logic)**[^o2] is a system of [syllogistic](wikipedia://en/48046) [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) invented by 19th-century British mathematician [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298), which attempts to incorporate the "empty set", that is, a class of non-existent entities, such as round squares, without resorting to uncertain [truth values](wikipedia://en/161711).

:::

In Boolean logic, the universal statements "all S is P" and "no S is P" (contraries in the traditional Aristotelian schema) are compossible provided that the set of "S" is the empty set. "All S is P" is construed to mean that "there is nothing that is both S and not-P"; "no S is P", that "there is nothing that is both S and P". For example, since there is nothing that is a round square, it is true both that nothing is a round square and purple, and that nothing is a round square and *not*-purple. Therefore, both universal statements, that "all round squares are purple" and "no round squares are purple" are true.

Similarly, the [subcontrary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcontrary) relationship is dissolved between the existential statements "some S is P" and "some S is not P". The former is interpreted as "there is some S such that S is P" and the latter, "there is some S such that S is not P", both of which are clearly false where S is nonexistent.

Thus, the subaltern relationship between universal and existential also does not hold, since for a nonexistent S, "All S is P" is true but does not entail "Some S is P", which is false. Of the Aristotelian [square of opposition](wikipedia://en/246976), only the contradictory relationships remain intact.
