# Random number

*Image: Two red dice 01.svg.* [Dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice) are an example of a mechanical hardware random number generator. When a cubical die is rolled, a random number from 1 to 6 is obtained.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Random number"}

A **random number** is generated by a random ([stochastic](wikipedia://en/47895)) process such as throwing dice. Individual numbers cannot be predicted, but the likely result of generating a large quantity of numbers can be predicted by specific mathematical series and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). Digits of the random [binary numbers](wikipedia://en/238686) are **random bits** or **coins**.

:::

## Algorithms and implementations {#Algorithms_and_implementations}

Random numbers are frequently used in [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) such as [Knuth's](wikipedia://en/8095) 1964-developed [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775)[^o3] for shuffling lists. (popularly known as *the [Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) shuffle* or *the [Fisher–Yates shuffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Yates_shuffle)*, based on work they did in 1938).

In 1999, a new feature was added to the [Pentium III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentium_III): a hardware-based [random number generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_number_generator).[^o4][^o5] It has been described as "several [oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillators) combine their outputs and that odd waveform is sampled asynchronously."[^o6] These numbers, however, were only 32 bit, at a time when export controls were on 56 bits and higher, so they were not state of the art.[^o7]

## Common understanding {#Common_understanding}

In common understanding, "1 2 3 4 5" is not as random as "3 5 2 1 4" and certainly not as random as "47 88 1 32 41" but "we can't say authoritavely that the first sequence is not random ... it could have been generated by chance."[^o9]

When a police officer claims to have done a "random .. door-to-door" search, there is a certain expectation that members of a jury will have.[^o10][^o11]

## Real world consequences {#Real_world_consequences}

Flaws in *randomness* have real-world consequences.[^o13][^o14]

A 99.8% randomness was shown by researchers to negatively affect an estimated 27,000 customers of a large service[^o13] and that the problem was not limited to just that situation.
