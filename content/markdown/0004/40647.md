# Viggo Brun

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Viggo Brun"}

**Viggo Brun** (13 October 1885 – 15 August 1978) was a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) professor, [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [number theorist](wikipedia://en/21527). [^o3] He is known for his work in the [twin prime conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_prime_conjecture). [Brun's theorem](wikipedia://en/2796181) shows that sum of the reciprocals of the [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997) converges.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: ViggoBrun.jpg.* Viggo Brun (published in 1911)

Brun was born at Lier in [Buskerud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buskerud), Norway. He studied at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) and began research at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1910. In 1923, Brun became a professor at the [Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Institute_of_Technology) in Trondheim and in 1946 a professor at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo).[^o5]

He retired in 1955 at the age of 70 and died in 1978 (at 92 years-old) at [Drøbak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drøbak) in [Akershus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus), Norway.[^o6]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In 1915, he introduced a new method, based on [Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695)'s version of the [sieve of Eratosthenes](wikipedia://en/73415), now known as the [Brun sieve](wikipedia://en/18731525), which addresses additive problems such as [Goldbach's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758) and the [twin prime conjecture](wikipedia://en/41997). He used it to prove that there exist infinitely many integers *n* such that *n* and *n*+2 have at most nine [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor), and that all large even integers are the sum of two numbers with at most nine prime factors.[^o8]

He also showed that the sum of the reciprocals of [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997) converges to a finite value, now called [Brun's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brun's_constant). In contrast, [the sum of the reciprocals of all primes is divergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_that_the_sum_of_the_reciprocals_of_the_primes_diverges). He developed a multi-dimensional [continued fraction](wikipedia://en/739199) algorithm in 1919–1920 and applied this to problems in [musical theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theory). He also served as [praeses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praeses) of the [Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters) in 1946.[^o9]
