# Hurwitz polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hurwitz polynomial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Hurwitz polynomial** (named after German mathematician [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690)) is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) whose [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) (zeros) are located in the left half-plane of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) or on the [imaginary axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_axis), that is, the [real part](wikipedia://en/5826) of every root is zero or negative.[^o2] Such a polynomial must have [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) that are positive [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). The term is sometimes restricted to polynomials whose roots have real parts that are strictly negative, excluding the imaginary axis (i.e., a Hurwitz [stable polynomial](wikipedia://en/2057699)).[^o3][^o4]

:::

A polynomial function $P(s)$ of a [complex variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable) $s$ is said to be Hurwitz if the following conditions are satisfied:

1. $P(s)$ is real when $s$ is real.
1. The roots of $P(s)$ have real parts which are zero or negative.

Hurwitz polynomials are important in [control systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_system), because they represent the [characteristic equations](wikipedia://en/218268#Characteristic_equation) of [stable](wikipedia://en/5493795) [linear systems](wikipedia://en/722503). Whether a polynomial is Hurwitz can be determined by solving the equation to find the roots, or from the coefficients without solving the equation by the [Routh–Hurwitz stability criterion](wikipedia://en/1705432).

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A simple example of a Hurwitz polynomial is:

$$x^2 + 2x + 1.$$

The only real solution is −1, because it factors as

$$(x+1)^2.$$

In general, all [quadratic polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_polynomial) with positive coefficients are Hurwitz. This follows directly from the [quadratic formula](wikipedia://en/59217):

$$x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac\ }}{2a}.$$

where, if the discriminant *b*<sup>2</sup>−4*ac* is less than zero, then the polynomial will have two [complex-conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) solutions with real part −*b*/2*a*, which is negative for positive *a* and *b*. If the discriminant is equal to zero, there will be two coinciding real solutions at −*b*/2*a*. Finally, if the discriminant is greater than zero, there will be two real negative solutions, because $\sqrt{b^2-4ac} < b$ for positive *a*, *b* and *c*.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

For a polynomial to be Hurwitz, it is necessary but not sufficient that all of its coefficients be positive (except for quadratic polynomials, which also imply sufficiency). A necessary and sufficient condition that a polynomial is Hurwitz is that it passes the [Routh–Hurwitz stability criterion](wikipedia://en/1705432). A given polynomial can be efficiently tested to be Hurwitz or not by using the Routh continued fraction expansion technique.
