# Franz Mertens

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Franz Mertens"}

**Franz Mertens** (20 March 1840 – 5 March 1927) (also known as **Franciszek Mertens**) was a German-Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was born in [Schroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Środa_Wielkopolska) in the [Grand Duchy of Posen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Posen), [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) (now [Środa Wielkopolska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Środa_Wielkopolska), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)) and died in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic).

:::

The [Mertens function](wikipedia://en/435639) *M*(*x*) is the sum function for the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961), in the theory of [arithmetic functions](wikipedia://en/3170). The [Mertens conjecture](wikipedia://en/379847) concerning its growth, conjecturing it bounded by *x*<sup>1/2</sup>, which would have implied the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125), is now known to be false ([Odlyzko](wikipedia://en/1986042) and [te Riele](wikipedia://en/1587570), 1985). The [Meissel–Mertens constant](wikipedia://en/435212) is analogous to the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant), but the [harmonic series](wikipedia://en/142488) sum in its definition is only over the [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) rather than over all integers and the logarithm is taken twice, not just once. [Mertens's theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mertens's_theorems) are three 1874 results related to the density of prime numbers.

[Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger) was taught calculus and algebra by Mertens.[^o3]

His memory is honoured by the Franciszek Mertens Scholarship granted (from 2017) to those outstanding pupils of foreign secondary schools who wish to study at the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science of the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) and were finalists of the [national-level mathematics](wikipedia://en/19710#National_mathematics_olympiads), or computer science olympiads, or they have participated in one of the following international olympiads: in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/82208) (IMO), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympiad_in_Informatics) (IOI), [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympiad_in_Artificial_Intelligence) (IOAI), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomy_Olympiad) (IAO), [astronomy and astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympiad_on_Astronomy_and_Astrophysics) (IOAA), [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Physics_Olympiad) (IPhO), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Linguistics_Olympiad) (IOL), [European Girls' Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/36583162) (EGMO), European Girls’ Olympiad in Informatics (EGOI), Romanian Masters of Mathematics (RMM), Romanian Masters of Informatics (RMI) or International Zhautykov Olympiad (IZhO).[^o4]
