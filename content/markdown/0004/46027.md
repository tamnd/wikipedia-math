# Gerhard Gentzen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gerhard Gentzen"}

**Gerhard Karl Erich Gentzen** (24 November 1909 – 4 August 1945) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician). He made major contributions to the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358), [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), especially on [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072) and [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329). He died of starvation in a Czech prison camp in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) in 1945.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Gentzen was a student of [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580) at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). Bernays was fired as "non-[Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan)" in April 1933 and therefore [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) formally acted as Gentzen's supervisor after that. Gentzen joined the [Sturmabteilung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung) in November 1933, although he was by no means compelled to do so.[^n1] Nevertheless, he kept in contact with Bernays until the beginning of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). In 1935, he corresponded with [Abraham Fraenkel](wikipedia://en/528451) in Jerusalem and was implicated by the Nazi teachers' union as one who "keeps contacts to the [Chosen People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosen_People)." In 1935 and 1936, [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), head of the Göttingen mathematics department in 1933 until his resignation under Nazi pressure, made strong efforts to bring him to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton.

Between November 1935 and 1939 he was an assistant of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) in Göttingen. Gentzen joined the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) in 1937. In April 1939, Gentzen swore the [oath of loyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_Oath) to [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) as part of his academic appointment.[^n2] From 1943 he was a teacher at the German [Charles-Ferdinand University of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Ferdinands-Universität).[^n3] Under a contract from the [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel), Gentzen worked for the [V-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2) project.[^n4]

Gentzen was arrested during the [citizens uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_uprising) against the occupying German forces on 5 May 1945. He, along with the rest of the staff of the German University in Prague, were detained in a Soviet prison camp, where he died of starvation on 4 August 1945.[^n5] He is probably buried in an unmarked grave at the [Ďáblice Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ďáblice_Cemetery) in Prague.[^o10]

## Work {#Work}

Gentzen's main work was on the foundations of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), specifically [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072) and the [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329), building off of previous work of [Paul Hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Hertz). His [cut-elimination theorem](wikipedia://en/910234) is the cornerstone of [proof-theoretic semantics](wikipedia://en/910505), and some philosophical remarks in his "Investigations into Logical Deduction", together with [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741)'s later work, constitute the starting point for [inferential role semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferential_role_semantics).

One of Gentzen's papers had a second publication in the ideological *[Deutsche Mathematik](wikipedia://en/31018614)* that was founded by [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) who promoted "Aryan" mathematics.[^n6]

Gentzen [proved the consistency](wikipedia://en/4076831) of the [Peano axioms](wikipedia://en/25005) in a paper published in 1936.[^n7] In his [*Habilitationsschrift*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation), finished in 1939, he determined the proof-theoretical strength of Peano arithmetic. This was done by a direct proof of the unprovability of the principle of transfinite induction, used in his 1936 proof of consistency, within Peano arithmetic. The principle can, however, be expressed in arithmetic, so that a direct proof of [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem) followed. [Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) used a coding procedure to construct an unprovable formula of arithmetic. Gentzen's proof was published in 1943 and marked the beginning of [ordinal proof theory](wikipedia://en/16084282).
