# Gábor Szegő

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gábor Szegő"}

**Gábor Szegő** () (January 20, 1895 – August 7, 1985) was a Hungarian-American mathematician. He was one of the foremost [mathematical analysts](wikipedia://en/48396) of his generation and made fundamental contributions to the theory of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) and [Toeplitz matrices](wikipedia://en/151569) building on the work of his contemporary [Otto Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/547682).

:::

## Life {#Life}

*Image: George Polya and Gabor Szego in Berlin.jpg.* Szegő with [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) in Berlin, delivering the manuscript of *[Problems and Theorems in Analysis](wikipedia://en/77356586)* to [Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media).

Szegő was born in [Kunhegyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunhegyes), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (today [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary)), into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) family as the son of Adolf Szegő and Hermina Neuman.[^o4] He married the chemist Anna Elisabeth Neményi in 1919, with whom he had two children.

In 1912 he started studies in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) at the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Budapest), with summer visits to the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he attended lectures by [Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048) and [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), amongst others. In Budapest he was taught mainly by [Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357), Beke, [Kürschák](wikipedia://en/6023762) and [Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihály_Bauer)[^o5] and made the acquaintance of his future collaborators [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) and [Michael Fekete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fekete). His studies were interrupted in 1915 by [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), in which he served in the infantry, artillery and air corps. In 1918 while stationed in Vienna, he was awarded a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) by the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) for his work on [Toeplitz determinants](wikipedia://en/151569).[^o7] He received his [Privat-Dozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privat-Dozent) from the University of Berlin in 1921, where he stayed until being appointed as successor to [Knopp](wikipedia://en/5630788) at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) in 1926. Intolerable working conditions during the [Nazi regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_regime) resulted in a temporary position at the [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis), [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri) in 1936, before his appointment as chairman of the mathematics department at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1938, where he helped build up the department until his retirement in 1966. He died in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California). His doctoral students include [Paul Rosenbloom](wikipedia://en/37395137) and [Joseph Ullman](wikipedia://en/36836800). [The Gábor Szegö Prize](https://www.siam.org/programs-initiatives/prizes-awards/activity-group-prizes/gabor-szego-prize/), Szegő Gábor Primary School, and Szegő Gábor Matematikaverseny (a mathematics competition in his former school) are all named in his honor.

## Works {#Works}

Szegő's most important work was in [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_%28mathematics%29). He was one of the foremost analysts of his generation and made fundamental contributions to the theory of [Toeplitz matrices](wikipedia://en/151569) and [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718). He wrote over 130 papers in several languages. Each of his four books, several written in collaboration with others, has become a classic in its field. The monograph *[Orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718)*, published in 1939, contains much of his research and has had a profound influence in many areas of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), including [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).

## Tutoring von Neumann {#Tutoring_von_Neumann}

At the age of 15, the young [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), recognised as a mathematical prodigy, was sent to study advanced calculus under Szegő. On their first meeting, Szegő was so astounded by von Neumann's mathematical talent and speed that, as recalled by his wife, he came back home with tears in his eyes.[^o10] Szegő subsequently visited the von Neumann house twice a week to tutor the child prodigy. Some of von Neumann's instant solutions to the problems in calculus posed by Szegő, sketched out on his father's stationery, are now on display at the von Neumann archive at Budapest.[^o11]

## Honours {#Honours}

*Image: Gabor Szego (cropped).jpg.* Bust of Gábor Szegő in his hometown of Kunhegyes

Amongst the many honours received during his lifetime were:

- [Julius König Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_König) of the Hungarian Mathematical Society (1928)
- Member of the [Königsberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) Gelehrten Gesellschaft (1928)
- Corresponding member of the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences) in Vienna (1960)
- Honorary member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) (1965)
