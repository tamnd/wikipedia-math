# Lagged Fibonacci generator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lagged Fibonacci generator"}

A **Lagged Fibonacci generator** (**LFG** or sometimes **LFib**) is an example of a [pseudorandom number generator](wikipedia://en/45524). This class of [random number generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_number_generator) is aimed at being an improvement on the 'standard' [linear congruential generator](wikipedia://en/45527). These are based on a generalisation of the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918).

:::

The Fibonacci sequence may be described by the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806):

$$S_n = S_{n-1} + S_{n-2}$$

Hence, the new term is the sum of the last two terms in the sequence. This can be generalised to the sequence:

$$S_n \equiv S_{n-j} \star S_{n-k} \pmod{m}, 0 < j < k$$

In which case, the new term is some combination of any two previous terms. *m* is usually a power of 2 (*m* = 2<sup>*M*</sup>), often 2<sup>32</sup> or 2<sup>64</sup>. The $\star$ operator denotes a general [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948). This may be either addition, subtraction, multiplication, or the [bitwise](wikipedia://en/264399) exclusive-or operator ([XOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR)). The theory of this type of generator is rather complex, and it may not be sufficient simply to choose random values for $j$ and $k$. These generators also tend to be very sensitive to initialisation.

Generators of this type employ *k* words of state (they 'remember' the last *k* values).

If the operation used is addition, then the generator is described as an *Additive Lagged Fibonacci Generator* or ALFG, if multiplication is used, it is a *Multiplicative Lagged Fibonacci Generator* or MLFG, and if the XOR operation is used, it is called a *Two-tap [generalised feedback shift register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalised_feedback_shift_register)* or GFSR. The [Mersenne Twister](wikipedia://en/45538) algorithm is a variation on a GFSR. The GFSR is also related to the [linear-feedback shift register](wikipedia://en/58992), or LFSR.

## Properties of lagged Fibonacci generators {#Properties_of_lagged_Fibonacci_generators}

The maximum period of lagged Fibonacci generators depends on the binary operation $\star$. If addition or subtraction is used, the maximum period is (2<sup>*k*</sup> − 1) × 2<sup>*M*−1</sup> . If multiplication is used, the maximum period is (2<sup>*k*</sup> − 1) × 2<sup>*M*−3</sup>, or 1/4 of period of the additive case. If bitwise xor is used, the maximum period is 2<sup>*k*</sup> − 1.

For the generator to achieve this maximum period, the polynomial:

*y* = *x*<sup>*k*</sup> + *x*<sup>*j*</sup> + 1

must be [primitive](wikipedia://en/1029177) over the integers mod 2. Values of *j* and *k* satisfying this constraint have been published in the literature.

*Popular pairs of primitive polynomial degrees[^o4][^o5]*

::: {#o3 .table cue=table name="Popular pairs of primitive polynomial degrees"}

| *j* | 7 | 5 | 24 | 65 | 128 | 6 | 31 | 97 | 353 | 168 | 334 | 273 | 418 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *k* | 10 | 17 | 55 | 71 | 159 | 31 | 63 | 127 | 521 | 521 | 607 | 607 | 1279 |

:::

Another list of possible values for *j* and *k* is on page 29 of volume 2 of *[The Art of Computer Programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Computer_Programming)*:

(24, 55), (38, 89), (37, 100), (30, 127), (83, 258), (107, 378), (273, 607), (1029, 2281), (576, 3217), (4187, 9689), (7083, 19937), (9739, 23209)

Note that the smaller number have short periods (only a few "random" numbers are generated before the first "random" number is repeated and the sequence restarts).

If addition is used, it is required that at least one of the first *k* values chosen to initialise the generator be odd. If multiplication is used, instead, it is required that all the first *k* values be odd, and further that at least one of them is ±3 mod 8.[^o6]

It has been suggested that good ratios between $j$ and $k$ are approximately the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).[^o7]

## Problems with LFGs {#Problems_with_LFGs}

In a paper on four-tap shift registers, [Robert M. Ziff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Ziff), referring to LFGs that use the XOR operator, states that "It is now widely known that such generators, in particular with the two-tap rules such as R(103, 250), have serious deficiencies. [Marsaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marsaglia) observed very poor behavior with R(24, 55) and smaller generators, and advised against using generators of this type altogether. ... The basic problem of two-tap generators R(a, b) is that they have a built-in three-point correlation between $x_{n}$, $x_{n-a}$, and $x_{n-b}$, simply given by the generator itself ... While these correlations are spread over the size $p = max(a, b, c, \ldots )$ of the generator itself, they can evidently still lead to significant errors.".[^o9] This only refers to the standard LFG where each new number in the sequence depends on two previous numbers. A three-tap LFG has been shown to eliminate some statistical problems such as failing the [Birthday Spacings](wikipedia://en/2167005) and Generalized Triple tests.[^o7]

## Example implementation {#Example_implementation}

A simple implementation in the [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) programming language may look as shown below. This implementation uses 64-bit words and has a period of (2<sup>607</sup> -1) × 2<sup>63</sup>

```c
#define R (607)
#define S (273)

#include <stdint.h>

uint64_t  X[R];

uint64_t  gen_rand()
{
    static int j = S - 1, k = R - 1;
	uint64_t  r;
    
    r = X[k] = X[k] + X[j--];
	k--;
	if (j < 0)
		j = R - 1;
	else if (k < 0)
		k = R - 1;
	return r;
}
```

## Usage {#Usage}

- [Freeciv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeciv) uses a lagged Fibonacci generator with {j = 24, k = 55} for its random number generator.
- The [Boost library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost_library) includes an implementation of a lagged Fibonacci generator.
- [Subtract with carry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtract_with_carry), a lagged Fibonacci generator engine, is included in the [C++11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++11) library.
- The [Oracle Database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Database) implements this generator in its DBMS\_RANDOM package (available in Oracle 8 and newer versions).
