# Émile Picard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Émile Picard"}

**Charles Émile Picard** (; 24 July 1856 – 11 December 1941) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was elected the fifteenth member to occupy seat 1 of the [Académie française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_française) in 1924.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) on 24 July 1856 and educated there at the [Lycée Henri-IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Henri-IV). He then studied mathematics at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29).[^o6]

Picard's mathematical papers, textbooks, and many popular writings exhibit an extraordinary range of interests, as well as an impressive mastery of the mathematics of his time. [Picard's little theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard's_little_theorem) states that every nonconstant [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372) takes every value in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), with perhaps one exception. [Picard's great theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard's_great_theorem) states that an [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) with an [essential singularity](wikipedia://en/80862) takes every value infinitely often, with perhaps one exception, in any neighborhood of the singularity. He made important contributions in the theory of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), including work on [Picard–Vessiot theory](wikipedia://en/30247996), [Painlevé transcendents](wikipedia://en/2033003) and his introduction of a kind of [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) for a [linear differential equation](wikipedia://en/379868). He also introduced the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) in the theory of [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311), which describes the classes of [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) on the surface modulo linear equivalence. In connection with his work on function theory, he was one of the first mathematicians to use the emerging ideas of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). In addition to his theoretical work, Picard made contributions to [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), including the theories of [telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraphy) and [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29). His collected papers run to four volumes.

[Louis Couturat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Couturat) studied [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus) with Picard in 1891–1892, taking detailed notes of the lectures. These notes were preserved and now are available in three cahiers from [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive).[^o7]

Like his contemporary, [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740), Picard was much concerned with the training of mathematics, physics, and engineering students. He wrote a classic textbook on [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and one of the first textbooks on the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001). Picard's popular writings include biographies of many leading French mathematicians, including his father in law, [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422).

Picard was an International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences),[^o8] an International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o9] and an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o10]

## Family {#Family}

In 1881 he married Marie, the daughter of [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422).

## Society {#Society}

Charles Émile Picard was one of the founder members in 1930 of the [Society of Friends of André-Marie Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Friends_of_André-Marie_Ampère) which was created to develop the first science museum in France, the [Ampère Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampère_Museum) close to [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon).[^o13]
