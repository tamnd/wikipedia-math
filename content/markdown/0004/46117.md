# Eratosthenes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eratosthenes"}

**Eratosthenes of Cyrene** ( ; Ἐρατοσθένης ; c. 276 BC – c. 195/194 BC) was an Ancient Greek [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath): a philosopher, scholar, mathematician, geographer, poet, astronomer, and music theorist. Eratosthenes eventually became the chief librarian at the [Library of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Alexandria). His work was the precursor to the modern discipline of geography, and he introduced some of its terminology, coining the terms *geography* and *geographer*.[^o3]

:::

He is best remembered as the first known person to calculate the [Earth's circumference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_circumference#Eratosthenes). He was also the first to calculate [Earth's axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_axial_tilt), which similarly proved to have remarkable accuracy.[^o4][^o64] He created the [first global projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eratosthenes'_Map_of_the_World) of the world incorporating [parallels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_latitude) and [meridians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) based on the geographic knowledge of his era.[^o4] Eratosthenes was the founder of scientific [chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology); he used Egyptian and Persian records to estimate the dates of the main events of the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War), dating the sack of [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy) to 1184 BC.[^o5] In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), he introduced the [sieve of Eratosthenes](wikipedia://en/73415), an efficient method of identifying [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) and composite numbers.

His devotees nicknamed him *Pentathlos* after the Olympians who were well-rounded competitors, for he had proven himself to be knowledgeable in every area of learning. Yet, according to an entry in the *[Suda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suda)* (a 10th-century Byzantine encyclopedia), some critics scorned him, calling him *Beta* (Second, or Number 2*)* because he always came in second in all his endeavors.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

## Life {#Life}

The son of Aglaos, Eratosthenes was born in [276 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/276_BC) in [Cyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrene_%28city%29). Now part of modern-day [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya), Cyrene had been founded by Greeks during the second half of the 7th century BCE,[^o3] and its proximity to the sea, its defensible position, its abundant water sources and its rich soil all contributed to its status as the capital city of [Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrenaica).[^o10] Cyrene came under the rule of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in [332 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/332_BC),[^o10] and following his death in [323 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/323_BC), after a local civil war, it was seized by one of his generals, [Ptolemy I Soter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_I_Soter), the founder of the [Ptolemaic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom).[^o10] When Cyrene came under Ptolemaic rule, it had a rich economy, based largely on the export of horses and [silphium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silphium_%28antiquity%29),[^o3] and was long known as a prosperous hub of Greek culture.[^o3]

According to Roller, the rarity of both Eratosthenes' and his father's names are indicative of his humble origins, although due to the possibilities of upward mobility in the [Hellenistic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_world) he was not limited by them.[^o3] However, Matthew suggests that his name, meaning "lovely strength" suggests noble upbringing,[^n1] as does his education from a young age, which could imply his belonging to the aristocracy of Cyrene.[^o3] Like any young Greek at the time, Eratosthenes would have [studied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_ancient_Greece) in the local [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28ancient_Greece%29), where he would have learned physical skills as well as reading, writing, arithmetic, poetry, and music.[^o11][^o12]

By the late [260s BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/260s_BCE), Eratosthenes went to [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens) to further his studies.[^o3] According to [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo), he was taught [Stoicism](wikipedia://en/19224834) there by the school's founder, [Zeno of Citium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeno_of_Citium), though their interaction would have been minimal, since Zeno died shortly after Eratosthenes arrived.[^o3] Strabo also lists the little-known Apelles of [Chios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chios) among his teachers.[^o3] Eratosthenes is said to have studied under the [cynic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynicism_%28philosophy%29) [Aristo of Chios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristo_of_Chios),[^o13] and the eclectic [Bion of Borysthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bion_of_Borysthenes).[^o3] He was further taught by the recently appointed head of the [Platonic Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_Academy), [Arcesilaus of Pitane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcesilaus_of_Pitane).[^o3] Eratosthenes' later mathematical work implies that he received mathematical training there.[^o3] According to the *Suda* Eratosthenes was also a student of Lysanias of Cyrene, a philologist and grammarian who focused on [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer).[^o6] The poet, scholar, and librarian [Callimachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callimachus) is likely to have crossed paths with Eratosthenes in local debates and scholarly discourse,[^o3] even though it is unlikely he was ever formally his teacher.

*Image: Eratosthenes\_Teaching\_in\_Alexandria\_(Bernardo\_Strozzi,\_Montreal).jpg.* *Eratosthenes teaching in Alexandria* by [Bernardo Strozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Strozzi) (1635)

Strabo complained that Eratosthenes did not pay enough respect to Zeno[^o3] and criticized him for his association with such varied schools of thought. He believed Eratosthenes was unwilling to commit to philosophy and had learned only enough to appear as a philosopher, seeing it as nothing more than a distraction from his regular work.[^n1][^o3] Later authors may have shared this view to some extent: The *Suda* states that Eratosthenes was referred to as *Beta* (Second, or Number 2), because he was not seen as the leading expert in any given field.[^o3][^o6] Others dubbed him *Pentathlos* (Πένταθλος - All-Rounded), given his various skills and areas of knowledge;[^o13][^o6] *Pentathlos*, however, is also the title of an athlete who competes in many events but comes in second in all of them.[^o55] Strabo described Eratosthenes as a mathematician among geographers and a geographer among mathematicians.[^o72]

The majority of Eratosthenes' studies focused on philosophy; mathematics was less prominent, and philology even less so.[^o3] Despite his later contributions to the field, Eratosthenes could not formally study geography, as such a discipline did not exist at the time.[^o3] Eratosthenes was however exposed to extensive geographic literature, such as the works of Homer, whom he considered the first geographer, [Hecataeus of Miletus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecataeus_of_Miletus) (*Circuit of the Earth*), [Aeschylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeschylus), [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus), and others.[^o3] Additionally, Eratosthenes was born forty years after the death of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), and he would also have encountered the works of Alexander's travel companions [Androsthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androsthenes_of_Thasos), [Nearchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearchus), [Onesikratos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onesicritus), [Ptolemy I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_I_Soter), and others, who wrote about their journeys with him, and whose conquests cleared the path for Hellenistic explorers.[^o3]

Eratosthenes remained in Athens for some twenty years, studying and writing.[^o3] During this period, he wrote *Platonikos*, inquiring into the mathematics and music in Plato's philosophy, as well as the poetic works of *Hermes* and *Erigone*. His *Chronographies* focused on the important dates of the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War), and his *Olympic Victors* compiled a list of the winners of the Olympic games.[^n1] Little more is known about this period of his life.[^o3]

In [246 BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/246_BCE), [Ptolemy III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_III_Euergetes) succeeded his father, [Ptolemy II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_II_Philadelphus). Over the next twenty-five years, the [Ptolemaic Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom) reached its greatest extent, and [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) attained its zenith as an intellectual center.[^o3] The post of [librarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Alexandria), which included the position of royal tutor to [Ptolemy IV Philopator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_IV_Philopator),[^o55] became the most prestigious academic appointment.[^o3] The reigning librarian, [Apollonius of Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_of_Rhodes), was forced into retirement by the new king (possibly through the influence of Callimachus), and Eratosthenes, who by this time was gaining fame as a scholar and a poet in the tradition of Callimachus, was summoned from Athens to replace him.[^o3] Roller suggests that Eratosthenes' roots in Cyrene, the native city of Callimachus, and more importantly Queen [Berenike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berenice_II), contributed favorably to his appointment.[^o3]

The beginning of Eratosthenes' career in Alexandria was focused on mathematics. He was closely affiliated with [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844), who sent him material for comment and praised him enthusiastically for his contributions;[^o3] his [*Method of Mechanical Theorems*](wikipedia://en/261272) was written as a letter to Eratosthenes,[^o14] and he sent Eratosthenes the famous *[Cattle Problem](wikipedia://en/3804457)* to be presented to the mathematicians of Alexandria.[^o13] [^o55] [^o15] Eratosthenes subsequently wrote compositions on [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447), [literary criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_criticism), [grammar](wikipedia://en/12569), poetry and [star lore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_lore).[^n1] [^o13] D. R. Dicks suggests that his astronomical contributions were hardly notable,[^o13] and it was said that his poetry strangely contained the very [didactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didacticism) elements which he condemned.[^o3]

Toward the end of his days, he served as an advisor and companion to [Arsinoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsinoe_III), sister and wife of Ptolemy IV.[^n1] According to the *Suda*, as he aged his eyesight began to fail.[^n1] [^o6] Losing the ability to read and to observe nature plagued and depressed him, leading him to voluntarily starve himself to death.[^n1] He died at the age of 80 in Alexandria[^n1] around [196 BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/196_BCE).[^n1] Roller notes that Dionysios of Kyzikos recorded the genuine epitaph of Eratosthenes, bemoaning the fact that he was buried in a foreign land, with "the shore of Proteus" being a Homeric allusion to the land of Egypt:[^o3]

> A softening old age with no darkening through disease quenched you and put you to deserved sleep pondering great things, Eratosthenes. Mother Kyrene did not receive you into the paternal tombs, son of Aglaos, but you are buried as a friend in a foreign land, here on the edge of the shore of Proteus.[^o3]

The *Suda* records four students of Eratosthenes: [Aristophanes of Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristophanes_of_Byzantium), his successor as Librarian of Alexandria, the geographer [Mnaseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnaseas) of [Patara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patara_%28Lycia%29) in [Lycia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycia), the historian Menander, probably of Ephesos, and Aristis, who was otherwise unknown.[^o6][^n1]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

### Astronomy {#Astronomy}

#### Measurement of Earth's circumference {#Measurement_of_Earth's_circumference}

*Image: Eratosthenes\_measure\_of\_Earth\_circumference.svg.* Measure of Earth's circumference according to Cleomedes's simplified version, based on the approximation that [Syene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syene) is on the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer) and on the same meridian as [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria)

The [Earth's circumference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_circumference) is the most famous measurement obtained by Eratosthenes.[^o69] He described his [arc measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_measurement) technique in his book *On the Measure of the Earth*, which has not been preserved.[^o19] However, a simplified version of the method as described by [Cleomedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleomedes) was preserved.[^o20]

The simplified method works by considering two cities along the same [meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_%28geography%29), and the difference in angles of the shadows cast by the sun on a vertical rod (a [gnomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomon)). The two cities used by Eratosthenes were [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) and [Syene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syene) (modern Aswan). At noon on the summer [solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solstice), there were still shadows in Alexandria. However, in Syene, rods cast no shadows, and the Sun's rays shone straight down into the city-center well.[^o21]

According to Cleomedes, Eratosthenes then measured the shadow's angle to be about 7.2 degrees, which is 1/50 of a full circle, and reasoned using [alternate interior angles](wikipedia://en/13295107) that this angle represented the portion of Earth's curvature between the two cities. The distance between Alexandria and Syene was reported to be about 5,000 stadia, as measured by professional [bematists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bematist).[^o22] Eratosthenes multiplied this number by 50 and arrived at a total of roughly 250,000 stadia for the Earth's circumference.[^o55]

This calculation is expressed algebraically as $C = \frac{360^\circ d}{\Delta\theta}$

where $C$ is the Earth's circumference, $d$ is the distance between the two cities, and ${\Delta\theta}$ is the difference in the two cities' shadow angles.

According to Matthew, the result of Eratosthenes calculation is approximately 40338,[^n1] while the modern day measurement of the circumference around the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) is 40075.017; passing through the [poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_pole) the circumference is 40007.863.[^o23]

While Eratosthenes' method was sound he made two false assumptions which fortuitously cancelled each other out. The first was that Syene was on the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer) and the second was that it lay on the same meridian of longitude (directly south) of Alexandria. In fact Syene is 1° north of the Tropic and 3° east of Alexandria.[^o24]

#### Sun measurements {#Sun_measurements}

[Eusebius of Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea) in his *[Preparatio Evangelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatio_Evangelica)* includes a brief chapter of three sentences on celestial distances.[^o26] He states simply that Eratosthenes found the distance to the Sun to be "σταδίων μυριάδας τετρακοσίας καὶ ὀκτωκισμυρίας" (literally "of [stadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadia_%28length%29) [myriads](wikipedia://en/20088) 400 and 80,000") and the distance to the Moon to be 780,000 stadia. The expression for the distance to the Sun has been translated either as 4,080,000 stadia[^o26] or as 804,000,000 stadia.[^o27] The meaning depends on whether Eusebius meant 400 myriad plus 80,000 or "400 and 80,000" myriad. With a stade of 185, 804,000,000 stadia is 149000000, approximately the distance from the Earth to the Sun.

Eratosthenes also calculated the Sun's diameter. According to [Macrobius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrobius), Eratosthenes made the diameter of the Sun to be about 27 times that of the Earth.[^o65] The actual figure is approximately 109 times.[^o68]

#### Obliquity of the ecliptic {#Obliquity_of_the_ecliptic}

Eratosthenes determined the [obliquity of the ecliptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt).[^o29] The [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264) is the apparent circular orbit of the sun projected onto the imaginary celestial sphere over the course of a year; its obliquity is the inclination of its plane relative to the plane of the equator.[^o29] The value of this angle is not constant; at the time of Eratosthenes, it was 23° 43′ 40″. As early as the 5th century BC, [Oenopides of Chios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oenopides) had determined 24°; Eratosthenes improved the accuracy of the measurement.[^o29] He determined the angular distance between the two tropics as of the full circle (360°), i.e., 47° 42′ 40″, which, when halved, yields a value of 23° 51′ 20″.[^o29] How he arrived at this result is unknown; the hypotheses considered in research are speculative. While at the Library of Alexandria, Eratosthenes devised a calendar using his predictions about the ecliptic of the Earth. He calculated that there are 365 days in a year and that every fourth year there would be 366 days.[^o70] The [Greek astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_astronomy) [Hipparchus](wikipedia://en/13600) (c. 190) credited Eratosthenes (276 – 194 BC) as the inventor of the armillary sphere,[^o5] [^o30][^o31][^o32][^o33] a model of [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_object) in the sky (on the [celestial sphere](wikipedia://en/48239)), consisting of a spherical framework of rings, centered on [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228) or the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun), that represent lines of [celestial longitude and latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_coordinate_system) and other astronomically important features, such as the ecliptic.[^o34]

### Geography {#Geography}

*Image: Mappa\_di\_Eratostene.jpg.* 19th-century reconstruction of [Eratosthenes's map of the (for the Greeks) known world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eratosthenes'_Map_of_the_World), c. 194 BC

Eratosthenes continued to study the Earth, and began to sketch it. In the Library of Alexandria he had access to travel books, which contained information and representations of the world that needed to be pieced together in some organized format.[^o65] In his three-volume work *Geography* (Geographika), he described and mapped his entire known world, even dividing the Earth into five [climate zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_classification):[^o66] two freezing zones around the poles, two [temperate zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_zones), and a zone encompassing the equator and the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics).[^o36] He placed grids of overlapping lines over the surface of the Earth. He used parallels and meridians to link together every place in the world. It was then possible to estimate the distance from remote locations with this network over the surface of the Earth. In the *Geography* he recorded the names of over 400 cities and their locations were shown, a feat without precedent.[^o3]

According to Strabo, Eratosthenes argued against the Greek-[Barbarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian) dichotomy and said Alexander ignored his advisers by his regard for all people with law and government. Though he argued that Eratosthenes was wrong to claim that Alexander had disregarded the counsel of his advisers asserting that it was Alexander's interpretation of their "real intent" in recognizing that "in some people there prevail the law-abiding and the political instinct, and the qualities associated with education and powers of speech".[^o37]

### Mathematics, music theory and metaphysics {#Mathematics,_music_theory_and_metaphysics}

In *Platonikos*, primarily mathematical questions were dealt with; the concepts discussed included distance, ratio, continuous and discontinuous proportion, mathematical mean, prime number and point. The focus was on the theory of proportions, in which Eratosthenes saw the key to [Platonic philosophy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism) He applied the tool of the ratio equation ("a is to b as c is to d"), which he called "analogy", to both mathematics and philosophy.[^o39] Friedrich Solmsen states that in proportion, he believed he had found the unifying bond of the "mathematical" sciences ([arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_theory)), since all statements of these sciences could ultimately be traced back to statements about proportions.[^o40]

According to Theon of Smyrna, he perceived ratio as the foundational principle which underlies proportion, as well as the "primary cause of the creation of all orderly things",[^o41] while he saw the number one as the starting point *([archḗ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_principle))* and the primary element *(stoicheíon)* of numbers and quantity.[^o41][^o42]

For Eratosthenes, numbers are unproblematic; but lines, on the other hand, are curious, as they cannot be produced by the combination of individual points, since the individual point has no extension. Eratosthenes contends rather it arises from the continuous movement of a point.[^o43] This view was later criticized by the skeptic [Sextus Empiricus](wikipedia://en/177582).[^o43]

*Image: Sieve of Eratosthenes animation.gif.* Sieve of Eratosthenes: algorithm steps for primes below 121 (including optimization of starting from the prime's square)

Eratosthenes proposed a mathematical approximate solution to the problem of [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110), which was unsolvable with compass and ruler. In order to solve this problem, he constructed a mechanical line drawing device to calculate the cube, called the [Mesolabio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolabio).[^o44] He dedicated his solution to King Ptolemy, presenting a model in bronze, with a letter and an epigram.[^o45][^o71]

Eratosthenes used an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) that allows one to separate all prime numbers from the set of all odd natural numbers that are less than or equal to a given number. This method is known as the *[Sieve of Eratosthenes](wikipedia://en/73415)* (Greek: κόσκινον Ἐρατοσθένους). However, according to Hans-Joachim Waschkies, he did not invent it - as was previously believed; rather, it was already known, and he only coined the term "sieve".[^o46][^o47]

Eratosthenes' sieve is one of a number of [prime number sieves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number_sieve), and is a simple, ancient algorithm for finding all prime numbers up to any given limit. It does so by iteratively marking as composite, *i.e.*, not prime, the multiples of each prime, starting with the multiples of 2. The multiples of a given prime are generated starting from that prime, as a sequence of numbers with the same difference, equal to that prime, between consecutive numbers. This is the sieve's key distinction from using trial division to sequentially test each candidate number for divisibility by each prime.

A secondary subject of *Platonikos* was [music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_theory), in which Eratosthenes applied the theory of proportions to music,[^o48] Since antiquity, he is considered an authority in the field of music.[^o48] The scholar [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979) preserved Eratosthenes' calculations for the tetrachord, which show that he used the "Pythagorean" tuning, which he then refined.[^o49] Eratosthenes knew and considered the system of the music theorist [Aristoxenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristoxenus).[^o49] However, Ptolemy does not disclose how he proceeded with his calculations.

Eratosthenes addressed metaphysics such as the doctrine of the [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul) in the *Platonikos*. Like the Platonist [Crantor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crantor), by whom he was probably influenced, he held the view that the soul could not be purely immaterial, but must have something corporeal about it, for it exists in the world of sensible things; moreover, it is always in a body.[^o50][^o51] This is based on the idea that the soul can only grasp sensible objects if it has a corresponding disposition in its own structure. Accordingly, it is a mixture of two components, one incorporeal and one corporeal.[^o47]

## Works {#Works}

*Image: Eratosthenes profile.png.* Etching by Philipp Daniel Lippert, *Dactyliothec*, 1767

Eratosthenes was one of the most eminent scholars of his time, and produced works covering a vast area of knowledge before and during his time at the Library. There are no documents left of his work after the [destruction of the Library of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Library_of_Alexandria).

### Athenian period {#Athenian_period}

- *Platonikos* - Most probably Eratosthenes' main mathematical treatise, of which only few extracts remain, found in the *Expositio rerum mathematicarum ad legendum Platonem utilium*, by [Theon of Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theon_of_Smyrna).[^o13] It is unclear whether the work was a commentary on [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954)'s *[Timaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaeus_%28dialogue%29)* or a dialogue with Plato as the principal speaker.[^o55] It is suggested that it served as a handbook intended to make Plato's works easier for a wider audience to access by clarifying terms and explaining difficult passages.[^o47]
- *On the Old Comedy* - A work of literary criticism consisting of twelve books, which attempted to derive the authorship of plays from the dates they were performed, included discussions of textual criticism and contained a section on the meaning and usage of words.[^o13] The latter was highly praised and often cited by ancient authors.[^o13]
- *Anterinys/Hesiod* - A poetic work, now lost, the contents of which are unknown.[^o13]
- *Erigone* - A poetic work depicting the star legend of [Icarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icarius), his daughter [Erigone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erigone_%28daughter_of_Icarius%29) and her dog [Maera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maera_%28hound%29),[^o13] according to which Erigone committed suicide upon hearing about the death of her father.[^o3] The work contained astronomical elements, as the characters were translated as the heavenly bodies of [Boötes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boötes), [Virgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgo_Cluster), and [Sirius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius).[^o13]
- *Hermes* - A poetic work, of which some sixteen lines have survived.[^o13] It paralleled the beginning of the [Homeric hymn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeric_hymn), but added to it the heavenward ascent of Hermes which included a vivid description of the different climate zones of the inhabited world,[^o3][^o13] and contained "a good deal of descriptive astronomy" in the words of Thomas Heath.[^o55]

### Alexandrian period {#Alexandrian_period}

- *On Intermediate Terms (Peri mesotḗtōn)* - A work attributed to Eratosthenes by [Pappus](wikipedia://en/509300), of the late [third century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_century) CE.[^o55] Its contents were lost, but it can be said that it consisted of two books, and was of enough importance to be included in what Pappus called the "Treasury of Analysis" together with the writings of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331), [Apollonius](wikipedia://en/257242), and [Aristaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristaeus_the_Elder), thus implying that it was a systematic geometrical composition.[^o55] In another passage, Pappus refers to "loci with reference to means" which were discussed by Eratosthenes, supposedly in the work mentioned, the nature of these loci in unknown.[^o55] Since this work is not mentioned anywhere else in ancient sources, some have suggested that it is identical with *Platonikos*.[^o47] In 1981, a medieval Arabic translation of a text by "Aristanes" (Eratosthenes) on mean proportionals was published. However, this is not the lost work *On Intermediate Terms* mentioned by Pappus, but an alleged letter from Eratosthenes to [King Ptolemy III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_III_Euergetes) about the doubling of a cube, which is preserved in the original Greek text. The authenticity of the letter is disputed.
- *The Catasterismi* ("Placings among stars"), cited in the *Suda* under the title *Astronomy*.[^o56] The extant work by this name in its current form cannot be attributed to Eratosthenes, however it is rooted in a genuine work by him with the same name.[^o55] The *Catasterismi* contained a [star catalogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_catalogue), which references the writings of Aratus, but as opposed to the largely technical descriptions of Aratus, it includes a collection of legends relating to individual stars and constellations.[^o56] The catalogue contains 42 entries covering all the constellations, one entry on the planets and one entry on the milky way; it includes a list of stars belonging to each constellation, with their locations within the constellation, all together number 736,[^o56] (though Hipparchus has approximately 1,000).[^o57] It has been pointed out, that Eratosthenes did not invent the myths, which had been transmitted over centuries through Greek traditions, rather he connected these tales to the constellations and attributed the different mythical characters to them.[^o56]
- *Arsinoe* (a memoir of queen [Arsinoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsinoe_III_of_Egypt); lost; quoted by [Athenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenaeus) in the *[Deipnosophistae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deipnosophistae)*) - A biography or eulogy of Arsinoe III, wife and sister of Ptolemy IV, who was murdered at the age of 30 after her husband's death.[^n1] Eratosthenes had been her advisor and companion in public events.[^n1] The writing of the work is the last datable event in the life of Eratosthenes, and the work itself is likely the last that he wrote, as Arsinoe's death occurred in 204 BCE, Eratosthenes was about eighty years old at the time, and he did not live for much longer.[^n1]
- *On the Measurement of the Earth (Περὶ τῆς ἀναμετρήσεως τῆς γῆς)* - Described as a separate work by Heron in his Dioptra, and according to Galen it dealt with astronomical or mathematical geography.[^o55] Among the topics discussed were the size of the equator, the distance of the tropic and polar circles, the size of the polar area, the sizes of the sun and the moon and the distances from them and their total and partial eclipses and the changes in the length of the day according to location and date.
- *Geographica (ГεωγραΦικά)* - The work was the first attempt at providing a mathematical foundation for geographical studies, as well as the first recorded instance of many terms still in use, including the name of the science [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography).[^o58] It is now lost, but 155 [fragments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_fragment) survive, 105 in the writings of Strabo, 16 in the writings of [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder), and the rest scattered in Byzantine sources.[^n1] According to Strabo, who is the primary source for its form and content, it consisted of three parts.[^o13] For a long time it was the main authority on geographical matters, and was referred to by [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar) in *De Bello Gallico*, when he mentioned that Eratosthenes knew of the [Hercynian Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercynian_Forest).[^o13] Even the critical Strabo admitted that Eratosthenes was the leading authority on the southeastern quarter of the inhabited world.[^o13] The work described the global landmass as a whole, discussed its division into regions, estimated distances, landscape alterations, the location of the inhabited world, and included limited descriptions of lands and peoples.[^o13] The work was criticized by Strabo, who complained that Eratosthenes' approach was too mathematical, and by Hipparchus, who argued that it was not mathematical enough, as Eratosthenes did not make sufficient use of astronomical data in establishing the reference lines of his map.[^o13] It is possible that the circumference of the Earth was written as part of the *Geographica*, though if it wasn't, it was most likely mentioned in it.[^o13] Its detailed description is now known only through *De Motu Circulari* by Cleomedes.[^o13] The first book was something of an introduction and gave a review of his predecessors, recognizing their contributions that he compiled in the library. In this book Eratosthenes denounced [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer) as not providing any insight into what he described as geography. His disapproval of Homer's topography angered many who believed the world depicted in the *Odyssey* to be legitimate.[^o59][^o67] He commented on the ideas of the nature and origin of the Earth: he thought of Earth as an immovable globe while its surface was changing. He hypothesized that at one time the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) had been a vast lake that covered the countries that surrounded it and that it only became connected to the ocean to the west when a passage opened up sometime in its history. The second book contains his calculation of the circumference of the Earth. This is where, according to Pliny, "The world was grasped." Here Eratosthenes described his famous story of the well in Syene.[^o21] This book would later be considered a text on [mathematical geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_geography). His third book of the *Geography* contained [political geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_geography). He cited countries and used parallel lines to divide the map into sections, to give accurate descriptions of the realms. This was a breakthrough that can be considered the beginning of geography. For this, Eratosthenes was named the "Father of Modern Geography".[^o65]
- *Chronographies and Olympic Victors* - Two works that represent the first systematic, scientific treatment of chronological questions by a Greek author[^o13] and that established a dating system based on the Olympiads.[^o13] *Olympic Victors* was likely a popularizing work and included numerous anecdotes, some preserved by Plutarch.[^o13] Clement of Alexandria summarized its main results.[^o55] It provides dates for several events: the fall of Troy (1184/1183 BCE), the Dorian migration (1104/1103 BCE), the first Olympiad (777/776 BCE), Xerxes' invasion (480/479 BCE), and the outbreak of the Peloponnesian War (432/431 BCE), Eratosthenes' dates are still considered authoritative.[^o13]
