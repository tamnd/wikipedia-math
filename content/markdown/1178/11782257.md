# Concordance correlation coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Concordance correlation coefficient"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **concordance correlation coefficient** measures the agreement between two variables, e.g., to evaluate [reproducibility](wikipedia://en/47651) or for [inter-rater reliability](wikipedia://en/7837393).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The form of the concordance correlation coefficient $\rho_c$ as[^o4]

$$\rho_c = \frac{2\rho\sigma_x\sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + (\mu_x - \mu_y)^2},$$

where $\mu_x$ and $\mu_y$ are the [means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) for the two variables and $\sigma^2_x$ and $\sigma^2_y$ are the corresponding [variances](wikipedia://en/32344). $\rho$ is [Pearson's correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/221708) between the two variables.

This follows from its definition[^o4], as:

$$\rho_c = 1 - \frac{{\rm Expected\ orthogonal\ squared\ distance\ from\ the\ diagonal\ }x=y} {{\rm Expected\ orthogonal\ squared\ distance\ from\ the\ diagonal\ }x=y{\rm \ assuming\ independence}}.$$

When the concordance correlation coefficient is computed on a length-$N$ data set (i.e., $N$ paired values $(x_n, y_n)$, for $n=1,...,N$), the form is

$$\hat{\rho}_c = \frac{2 s_{xy}}{s_x^2 + s_y^2 + (\bar{x} - \bar{y})^2},$$

where the mean is computed as

$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N x_n$$

and the variance

$$s_x^2 = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N (x_n - \bar{x})^2$$

and the covariance

$$s_{xy} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N (x_n - \bar{x})(y_n - \bar{y}) .$$

Whereas the ordinary [correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_product-moment_correlation_coefficient) (Pearson's) is immune to whether the biased or unbiased versions for estimation of the variance is used, the concordance correlation coefficient is not. In the original article, Lin suggested normalizing with $1/N$ normalization,[^o4] while in another article Nickerson appears to have used $1/(N-1)$,[^o5] i.e., the concordance correlation coefficient may be computed slightly differently between implementations.

:::

## Relation to other measures of correlation {#Relation_to_other_measures_of_correlation}

The concordance correlation coefficient is nearly identical to some of the measures called [intra-class correlations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intra-class_correlation). Comparisons of the concordance correlation coefficient with an "ordinary" intraclass correlation on different data sets found only small differences between the two correlations, in one case on the third decimal.[^o5] It has also been stated[^o7] that the ideas for concordance correlation coefficient "are quite similar to results already published by Krippendorff[^o8] in 1970".

In the original article[^o4] Lin suggested a form for multiple classes (not just 2). Over ten years later a correction to this form was issued.[^o9]

One example of the use of the concordance correlation coefficient is in a comparison of analysis method for [functional magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_magnetic_resonance_imaging) brain scans.
