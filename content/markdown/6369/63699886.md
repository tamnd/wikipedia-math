# Knee of a curve

*Image: DataClustering ElbowCriterion.JPG.* Explained variance. The "elbow" is indicated by the red circle. The number of clusters chosen should therefore be 4.

*Image: Solar-Cell-IV-curve-with-MPP.png.* Photovoltaic solar cell I-V curves where a line intersects the knee of the curves where the maximum power transfer point is located.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Knee of a curve"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **knee of a curve** (or **elbow of a curve**) is a point where the curve visibly bends, specifically from high slope to low slope (flat or close to flat), or in the other direction. This is particularly used in [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), where a knee point is the optimum point for some decision, for example when there is an [increasing function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Increasing_function) and a trade-off between the benefit (vertical [*y* axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_axis)) and the cost (horizontal [*x* axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_axis)): the knee is where the benefit is no longer increasing rapidly, and is no longer worth the cost of further increases – a cutoff point of [diminishing returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishing_returns).

:::

In [heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic) use, the term may be used informally, and a knee point identified visually, but in more formal use an explicit [objective function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objective_function) is used, and depends on the particular optimization problem. A knee may also be defined purely geometrically, in terms of the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) or the [second derivative](wikipedia://en/3224219).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The knee of a curve can be defined as a [vertex](wikipedia://en/6865890) of the graph. This corresponds with the graphical intuition (it is where the curvature has a maximum), but depends on the choice of scale.

The term "knee" as applied to curves dates at least to the 1910s,[^o4] and is found more commonly by the 1940s,[^o5] being common enough to draw criticism.[^n1][^o7] The unabridged *[Webster's Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster's_Dictionary)* (1971 edition) gives definition 3h of *knee* as:[^n2] an abrupt change in direction in a curve (as on a graph); *esp* one approaching a right angle in shape.

:::

## Criticism {#Criticism}

Graphical notions of a "knee" of a curve, based on curvature, are criticized due to their dependence on the coordinate scale: different choices of scale result in different points being the "knee". This criticism dates at least to the 1940s, being found in (Worthing Geffner 1943, Preface), who criticize:[^o7] references to the significance of a so-called knee of a curve when the location of the knee was a function of the chosen coordinate scales

## Detection methods {#Detection_methods}

### Kneedle algorithm {#Kneedle_algorithm}

The Kneedle algorithm detects the best balanced tradeoff based on the mathematical [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) concept, which is defined and well studied for continuous functions.[^o13][^o14] Alternatively, the kneepointDetection() function[^o15] from the SamSPECTRAL[^o16] [R](wikipedia://en/376707) package can be used to find the knee point, where is a "phase change" in the data, by fitting two lines using linear regression.
