# Richard Gaskin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Gaskin"}

**Richard Maxwell Gaskin** (born 8 May 1960) is a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) who is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool). He has published on [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language) and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), and [history of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_philosophy), as well as on [philosophy of literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_and_literature), [literary theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_theory), and the [European literary tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_canon). Gaskin received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts), [Bachelor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Philosophy), and [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degrees in [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics) and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at [University College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Oxford), and has held academic posts at [St Edmund Hall, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Edmund_Hall,_Oxford), as well as at the [University of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex).[^o3]

:::

Gaskin is the author of many published articles and nine books, including: *Language and World: A Defence of Linguistic Idealism* (2020), *Tragedy and Redress in Western Literature: a Philosophical Perspective* (2018), *Language, Truth, and Literature: a Defence of Literary Humanism* (2013), *The Unity of the Proposition* (2008), *Experience and the World's Own Language: a Critique of John McDowell's Empiricism* (2006), and *The Sea Battle and the Master Argument: Aristotle and Diodorus Cronus on the metaphysics of the future* (1995).[^o4]

## Early life, education, and career {#Early_life,_education,_and_career}

Gaskin was born in 1960 in [Milngavie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milngavie), [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), and attended [Robert Gordon's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gordon's_College), [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen), where his father, Maxwell Gaskin, held the Jaffrey Chair of Political Economy at the University of Aberdeen.[^o6] He studied *[literae humaniores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literae_humaniores)* (classics and philosophy) at University College, Oxford, and obtained his BA (first class) in 1982. While an undergraduate at Oxford he was secretary of the [Oxford University Dramatic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Dramatic_Society) from 1981 to 82, and directed a production of Marlowe's *Dr Faustus* at the Oxford Playhouse in March 1981.[^o7] He took the BPhil exam in 1986, supervised by [John McDowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McDowell). In 1987 he won the [Gaisford Dissertation Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaisford_Prize#Winners_of_the_Gaisford_Dissertation_Prize) in classical literature for his essay *Tragedy and Subjectivity in Virgil’s 'Aeneid'*.[^o8] He was awarded the DPhil in 1988 for a thesis supervised by [Michael Dummett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dummett), [David Wiggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wiggins), and [Barry Stroud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Stroud), entitled *Experience, Agency, and the Self*.[^o9] From 1988 to 1989 Gaskin spent a year as an Alexander von Humboldt visiting fellow at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz), Germany, researching decision-making in classical literature under the Virgilian scholar Antonie Wlosok.[^o10] From 1991 to 2001, he was a Lecturer (from 1997 Reader) in Philosophy at the University of Sussex.[^o11] In 2001 he became Professor in the Department of Philosophy at the University of Liverpool.[^o12]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

A central part of Gaskin's work focuses on the doctrine of [linguistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic) [idealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealism), the idea that the world is produced by, and depends on, [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language). Gaskin argues that the dependence of the world on language is a logical and constitutive one, rather than a temporal one: objects (such as tables and chairs) exist in virtue of, and are constituted as objects by, the existence of sentences about them; language 'makes the world', but not in the sense that there was a time at which it pre-existed the world. Although human language is a purely contingent product of evolution, there is a transcendental sense in which the existence of the world depends on the existence of language—more precisely, on the capacity of language to talk about the world. In Gaskin's view the world is constitutively composed of propositions, which are referents of sentences; these propositions contain the ordinary objects of our discourse. In *Language and World* (2020), Gaskin develops the theory of linguistic idealism and defends it against several objections. He addresses the problem that some mathematical entities, in particular uncomputable sets of real numbers, cannot be distinguished by language; he does this by developing a ‘split-level' version of linguistic idealism. In his approach all the basic entities of the world can be named in language, and all further entities, even if they cannot be named, can be derived from these basic entities by describable constructive operations. In *Tragedy and Redress in Western Literature: A Philosophical Perspective* (2018), Gaskin argues that not even the tragic aspects of life (such as pain and suffering) are beyond language, an objection commonly raised against the idea that language is omnicompetent to talk about and describe reality.

In his writings on literature, Gaskin has defended a version of literary [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism), according to which works of imaginative literature have an [objective meaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objectivity_%28philosophy%29) which is fixed at the time of their production and is the same for all readers.[^o14] In addition to his publications in philosophy of literature, he has written a study of the [poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poets) [Horace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace) and [Housman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housman),[^o15] essays on [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil) (e.g. *On being pessimistic about the end of the 'Aeneid**[^o16]), [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer) (e.g.*****Do Homeric Heroes make real decisions?*[^o17]), the classicist [Richard Bentley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bentley),[^o18] and the essayist [Charles Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lamb).[^o19] Gaskin has translated selections from Apollonius of Rhodes's Greek poem*Argonautica*into English verse.[^o20] Gaskin has written on [ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_philosophy) and on [medieval philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_philosophy),[^o21][^o22] and on Wittgenstein.[^o23] He maintains a website on which he mounts recordings of [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry).[^o24]***
