# Ubiratan D'Ambrosio

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ubiratan D'Ambrosio"}

**Ubiratan D'Ambrosio** (December 8, 1932 – May 12, 2021) was a Brazilian mathematics educator and historian of mathematics. He is most well known for being the originator of the term [Ethnomathematics](wikipedia://en/324872), and his efforts to promote mathematics education in his home country.

:::

## Life {#Life}

D'Ambrosio was born in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Paulo), and earned his doctorate from the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo) in 1963. In 1964, he took a post-doctoral position at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), intending to stay for one year, but was forced to stay in the United States due to the [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Brazilian_coup_d'état) in Brazil.[^o5] He later worked at [SUNY Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY_Buffalo), where he studied [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [measure theory](wikipedia://en/79849108),[^o4] before returning to Brazil in 1972 where he took a position directing the department of mathematics, statistics and scientific computing at the [State University of Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Campinas) in São Paulo.[^o5] He also served as a visiting professor at the graduate program at the Center Pédagogique Supérieur in [Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako), Mali.[^o9] During this time he was elected president of the Inter-American Committee on Mathematics Education.[^o5] He retired as a professor of mathematics from the [State University of Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Campinas), São Paulo, Brazil in 1993, but continued researching at the [Pontifical Catholic University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_São_Paulo).[^o5]

He was a member of many societies, including [Pugwash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pugwash_Conferences_on_Science_and_World_Affairs), and served the [International Commission on the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/35011217) (ICHM) for five years.

D'Ambrosio was also the founder of the Brazilian Society for Mathematics and History of the International Group of Ethnomathematicians.

In 2001, he and [Lam Lay Yong](wikipedia://en/26926721) were jointly awarded the [Kenneth O. May Prize](wikipedia://en/25758309).[^o6][^o7]

## Ethnomathematics {#Ethnomathematics}

D'Ambriosio is most well known for popularizing the field of study of Ethnomathematics. While Ethnomathematics has been defined many ways by different people, D'Ambrosio defined it as a study of mathematics related to members of distinct cultural groups groups,including indigenous people, workers and even children.[^o9] He first coined the term in 1977 during a presentation at The Annual Meeting of the American Association for the Advancement of Science in Denver, Colorado.[^o9] Some of D'Ambrosio's early studies of ethnomathematics included research on the mathematics done by residents of favelas in [Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campinas), in the Brazilian state of São Paolo.[^o10] These studies were led by students of [Paulo Freire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Freire), who incorporated Freire's educational ideas.[^o10] These projects eventually developed into a master's program in mathematics education, which incorporated both community engagement in teaching and formal study of the mathematics involved.[^o10] He wrote about many different forms of ethnomathematics, including a discussion of [Australopithecus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australopithecus)' spatial reasoning, although he primarily focused on methods of mathematics education.[^o9] He continued presenting and writing about the subject in subsequent years, and his work included both mathematical history, research in mathematics education and ethnomathematical studies. He eventually becoming a founding member of the International Study Group on Ethnomathematics in 1985, and served as its president from 1996 until 2000.[^o9] The group published biannual newsletters between 1985 and 1998, before resuming in 2020.[^o11]
