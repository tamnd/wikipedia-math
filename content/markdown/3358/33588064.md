# Jun-Ichi Igusa

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jun-Ichi Igusa"}

**Jun-ichi Igusa** was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who for over three decades was on the faculty at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). He is known for his contributions to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). The [Igusa zeta-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igusa_zeta-function), the [Igusa quartic](wikipedia://en/23421089), [Igusa subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igusa_subgroup), [Igusa curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igusa_curve), and [Igusa varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igusa_varieties) are named after him.[^o10]

:::

He was an invited speaker for the 1962 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm).[^o3] He was awarded Japan's [Order of the Sacred Treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Sacred_Treasure).[^o4] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Igusa was born in Kiyosato village, [Gunma Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunma_Prefecture), Japan, on 30 January 1924.[^o7] He graduated from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1945 and received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) in 1953, after which he became professor of mathematics at the [University of Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tsukuba). After a brief period spent at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), he took up a permanent position at Johns Hopkins University, in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore). Igusa taught at Johns Hopkins from 1955 to 1993.[^o8] He joined the staff of the *[American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics)* as an associate editor in 1964, and served as chief editor between 1978 and 1993.[^o9] Igusa died, aged 89, of a stroke at Holly Hill Nursing Home in [Towson, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towson,_Maryland), on 24 November 2013.[^o8]

He had three sons, Kiyoshi, Takeru and Mitsuru.[^o10] Takeru Igusa is a professor of civil engineering at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). [Kiyoshi Igusa](wikipedia://en/62079633) is a professor of mathematics at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University).
