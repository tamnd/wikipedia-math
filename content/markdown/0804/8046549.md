# Hamming graph

*Image: Hamming 3-3 unit distance.svg.* $H(3,3)$ drawn as a [unit distance graph](wikipedia://en/7158665)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Hamming graph"}

**Hamming graphs** are a special class of [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) named after [Richard Hamming](wikipedia://en/99326) and used in several branches of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) ([graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401)) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). Let $S$ be a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of $q$ elements and $d$ a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). The Hamming graph $H(d,q)$ has [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) set $S^{d}$, the set of ordered $d$-[tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) of elements of $S$, or sequences of length $d$ from $S$. Two vertices are [adjacent](wikipedia://en/325806) if they differ in precisely one coordinate; that is, if their [Hamming distance](wikipedia://en/41227) is one. The Hamming graph $H(d,q)$ is, equivalently, the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of $d$ [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{q}$.[^o4]

:::

In some cases, Hamming graphs may be considered more generally as the Cartesian products of complete graphs that may be of varying sizes.[^o5] Unlike the Hamming graphs $H(d,q)$, the graphs in this more general class are not necessarily [distance-regular](wikipedia://en/7768943), but they continue to be [regular](wikipedia://en/85821) and [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175).

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

- $H(2,3)$, which is the generalized [quadrangle](wikipedia://en/2605272) $G Q (2,1)$[^o8]
- $H(1,q)$, which is the complete graph $K_{q}$[^o9]
- $H(2,q)$, which is the [lattice graph](wikipedia://en/19635706) $L_{q,q}$ and also the [rook's graph](wikipedia://en/7324959)[^o10]
- $H(d,1)$, which is the singleton graph $K_{1}$
- $H(d,2)$, which is the [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815) $Q_{d}$.[^o4] [Hamiltonian paths](wikipedia://en/244437) in these graphs form [Gray codes](wikipedia://en/50564).
- Because [Cartesian products of graphs](wikipedia://en/2856852) preserve the property of being a [unit distance graph](wikipedia://en/7158665),[^o11] the Hamming graphs $H(d,2)$ and $H(d,3)$ are all unit distance graphs.

:::

::: {#o13 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Hamming graphs are interesting in connection with [error-correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error-correcting_codes)[^o14] and [association schemes](wikipedia://en/2238152),[^o15] to name two areas. They have also been considered as a communications network topology in [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing).[^o9]

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

It is possible in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) to test whether a graph is a Hamming graph, and in the case that it is, find a labeling of it with tuples that realizes it as a Hamming graph.[^o5]
