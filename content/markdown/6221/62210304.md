# Johannes Buchmann

*Image: JohannesBuchmann2016.jpg.* Johannes Buchmann in 2016

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johannes Buchmann"}

**Johannes Alfred Buchmann** (born November 20, 1953, in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne))[^o2] is a German [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at the [department of computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Computer_Science_of_TU_Darmstadt) of the [Technische Universität Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt).

:::

He is known for his research in [algorithmic number theory](wikipedia://en/511466), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [post-quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/26605226) and [IT security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_security). In 1993, he received the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize) together with [Claus Peter Schnorr](wikipedia://en/9439931) for his work in algorithmic number theory and cryptography. Buchmann also developed the stateful hash-based signature scheme XMSS, the first future-proof secure and practical signature scheme with minimal security requirements, which was declared the first international standard for post-quantum [signature schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_scheme) in 2018. In addition, he further developed IT security research in Germany. His efforts led to the creation of [ATHENE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athene_%28research_center%29), the largest research center for IT security in Europe. For this he received the [Konrad Zuse Medal for Services to Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Zuse_Medal) of the [Gesellschaft für Informatik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesellschaft_für_Informatik) (GI) in 2017.

## Life {#Life}

Johannes Buchmann studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [pedagogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy) and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) from 1974 to 1979 after graduating from high school in 1972 and completing his [military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_service). After passing the first [state examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsexamen) for teaching at [grammar schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) in 1979, he taught mathematics at a Cologne secondary school from 1980 to 1983 while at the same time working as a research assistant at the university. In 1982 he did his doctorate at the university under the supervision of Hans-Joachim Stender. In 1984 he passed the second state examination. In 1985/86 he was with [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825) at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) on a [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) from the [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation). From 1986 to 1988 he was research assistant of Michael Pohst at the [University of Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Heine_University_Düsseldorf), where he [habilitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1988. Then he worked as professor of computer science at [Saarland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_University). In 1996, he then was professor of computer science and mathematics at the [Technische Universität Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt). He retired in 2019.[^o2]

From 2001 to 2007, he was vice president for Research at the [Technische Universität Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt). Since 2004, he has been chairman of the Board of the Competence Center for Applied Security (CAST), the largest network for [cyber security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_security) in [German-speaking countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_territorial_entities_where_German_is_an_official_language). From 2011 to 2013, Buchmann headed the project *Internet Privacy - A Culture of Privacy and Trust on the Internet* of the [German Academy of Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Science_and_Engineering). He was founding director of the [Center for Advanced Security Research Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Advanced_Security_Research_Darmstadt) (CASED) and held this position from 2008 to 2011. From 2011 to 2016, he was a member of the board of directors of the [European Center for Security and Privacy by Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Center_for_Security_and_Privacy_by_Design) (EC SPRIDE). From 2016 to 2018, Buchmann was Vice Director of the [Center for Research in Security and Privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Research_in_Security_and_Privacy) (CRISP), the largest research institute for IT security in Europe. From 2014 to 2019, he was spokesman of the [Collaborative Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaborative_Research_Centres) CROSSING and from 2015 to 2019 spokesman of the profile area CYSEC of TU Darmstadt. He gave his farewell lecture on October 24, 2019.[^o4]

From 2017 to 2018, Johannes Buchmann trained as an MBSR ([Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindfulness-based_stress_reduction)) teacher at the Institute for Mindfulness. Since then he has been working as a certified MBSR teacher.[^o5]

Buchmann is married and has two sons.

## Work {#Work}

Buchmann's achievements include scientific essays on algorithms in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), the construction of new cryptographic methods and the use of cryptographic methods in practice. Due to his collaboration with Kálmán Győry he has the [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) 2.[^o7] Buchmann dealt with algorithms in algebraic number theory and their application in [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432). In 1988, he proposed with [Hugh C. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_C._Williams) a cryptographic system based on the [discrete logarithmic problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_Logarithm_Problem) in the ideal class group of imaginary-square number fields (which, according to [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), is related to the theory of binary-square forms), which triggered further developments in cryptography with number fields.[^o8]

Since 1996, Buchmann has been working intensively on the topic of [public key infrastructures](wikipedia://en/194112), for which he published the book *Introduction to Public Key Infrastructures* together with Evangelos Karatsiolis and Alexander Wiesmaier in 2013.[^o9] Buchmann has been working on [post-quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/26605226) since 2003 and published a book of the same name together with [Daniel J. Bernstein](wikipedia://en/198983) and Erik Dahmen in 2009.[^o10] Buchmann is also the author of the textbook *Introduction to Cryptography*, which has been translated into seven languages.[^o11]

In Saarbrücken, the research work of Buchmann concentrated on the theoretical cryptography and cryptanalysis of number theory-based public-key methods like RSA. During his time in Saarbrücken, Buchmann also founded the [German Research Foundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft) first [graduate school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_school) for computer science and was able to establish research in cryptography and IT security as an integral part of the university.[^o12]

In 2018, the stateful hash-based signature scheme [XMSS](wikipedia://en/53957696) developed by a team of researchers under the direction of Buchmann became the first international standard for post-quantum [signature schemes](wikipedia://en/59644). XMSS is the first future-proof secure and practical signature scheme with minimal security requirements. The work began in 2003.[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16]
