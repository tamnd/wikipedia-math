# Simplicial space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial space"}

In mathematics, a **simplicial space** is a [simplicial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_object) in the category of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450). In other words, it is a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from the [simplex category](wikipedia://en/1028841) Δ to the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051).[^o2]

:::

A **Segal category**[^o3] is a kind of simplicial space, this is a model of an [infinity category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_category) introduced by (Hirschowitz Simpson 1998), based on work of [Graeme Segal](wikipedia://en/6713316) in 1974.

A **Segal space** is a simplicial space satisfying some [pullback](wikipedia://en/9413508) conditions, making it look like a [homotopical](wikipedia://en/212250) version of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759). More precisely, a [simplicial set](wikipedia://en/1027784), considered as a simplicial discrete space, satisfies the [Segal conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segal_condition) if and only if it is the [nerve](wikipedia://en/5127933) of a category. The condition for Segal spaces is a homotopical version of this. Complete Segal spaces were introduced by (Rezk 2001) as models for [(∞, 1)-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28infinity,1%29-category).
