# Levene's test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Levene's test"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Levene's test** is an inferential statistic used to assess the equality of [variances](wikipedia://en/32344) for a variable calculated for two or more groups.[^o2] This test is used because some common statistical procedures assume that variances of the populations from which different samples are drawn are equal. Levene's test assesses this assumption. It tests the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) that the population variances are equal (called *homogeneity of variance* or *[homoscedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoscedasticity)*). If the resulting [*p*-value](wikipedia://en/554994) of Levene's test is less than some significance level (typically 0.05), the obtained differences in sample variances are unlikely to have occurred based on random sampling from a population with equal variances. Thus, the null hypothesis of equal variances is rejected and it is concluded that there is a difference between the variances in the population.

:::

Levene's test has been used in the past before a comparison of means to inform the decision on whether to use a pooled t-test or the [Welch's t-test](wikipedia://en/7849883) for two sample tests or [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634) or Welch's modified oneway ANOVA for multi-level tests. However, it was shown that such a two-step procedure may markedly inflate the [type 1 error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_error) obtained with the t-tests and thus is not recommended.[^o3] Instead, the preferred approach is to just use Welch's test in all cases.[^o4]

Levene's test may also be used as a main test for answering a stand-alone question of whether two sub-samples in a given population have equal or different variances.[^o5]

Levene's test was developed by and named after American statistician and geneticist [Howard Levene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Levene).

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Levene's test is equivalent to a 1-way between-groups analysis of variance (ANOVA) with the [dependent variable](wikipedia://en/437701) being the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of the difference between a score and the mean of the group to which the score belongs (shown below as $Z_{ij} = |Y_{ij} - \bar{Y}_{i\cdot}|$ ). The [test statistic](wikipedia://en/1416975), $W$, is equivalent to the $F$ statistic that would be produced by such an ANOVA, and is defined as follows:

$$W = \frac{(N-k)}{(k-1)} \cdot \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k N_i (Z_{i\cdot}-Z_{\cdot\cdot})^2} {\sum_{i=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} (Z_{ij}-Z_{i\cdot})^2},$$

where

- $k$ is the number of different groups to which the sampled cases belong,
- $N_i$ is the number of cases in the $i$th group,
- $N$ is the total number of cases in all groups,
- $Y_{ij}$ is the value of the measured variable for the$j$th case from the $i$th group,
- $$Z_{ij} = \begin{cases} |Y_{ij} - \bar{Y}_{i\cdot}|, & \bar{Y}_{i\cdot} \text{ is a mean of the } i\text{-th group}, \\ |Y_{ij} - \tilde{Y}_{i\cdot}|, & \tilde{Y}_{i\cdot} \text{ is a median of the } i\text{-th group}. \end{cases}$$

(Both definitions are in use though the second one is, strictly speaking, the [Brown–Forsythe test](wikipedia://en/15317159) – see below for comparison.)

- $Z_{i\cdot} = \frac{1}{N_i} \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} Z_{ij}$ is the mean of the $Z_{ij}$ for group $i$,
- $Z_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} Z_{ij}$ is the mean of all $Z_{ij}$.

The test statistic $W$ is approximately [F-distributed](wikipedia://en/332116) with $k-1$ and $N-k$ degrees of freedom, and hence is the significance of the outcome $w$ of $W$ tested against $F(1-\alpha;k-1,N-k)$ where $F$ is a quantile of the F-distribution, with $k-1$ and $N-k$ degrees of freedom, and $\alpha$ is the chosen level of significance (usually 0.05 or 0.01).

:::

## Comparison with the Brown–Forsythe test {#Comparison_with_the_Brown–Forsythe_test}

The [Brown–Forsythe test](wikipedia://en/15317159) uses the median instead of the mean in computing the spread within each group ($\bar{Y}$ vs. $\tilde{Y}$, above). Although the optimal choice depends on the underlying distribution, the definition based on the median is recommended as the choice that provides good [robustness](wikipedia://en/2885691) against many types of non-normal data while retaining good [statistical power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power).[^o5] If one has knowledge of the underlying distribution of the data, this may indicate using one of the other choices. Brown and Forsythe performed [Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/56098) studies that indicated that using the [trimmed mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimmed_mean) performed best when the underlying data followed a [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003) (a [heavy-tailed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-tailed) distribution) and the median performed best when the underlying data followed a [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424) with four degrees of freedom (a heavily [skewed distribution](wikipedia://en/28212)). Using the mean provided the best power for symmetric, moderate-tailed, distributions.

## Software implementations {#Software_implementations}

Many [spreadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spreadsheet) programs and statistics packages, such as [R](wikipedia://en/376707), [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29), [Stata](wikipedia://en/1809002), [Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_%28programming_language%29), and [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) include implementations of Levene's test.

::: {#o10 .table cue=table}

| Language/Program | Function | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) | `scipy.stats.levene(group1, group2, group3)` | See <https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.levene.html> |
| [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) | `vartestn(data,groups,'TestType','LeveneAbsolute')` | See <https://www.mathworks.com/help/stats/vartestn.html> |
| [R](wikipedia://en/376707) | `leveneTest(lm(y ~ x, data=data))` | See <https://rdocumentation.org/packages/car/versions/3.1-2/topics/leveneTest> |
| [Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_%28programming_language%29) | `HypothesisTests.LeveneTest(group1, group2, group3)` | See <https://juliastats.org/HypothesisTests.jl/stable/parametric/#Levene's-Test> |
| [Stata](wikipedia://en/1809002) | `robvar` `varname, by(groupvar)` | Output includes both the Levene's and Brown and Forsythe statistics. See <https://www.stata.com/users/mcleves/robvar/robvar.hlp> |

:::
