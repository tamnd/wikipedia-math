# Cartan subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cartan subgroup"}

In the theory of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924), a **Cartan subgroup** of a connected [linear algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) $G$ over a (not necessarily algebraically closed) field $k$ is the centralizer of a maximal torus. Cartan subgroups are smooth (equivalently reduced), connected and nilpotent. If $k$ is algebraically closed, they are all conjugate to each other. [^n1]

:::

Notice that in the context of algebraic groups a *torus* is an algebraic group $T$ such that the base extension $T_{(\bar{k})}$ (where $\bar{k}$ is the algebraic closure of $k$) is isomorphic to the product of a finite number of copies of the $\mathbf{G}_m=\mathbf{GL}_1$. Maximal such subgroups have in the theory of algebraic groups a role that is similar to that of [maximal tori](wikipedia://en/360136) in the theory of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).

If $G$ is [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386) (in particular, if it is semi-simple), then a torus is maximal if and only if it is its own centraliser [^n2] and thus Cartan subgroups of $G$ are precisely the maximal tori.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The general linear groups $\mathbf{GL}_n$ are reductive. The diagonal subgroup is clearly a torus (indeed a *split* torus, since it is product of n copies of $\mathbf{G}_m$ already before any base extension), and it can be shown to be maximal. Since $\mathbf{GL}_n$ is reductive, the diagonal subgroup is a Cartan subgroup.

:::
