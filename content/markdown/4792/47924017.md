# Lloyd Dines

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lloyd Dines"}

**Lloyd Lyne Dines** (29 March 1885, in [Shelbyville, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelbyville,_Missouri) – 17 January 1964, in [Quincy, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quincy,_Illinois)) was an American-Canadian mathematician, known for his pioneering work on linear inequalities.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Dines received B.A. in 1906 and M.A. in 1907 from [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University)[^o4] and Ph.D. in 1911 from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) under [Gilbert Bliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Bliss) with thesis *The highest common factor of a system of polynomials in one variable, with an application to implicit functions*. In 1911 he became an instructor of mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and then became an associate professor at the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona). From 1915 to 1934 he was a professor at the [University of Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saskatchewan). In 1928 he was elected to the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada). In 1932 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich). From 1934 to 1945 he was a professor at the [Carnegie Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institute_of_Technology) and chair of the mathematics department. After his retirement in 1945, Dines held visiting professorships at the University of Saskatchewan, [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College), and Northwestern University.[^o2]
