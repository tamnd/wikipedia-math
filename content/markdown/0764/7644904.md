# Paolo Gorini

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paolo Gorini"}

**Paolo Gorini** (18 January 1813 – 2 February 1881) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor), [scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist), and [politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician)[^o3] renowned as a pioneer of [cremation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation) in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe), primarily in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia), Gorini obtained a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia) in 1832 and subsequently moved to [Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodi,_Lombardy) in 1834, where he worked as [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) of [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) in the local lyceum.[^o3] There he achieved noteworthy discoveries about [organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_compound) substances.[^o3] Gorini became interested in [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics) at a very young age and joined the emerging [Italian nationalist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risorgimento) during the [First Italian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Italian_War_of_Independence).[^o3]

After the [Five Days of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Days_of_Milan) (1848), he fled to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) and was further exiled to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), where he continued his eclectic studies in [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences) ([anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology), and [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology)).[^o3] Back in Lodi, in 1871, he published *Sull'origine del vulcani* ("On the Origin of Volcanoes") and, in 1872, he became famous for the [embalming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embalming) of the bodies of [Giuseppe Mazzini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Mazzini) and [Giuseppe Rovani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Rovani).[^o3][^o5]

In 1878, he was commissioned by the [Cremation Society of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation_Society_of_Great_Britain) to construct the cremator at [Woking Crematorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woking_Crematorium) in England. He died in Lodi in 1881; following a [civil ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_funeral), his remains were [cremated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation) and buried in the local cemetery of Riolo.[^o3] A monument and a museum are dedicated to him in Lodi.[^o6] In 2021, the [documentary film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film) *Il mago di Lodi* ("The wizard of Lodi") dedicated to his life and works as [embalmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embalmer) was directed by Silvia Onegli and produced by Magestic Film.
