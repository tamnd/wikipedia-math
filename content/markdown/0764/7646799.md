# Bessel process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bessel process"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Bessel process**, named after [Friedrich Bessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Bessel). The *n*-dimensional Bessel process is the solution to the [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454) (SDE)

:::

$$dX_t = dW_t + \frac{n-1}{2}\frac{dt}{X_t}$$

where W is a 1-dimensional [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) ([Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436))

*Image: BesselProcess1D.svg.* Three realizations of Bessel Processes.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

The Bessel process of order *n* is the [real-valued](wikipedia://en/20646438) process *X* given (when *n* ≥ 2) by

$$X_t = \| W_t \|,$$

where ||·|| denotes the [Euclidean norm](wikipedia://en/990534#Euclidean_norm) in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and *W* is an *n*-dimensional [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) ([Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436)). Note that this SDE makes sense for any real parameter $n$ (although the drift term is singular at zero).

:::

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

A notation for the Bessel process of dimension $n$ started at zero is $\operatorname{BES}_{0}(n)$.

:::

## In specific dimensions {#In_specific_dimensions}

For *n* ≥ 2, the *n*-dimensional Wiener process started at the origin is [transient](wikipedia://en/60876#Properties) from its starting point: [with probability one](wikipedia://en/351908), i.e., *X*<sub>*t*</sub> > 0 for all *t* > 0. It is, however, neighbourhood-recurrent for *n* = 2, meaning that with probability 1, for any *r* > 0, there are arbitrarily large *t* with *X*<sub>*t*</sub> \< *r*; on the other hand, it is truly transient for *n* > 2, meaning that *X*<sub>*t*</sub> ≥ *r* for all *t* sufficiently large.

For *n* ≤ 0, the Bessel process is usually started at points other than 0, since the drift to 0 is so strong that the process becomes stuck at 0 as soon as it hits 0.

### Relationship with Brownian motion {#Relationship_with_Brownian_motion}

0- and 2-dimensional Bessel processes are related to local times of Brownian motion via the [Ray–Knight theorems](wikipedia://en/5678101#Ray–Knight_theorems).[^o8]

The law of a Brownian motion near x-extrema is the law of a 3-dimensional Bessel process (theorem of Tanaka).
