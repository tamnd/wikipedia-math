# Degasperis–Procesi equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Degasperis–Procesi equation"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the **Degasperis–Procesi equation**

:::

$$\displaystyle u_t - u_{xxt} + 2\kappa u_x + 4u u_x = 3 u_x u_{xx} + u u_{xxx}$$

is one of only two [exactly solvable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exactly_solvable_model) equations in the following family of third-[order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_%28differential_equation%29), non-linear, [dispersive PDEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersive_PDE):

$$\displaystyle u_t - u_{xxt} + 2\kappa u_x + (b+1)u u_x = b u_x u_{xx} + u u_{xxx},$$

where $\kappa$ and *b* are real parameters (*b*=3 for the Degasperis–Procesi equation). It was discovered by Antonio Degasperis and [Michela Procesi](wikipedia://en/77615848) in a search for [integrable equations](wikipedia://en/2573213) similar in form to the [Camassa–Holm equation](wikipedia://en/16871767), which is the other integrable equation in this family (corresponding to *b*=2); that those two equations are the only integrable cases has been verified using a variety of different integrability tests. Although discovered solely because of its mathematical properties, the Degasperis–Procesi equation (with $\kappa > 0$) has later been found to play a similar role in [water wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_wave) theory as the Camassa–Holm equation.

## Soliton solutions {#Soliton_solutions}

Among the solutions of the Degasperis–Procesi equation (in the special case $\kappa=0$) are the so-called [multipeakon](wikipedia://en/16866923) solutions, which are functions of the form

$$\displaystyle u(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^n m_i(t) e^{-|x-x_i(t)|}$$

where the functions $m_i$ and $x_i$ satisfy[^n1]

$$\dot{x}_i = \sum_{j=1}^n m_j e^{-|x_i-x_j|},\qquad \dot{m}_i = 2 m_i \sum_{j=1}^n m_j\, \operatorname{sgn}{(x_i-x_j)} e^{-|x_i-x_j|}.$$

These [ODEs](wikipedia://en/32742753) can be solved explicitly in terms of elementary functions, using [inverse spectral methods](wikipedia://en/2573213#Solitons_and_inverse_spectral_methods).

When $\kappa > 0$ the [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) solutions of the Degasperis–Procesi equation are smooth; they converge to peakons in the limit as $\kappa$ tends to zero.

## Discontinuous solutions {#Discontinuous_solutions}

The Degasperis–Procesi equation (with $\kappa=0$) is formally equivalent to the (nonlocal) [hyperbolic conservation law](wikipedia://en/1575825#Hyperbolic_system_and_conservation_laws)

$$\partial_t u + \partial_x \left[\frac{u^2}{2} + \frac{G}{2} * \frac{3 u^2}{2} \right] = 0,$$

where $G(x) = \exp(-|x|)$, and where the star denotes [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) with respect to *x*. In this formulation, it admits [weak solutions](wikipedia://en/3555416) with a very low degree of regularity, even discontinuous ones ([shock waves](wikipedia://en/146253)). In contrast, the corresponding formulation of the Camassa–Holm equation contains a convolution involving both $u^2$ and $u_x^2$, which only makes sense if *u* lies in the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $H^1 = W^{1,2}$ with respect to *x*. By the [Sobolev embedding theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobolev_embedding_theorem), this means in particular that the weak solutions of the Camassa–Holm equation must be continuous with respect to *x*.
