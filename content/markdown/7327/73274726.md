# Wu–Yang dictionary

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wu–Yang dictionary"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [high energy physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_energy_physics), the **Wu–Yang dictionary** refers to the mathematical identification that allows back-and-forth translation between the concepts of [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) and those of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). The dictionary appeared in 1975 in an article by [Tai Tsun Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Tsun_Wu) and [C. N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Ning_Yang) comparing [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism) and [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) theory.[^o2] This dictionary has been credited as bringing mathematics and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) closer together.[^o3]

:::

A crucial example of the success of the dictionary is that it allowed the understanding of [monopole quantization](wikipedia://en/175470#Dirac's_quantization) in terms of [Hopf fibrations](wikipedia://en/580384).[^o4][^o5]

## History {#History}

Equivalences between fiber bundle theory and gauge theory were hinted at the end of the 1960s. In 1967, mathematician [Andrzej Trautman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Trautman) started a series of lectures aimed at physicists and mathematicians at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) regarding these connections.[^o5]

Theoretical physicists [Tai Tsun Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Tsun_Wu) and [C. N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Ning_Yang) working in [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University), published a paper in 1975 on the mathematical framework of [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism) and the [Aharonov–Bohm effect](wikipedia://en/277664) in terms of [fiber bundles.](wikipedia://en/276526) A year later, mathematician [Isadore Singer](wikipedia://en/324564) came to visit and brought a copy back to the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o3][^o7][^o8] Singer showed the paper to [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and other mathematicians, sparking a close collaboration between physicists and mathematicians.[^o3]

Yang also recounts a conversation that he had with one of the mathematicians that founded fiber bundle theory, [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905):[^o3]

In 1977, Trautman used these results to demonstrate an equivalence between a quantization condition for [magnetic monopoles](wikipedia://en/175470) used by [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) back in 1931 and [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384), a fibration of a 3-sphere proposed in the same year by mathematician [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082).[^o5] Mathematician [Jim Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844) discussing this equivalence with Yang expressed that “Dirac had discovered trivial and nontrivial bundles before mathematicians.”[^o5]

In the original paper, Wu and Yang added sources (like the [electric current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current)) to the dictionary next to a blank spot, indicating a lack of any equivalent concept on the mathematical side. During interviews, Yang recalls that Singer and Atiyah found great interest in this concept of sources, which was unknown for mathematicians but that physicists knew since the 19th century. Mathematicians started working on that, which lead to the development of [Donaldson theory](wikipedia://en/7684340) by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305), a student of Atiyah.[^o9][^o10]

## Description {#Description}

### Summarized version {#Summarized_version}

The Wu-Yang dictionary relates terms in [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) with terms in mathematics, specifically fiber bundle theory. Many versions and generalization of the dictionary exist. Here is an example of a dictionary, which puts each physics term next to its mathematical analogue:[^o13]

::: {#o14 .table cue=table}

| Physics | Mathematics |
|---|---|
| Potential | [Connection](wikipedia://en/470632) |
| Field tensor (interaction) | [Curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) |
| Field tensor-potential relation | Structural equation |
| Gauge transformation | Change of bundle coordinates |
| [Gauge group](wikipedia://en/39240530) | Structure group |

:::

### Original version for electromagnetism {#Original_version_for_electromagnetism}

*Image: AharonovBohmEffect.svg.* Aharonov-Bohm experiment: electrons pass around a cylinder where there is a non-zero magnetic field. Outside the cylinder the field strength is 0.

Wu and Yang considered the description of an electron traveling around a cylinder in the presence of a magnetic field inside the cylinder (outside the cylinder the field vanishes i.e. $f_{\mu\nu}=0$). According to the [Aharonov–Bohm effect](wikipedia://en/277664), the interference patterns shift by a factor $\exp(-i \Omega/\Omega_0)$, where $\Omega$ is the magnetic flux and $\Omega_0$ is the [magnetic flux quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux_quantum). For two different fluxes *a* and *b*, the results are identical if $\Omega_a-\Omega_b=N\Omega_0$, where $N$ is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). We define the operator $S_{ab}$ as the gauge transformation that brings the electron [wave function](wikipedia://en/145343) from one configuration to the other $\psi_b=S_{ba}\psi_a$. For an electron that takes a path from point *P* to point *Q*, we define the [phase factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_factor) as

$$\Phi_{QP} =\exp \left(-\frac{i}{\Omega_0} \int_P^Q A_\mu \mathrm{d} x^{\mu}\right),$$

where $A_\mu$ is the [electromagnetic four-potential](wikipedia://en/731884). For the case of a [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29) gauge field, we can make the substitution

$$A_\mu=ib_\mu^kX_k,$$

where $X_k=-i\sigma_k/2$ are the generators of SU(2), $\sigma_k$ are the [Pauli matrices](wikipedia://en/24868). Under these concepts, Wu and Yang showed the relation between the language of gauge theory and [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526), was codified in following dictionary:[^o3][^o17][^o18]

*Wu–Yang dictionary (1975)[^o2]*

::: {#o19 .table cue=table name="Wu–Yang dictionary (1975)"}

| Gauge field terminology | Bundle terminology |
|---|---|
| gauge (or global gauge) | principal coordinate bundle |
| gauge type | [principal fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) |
| gauge potential $b_\mu^k$ | [connection on principal fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/470632) |
| $S_{ba}$ (see above in this section) | [transition function](wikipedia://en/7762521) |
| phase factor $\Phi_{QP}$ | [parallel displacement](wikipedia://en/285595) |
| [field strength](wikipedia://en/1912367) $f_{\mu\nu}^k$ | [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) |
| [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-current) $J^k_\mu$ | ? |
| electromagnetism | connection on a [U(1) bundle](wikipedia://en/80364893) |
| [isotopic spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotopic_spin) gauge field | connection on a [SU(2) bundle](wikipedia://en/80364907) |
| [Dirac's monopole quantization](wikipedia://en/175470#Dirac's_quantization) | classification of U(1) bundles according to first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) |
| electromagnetism without monopole | connection on a trivial U(1) bundle |
| electromagnetism with monopole | connection on a nontrivial U(1) bundle |
