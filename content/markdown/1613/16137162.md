# Ackermann ordinal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ackermann ordinal"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the **Ackermann ordinal** is a certain [large countable ordinal](wikipedia://en/4376814), named after [Wilhelm Ackermann](wikipedia://en/62727). The term "Ackermann ordinal" is also occasionally used for the [small Veblen ordinal](wikipedia://en/16110499), a somewhat larger ordinal.

:::

There is no standard notation for ordinals beyond the [Feferman–Schütte ordinal](wikipedia://en/16084455) $\Gamma_0$. Most systems of notation use symbols such as $\psi(\alpha)$, $\theta(\alpha)$, or $\psi_\alpha(\beta)$, some of which are modifications of the [Veblen functions](wikipedia://en/16124925) to produce countable ordinals even for uncountable arguments, and some of which are [ordinal collapsing functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_collapsing_functions). The last one is an extension of the Veblen functions for more than two arguments.

The smaller Ackermann ordinal is the limit of a system of ordinal notations invented by Ackermann,[^n1] and is sometimes denoted by $\theta(\Omega^2)$, $\psi(\Omega^{\Omega^2})$, or $\varphi(1,0,0,0)$, where $\Omega$ is the [smallest uncountable ordinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallest_uncountable_ordinal). Ackermann's system of notation is weaker than the system introduced much earlier by [Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen),[^n2] which he seems to have been unaware of.
