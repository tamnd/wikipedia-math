# Conchoid of Dürer

*Image: DuererMuschellinie.png.* Conchoid of Dürer, constructed by himself

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conchoid of Dürer"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **conchoid of Dürer**, also called **Dürer's shell curve**, is a [plane](wikipedia://en/971691), [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260), named after [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_Dürer) and introduced in 1525. It is not a true [conchoid](wikipedia://en/898856).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Dconchoid2.svg.* Construction of Dürer's conchoid

Suppose two perpendicular lines are given, with intersection point *O*. For concreteness we may assume that these are the coordinate axes and that *O* is the origin, that is (0, 0). Let points $Q = (q, 0)$ and $R = (0, r)$ move on the axes in such a way that $q + r = b$, a constant. On the line $QR$, extended as necessary, mark points $P$ and $P'$ at a fixed distance $a$ from $Q$. The locus of the points $P$ and $P'$ is Dürer's conchoid.[^o4]

:::

## Equation {#Equation}

The equation of the conchoid in Cartesian form is

$$2y^2(x^2+y^2) - 2by^2(x+y) + (b^2-3a^2)y^2 - a^2x^2 + 2a^2b(x+y) + a^2(a^2-b^2) = 0 .$$

In parametric form the equation is given by

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \frac{b \cos(t)}{\cos(t) - \sin(t)} + a \cos(t),\\ y &= a \sin(t), \end{aligned}$$

where the parameter $t$ is measured in [radians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radians).[^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The curve has two components, asymptotic to the lines $y = \pm a / \sqrt2$.[^o9] Each component is a [rational curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_curve). If *a* > *b* there is a loop, if *a* = *b* there is a cusp at (0,*a*).

Special cases include:

- *a* = 0: the line *y* = 0;
- *b* = 0: the line pair $y = \pm a / \sqrt2$ together with the circle $x^2+y^2=a^2$;

The envelope of straight lines used in the construction form a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) (as seen in Durer's original diagram above) and therefore the curve is a point-[glissette](wikipedia://en/53708787) formed by a line and one of its points sliding respectively against a parabola and one of its tangents.[^o10]

:::

## History {#History}

It was first described by the German [painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_Dürer) (1471–1528) in his book *Underweysung der Messung* (*Instruction in Measurement with Compass and Straightedge* p. 38), calling it *Ein muschellini* (*Conchoid* or *Shell*). Dürer only drew one branch of the curve.
