# Polynomial matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polynomial matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **polynomial matrix** or **matrix of polynomials** is a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) whose elements are univariate or multivariate [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000). Equivalently, a polynomial matrix is a polynomial whose coefficients are matrices.

:::

A univariate polynomial matrix $A$ of degree $n$ is defined as:[^n1]

$$A(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n A_{i}x^{i} = A_{0} + A_{1}x + A_{2}x^2 + \cdots +A_{n}x^{n},$$

where $A_{i}$ denotes a matrix of constant coefficients, and $A_{n}$ is non-zero. An example 3×3 polynomial matrix, degree 2:

$$A(x)=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & x^2 & x \\ 0 & 2x & 2 \\ 3x+2 & x^2-1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} +\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 3 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}x+\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}x^2.$$

We can express this by saying that for a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R*, the rings $M_n(R[X])$ and $(M_n(R))[X]$ are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/26411).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A polynomial matrix over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) with [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) equal to a non-zero element of that field is called [unimodular](wikipedia://en/593773), and has an [inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_inverse) that is also a polynomial matrix. Note that the only scalar unimodular polynomials are polynomials of degree 0 – nonzero constants, because an inverse of an arbitrary polynomial of higher degree is a rational function.
- The roots of a polynomial matrix over the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) are the points in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) where the matrix loses [rank](wikipedia://en/26561).

Note that polynomial matrices are *not* to be confused with [monomial matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monomial_matrix), which are simply matrices with exactly one non-zero entry in each row and column.

If by λ we denote any element of the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) over which we constructed the matrix, by *I* the identity matrix, and we let *A* be a polynomial matrix, then the matrix λ*I* − *A* is the **characteristic matrix** of the matrix *A*. Its determinant, |λ*I* − *A*| is the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the matrix *A*.
