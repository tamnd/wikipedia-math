# Hamiltonian matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hamiltonian matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Hamiltonian matrix** is a $2n$-by-$2n$ [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) $A$ such that $JA$ is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474), where $J$ is the [skew-symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/174055)

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 0_n & I_n \\ -I_n & 0_n \end{bmatrix}$$

and $I_{n}$ is the $n$-by-$n$ [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718). In other words, $A$ is Hamiltonian if and only if $(JA)^{T} = JA$ where $( )^{T}$ denotes the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844).[^o2] The collection of all Hamiltonian matrices forms a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) (the [symplectic Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_Lie_algebra)); its associated [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) is the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977), whose elements are the [symplectic matrices](wikipedia://en/173983).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Suppose that the $2n$-by-$2n$ matrix $A$ is written as the [block matrix](wikipedia://en/457464)

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$$

where $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ are $n$-by-$n$ matrices. Then the condition that $A$ be Hamiltonian is equivalent to requiring that the matrices $b$ and $c$ are symmetric, and that $a + d^{T} = 0$.[^o2][^o5] Another equivalent condition is that $A$ is of the form $A = JS$ with $S$ symmetric.[^o5]

It follows easily from the definition that the transpose of a Hamiltonian matrix is Hamiltonian. Furthermore, the sum (and any [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632)) of two Hamiltonian matrices is again Hamiltonian, as is their [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193). It follows that the space of all Hamiltonian matrices is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), denoted $\operatorname{sp}(2n)$. The dimension of $\operatorname{sp}(2n)$ is $2n^{2} + n$. The corresponding [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) is the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) $\operatorname{Sp}(2n)$. This group consists of the [symplectic matrices](wikipedia://en/173983), those matrices $A$ which satisfy $A^{T}JA = J$. Thus, the [matrix exponential](wikipedia://en/672731) of a Hamiltonian matrix is symplectic. However the logarithm of a symplectic matrix is not necessarily Hamiltonian because the exponential map from the Lie algebra to the group is not surjective.[^o5][^o6]

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of a real Hamiltonian matrix is [even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_function). Thus, if a Hamiltonian matrix has $\lambda$ as an [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue), then $-\lambda$, $\lambda^{*}$ and $-\lambda^{*}$ are also eigenvalues.[^o5] It follows that the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of a Hamiltonian matrix is zero.

The square of a Hamiltonian matrix is [skew-Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/11380117) (a matrix $A$ is skew-Hamiltonian if $(JA)^{T} = -JA$). Conversely, every skew-Hamiltonian matrix arises as the square of a Hamiltonian matrix.[^o7]

:::

## Extension to complex matrices {#Extension_to_complex_matrices}

As for symplectic matrices, the definition for Hamiltonian matrices can be extended to complex matrices in two ways. One possibility is to say that a matrix $A$ is Hamiltonian if $(JA)^{T} = JA$, as above.[^o2][^o7] Another possibility is to use the condition $(JA)^{*} = JA$ where the superscript asterisk ($(\cdot)^{*}$) denotes the [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548).[^o9]

## Hamiltonian operators {#Hamiltonian_operators}

Let $V$ be a vector space, equipped with a symplectic form $\Omega$. A linear map $A : \; V \mapsto V$ is called **a Hamiltonian operator** with respect to $\Omega$ if the form $x, y \mapsto \Omega(A(x), y)$ is symmetric. Equivalently, it should satisfy

$$\Omega(A(x), y) = -\Omega(x, A(y))$$

Choose a basis $e_{1}, \ldots, e_{2n}$ in $V$, such that $\Omega$ is written as $\sum_i e_i \wedge e_{n+i}$. A linear operator is Hamiltonian with respect to $\Omega$ if and only if its matrix in this basis is Hamiltonian.[^o7]
