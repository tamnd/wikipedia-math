# Medial triangle

*Image: Medial Triangle.svg.* The red triangle is the medial triangle of the black. The endpoints of the red triangle coincide with the midpoints of the black triangle.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Medial triangle"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **medial triangle** or **midpoint triangle** of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) $\triangle ABC$ is the triangle with [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the triangle's sides $AB$, $AC$, and $BC$. It is the $n = 3$ case of the [midpoint polygon](wikipedia://en/33456840) of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with $n$ sides. The medial triangle is not the same thing as the [median triangle](wikipedia://en/64533141), which is the triangle whose sides have the same lengths as the [medians](wikipedia://en/1072006) of $\triangle ABC$.

:::

Each side of the medial triangle is called a *midsegment* (or *midline*). In general, a midsegment of a triangle is a line segment which joins the midpoints of two sides of the triangle. It is parallel to the third side and has a length equal to half the length of the third side.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Mittendreieck.svg.* $M$: [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of $\triangle ABC$, [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of $\triangle DEF$ $N$: [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) of $\triangle ABC$, [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) of $\triangle DEF$ $S$: [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$

The medial triangle can also be viewed as the image of triangle $\triangle ABC$ transformed by a [homothety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation) centered at the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) with ratio -1/2. Thus, the sides of the medial triangle are half and parallel to the corresponding sides of $\triangle ABC$. Hence, the medial triangle is inversely [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) and shares the same centroid and [medians](wikipedia://en/1072006) with $\triangle ABC$. It also follows from this that the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the medial triangle equals the [semiperimeter](wikipedia://en/886617) of $\triangle ABC$, and that the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) is one quarter of the area of $\triangle ABC$. Furthermore, the four triangles that the original triangle is subdivided into by the medial triangle are all mutually [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) by [SSS](wikipedia://en/30654#Similarity_and_congruence), so their areas are equal and thus the area of each is 1/4 the area of the original triangle.[^o4]

The [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of the medial triangle coincides with the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of $\triangle ABC$. This fact provides a tool for proving [collinearity of the circumcenter, centroid and orthocenter](wikipedia://en/203622). The medial triangle is the [pedal triangle](wikipedia://en/152983) of the circumcenter. The [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243) circumscribes the medial triangle, and so the nine-point center is the circumcenter of the medial triangle.

The [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) of the medial triangle is the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) of its reference triangle.[^o5] In particular, this means that the incenter of a triangle must lie in its medial triangle.[^o6]

The incenter of the medial triangle is the [Spieker center](wikipedia://en/35728942) of its reference triangle.

A reference triangle's medial triangle is [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to the triangle whose vertices are the midpoints between the reference triangle's [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) and its vertices.[^o5]

A point in the interior of a triangle is the center of an [inellipse](wikipedia://en/9610491) of the triangle if and only if the point lies in the interior of the medial triangle.[^o7]

The medial triangle is the only [inscribed triangle](wikipedia://en/4470203) for which none of the other three interior triangles has smaller area.[^o8]

The reference triangle and its medial triangle are [orthologic triangles](wikipedia://en/69531104).

:::

## Coordinates {#Coordinates}

Let $a = \lvert BC\rvert$, $b = \lvert CA\rvert$, $c = \lvert AB\rvert$ be the sidelengths of triangle $\triangle ABC$. [Trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) for the vertices of the medial triangle $\triangle EFD$ are given by:

$$\begin{array}{ccc} E =& 0 &:& \frac{1}{b} &:& \frac{1}{c} \\[2pt] F =& \frac{1}{a} &:& 0 &:& \frac{1}{c} \\[2pt] D =& \frac{1}{a} &:& \frac{1}{b} &:& \, 0 \end{array}$$

## Anticomplementary triangle {#Anticomplementary_triangle}

If $\triangle EFD$ is the medial triangle of $\triangle ABC$, then $\triangle ABC$ is the **anticomplementary triangle** or **antimedial triangle** of $\triangle EFD$. The anticomplementary triangle of $\triangle ABC$ is formed by three lines parallel to the sides of $\triangle ABC$: the parallel to $AB$ through $C$, the parallel to $AC$ through $B$, and the parallel to $BC$ through $A$.

[Trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) for the vertices of the triangle $\triangle E'F'D'$ anticomplementary to $\triangle ABC$ are given by:

$$\begin{array}{crrr} E' =& -\frac{1}{a} &:& \frac{1}{b} &:& \frac{1}{c} \\[2pt] F' =& \frac{1}{a} &:& -\frac{1}{b} &:& \frac{1}{c} \\[2pt] D' =& \frac{1}{a} &:& \frac{1}{b} &:& -\frac{1}{c} \end{array}$$

The name "anticomplementary triangle" corresponds to the fact that its vertices are the anticomplements of the vertices $A$, $B$, and $C$ of the reference triangle. The vertices of the medial triangle are the complements of $A$, $B$, and $C$.

:::
