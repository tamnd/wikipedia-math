# Integer lattice

*Image: Regular pentagram lattice polygon approximation.svg.* Approximations of regular [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544) with vertices on a square lattice with coordinates indicated

*Image: Diophantine\_approximation\_graph.svg.* [Rational approximants](wikipedia://en/251558) of [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689) values can be mapped to points lying close to lines having gradients corresponding to the values

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Integer lattice"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the $n$-dimensional **integer lattice**, denoted $\mathbb{Z}^n$, is the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) in the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$ whose lattice points are [$n$-tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563). The two-dimensional integer lattice is also called the *[square lattice](wikipedia://en/2874853)* (or *grid lattice*) and the three-dimensional integer lattice is called the **cubic lattice**. $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is the simplest example of a [root lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_lattice). The integer lattice is an odd [unimodular lattice](wikipedia://en/1648605).

:::

## Automorphism group {#Automorphism_group}

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) (or [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [congruences](wikipedia://en/48167)) of the integer lattice consists of all [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) and sign changes of the coordinates, and is of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) 2<sup>*n*</sup> *n*!. As a [matrix group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_group) it is given by the set of all *n* × *n* [signed permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_permutation_matrices). This group is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246)

$$(\mathbb Z_2)^n \rtimes S_n$$

where the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>*n*</sub> acts on (**Z**<sub>2</sub>)<sup>*n*</sup> by permutation (this is a classic example of a [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625)).

For the square lattice, this is the group of the [square](wikipedia://en/659939), or the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of order 8; for the three-dimensional cubic lattice, we get the group of the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), or [octahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_group), of order 48.

## Diophantine geometry {#Diophantine_geometry}

In the study of [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), the square lattice of points with integer coordinates is often referred to as the **Diophantine plane**. In mathematical terms, the Diophantine plane is the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $\scriptstyle\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ of the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of all integers $\scriptstyle\mathbb{Z}$. The study of [Diophantine figures](wikipedia://en/12600886) focuses on the selection of nodes in the Diophantine plane such that all pairwise distances are integers.

## Coarse geometry {#Coarse_geometry}

In [coarse geometry](wikipedia://en/4960605), the integer lattice is coarsely equivalent to [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697).

## Pick's theorem {#Pick's_theorem}

*Image: Pick\_theorem\_simple.svg.* red, green, $A = \operatorname{red} + \tfrac{\operatorname{green}}{2} - 1 = 10$

[Pick's theorem](wikipedia://en/328252), first described by [Georg Alexander Pick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Alexander_Pick) in 1899, provides a formula for the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530) with all [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) lying on the 2-dimensional integer lattice, in terms of the number of integer points within it and on its boundary.[^o7]

Let $i$ be the number of integer points interior to the polygon, and let $b$ be the number of integer points on its boundary (including both vertices and points along the sides). Then the area $A$ of this polygon is:[^o8]

$$A = i + \frac{b}{2} - 1.$$

The example shown has $i=7$ interior points and $b=8$ boundary points, so its area is $A=7+\tfrac{8}{2}-1=10$ square units.

:::
