# Local Langlands conjectures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local Langlands conjectures"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **local Langlands conjectures**, introduced by [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986),[^n1][^n2] are part of the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905). They describe a correspondence between the [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) of a [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386) [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) $G$ over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) $F$, and representations of the [Langlands group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_group) of $F$ into the $L$-group of $G$. This correspondence is not a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) in general. The [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) can be thought of as a generalization of [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532) from [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673) to non-abelian Galois groups.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Local Langlands conjectures for GL1"}

## Local Langlands conjectures for GL<sub>1</sub> {#Local_Langlands_conjectures_for_GL1}

The local Langlands conjectures for $\operatorname{GL}_1(K)$ follow from (and are essentially equivalent to) [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532). More precisely, the [Artin map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_map) gives an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) from the group $\operatorname{GL}_1(K)=K^\times$ to the [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) of the [Weil group](wikipedia://en/6375618). In particular, [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) smooth representations of $\operatorname{GL}_1(K)$ are 1-dimensional as the group is abelian, so can be identified with [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) of the Weil group to $\operatorname{GL}_1(\mathbb{C})$. This gives the Langlands correspondence between homomorphisms of the Weil group to $\operatorname{GL}_1(\mathbb{C})$ and irreducible smooth representations of $\operatorname{GL}_1(K)$.

:::

## Representations of the Weil group {#Representations_of_the_Weil_group}

Representations of the Weil group do not quite correspond to irreducible smooth representations of [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564). To get a bijection, one has to slightly modify the notion of a representation of the Weil group, to something called a Weil–Deligne representation. This consists of a representation of the Weil group on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $V$ together with a [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235) endomorphism $N$ of $V$ such that $wNw^{-1}=\|w\|N$, or equivalently a representation of the [Weil–Deligne group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil–Deligne_group). In addition, the representation of the Weil group should have an open kernel and be (Frobenius) semisimple.

For every Frobenius semisimple complex $n$-dimensional Weil–Deligne representation $\rho$ of the Weil group of $F$ there is an [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283) $L(s,\rho)$ and a [local ε-factor](wikipedia://en/32103592) $\varepsilon(s,\rho,\psi)$ (depending on a character $\psi$ of $F$).

## Representations of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) {#Representations_of_GLn(F)}

The representations of $\operatorname{GL}_n(F)$ appearing in the local Langlands correspondence are smooth irreducible complex representations.

- "Smooth" means that every vector is fixed by some open subgroup.
- "Irreducible" means that the representation is nonzero and has no [subrepresentations](wikipedia://en/55943952) other than 0 and itself.

Smooth irreducible complex representations are automatically admissible.

The [Bernstein–Zelevinsky classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein–Zelevinsky_classification) reduces the classification of irreducible smooth representations to cuspidal representations.

For every irreducible admissible complex representation $\pi$ there is an *L*-function $L(s,\pi)$ and a local $\varepsilon$-factor $\varepsilon(s,\pi,\psi)$ (depending on a character $\psi$ of $F$). More generally, if there are two irreducible admissible representations $\pi$ and $\pi'$ of general linear groups, there are local Rankin–Selberg convolution L-functions $L(s,\pi\times \pi')$ and $\varepsilon$-factors $\varepsilon(s,\pi\times \pi',\psi)$.

[Bushnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Bushnell) and [Kutzko](wikipedia://en/33587856)[^n3] described the irreducible admissible representations of general linear groups over local fields.

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Local Langlands conjectures for GL2"}

## Local Langlands conjectures for GL<sub>2</sub> {#Local_Langlands_conjectures_for_GL2}

The local Langlands conjecture for $\operatorname{GL}_2$ of a local field says that there is a (unique) bijection $\pi$ from 2-dimensional semisimple Weil-Deligne representations of the Weil group to irreducible smooth representations of $\operatorname{GL}_2(F)$ that preserves *L*-functions, $\varepsilon$-factors, and commutes with twisting by characters of $F^\times$.

[Jacquet](wikipedia://en/25600925) and Langlands[^n4] verified the local Langlands conjectures for $\operatorname{GL}_2$ in the case when the residue field does not have [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 2. In this case, the representations of the Weil group are all of cyclic or dihedral type.

(Gelfand Graev 1962) classified the smooth irreducible representations of $\operatorname{GL}_2(F)$ when $F$ has odd residue characteristic (see also (Gelfand Graev Pyatetskii-Shapiro 1969, chapter 2)), and claimed incorrectly that the classification for even residue characteristic differs only insignifictanly from the odd residue characteristic case. [Weil](wikipedia://en/2019)[^n5] pointed out that when the residue field has characteristic 2, there are some extra exceptional 2-dimensional representations of the Weil group whose image in $\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is of tetrahedral or octahedral type. (For global Langlands conjectures, 2-dimensional representations can also be of icosahedral type, but this cannot happen in the local case as the Galois groups are [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012).)

[Tunnell](wikipedia://en/41107257)[^n6] proved the local Langlands conjectures for the general linear group $\operatorname{GL}_2(K)$ over the [2-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423), and over local fields containing a [cube root of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_root_of_unity). Kutzko[^n7] proved the local Langlands conjectures for the general linear group $\operatorname{GL}_2(K)$ over all local fields.

[Cartier](wikipedia://en/1470549),[^n8] as well as Bushnell and [Henniart](wikipedia://en/31961003),[^n9] gave expositions of the proof.

:::

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Local Langlands conjectures for GLn"}

## Local Langlands conjectures for GL<sub>*n*</sub> {#Local_Langlands_conjectures_for_GLn}

The local Langlands conjectures for general linear groups state that there are unique bijections $\pi\leftrightarrow \rho_\pi$ from [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of irreducible admissible representations $\pi$ of $\operatorname{GL}_n(F)$ to equivalence classes of continuous Frobenius semisimple complex $n$-dimensional Weil–Deligne representations $\rho_\pi$ of the Weil group of $F$ that preserve *L*-functions and $\varepsilon$-factors of pairs of representations, and coincide with the Artin map for 1-dimensional representations. In other words,

- $$L(s,\rho_\pi\otimes \rho_{\pi'}) = L(s,\pi\times \pi')$$
- $$\varepsilon(s,\rho_\pi\otimes \rho_{\pi'},\psi) = \varepsilon(s,\pi\times \pi',\psi).$$

[Laumon](wikipedia://en/22565856), [Rapoport](wikipedia://en/26629548) and [Stuhler](wikipedia://en/32886316)[^n10] proved the local Langlands conjectures for the general linear group $\operatorname{GL}_n(K)$ for positive characteristic local fields $K$. Carayol[^n11] gave an exposition of their work.

[Harris](wikipedia://en/31954452) and [Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931)[^n12] proved the local Langlands conjectures for the general linear group $\operatorname{GL}_n(K)$ for characteristic 0 local fields $K$. Henniart[^n13] gave another proof. Carayol[^n14] and Wedhorn[^n15] gave expositions of their work.

:::

::: {#o28 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Local Langlands conjectures for other groups"}

## Local Langlands conjectures for other groups {#Local_Langlands_conjectures_for_other_groups}

[Borel](wikipedia://en/365672)[^n16] and [Vogan](wikipedia://en/10229758)[^n17] discuss the Langlands conjectures for more general groups. The Langlands conjectures for arbitrary reductive groups $G$ are more complicated to state than the ones for general linear groups, and it is unclear what the best way of stating them should be. Roughly speaking, admissible representations of a reductive group are grouped into disjoint finite sets called *L*-packets, which should correspond to some classes of homomorphisms, called *L*-parameters, from the [local Langlands group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Langlands_group) to the [*L*-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual) of $G$. Some earlier versions used the Weil−Deligne group or the Weil group instead of the local Langlands group, which gives a slightly weaker form of the conjecture.

Langlands[^n18] proved the Langlands conjectures for groups over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ by giving the [Langlands classification](wikipedia://en/10985992) of their irreducible admissible representations (up to infinitesimal equivalence), or, equivalently, of their irreducible [$(\mathfrak{g},K)$-modules](wikipedia://en/21974644).

[Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_Wee_Teck) and Takeda[^n19] proved the local Langlands conjectures for the [symplectic similitude group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_similitude_group) $\operatorname{GSp}(4)$ and used that to deduce it for the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) $\operatorname{Sp}(4)$.[^n20]

:::
