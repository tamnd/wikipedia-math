# Nesmith Ankeny

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nesmith Ankeny"}

**Nesmith Cornett Ankeny** (1927, [Walla Walla, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walla_Walla,_Washington) – 4 August 1993, [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle)) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specialising in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

After Army service, he studied at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and obtained his Ph.D. at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1950 under the supervision of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351). He was a Fellow at Princeton and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179),[^o2] then assistant professor at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) from 1952 to 1955, when he joined [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT). He was a [Guggenheim fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in 1958; he became a full professor in 1964 and retired in 1992.

His research was mainly in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), on consequences of the [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567).

He was also interested in [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and gaming: he wrote a book on mathematical analysis of [poker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poker) strategies, especially [bluffing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluff_%28poker%29).
