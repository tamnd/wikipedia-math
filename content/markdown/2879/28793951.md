# Milne-Thomson circle theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Milne-Thomson circle theorem"}

In [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) the **Milne-Thomson circle theorem** or the **circle theorem** is a statement giving a new [stream function](wikipedia://en/218091) for a fluid flow when a cylinder is placed into that flow.[^o2][^o3] It was named after the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) mathematician [L. M. Milne-Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._M._Milne-Thomson).

:::

Let $f(z)$ be the [complex potential](wikipedia://en/58320) for a fluid flow, where all [singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) of $f(z)$ lie in $|z| > a$. If a circle $|z| = a$ is placed into that flow, the complex potential for the new flow is given by[^o4]

$$w = f(z) + \overline{f\left( \frac{a^2}{\bar{z}} \right)} = f(z) + \overline f\left( \frac{a^2}{z} \right).$$

with same singularities as $f(z)$ in $|z| > a$ and $|z| = a$ is a streamline. On the circle $|z| = a$, $z\bar z = a^2$, therefore

$$w = f(z) + \overline{f(z)}.$$

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider a uniform irrotational flow $f(z) = Uz$ with velocity $U$ flowing in the positive $x$ direction and place an infinitely long cylinder of radius $a$ in the flow with the center of the cylinder at the origin. Then $f\left(\frac{a^2}{\bar z}\right) = \frac{Ua^2}{\bar z}, \ \Rightarrow \ \overline{f\left( \frac{a^2}{\bar{z}} \right)} = \frac{Ua^2}{ z}$, hence using circle theorem,

$$w(z) = U \left(z + \frac{a^2}{z}\right)$$

represents the complex potential of uniform flow over a cylinder.

:::
