# Alexander Ivanovich Chuprov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Ivanovich Chuprov"}

*Image: Chuprov.jpg.*

**Alexander Ivanovich Chuprov** (Александр Иванович Чупров; 1841–1908) was a professor of political economy and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University) whose lectures provided the standard introduction to economics for late 19th-century Russian students.

:::

Chuprov's father was an Orthodox priest based in [Mosalsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosalsk). Alexander attended the Law Department of the [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University) where he became interested in [Wilhelm Roscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Roscher)'s research. He founded the Moscow Society to Disseminate Technical Knowledge in 1869 and was elected into the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1887.

Chuprov has been described as the founder of [transport economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_economics) and the multiple-factor analysis of economic regions.[^o2] In his landmark work *The Railway Economy* (1875–78) he analyzed statistics on railway traffic. He distinguished one region from another according to "the value of the market, the cost of transportation, and demographic indicators".[^o3]

Chuprov became known as "the heart and soul" of [zemstvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemstvo) statistical investigations and [sample surveys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_survey) in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).[^o4] His researchers are said to have interviewed 4.5 million Russian [muzhiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzhik). Their mission was to provide a modern statistical description of the Russian peasant commune, or [obschina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obschina). Chuprov viewed the Russian obshchina as a valuable social institution which should be preserved.

Chuprov was a lifelong friend of another prominent statistician, [Ivan Yanzhul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Yanzhul). One of his students at the Moscow University was [Wassily Kandinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassily_Kandinsky). His son [Alexander A. Chuprov](wikipedia://en/1614831) (1874–1926) is said to have given "much impetus to statistics in pre-revolutionary Russia".[^o5]
