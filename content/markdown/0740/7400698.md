# AC0

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="AC0"}

*Image: Diagram of an AC0 Circuit.svg.* Diagram of an AC<sup>0</sup> circuit: The n input bits are on the bottom and the top gate produces the output; the circuit consists of AND- and OR-gates of polynomial fan-in each, and the alternation depth is bounded by a constant.

**AC<sup>0</sup>** (alternating circuit) is a [complexity class](wikipedia://en/502426) used in [circuit complexity](wikipedia://en/7641969). It is the smallest class in the [AC](wikipedia://en/7401755) hierarchy, and consists of all families of circuits of depth O(1) and polynomial size, with unlimited-[fanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanin) [AND gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AND_gate) and [OR gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OR_gate) (we allow [NOT gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOT_gate) only at the inputs).[^o9] It thus contains [**NC**<sup>0</sup>](wikipedia://en/22073#NC0), which has only bounded-fanin AND and OR gates.[^o9] Such circuits are called "alternating circuits", since it is only necessary for the layers to alternate between all-AND and all-OR, since one AND after another AND is equivalent to a single AND, and the same for OR.

:::

## Example problems {#Example_problems}

Integer addition and subtraction are computable in AC<sup>0</sup>,[^o3] but multiplication is not (specifically, when the inputs are two integers under the usual binary[^o4] or base-10 representations of integers).

Since it is a circuit class, like [P/poly](wikipedia://en/2052415), AC<sup>0</sup> also contains every [unary language](wikipedia://en/12769341).

## Descriptive complexity {#Descriptive_complexity}

From a [descriptive complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_complexity) viewpoint, [DLOGTIME](wikipedia://en/7404967)-[uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformity_%28complexity%29) AC<sup>0</sup> is equal to the descriptive class [FO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FO_%28complexity%29)+BIT of all [languages](wikipedia://en/10939) describable in first-order logic with the addition of the [BIT predicate](wikipedia://en/7639504), or alternatively by FO(+, ×), or by [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) in the [logarithmic hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LH_%28complexity%29).[^o6]

## Separations {#Separations}

In 1984 Furst, Saxe, and [Sipser](wikipedia://en/3644015) showed that calculating the [PARITY](wikipedia://en/143135) of the input bits (unlike the aforementioned addition/subtraction problems above which had two inputs) cannot be decided by any AC<sup>0</sup> circuits, even with non-uniformity. Similarly, computing the majority is also not in $\mathsf{AC}^0$.[^o8][^o9] It follows that AC<sup>0</sup> is strictly smaller than [TC<sup>0</sup>](wikipedia://en/7399828). Note that "PARITY" is also called "[XOR](wikipedia://en/105979)" in the literature.

However, PARITY is only barely out of AC<sup>0</sup>, in the sense that for any $k > 0$, there exists a family of alternating circuits using depth $\lceil k \ln n / \ln\ln n \rceil$ and size $O\left(2^{(\ln n)^{1/k}} \frac{n}{(\ln n)^{1/k}} \right)$.[^o10] In particular, setting $k$ to be a large constant, then there exists a family of alternating circuits using depth $O(\ln n / \ln\ln n)\ll O(\ln n)$, and size only slightly superlinear.

$\mathsf{AC}^0$ can be divided further, into a hierarchy of languages requiring up to 1 layer, 2 layers, etc. Let $\mathsf{AC}^0_d$ be the class of languages decidable by a threshold circuit family of up to depth $d$:

$$\mathsf{AC}^0_1 \subset \mathsf{AC}^0_2 \subset \cdots \subset \mathsf{AC}^0 = \bigcup_{d=1}^\infty \mathsf{AC}^0_d$$

The following problem is $\mathsf{AC}^0_d$-complete under a uniformity condition: given a grid graph of polynomial length and width $d$, decide whether two given vertices are connected.[^o11]

The addition of two $n$-bit integers is in $\mathsf{AC}^0_3$ but not in $\mathsf{AC}^0_2$.[^o10]
