# Regular isotopy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regular isotopy"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) subject of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), **regular isotopy** is the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) of [link diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram#Knot_diagrams) that is generated by using the 2nd and 3rd [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374) only. The notion of regular isotopy was introduced by [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman) (Kauffman 1990). It can be thought of as an isotopy of a ribbon pressed flat against the plane which keeps the ribbon flat. For diagrams in the plane this is a finer equivalence relation than [ambient isotopy](wikipedia://en/7404467) of [framed links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_link), since the 2nd and 3rd Reidemeister moves preserve the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) of the diagram (Kauffman 1990, pp. 450ff.). However, for diagrams in the sphere (considered as the plane plus infinity), the two notions are equivalent, due to the extra freedom of passing a strand through infinity.

:::
