# Ambient isotopy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ambient isotopy"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) subject of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), an **ambient isotopy**, also called an *h-isotopy*, is a kind of [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) distortion of an [ambient space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_space), for example a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), taking a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) to another submanifold. For example in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), one considers two [knots](wikipedia://en/366808) the same if one can distort one knot into the other without breaking it. Such a distortion is an example of an ambient isotopy.

:::

More precisely, let $N$ and $M$ be manifolds and $g$ and $h$ be [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) of $N$ in $M$. A [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map)

$$F:M \times [0,1] \rightarrow M$$

is defined to be an ambient isotopy taking $g$ to $h$ if each $F_t: M \rightarrow M, F_t(\cdot) = F(\cdot, t)$ is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) from $M$ to itself, $F_0$ is the [identity map](wikipedia://en/15069) and $F_1 \circ g = h$. This implies that the [orientation](wikipedia://en/2544098) must be preserved by ambient isotopies. For example, two knots that are [mirror images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) of each other are, in general, not equivalent.
