# Mary Celine Fasenmyer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mary Celine Fasenmyer"}

**Mary Celine Fasenmyer**, [RSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_Mercy) (October 4, 1906, [Crown, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown,_Pennsylvania) – December 27, 1996, [Erie, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie,_Pennsylvania)) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) religious [sister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_sister). She is most noted for her work on [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Fasenmyer grew up in [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania)'s oil country, and displayed mathematical talent in high school. For ten years after her graduation she taught and studied at [Mercyhurst College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercyhurst_College) in [Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie,_Pennsylvania), where she joined the [Sisters of Mercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_Mercy). She pursued her mathematical studies in Pittsburgh and the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), obtaining her [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1946 under the direction of [Earl Rainville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_D._Rainville), with a dissertation entitled *Some Generalized Hypergeometric Polynomials*.[^o4]

After earning her Ph.D., Fasenmyer published two papers which expanded on her doctorate work. These would be further elaborated by [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) and [Herbert Wilf](wikipedia://en/3782688) into "[WZ theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WZ_theory)", which allowed computerized proof of many [combinatorial identities](wikipedia://en/5170). After this, she returned to Mercyhurst to teach and did not engage in further research.

Fasenmyer died in 1996.

## "Sister Celine's" method {#"Sister_Celine's"_method}

Fasenmyer is most remembered for the method that bears her name, first described in her Ph.D. thesis concerning recurrence relations in hypergeometric series.[^o2] The thesis demonstrated a purely algorithmic method to find recurrence relations satisfied by sums of terms of a hypergeometric polynomial, and requires only the series expansions of the polynomial. The beauty of her method is that it lends itself readily to computer automation. The work of Wilf and Zeilberger generalized the algorithm and established its correctness.

The hypergeometric polynomials she studied are called [Sister Celine's polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_Celine's_polynomials).
