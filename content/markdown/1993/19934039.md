# The Whetstone of Witte

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="The Whetstone of Witte"}

***The Whetstone of Witte*** is the shortened title of [Robert Recorde](wikipedia://en/310247)'s [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) book published in 1557, the full title being ***The whetstone of witte, whiche is the seconde parte of Arithmetike: containyng thextraction of Rootes: The*Coßike*practise, with the rule of*Equation*: and the woorkes of*Surde Nombers**. The book covers topics including whole numbers, the extraction of roots and irrational numbers.[^o3] The work is notable for containing the first recorded use of the [equals sign](wikipedia://en/501828)[^o4] and also for being the first book in English to use the [plus and minus signs](wikipedia://en/245206).[^o5]

:::

[Recordian notation](wikipedia://en/31228704) for [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491), however, differed from the later [Cartesian notation](wikipedia://en/277184) $p^q = p \times p \times p \cdots \times p$. Recorde expressed [indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent) and [surds](wikipedia://en/235029) larger than 3 in a systematic form based on the [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization) of the exponent: a factor of two he termed a *zenzic*, and a factor of three, a *cubic*. Recorde termed the larger prime numbers appearing in this factorization *sursolids*, distinguishing between them by use of ordinal numbers: that is, he defined 5 as the *first sursolid*, written as **ʃz** and 7 as the *second sursolid*, written as **Bʃz**.[^o6] He also devised symbols for these factors: a zenzic was denoted by **z**, and a cubic by **&**. For instance, he referred to *p<sup>8</sup>=p<sup>2×2×2</sup>* as **zzz** (the [zenzizenzizenzic](wikipedia://en/499002)), and *q<sup>12</sup>=q<sup>2×2×3</sup>* as **zz&** (the zenzizenzicubic).[^o7]

*Image: Recorde - The Whetstone of Witte - equals.jpg.* The passage in *The Whetstone of Witte* introducing the equals sign[^o8]

Later in the book he includes a chart of exponents all the way up to *p<sup>80</sup>=p<sup>2×2×2×2×5</sup>* written as **zzzzʃz**. There is an error in the chart, however, writing *p<sup>69</sup>* as **Sʃz**, despite it not being a prime. It should be *p<sup>3×23</sup>* or **&Gʃz**.[^o9]

Page images have been made available by [Victor Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Katz) and Frank Swetz through *Convergence*, a publication of [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o10]
