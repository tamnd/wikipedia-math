# Triangular bipyramid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triangular bipyramid"}

A **triangular bipyramid** is a [hexahedron](wikipedia://en/237209), a polyhedron with six triangular faces. It is constructed by attaching two [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) face-to-face. The same shape is also known as a **triangular dipyramid** or **trigonal bipyramid**. If these tetrahedra are regular, all faces of a triangular bipyramid are [equilateral](wikipedia://en/173285). It is an example of a [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995), [composite polyhedron](wikipedia://en/77684519), and [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034).

:::

Many polyhedra are related to the triangular bipyramid, such as similar shapes derived from different approaches and the [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584) as its [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815). Applications of a triangular bipyramid include [trigonal bipyramidal molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_bipyramidal_molecular_geometry), which describes its [atom cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_cluster), a solution of the [Thomson problem](wikipedia://en/3104018), and the representation of [color order systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_model) by the eighteenth century.

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

### As a right bipyramid {#As_a_right_bipyramid}

Like other [bipyramids](wikipedia://en/4153), a triangular bipyramid can be constructed by attaching two tetrahedra face-to-face. These tetrahedra cover their triangular base, and the resulting polyhedron has six triangles, five [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), and nine edges. Because of its triangular faces with any type, the triangular bipyramid is a [simplicial polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_polyhedron) like other infinitely many bipyramids. A *right* bipyramid is one in which the [apices](wikipedia://en/6022079) of both pyramids are on a line passing through the center of the base, such that its faces are isosceles triangles. If two tetrahedra are otherwise, the triangular bipyramid is oblique.

*Image: Graph of triangular bipyramid.svg.* Graph of a triangular bipyramid

According to [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817), a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) can be represented as the [skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910) of a polyhedron if it is a [planar](wikipedia://en/24314) (can be drawn without crossing any edges) and [three-connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) (it remains connected if any two vertices are removed). A triangular bipyramid is represented by a graph with nine edges, constructed by adding one vertex to the vertices of a [wheel graph](wikipedia://en/1655876) representing [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra).

Like other right bipyramids, a triangular bipyramid has [three-dimensional point-group symmetry](wikipedia://en/2694525), the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) $D_{3 \mathrm{h}}$ of order twelve: the appearance of a triangular bipyramid is unchanged as it rotated by one-, two-thirds, and full angle around the [axis of symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673) (a line passing through two vertices and the base's center vertically), and it has [mirror symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_symmetry) with any bisector of the base; it is also symmetrical by reflection across a horizontal plane. A triangular bipyramid is [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) (or isohedral).

### As a Johnson solid {#As_a_Johnson_solid}

*Image: J12 triangular bipyramid.stl.* 3D model of a triangular bipyramid as a Johnson solid

If the tetrahedra are regular, all edges of a triangular bipyramid are equal in length and form [equilateral triangular](wikipedia://en/173285) faces. A polyhedron with only equilateral triangles as faces is called a [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995). There are eight convex deltahedra, one of which is a triangular bipyramid with [regular polygonal](wikipedia://en/333306) faces. A convex polyhedron in which all of its faces are regular polygons is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). A triangular bipyramid with regular faces is numbered as the twelfth Johnson solid $J_{12}$. It is an example of a [composite polyhedron](wikipedia://en/77684519), because it can be sliced, by a plane intersecting it only in edges, into two [regular tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606).

A triangular bipyramid's surface area $A$ is six times that of each triangle. Its volume $V$ can be calculated by slicing it into two tetrahedra and adding their volume. In the case of edge length $a$, this is:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}a^2 &\approx 2.598a^2, \\ V &= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{6}a^3 &\approx 0.238a^3. \end{aligned}$$

The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) of a triangular bipyramid can be obtained by adding the dihedral angle of two regular tetrahedra. The dihedral angle of a triangular bipyramid between adjacent triangular faces is that of the regular tetrahedron: 70.5 degrees. In an edge where two tetrahedra are attached, the dihedral angle of adjacent triangles is twice that: 141.1 degrees.

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

*Image: Kleetope of a triangular bipyramid.svg.* The Kleetope of a triangular bipyramid is obtained by augmenting a triangular bipyramid with tetrahedra on each face.

Some types of triangular bipyramids may be derived in different ways. The [Kleetope](wikipedia://en/31165912) of a triangular bipyramid, its Kleetope can be constructed from a triangular bipyramid by attaching tetrahedra to each of its faces, replacing them with three other triangles; the skeleton of the resulting polyhedron represents the [Goldner–Harary graph](wikipedia://en/24674758). Another type of triangular bipyramid results from cutting off its vertices, a process known as [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429).

Bipyramids are the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007). This means the bipyramids' vertices correspond to the faces of a prism, and the edges between pairs of vertices of one correspond to the edges between pairs of faces of the other; doubling it results in the original polyhedron. A triangular bipyramid is the dual polyhedron of a [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584), and vice versa. A triangular prism has five faces, nine edges, and six vertices, with the same symmetry as a triangular bipyramid.

## Applications {#Applications}

*Image: N 2 to 5 ThomsonSolutions.png.* The known solution of the Thomson problem, with one a triangular bipyramid

The [Thomson problem](wikipedia://en/3104018) concerns the minimum energy configuration of charged particles on a sphere. A triangular bipyramid is a known solution in the case of five electrons, placing vertices of a triangular bipyramid [within a sphere](wikipedia://en/991784). This solution is aided by a mathematically rigorous computer.

A [chemical compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_compound)'s [trigonal bipyramidal molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_bipyramidal_molecular_geometry) may be described as the [atom cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_cluster) of a triangular bipyramid. This molecule has a [main-group element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-group_element) without an active [lone pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_pair), described by a model which predicts the geometry of molecules known as [VSEPR theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSEPR_theory). Examples of this structure include [phosphorus pentafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus_pentafluoride) and [phosphorus pentachloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus_pentachloride) in the gaseous [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28matter%29).

In [color theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_theory), the triangular bipyramid was used to represent the three-dimensional [color-order system in primary colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_color#Color_order_systems). German astronomer [Tobias Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Mayer) wrote in 1758 that each of its vertices represents a color: white and black are the top and bottom axial vertices, respectively, and the rest of the vertices are red, blue, and yellow.
