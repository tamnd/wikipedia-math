# Kunihiko Kodaira

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kunihiko Kodaira"}

**Kunihiko Kodaira** was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for distinguished work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and the theory of [complex](wikipedia://en/509742) [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), and as the founder of the Japanese school of algebraic geometers.[^o3] He was awarded a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1954, being the first Japanese national to receive this honour.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Kunihiko Kodaira was born in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo) on Tuesday, March 16, 1915. He graduated from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1938 with a degree in mathematics and also graduated from the physics department at the University of Tokyo in 1941. During the [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) years he worked in isolation, but was able to master [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) as it then stood. He obtained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1949, with a thesis entitled *Harmonic fields in Riemannian manifolds*.[^o5] He was involved in [cryptographic](wikipedia://en/18934432) work from about 1944, while holding an academic post in Tokyo.

## Institute for Advanced Study and Princeton University {#Institute_for_Advanced_Study_and_Princeton_University}

In 1949 he travelled to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) at the invitation of [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544). He was subsequently also appointed Associate Professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1952 and promoted to Professor in 1955. At this time the foundations of Hodge theory were being brought in line with contemporary technique in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675). Kodaira rapidly became involved in exploiting the tools it opened up in algebraic geometry, adding [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory) as it became available. This work was particularly influential, for example on [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495).

In a second research phase, Kodaira wrote a long series of papers in collaboration with [Donald C. Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer), founding the [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) of complex structures on manifolds. This gave the possibility of constructions of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609), since in general such structures depend [continuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28mathematics%29) on parameters. It also identified the [sheaf cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_cohomology_group), for the sheaf associated with the [holomorphic tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/27540773), that carried the basic data about the dimension of the moduli space, and obstructions to deformations. This theory is still foundational, and also had an influence on the (technically very different) [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) of [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck). Spencer then continued this work, applying the techniques to structures other than complex ones, such as [G-structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure).

In a third major part of his work, Kodaira worked again from around 1960 through the [classification of algebraic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_algebraic_surfaces) from the point of view of [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733) of complex manifolds. This resulted in a typology of seven kinds of two-dimensional [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) complex manifolds, recovering the five algebraic types known classically; the other two being non-algebraic. He provided also detailed studies of [elliptic fibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_fibration) of surfaces over a curve, or in other language [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) over [algebraic function fields](wikipedia://en/447645), a theory whose arithmetic analogue proved important soon afterwards. This work also included a characterisation of [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116) as deformations of [quartic surfaces](wikipedia://en/20133775) in *P*<sup>3</sup>, and the theorem that they form a single [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) class. Again, this work has proved foundational. (The K3 surfaces were named after [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117), [Erich Kähler](wikipedia://en/383501), and Kodaira).

## Later years {#Later_years}

Kunihiko Kodaira left Princeton University and the Institute for Advanced Study in 1961, and briefly served as chair at the [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). In 1967, he returned to the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo). He was awarded a [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) in 1984/5. He died in [Kofu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofu) on Saturday, 26 July 1997, at the age of 82.

He was honoured with the membership of the [Japan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy), the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786) and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1978. He was the foreign associate of the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1975, member of the [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) Academy of Sciences in 1974 and honorary member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1979. He received the Order of Culture and the [Japan Academy Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy_Prize_%28academics%29) in 1957 and the Fujiwara Prize in 1975.
