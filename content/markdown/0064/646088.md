# Sparse grid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sparse grid"}

**Sparse grids** are numerical techniques to represent, integrate or interpolate high [dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) functions. They were originally developed by the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Sergey A. Smolyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_A._Smolyak), a student of [Lazar Lyusternik](wikipedia://en/4201372), and are based on a sparse tensor product construction. Computer algorithms for efficient implementations of such grids were later developed by [Michael Griebel](wikipedia://en/21108819), [Christoph Zenger](wikipedia://en/17526934), and [Dirk Pflüger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Pflüger).

:::

## Curse of dimensionality {#Curse_of_dimensionality}

The standard way of representing multidimensional functions are tensor or full grids. The number of basis functions or nodes (grid points) that have to be stored and processed [depend exponentially](wikipedia://en/9678) on the number of dimensions.

The [curse of dimensionality](wikipedia://en/787776) is expressed in the order of the integration error that is made by a quadrature of level $l$, with $N_{l}$ points. The function has regularity $r$, i.e. is $r$ times differentiable. The number of dimensions is $d$.

$$|E_l| = O(N_l^{-\frac{r}{d}})$$

## Smolyak's quadrature rule {#Smolyak's_quadrature_rule}

Smolyak found a computationally more efficient method of integrating multidimensional functions based on a [univariate](wikipedia://en/2398982) quadrature rule $Q^{(1)}$. The $d$-dimensional Smolyak integral $Q^{(d)}$ of a function $f$ can be written as a recursion formula with the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844).

$$Q_l^{(d)} f = \left(\sum_{i=1}^l \left(Q_i^{(1)}-Q_{i-1}^{(1)}\right)\otimes Q_{l-i+1}^{(d-1)}\right)f$$

The index to $Q$ is the level of the [discretization](wikipedia://en/330017). If a 1-dimension integration on level $i$ is computed by the evaluation of $O(2^{i})$ points, the error estimate for a function of regularity $r$ will be $|E_l| = O\left(N_l^{-r}\left(\log N_l\right)^{(d-1)(r+1)}\right)$
