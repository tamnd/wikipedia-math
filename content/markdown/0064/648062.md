# G2 manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="G2 manifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a ***G*<sub>2</sub> manifold** or **Joyce manifold** is a seven-dimensional [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with [holonomy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomy_group) contained in [*G*<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864). The [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G_2$ is one of the five exceptional [simple Lie groups](wikipedia://en/292831). It can be described as the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436), or equivalently, as a proper subgroup of [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) SO(7) that preserves a [spinor](wikipedia://en/29276) in the eight-dimensional [spinor representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinor_representation) or lastly as the subgroup of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(7) which preserves the non-degenerate 3-form $\phi$, the associative form. The [Hodge dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_dual), $\psi=*\phi$ is then a parallel 4-form, the coassociative form. These forms are [calibrations](wikipedia://en/19364294) in the sense of Reese Harvey and [H. Blaine Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson),[^o2] and thus define special classes of 3- and 4-dimensional submanifolds.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

All $G_2$-manifold are 7-dimensional, [Ricci-flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci-flat), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) [spin manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold). In addition, any compact manifold with holonomy equal to $G_2$ has finite [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004), non-zero first [Pontryagin class](wikipedia://en/385944), and non-zero third and fourth [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262).

:::

## History {#History}

The fact that $G_2$ might possibly be the holonomy group of certain Riemannian 7-manifolds was first suggested by the 1955 [classification theorem](wikipedia://en/4576710) of [Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363), and this remained consistent with the simplified proof later given by [Jim Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Harris_Simons) in 1962. Although not a single example of such a manifold had yet been discovered, [Edmond Bonan](wikipedia://en/39597334) nonetheless made a useful contribution by showing that, if such a manifold did in fact exist, it would carry both a parallel 3-form and a parallel 4-form, and that it would necessarily be Ricci-flat.[^o6]

The first local examples of 7-manifolds with holonomy $G_2$ were finally constructed around 1984 by [Robert Bryant](wikipedia://en/24204031), and his full proof of their existence appeared in the Annals in 1987.[^o7] Next, complete (but still noncompact) 7-manifolds with holonomy $G_2$ were constructed by Bryant and Simon Salamon in 1989.[^o8] The first compact 7-manifolds with holonomy $G_2$ were constructed by [Dominic Joyce](wikipedia://en/1120202) in 1994. Compact $G_2$ manifolds are therefore sometimes known as "Joyce manifolds", especially in the physics literature.[^o9]

In 2015, a new construction of compact $G_2$ manifolds, due to [Alessio Corti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessio_Corti), Mark Haskins, Johannes Nordstrőm, and Tommaso Pacini, combined a gluing idea suggested by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305) with new algebro-geometric and analytic techniques for constructing [Calabi–Yau manifolds](wikipedia://en/188932) with cylindrical ends, resulting in tens of thousands of diffeomorphism types of new examples.[^o10]

## Connections to physics {#Connections_to_physics}

These manifolds are important in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305). They break the original [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636) to 1/8 of the original amount. For example, [M-theory](wikipedia://en/20406) compactified on a $G_2$ manifold leads to a realistic four-dimensional (11-7=4) theory with N=1 supersymmetry. The resulting low energy effective [supergravity](wikipedia://en/432632) contains a single supergravity [supermultiplet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermultiplet), a number of [chiral supermultiplets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_supermultiplet) equal to the third [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) of the $G_2$ manifold and a number of U(1) [vector supermultiplets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_supermultiplet) equal to the second Betti number.
