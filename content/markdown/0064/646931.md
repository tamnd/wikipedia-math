# Richard Taylor (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Taylor"}

**Richard Lawrence Taylor** (born 19 May 1962) is a British-American[^o4] mathematician specialising in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o5] He is currently the Barbara Kimball Browning Professor in Humanities and Sciences at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in California.[^o6]

:::

Taylor received the 2002 [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177), the 2007 [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) with [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986), and the 2015 [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933).

## Career {#Career}

He received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) (B.A.) degree from the [Clare College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge) of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o8][^o12] During his time at Cambridge, he was president of [The Archimedeans](wikipedia://en/49288659) in 1981 and 1982, following the resignation of his predecessor.[^o9] He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in the United States in 1988, after completing a doctoral dissertation, titled "On congruences between [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000)", under the supervision of [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027).[^o10]

He was an assistant lecturer, lecturer, and then reader at the University of Cambridge from 1988 to 1995.[^o11] From 1995 to 1996 he held the [Savilian chair of geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_chair_of_geometry)[^o8] at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and Fellow of [New College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford).[^o11][^o12] He was a professor of mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1996 to 2012, at one point becoming the [Herchel Smith Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herchel_Smith_Professor_of_Pure_Mathematics).[^o11] He moved to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) as the Robert and Luisa Fernholz Professorship from 2012 to 2019.[^o11] He has been the Barbara Kimball Browning Professor in Humanities & Sciences at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) since 2018.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

When a gap was found in the 1993 attempt by [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) to prove the semistable case of the [Taniyama-Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama-Shimura_conjecture) for elliptic curves, Wiles asked Taylor to help him clarify what was required to complete the proof. Wiles eventually realized that work of [Ehud de Shalit](wikipedia://en/60008192) could be generalized to bypass the lacuna of 1993. Wiles and Taylor proved that a constructed [Gorenstein ring](wikipedia://en/515613) that arose from this approach was also a [complete intersection](wikipedia://en/3116835).[^o14] This ring theoretic result essentially completed the proof[^o15] of the semistable case of Taniyama-Shimura, which Wiles expounded in the same issue of the [Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785). This proof strategy has been dubbed "Taylor-Wiles patching".

In subsequent work, Taylor (along with [Michael Harris](wikipedia://en/31954452)) proved the [local Langlands conjectures](wikipedia://en/7676971) for [GL(*n*)](wikipedia://en/113564) over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).[^o16] A simpler proof was suggested almost at the same time by [Guy Henniart](wikipedia://en/31961003),[^o17] and ten years later by [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306).

Taylor, together with [Christophe Breuil](wikipedia://en/20281471), [Brian Conrad](wikipedia://en/4099038) and [Fred Diamond](wikipedia://en/8356786), completed the proof of the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture), by performing quite heavy technical computations in the case of additive reduction.[^o18]

In 2008, Taylor, following the ideas of Michael Harris and building on his joint work with [Laurent Clozel](wikipedia://en/51408473), Michael Harris, and [Nick Shepherd-Barron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Shepherd-Barron), announced a proof of the [Sato–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/597837), for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) with non-integral [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988). This partial proof of the Sato–Tate conjecture uses Wiles's theorem about modularity of semistable elliptic curves.[^o19]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He received the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) in 1990, the [Fermat Prize](wikipedia://en/10919195) and the [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411) in 2001, the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2002, and the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) for Mathematics in 2007.[^o11] He received the 2015 [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933) "for numerous breakthrough results in the theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), including the [Taniyama–Weil conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Weil_conjecture), the [local Langlands conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Langlands_conjecture) for [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564), and the [Sato–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/597837)."[^o21]

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1995.[^o11] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o22] In 2015 he was inducted into the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o23] He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 2018.[^o24]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Taylor is the son of British physicist [John C. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Clayton_Taylor). He is married and has two children.[^o26]
