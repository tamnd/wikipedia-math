# Anatoly Fomenko

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anatoly Fomenko"}

**Anatoly Timofeyevich Fomenko** (Анато́лий Тимофе́евич Фоме́нко; born 13 March 1945 in [Stalino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk), [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR)) is a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) professor of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University). He is well-known as a [topologist](wikipedia://en/29954) and member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences). He is a painter and illustrator of original artworks inspired by [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) objects and structures.

:::

Fomenko is also widely known as a [conspiracy theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theorist). He originated a [pseudoscientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscientific) theory called [New Chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_chronology_%28Fomenko%29), based on works of Russian-Soviet writer [Nikolai Alexandrovich Morozov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Alexandrovich_Morozov).[^o3][^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

Fomenko is the son of Timofey Grigoryevich Fomenko, an industrial engineer, and Valentina Polikarpovna Markova, a [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology) and teacher of Russian language and literature. His parents would later co-author his works on New Chronology in 1983 and 1996. Born in what is now Donetsk, he was raised and schooled in [Magadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan). In 1959, his family returned to Eastern [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) and settled in the city of [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk), where Fomenko attended Secondary School No. 26. During secondary school, Fomenko participated in many competitions relating to mathematics and won several medals as a result. Also in 1959, the magazine *[Pionerskaya Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pionerskaya_Pravda)* (Russian: Пионерская правда, *Pioneer Truth*) published his first known science fiction story, "The Mystery of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way)".

Fomenko graduated from the Mechanics and Mathematics Department of [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1967. In 1969, he began studying [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) in the same department. In 1970, he defended his thesis "Classification of totally [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) manifolds realizing nontrivial cycles in Riemannian homogeneous spaces", and in 1972 defended his doctoral thesis, "The decision of the multidimensional plateau problems on [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652)." In December 1981, he became a professor of Higher Geometry and Topology, and in 1992, was appointed as head of Differential Geometry and Applications in the Department of Mathematics and Mechanics at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University).

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Fomenko developed the theory of topological invariants of an integrable [Hamiltonian system](wikipedia://en/1197531). He is the author of 180 scientific publications and 26 textbooks (including monographs) on mathematics. Fomenko is a specialist in geometry and topology, [variational calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_calculus), [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology), [Hamiltonian geometry and mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319), and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927).

Fomenko has served as the editor of several Russian-language mathematics journals and is a member of many councils overseeing dissertations in his field. In 1996, he won the [State Prize of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Prize_of_the_Russian_Federation) for excellence in mathematics. He is a full member (Academician) of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) (1994), the International Higher Education Academy of Sciences (1993), and the Russian Academy of Technological Sciences (2009). He is also a member of the Russian Academy of Natural Sciences (1991).

Fomenko is the author of extensive writings in his original fields of mathematics, and is also known for his original drawings inspired by [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) objects and structures.[^o7]

## Historical revisionism {#Historical_revisionism}

Fomenko is one of the authors of a concept that manipulates historical chronology called [New Chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Chronology_%28Fomenko%29). Fomenko claims to have discovered new empirico‑statistical methods and applied them to show many historical events are misdated. Based on this, he asserts that ancient history (including [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt)) merely reflects [medieval events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), while Chinese and Arab histories are fabrications by 17th‑ and 18th‑century [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus). Fomenko is the author or co‑author of several books on the analysis of historical chronicles and on the chronology of antiquity and the Middle Ages.

He also claims that [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus) lived in the 12th century A.D. and was crucified on [Joshua's Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua's_Hill); that the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War) and the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) were the same historical event; and that [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) and the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) were actually Russians, that the lands west of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) that now constitute the [American West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_West) and [Middle West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) were a far eastern part of "Siberian-American Empire" prior to its disintegration in 1775, and many other claims that contradict conventional [historiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography). As well as disputing [written chronologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_source), Fomenko also disputes scientific dating techniques such as [dendrochronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrochronology) and [radiocarbon dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating) (see Radiocarbon dating#Carbon exchange reservoir for an examination of the latter criticism). His books include *Empirico-statistical Analysis of Narrative Material and Its Applications*, and *History: Fiction or Science?*.

Most Russian scientists and worldwide historians consider Fomenko's historical works to be either [pseudoscientific](wikipedia://en/23047) or antiscientific.[^o10][^o11][^o12]

:::

## Artwork {#Artwork}

Fomenko is a painter and illustrator whose work often depicts objects from mathematics, many related to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).[^o14][^o15]
