# Hyperkähler manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperkähler manifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **hyperkähler manifold** is a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M, g)$ endowed with three [integrable almost complex structures](wikipedia://en/514258#Integrable_almost_complex_structures) $I, J, K$ that are [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538#Riemannian_viewpoint) with respect to the [Riemannian metric](wikipedia://en/195795) $g$ and satisfy the [quaternionic relations](wikipedia://en/51440) $I^2=J^2=K^2=IJK=-1$. In particular, it is a [hypercomplex manifold](wikipedia://en/6990840). All hyperkähler manifolds are [Ricci-flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci-flat) and are thus [Calabi–Yau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabi–Yau) manifolds.

:::

Hyperkähler manifolds were first given this name by [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000) in 1979.[^o2]

## Early history {#Early_history}

[Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363)'s 1955 paper[^o4] on the classification of Riemannian holonomy groups first raised the issue of the existence of non-symmetric manifolds with holonomy Sp(*n*)·Sp(1). Interesting results were proved in the mid-1960s in pioneering work by [Edmond Bonan](wikipedia://en/39597334)[^o5] and Kraines[^o6] who have independently proven that any such manifold admits a parallel 4-form $\Omega$. Bonan's later results[^o7] include a Lefschetz-type result: wedging with this powers of this 4-form induces isomorphisms $\Omega^{n-k}\wedge\bigwedge^{2k}T^*M=\bigwedge^{4n-2k}T^*M.$

## Equivalent definition in terms of holonomy {#Equivalent_definition_in_terms_of_holonomy}

Equivalently, a hyperkähler manifold is a Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$ of dimension $4n$ whose [holonomy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomy_group) is contained in the [compact symplectic group $\operatorname{Sp}(n)$](wikipedia://en/173977#Sp%28n%29).[^o2]

Indeed, if $(M, g, I, J, K)$ is a hyperkähler manifold, then the tangent space $T_{x}M$ is a [quaternionic vector space](wikipedia://en/19358248) for each point $x$ of $M$, i.e. it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}^n$ for some integer $n$, where $\mathbb{H}$ is the algebra of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440). The [compact symplectic group $\operatorname{Sp}(n)$](wikipedia://en/173977#Sp%28n%29) can be considered as the group of orthogonal transformations of $\mathbb{H}^n$ which are linear with respect to $I$, $J$ and $K$. From this, it follows that the [holonomy group](wikipedia://en/644921#Riemannian_holonomy) of the Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$ is contained in $\operatorname{Sp}(n)$. Conversely, if the holonomy group of a Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$ of dimension $4n$ is contained in $\operatorname{Sp}(n)$, choose complex structures $I_{x}$, $J_{x}$ and $K_{x}$ on $T_{x}M$ which make $T_{x}M$ into a quaternionic vector space. [Parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595) of these complex structures gives the required complex structures $I, J, K$ on $M$ making $(M, g, I, J, K)$ into a hyperkähler manifold.

## Two-sphere of complex structures {#Two-sphere_of_complex_structures}

Every hyperkähler manifold $(M, g, I, J, K)$ has a [2-sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) of [complex structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure) with respect to which the [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) $g$ is [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538). Indeed, for any real numbers $a, b, c$ such that

$$a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 1 \,$$

the linear combination

$$aI + bJ + cK \,$$

is a [complex structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure) that is Kähler with respect to $g$. If $\omega_I, \omega_J, \omega_K$ denotes the [Kähler forms](wikipedia://en/390538#Symplectic_viewpoint) of $(g, I), (g, J), (g, K)$, respectively, then the Kähler form of $aI + bJ + cK$ is

$$a \omega_I + b \omega_J + c \omega_K.$$

## Holomorphic symplectic form {#Holomorphic_symplectic_form}

A hyperkähler manifold $(M, g, I, J, K)$, considered as a complex manifold $(M, I)$, is holomorphically symplectic (equipped with a holomorphic, non-degenerate, closed 2-form). More precisely, if $\omega_I, \omega_J, \omega_K$ denotes the [Kähler forms](wikipedia://en/390538#Symplectic_viewpoint) of $(g, I), (g, J), (g, K)$, respectively, then

$$\Omega := \omega_J + i\omega_K$$

is holomorphic symplectic with respect to $I$.

Conversely, [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137)'s proof of the [Calabi conjecture](wikipedia://en/5263012) implies that a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538), holomorphically symplectic manifold $(M,I,\Omega)$ is always equipped with a compatible hyperkähler metric.[^o15] Such a metric is unique in a given Kähler class. Compact hyperkähler manifolds have been extensively studied using techniques from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), sometimes under the name *holomorphically symplectic manifolds*. The holonomy group of any Calabi–Yau metric on a simply connected compact holomorphically symplectic manifold of complex dimension $2n$ with $H^{2,0}(M)=1$ is exactly $\operatorname{Sp}(n)$; and if the simply connected Calabi–Yau manifold instead has $H^{2,0}(M)\geq 2$, it is just the [Riemannian product](wikipedia://en/144652#Product_metrics) of lower-dimensional hyperkähler manifolds. This fact immediately follows from the Bochner formula for holomorphic forms on a Kähler manifold, together the [Berger classification of holonomy groups](wikipedia://en/644921#The_Berger_classification); ironically, it is often attributed to Bogomolov, who incorrectly went on to claim in the same paper that compact hyperkähler manifolds actually do not exist!

## Examples {#Examples}

For any integer $n \ge 1$, the space $\mathbb{H}^n$ of $n$-tuples of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) endowed with the [flat Euclidean metric](wikipedia://en/144652#Euclidean_space) is a hyperkähler manifold. The first non-trivial example discovered is the [Eguchi–Hanson metric](wikipedia://en/29321898) on the cotangent bundle $T^*S^2$ of the [two-sphere](wikipedia://en/27859). It was also independently discovered by [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000), who showed the more general statement that cotangent bundle $T^*\mathbb{CP}^n$ of any [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) has a [complete](wikipedia://en/144652#Geodesic_completeness) hyperkähler metric.[^o2] More generally, Birte Feix and Dmitry Kaledin showed that the cotangent bundle of any [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) has a hyperkähler structure on a [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of its [zero section](wikipedia://en/276524#Sections_and_locally_free_sheaves), although it is generally incomplete.[^o13][^o14]

Due to [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326)'s classification of complex surfaces, we know that any [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) hyperkähler 4-manifold is either a [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116) or a compact [torus](wikipedia://en/74800#n-dimensional_torus) $T^4$. (Every [Calabi–Yau manifold](wikipedia://en/188932) in 4 (real) dimensions is a hyperkähler manifold, because $\operatorname{SU}(2)$ is isomorphic to $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$.)

As was shown by [Beauville](wikipedia://en/28767637), the [Hilbert scheme](wikipedia://en/6612581) of $k$ points on a compact hyperkähler 4-manifold is a hyperkähler manifold of dimension $4k$.[^o15] This gives rise to two series of compact examples: Hilbert schemes of points on a K3 surface and [generalized Kummer varieties](wikipedia://en/6612581).

Non-compact, complete, hyperkähler 4-manifolds which are asymptotic to $\mathbf{H}/G$, where $\mathbf{H}$ denotes the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and $G$ is a finite [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$, are known as [asymptotically locally Euclidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotically_locally_Euclidean), or ALE, spaces. These spaces, and various generalizations involving different asymptotic behaviors, are studied in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) under the name [gravitational instantons](wikipedia://en/2618270). The [Gibbons–Hawking ansatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbons–Hawking_ansatz) gives examples invariant under a circle action.

Many examples of noncompact hyperkähler manifolds arise as moduli spaces of solutions to certain gauge theory equations which arise from the dimensional reduction of the anti-self dual [Yang–Mills equations](wikipedia://en/672202): instanton moduli spaces,[^o16] [monopole moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/35169943),[^o17] spaces of solutions to [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164)'s [self-duality equations](wikipedia://en/68430334) on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181),[^o18] space of solutions to [Nahm equations](wikipedia://en/10451852). Another class of examples are the [Nakajima](wikipedia://en/4383763) [quiver](wikipedia://en/355100) varieties,[^o19] which are of great importance in representation theory.

:::

## Cohomology {#Cohomology}

(Kurnosov Soldatenkov Verbitsky 2019) show that the cohomology of any compact hyperkähler manifold embeds into the cohomology of a torus, in a way that preserves the [Hodge structure](wikipedia://en/10297305).
