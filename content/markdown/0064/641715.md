# Limaçon

*Image: EpitrochoidIn1.gif.* Construction of the limaçon $r = 2 + \cos(\pi - \theta)$ with [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates)' origin at $(x, y) = (\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Limaçon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **limaçon** or **limacon** (), also known as a **limaçon of Pascal** or **Pascal's Snail**, is defined as a [roulette curve](wikipedia://en/846500) formed by the path of a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) fixed to a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) when that circle [rolls](wikipedia://en/1156993) around the outside of a circle of equal radius. It can also be defined as the roulette formed when a circle rolls around a circle with half its radius so that the smaller circle is inside the larger circle. Thus, they belong to the family of curves called [centered trochoids](wikipedia://en/18079550); more specifically, they are [epitrochoids](wikipedia://en/1547292). The **[cardioid](wikipedia://en/642101)** is the special case in which the point generating the roulette lies on the rolling circle; the resulting curve has a [cusp](wikipedia://en/6851758).

:::

Depending on the position of the point generating the curve, it may have inner and outer loops (giving the family its name), it may be [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_%28symbol%29)-shaped, or it may be oval.

A limaçon is a [bicircular](wikipedia://en/24215692) rational plane [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) 4.

## History {#History}

The earliest formal research on limaçons is generally attributed to [Étienne Pascal](wikipedia://en/2121127), father of [Blaise Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068). However, some insightful investigations regarding them had been undertaken earlier by the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) artist [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_Dürer). Dürer's *Underweysung der Messung (Instruction in Measurement)* contains specific geometric methods for producing limaçons. The curve was named by [Gilles de Roberval](wikipedia://en/196880) when he used it as an example for finding tangent lines, deriving the word from the latin *limax*: snail.

## Equations {#Equations}

*Image: Limacons.svg.* Three limaçons: dimpled, with cusp (a [cardioid](wikipedia://en/642101)), and looped. Not shown: the [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) limaçon

The equation (up to translation and rotation) of a limaçon in [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates) has the form

$$r = b + a \cos \theta.$$

This can be converted to [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) by multiplying by *r* (thus introducing a point at the origin which in some cases is spurious), and substituting $r^2 = x^2+y^2$ and $r \cos \theta = x$ to obtain[^o4]

$$\left(x^2 + y^2 - ax\right)^2 = b^2\left(x^2 + y^2\right).$$

Applying the parametric form of the polar to Cartesian conversion, we also have[^o5]

$$x = (b + a\cos \theta)\cos \theta = {a\over 2} + b \cos \theta + {a\over 2} \cos 2 \theta,$$

$$y = (b + a\cos \theta)\sin \theta = b \sin \theta + {a\over 2} \sin 2 \theta;$$

while setting

$$z = x + i y = ( b + a \cos \theta )( \cos \theta + i \sin \theta )$$

yields this parameterization as a curve in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628):

$$z = {a \over 2} + b e^{i\theta} + {a\over 2} e^{2i\theta}.$$

If we were to shift horizontally by $-\frac{1}{2}a$, i.e.,

$$z = b e^{it} + {a \over 2} e^{2it},$$

we would, by changing the location of the origin, convert to the usual form of the equation of a centered trochoid. Note the change of independent variable at this point to make it clear that we are no longer using the default polar coordinate parameterization $\theta = \arg z$.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

### Special cases {#Special_cases}

In the special case $a = b$, the polar equation is

$$r = b(1 + \cos \theta) = 2b\cos^2 \frac{\theta}{2}$$

or

$$r^{1 \over 2} = (2b)^{1 \over 2} \cos \frac{\theta}{2},$$

making it a member of the [sinusoidal spiral](wikipedia://en/18633469) family of curves. This curve is the [cardioid](wikipedia://en/642101).

In the special case $a = 2b$, the centered trochoid form of the equation becomes

$$z = b \left(e^{it} + e^{2it}\right) = b e^{3it\over 2} \left(e^{it\over 2} + e^{-it\over 2}\right) = 2b e^{3it\over 2} \cos{t \over 2},$$

or, in polar coordinates,

$$r = 2b\cos{\theta \over 3}$$

making it a member of the [rose](wikipedia://en/727472) family of curves. This curve is a [trisectrix](wikipedia://en/2909893), and is sometimes called the [limaçon trisectrix](wikipedia://en/18635261).

:::

## Form {#Form}

When $b > a$, the limaçon is a simple closed curve. However, the origin satisfies the Cartesian equation given above, so the graph of this equation has an [acnode](wikipedia://en/3090256) or isolated point.

When $b > 2a$, the area bounded by the curve is convex, and when $a < b < 2a$, the curve has an indentation bounded by two [inflection points](wikipedia://en/379845). At $b = 2a$, the point $(-a, 0)$ is a point of 0 [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770).

As $b$ is decreased relative to $a$, the indentation becomes more pronounced until, at $b = a$, the curve becomes a cardioid, and the indentation becomes a [cusp](wikipedia://en/6851758). For $0 < b < a$, the cusp expands to an inner loop, and the curve crosses itself at the origin. As $b$ approaches 0, the loop fills up the outer curve and, in the limit, the limaçon becomes a circle traversed twice.

## Measurement {#Measurement}

The area enclosed by the limaçon $r = b + a \cos \theta$ is $\left(b^2 + {{a^2} \over 2}\right)\pi$. When $b < a$ this counts the area enclosed by the inner loop twice. In this case the curve crosses the origin at angles $\pi \pm \arccos {b \over a}$, the area enclosed by the inner loop is

$$\left (b^2 + {{a^2}\over 2} \right )\arccos {b \over a} - {3\over 2} b \sqrt{a^2 - b^2},$$

the area enclosed by the outer loop is

$$\left(b^2 + {{a^2}\over 2} \right ) \left (\pi - \arccos {b \over a} \right ) + {3\over 2} b \sqrt{a^2 -b^2},$$

and the area between the loops is

$\left (b^2 + {{a^2}\over 2} \right ) \left (\pi - 2\arccos {b \over a} \right ) + 3b \sqrt{a^2 -b^2}.$[^o4]

The circumference of the limaçon is given by a [complete elliptic integral of the second kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_Integral):

$$4(a + b)E\left({{2\sqrt{ab}} \over a + b}\right).$$

## Relation to other curves {#Relation_to_other_curves}

- Let $P$ be a point and $C$ be a circle whose center is not $P$. Then the envelope of those circles whose center lies on $C$ and that pass through $P$ is a limaçon.

*Image: PedalCurve2.gif.* Limaçon — [pedal curve](wikipedia://en/845864) of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220)

- A [pedal](wikipedia://en/845864) of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) is a limaçon. In fact, the pedal with respect to the origin of the circle with radius $b$ and center $(a, 0)$ has polar equation $r = b + a \cos \theta$.
- The [inverse](wikipedia://en/295844#Inversion_of_an_algebraic_curve) with respect to the unit circle of $r = b + a \cos \theta$ is

$$r = {1 \over {b + a \cos \theta}}$$

which is the equation of a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673) with eccentricity $\tfrac{a}{b}$ and focus at the origin. Thus a limaçon can be defined as the inverse of a conic where the center of inversion is one of the foci. If the conic is a parabola then the inverse will be a cardioid, if the conic is a hyperbola then the corresponding limaçon will have an inner loop, and if the conic is an ellipse then the corresponding limaçon will have no loop.

- The [conchoid](wikipedia://en/898856) of a circle with respect to a point on the circle is a limaçon.
- A particular special case of a [Cartesian oval](wikipedia://en/29131481) is a limaçon.
