# Spin foam

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spin foam"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) structure of **spinfoam** or **spin foam**[^o2] consists of two-dimensional faces representing a configuration required by [functional integration](wikipedia://en/301521) to obtain a [Feynman's path integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman's_path_integral) description of [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312). These structures are employed in [loop quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/152664) as a version of [quantum foam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_foam).

:::

## In loop quantum gravity {#In_loop_quantum_gravity}

The [covariant formulation](wikipedia://en/418156) of loop quantum gravity provides the best formulation of the dynamics of the theory of [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312) – a [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) where the invariance under [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/681875) of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) applies. The resulting path integral represents a sum over all the possible configurations of spin foam.

### Spin network {#Spin_network}

A spin network is a two-dimensional [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), together with labels on its vertices and edges which encode aspects of a spatial geometry.

A spin network is defined as a diagram like the [Feynman diagram](wikipedia://en/11617) which makes a basis of [connections](wikipedia://en/308815) between the elements of a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) for the [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932) defined over them, and for computations of amplitudes between two different [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101) of the [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). Any evolution of the spin network provides a spin foam over a manifold of one dimension higher than the dimensions of the corresponding spin network. A spin foam is analogous to [quantum history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_history).

### Spacetime {#Spacetime}

Spin networks provide a language to describe the [quantum geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_geometry) of space. Spin foam does the same job for spacetime.

Spacetime can be defined as a superposition of spin foams, which is a generalized Feynman diagram where instead of a graph, a higher-dimensional complex is used. In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) this sort of space is called a 2-[complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_complex). A spin foam is a particular type of 2-complex, with labels for [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), edges and [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). The boundary of a spin foam is a spin network, just as in the theory of manifolds, where the boundary of an n-manifold is an (n-1)-manifold.

In loop quantum gravity, the present spin foam theory has been inspired by the [Ponzano–Regge model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponzano–Regge_model). The idea was introduced by Reisenberger and [Rovelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Rovelli) in 1997,[^o6] and later developed into the [Barrett–Crane model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrett–Crane_model). The formulation that is used nowadays is commonly called EPRL after the names of the authors of a series of seminal papers,[^o7] but the theory has also seen fundamental contributions from the work of many others, such as [Laurent Freidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Freidel) (FK model) and [Jerzy Lewandowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Lewandowski) (KKL model).

## Definition {#Definition}

The summary partition function for a **spin foam model** is

$$Z:=\sum_{\Gamma}w(\Gamma)\left[ \sum_{j_f,i_e}\prod_f A_f(j_f) \prod_e A_e(j_f,i_e)\prod_v A_v(j_f,i_e) \right]$$

with:

- a set of 2-complexes $\Gamma$ each consisting out of faces $f$, edges $e$ and vertices $v$. Associated to each 2-complex $\Gamma$ is a weight $w(\Gamma)$
- a set of irreducible representations $j$ which label the faces and intertwiners $i$ which label the edges.
- a vertex amplitude $A_v(j_f,i_e)$ and an edge amplitude $A_e(j_f,i_e)$
- a face amplitude $A_f(j_f)$, for which we almost always have $A_f(j_f)=\dim(j_f)$
