# Transfer (group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transfer"}

In the mathematical field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **transfer** defines, given a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* and a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581), a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) from *G* to the [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) of *H*. It can be used in conjunction with the [Sylow theorems](wikipedia://en/53993) to obtain certain numerical results on the existence of finite simple groups.

:::

The transfer was defined by (txt) and rediscovered by (txt).[^o14]

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The construction of the map proceeds as follows:[^o6] Let \[*G*:*H*\] = *n* and select [coset](wikipedia://en/98759) [representatives](wikipedia://en/43066470#Transversals_of_a_subgroup), say

$$x_1, \dots, x_n,\,$$

for *H* in *G*, so *G* can be written as a disjoint union

$$G = \bigcup\ x_i H.$$

Given *y* in *G*, each *yx<sub>i</sub>* is in some coset *x<sub>j</sub>H* and so

$$yx_i = x_jh_i$$

for some index *j* and some element *h*<sub>*i*</sub> of *H*. The value of the transfer for *y* is defined to be the image of the product

$$\textstyle \prod_{i=1}^n h_i$$

in *H*/*H*′, where *H*′ is the commutator subgroup of *H*. The order of the factors is irrelevant since *H*/*H*′ is abelian.

It is [straightforward](wikipedia://en/43066470#Independence_of_the_transversal) to show that, though the individual *h<sub>i</sub>* depends on the choice of coset representatives, the value of the transfer does not. It is also [straightforward](wikipedia://en/43066470#Homomorphisms) to show that the mapping defined this way is a homomorphism.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

If *G* is cyclic then the transfer takes any element *y* of *G* to *y*<sup>\[*G*:*H*\]</sup>.

A simple case is that seen in the [Gauss lemma](wikipedia://en/2310978) on [quadratic residues](wikipedia://en/200091), which in effect computes the transfer for the multiplicative group of non-zero [residue classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_class) modulo a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*, with respect to the subgroup {1, −1}.[^o14] One advantage of looking at it that way is the ease with which the correct generalisation can be found, for example for cubic residues in the case that *p* − 1 is divisible by three.

:::

## Homological interpretation {#Homological_interpretation}

This homomorphism may be set in the context of [group homology](wikipedia://en/248635#Group_homology). In general, given any subgroup *H* of *G* and any *G*-module *A*, there is a corestriction map of homology groups $\mathrm{Cor} : H_n(H,A) \to H_n(G,A)$ induced by the inclusion map $i: H \to G$, but if we have that *H* is of finite index in *G*, there are also restriction maps $\mathrm{Res} : H_n(G,A) \to H_n(H,A)$. In the case of *n =* 1 and $A=\mathbb{Z}$ with the trivial *G*-module structure, we have the map $\mathrm{Res} : H_1(G,\mathbb{Z}) \to H_1(H,\mathbb{Z})$. Noting that $H_1(G,\mathbb{Z})$ may be identified with $G/G'$ where $G'$ is the commutator subgroup, this gives the transfer map via $G \xrightarrow{\pi} G/G' \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Res}} H/H'$, with $\pi$ denoting the natural projection.[^o10] The transfer is also seen in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), when it is defined between [classifying spaces](wikipedia://en/923556) of groups.

::: {#o12 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

The name *transfer* translates the German *Verlagerung*, which was coined by [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118).

:::

## Commutator subgroup {#Commutator_subgroup}

If *G* is finitely generated, the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) *G*′ of *G* has finite index in *G* and *H=G*′, then the corresponding transfer map is trivial. In other words, the map sends *G* to 0 in the abelianization of *G*′. This is important in proving the [principal ideal theorem](wikipedia://en/3136164) in [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695).[^o14] See the [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351)-[John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) *Class Field Theory* notes.
