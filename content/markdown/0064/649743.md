# Fundamental representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fundamental representation"}

In [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), a **fundamental representation** is an [irreducible finite-dimensional representation](wikipedia://en/385982) of a [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) Lie group or Lie algebra whose [highest weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight) is a [fundamental weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_weight). For example, the defining module of a [classical Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Lie_group) is a fundamental representation. Any finite-dimensional irreducible representation of a semisimple Lie group or Lie algebra can be constructed from the fundamental representations by a procedure due to [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274). Thus in a certain sense, the fundamental representations are the elementary building blocks for arbitrary finite-dimensional representations.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- In the case of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564), all fundamental representations are [exterior products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_product) of the defining module.
- In the case of the special unitary group [SU(*n*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%28n%29), the *n* − 1 fundamental representations are the wedge products $\operatorname{Alt}^k\ {\mathbb C}^n$ consisting of the [alternating tensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_tensor), for *k* = 1, 2, ..., *n* − 1.
- The [spin representation](wikipedia://en/10160091) of the twofold cover of an odd [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954), the odd [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231), and the two half-spin representations of the twofold cover of an even orthogonal group, the even spinor group, are fundamental representations that cannot be realized in the space of tensors.
- The [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_group) of the simple Lie group of type [E<sub>8</sub>](wikipedia://en/647994) is a fundamental representation.

:::

## Explanation {#Explanation}

The [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) of a [simply-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply-connected) [compact](wikipedia://en/750326) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) are indexed by their highest [weights](wikipedia://en/277179). These weights are the lattice points in an [orthant](wikipedia://en/1283725) *Q*<sub>\+</sub> in the [weight lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_lattice) of the Lie group consisting of the dominant integral weights. It can be proved that there exists a set of *fundamental weights*, indexed by the vertices of the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109), such that any dominant integral weight is a non-negative integer linear combination of the fundamental weights.[^o5] The corresponding irreducible representations are the **fundamental representations** of the Lie group. From the expansion of a dominant weight in terms of the fundamental weights one can take a corresponding tensor product of the fundamental representations and extract one copy of the irreducible representation corresponding to that dominant weight.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Anti-fundamental"}

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the term *fundamental representation* is also used to refer to the *defining representation* of a [matrix Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_Lie_group) or [matrix Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_Lie_algebra),[^o9][^o10] that is, its standard representation as a subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})$ or subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})$.[^o11][^o12] The [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/1442618) of the fundamental representation is known as the *antifundamental* (or *anti-fundamental*) *representation*.[^o10][^o13] Although the distinction between the fundamental and the antifundamental representation is a matter of convention, these two are often non-equivalent, because each of them is a [complex representation](wikipedia://en/695060).

:::
