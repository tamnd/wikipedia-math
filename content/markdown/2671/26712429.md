# Local Tate duality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local Tate duality"}

In [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315), **local Tate duality** (or simply **local duality**) is a [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) for [Galois modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module) for the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of a [non-archimedean local field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-archimedean_local_field). It is named after [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) who first proved it. It shows that the dual of such a Galois module is the [Tate twist](wikipedia://en/3094946) of usual linear dual. This new dual is called the (**local**) **Tate dual**.

:::

Local duality combined with Tate's [local Euler characteristic formula](wikipedia://en/26723418) provide a versatile set of tools for computing the Galois cohomology of local fields.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *K* be a non-archimedean local field, let *K<sup>s</sup>* denote a [separable closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_closure) of *K*, and let *G<sub>K</sub>* = Gal(*K<sup>s</sup>*/*K*) be the absolute Galois group of *K*.

### Case of finite modules {#Case_of_finite_modules}

Denote by μ the Galois module of all [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) in *K<sup>s</sup>*. Given a finite *G<sub>K</sub>*-module *A* of order prime to the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of *K*, the Tate dual of *A* is defined as

$$A^\prime=\mathrm{Hom}(A,\mu)$$

(i.e. it is the Tate twist of the usual dual *A*<sup>∗</sup>). Let *H<sup>i</sup>*(*K*, *A*) denote the [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) of *G<sub>K</sub>* with coefficients in *A*. The theorem states that the pairing

$$H^i(K,A)\times H^{2-i}(K,A^\prime)\rightarrow H^2(K,\mu)=\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}$$

given by the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) sets up a duality between *H<sup>i</sup>*(*K*, *A*) and *H*<sup>2−*i*</sup>(*K*, *A*<sup>′</sup>) for *i* = 0, 1, 2.[^o5] Since *G<sub>K</sub>* has [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) equal to two, the higher cohomology groups vanish.[^o6]

### Case of *p*-adic representations {#Case_of_p-adic_representations}

Let *p* be a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). Let **Q**<sub>*p*</sub>(1) denote the [*p*-adic cyclotomic character](wikipedia://en/18846136) of *G<sub>K</sub>* (i.e. the [Tate module](wikipedia://en/3094921) of μ). A [*p*-adic representation](wikipedia://en/486929) of *G<sub>K</sub>* is a [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) [representation](wikipedia://en/12695)

$$\rho:G_K\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)$$

where *V* is a [finite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) **Q**<sub>*p*</sub> and GL(*V*) denotes the group of [invertible linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) from *V* to itself.[^o8] The Tate dual of *V* is defined as

$$V^\prime=\mathrm{Hom}(V,\mathbf{Q}_p(1))$$

(i.e. it is the Tate twist of the usual dual *V*<sup>∗</sup> = Hom(*V*, **Q**<sub>*p*</sub>)). In this case, *H<sup>i</sup>*(*K*, *V*) denotes the [continuous group cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_group_cohomology) of *G<sub>K</sub>* with coefficients in *V*. Local Tate duality applied to *V* says that the cup product induces a pairing

$$H^i(K,V)\times H^{2-i}(K,V^\prime)\rightarrow H^2(K,\mathbf{Q}_p(1))=\mathbf{Q}_p$$

which is a duality between *H<sup>i</sup>*(*K*, *V*) and *H*<sup>2−*i*</sup>(*K*, *V* ′) for *i* = 0, 1, 2.[^o9] Again, the higher cohomology groups vanish.
