# Unknotting number

*Image: Unknotting trefoil.svg.* Trefoil knot without 3-fold symmetry being unknotted by one crossing switch.

*Image: Unknotting Whitehead link.svg.* [Whitehead link](wikipedia://en/1966668) being unknotted by undoing one crossing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unknotting number"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) area of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **unknotting number** of a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) is the minimum number of times the knot must be passed through itself (**crossing switch**) to untie it. If a knot has unknotting number $n$, then there exists a [diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram) of the knot which can be changed to [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) by switching $n$ crossings.[^o2] The unknotting number of a knot is always less than half of its [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598).[^o3] This [invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) was first defined by Hilmar Wendt in 1936.[^o4]

:::

Any [composite knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_knot) has unknotting number at least two, and therefore every knot with unknotting number one is a [prime knot](wikipedia://en/315030). The unknotting number is not additive under [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134),[^o5] although that possibility, implicit in \[Wendt,1937[^o4]\] and explicitly asked by Gordon in 1977[^o6] and many others, was not resolved until 2025. A counterexample showed that the unknotting number of the connected sum of [7<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/26657115) and its mirror image was at most 5, one less than the sum of the numbers from its components.[^o7]

In general, it is relatively difficult to determine the unknotting number of a given knot. Known cases include:

- The unknotting number of a nontrivial [twist knot](wikipedia://en/26701261) is always equal to one.
- The unknotting number of a $(p,q)$-[torus knot](wikipedia://en/1165182) is equal to $(p-1)(q-1)/2$.[^o8]
- The unknotting numbers of [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) with nine or fewer [crossings](wikipedia://en/18298598) have all been determined. (The unknotting number of the 10<sub>11</sub> prime knot is unknown.)
