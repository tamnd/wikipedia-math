# Weitzenböck identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weitzenböck identity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), a **Weitzenböck identity**, named after [Roland Weitzenböck](wikipedia://en/35767598), expresses a relationship between two second-order [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) on a [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_%28mathematics%29) with the same principal symbol. Usually Weitzenböck formulae are implemented for *G*-invariant [self-adjoint operators](wikipedia://en/187408) between [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) associated to some [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950), although the precise conditions under which such a formula exists are difficult to formulate. This article focuses on three examples of Weitzenböck identities: from [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), [spin geometry](wikipedia://en/32013990), and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).

:::

## Riemannian geometry {#Riemannian_geometry}

In [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) there are two notions of the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) on [differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms) over an oriented compact Riemannian manifold *M*. The first definition uses the [divergence operator](wikipedia://en/8328) *δ* defined as the formal adjoint of the de Rham operator *d*:

$$\int_M \langle \alpha,\delta\beta\rangle := \int_M\langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle$$

where *α* is any *p*-form and *β* is any (*p* + 1)-form, and $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ is the metric induced on the bundle of (*p* + 1)-forms. The usual **form Laplacian** is then given by

$$\Delta = d\delta +\delta d.$$

On the other hand, the [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176) supplies a differential operator

$$\nabla:\Omega^p M \rightarrow \Omega^1 M \otimes \Omega^p M ,$$

where Ω<sup>*p*</sup>*M* is the bundle of *p*-forms. The **Bochner Laplacian** is given by

$$\Delta'=\nabla^*\nabla$$

where $\nabla^*$ is the adjoint of $\nabla$. This is also known as the connection or rough Laplacian.

The Weitzenböck formula then asserts that

$$\Delta' - \Delta = A$$

where *A* is a linear operator of order zero involving only the curvature.

The precise form of *A* is given, up to an overall sign depending on curvature conventions, by

$$A=\frac{1}{2}\langle R(\theta,\theta)\#,\#\rangle + \operatorname{Ric}(\theta,\#) ,$$

where

- *R* is the Riemann curvature tensor,
- Ric is the Ricci tensor,
- $\theta:T^*M\otimes\Omega^pM\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1}M$ is the map that takes the wedge product of a 1-form and *p*-form and gives a (*p*+1)-form,
- $\#:\Omega^{p+1}M\rightarrow T^*M\otimes\Omega^pM$ is the universal derivation inverse to *θ* on 1-forms.

## Spin geometry {#Spin_geometry}

If *M* is an oriented [spin manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold) with [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448) ð, then one may form the spin Laplacian Δ = ð<sup>2</sup> on the spin bundle. On the other hand, the Levi-Civita connection extends to the spin bundle to yield a differential operator

$$\nabla:\Gamma(SM)\rightarrow \Gamma(T^*M\otimes SM).$$

As in the case of Riemannian manifolds, let $\Delta'=\nabla^*\nabla$. This is another self-adjoint operator and, moreover, has the same leading symbol as the spin Laplacian. The Weitzenböck formula yields:

$$\Delta' - \Delta = -\frac{1}{4}Sc$$

where *Sc* is the scalar curvature. This result is also known as the [Lichnerowicz formula](wikipedia://en/18146910).

## Complex differential geometry {#Complex_differential_geometry}

If *M* is a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538), there is a Weitzenböck formula relating the $\bar{\partial}$-Laplacian (see [Dolbeault complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolbeault_complex)) and the Euclidean Laplacian on [(*p*,*q*)-forms](wikipedia://en/2497815). Specifically, let

$$\Delta = \bar{\partial}^*\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^*,$$

and

$$\Delta' = -\sum_k\nabla_k\nabla_{\bar{k}}$$

in a unitary frame at each point.

According to the Weitzenböck formula, if $\alpha\in\Omega^{(p,q)}M$, then

$$\Delta^\prime\alpha-\Delta\alpha=A(\alpha)$$

where $A$ is an operator of order zero involving the curvature. Specifically, if

$$\alpha = \alpha_{i_1 i_2 \dots i_p\bar{j}_1 \bar{j}_2 \dots \bar{j}_q}$$

in a unitary frame, then

$$A(\alpha) = -\sum_{k,j_s} \operatorname{Ric}_{\bar{j}_\alpha}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{i_1 i_2 \dots i_p \bar{j}_1 \bar{j}_2 \dots \bar{k}\dots \bar{j}_q}$$

with *k* in the *s*-th place.
