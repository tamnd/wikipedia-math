# Rajeev Motwani

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Rajeev Motwani"}

**Rajeev Motwani** ([Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi): राजीव मोटवानी , 24 March 1962 – 5 June 2009) was an Indian-American professor of computer science at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) whose research focused on [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392). He was a special advisor to [Sequoia Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequoia_Capital). He was a winner of the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) in 2001.[^o6]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rajeev Motwani was born in [Jammu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_%28city%29), [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_%28union_territory%29), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India), on 24 March 1962, and grew up in [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi).[^o8] His father was in the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army). He had two brothers. As a child, inspired by luminaries like [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss), he wanted to become a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Motwani went to [St Columba's School, New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Columba's_School_%28Delhi%29). He completed his B.Tech. in [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science) from the [Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Kanpur) in [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur), [Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh) in 1983 and got his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in computer science from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California), [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1988, under the supervision of [Richard M. Karp](wikipedia://en/298763).[^o2]

## Career {#Career}

Motwani joined Stanford soon after U.C. Berkeley. He founded the Mining Data at Stanford project (MIDAS), an umbrella organization for several groups looking into new and innovative data management concepts. His research included [data privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_privacy), [web search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_search), [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics), and [computational drug design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_design#computer-assisted_drug_design). He is also one of the originators of the [Locality-sensitive hashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locality-sensitive_hashing) algorithm.

Motwani was one of the co-authors (with [Larry Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Page) and [Sergey Brin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Brin), and [Terry Winograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Winograd)) of an influential early paper on the [PageRank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PageRank) [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775). He also co-authored another seminal search paper *What Can You Do With A Web In Your Pocket* with those same authors.[^o10] PageRank was the basis for search techniques of Google (founded by Page and Brin), and Motwani advised or taught many of Google's developers and researchers,[^o11] including the first employee, [Craig Silverstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Silverstein).[^o12]

He was an author of two widely used theoretical computer science textbooks: *Randomized Algorithms* with [Prabhakar Raghavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabhakar_Raghavan)[^o13] and *[Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages, and Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_to_Automata_Theory,_Languages,_and_Computation)* with [John Hopcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hopcroft) and [Jeffrey Ullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Ullman).[^o14]

He was an avid [angel investor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_investor) and helped fund a number of startups to emerge from Stanford. He sat on boards including Google, Kaboodle, Mimosa Systems (acquired by [Iron Mountain Incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Mountain_Incorporated)), Adchemy, [Baynote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baynote), [Vuclip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuclip), NeoPath Networks (acquired by [Cisco Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco_Systems) in 2007), [Tapulous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapulous) and [Stanford Student Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StartX). He was active in the Business Association of Stanford Entrepreneurial Students (BASES).[^o15][^o16][^o17]

He was a winner of the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) in 2001 for his work on the [PCP theorem](wikipedia://en/3001241) and its applications to [hardness of approximation](wikipedia://en/20677277).[^o18][^o19]

## Death {#Death}

Motwani was found dead in his pool in the backyard of his [Atherton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atherton,_California), [San Mateo County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mateo_County,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California) home on 5 June 2009. The [San Mateo County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mateo_County,_California) coroner, [Robert Foucrault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Foucrault), ruled the death an accidental drowning. Toxicology tests showed that Motwani's [blood alcohol content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_alcohol_content) was 0.26 percent.[^o21] He could not swim, but was planning on taking lessons, according to his friends.[^o22]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Motwani, and his wife Asha Jadeja Motwani, had two daughters named Naitri and Anya.[^o24] After his death, his family donated US\$1.5 million in 2011 and a building was named in his honor at IIT Kanpur.[^o25]
