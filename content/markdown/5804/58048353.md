# James Porter (Master of Peterhouse, Cambridge)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Porter"}

*Image: James Porter by WW Ouless.jpg.* James Porter by [Walter William Ouless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_William_Ouless)

**James Porter** (2 October 1827, [Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast) – 2 October 1900, [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge))[^o2] was a British academic[^o3] in the second half of the 19th century.

:::

Porter was born on 2 October 1827 in Belfast, the son of Rev. James Porter, Presbyterian minister, Rector of Drumlee, [Castlewellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlewellan), [County Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Down). Porter's siblings were:

- [William Archer Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Archer_Porter), a [lawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyer) and [educationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educationist) who served as the principal of [Government Arts College, Kumbakonam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Arts_College,_Kumbakonam) and tutor and secretary to the Maharaja of [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_kingdom);
- Jane Bailie Porter, who married [Alexander Crum Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Crum_Brown), the Scottish organic chemist;
- Margaret Archer Porter, who married [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait), the physicist.

Porter graduated [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Scotland%29) from the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) in 1847, and continued to [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge) (admitted 8 July 1847, graduated [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) as [9th wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) 1851, [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxford,_Cambridge,_and_Dublin%29) 1854, [D.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity) 1881).[^o5]

Porter taught mathematics at [Liverpool College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_College)[^o6] 1851–55, and was ordained deacon in 1853 and priest in 1856.[^o5]

He was to spend the rest of his career at Peterhouse. He was appointed [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) in 1853; [Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) in 1856; [Bursar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursar) in 1861; Assistant Tutor in 1863; [Tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor) 1866–76; and Master 1876–1900. He also served as [Senior proctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_proctor) in 1868–69 and 1876–77; [Vicar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicar) of [Cherry Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Hinton) 1880–82;[^o5] and [Vice-Chancellor of the University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chancellor_of_the_University_of_Cambridge) 1881–84.[^o7]

"He was endowed with a great activity both mental and physical, which found expression on the one hand in a keen participation in athletic sports, and on the other in whole-hearted efforts to promote the highest interests of the University."[^o8] In Dr T. A. Walker's History of Peterhouse (1906) the Rev. James Porter is described as a “man of notable business qualifications and of a rare generosity of spirit.”[^o9]
