# John Meadows Jackson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Meadows Jackson"}

Dr **John Meadows Jackson** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) [FIMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Institute_of_Mathematics_and_its_Applications) [FRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society) (1907–1998) was a British mathematician.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Chorlton-cum-Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorlton-cum-Hardy) on the edge of [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester) on 8 February 1907. His mother died during his birth and he was raised by his grandparents. He was educated at [Manchester Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Grammar_School) having won a scholarship on the basis of his intellect.

He obtained two BSc degrees at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London): Physics in 1927 and Mathematics in 1928. He won a further scholarship, enabling him to undertake postgraduate studies at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), under Sir [Nevill Mott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevill_Mott) where he obtained a PhD in Physics in 1933. He then took a job as Assistant Lecturer at [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester). He began lecturing in Mathematics at [Westfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_College) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) in 1937. At that time the college was exclusively for women.[^o3]

His career was interrupted by the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). Westfield College was relocated to Oxfordshire in 1939 to avoid bombing. In 1941 he was seconded to the [Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_mine) Design Department of the Admiralty based in [Helensburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensburgh). The department later relocated to [Fettes College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fettes_College) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) where he worked on the design of mine-sweeping equipment. At the end of the war the family decided not to return to London but to stay in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland).[^o3] In January 1946 he began lecturing in Mathematics at [University College, Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Dundee), initially living with his family in [Wormit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormit) in Fife on the opposite side of the Tay estuary. He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1947. His proposers were [Edward Copson](wikipedia://en/8918906), [George Dawson Preston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Dawson_Preston), [Robert Campbell Garry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Campbell_Garry), and [Robert Percival Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Percival_Cook).[^o4]

He retired in 1974 and died at his home, the Cedars on Perth Road in Dundee, on 23 March 1998.

## Family {#Family}

In 1940 he was married to Pat Harris, a lecturer in Chemistry whom he had met at a conference in 1938. They had three daughters: Kathleen, Esther and Margaret.

## Publications {#Publications}
