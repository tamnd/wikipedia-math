# Struve function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Struve function"}

*Image: Mplwp Struve function05.svg.* Graph of $\mathrm{H}_n(x)$ for $n\in [0,1,2,3,4,5]$

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Struve functions** $\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}(x)$, are solutions $y(x)$ of the non-homogeneous [Bessel's differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel's_differential_equation):

:::

$$x^2 \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} + x \frac{dy}{dx} + \left (x^2 - \alpha^2 \right )y = \frac{4\left (\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\alpha+1}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma \left (\alpha+\frac{1}{2} \right )}$$

introduced by (txt). The [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) α is the **order** of the Struve function, and is often an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563).

And further defined its second-kind version $\mathbf{K}_\alpha(x)$ as $\mathbf{K}_\alpha(x)=\mathbf{H}_\alpha(x)-Y_\alpha(x)$, where $Y_\alpha(x)$ is the [Neumann function](wikipedia://en/4700#Bessel_functions_of_the_second_kind_:_Y.CE.B1).

The **modified Struve functions** $\mathbf{L}_{\alpha}(x)$ are equal to $-ie^{-i\alpha\pi / 2}\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}(ix)$ and are solutions $y(x)$ of the non-homogeneous [Bessel's differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel's_differential_equation):

*Image: Plot of the Struve function H n(z) with n=2 in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D.svg.* Plot of the Struve function H n(z) with n=2 in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1 function ComplexPlot3D

$$x^2 \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} + x \frac{dy}{dx} - \left (x^2 + \alpha^2 \right )y = \frac{4\left (\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\alpha+1}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma \left (\alpha+\frac{1}{2} \right )}$$

And further defined its second-kind version $\mathbf{M}_\alpha(x)$ as $\mathbf{M}_\alpha(x)=\mathbf{L}_\alpha(x)-I_\alpha(x)$, where $I_\alpha(x)$ is the [modified Bessel function of the first kind](wikipedia://en/4700#Modified_Bessel_functions:_Iα,_KαBessel_function#Bessel_functions_of_the_second_kind_:_Y.CE.B1).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Since this is a [non-homogeneous](wikipedia://en/32742753#homogeneous) equation, solutions can be constructed from a single particular solution by adding the solutions of the homogeneous problem. In this case, the homogeneous solutions are the [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700), and the particular solution may be chosen as the corresponding Struve function.

### Power series expansion {#Power_series_expansion}

Struve functions, denoted as $\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}(z)$ have the [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) form

$$\mathbf{H}_\alpha(z) = \sum_{m=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^m}{\Gamma \left (m+\frac{3}{2} \right ) \Gamma \left (m+\alpha+\frac{3}{2} \right )} \left({\frac{z}{2}}\right)^{2m+\alpha+1},$$

where $\Gamma(z)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

The modified Struve functions, denoted $\mathbf{L}_{\alpha}(z)$, have the following power series form

$$\mathbf{L}_\alpha(z) = \sum_{m=0}^\infty \frac{1}{\Gamma \left (m+\frac{3}{2} \right ) \Gamma \left (m+\alpha+\frac{3}{2} \right )} \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2m+\alpha+1}.$$

### Integral form {#Integral_form}

Another definition of the Struve function, for values of $\alpha$ satisfying $\operatorname{Re}(\alpha) > -\,\tfrac{1}{2}$, is possible expressing in term of the Poisson's integral representation:

$$\mathbf{H}_\alpha(x)=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^1(1-t^2)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\sin xt~dt=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\frac{\pi}{2}\sin(x\cos\tau)\sin^{2\alpha}\tau~d\tau=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\frac{\pi}{2}\sin(x\sin\tau)\cos^{2\alpha}\tau~d\tau$$

$$\mathbf{K}_\alpha(x)=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\infty(1+t^2)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-xt}~dt=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\infty e^{-x\sinh\tau}\cosh^{2\alpha}\tau~d\tau$$

$$\mathbf{L}_\alpha(x)=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^1(1-t^2)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\sinh xt~dt=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh(x\cos\tau)\sin^{2\alpha}\tau~d\tau=\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh(x\sin\tau)\cos^{2\alpha}\tau~d\tau$$

$$\mathbf{M}_\alpha(x)=-\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^1(1-t^2)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-xt}~dt=-\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-x\cos\tau}\sin^{2\alpha}\tau~d\tau=-\frac{2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^\alpha}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_0^\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-x\sin\tau}\cos^{2\alpha}\tau~d\tau$$

:::

## Asymptotic forms {#Asymptotic_forms}

For small $x$, the power [series expansion](wikipedia://en/1575813) is given above.

For large $x$, one obtains:

$$\mathbf{H}_\alpha(x) - Y_\alpha(x) = \frac{\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\alpha-1}}{\sqrt{\pi} \Gamma \left (\alpha+\frac{1}{2} \right )} + O\left(\left (\tfrac{x}{2}\right)^{\alpha-3}\right),$$

where $Y_{\alpha}(x)$ is the [Neumann function](wikipedia://en/4700#Bessel_functions_of_the_second_kind_:_Y.CE.B1).

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Struve functions satisfy the following [recurrence relations](wikipedia://en/146806):

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{H}_{\alpha -1}(x) + \mathbf{H}_{\alpha+1}(x) &= \frac{2\alpha}{x} \mathbf{H}_\alpha (x) + \frac{\left (\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma \left (\alpha + \frac{3}{2} \right )}, \\ \mathbf{H}_{\alpha -1}(x) - \mathbf{H}_{\alpha+1}(x) &= 2 \frac{d}{dx} \left (\mathbf{H}_\alpha(x) \right) - \frac{ \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^\alpha}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma \left (\alpha + \frac{3}{2} \right )}. \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Relation to other functions {#Relation_to_other_functions}

Struve functions of integer order can be expressed in terms of [Weber functions](wikipedia://en/16978003) $\mathbf{E}_{n}$ and vice versa: if $n$ is a non-negative integer then

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{E}_n(z) &= \frac{1}{\pi} \sum_{k=0}^{\left \lfloor \frac{n-1}{2} \right \rfloor} \frac{\Gamma \left (k+ \frac{1}{2} \right) \left (\frac{z}{2} \right )^{n-2k-1}}{\Gamma \left (n- k + \frac{1}{2}\right )} -\mathbf{H}_n(z),\\ \mathbf{E}_{-n}(z) &= \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\left \lceil \frac{n-3}{2} \right \rceil} \frac{\Gamma(n-k-\frac{1}{2}) \left (\frac{z}{2} \right )^{-n+2k+1}}{\Gamma \left (k+ \frac{3}{2} \right)}-\mathbf{H}_{-n}(z). \end{aligned}$$

Struve functions of order $n + \tfrac{1}{2}$ where $n$ is an integer can be expressed in terms of [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412). In particular if $n$ is a non-negative integer then

$$\mathbf{H}_{-n-\frac{1}{2}} (z) = (-1)^n J_{n+\frac{1}{2}}(z),$$

where the right hand side is a [spherical Bessel function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_Bessel_function).

Struve functions (of any order) can be expressed in terms of the [generalized hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/361184) ${}_{1}F_{2}$:

$$\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}(z) = \frac{z^{\alpha+1}}{2^{\alpha}\sqrt{\pi} \Gamma \left (\alpha+\tfrac{3}{2} \right )} {}_1F_2 \left (1;\tfrac{3}{2}, \alpha+\tfrac{3}{2};-\tfrac{z^2}{4} \right ).$$

## Applications {#Applications}

The Struve and Weber functions were shown to have an application to [beamforming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beamforming) in.,[^o13] and in describing the effect of confining interface on [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) of colloidal particles at low Reynolds numbers.[^o14]
