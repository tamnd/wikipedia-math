# Support of a module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Support of a module"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), the **support** of a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) *M* over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* is the set of all [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) $\mathfrak{p}$ of *R* such that $M_\mathfrak{p} \ne 0$ (that is, the [localization](wikipedia://en/252216) of *M* at $\mathfrak{p}$ is not equal to zero).[^o2] It is denoted by $\operatorname{Supp}M$. The support is, by definition, a subset of the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of *R*.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- $M = 0$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its support is [empty](wikipedia://en/9566).
- Let $0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$ be a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of *R*-modules. Then

$$\operatorname{Supp}M = \operatorname{Supp}M' \cup \operatorname{Supp}M''.$$

Note that this union may not be a [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946).

- If $M$ is a sum of [submodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) $M_\lambda$, then $\operatorname{Supp}M = \bigcup_\lambda \operatorname{Supp}M_\lambda.$
- If $M$ is a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) *R*-module, then $\operatorname{Supp}M$ is the set of all prime ideals containing the [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of *M*. In particular, it is closed in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) on Spec *R*.
- If $M, N$ are finitely generated *R*-modules, then

$$\operatorname{Supp}(M \otimes_R N) = \operatorname{Supp}M \cap \operatorname{Supp}N.$$

- If $M$ is a finitely generated *R*-module and *I* is an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of *R*, then $\operatorname{Supp}(M/IM)$ is the set of all prime ideals containing $I + \operatorname{Ann}M.$ This is $V(I) \cap \operatorname{Supp}M$.

:::

## Support of a quasicoherent sheaf {#Support_of_a_quasicoherent_sheaf}

If *F* is a [quasicoherent sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicoherent_sheaf) on a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X*, the support of *F* is the set of all points *x* in *X* such that the [stalk](wikipedia://en/15879621) *F*<sub>*x*</sub> is nonzero. This definition is similar to the definition of the [support of a function](wikipedia://en/381013) on a space *X*, and this is the motivation for using the word "support". Most properties of the support generalize from modules to quasicoherent sheaves word for word. For example, the support of a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) (or more generally, a finite type sheaf) is a closed subspace of *X*.[^o6]

If *M* is a module over a ring *R*, then the support of *M* as a module coincides with the support of the [associated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_associated_to_a_module) quasicoherent sheaf $\tilde{M}$ on the [affine scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) Spec *R*. Moreover, if $\{ U_\alpha = \operatorname{Spec}(R_\alpha) \}$ is an affine cover of a scheme *X*, then the support of a quasicoherent sheaf *F* is equal to the union of supports of the associated modules *M*<sub>α</sub> over each *R*<sub>α</sub>.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

As noted above, a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ is in the support if and only if it contains the annihilator of $M$.[^o10] For example, over $R = \mathbb{C}[x,y,z,w]$, the annihilator of the module

$$M = R/I = \frac{\mathbb{C}[x,y,z,w]}{(x^4 + y^4 + z^4 + w^4)}$$

is the ideal $I = (f) = (x^4+ y^4 + z^4 + w^4)$. This implies that $\operatorname{Supp}M \cong \operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$, the vanishing locus of the [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) *f*. Looking at the short exact sequence

$$0 \to I \to R \to R/I \to 0$$

we might mistakenly conjecture that the support of *I* = (*f*) is Spec(*R*<sub>(*f*)</sub>), which is the complement of the vanishing locus of the polynomial *f*. In fact, since *R* is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), the ideal *I =* (*f*) = *Rf* is isomorphic to *R* as a module, so its support is the entire space: Supp(*I*) = Spec(*R*).

The support of a finite module over a [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) is always closed under specialization.

Now, if we take two polynomials $f_1,f_2 \in R$ in an integral domain which form a complete intersection ideal $(f_1,f_2)$, the tensor property shows us that

$$\operatorname{Supp}\left( R/(f_1)\otimes_R R/(f_2) \right) =\, \operatorname{Supp}\left( R/(f_1)\right) \cap\, \operatorname{Supp}\left( R/(f_2)\right) \cong\, \operatorname{Spec}(R/(f_1,f_2)).$$

:::
