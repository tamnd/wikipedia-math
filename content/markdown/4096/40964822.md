# Peter McMullen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter McMullen"}

**Peter McMullen** (born 11 May 1942)[^o3] is a British mathematician, a professor emeritus of mathematics at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

McMullen earned bachelor's and master's degrees from [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), and studied at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham), where he received his doctorate in 1968.[^o6] He taught at [Western Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Washington_University) from 1968 to 1969.[^o7] In 1978 he earned his Doctor of Science at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) where he still works as a professor emeritus. In 2006 he was accepted as a corresponding member of the Austrian Academy of Sciences.[^o8]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

McMullen is known for his work in [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519) and [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), and in particular for proving what was then called the upper bound conjecture and now is the [upper bound theorem](wikipedia://en/40964551). This result states that [cyclic polytopes](wikipedia://en/26652374) have the maximum possible number of faces among all polytopes with a given dimension and number of vertices.[^o10] McMullen also formulated the g-conjecture, later the [g-theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-theorem) of [Louis Billera](wikipedia://en/22444348), Carl W. Lee, and [Richard P. Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397), characterizing the [*f*-vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-vector) of [simplicial spheres](wikipedia://en/22359636).[^o11]

The [McMullen problem](wikipedia://en/35695532) is an unsolved question in discrete geometry named after McMullen, concerning the number of points in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) for which a [projective transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation) into [convex position](wikipedia://en/40079283) can be guaranteed to exist. It was credited to a private communication from McMullen in a 1972 paper by David G. Larman.[^o12]

He is also known for his 1960s drawing, by hand, of a 2-dimensional representation of the Gosset polytope [4<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236362), the vertices of which form the vectors of the [E8 root system](wikipedia://en/647994).[^o13]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

McMullen was invited to speak at the 1974 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver); his contribution there had the title *Metrical and combinatorial properties of convex polytopes*.[^o15]

He was elected as a foreign member of the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2006.[^o16] In 2012 he became an inaugural [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o17]
