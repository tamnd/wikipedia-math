# K-statistic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K-statistic"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **k-statistic** is a [statistic](wikipedia://en/26703) constructed from a sample to estimate a [cumulant](wikipedia://en/359684) of the underlying population distribution. For a sample of [independent and identically distributed](wikipedia://en/30876902) random variables, the *r*th k-statistic is the unique symmetric [unbiased estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbiased_estimator) of the *r*th cumulant and has minimum variance among unbiased estimators.[^o2]

:::

The first k-statistic is the [sample mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_mean), which estimates the first cumulant. The second k-statistic is the usual unbiased [sample variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_variance), which estimates the second cumulant.[^o2] Higher-order k-statistics provide corresponding unbiased estimators of higher-order cumulants.

## History {#History}

K-statistics were developed by [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824) in his work on the moments and product moments of sampling distributions.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Multivariate k-statistics can be used to estimate joint cumulants. In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), they have been applied to the unbiased estimation of [polyspectra](wikipedia://en/82524224) (higher-order spectra), including the [bispectrum](wikipedia://en/2496000) and [trispectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trispectrum).[^o7]

:::
