# John Müller

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Müller"}

*Image: Müller - Mathematical Treatise, 1760 - 1378597.jpg.* *Mathematical Treatise*, 1760

**John Muller** (1699 – June 1784) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) from the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire).[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born **Johann Müller** in the Holy Roman Empire, he moved to London in 1736.

In 1741 he was appointed deputy head of the [Royal Military Academy, Woolwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy,_Woolwich), where he performed all the teaching duties of [Martin Folkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Folkes).[^o4] He transformed the institution into a disciplined cadet academy with the help of [Thomas Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Simpson).[^o5] In 1754 he became first master of the Academy, after Folkes's death.[^o4] He was later appointed Professor of Artillery and Fortification (and "Preceptor of Engineering, etc. to his Royal Highness the Duke of Gloucester"); he retired in 1766. During his time at the Academy Muller carried out gunnery experiments with [Colonel Desaguliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Desaguliers), described in the second edition of *A Treatise of Artillery*.[^o6]

Muller married Mary Horn on 29 December 1774, at St Martin-in-the-Fields, Westminster. He died at his home in Eaton Street (now Greet Street), London in June 1784.[^o7] The contents of his library were auctioned at the Military Library, Whitehall early in 1785.[^o8]

Muller’s works were concerned with mathematics and fortification. At the Academy there were strict instructions on which topics each instructor was to teach,[^o9] which included a list of works to be used; several of Muller's books were his included.

## Works {#Works}

- A Mathematical Treatise: Containing a System of Conic-sections; with the doctrine of fluxions and fluents, applied to various subjects (1736)[^o11]
- A Treatise Containing the Elementary Part of Fortification (1746)[^o12]
- Elements of Mathematics (1748, also titled A System of Mathematics)
- A Treatise Containing the Practical Part of Fortification (1755)[^o13]
- A Treatise on Artillery (1757; later editions were titled A Treatise of Artillery)
- The Field Engineer; translated from the French (1759)
- Traité analytique des sections coniques, fluxions et fluentes. Avec un essai sur les quadratures, et un traité du mouvement (1760)[^o14]
- A New System of Mathematics (1769)
- New Elements of Mathematics: or, Euclid corrected (1773)[^o15]
