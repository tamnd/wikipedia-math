# Unusual number

*Image: Unusual number Cuisenaire rods 10.png.* Demonstration, with [Cuisenaire rods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisenaire_rods), that the number 10 is an unusual number, its largest prime factor being 5, which is greater than √10 ≈ 3.16

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unusual number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **unusual number** is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n* whose largest [prime factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) is strictly greater than [$\sqrt{n}$](wikipedia://en/29208).

:::

A *k*-[smooth number](wikipedia://en/1035039) has all its prime factors less than or equal to *k*, therefore, an unusual number is non-$\sqrt{n}$-smooth.

The term "unusual number" was coined by Daniel Greene and [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095), who also showed that, somewhat confusingly, they are asymptotically more [dense](wikipedia://en/1819983) than their "usual" counterparts.[^o2]

## Relation to prime numbers {#Relation_to_prime_numbers}

All [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) are unusual. For any prime *p*, its multiples less than *p*<sup>2</sup> are unusual, that is *p*, ... (*p* − 1)*p*, which have a density 1/*p* in the interval (*p*, *p*<sup>2</sup>).

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Distribution {#Distribution}

If we denote the number of unusual numbers less than or equal to *n* by *u*(*n*) then *u*(*n*) behaves as follows:

::: {#o7 .table cue=table}

| ***n*** | ***u*(*n*)** | ***u*(*n*) / *n*** |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 6 | \0.6 |
| 100 | 67 | \0.67 |
| 1000 | 715 | \0.72 |
| 10000 | 7319 | \0.73 |
| 100000 | 73322 | \0.73 |
| 1000000 | 731660 | \0.73 |
| 10000000 | 7280266 | \0.73 |
| 100000000 | 72467077 | \0.72 |
| 1000000000 | 721578596 | \0.72 |

:::

[Richard Schroeppel](wikipedia://en/1649527) stated in the [HAKMEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAKMEM) (1972), Item #29[^o8] that the asymptotic [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that a randomly chosen number is unusual is [ln(2)](wikipedia://en/27153398). In other words:

$$\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{u(n)}{n} = \ln(2) = 0.693147 \dots\, .$$
