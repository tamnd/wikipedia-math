# List of Laplace transforms

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of Laplace transforms"}

The following is a **list of Laplace transforms** for many common functions of a single variable.[^o2] The [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) is an [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912) that takes a function of a positive real variable $t$ (often time) to a function of a complex variable $s$ (complex [angular frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency)).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Laplace transform of a function $f(t)$ can be obtained using the [formal definition](wikipedia://en/18610#Formal_definition) of the Laplace transform. However, some properties of the Laplace transform can be used to obtain the Laplace transform of some functions more easily.

### Linearity {#Linearity}

For functions $f$ and $g$ and for scalar $a$, the Laplace transform satisfies

$$\mathcal{L}\{a f(t) + b g(t)\} = a \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} + b \mathcal{L}\{ g(t)\}$$

and is, therefore, regarded as a linear operator.

### Time shifting {#Time_shifting}

The Laplace transform of $f(t - a) u(t - a)$, where $u$ is the Heaviside step function, is $e^{-as} F(s)$.

### Frequency shifting {#Frequency_shifting}

The Laplace transform of $e^{at} f(t)$ is $F(s - a)$.

:::

## Explanatory notes {#Explanatory_notes}

The unilateral Laplace transform takes as input a function whose time domain is the [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) reals, which is why all of the time domain functions in the table below are multiples of the [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299), $u(t)$.

The entries of the table that involve a time delay $\tau$ are required to be [causal](wikipedia://en/554087) (meaning that $\tau > 0$). A causal system is a system where the [impulse response](wikipedia://en/545825) $h(t)$ is zero for all time $t$ prior to $t = 0$. In general, the region of convergence for causal systems is not the same as that of [anticausal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticausal_system).

The following functions and variables are used in the table below:

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dirac delta function"}

- $\delta$ represents the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021).

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Heaviside step function"}

- $u(t)$ represents the [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299). Literature may refer to this by other notation, including $1(t)$ or $H(t)$.

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gamma function"}

- $\Gamma(z)$ represents the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler&ndash;Mascheroni constant"}

- $\gamma$ is the [Euler&ndash;Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler&ndash;Mascheroni_constant).

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="Real number"}

- $t$ is a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438). It typically represents *time*, although it can represent *any* independent dimension.

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="complex"}

- $s$ is the [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) frequency domain parameter, and $\operatorname{Re}(s)$ is its [real part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part).

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Integer"}

- $n$ is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563).

:::

- $\alpha, \tau,\text{ and } \omega$ are real numbers.
- $q$ is a complex number.

## Table {#Table}

::: {#o17 .table cue=table}

| Function | Time domain $f(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)\}$ | Laplace $s$-domain $F(s) = \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\}$ | Region of convergence | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [unit impulse](wikipedia://en/37021) | $\delta(t)$ | $1$ | all $s$ | inspection |
| delayed impulse | $\delta(t - \tau)$ | $e^{-\tau s}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | time shift of unit impulse[^o19] |
| [unit step](wikipedia://en/87299) | $u(t)$ | ${ 1 \over s }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | integrate unit impulse |
| delayed unit step | $u(t - \tau)$ | $\frac{1}{s} e^{-\tau s}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | time shift of unit step[^o21] |
| [ramp](wikipedia://en/5092636) | $t \cdot u(t)$ | $\frac{1}{s^2}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | integrate unit impulse twice |
| $n$th power (for integer $n$) | $t^n \cdot u(t)$ | ${ n! \over s^{n + 1} }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ ($n > -1$) | Integrate unit step $n$ times |
| $q$th power (for complex $q$) | $t^q \cdot u(t)$ | ${ \operatorname{\Gamma}(q + 1) \over s^{q + 1} }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ $\operatorname{Re}(q) > -1$ | [^o23][^o24] |
| $n$th root | $\sqrt[n]{t} \cdot u(t)$ | ${ 1 \over s^{\frac{1}{n}+1} } \operatorname{\Gamma}\left(\frac{1}{n} + 1\right)$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | Set $q = 1/n$ above. |
| $n$th power with frequency shift | $t^{n} e^{-\alpha t} \cdot u(t)$ | $\frac{n!}{(s+\alpha)^{n+1}}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > -\alpha$ | Integrate unit step, apply frequency shift |
| delayed $n$th power with frequency shift | $(t-\tau)^n e^{-\alpha (t-\tau)} \cdot u(t-\tau)$ | $\frac{n! \cdot e^{-\tau s}}{(s+\alpha)^{n+1}}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > -\alpha$ | Integrate unit step, apply frequency shift, apply time shift |
| [exponential decay](wikipedia://en/330320) | $e^{-\alpha t} u(t)$ | ${ 1 \over s+\alpha }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > -\alpha$ | Frequency shift of unit step |
| [two-sided](wikipedia://en/1502985) exponential decay (only for bilateral transform) | $e^{-\alpha\|t\|}$ | ${ 2\alpha \over \alpha^2 - s^2 }$ | $-\alpha < \operatorname{Re}(s) < \alpha$ | Frequency shift of unit step |
| exponential approach | $( 1-e^{-\alpha t}) \cdot u(t)$ | $\frac{\alpha}{s(s+\alpha)}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | Unit step minus exponential decay |
| [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) | $\sin(\omega t) \cdot u(t)$ | ${ \omega \over s^2 + \omega^2 }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | [^o28] |
| [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine) | $\cos(\omega t) \cdot u(t)$ | ${ s \over s^2 + \omega^2 }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | [^o28] |
| [hyperbolic sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_sine) | $\sinh(\alpha t) \cdot u(t)$ | ${ \alpha \over s^2 - \alpha^2 }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > \lvert \alpha\rvert$ | [^o31] |
| [hyperbolic cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_cosine) | $\cosh(\alpha t) \cdot u(t)$ | ${ s \over s^2 - \alpha^2 }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > \lvert \alpha\rvert$ | [^o31] |
| exponentially decaying sine wave | $e^{-\alpha t} \sin(\omega t) \cdot u(t)$ | ${ \omega \over (s+\alpha )^2 + \omega^2 }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > -\alpha$ | [^o28] |
| exponentially decaying cosine wave | $e^{-\alpha t} \cos(\omega t) \cdot u(t)$ | ${ s+\alpha \over (s+\alpha )^2 + \omega^2 }$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > -\alpha$ | [^o28] |
| [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) | $\ln (t) \cdot u(t)$ | $\frac{-\ln(s)-\gamma}{s}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | [^o31] |
| [Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700) of the first kind, of order *n* | $J_n( \omega t) \cdot u(t)$ | $\frac{ \left(\sqrt{s^2 + \omega^2}-s\right)^{\!n}}{\omega^n \sqrt{s^2 + \omega^2}}$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ ($n > -1$) | [^o31] |
| [Error function](wikipedia://en/177111) | $\operatorname{erf}(t) \cdot u(t)$ | $\frac{e^{s^2/4}}{s} \!\left(1 - \operatorname{erf} \left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\right)$ | $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$ | [^o31] |

:::
