# Local rigidity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local rigidity"}

**Local rigidity** theorems in the theory of discrete subgroups of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) are results which show that small deformations of certain such subgroups are always trivial. It is different from [Mostow rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostow_rigidity) and weaker (but holds more frequently) than [superrigidity](wikipedia://en/15783129).

:::

## History {#History}

The first such theorem was proven by [Atle Selberg](wikipedia://en/2021) for co-compact discrete subgroups of the unimodular groups $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb R)$.[^o3] Shortly afterwards a similar statement was proven by [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000) in the setting of fundamental groups of compact hyperbolic manifolds. Finally, the theorem was extended to all co-compact subgroups of semisimple Lie groups by [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019).[^o4][^o5] The extension to non-cocompact lattices was made later by Howard Garland and [Madabusi Santanam Raghunathan](wikipedia://en/18730540).[^o6] The result is now sometimes referred to as Calabi—Weil (or just Weil) rigidity.

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

### Deformations of subgroups {#Deformations_of_subgroups}

Let $\Gamma$ be a group [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) by a finite number of elements $g_1, \ldots, g_n$ and $G$ a Lie group. Then the map $\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma, G) \to G^n$ defined by $\rho \mapsto (\rho(g_1), \ldots, \rho(g_n))$ is injective and this endows $\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma, G)$ with a topology [induced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_topology) by that of $G^n$. If $\Gamma$ is a subgroup of $G$ then a *deformation* of $\Gamma$ is any element in $\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma, G)$. Two representations $\phi, \psi$ are said to be conjugated if there exists a $g \in G$ such that $\phi(\gamma) = g\psi(\gamma) g^{-1}$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$. See also [character variety](wikipedia://en/14688049).

### Lattices in simple groups not of type A1 or A1 × A1 {#Lattices_in_simple_groups_not_of_type_A1_or_A1_×_A1}

The simplest statement is when $\Gamma$ is a lattice in a simple Lie group $G$ and the latter is not locally isomorphic to $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ or $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$ and $\Gamma$ (this means that its Lie algebra is not that of one of these two groups).

*There exists a neighbourhood $U$ in $\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma, G)$ of the inclusion $i : \Gamma \subset G$ such that any $\phi \in U$ is conjugated to $i$.*

Whenever such a statement holds for a pair $G \supset \Gamma$ we will say that local rigidity holds.

### Lattices in SL(2,C) {#Lattices_in_SL(2,C)}

Local rigidity holds for cocompact lattices in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$. A lattice $\Gamma$ in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$ which is not cocompact has nontrivial deformations coming from Thurston's [hyperbolic Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/4450009) theory. However, if one adds the restriction that a representation must send parabolic elements in $\Gamma$ to parabolic elements then local rigidity holds.

### Lattices in SL(2,R) {#Lattices_in_SL(2,R)}

In this case local rigidity never holds (except [cocompact](wikipedia://en/12746615) [triangle groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_groups#The_hyperbolic_case)). For cocompact lattices a small deformation remains a cocompact lattice but it may not be conjugated to the original one (see [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) for more detail). Non-cocompact lattices are virtually free and hence have non-lattice deformations.

### Semisimple Lie groups {#Semisimple_Lie_groups}

Local rigidity holds for lattices in [semisimple Lie groups](wikipedia://en/292831#Semisimple_Lie_groups) providing the latter have no factor of type A1 (i.e. locally isomorphic to $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ or $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$) or the former is irreducible.

:::

## Other results {#Other_results}

There are also local rigidity results where the ambient group is changed, even in case where superrigidity fails. For example, if $\Gamma$ is a lattice in the [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) $\mathrm{SU}(n,1)$ and $n \ge 2$ then the inclusion $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SU}(n,1) \subset \mathrm{SU}(n+1,1)$ is locally rigid.[^o15]

A uniform lattice $\Gamma$ in any compactly generated topological group $G$ is *topologically locally rigid*, in the sense that any sufficiently small deformation $\varphi$ of the inclusion $i : \Gamma \subset G$ is injective and $\varphi(\Gamma)$ is a uniform lattice in $G$. An irreducible uniform lattice in the isometry group of any proper geodesically complete $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$-space not isometric to the hyperbolic plane and without Euclidean factors is locally rigid.[^o16]

::: {#o18 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs of the theorem"}

## Proofs of the theorem {#Proofs_of_the_theorem}

Weil's original proof is by relating deformations of a subgroup $\Gamma$ in $G$ to the first [cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) group of $\Gamma$ with coefficients in the Lie algebra of $G$, and then showing that this cohomology vanishes for cocompact lattices when $G$ has no simple factor of absolute type A1. A more geometric proof which also work in the non-compact cases uses [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108) (and [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s) theory of $(G, X)$ structures.[^o19]

:::
