# Krivine–Stengle Positivstellensatz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Krivine–Stengle Positivstellensatz"}

In [real algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/7252198), **Krivine–Stengle** **Positivstellensatz** (German for "positive-locus-[theorem](wikipedia://en/30977)") characterizes [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) that are positive on a [semialgebraic set](wikipedia://en/13329722), which is defined by systems of [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489) of polynomials with [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficients), or more generally, coefficients from any [real closed field](wikipedia://en/1066093).

:::

It can be thought of as a real analogue of [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215) (which concern [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [zeros](wikipedia://en/264210) of polynomial ideals), and this analogy is at the origin of its name. It was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by French mathematician Jean-Louis Krivine and then rediscovered by the Canadian Gilbert Stengle.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $R$ be a [real closed field](wikipedia://en/1066093), and $F$ = {*f*<sub>1</sub>, *f*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *f*<sub>*m*</sub>} and $G$ = {*g*<sub>1</sub>, *g*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *g*<sub>*r*</sub>} finite sets of polynomials over $R$ in $n$ variables. Let $W$ be the semialgebraic set

$$W=\{x\in R^n\mid\forall f\in F,\,f(x)\ge0;\, \forall g\in G,\,g(x)=0\},$$

and define the preorder (in the sense of a [prepositive cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preordered_field)) associated with $W$ as the set

$$P(F,G) = \left\{ \sum_{\alpha \in \{0,1\}^m} \sigma_\alpha f_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots f_m^{\alpha_m} + \sum_{\ell=1}^r \varphi_\ell g_\ell : \sigma_\alpha \in \Sigma^2[X_1,\ldots,X_n];\ \varphi_\ell \in R[X_1,\ldots,X_n] \right\}$$

where Σ<sup>2</sup>\[$X$<sub>1</sub>,...,$X$<sub>$n$</sub>\] is the set of [sum-of-squares polynomials](wikipedia://en/17001761). In other words, $P$($F$, $G$) = C + I, where C is the [cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) generated by $F$ (i.e., the [subsemiring](wikipedia://en/371468) of $R$\[$X$<sub>1</sub>,...,$X$<sub>$n$</sub>\] generated by $F$ and arbitrary squares) and I is the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by $G$.

Let $p$ ∈ $R$\[$X$<sub>1</sub>,...,$X$<sub>$n$</sub>\] be a polynomial. *Krivine–Stengle Positivstellensatz* states that

(i) $\forall x\in W\;p(x)\ge 0$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $\exists q_1,q_2\in P(F,G)$ and $s \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $q_1 p = p^{2s} + q_2$.

(ii) $\forall x\in W\;p(x)>0$ if and only if $\exists q_1,q_2\in P(F,G)$ such that $q_1 p = 1 + q_2$.

The *weak Positivstellensatz* is the following variant of the Positivstellensatz. Let $R$ be a real closed field, and $F$, $G$, and $H$ finite subsets of $R$\[$X$<sub>1</sub>,...,$X$<sub>$n$</sub>\]. Let $C$ be the cone generated by $F$, and I the ideal generated by $G$. Then

$$\{x\in R^n\mid\forall f\in F\,f(x)\ge0\land\forall g\in G\,g(x)=0\land\forall h\in H\,h(x)\ne0\}=\emptyset$$

if and only if

$$\exists f \in C,g \in I,n \in \mathbb{N}\; f+g+\left(\prod H\right)^{\!2n} = 0.$$

(Unlike Nullstellensatz, the "weak" form actually includes the "strong" form as a special case, so the terminology is a misnomer.)

:::

## Variants {#Variants}

The Krivine–Stengle Positivstellensatz also has the following refinements under additional assumptions. It should be remarked that Schmüdgen's Positivstellensatz has a weaker assumption than Putinar's Positivstellensatz, but the conclusion is also weaker.

::: {#o7 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Schmudgen Positivstellensatz"}

### Schmüdgen's Positivstellensatz {#Schmüdgen's_Positivstellensatz}

Suppose that $R = \mathbb{R}$. If the semialgebraic set $W=\{x\in \mathbb{R}^n\mid\forall f\in F,\,f(x)\ge0\}$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), then each polynomial $p \in \mathbb{R}[X_1, \dots, X_n]$ that is strictly positive on $W$ can be written as a polynomial in the defining functions of $W$ with sums-of-squares coefficients, i.e. $p \in P(F, \emptyset)$. Here $P$ is said to be *strictly positive on $W$* if $p(x)>0$ for all $x \in W$.[^o8] Note that Schmüdgen's Positivstellensatz is stated for $R = \mathbb{R}$ and does not hold for arbitrary real closed fields.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Putinar's Positivstellensatz"}

### Putinar's Positivstellensatz {#Putinar's_Positivstellensatz}

Define the quadratic module associated with $W$ as the set

$$Q(F,G) = \left\{ \sigma_0 + \sum_{j=1}^m \sigma_j f_j + \sum_{\ell=1}^r \varphi_\ell g_\ell : \sigma_j \in \Sigma^2 [X_1,\ldots,X_n];\ \varphi_\ell \in \mathbb{R}[X_1,\ldots,X_n] \right\}$$

Assume there exists *L* > 0 such that the polynomial $L - \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 \in Q(F,G).$ If $p(x)>0$ for all $x \in W$, then $p$ ∈ $Q$($F$,*$G$*).[^o12]

:::
