# Girth (graph theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Girth"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **girth** of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is the length of a shortest [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) contained in the graph.[^o2] If the graph does not contain any cycles (that is, it is a [forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_%28graph_theory%29)), its girth is defined to be [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824). For example, a 4-cycle (square) has girth 4. A grid has girth 4 as well, and a triangular mesh has girth 3. A graph with girth four or more is [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996).

:::

## Cages {#Cages}

A [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) (all vertices have degree three) of girth $g$ that is as small as possible is known as a $g$-[cage](wikipedia://en/7735022) (or as a $(3,g)$-cage). The [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) is the unique 5-cage (it is the smallest cubic graph of girth 5), the [Heawood graph](wikipedia://en/1396870) is the unique 6-cage, the [McGee graph](wikipedia://en/23714466) is the unique 7-cage and the [Tutte eight cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutte_eight_cage) is the unique 8-cage.[^o5] There may exist multiple cages for a given girth. For instance there are three nonisomorphic 10-cages, each with 70 vertices: the [Balaban 10-cage](wikipedia://en/23714047), the [Harries graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harries_graph) and the [Harries–Wong graph](wikipedia://en/23702758).

## Girth and graph coloring {#Girth_and_graph_coloring}

For any positive integers $g$ and $\chi$, there exists a graph with girth at least $g$ and [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) at least $\chi$; for instance, the [Grötzsch graph](wikipedia://en/7685783) is triangle-free and has chromatic number 4, and repeating the [Mycielskian](wikipedia://en/7685346) construction used to form the Grötzsch graph produces triangle-free graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number. [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) was the first to prove the general result, using the [probabilistic method](wikipedia://en/173525).[^o7] More precisely, he showed that a [random graph](wikipedia://en/358477) on $n$ vertices, formed by choosing independently whether to include each edge with probability $n^{(1-g)/g}$, has, with probability tending to 1 as $n$ goes to infinity, at most $\tfrac{n}{2}$ cycles of length $g$ or less, but has no [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) of size $\tfrac{n}{2k}$. Therefore, removing one vertex from each short cycle leaves a smaller graph with girth greater than $g$, in which each color class of a coloring must be small and which therefore requires at least $k$ colors in any coloring.

Explicit, though large, graphs with high girth and chromatic number can be constructed as certain [Cayley graphs](wikipedia://en/358277) of [linear groups](wikipedia://en/3983656) over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615).[^o8] These remarkable *[Ramanujan graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan_graphs)* also have large [expansion coefficient](wikipedia://en/9313).

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

The **odd girth** and **even girth** of a graph are the lengths of a shortest odd cycle and shortest even cycle respectively.

The **circumference** of a graph is the length of the *longest* (simple) cycle, rather than the shortest.

Thought of as the least length of a non-trivial cycle, the girth admits natural generalisations as the 1-systole or higher systoles in [systolic geometry](wikipedia://en/2545455).

Girth is the dual concept to [edge connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph), in the sense that the girth of a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) is the edge connectivity of its [dual graph](wikipedia://en/2536864), and vice versa. These concepts are unified in [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory) by the [girth of a matroid](wikipedia://en/47482411), the size of the smallest dependent set in the matroid. For a [graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714), the matroid girth equals the girth of the underlying graph, while for a co-graphic matroid it equals the edge connectivity.[^o11]

## Computation {#Computation}

The girth of an undirected graph can be computed by running a [breadth-first search](wikipedia://en/97026) from each node, with complexity $O(nm)$ where $n$ is the number of vertices of the graph and $m$ is the number of edges.[^o14] A practical optimization is to limit the depth of the BFS to a depth that depends on the length of the smallest cycle discovered so far.[^o15] Better algorithms are known in the case where the girth is even[^o16] and when the graph is planar.[^o17] In terms of lower bounds, computing the girth of a graph is at least as hard as solving the [triangle finding problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_finding_problem) on the graph.
