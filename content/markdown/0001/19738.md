# Metrizable space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Metrizable space"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **metrizable space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). That is, a topological space $(X, \tau)$ is said to be metrizable if there is a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) $d : X \times X \to [0, \infty)$ such that the topology induced by $d$ is $\tau.$[^o2][^o3] *Metrization theorems* are [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) that give [sufficient conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition) for a topological space to be metrizable.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Metrizable spaces inherit all topological properties from metric spaces. For example, they are [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) spaces (and hence [normal](wikipedia://en/48629) and [Tychonoff](wikipedia://en/30554)) and [first-countable](wikipedia://en/904940). However, some properties of the metric, such as [completeness](wikipedia://en/7832), cannot be said to be inherited. This is also true of other structures linked to the metric. A metrizable [uniform space](wikipedia://en/32339), for example, may have a different set of [contraction maps](wikipedia://en/6239) than a metric space to which it is homeomorphic.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Metrization theorems"}

## Metrization theorems {#Metrization_theorems}

One of the first widely recognized metrization theorems was *Urysohn's metrization theorem*. This states that every Hausdorff [second-countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-countable) [regular space](wikipedia://en/53058) is metrizable. So, for example, every second-countable [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) is metrizable. (Historical note: The form of the theorem shown here was in fact proved by [Tikhonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Nikolayevich_Tychonoff) in 1926. What [Urysohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Samuilovich_Urysohn) had shown, in a paper published posthumously in 1925, was that every second-countable *[normal](wikipedia://en/48629)* Hausdorff space is metrizable.) The converse does not hold: there exist metric spaces that are not second countable, for example, an uncountable set endowed with the discrete metric.[^o8] The [Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem](wikipedia://en/1236569), described below, provides a more specific theorem where the converse does hold.

Several other metrization theorems follow as simple corollaries to Urysohn's theorem. For example, a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) Hausdorff space is metrizable if and only if it is second-countable.

Urysohn's Theorem can be restated as: A topological space is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) and metrizable if and only if it is regular, Hausdorff and second-countable. The Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem extends this to the non-separable case. It states that a topological space is metrizable [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is regular, Hausdorff and has a σ-locally finite base. A σ-locally finite base is a base which is a union of countably many [locally finite collections](wikipedia://en/11611098) of open sets. For a closely related theorem see the [Bing metrization theorem](wikipedia://en/9712648).

Separable metrizable spaces can also be characterized as those spaces which are [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to a subspace of the [Hilbert cube](wikipedia://en/216389) $\lbrack 0, 1 \rbrack ^\mathbb{N},$ that is, the countably infinite product of the unit interval (with its natural subspace topology from the reals) with itself, endowed with the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910).

A space is said to be *locally metrizable* if every point has a metrizable [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485). Smirnov proved that a locally metrizable space is metrizable if and only if it is Hausdorff and [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact). In particular, a manifold is metrizable if and only if it is paracompact.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The group of unitary operators $\mathbb{U}(\mathcal{H})$ on a separable Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ endowed with the [strong operator topology](wikipedia://en/458699) is metrizable (see Proposition II.1 in [^o11]).

Non-normal spaces cannot be metrizable; important examples include

- the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) on an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) or on the [spectrum of a ring](wikipedia://en/37291), used in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997),
- the [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) of all [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) from the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) $\mathbb{R}$ to itself, with the [topology of pointwise convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_of_pointwise_convergence).

The real line with the [lower limit topology](wikipedia://en/310517) is not metrizable. The usual distance function is not a metric on this space because the topology it determines is the usual topology, not the lower limit topology. This space is Hausdorff, paracompact and first countable.

### Locally metrizable but not metrizable {#Locally_metrizable_but_not_metrizable}

::: {#o13 .definition cue=dfni name="bug-eyed line"}

The [Line with two origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_with_two_origins), also called the **bug-eyed line** is a [non-Hausdorff manifold](wikipedia://en/14194283) (and thus cannot be metrizable). Like all manifolds, it is [locally homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_homeomorphic) to [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) and thus [locally metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_metrizable_space) (but not metrizable) and [locally Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/5075766) (but not [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637)). It is also a [T<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/148721) [locally regular space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_regular_space) but not a [semiregular space](wikipedia://en/1885104).

:::

The [long line](wikipedia://en/338403) is locally metrizable but not metrizable; in a sense, it is "too long".

:::
