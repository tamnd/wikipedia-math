# Demographics of Greece

::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Greece"}

The **Demographics of Greece** refer to the [demography](wikipedia://en/45642) of the population that inhabits the country. The population of [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) was estimated by the Hellenic Statistical Authority to be 10,372,335 in 2025. The latest census in Greece was conducted in 2021.[^o9]

:::

## Historical overview {#Historical_overview}

[Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) was inhabited as early as the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) period. The [Greek language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) ultimately dominated the peninsula and Greece's mosaic of small city-states became culturally similar. The population estimates on the Greeks during the 4th century BC, is approximately 3.5 million on the Greek peninsula and 4 to 6.5 million in the rest of the entire [Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_basin),[^o11] including all colonies such as those in [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia), [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) and the shores of the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea).

During the history of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), the Greek peninsula was occasionally invaded by the foreign peoples like [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths), [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars), [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs), [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans), [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) and other [Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages)-speaking peoples who had betrayed the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades). The only group, however, that planned to establish permanent settlements in the region were the Slavs. They settled in isolated valleys of the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) and [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly), establishing segregated communities that were referred by the Byzantines as [Sclaveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sclaveni). Traces of Slavic culture in Greece are very rare and by the 9th century, the Sclaveni in Greece were largely assimilated. However, some Slavic communities managed to survive in rural [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_%28region%29). At the same time a large [Sephardi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews) emigrant community from the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) established itself in [Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaloniki), while there were population movements of [Arvanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvanites) and "[Vlachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlachs)" ([Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians) and [Megleno-Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megleno-Romanians)), who established communities in several parts of the Greek peninsula. The Byzantine Empire ultimately fell to [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) in the 15th century and as a result Ottoman colonies were established in the [Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans), notably in Macedonia, the Peloponnese and [Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete). Many Greeks either fled to other European nations or to geographically isolated areas (i.e. mountains and heavily forested territories) to escape foreign rule. For those reasons, the population decreased in the plains, while increasing on the mountains. The [population transfers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_transfer) with [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria) and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) that took place in the early 20th century, added in total some two million Greeks to the demography of the [Greek Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece).[^o12]

During the next decades, the population of Greece continued to increase, except during a large part of 1940s due to World War II and subsequent events. After 1940s the population of Greece continued to grow, though on a decreased pace after 1960s, due to a gradual decrease in fertility and emigration to various countries, such as [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), Australia, United Kingdom and many others. The birth rate decreased significantly in 1980s, while in 1987 the Greek population surpassed 10 million. At this time Greece had started to see a positive migration rate, due to the return of [Greek Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Civil_War) refugees and international immigration. During the 1990s the population increased by close to 1 million, as the collapse of the communist governments in Eastern Europe and the economic downturn resulted in a significant influx of Eastern European immigrants to Greece, especially from the Balkans, including many diaspora Greeks returning home. In the 2000s the population continued to increase reaching 11 million, thanks to an increased birth rate, a stable influx of migrants from other countries and the return of Greeks from United States, Germany, Australia and other countries. In the 2010s, in the wake of the [Greek financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_government-debt_crisis), the population started to decrease and birthrates plummeted, while death rates increased due to an aging population. Many Greeks emigrated abroad, while more recently the population decrease has been largely stabilized due to foreign immigration.

### Age structure {#Age_structure}

Being part of the phenomenon of the [aging of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageing_of_Europe), the Greek population shows a rapid increase of the percentage of the elderly people. Greece's population census of 1961 found that 10.9% of the total population was above the age of 65, while the percentage of this group age increased to 19.0% in 2011. In contrast, the percentage of the population of the ages 0–14 had a total decrease of 10.2% between 1961 and 2011.

### Illegal immigration {#Illegal_immigration}

Greece has received many [illegal immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Greece#Illegal_immigration_to_Greece) beginning in the 1990s and continuing during the 2000s and 2010s. Migrants make use of the many islands in the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea), directly west of Turkey. A spokesman for the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union)'s border control agency said that the Greek–Albanian border is "one of Europe's worst-affected external land borders." Migrants across the [Evros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evros_%28regional_unit%29) region bordering Turkey face land-mines. Principal illegal immigrants include Albanians, Pakistanis, Kurds, Afghans, Iraqis and Somalis.[^o94][^o95]

## Ethnic groups, languages and religion {#Ethnic_groups,_languages_and_religion}

*Image: Hellenism in the Near East Ethnological Map of Greece by George Soteriadis c1918.jpg.* Ethnic map of Greece in 1918 during the [Greek genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_genocide).

The population of [northern Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Greece) has primarily been ethnically, religiously and linguistically diverse.[^o97] The [Muslim minority of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_minority_of_Greece) is the only explicitly recognized [minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group) in Greece by the government. The officials define it as a group of Greek Muslims numbering 98,000 people, consisting of [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) (50%), [Pomaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomaks) (35%) and [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (15%). No other minorities are officially acknowledged by the government.[^o98][^o99][^o100] There is no official information for the size of the ethnic, linguistic and religious [minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minorities_in_Greece) because asking the population questions pertaining to the topic have been abolished since 1951.[^o101][^o102]

*Image: Greece linguistic minorities.svg.* Note: Greek is the dominant language throughout Greece; inclusion in a non-Greek language zone does not necessarily imply that the relevant minority language is still spoken there, or that its speakers consider themselves an ethnic minority.

[Minorities in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minorities_in_Greece) according to [Minority Rights Group International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_Rights_Group_International) in 2015:[^o104]

- [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Greece): 265,000
- [Vlachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlachs) ([Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians) and [Megleno-Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megleno-Romanians)): 200,000
- [Ethnic Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_speakers_of_Greek_Macedonia): 100,000–200,000
- [Arvanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvanites): 95,000
- [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_of_Western_Thrace): 90,000
- [Pomaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomaks): 35,000–40,000
- [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews): 5,000

The official language of Greece is [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), spoken by almost all as a second language at least. Additionally, there are a number of [linguistic minority groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minorities_in_Greece#Linguistic_and_cultural_communities) that are bilingual in a variety of non-Greek languages, and parts of these groups identify ethnically as [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks).

According to the [Greek constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Greece), [Eastern Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church) is recognized as the "prevailing religion" in Greece. During the centuries that Greece was part of the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Greece), besides its spiritual mandate, the [Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople), based in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) (present-day [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul)), also functioned as an official representative of the Christian population of the empire. The Church is often credited with the preservation of the [Greek language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), values, and national identity during Ottoman times. The Church was also an important rallying point in the war for independence against the Ottoman Empire, although the official Church in Constantinople initially condemned the breakout of the armed struggle in fear of retaliation from the Ottoman side. The [Church of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Greece) was established shortly after the formation of a Greek national state. Its authority to this day extends only to the areas included in the independent Greek state before the [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars) of 1912–1913. There is a [Muslim minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_minority_of_Greece) concentrated in [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Thrace) and officially protected by the [Treaty of Lausanne (1923)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne). Besides [Pomaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomaks) ([Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims) [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language)[^o170] speakers) and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people), it consists mainly of ethnic [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), who speak [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) and receive instruction in Turkish at special government-funded schools. There are also a number of [Jews in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Greece), most of whom live in [Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaloniki). There are also some Greeks who adhere to a [reconstruction of the ancient Greek religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenism_%28modern_religion%29).[^o171] A place of worship has been recognized as such by court.[^o172]

## Education {#Education}

[Greek education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Greece) is free and compulsory for children between the ages of 5 and 15. English study is compulsory from first grade through high school. University education, including books, is also free, contingent upon the student's ability to meet stiff entrance requirements. A high percentage of the student population seeks higher education. More than 100,000 students are registered at [Greek universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Greece), and 15% of the population currently holds a university degree. Admission in a university is determined by state-administered exams, the candidate's grade-point average from high school, and his/her priority choices of major. About one in four candidates gains admission to Greek universities.

Greek law does not currently offer official recognition to the graduates of private universities that operate in the country, except for those that offer a degree valid in another [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) country, which is automatically recognized by reciprocity. As a result, a large and growing number of students are pursuing higher education abroad. The [Greek Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Greece) decides through an evaluation procedure whether to recognize degrees from specific foreign universities as qualification for public sector hiring. Other students attend private, post-secondary educational institutions in Greece that are not recognized by the Greek Government. At the moment extensive public talk is made for the reform of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Greece) to recognize private higher education in Greece as equal with public and to place common regulations for both.

The number of Greek students studying at European institutions is increasing along with EU support for educational exchange. In addition, nearly 5,000 Greeks are studying in the United States, about half of whom are in graduate school. Greek per capita student representation in the US (one every 2,200) is among the highest in Europe.
