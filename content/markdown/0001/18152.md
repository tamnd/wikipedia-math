# Logical conjunction

*Image: Venn 0000 0001.svg.* [Venn diagram](wikipedia://en/61701) of $A \wedge B \land C$

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Logical conjunction"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), *and* ($\wedge$) is the [truth-functional](wikipedia://en/604707) operator of **conjunction** or **logical conjunction**. The [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) of this operator is typically represented as $\wedge$[^o3] or $\&$ or $K$ (prefix) or $\times$ or $\cdot$[^o4] in which $\wedge$ is the most modern and widely used.

:::

The *and* of a set of operands is true if and only if *all* of its operands are true, i.e., $A \land B$ is true if and only if $A$ is true and $B$ is true.

An operand of a conjunction is a **conjunct**.[^o5]

Beyond logic, the term "conjunction" also refers to similar concepts in other fields:

- In [natural language](wikipedia://en/21173), the [denotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denotation) of expressions such as [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) "[and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunction_%28grammar%29)";
- In [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015), the [short-circuit and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-circuit_evaluation) [control structure](wikipedia://en/45459);
- In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429).
- In [lattice theory](wikipedia://en/469304), logical conjunction ([greatest lower bound](wikipedia://en/39382)).

::: {#o7 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

**And** is usually denoted by an infix operator: in mathematics and logic, it is denoted by a "wedge" $\wedge$ (Unicode 2227),[^o3] $\&$ or $\times$; in electronics, $\cdot$; and in programming languages **`&`**, **`&&`**, or **`and`**. In [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064)'s [prefix notation for logic](wikipedia://en/25056#Polish_notation_for_logic), the operator is $K$, for Polish *koniunkcja*.[^o8]

In mathematics, the conjunction of an arbitrary number of elements $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ can be denoted as an [iterated binary operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterated_binary_operation) using a "big wedge" ⋀ (Unicode 22C0):[^o9]

$$\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n} a_i = a_1 \wedge a_2 \wedge \ldots a_{n-1} \wedge a_{n}$$

:::

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868), **logical conjunction** is an [operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_operation) on two [logical values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_value), typically the values of two [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094), that produces a value of *true* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) (also known as iff) both of its operands are true.[^o4][^o3]

The conjunctive [identity](wikipedia://en/14962) is true, which is to say that AND-ing an expression with true will never change the value of the expression. In keeping with the concept of [vacuous truth](wikipedia://en/51672), when conjunction is defined as an operator or function of arbitrary [arity](wikipedia://en/42301), the empty conjunction (AND-ing over an empty set of operands) is often defined as having the result true.

## Introduction and elimination rules {#Introduction_and_elimination_rules}

As a rule of inference, [conjunction introduction](wikipedia://en/6563) is a classically [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742), simple [argument form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_form). The argument form has two premises, $A$ and $B$. Intuitively, it permits the inference of their conjunction.

$$A,$$

$$B.$$

Therefore, *A* and *B*.

or in [logical operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_operator) notation, where $\vdash$ expresses provability:

$$\vdash A,$$

$$\vdash B$$

$$\vdash A \land B$$

Here is an example of an argument that fits the form *[conjunction introduction](wikipedia://en/6563)*:

Bob likes apples.

Bob likes oranges.

Therefore, Bob likes apples and Bob likes oranges.

[Conjunction elimination](wikipedia://en/788704) is another classically [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742), simple [argument form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_form). Intuitively, it permits the inference from any conjunction of either element of that conjunction.

$A$ and $B$.

Therefore, $A$.

...or alternatively,

$A$ and $B$.

Therefore, $B$.

In [logical operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_operator) notation:

$$\vdash A \land B$$

$$\vdash A$$

...or alternatively,

$$\vdash A \land B$$

$$\vdash B$$

## Negation {#Negation}

::: {#o18 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

A conjunction $A\land B$ is proven false by establishing either $\neg A$ or $\neg B$. In terms of the object language, this reads

$$\neg A\to\neg(A\land B)$$

This formula can be seen as a special case of

$$(A\to C) \to ( (A\land B)\to C )$$

when $C$ is a false proposition.

:::

### Other proof strategies {#Other_proof_strategies}

If $A$ implies $\neg B$, then both $\neg A$ as well as $A$ prove the conjunction false:

$$(A\to\neg{}B)\to\neg(A\land B)$$

In other words, a conjunction can actually be proven false just by knowing about the relation of its conjuncts, and not necessary about their truth values.

This formula can be seen as a special case of

$$(A\to(B\to C))\to ( (A\land B)\to C )$$

when $C$ is a false proposition.

Either of the above are constructively valid proofs by contradiction.

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Applications in computer engineering {#Applications_in_computer_engineering}

*Image: AND Gate diagram.svg.* AND [logic gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_gate)

In high-level computer programming and [digital electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_electronics), logical conjunction is commonly represented by an infix operator, usually as a keyword such as "`AND`", an algebraic multiplication, or the ampersand symbol `&` (sometimes doubled as in `&&`). Many languages also provide [short-circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-circuit_evaluation) control structures corresponding to logical conjunction.

Logical conjunction is often used for bitwise operations, where `0` corresponds to false and `1` to true:

- `0 AND 0` = `0`,
- `0 AND 1` = `0`,
- `1 AND 0` = `0`,
- `1 AND 1` = `1`.

The operation can also be applied to two binary [words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words) viewed as [bitstrings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitstring) of equal length, by taking the bitwise AND of each pair of bits at corresponding positions. For example:

- `11000110 AND 10100011` = `10000010`.

This can be used to select part of a bitstring using a [bit mask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mask_%28computing%29). For example, `1001'''1'''101 AND 0000'''1'''000` = `0000'''1'''000` extracts the fourth bit of an 8-bit bitstring.

In [computer networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_networking), bit masks are used to derive the network address of a [subnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnetwork) within an existing network from a given [IP address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address), by ANDing the IP address and the [subnet mask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnetwork#Binary_subnet_masks).

Logical conjunction "`AND`" is also used in [SQL](wikipedia://en/29004) operations to form [database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database) queries.

The [Curry–Howard correspondence](wikipedia://en/254299) relates logical conjunction to [product types](wikipedia://en/14892710).

## Set-theoretic correspondence {#Set-theoretic_correspondence}

The membership of an element of an [intersection set](wikipedia://en/23476429) in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) is defined in terms of a logical conjunction: $x\in A\cap B$ if and only if $(x\in A)\wedge (x\in B)$. Through this correspondence, set-theoretic intersection shares several properties with logical conjunction, such as [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity), [commutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity) and [idempotence](wikipedia://en/14972).

## Natural language {#Natural_language}

As with other notions formalized in mathematical logic, the logical conjunction *and* is related to, but not the same as, the [grammatical conjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_conjunction) *and* in natural languages.

English "and" has properties not captured by logical conjunction. For example, "and" sometimes implies order having the sense of "then". For example, "They got married and had a child" in common discourse means that the marriage came before the child.

The word "and" can also imply a partition of a thing into parts, as "The American flag is red, white, and blue." Here, it is not meant that the flag is *at once* red, white, and blue, but rather that each color is a part of the flag.
