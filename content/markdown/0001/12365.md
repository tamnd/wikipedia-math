# Googolplex

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Googolplex"}

A **googolplex** is the [large number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_number) $10^{10^{100}}$, that is, $10$ [raised to the power](wikipedia://en/1161104) of a [googol](wikipedia://en/18994679). If written out in ordinary [decimal notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_notation), it would be $1$ followed by a googol (10<sup>100</sup>) zeroes—‌a physically impossible number to write explicitly.

:::

## History {#History}

In 1920, [Edward Kasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kasner)'s nine-year-old nephew, Milton Sirotta, coined the term *[googol](wikipedia://en/18994679)*, which is 10${}^{100}$, and then proposed the further term *googolplex* to be "one, followed by writing zeroes until you get tired".[^o3] Kasner decided to adopt a more formal definition because "different people get tired at different times and it would never do to have [Carnera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primo_Carnera) \[be\] a better mathematician than [Dr. Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), simply because he had more endurance and could write for longer".[^o4] It thus became standardized to 10<sup>(10<sup>100</sup>)</sup>, which is usually written as 10<sup>10<sup>100</sup></sup> using the conventional interpretation for [serial exponentiation](wikipedia://en/212980#Serial_exponentiation).[^o5]

## Size {#Size}

A typical book can be printed with one million zeros (around 400 pages with 50 lines per page and 50 zeros per line). Therefore, it requires 94 such books to print all the zeros of a googolplex (that is, printing a googol zeros).[^o7] If each book had a mass of 100 grams, all of them would have a total mass of 93 kilograms. In comparison, [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228)'s mass is \5.97e24 kilograms,[^o8] the mass of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way) galaxy is estimated at \1.8e42 kilograms,[^o9] and the total mass of all the stars in the [observable universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable_universe) is estimated at 2e52 kg.[^o10]

Thus, the mass of all such books required to write out a googolplex would be vastly greater than the mass of the observable universe by a factor of roughly 5e40.

### In the physical universe {#In_the_physical_universe}

In the [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) science program *[Cosmos: A Personal Voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmos:_A_Personal_Voyage)*, [Episode 9: "The Lives of the Stars"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmos:_A_Personal_Voyage#Episode_9:_"The_Lives_of_the_Stars"), [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) and television personality [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan) estimated that writing a googolplex in full decimal form (i.e., "10,000,000,000...") would be physically impossible, since doing so would require more space than is available in the known universe. Sagan gave an example that if the entire volume of the [observable universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable_universe) is filled with fine [dust particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_dust) roughly 1.5 micrometers in size (0.0015 millimeters), then the number of different [combinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinations) in which the particles could be arranged and numbered would be about one googolplex.[^o12][^o13]

97 is a high estimate of the elementary particles existing in the visible universe (not including [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter)), mostly photons and other massless force carriers.[^o14]

## Mod *n* {#Mod_n}

The [residues (mod *n*)](wikipedia://en/20087) of a googolplex, starting with mod 1, are:

0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 4, 4, 0, 1, 0, 1, 4, 3, 4, 10, 0, 1, 10, 9, 0, 4, 12, 13, 16, 0, 16, 10, 4, 24, 10, 5, 0, 1, 18, 25, 28, 10, 28, 16, 0, 1, 4, 24, 12, 10, 36, 9, 16, 4, 0, ... [^o16]

This sequence is the same as the sequence of [residues (mod *n*) of a googol](wikipedia://en/18994679#Properties) up until the 17th position.[^o17]
