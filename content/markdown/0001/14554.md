::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Imaginary number"}

An **imaginary number** is the product of a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) and the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358) $i$,[^o4] which is defined by its property $i^{2} = -1$.[^o5][^o6] The [square](wikipedia://en/659942) of an imaginary number $bi$ is $-b^{2}$. For example, $5i$ is an imaginary number, and its square is $-25$. The number [zero](wikipedia://en/34513) is considered to be both real and imaginary.[^o7]

:::

Originally coined in the 17th century by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525)[^o8] as a derogatory term and regarded as fictitious or useless, the concept gained wide acceptance following the work of [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) in the 18th century, and [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842) and [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) in the early 19th century.

An imaginary number $bi$ can be added to a real number $a$ to form a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) of the form $a + bi$, where the real numbers $a$ and $b$ are called, respectively, the *real part* and the *imaginary part* of the complex number.[^o9] Imaginary numbers are often called *purely imaginary* to distinguish them from complex numbers more generally; the set of all imaginary numbers is sometimes denoted $i\mathbb{R}$, where $\mathbb{R}$ denotes the set of real numbers.

## History {#History}

*Image: Complex conjugate picture.svg.* An illustration of the complex plane. The imaginary numbers are on the vertical coordinate axis.

Although the Greek [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) [Heron of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heron_of_Alexandria) is noted as the first to present a calculation involving the square root of a negative number,[^o11][^o12] it was [Rafael Bombelli](wikipedia://en/1548091) who first set down the rules for multiplication of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) in 1572. The concept had appeared in print earlier, such as in *Ars Magna* (1545) by [Gerolamo Cardano](wikipedia://en/13145).[^o13] At the time, imaginary numbers and negative numbers were poorly understood and were regarded by some as fictitious or useless, much as zero once was. Many other mathematicians were slow to adopt the use of imaginary numbers, including [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525), who wrote about them in his *[La Géométrie](wikipedia://en/239981)* in which he coined the term *imaginary* and meant it to be derogatory.[^o14][^o15] The use of imaginary numbers was not widely accepted until the work of [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) (1707–1783) and [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) (1777–1855). The geometric significance of complex numbers as points in a plane was first described by [Caspar Wessel](wikipedia://en/21418268) (1745–1818).[^o16]

In 1843, [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) extended the idea of an axis of imaginary numbers in the plane to a four-dimensional space of [quaternion imaginaries](wikipedia://en/51440#Definition) in which three of the dimensions are analogous to the imaginary numbers in the complex field.

## Geometric interpretation {#Geometric_interpretation}

*Image: Rotations on the complex plane.svg.* $90$-degree rotations in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628)

Geometrically, imaginary numbers are found on the vertical axis of the [complex number plane](wikipedia://en/217628), which allows them to be presented [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to the real axis. One way of viewing imaginary numbers is: Firstly, to consider a standard [number line](wikipedia://en/287188), i.e., a horizontal $x$-axis on which positive real numbers increase in magnitude to the right and negative real numbers increase in magnitude to the left. Secondly, at $0$ on the $x$-axis, to consider a vertical $y$-axis with "positive" direction going up; then, "positive" imaginary numbers increase in magnitude upwards and "negative" imaginary numbers increase in magnitude downwards. This vertical axis is often called the "imaginary axis".[^o18]

In this representation, multiplication by $i$ corresponds to a counterclockwise [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789) of $90$ degrees about the origin, which is a quarter of a turn. Multiplication by $-i$ corresponds to a clockwise rotation of $90$ degrees about the origin. Similarly, multiplying by a purely imaginary number $bi$, with $b$ a real positive number, both causes a counterclockwise rotation about the origin by $90$ degrees and scales the answer by a factor of $b$. When $b < 0$, this can instead be described as a clockwise rotation by $90$ degrees and a scaling by $\lvert b\rvert$.[^o19]

## Square roots of negative numbers {#Square_roots_of_negative_numbers}

Care must be used when working with imaginary numbers that are expressed as the [principal values](wikipedia://en/408225) of the [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208) of [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616).[^o22] For example, the second equality in

$$\textstyle \sqrt{6} =\sqrt{(-2) \cdot (-3)} \mathrel{\stackrel{\text{ (invalid) }}{=}} \sqrt{-2} \cdot \sqrt{-3} = i\sqrt{2} \cdot i\sqrt{3} = -\sqrt{6}\,$$

is invalid: the identity $\sqrt{xy} = \sqrt{x} \sqrt{y}$ for nonnegative real numbers does not always hold for the principal branch of the complex square root function.

:::
