# Gregory Chaitin

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Gregory Chaitin"}

**Gregory John Chaitin** ( ; born 25 June 1947) is an [Argentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). His work was foundational to the development of [algorithmic information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_information_theory), and has been influential on [metamathematics](wikipedia://en/183508).[^o4][^o5] He independently discovered what is today known as algorithmic (Kolmogorov or Solomonoff–Kolmogorov–Chaitin) [complexity](wikipedia://en/1635) simultaneously with [Andrei Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Kolmogorov) and [Ray Solomonoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Solomonoff).[^o6]

:::

## Mathematics and computer science {#Mathematics_and_computer_science}

Gregory Chaitin is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish). He attended the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science) and the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York), where he (still in his teens) developed the theory that led to his independent discovery of [algorithmic complexity](wikipedia://en/1635).[^o8][^o9]

In 1975, Chaitin defined [Chaitin's constant](wikipedia://en/6205) Ω, a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) whose digits are [equidistributed](wikipedia://en/204277) and which is sometimes informally described as an expression of the probability that a random program will halt. Ω has the mathematical property that it is [definable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definable_number), with asymptotic approximations from below (but not from above), but not [computable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computability_theory_%28computation%29).

Chaitin is also the originator of using [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) to do [register allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_allocation) in [compiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiling), a process known as [Chaitin's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitin's_algorithm).[^o10]

He was formerly a researcher at IBM's Thomas J. Watson Research Center in New York, where he wrote more than 10 books that have been translated into about 15 languages.

Afterwards Chaitin became interested in questions of [metabiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabiology) and [information-theoretic](wikipedia://en/14773) formalizations of the theory of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution), and he was one of the founding members of the Institute for Advanced Studies at [Mohammed VI Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_VI_Polytechnic_University) in Morocco.

## Other scholarly contributions {#Other_scholarly_contributions}

Chaitin also writes about [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), especially [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) (particularly about epistemological matters in mathematics). In metaphysics, Chaitin claims that [algorithmic information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_information_theory) is the key to solving problems in the field of [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) (obtaining a formal definition of 'life', its origin and [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution)) and [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience) (the problem of [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness) and the study of the mind).

In recent writings, he defends a position known as [digital philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_physics). In the [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology) of mathematics, he claims that his findings in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and algorithmic information theory show there are "mathematical facts that are true for no reason, that are true by accident".[^o12] Chaitin proposes that mathematicians must abandon any hope of proving those mathematical facts and adopt a [quasi-empirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-empirical) methodology.

## Honors {#Honors}

In 1995 he was given the degree of doctor of science *[honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa)* by the [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine). In 2002 he was given the title of honorary professor by the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires) in Argentina, where his parents were born and where Chaitin spent part of his youth. In 2007 he was given a [Leibniz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_Medal_%28Wolfram_Research%29)[^o14] by [Wolfram Research](wikipedia://en/594109); the medal was designed by [Stephen Wolfram](wikipedia://en/52469) and Hector Zenil, using [Chaitin’s number](wikipedia://en/6205) calculated by [Cristian Calude](wikipedia://en/23907691). In 2009 he was given the degree of doctor of philosophy *honoris causa* by the [National University of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Córdoba). He was formerly a researcher at [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)'s [Thomas J. Watson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center) and a professor at the [Federal University of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Rio_de_Janeiro).
