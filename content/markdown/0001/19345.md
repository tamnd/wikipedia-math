# May

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="May"}

**May** is the fifth month of the year in the [Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar) and [Gregorian calendars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar). Its length is 31 days.

:::

May is a month of [spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_%28season%29) in the Northern Hemisphere, and [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn) in the Southern Hemisphere. Therefore, May in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere) is the seasonal equivalent of [November](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November) in the Northern Hemisphere and vice versa. Late May typically marks the start of the summer vacation season in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) ([Memorial Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day)) and [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) ([Victoria Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Day)) that ends on [Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day), the first Monday of September.

May (in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), *[Maius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maius)*) was named for the [Greek goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_god) [Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maia_%28mythology%29), who was identified with the [Roman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_era) [goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddess) of fertility, [Bona Dea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bona_Dea), whose [festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival) was held in May. Conversely, the Roman poet [Ovid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovid) provides a second etymology, in which he says that the month of May is named for the maiores*,* Latin for "elders", and that the following month (June) is named for the iuniores*,* or "young people" (*Fasti VI.88*).

[Eta Aquariids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eta_Aquariids) meteor shower appears in May. It is visible from about April 23 to about May 20 each year with peak activity on or around May 6. The [Arietids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arietids) shower from May 22 to July 2, and peaks on June 7. The [Virginids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginids) also shower at various dates in May.

## Ancient Roman observances {#Ancient_Roman_observances}

Under the calendar of ancient Rome, the festival of [Bona Dea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bona_Dea) fell on May 1, [Argei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argei) fell on May 14 or May 15, [Agonalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agonalia) fell on May 21, and [Ambarvalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambarvalia) on May 29. [Floralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floralia) was held April 27 during the [Republican era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic), or April 28 on the [Julian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar), and lasted until May 3. [Lemuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria_%28festival%29) fell on 9,11, and 13 May under the Julian calendar. The [College of Aesculapius and Hygia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Aesculapius_and_Hygia) celebrated two festivals of [Rosalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalia_%28festival%29), one on May 11 and one on May 22. Rosalia was also celebrated at [Pergamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon) on May 24–26. A military Rosalia festival, [Rosaliae signorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosaliae_signorum), also occurred on May 31. [Ludi Fabarici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalia_%28festival%29#Military_Rosaliae) was celebrated May 29 – June 1. [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_%28god%29) would receive a sacrifice on the Ides of May (May 15). [Tubilustrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubilustrium) took place on May 23 as well as in March. These dates do not correspond to the modern [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar).

## Symbols {#Symbols}

*Image: Hooker emerald.jpg.* [Emerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald) brooch

May's [birthstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthstone) is the [emerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald), which is emblematic of love and success. Birth flowers are the [Lily of the Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_of_the_Valley) and *[Crataegus monogyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crataegus_monogyna)*.[^o6] Both are native throughout the cool temperate Northern Hemisphere in Asia, Europe, and in the southern Appalachian Mountains in the United States, but have been naturalized throughout the temperate climatic world.

*Image: Lily of the valley.jpg.* The [Lily of the Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_of_the_Valley)

*Image: Crataegus-monogyna.JPG.* *[Crataegus monogyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crataegus_monogyna)*

*Image: Trailing arbutus.jpg.* Mayflowers

The "Mayflower" *[Epigaea repens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigaea_repens)* is a North American harbinger of May, and the floral emblem of both [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia) and [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts). Its native range extends from Newfoundland south to Florida, west to Kentucky in the southern range, and to Northwest Territories in the north. The [zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zodiac) signs are [Taurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_%28astrology%29) (until May 20) and [Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_%28astrology%29) (May 21 onward).[^o7][^o8]

- [April 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_29) to [May 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_5) in Japan, which includes four different holidays, is called "[Golden Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Week_%28Japan%29)". Many workers have up to 10 days off. There is also 'May sickness', where new students or workers start to be tired of their new routine. (In Japan the [school year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_year) and [fiscal year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year) start on April 1.)
- [Mayovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayovka), in the context of the late [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), was a [picnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picnic) in the countryside or in a park in the early days of May, hence the name. Eventually, "mayovka" (specifically, "proletarian mayovka") came to mean an illegal celebration of [May 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1) by revolutionary public, typically presented as an innocent picnic.[^o84]
