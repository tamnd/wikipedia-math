# Enrico Bombieri

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Enrico Bombieri"}

**Enrico Bombieri** (born 26 November 1940) is an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).[^o7] Bombieri is currently professor emeritus in the School of Mathematics at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey).[^o8] Bombieri won the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1974[^o7] for his work on the [large sieve](wikipedia://en/1967859) and its application to the distribution of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).[^o9]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Bombieri published his first mathematical paper in 1957, when he was 16 years old. In 1963, at age 22, he earned his first degree (Laurea) in mathematics from the [Università degli Studi di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_degli_Studi_di_Milano) under the supervision of Giovanni Ricci[^o11] and then studied at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), with [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111).[^o12]

Bombieri was an assistant professor (1963–1965) and then a full professor (1965–1966) at the [Università di Cagliari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cagliari), at the [Università di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_di_Pisa) in 1966–1974, and then at the [Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa) in 1974–1977. From Pisa, he emigrated in 1977 to the United States, where he became a professor at the School of Mathematics at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). In 2011, he became professor emeritus.[^o12]

Bombieri is also known for his *[pro bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_bono)* service on behalf of the [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) [profession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profession), e.g. for serving on external review boards and for [peer-reviewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review) extraordinarily complicated manuscripts (like the paper of [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897) on the [invariant subspace problem](wikipedia://en/691927)).[^o13]

## Research {#Research}

The [Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem](wikipedia://en/4632596) is one of the major applications of the [large sieve method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_sieve_method). It improves [Dirichlet's theorem](wikipedia://en/101453) on prime numbers in [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389), by showing that by averaging over the modulus over a range, the mean error is much less than can be proved in a given case. This result can sometimes substitute for the still-unproved [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567).

In 1969, Bombieri, [De Giorgi](wikipedia://en/1752401), and [Giusti](wikipedia://en/32836383) solved [Bernstein's problem](wikipedia://en/30486183) on minimal surfaces in dimensions above eight.[^o15]

In 1976, Bombieri developed the technique known as the "asymptotic sieve".[^o16] In 1980, he supplied the completion of the proof of the uniqueness of [finite groups of Ree type](wikipedia://en/1739637) in [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 3; at the time of its publication, it was one of the missing steps in the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).[^o17]

## Awards {#Awards}

Bombieri's research in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) has earned him many international prizes — a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1974 and the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize) in 1980. He was a [plenary speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065), which took place in 1974 in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver). He is a member, or foreign member, of several learned academies, including the [Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_dei_Lincei) (elected 1976), the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) (elected 1984), the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) (elected 1995),[^o19] and the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) (elected 1996).[^o20] In 2002 he was made *Cavaliere di Gran Croce al Merito della Repubblica Italiana*.[^o21] In 2010, he received the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize) (jointly with [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498)).[^o22][^o23] and in 2020 he was awarded the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in Mathematics.[^o24]

## Other interests {#Other_interests}

Bombieri, accomplished also in the arts, explored for wild orchids and other plants as a hobby in the Alps when a young man.[^o26]

With his powder-blue shirt open at the neck, khaki pants and running shoes, he might pass for an Italian film director at Cannes. Married with a grown daughter, he is a gourmet cook and a serious painter: He carries his paints and brushes with him whenever he travels. Still, mathematics never seems far from his mind. In a recent painting, Bombieri, a one-time member of the [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) chess team, depicts a giant chessboard by a lake. He's placed the pieces to reflect a critical point in the historic match in which [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)'s chess-playing computers, [Deep Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_%28chess_computer%29), beat [Garry Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov).[^o27]
