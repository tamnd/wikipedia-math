# Frederick Soddy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frederick Soddy"}

**Frederick Soddy** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)[^o3] (2 September 1877 – 22 September 1956) was an English [radiochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiochemistry) who explained, with [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford), that [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity) is due to the [transmutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_transmutation) of [elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element), now known to involve [nuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactions). He also proved the existence of [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope) of certain radioactive elements.[^o4][^o5][^o6] In 1921, he received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Chemistry) "for his contributions to our knowledge of the chemistry of radioactive substances, and his investigations into the origin and nature of isotopes". Soddy was a [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) who mastered [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics), [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics).[^o7][^o8]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Soddy was born at 6 Bolton Road, [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne), England,[^o10] the son of Benjamin Soddy, corn merchant, and his wife Hannah Green. He went to school at [Eastbourne College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_College), before going on to study at [University College of Wales at Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University) and at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford), where he graduated in 1898 with first class honours in chemistry.[^o11] He was a researcher at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) from 1898 to 1900.

## Scientific career {#Scientific_career}

In 1900, he became a demonstrator in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal), [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec), where he worked with [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford) on [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity).[^o13][^o11] He and Rutherford realized that the anomalous behaviour of radioactive elements was because they [decayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay) into other elements. This decay also produced [alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_ray), [beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_ray), and [gamma radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_ray). When radioactivity was first discovered, no one was sure what the cause was. It needed careful work by Soddy and Rutherford to prove that atomic [transmutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_transmutation) was in fact occurring.[^o14]

In 1903, with Sir [William Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ramsay) at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), Soddy showed that the decay of [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium) produced [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium) gas.[^o11] In the experiment a sample of radium was enclosed in a thin-walled glass envelope sited within an evacuated glass bulb. After leaving the experiment running for a long period of time, a spectral analysis of the contents of the former evacuated space revealed the presence of helium.[^o15] Later in 1907, Rutherford and [Thomas Royds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Royds) showed that the helium was first formed as positively charged nuclei of helium (He<sup>2+</sup>) which were identical to [alpha particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particle), which could pass through the thin glass wall but were contained within the surrounding glass envelope.[^o16]

From 1904 to 1914, Soddy was a lecturer at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow). [Ruth Pirret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Pirret) worked as his research assistant during this time.[^o17] In May 1910 Soddy was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).[^o3] In 1914 he was appointed to a chair at the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen), where he worked on research related to [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I).

In 1913, Soddy showed that an atom moves lower in [atomic number](wikipedia://en/673) by two places on alpha emission, higher by one place on beta emission. This was discovered at about the same time by [Kazimierz Fajans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Fajans), and is known as the [radioactive displacement law of Fajans and Soddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_displacement_law_of_Fajans_and_Soddy), a fundamental step toward understanding the relationships among families of radioactive elements. In 1913 Soddy also described the phenomenon in which a radioactive element may have more than one [atomic mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_mass) though the chemical properties are identical. He named this concept [isotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope) meaning "same place".[^o18][^o19] The word was initially suggested to him by [Margaret Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Todd_%28doctor%29).[^o20] Later, [J. J. Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Thomson) showed that non-radioactive elements can also have multiple isotopes.

The work that Soddy and his research assistant [Ada Hitchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Hitchins) did at Glasgow and Aberdeen showed that [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium) decays to [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium).[^o21][^o22]

Soddy published [*The Interpretation of Radium*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iarchive:interpretationof00sodd) (1909) and *Atomic Transmutation* (1953).

In 1918, working with the Scottish scientist [John Arnold Cranston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Arnold_Cranston), he announced the discovery of an isotope of the element later named [protactinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protactinium).[^o23] This slightly post-dated its discovery by the Germans [Lise Meitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Meitner) and [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn); however, it is said their discovery was actually made in 1915 but its announcement was delayed due to Cranston's notes being locked away whilst on active service in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War).[^o24]

In 1919, he moved to the University of Oxford as the first [Dr. Lee's Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Lee's_Professorships) of Chemistry, where, in the period up till 1936, he reorganized the laboratories and the syllabus in chemistry. He received the 1921 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry) for his research in radioactive decay and particularly for his formulation of the theory of isotopes.

In Soddy's 1922 paper "Radioactivity and the Evolution of the World" there is a section on Soddy's belief that knowledge in the form of myths had been passed down to the present day from an ancient, advanced civilisation.[^o25][^o26]

His work and essays popularising the new understanding of radioactivity was the main inspiration for [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells)'s *[The World Set Free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Set_Free)* (1914), which features atomic bombs dropped from biplanes in a war set many years in the future. Wells's novel is also known as *The Last War* and imagines a peaceful world emerging from the chaos. In *[Wealth, Virtual Wealth and Debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth,_Virtual_Wealth_and_Debt)* Soddy praises Wells's *The World Set Free*. He also says that radioactive processes probably power the stars.

## Economics {#Economics}

People In four books written from 1921 to 1934, Soddy carried on a "campaign for a radical restructuring of global monetary relationships",[^o28] offering a perspective on economics rooted in physics – the [laws of thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/778700), in particular – and was "roundly dismissed as a crank".[^o28] While most of his proposals – "to abandon the [gold standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_standard), let international [exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate) float, use federal [surpluses and deficits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficit_spending) as [macroeconomic policy tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_policy_instruments) that could counter [cyclical trends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cycle), and establish bureaus of [economic statistics](wikipedia://en/340363) (including a [consumer price index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_price_index)) in order to facilitate this effort" – are now conventional practice, his critique of [fractional-reserve banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional-reserve_banking) still "remains outside the bounds of conventional wisdom" although a recent paper by the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund) reinvigorated his proposals.[^o28][^o29] Soddy wrote that financial debts grew exponentially at compound interest but the real economy was based on exhaustible stocks of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels). Energy obtained from the fossil fuels could not be used again. This criticism of [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) is echoed by his intellectual heirs in the emergent field of [ecological economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_economics).[^o28]

*[The New Palgrave Dictionary of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Palgrave_Dictionary_of_Economics)*, an influential reference text in economics, recognized Soddy as a "reformer" for his works on monetary reforms.[^o30]

## Political views {#Political_views}

In *[Wealth, Virtual Wealth and Debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth,_Virtual_Wealth_and_Debt),* Soddy cited the *[Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocols_of_the_Learned_Elders_of_Zion)*, which had been widely disseminated by [Henry Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford) in the United States, as evidence that the belief in a "financial conspiracy to enslave the world" was widespread at the time.[^o32] He further wrote that "conscious conspiracy or not a corrupt monetary system strikes at the very life of the nation".[^o33] Later in life he published a pamphlet *Abolish Private Money, or Drown in Debt* (1939).[^o34]

The influence of his writing can be gauged, for example, in this quote from [Ezra Pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Pound):

Though some activists have accused Soddy of anti-Semitism, some argue that among Soddy's friends and students were some Jews who held positive views of him.[^o6][^o7]

## Descartes' theorem {#Descartes'_theorem}

He rediscovered [Descartes' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes'_theorem) in 1936 and published it as a poem, "The Kiss Precise",[^o38] quoted at [Problem of Apollonius](wikipedia://en/2642185#kiss_precise). The [kissing circles](wikipedia://en/1896705) in this problem are sometimes known as **Soddy circles**.[^o39]

:::

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

He received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry) in 1921 and the same year was elected member of the [International Atomic Weights Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Isotopic_Abundances_and_Atomic_Weights). A small [crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soddy_%28crater%29) on the [far side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon) as well as the radioactive uranium mineral [soddyite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soddyite) are named after him.[^o41] The author [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells) dedicated his novel *[The World Set Free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Set_Free)* to Soddy's *Interpretation of Radium* (1909).[^o42]

::: {#o44 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Winifred Moller Soddy"}

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1908, Soddy married Winifred Moller Beilby (1885–1936), the daughter of industrial chemist Sir [George Beilby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Beilby) and Lady Emma Bielby, a philanthropist to women's causes. The couple worked together and co-published a paper in 1910 on the absorption of gamma rays from radium.[^o45] He died in [Brighton, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton,_England) in 1956, twenty days after his 79th birthday.[^o11]

:::
