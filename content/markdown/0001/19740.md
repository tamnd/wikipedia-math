# Max August Zorn

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Max August Zorn"}

**Max August Zorn** (; June 6, 1906 – March 9, 1993) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was an [algebraist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_Algebra), [group theorist](wikipedia://en/19447), and [numerical analyst](wikipedia://en/21506). He is best known for [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442), a method used in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) that is applicable to a wide range of mathematical constructs such as [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), and [ordered sets](wikipedia://en/23572) amongst others. Zorn's lemma was first postulated by [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) in 1922, and then independently by Zorn in 1935.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Zorn was born in [Krefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krefeld), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). He attended the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in April 1930 for a thesis on [alternative algebras](wikipedia://en/3160). He published his findings in *[Abhandlungen aus dem Mathematischen Seminar der Universität Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhandlungen_aus_dem_Mathematischen_Seminar_der_Universität_Hamburg)*.[^o4][^o5] Zorn showed that [split-octonions](wikipedia://en/3861649) could be represented by a mixed-style of matrices called [Zorn's vector-matrix algebra](wikipedia://en/3861649#Zorn.27s_vector-matrix_algebra).

Max Zorn was appointed to an assistant position at the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle). However, he did not have the opportunity to work there for long as he was forced to leave Germany in 1933 because of policies enacted by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi). According to grandson Eric, "\[Max\] spoke with a raspy, airy voice most of his life. Few people knew why, because he only told the story after significant prodding, but he talked that way because pro-Hitler thugs who objected to his politics had battered his throat in a 1933 street fight."[^o6] Zorn had stood as a candidate on a socialist list for the [AStA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AStA) elections in Hamburg and was a member of the [Communist Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Germany).[^o7]

Zorn immigrated to the United States and was appointed a [Sterling Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Fellow) at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). While at Yale, Zorn wrote his paper "A Remark on Method in Transfinite Algebra"[^o8] that stated his Maximum Principle, later called [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442). It requires a set that contains the union of any [chain](wikipedia://en/30330#Chains) of [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) to have one chain not contained in any other, called the [maximal element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element). He illustrated the principle with applications in ring theory and field extensions. Zorn's lemma is an alternative expression of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), and thus a subject of interest in [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory).

In 1936 he moved to [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) and remained until 1946. While at UCLA Zorn revisited his study of alternative rings and proved the existence of the [nilradical](wikipedia://en/289636) of certain [alternative rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_ring).[^o9] According to [Angus E. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_E._Taylor), Max was his most stimulating colleague at UCLA.[^o10]

In 1946 Zorn became a professor at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_%28Bloomington%29), where he taught until retiring in 1971. He was thesis advisor for [Israel Nathan Herstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Nathan_Herstein).

Zorn died in [Bloomington, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Indiana), in March 1993, of congestive heart failure.[^o11]

## Family {#Family}

Max Zorn married Alice Schlottau and they had one son, Jens, and one daughter, Liz. Jens (June 19, 1931 — Jan. 5, 2026)[^o13] was a professor of physics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and an accomplished sculptor. Max Zorn's grandson [Eric Zorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Zorn) was a columnist for the *[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune)* from 1986 until 2021; after retirement Eric Zorn started a newsletter titled *The Picayune Sentinel,*[^o14] named after the mathematics newsletter that his grandfather had distributed during his years at Indiana University. Max's great-grandson, Alexander Wolken Zorn, received a PhD in mathematics from the [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 2018.[^o15]
