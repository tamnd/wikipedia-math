# Fundamental theorem on homomorphisms

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fundamental theorem on homomorphisms"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **fundamental theorem on homomorphisms**, also known as the **fundamental homomorphism theorem**, the **first isomorphism theorem**, or just the **homomorphism theorem**, relates the structure of two objects between which a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) is given, and of the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) and [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of the homomorphism.

:::

The homomorphism theorem is used to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) the [isomorphism theorems](wikipedia://en/45241). Similar theorems are valid for [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), and [rings](wikipedia://en/48404).

It dates back to the work of [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106), and was further formalized by [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) into the isomorphism theorems.[^o2][^o3]

## Group-theoretic version {#Group-theoretic_version}

*Image: Diagram of the fundamental theorem on homomorphisms.svg.* Diagram of the fundamental theorem on homomorphisms, where $f$ is a homomorphism, $N$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ and $e$ is the identity element of $G$.

Given two [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ and $H$ and a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $f: G \rightarrow H$, let $N$ be a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) in $G$ and $\varphi$ the natural [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) homomorphism $G \rightarrow G / N$ (where $G / N$ is the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) of $G$ by $N$). If $N$ is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of $\ker(f)$ (where $\ker$ represents a [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240)) then there exists a unique homomorphism $h: G / N \rightarrow H$ such that $f = h \circ \varphi$.

In other words, the natural projection $\varphi$ is [universal](wikipedia://en/32245) among homomorphisms on $G$ that map $N$ to the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962).

The situation is described by the following [commutative diagram](wikipedia://en/138484):

*Image: Fundamental Homomorphism Theorem v2.svg.*

$h$ is injective if and only if $N = \ker(f)$. Therefore, by setting $N = \ker(f)$, we immediately get the [first isomorphism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_isomorphism_theorem).

We can write the statement of the fundamental theorem on homomorphisms of groups as "every homomorphic image of a group is isomorphic to a quotient group".

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The proof follows from two basic facts about homomorphisms, namely their preservation of the group operation, and their mapping of the identity element to the identity element. We need to show that if $f: G \to H$ is a homomorphism of groups, then:

1. $\text{im}(f)$ is a subgroup of $H$.
1. $G / \ker(f)$ is isomorphic to $\text{im}(f)$.

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of 1"}

### Proof of 1 {#Proof_of_1}

The operation that is preserved by $f$ is the group operation. If $a, b \in \text{im}(f)$, then there exist elements $a', b' \in G$ such that $f(a')=a$ and $f(b')=b$. For these $a$ and $b$, we have $ab = f(a')f(b') = f(a'b') \in \text{im}(f)$ (since $f$ preserves the group operation), and thus, the closure property is satisfied in $\text{im}(f)$. The identity element $e \in H$ is also in $\text{im}(f)$ because $f$ maps the identity element of $G$ to it. Since every element $a'$ in $G$ has an inverse $(a')^{-1}$ such that $f((a')^{-1}) = (f(a'))^{-1}$ (because $f$ preserves the inverse property as well), we have an inverse for each element $f(a') = a$ in $\text{im}(f)$, therefore, $\text{im}(f)$ is a subgroup of $H$.

:::

::: {#o10 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of 2"}

### Proof of 2 {#Proof_of_2}

Construct a map $\psi: G / \ker(f) \to \text{im}(f)$ by $\psi(a\ker(f)) = f(a)$. This map is well-defined, as if $a\ker(f) = b\ker(f)$, then $b^{-1}a \in \ker(f)$ and so $f(b^{-1}a) = e \Rightarrow f(b^{-1})f(a) = e$ which gives $f(a) = f(b)$. This map is an isomorphism. $\psi$ is surjective onto $\text{im}(f)$ by definition. To show injectivity, if $\psi(a\ker(f)) = \psi(b\ker(f))$, then $f(a) = f(b)$, which implies $b^{-1}a \in\ker(f)$ so $a\ker(f) = b\ker(f)$.

Finally,

$$\psi((a\ker(f))(b\ker(f))) = \psi(ab\ker(f)) = f(ab)$$

$$= f(a)f(b) = \psi(a\ker(f))\psi(b\ker(f)),$$

hence $\psi$ preserves the group operation. Hence $\psi$ is an isomorphism between $G / \ker(f)$ and $\text{im}(f)$, which completes the proof.

:::

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

The group theoretic version of the fundamental homomorphism theorem can be used to show that two selected groups are isomorphic. Two examples are shown below.

### Integers modulo *n* {#Integers_modulo_n}

For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, consider the groups $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_n$ and a group homomorphism $f:\mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}_n$ defined by $m \mapsto m \text{ mod }n$ (see [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087)). Next, consider the kernel of $f$, $\text{ker} (f) = n \mathbb{Z}$, which is a normal subgroup in $\mathbb{Z}$. There exists a natural surjective homomorphism $\varphi : \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ defined by $m \mapsto m+n\mathbb{Z}$. The theorem asserts that there exists an isomorphism $h$ between $\mathbb{Z}_n$ and $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, or in other words $\mathbb{Z}_n \cong \mathbb{Z}/n \mathbb{Z}$. ### *N / C* theorem {#N_/_C_theorem}

Let $G$ be a group with [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $H$. Let $C_G(H)$, $N_G(H)$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ be the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer), the [normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) and the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of $H$ in $G$, respectively. Then, the $N/C$ theorem states that $N_G(H)/C_G(H)$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$.

::: {#o17 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

#### Proof {#Proof}

We are able to find a group homomorphism $f: N_G(H) \rightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(H)$ defined by $g \mapsto ghg^{-1}$, for all $h \in H$. Clearly, the kernel of $f$ is $C_G(H)$. Hence, we have a natural surjective homomorphism $\varphi : N_G(H) \rightarrow N_G(H)/C_G(H)$ defined by $g \mapsto gC(H)$. The fundamental homomorphism theorem then asserts that there exists an isomorphism between $N_G(H)/C_G(H)$ and $\varphi(N_G(H))$, which is a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$.

:::
