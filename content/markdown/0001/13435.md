# Hydrology

*Image: Rain\_over\_Beinn\_Eich,\_Luss\_Hills,\_Scotland.jpg.* Rain falling over a [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland). Understanding the cycling of water into, through, and out of catchments is a key element of hydrology.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hydrology"}

**Hydrology** (grc) is the scientific study of the movement, distribution, and management of water on Earth and other planets, including the [water cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_cycle), [water resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources), and [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) sustainability. A practitioner of hydrology is called a **hydrologist**. Hydrologists are scientists studying [earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_science) or [environmental science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_science), [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) or [environmental engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_engineering), and [physical geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_geography).[^o2] Using various analytical methods and scientific techniques, they collect and analyze data to help solve water related problems such as [environmental preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism), [natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disaster), and [water management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resource_management).[^o2]

:::

Hydrology subdivides into surface water hydrology, [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater) hydrology ([hydrogeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogeology)), and marine hydrology. Domains of hydrology include [hydrometeorology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrometeorology), [surface hydrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-water_hydrology), [hydrogeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogeology), [drainage-basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) management, and [water quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_quality).

[Oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography) and [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904) are not included because water is only one of many important aspects within those fields.

Hydrological research can inform environmental engineering, [policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy), and [planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_planning).

## Branches {#Branches}

- [Chemical hydrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_hydrology) is the study of the chemical characteristics of water.
- [Ecohydrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecohydrology) is the study of interactions between organisms and the hydrologic cycle.
- [Hydrogeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogeology) is the study of the presence and movement of [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater).
- [Hydrogeochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogeochemistry) is the study of how terrestrial water dissolves minerals [weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weathering) and this effect on water chemistry.
- [Hydroinformatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroinformatics) is the adaptation of information technology to hydrology and water resources applications.
- [Hydrometeorology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrometeorology) is the study of the transfer of water and energy between land and water body surfaces and the lower atmosphere.
- [Isotope hydrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_hydrology) is the study of the isotopic signatures of water.
- [Surface hydrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-water_hydrology) is the study of hydrologic processes that operate at or near Earth's surface.
- [Drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) management covers water storage, in the form of reservoirs, and floods protection.
- [Water quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_quality) includes the chemistry of water in rivers and lakes, both of pollutants and natural solutes.

- Providing [drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water).

- Designing [bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge).
- Designing [sewers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitary_sewer) and urban drainage systems.
- Analyzing the impacts of [antecedent moisture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antecedent_moisture) on sanitary sewer systems.
- Predicting [geomorphologic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphology) changes, such as [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) or [sedimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentation).
- Assessing the impacts of natural and anthropogenic environmental change on [water resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources).
- Assessing [contaminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) transport risk and establishing environmental policy guidelines.
- Estimating the water resource potential of river basins.
- [Water resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources) management.
- [Water resources engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources_engineering) - application of hydrological and hydraulic principles to the planning, development, and management of water resources for beneficial human use. It involves assessing water availability, quality, and demand; designing and operating water infrastructure; and implementing strategies for sustainable water management.[^o6]

## History {#History}

*Image: Caesarea\_maritima\_BW\_3.JPG.* The Roman aqueduct at [Caesarea Maritima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima), bringing water from the wetter [Carmel mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel) to the settlement

Hydrology has been subject to investigation and engineering for millennia. [Ancient Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) were some of the first to employ hydrology in their engineering and agriculture, inventing a form of water management known as basin irrigation.[^o9] [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) towns were protected from flooding with high earthen walls. [Aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_%28watercourse%29) were built by the [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) and [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Romans), while [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China) shows that the Chinese built irrigation and flood control works. The ancient [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) used hydrology to build complex irrigation works in [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka), also known for the invention of the [valve pit (*bisokotuwas*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_cascade_system) which allowed construction of large reservoirs, [anicuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anicut) and canals which still function.

[Marcus Vitruvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitruvius), in the first century BC, described a philosophical theory of the hydrologic cycle, in which precipitation falling in the mountains infiltrated the Earth's surface and led to streams and springs in the lowlands.[^o10] With the adoption of a more scientific approach, [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) and [Bernard Palissy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Palissy) independently reached an accurate representation of the hydrologic cycle. It was not until the 17th century that hydrologic variables began to be quantified.

Pioneers of the modern science of hydrology include [Pierre Perrault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Perrault_%28scientist%29), [Edme Mariotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edme_Mariotte) and [Edmund Halley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Halley). By measuring rainfall, runoff, and drainage area, Perrault showed that rainfall was sufficient to account for the flow of the Seine. Mariotte combined velocity and river cross-section measurements to obtain a discharge value, again in the Seine. Halley showed that the evaporation from the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea) was sufficient to account for the outflow of rivers flowing into the sea.[^o11]

Advances in the 18th century included the [Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784) [piezometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezometer) and [Bernoulli's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli's_equation), by [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784), and the [Pitot tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitot_tube), by [Henri Pitot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Pitot). The 19th century saw development in groundwater hydrology, including [Darcy's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy's_law), the Dupuit-Thiem well formula, and [Hagen-Poiseuille](wikipedia://en/17016531)'s capillary flow equation.

Rational analyses began to replace empiricism in the 20th century, while governmental agencies began their own hydrological research programs. Of particular importance were Leroy Sherman's [unit hydrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_hydrograph), the infiltration theory of [Robert E. Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Horton), and [C.V. Theis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.V._Theis)' aquifer test/equation describing well hydraulics.

Since the 1950s, hydrology has been approached with a more theoretical basis than in the past, facilitated by advances in the physical understanding of hydrological processes and by the advent of computers and especially [geographic information systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_systems) (GIS). (See also [GIS and hydrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIS_and_hydrology))

:::

## Themes {#Themes}

The central theme of hydrology is that water circulates throughout the [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228) through different pathways and at different rates. The most vivid image of this is in the evaporation of water from the ocean, which forms clouds. These clouds drift over the land and produce rain. The rainwater flows into lakes, rivers, or aquifers. The water in lakes, rivers, and aquifers then either evaporates back to the atmosphere or eventually flows back to the ocean, completing a cycle. Water changes its state of being several times throughout this cycle.

The areas of research within hydrology concern the movement of water between its various states, or within a given state, or simply quantifying the amounts in these states in a given region. Parts of hydrology concern developing methods for directly measuring these flows or amounts of water, while others concern modeling these processes either for scientific knowledge or for making a prediction in practical applications.

### Groundwater {#Groundwater}

*Image: Building a map of groundwater countours.gif.* Building a map of groundwater contours

Ground water is water beneath Earth's surface, often pumped for drinking water.[^o2] Groundwater hydrology ([hydrogeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogeology)) considers quantifying groundwater flow and solute transport.[^o14] Problems in describing the saturated zone include the characterization of aquifers in terms of flow direction, groundwater pressure and, by inference, groundwater depth (see: [aquifer test](wikipedia://en/1079129)). Measurements here can be made using a [piezometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezometer). Aquifers are also described in terms of hydraulic conductivity, storativity and transmissivity. There are a number of geophysical methods[^o79] for characterizing aquifers. There are also problems in characterizing the vadose zone (unsaturated zone).[^o78]

### Infiltration {#Infiltration}

Infiltration is the process by which water enters the soil. Some of the water is absorbed, and the rest [percolates](wikipedia://en/299184) down to the [water table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_table). The infiltration capacity, the maximum rate at which the soil can absorb water, depends on several factors. The layer that is already saturated provides a resistance that is proportional to its thickness, while that plus the depth of water above the soil provides the driving force ([hydraulic head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_head)). Dry soil can allow rapid infiltration by [capillary action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_action); this force diminishes as the soil becomes wet. [Compaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compaction_%28geology%29) reduces the porosity and the pore sizes. Surface cover increases capacity by retarding runoff, reducing compaction and other processes. Higher temperatures reduce [viscosity](wikipedia://en/18963754), increasing infiltration.[^o16]

### Soil moisture {#Soil_moisture}

Soil moisture can be measured in various ways; by [capacitance probe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitance_probe), [time domain reflectometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_domain_reflectometer) or [tensiometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensiometer_%28soil_science%29). Other methods include solute sampling and geophysical methods.[^o18]

### Surface water flow {#Surface_water_flow}

*Image: Wlmm3\_hg.png.* A [flood hydrograph](wikipedia://en/881602) showing [stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_%28hydrology%29) for the [Shawsheen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawsheen_River) at Wilmington

Hydrology considers quantifying surface water flow and solute transport, although the treatment of flows in large rivers is sometimes considered as a distinct topic of hydraulics or hydrodynamics. Surface water flow can include flow both in recognizable river channels and otherwise. Methods for measuring flow once the water has reached a river include the [stream gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_gauge) (see: [discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_%28hydrology%29)), and tracer techniques. Other topics include chemical transport as part of surface water, sediment transport and erosion.

One of the important areas of hydrology is the interchange between rivers and aquifers. Groundwater/surface water interactions in streams and aquifers can be complex and the direction of net water flux (into surface water or into the aquifer) may vary spatially along a stream channel and over time at any particular location, depending on the relationship between stream stage and groundwater levels.

### Precipitation and evaporation {#Precipitation_and_evaporation}

*Image: 2013-10-14 12 27 49 National Weather Service Standard Rain Gauge.JPG.* A standard [NOAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA) [rain gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_gauge)

In some considerations, hydrology is thought of as starting at the land-atmosphere boundary[^o21] and so it is important to have adequate knowledge of both precipitation and evaporation. Precipitation can be measured in various ways: [disdrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disdrometer) for precipitation characteristics at a fine time scale; [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar) for cloud properties, rain rate estimation, hail and snow detection; [rain gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_gauge) for routine accurate measurements of rain and snowfall; [satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite) for rainy area identification, rain rate estimation, land-cover/land-use, and soil moisture, snow cover or snow water equivalent for example.[^o22]

[Evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation) is an important part of the water cycle. It is partly affected by humidity, which can be measured by a [sling psychrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sling_psychrometer). It is also affected by the presence of snow, hail, and ice and can relate to dew, mist and fog. Hydrology considers evaporation of various forms: from water surfaces; as transpiration from plant surfaces in natural and agronomic ecosystems. Direct measurement of evaporation can be obtained using Simon's [evaporation pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation_pan).

Detailed studies of evaporation involve boundary layer considerations as well as momentum, heat flux, and energy budgets.

### Remote sensing {#Remote_sensing}

*Image: NASA's\_GRACE\_Sees\_Major\_Water\_Losses\_in\_Middle\_East.jpg.* Estimates of changes in water storage around the [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris) and [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) Rivers, measured by NASA's [GRACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRACE_%28satellite%29) satellites. The satellites measure tiny changes in gravitational acceleration, which can then be processed to reveal movement of water due to changes in its total mass.

Remote sensing of hydrologic processes can provide information on locations where *in situ* sensors may be unavailable or sparse. It also enables observations over large spatial extents. Many of the variables constituting the terrestrial water balance, for example [surface water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_water) storage, [soil moisture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_moisture), [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation), [evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration), and [snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow) and [ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice), are measurable using remote sensing at various spatial-temporal resolutions and accuracies.[^o24] Sources of remote sensing include land-based sensors, airborne sensors and [satellite sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_observation_satellite) which can capture [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_microwave_sensor), [thermal and near-infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-infrared) data or use [lidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidar), for example.

### Water quality {#Water_quality}

In hydrology, studies of water quality concern organic and inorganic compounds, and both dissolved and sediment material. In addition, water quality is affected by the interaction of dissolved oxygen with organic material and various chemical transformations that may take place. Measurements of water quality may involve either in-situ methods, in which analyses take place on-site, often automatically, and laboratory-based analyses and may include [microbiological analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriological_water_analysis).

### Prediction {#Prediction}

Observations of hydrologic processes are used to make [predictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictions) of the future behavior of hydrologic systems (water flow, water quality).[^o28] One of the major current concerns in hydrologic research is "Prediction in Ungauged Basins" (PUB), i.e. in basins where no or only very few data exist.[^o29]

::: {#o31 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Statistical hydrology"}

### Statistical hydrology {#Statistical_hydrology}

The aims of Statistical hydrology is to provide appropriate statistical methods for analyzing and modeling various parts of the hydrological cycle.[^o32] By analyzing the statistical properties of hydrologic records, such as rainfall or river flow, hydrologists can estimate future hydrologic phenomena. When making assessments of how often relatively rare events will occur, analyses are made in terms of the [return period](wikipedia://en/1039962) of such events. Other quantities of interest include the average flow in a river, in a year or by season.

These estimates are important for [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineers) and economists so that proper [risk analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_analysis_%28business%29) can be performed to influence investment decisions in future infrastructure and to determine the yield reliability characteristics of water supply systems. Statistical information is utilized to formulate operating rules for large dams forming part of systems which include agricultural, industrial and [residential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residential) demands.

:::

### Modeling {#Modeling}

*Image: shetran plan view dunsop.jpg.* Plan view of water flow through a [catchment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchment) simulated by the [SHETRAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHETRAN) hydrological modelling system

Hydrological models are simplified, conceptual representations of a part of the hydrologic cycle. They are primarily used for hydrological prediction and for understanding hydrological processes, within the general field of [scientific modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_modeling). Two major types of hydrological models can be distinguished:[^o34]

- Models based on data. These models are [black box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box_%28systems%29) systems, using mathematical and statistical concepts to link a certain input (for instance rainfall) to the model output (for instance [runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff)). Commonly used techniques are [regression](wikipedia://en/826997), [transfer functions](wikipedia://en/31146), and [system identification](wikipedia://en/552466). The simplest of these models may be linear models, but it is common to deploy non-linear components to represent some general aspects of a catchment's response without going deeply into the real physical processes involved. An example of such an aspect is the well-known behavior that a catchment will respond much more quickly and strongly when it is already wet than when it is dry.
- Models based on process descriptions. These models try to represent the physical processes observed in the real world. Typically, such models contain representations of [surface runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff), [subsurface flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsurface_flow), [evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration), and [channel flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_channel_flow), but they can be far more complicated. Within this category, models can be divided into conceptual and deterministic. Conceptual models link simplified representations of the hydrological processes in an area, whereas deterministic models seek to resolve as much of the physics of a system as possible. These models can be subdivided into single-event models and continuous simulation models.

Recent research in hydrological modeling tries to have a more global approach to the understanding of the [behavior of hydrologic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_modeling_in_hydrology) to make better predictions and to face the major challenges in water resources management.

### Transport {#Transport}

Water movement is a significant means by which other materials, such as soil, gravel, boulders or pollutants, are transported from place to place. Initial input to receiving waters may arise from a [point source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_source_%28pollution%29) discharge or a [line source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_source) or [area source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_source_%28pollution%29), such as [surface runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff). Since the 1960s rather complex [mathematical models](wikipedia://en/20590) have been developed, facilitated by the availability of high-speed computers. The most common pollutant classes analyzed are [nutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrient), [pesticides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesticide), [total dissolved solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_dissolved_solids) and [sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment).

**Other water-related fields**

- [Oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography) is the more general study of water in the oceans and estuaries.
- [Meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904) is the more general study of the atmosphere and of weather, including precipitation as snow and rainfall.
- [Limnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limnology) is the study of lakes, rivers and wetlands ecosystems. It covers the biological, chemical, physical, geological, and other attributes of all inland waters (running and standing waters, both fresh and saline, natural or man-made).[^o80]
- [Water resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources) are sources of water that are useful or potentially useful. Hydrology studies the availability of those resources, but usually not their uses.
