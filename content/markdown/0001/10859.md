# Fields Medal

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Fields Medal"}

The **Fields Medal** is a prize awarded to two, three, or four [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) under 40 years of age at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) (IMU), a [convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_%28meeting%29) which takes place every four years. The name of the award honors the [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian) mathematician [John Charles Fields](wikipedia://en/805652).[^o3] Its purpose is to give recognition and support to younger mathematical researchers who have made major contributions to the field of mathematics.

:::

The Fields Medal is regarded as one of the highest honors a mathematician can receive, according to the annual Academic Excellence Survey by [ARWU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities),[^o4] and has been [described as the "Nobel Prize of Mathematics"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_considered_the_highest_in_a_field).[^o5][^o6][^o7] In another reputation survey conducted by [IREG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IREG_Observatory_on_Academic_Ranking_and_Excellence) in 2013–2014, the Fields Medal came closely after the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285), as the second most prestigious international award in mathematics.[^o8][^o9]

The medal was first awarded in 1936 to [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) mathematician [Lars Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636) and [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) mathematician [Jesse Douglas](wikipedia://en/387571), and it has been awarded every four years since 1950. In 2014, the [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) mathematician [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655) became the first female Fields Medalist.[^o10][^o11][^o12] With the exception of two Ph.D. holders in physics ([Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) and [Martin Hairer](wikipedia://en/26920158)),[^o13] only people with a Ph.D. in mathematics have won the medal.[^o14] In total, 68 people have been awarded the Fields Medal .[^o15] The most recent group of Fields Medalists received their awards on 23 July 2026, in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia).

Fields was instrumental in establishing the award, designing the medal himself and funding the monetary component, though he died before it was established and his plan was overseen by [John Lighton Synge](wikipedia://en/1252509).[^o3] The prize includes a monetary award which, since 2006, has been 15,000.[^o16][^o17]

## Conditions of the award {#Conditions_of_the_award}

The Fields Medal is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious awards in the field of mathematics and is often described as the "Nobel Prize of Mathematics".[^o19] However, unlike the Nobel Prize, the Fields Medal is awarded once every four years and includes an age restriction: recipients must be under 40 years of age on 1 January of the year in which the medal is awarded.[^o20]

The age limit reflects the intention of John Charles Fields that the award should not only recognize outstanding work already completed, but also encourage further achievement by the recipients and stimulate renewed effort among other mathematicians. In addition, an individual may receive the Fields Medal only once and is not eligible for future awards.[^o20]

## List of Fields medalists {#List_of_Fields_medalists}

In certain years, the Fields medalists have been officially cited for particular mathematical achievements, while in other years such specificities have not been given. However, in every year that the medal has been awarded, noted mathematicians have lectured at the International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM) on each medalist's body of work. In the following table, official citations are quoted when possible (namely for the years 1958, 1998, and every year since 2006). For the other years through 1986, summaries of the ICM lectures, as written by Donald Albers, [Gerald L. Alexanderson](wikipedia://en/43156665), and [Constance Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_Reid), are quoted.[^o23] In the remaining years (1990, 1994, and 2002), part of the text of the ICM lecture itself has been quoted. The most recent awarding of the Fields Medal at the 2026 International Congress of the International Mathematical Union was in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia).[^o24]

## Landmarks {#Landmarks}

The medal was first awarded in 1936 to the Finnish mathematician [Lars Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636) and the American mathematician [Jesse Douglas](wikipedia://en/387571).

In 1954, [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) became the youngest winner of the Fields Medal, at 27.[^o128] He retains this distinction.[^o129]

In 1966, [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) boycotted the ICM, held in Moscow, to protest against Soviet military actions taking place in Eastern Europe.[^o130] [Léon Motchane](wikipedia://en/39434117), founder and director of the [Institut des hautes études scientifiques](wikipedia://en/14527), attended and accepted Grothendieck's Fields Medal on his behalf.[^o131]

In 1970, [Sergei Novikov](wikipedia://en/628515), because of restrictions placed on him by the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) government, was unable to travel to the congress in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) to receive his medal.[^o129]

In 1978, [Grigory Margulis](wikipedia://en/1104948), because of restrictions placed on him by the Soviet government, was unable to travel to the congress in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) to receive his medal. The award was accepted on his behalf by [Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570), who said in his address: "I cannot but express my deep disappointment—no doubt shared by many people here—in the absence of Margulis from this ceremony. In view of the symbolic meaning of this city of Helsinki, I had indeed grounds to hope that I would have a chance at last to meet a mathematician whom I know only through his work and for whom I have the greatest respect and admiration."[^o132]

In 1982, the congress was due to be held in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) but had to be rescheduled to the next year, because of [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Poland) introduced in Poland on 13 December 1981. The awards were announced at the ninth General Assembly of the IMU earlier in the year and awarded at the 1983 Warsaw congress.[^o133]

In 1990, [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) became the first [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) to win the award.[^o134]

In 1998, at the ICM, [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) was presented by the chair of the Fields Medal Committee, [Yuri I. Manin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_I._Manin), with the first-ever IMU silver plaque in recognition of his proof of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953). [Don Zagier](wikipedia://en/1408557) referred to the plaque as a "quantized Fields Medal". Accounts of this award frequently make reference that at the time of the award Wiles was over the age limit for the Fields medal.[^o135] Although Wiles was slightly over the age limit in 1994, he was thought to be a favorite to win the medal; however, a gap (later resolved by [Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) and Wiles) in the proof was found in 1993.[^o136][^o137]

In 2006, [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266), who proved the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798), refused his Fields Medal, stating "I'm not interested in money or fame; I don't want to be on display like an animal in a zoo."[^o16][^o138] He did not attend the congress.[^o139]

In 2014, [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655) became the first Iranian as well as the first woman to win the Fields Medal, [Artur Avila](wikipedia://en/23579022) became the first [South American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American), and [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088) became the first person of Indian origin to do so.[^o140][^o12]

## Medal {#Medal}

*Image: FieldsMedalBack.jpg.* The reverse of the Fields Medal

The medal was designed by the Canadian sculptor [R. Tait McKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Tait_McKenzie).[^o142] It is made of 14[KT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karat) gold, has a diameter of 63.5mm, and weighs 169g.[^o15]

- On the obverse is [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) and a quote attributed to 1st century AD poet [Manilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Manilius), which reads in Latin: Transire suum pectus mundoque potiri ("To surpass one's understanding and master the world").[^o143][^o144] The year number 1933 is written in Roman numerals and contains an error (MC**N**XXXIII rather than MC**M**XXXIII).[^o145] In capital Greek letters the word Ἀρχιμηδους, or "of Archimedes," is inscribed.
- On the reverse is the inscription:

Congregati ex toto orbe mathematici ob scripta insignia tribuere

Translation: "Mathematicians gathered from the entire world have awarded \[understood but not written: 'this prize'\] for outstanding writings."

In the background, there is the representation of Archimedes' [tomb](wikipedia://en/1844#Biography), with the carving illustrating his theorem [On the Sphere and Cylinder](wikipedia://en/18084436), behind an olive branch. (This is the mathematical result of which Archimedes was reportedly most proud: Given a sphere and a [circumscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumscribed) cylinder of the same height and diameter, the ratio between their volumes is equal to $\tfrac{2}{3}$.)

The rim bears the name of the prizewinner.[^o144]

## Female recipients {#Female_recipients}

The Fields Medal has had three female recipients: [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655) from [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) in 2014, [Maryna Viazovska](wikipedia://en/50004967) from [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) in 2022, and [Hong Wang](wikipedia://en/79379843) from [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) in 2026.[^o147]

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

The Fields Medal gained some recognition in popular culture due to references in the 1997 film *[Good Will Hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Will_Hunting)*. In the movie, Gerald Lambeau ([Stellan Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellan_Skarsgård)) is an [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) professor who won the award prior to the events of the story. Throughout the film, references made to the award are meant to convey its prestige in the field.[^o149] In the 2005–2010 TV series *[Numbers](wikipedia://en/1348989)*, the leading character, mathematics professor [Charlie Eppes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Eppes), is portrayed as a Fields nominee. The series also references the popular myth concerning the absence of a Nobel Prize in mathematics, with a character jokingly claiming it was excluded because of a mathematician's romantic prowess with Alfred Nobel's fiancée.[^o150]
