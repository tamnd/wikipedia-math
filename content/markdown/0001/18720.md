# Lodovico Ferrari

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lodovico Ferrari"}

*Image: Tartaglia - Terza risposta data a messer Hieronimo Cardano et a messer Lodovico Ferraro.jpg.* [Niccolò Tartaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Tartaglia), *Terza risposta data a messer Hieronimo Cardano et a messer Lodovico Ferraro*, 1547

**Lodovico de Ferrari** (2 February 1522 – 5 October 1565) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known today for solving the [quartic equation](wikipedia://en/251591).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna), Lodovico's grandfather, Bartolomeo Ferrari, was forced out of Milan to Bologna. Lodovico settled in Bologna, and he began his career as the servant of [Gerolamo Cardano](wikipedia://en/13145). He was extremely bright, so Cardano started teaching him mathematics. Ferrari aided Cardano on his solutions for [quartic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_equations) and [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787), and was mainly responsible for the solution of quartic equations that Cardano published. While still in his teens, Ferrari was able to obtain a prestigious teaching post in Rome after Cardano resigned from it and recommended him. Ferrari retired, when young at 42 years old, and wealthy.[^o4] He then moved back to his hometown of Bologna, where he lived with his widowed sister Maddalena to take up a professorship of mathematics at the University of Bologna in 1565. Shortly thereafter, he died of white [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic) poisoning, according to a legend, by his sister.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Cardano–Tartaglia formula"}

## Cardano–Tartaglia formula {#Cardano–Tartaglia_formula}

In 1545 a famous dispute erupted between Ferrari and Cardano's contemporary [Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Fontana_Tartaglia), involving the solution to cubic equations. Widespread stories that Tartaglia devoted the rest of his life to ruining Ferrari's teacher and erstwhile master Cardano, however, appear to be fabricated.[^o8] Mathematical historians now credit both Cardano and Tartaglia with the formula to solve cubic equations, referring to it as the "[Cardano–Tartaglia formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardano–Tartaglia_formula)".

:::
