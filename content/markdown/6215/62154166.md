# Henry Crapo (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Crapo"}

**Henry Howland Crapo** (;[^o3] August 12, 1932 – September 3, 2019) was an American-Canadian mathematician who worked in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582). Over the course of his career, he held positions at several universities and research institutes in Canada and France. He is noted for his work in [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory) and [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Crapo was born in [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit), [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan), in 1932.[^o5][^o6] He received his Ph.D. in 1964 under the supervision of [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891) and Kenneth Hoffman, with thesis *On the theory of combinatorial independence*.[^o8] While working on his thesis, he was an assistant professor at [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University) in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston).[^o8][^o9] He subsequently held academic positions at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo), [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal), [INRIA](wikipedia://en/318413) Rocquencourt, and [École des Hautes Études en Sciences Sociales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_des_Hautes_Études_en_Sciences_Sociales).[^o10] During his time in Waterloo, Crapo became a Canadian citizen.[^o6]

Crapo is known for his early work in [matroid](wikipedia://en/244321) theory, and for related work in lattice theory. He introduced the [beta invariant](wikipedia://en/244321#Beta_invariant) of a matroid,[^o11] and published the first paper on the [Tutte polynomial](wikipedia://en/3892303)[^o12] (though Tutte had already defined an equivalent polynomial in his thesis). Together with Gian-Carlo Rota, Crapo wrote the first book on matroid theory.[^o13][^o14] He is also known for Crapo's Complementation Theorem in [poset Möbius Inversion](wikipedia://en/20963#On_posets).[^o15][^o16] Crapo wrote 65 mathematical publications during his career.[^o17]

Upon his retirement, Crapo moved to the south of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France).[^o6] He continued some mathematical activity, and hosted several small conferences at his house there.[^o14] He died on September 3, 2019.[^o18]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- A special 1999 issue of the journal *[Advances in Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/30498353)* was dedicated to Crapo on the occasion of his 67th birthday.[^o10][^o20]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

*Image: UWaterloo Porcellino.jpg.* The University of Waterloo [Porcellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcellino), donated by Crapo

Crapo was a patron of the arts. At the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) he donated a collection of rare books on the history of dance and ballet,[^o22] as well as a copy of the [Porcellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcellino) sculpture of Florence; the latter shoulder-high bronze sculpture of a wild boar later became a mascot for the [University of Waterloo Faculty of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo_Faculty_of_Arts).[^o23] He also donated *The Temptation of St. Anthony* by [James Ensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ensor) to the [Royal Museum of Fine Arts in Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Museum_of_Fine_Arts_Antwerp)[^o6] and *Corpse of Man* by [Hyman Bloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyman_Bloom) to the [Rose Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Art_Museum) at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University).[^o24]
