# Francesco Tricomi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Francesco Tricomi"}

**Francesco Giacomo Tricomi** (5 May 1897 – 21 November 1978) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) famous for his studies on mixed type [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564).[^o3] He was also the author of a book on [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Young Skewes Schieldrop Tricomi Cartwright Zurich1932.tif.* Ms. Tricomi (3rd from right) accompanied Francesco Tricomi at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) 1932.

Tricomi was born in [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples). He first enrolled in the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), where he took chemistry courses. However, Tricomi realized that he preferred physics rather than chemistry; he moved to the University of Naples in 1915.[^o5] He graduated at the [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples) in 1918 and later was assistant to [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716), first in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) and then in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome). Later he was professor at [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), called by [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043), a position he held until his retirement in 1967.

He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1928 at Bologna[^o6] and in 1932 in Zurich. From 1943 to 1945 and from 1948 to 1951 at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) of [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California), he collaborated on the manual of special functions for the [Bateman manuscript project](wikipedia://en/3053039), together with [Arthur Erdélyi](wikipedia://en/4388684), [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666) and Fritz Oberhettinger.

Tricomi was a member of the [Accademia nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_nazionale_dei_Lincei) and of the *Accademia delle Scienze di Torino* (Turin Academy of Sciences), of which he was also president.
