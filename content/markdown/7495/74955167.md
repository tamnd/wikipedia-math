# Octant of a sphere

*Image: Octant of a sphere.png.* An octant of the sphere in [orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octant of a sphere"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **octant of a sphere** is a [spherical triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle) with three [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956) and three right sides. It is sometimes called a **trirectangular (spherical) triangle**.[^o2] It is one face of a [spherical octahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_octahedron).[^o3]

:::

For a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) embedded in [three-dimensional Euclidean space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional_Euclidean_space), the [vectors](wikipedia://en/32533) from the sphere's center to each vertex of an octant are the [basis vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_vector) of a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) relative to which the sphere is a [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090). The spherical octant itself is the intersection of the sphere with one [octant of space](wikipedia://en/36160226).

Uniquely among spherical triangles, the octant is its own [polar triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_triangle).[^o4]

The octant can be parametrized using a rational quartic [Bézier triangle](wikipedia://en/200007).[^o5]

The [solid angle](wikipedia://en/94102) subtended by a spherical octant is $\pi$/2 [steradian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steradian) or one-eight of a [spat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spat_%28angular_unit%29), the solid angle of a full sphere.[^o6]
