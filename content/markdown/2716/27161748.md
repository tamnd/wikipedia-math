# Ground field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ground field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **ground field** is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* fixed at the beginning of the discussion.

:::

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Use"}

## Use {#Use}

It is used in various areas of algebra:

### In linear algebra {#In_linear_algebra}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), the concept of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) may be developed over any field.

### In algebraic geometry {#In_algebraic_geometry}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), in the foundational developments of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) the use of fields other than the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) was essential to expand the definitions to include the idea of [abstract algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebraic_variety) over *K*, and [generic point](wikipedia://en/3043978) relative to *K*.

### In Lie theory {#In_Lie_theory}

Reference to a ground field may be common in the theory of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) (*qua* vector spaces) and [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) (*qua* algebraic varieties).

### In Galois theory {#In_Galois_theory}

In [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), given a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *L*/*K*, the field *K* that is being extended may be considered the ground field for an argument or discussion. Within algebraic geometry, from the point of view of [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory), the spectrum *Spec*(*K*) of the ground field *K* plays the role of [final object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_object) in the category of *K*-schemes, and its structure and symmetry may be richer than the fact that the space of the scheme is a point might suggest.

### In Diophantine geometry {#In_Diophantine_geometry}

In [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) the characteristic problems of the subject are those caused by the fact that the ground field *K* is not taken to be [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed). The [field of definition](wikipedia://en/9213667) of a variety given abstractly may be smaller than the ground field, and two varieties may become isomorphic when the ground field is enlarged, a major topic in [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315).

:::
