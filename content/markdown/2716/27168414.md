# Raikov's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raikov's theorem"}

**Raikov’s theorem,** named for Russian mathematician [Dmitrii Abramovich Raikov](wikipedia://en/69919109), is a result in the [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). It is well known that if each of two [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$ has a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144), then their sum $\xi = \xi_1 + \xi_2$ has a Poisson distribution as well. It turns out that the converse is also valid.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Suppose that a random variable $\xi$ has a Poisson distribution and admits a decomposition as a sum $\xi = \xi_1 + \xi_2$ of two independent random variables. Then the distribution of each summand is a shifted Poisson distribution.

Raikov's theorem is similar to [Cramér’s decomposition theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramér’s_decomposition_theorem). The latter result claims that if a sum of two independent random variables has a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462), then each summand is normally distributed as well. It was also proved by [Yu. V. Linnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Linnik) that a convolution of normal distribution and Poisson's distribution possesses a similar property (Linnik's theorem on convolution of normal distribution and Poisson's distribution). It follows from the Raikov theorem that the Poisson distribution belongs to the [Linnik class $I_0$](wikipedia://en/83308793).

:::

## An extension to locally compact Abelian groups {#An_extension_to_locally_compact_Abelian_groups}

Let $X$ be a [locally compact Abelian group](wikipedia://en/454325). Denote by $M^1(X)$ the convolution [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) of probability distributions on $X$, and by $E_x$the [degenerate distribution](wikipedia://en/101843) concentrated at $x\in X$. Let $x_0\in X, \lambda>0$.

The Poisson distribution generated by the measure $\lambda E_{x_0}$ is defined as a distribution of the form

$$\mu=e(\lambda E_{x_0})=e^{-\lambda} \left( E_0+\lambda E_{x_0}+ \frac{\lambda^2 E_{2x_0}}{2!} + \cdots+ \frac{\lambda^n E_{nx_0}}{n!}+\cdots \right).$$

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**[^o7] Let $\mu$ be the Poisson distribution generated by the measure $\lambda E_{x_0}$. Suppose that $\mu=\mu_1*\mu_2$, with $\mu_j\in M^1(X)$. Then each of $\mu_j$ is a shift of a Poisson distribution if and only if $x_0$ is either an infinite-order element or has order 2.

:::
