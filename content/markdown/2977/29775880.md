# Dade isometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dade isometry"}

In mathematical [finite group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_group_theory), the **Dade isometry** is an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) from [class function](wikipedia://en/1001329) on a subgroup *H* with [support](wikipedia://en/381013) on a subset *K* of *H* to class functions on a group *G* (Collins 1990, 6.1). It was introduced by (txt) as a generalization and simplification of an isometry used by (Feit Thompson 1963) in their proof of the [odd order theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_order_theorem), and was used by (Peterfalvi 2000) in his revision of the character theory of the odd order theorem.

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Suppose that *H* is a subgroup of a finite group *G*, *K* is an invariant subset of *H* such that if two elements in *K* are conjugate in *G*, then they are conjugate in *H*, and π a set of primes containing all prime divisors of the orders of elements of *K*. The Dade lifting is a linear map *f* → *f*<sup>σ</sup> from class functions *f* of *H* with support on *K* to class functions *f*<sup>σ</sup> of *G*, which is defined as follows: *f*<sup>σ</sup>(*x*) is *f*(*k*) if there is an element *k* ∈ *K* conjugate to the π-part of *x*, and 0 otherwise. The Dade lifting is an isometry if for each *k* ∈ *K*, the centralizer *C*<sub>*G*</sub>(*k*) is the semidirect product of a normal Hall π' subgroup *I*(*K*) with *C*<sub>*H*</sub>(*k*).

:::

## Tamely embedded subsets in the Feit–Thompson proof {#Tamely_embedded_subsets_in_the_Feit–Thompson_proof}

The [Feit–Thompson proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feit–Thompson_proof) of the odd-order theorem uses "tamely embedded subsets" and an isometry from class functions with support on a tamely embedded subset. If *K*<sub>1</sub> is a tamely embedded subset, then the subset *K* consisting of *K*<sub>1</sub> without the identity element 1 satisfies the conditions above, and in this case the isometry used by Feit and Thompson is the Dade isometry.
