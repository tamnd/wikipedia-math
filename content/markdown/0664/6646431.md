# William Browder (mathematician)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="William Browder"}

**William Browder** (January 6, 1934 – February 4, 2025) was an American mathematician, who specialized in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He served as president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1989 to 1991.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

William Browder was born in a Jewish hospital in [Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on January 6, 1934,[^o10][^o5][^o6] the son of Raisa (née Berkman), a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) woman from [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg), and [American Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA) leader [Earl Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Browder), from [Wichita, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas). His father had moved to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1927, where he met and married Raisa. Their first two sons, [Felix](wikipedia://en/10201445) and [Andrew](wikipedia://en/53382320), were born in Moscow in 1931.[^o7] William attended local public schools in Yonkers for early schooling and graduated from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) with a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) degree in 1954. He was a instructor at the University of Rochester from 1957 to 1958 and at Cornell University from 1958 until 1963. In August 1957, his original thesis fell apart when his advisor, [John Coleman Moore](wikipedia://en/13433862), found a problem with the idea. However, William came up with a new idea which was titled *[Homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of [Loop Spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_Space).* He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1958.[^o5][^o8][^o6]

## Career {#Career}

From 1964 onwards, Browder was a professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University); he was chair of the mathematics department at Princeton from 1971 to 1973. He was editor of the journal *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* from 1969 to 1981, and president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1989 to 1991.[^o5]

Browder was elected to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1980, the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1984, and the [Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters) in 1990.[^o5] In 1994, a conference was held at Princeton in celebration of his 60th birthday.[^o10] A conference was held at Princeton on the occasion of his retirement in 2012.[^o11] Browder advised 30 Ph.D. students in his career as well as multiple undergraduate students.[^o6]

## Death {#Death}

Browder died on February 4, 2025, at the age of 91.[^o13][^o14]
