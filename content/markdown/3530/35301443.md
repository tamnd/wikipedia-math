# Reye configuration

*Image: Reye configuration.svg.* The Reye configuration

*Image: Reye incidence graph.svg.* The [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of the Reye configuration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reye configuration"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Reye configuration**, introduced by (txt), is a [configuration](wikipedia://en/7200539) of 12 [points](wikipedia://en/593693) and 16 [lines](wikipedia://en/946975). Each point of the configuration belongs to four lines, and each line contains three points. Therefore, in the notation of configurations, the Reye configuration is written as $12_{4}16_{3}$. It is symmetric (both point and line transitive) and has 576 automorphisms.

:::

## Realization {#Realization}

The Reye configuration can be realized in three-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) by taking the lines to be the 12 edges and four long diagonals of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), and the points as the eight vertices of the cube, its center, and the three points where groups of four parallel cube edges meet the plane at infinity. Two regular [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) may be inscribed within a cube, forming a [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula); these two tetrahedra are perspective figures to each other in four different ways, and the other four points of the configuration are their centers of perspectivity. These two tetrahedra, together with the tetrahedron of the remaining 4 points, form a [desmic system](wikipedia://en/23457340) of three tetrahedra.

Any two disjoint spheres in three dimensional space, with different radii, have two [bitangent](wikipedia://en/2605212) [double cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29), the apexes of which are called the centers of similitude. If three spheres are given, with their centers non-collinear, then their six centers of similitude form the six points of a [complete quadrilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_quadrilateral), the four lines of which are called the axes of similitude. And if four spheres are given, with their centers non-coplanar, then they determine 12 centers of similitude and 16 axes of similitude, which together form an instance of the Reye configuration.[^n1]

The Reye configuration can also be realized by points and lines in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), by drawing the three-dimensional configuration in [three-point perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-point_perspective). An 8<sub>3</sub>12<sub>2</sub> configuration of eight points in the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) and 12 lines connecting them, with the connection pattern of a cube, can be extended to form the Reye configuration if and only if the eight points are a [perspective projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_projection) of a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975).[^n2]

The 24 permutations of the points $(\pm 1, \pm 1, 0, 0)$ form the vertices of a [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485) centered at the origin of four-dimensional Euclidean space. These 24 points also form the 24 roots in the [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) $D_4$. They can be grouped into pairs of points opposite each other on a line through the origin. The 12 axis lines can be grouped into 16 triples that lie in the same central plane of the 24-cell. Each central plane intersects 6 vertices in the form of a regular hexagon. Four hexagons intersect at each vertex of the 24-cell. The 12 axis lines and 16 [hexagon planes of the 24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485#Hexagons) correspond to the 12 points and 16 lines of the Reye configuration.[^n3]

The lines and planes through the origin of four-dimensional Euclidean space have the geometry of the points and lines of three-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135), and in this three-dimensional projective space the lines through opposite pairs of these 24 points and the central planes through these points become the points and lines of the Reye configuration.[^n4] The permutations of $(\pm 1, \pm 1, 0, 0)$ form the [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) of the 12 points in this configuration.

## Application {#Application}

(Aravind 2000) pointed out that the Reye configuration underlies some of the proofs of the [Bell–Kochen–Specker theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell–Kochen–Specker_theorem) about the non-existence of hidden variables in quantum mechanics.[^n3]

:::

## Related configurations {#Related_configurations}

The [Pappus configuration](wikipedia://en/2414085) may be formed from two triangles that are perspective figures to each other in three different ways, analogous to the interpretation of the Reye configuration involving desmic tetrahedra.

If the Reye configuration is formed from a cube in three-dimensional space, then there are 12 planes containing four lines each: the six face planes of the cube, and the six planes through pairs of opposite edges of the cube. Intersecting these 12 planes and 16 lines with another plane in general position produces a 16<sub>3</sub>12<sub>4</sub> configuration, the dual of the Reye configuration. The original Reye configuration and its dual together form a 28<sub>4</sub>28<sub>4</sub> configuration.[^n5]

There are 574 distinct configurations of type 12<sub>4</sub>16<sub>3</sub>.[^n6]
