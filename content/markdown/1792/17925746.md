::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform 10-polytope"}

In ten-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a 10-polytope is a 10-dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) whose boundary consists of [9-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-polytope) [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29), exactly two such facets meeting at each [8-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-polytope) [ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_%28geometry%29).

:::

A **uniform 10-polytope** is one which is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), and constructed from [uniform](wikipedia://en/6821726) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647).

## Regular 10-polytopes {#Regular_10-polytopes}

Regular 10-polytopes can be represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {p,q,r,s,t,u,v,w,x}, with **x** {p,q,r,s,t,u,v,w} 9-polytope [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) around each [peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_%28geometry%29).

There are exactly three such [convex regular 10-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#Convex_4):

1. {3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3} - [10-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-simplex)
1. {4,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3} - [10-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-cube)
1. {3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,4} - [10-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-orthoplex)

There are no nonconvex regular 10-polytopes.

## Euler characteristic {#Euler_characteristic}

The topology of any given 10-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o12]

The value of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) used to characterise polyhedra does not generalize usefully to higher dimensions, and is zero for all 10-polytopes, whatever their underlying topology. This inadequacy of the Euler characteristic to reliably distinguish between different topologies in higher dimensions led to the discovery of the more sophisticated Betti numbers.[^o12]

Similarly, the notion of orientability of a polyhedron is insufficient to characterise the surface twistings of toroidal polytopes, and this led to the use of torsion coefficients.[^o12]

### Regular and uniform hyperbolic honeycombs {#Regular_and_uniform_hyperbolic_honeycombs}

There are no compact hyperbolic Coxeter groups of rank 10, groups that can generate honeycombs with all finite facets, and a finite [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719).
