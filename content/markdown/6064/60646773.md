# ℓ-adic sheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="ℓ-adic sheaf"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), an **ℓ-adic sheaf** on a [Noetherian scheme](wikipedia://en/2783891) *X* is an [inverse system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_system) consisting of $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n$-modules $F_n$ in the [étale topology](wikipedia://en/5707590) and $F_{n+1} \to F_n$ inducing $F_{n+1} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n+1}} \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n \overset{\simeq}\to F_n$.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Bhatt–Scholze's [pro-étale topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-étale_topology) gives an alternative approach.[^o4]

## Motivation {#Motivation}

The development of [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) as a whole was fueled by the desire to produce a 'topological' theory of [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) for algebraic varieties, i.e. a [Weil cohomology theory](wikipedia://en/2960213) that works in any [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621). An essential feature of such a theory is that it admits coefficients in a field of characteristic 0. However, constant étale sheaves with no torsion have no interesting cohomology. For example, if $X$ is a smooth variety over a field $k$, then $H^i(X_\text{ét},\mathbb{Q})=0$ for all positive $i$. On the other hand, the constant sheaves $\mathbb{Z}/m$ do produce the 'correct' cohomology, as long as $m$ is invertible in the ground field $k$. So one takes a prime $\ell$ for which this is true and defines $\ell$-adic cohomology as $H^i(X_\text{ét}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell):= \varprojlim_n H^i(X_\text{ét}, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$, and $H^i(X_\text{ét}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell):= \varprojlim_n H^i(X_\text{ét}, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)\otimes \mathbb Q$.

This definition, however, is not completely satisfactory: As in the classical case of topological spaces, one might want to consider cohomology with coefficients in a [local system](wikipedia://en/3461103) of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-vector spaces, and there should be a [category equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) between such local systems and continuous $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-[representations](wikipedia://en/12695) of the [étale fundamental group](wikipedia://en/3022906).

Another problem with the definition above is that it behaves well only when $k$ is a [separably closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separably_closed). In this case, all the groups occurring in the inverse limit are finitely generated and taking the limit is [exact](wikipedia://en/453945). But if $k$ is for example a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), the cohomology groups $H^i(X_\text{ét}, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$ will often be infinite and the limit not exact, which causes issues with functoriality. For instance, there is in general no [Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochschild-Serre_spectral_sequence) relating $H^i(X_\text{ét}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ to the [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) of $H^i((X_{k^\text{sep}})_\text{ét}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$.[^o6]

These considerations lead one to consider the category of inverse systems of sheaves as described above. One has then the desired equivalence of categories with representations of the fundamental group (for $\mathbb Z_\ell$-local systems, and when $X$ is normal for $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-systems as well), and the issue in the last paragraph is resolved by so-called continuous étale cohomology, where one takes the [derived functor](wikipedia://en/433875) of the composite functor of taking the limit over global sections of the system.

## Constructible and lisse ℓ-adic sheaves {#Constructible_and_lisse_ℓ-adic_sheaves}

An ℓ-adic sheaf $\{ F_n \}_{\ge 0}$ is said to be

- *constructible* if each $F_n$ is [constructible](wikipedia://en/26131119).
- *lisse* if each $F_n$ is constructible and locally constant.

Some authors (e.g., those of SGA 4$\tfrac{1}{2}$)[^o8] assume an ℓ-adic sheaf to be constructible.

Given a connected scheme *X* with a geometric point *x*, SGA 1 defines the [étale fundamental group](wikipedia://en/3022906) $\pi^{\text{ét}}_1(X, x)$ of *X* at *x* to be the group classifying finite Galois coverings of *X*. Then the category of lisse ℓ-adic sheaves on *X* is equivalent to the category of continuous representations of $\pi^{\text{ét}}_1(X, x)$ on finite free $\mathbb{Z}_l$-modules. This is an analog of the correspondence between local systems and continuous representations of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) (because of this, a lisse ℓ-adic sheaf is sometimes also called a local system).

## ℓ-adic cohomology {#ℓ-adic_cohomology}

An ℓ-adic cohomology group is an inverse limit of étale cohomology groups with certain torsion coefficients.

## The "derived category" of constructible ℓ-adic sheaves {#The_"derived_category"_of_constructible_ℓ-adic_sheaves}

In a way similar to that for ℓ-adic cohomology, the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of constructible $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$-sheaves is defined essentially as $D^b_c(X, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell) := (\varprojlim_n D^b_c(X, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell} \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell.$

(Bhatt Scholze 2013) writes "in daily life, one pretends (without getting into much trouble) that $D^b_c(X, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ is simply the full [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) of some hypothetical derived category $D(X, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ ..."
