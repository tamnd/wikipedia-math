# Ellen Hamaker

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ellen Hamaker"}

**Ellen Louise "E.L." Hamaker** (born February 13, 1974) is a Dutch-American [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist), and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). Since 2018 she has been a [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor) at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University), holding the chair Longitudinal Data Analysis at the Department of Methodology and Statistics. [^o3] Her work focuses on the development of statistical models for the analysis of intensive longitudinal data in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology), mainly within the frameworks of [structural equation modeling](wikipedia://en/2007748) and [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Education {#Education}

Hamaker received a bachelor's and master's degree in psychology from Utrecht University, in 1997 and 1999 respectively. She earned her PhD in psychological methods in 2004 at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), under the supervision of [Peter Molenaar](wikipedia://en/57976933) and [Conor Dolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Dolan), for her thesis: "Time series analysis and the individual as the unit of psychological research"[^o6]

### Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 2005 Hamaker became a postdoctoral fellow at the Department of Psychology, [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia). Hamaker returned to the Netherlands in 2006, being appointed assistant professor at the Department of Methodology and Statistics, Utrecht University.[^o8] She was promoted to associate professor in 2011, before being promoted to full professor with the chair Longitudinal Data Analysis in 2018.[^o9] Since 2014 she has also been a research fellow at the Research Group of Quantitative Psychology and Individual Differences, [KU Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KU_Leuven), in Belgium.

### Research focus {#Research_focus}

Hamaker's early research focused on the application of time series techniques within psychology, to study process that unfold within individuals over time. Increased availability of data collected using the [experience sampling method](wikipedia://en/19250284) in psychology led to increased interest in this methodology in applied psychological research.[^o11] Most notable of her papers is her critique of the [cross-lagged panel model](wikipedia://en/64120536),[^o12] in which she proposes to better account for stable trait-like differences between individuals through the use of [multilevel models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilevel_models) with random intercepts. She is also an advocate of within-person thinking in social sciences,[^o13] pointing out that conclusions based on cross-sectional data do not necessarily generalize to the processes within a particular person.

Since 2015 Hamaker has collaborated frequently with [Bengt O. Muthén](wikipedia://en/53411130) and Tihomir Asparouhov in the development of the Dynamic Structural Equation Modeling, a module that allows for [Bayesian time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_time_series_analysis) in the software package Mplus.[^o14]

Hamaker has stated that the overarching goal of her research is to establish a connection between innovative statistical techniques and applied psychological research.[^o15]

### Achievements and awards {#Achievements_and_awards}

Throughout her academic career, Hamaker has served as [associate editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_editor) of *[Multivariate Behavioral Research](wikipedia://en/26785977)*. In 2019 she was appointed a fellow of the [Association for Psychological Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Psychological_Science).[^o17]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hamaker is the granddaughter of Dutch scientist [Hugo Christiaan Hamaker](wikipedia://en/14925133), noted for his contributions to physics (the [Hamaker constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamaker_constant)) and statistics.
