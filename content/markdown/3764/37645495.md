# Robert Horton Cameron

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Horton Cameron"}

**Robert Horton Cameron** (May 17, 1908 – July 17, 1989) was an American mathematician, who worked on analysis and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). He is known for the [Cameron–Martin theorem](wikipedia://en/6573013).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Cameron received his Ph.D. in 1932 from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) under the direction of [W. A. Hurwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallie_Abraham_Hurwitz).[^o6] He studied under a [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council) postdoc at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) from 1933 to 1935.[^o7] Cameron was a faculty member at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1935 to 1945. He was then a faculty member at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) until his retirement. He spent the academic year 1953–1954 on sabbatical leave at the Institute for Advanced Study.[^o7] His doctoral students include [Monroe D. Donsker](wikipedia://en/16594147) and [Elizabeth Cuthill](wikipedia://en/67390381). He had a total of 35 Ph.D. students at the University of Minnesota — his first two graduated in 1946 and his last one in 1977. Cameron published a total of 72 papers — his first in 1934 and his last, posthumously, in 1990.[^o8]

At MIT, he did some work with [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185). During the 1940s Cameron and [W. T. Martin](wikipedia://en/37661503), who was from 1943 to 1946 the chair of the mathematics department at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University), engaged in an ambitious program of extending Norbert Wiener's early work on mathematical models of Brownian motion.[^o9] In 1944, Cameron was awarded the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for ['"Some Introductory Exercises in the Manipulation of Fourier Transforms"](http://www.maa.org/programs/maa-awards/writing-awards/some-introductory-exercises-in-the-manipulation-of-fourier-transforms), which appeared in *National Mathematics Magazine*, 1941, vol. 15, pages 331–356.
