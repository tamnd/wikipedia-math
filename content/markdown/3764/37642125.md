# Modular symbol

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modular symbol"}

In mathematics, **modular symbols**, introduced independently by [Bryan John Birch](wikipedia://en/4949339) and by (txt), span a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) closely related to a space of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), on which the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the [Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/55907189) can be described explicitly. This makes them useful for computing with spaces of modular forms.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) of (universal weight 2) modular symbols is spanned by symbols {α,β} for α, β in the rational projective line **Q** ∪ {∞} subject to the relations

- {α,β} + {β,γ} = {α,γ}

Informally, {α,β} represents a homotopy class of paths from α to β in the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826).

The group *GL*<sub>2</sub>(**Q**) [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on the rational [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578), and this induces an action on the modular symbols.

There is a pairing between cusp forms *f* of weight 2 and modular symbols given by integrating the cusp form, or rather *fd*τ, along the path corresponding to the symbol.

:::
