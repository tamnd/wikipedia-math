# Estimation lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Estimation lemma"}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), the **estimation lemma**, also known as the **$ML$ inequality** (ML for Max times Length), gives an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) for a [contour integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_integral). If $f$ is a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued, [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) on the contour $\Gamma$ and if its [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991#Complex_numbers) $\lvert f\,(z)\rvert$ is bounded by a constant $M$ for all $z$ on $\Gamma$, then

:::

$$\left|\int_\Gamma f(z) \, dz\right| \le M\, l(\Gamma),$$

where $l(\Gamma)$ is the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of $\Gamma$. In particular, we may take the [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima)

$$M:= \sup_{z\in\Gamma}|f(z)|$$

as upper bound. Intuitively, the [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) is very simple to understand. If a contour is thought of as many smaller contour segments connected together, then there will be a maximum $\lvert f\,(z)\rvert$ for each segment. Out of all the maximum $\lvert f\,(z)\rvert$s for the segments, there will be an overall largest one. Hence, if the overall largest $\lvert f\,(z)\rvert$ is summed over the entire path then the integral of $f\,(z)$ over the path must be less than or equal to it.

Formally, the inequality can be shown to hold using the definition of contour integral, the [absolute value inequality for integrals](wikipedia://en/15532#Inequalities_for_integrals) and the formula for the [length of a curve](wikipedia://en/89246#Lengths_of_curves) as follows:

$$\left|\int_\Gamma f(z)\, dz \right| = \left|\int_\alpha^\beta f(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\, dt \right| \leq \int_\alpha^\beta \left|f(\gamma(t))\right|\left|\gamma'(t)\right|\, dt \leq M \int_\alpha^\beta \left|\gamma'(t)\right|\, dt = M\, l(\Gamma)$$

The estimation lemma is most commonly used as part of the [methods of contour integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methods_of_contour_integration) with the intent to show that the integral over part of a contour goes to zero as $\lvert z\rvert$ goes to infinity. An example of such a case is shown below.

## Example {#Example}

*Image: Upper halfcircle with i.svg.* The contour $\Gamma$.

**Problem.** Find an upper bound for

$$\left|\int_\Gamma \frac{1}{(z^2+1)^2} \, dz\right|,$$

where $\Gamma$ is the upper half-[circle](wikipedia://en/6220) $\lvert z\rvert = a$ with [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) $a > 1$ traversed once in the counterclockwise direction.

**Solution.** First observe that the length of the path of integration is half the [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of a circle with radius $a$, hence

$$l(\Gamma)=\tfrac{1}{2}(2\pi a)=\pi a.$$

Next we seek an upper bound $M$ for the integrand when $\lvert z\rvert = a$. By the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941) we see that

$$|z|^2=\left|z^2\right| = \left|z^2+1-1\right| \le \left|z^2+1\right|+1,$$

therefore

$$\left|z^2+1\right|\ge |z|^2 - 1 = a^2 - 1>0$$

because $\lvert z\rvert = a > 1$ on $\Gamma$. Hence

$$\left|\frac{1}{\left(z^2+1\right)^2}\right| \le \frac{1}{\left(a^2-1\right)^2}.$$

Therefore, we apply the estimation lemma with $M = \tfrac{1}{(a^{2} - 1)^{2}}$. The resulting bound is

$$\left|\int_\Gamma \frac{1}{\left(z^2+1\right)^2}\,dz\right| \le \frac{\pi a}{\left(a^2-1\right)^2}.$$

:::
