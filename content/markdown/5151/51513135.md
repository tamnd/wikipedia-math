# Graph (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **graph** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) which arises from a usual [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G = (E, V)$ by replacing vertices by points and each edge $e = xy \in E$ by a copy of the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) $I = [0,1]$, where $0$ is identified with the point associated to $x$ and $1$ with the point associated to $y$. That is, as topological spaces, graphs are exactly the [simplicial 1-complexes](wikipedia://en/252686) and also exactly the one-dimensional [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022).[^o7]

:::

Thus, in particular, it bears the [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology) of the [set](wikipedia://en/26691)

$$X_0 \sqcup \bigsqcup_{e \in E} I_e$$

under the quotient map used for gluing. Here $X_0$ is the 0-skeleton (consisting of one point for each vertex $x \in V$), $I_e$ are the [closed intervals](wikipedia://en/49172) glued to it, one for each edge $e \in E$, and $\sqcup$ is the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/1564380).[^o7]

The [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) on this space is called the **graph topology**.

## Subgraphs and trees {#Subgraphs_and_trees}

A **subgraph** of a graph $X$ is a [subspace](wikipedia://en/910926) $Y \subseteq X$ which is also a graph and whose nodes are all contained in the 0-skeleton of $X$. $Y$ is a subgraph [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it consists of vertices and edges from $X$ and is closed.[^o7]

A subgraph $T \subseteq X$ is called a **tree** if it is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414) as a topological space.[^o7] This can be shown equivalent to the usual definition of a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), namely a [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) graph without [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The associated topological space of a graph is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) (with respect to the graph topology) if and only if the original graph is [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492).
- Every connected graph $X$ contains at least one [maximal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) tree $T \subseteq X$, that is, a tree that is maximal with respect to the order induced by set inclusion on the subgraphs of $X$ which are trees.[^o7]
- If $X$ is a graph and $T \subseteq X$ a maximal tree, then the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi_1(X)$ equals the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) generated by elements $(f_\alpha)_{\alpha \in A}$, where the $\{f_\alpha\}$ correspond [bijectively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) to the edges of $X \setminus T$; in fact, $X\setminus T$ is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) to a [wedge sum](wikipedia://en/684143) of [circles](wikipedia://en/6220).[^o7]
- Forming the topological space associated to a graph as above amounts to a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of graphs to the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051).
- Every [covering space](wikipedia://en/250323) projecting to a graph is also a graph.[^o7]

:::
