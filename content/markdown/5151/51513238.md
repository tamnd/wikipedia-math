# Contact graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Contact graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) area of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **contact graph** or **tangency graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) whose vertices are represented by geometric objects (e.g. [curves](wikipedia://en/89246), [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860), or [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621)), and whose edges correspond to two objects [touching](wikipedia://en/31482) (but not crossing) according to some specified notion. It is similar to the notion of an [intersection graph](wikipedia://en/7726759) but differs from it in restricting the ways that the underlying objects are allowed to intersect each other.

:::

The [circle packing theorem](wikipedia://en/16648043) states that every [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) can be represented as a contact graph of circles, known as a [coin graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_graph). [Oded Schramm](wikipedia://en/9605580) monster packing theorem generalizes this: every planar graph is a contact graph of [homothetic copies](wikipedia://en/386138) of any given smooth [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292). The contact graphs of [unit circles](wikipedia://en/27072071) are called [penny graphs](wikipedia://en/53242630). Representations as contact graphs of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654), [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048), [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860), or [circular arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) have also been studied.
