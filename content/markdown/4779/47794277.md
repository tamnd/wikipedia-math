# Pretty Easy privacy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pretty Easy privacy"}

**pretty Easy privacy** (**p≡p** or **pEp**) was a pluggable [data encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_encryption) and verification system that provided automatic [cryptographic key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_key) management through a set of libraries for written digital communications.

:::

It existed as a [plugin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_%28computing%29) for [Microsoft Outlook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Outlook)[^o3] and [Mozilla Thunderbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla_Thunderbird)[^o4] as well as a mobile app for [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29)[^o5][^o6] and iOS.[^o7] p≡p also worked under [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows), [Unix-like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix-like) and [Mac OS X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_OS_X) [operating systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system). Its [cryptographic](wikipedia://en/18934432) functionality was handled by an [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) p≡p engine relying on already existing cryptographic implementations in software like [GnuPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GnuPG), a modified version of netpgp (used only in iOS), and (as of p≡p v2.0) [GNUnet](wikipedia://en/293685).

pretty Easy privacy was first released in 2016.[^o8] It is a [free and open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software).

p≡p was advertised as being easy to install, use, and understand. p≡p did not depend on any specific platform, message transport system (SMS, email, [XMPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XMPP), etc.), or centrally provided client–server or "[cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_storage)" infrastructures; p≡p is fully [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer) by design.[^o9]

Keys are exchanged opportunistically by transferring via email.[^o10]

## Enigmail support {#Enigmail_support}

[Enigmail](wikipedia://en/435217) announced its support for the new "pretty Easy privacy" (p≡p) encryption in a joint [Thunderbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla_Thunderbird) extension to be released in December 2015.[^o12] Patrick Brunschwig, the head of Enigmail, announced that p≡p core functionality was implemented in Enigmail in October 2016, ready for the [Mozilla Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla_Festival) then taking place in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London).[^o13]

In July 2020, Thunderbird 78 dropped support for the Enigmail Add-On.[^o14] Thunderbird 78 includes OpenPGP functionality and no longer requires the installation of external software.[^o15]

## ISOC support {#ISOC_support}

The Internet Society Switzerland Chapter (ISOC-CH) and the Swiss p≡p foundation teamed up[^o17] to implement privacy-enhancing standards at the basic level of internet protocols, and document them in the work of the [Internet Engineering Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Engineering_Task_Force) (IETF).

## Controversy and Closure {#Controversy_and_Closure}

In March 2021, reports surfaced that p≡p had paid for fake reviews for their apps.[^o19]

As of January 2024, the company overseeing p≡p is not operational. Its website no longer functions, and development of the system has ceased.
