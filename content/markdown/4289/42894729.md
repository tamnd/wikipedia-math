# Celia Hoyles

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Celia Hoyles"}

**Professor Dame Celia Mary Hoyles** ( **French**; born 18 May 1946) is a British mathematician, educationalist[^o3][^o5] and [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Mathematics Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_Education) at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) (UCL), in the [Institute of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Education) (IoE).[^o6][^o7][^o8]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Celia Mary French was born on 18 May 1946.[^o2] She was educated at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) where she graduated with a first class degree in mathematics from the [Department of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/10387568) in 1967.[^o2] She subsequently completed a [Postgraduate Certificate in Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_Certificate_in_Education) (PGCE) in 1971, and a [Master of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Education) degree (MEd) in 1973.[^o2] She completed a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (PhD) degree in 1980, with a thesis titled "Factors in school learning - the pupils' view: a study with particular reference to mathematics".[^o11] All her degrees are from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London).[^o2]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Hoyles began her career as a [secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school) teacher,[^o2] later becoming an academic. In the late 1980s she was co-presenter of *Fun and Games*, a prime time television quiz show about mathematics.[^o26] With Arthur Bakker, Phillip Kent, and Richard B. Noss she is the co-author of *Improving Mathematics at Work: The Need for Techno-Mathematical Literacies*.[^o13][^o14]

Hoyles served as president of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) (IMA) from 2014 to 2015.[^o15][^o16] She served as chief adviser for mathematics to the [government of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom) from 2004 to 2007 and as director of the [National Centre for Excellence in the Teaching of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9057460) (NCETM) from 2007 to 2013.[^o17][^o18]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In the [2004 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_New_Year_Honours), Hoyles was appointed [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (OBE) 'for services to education'. In the [2014 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_New_Year_Honours), she was appointed [Dame Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dame_Commander) of the Order of the British Empire (DBE) in recognition of her service as director of the National Centre for Excellence in the Teaching of Mathematics.[^o16][^o22] She is a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Academy of Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Social_Sciences) (FAcSS).[^o23]

In 2003, she was awarded the first [Hans Freudenthal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Freudenthal_Medal) by the [International Commission on Mathematical Instruction](wikipedia://en/8818410) (ICMI) in recognition of 'the outstanding contribution that \[she\] has made to research in the domain of technology and mathematics education'.[^o24] In 2010, she was awarded the first [Kavli Education Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Education_Medal) by the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) 'in recognition of her outstanding contribution to research in mathematics education'.[^o25][^o26]

Hoyles has [honorary degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University) (2006), [Loughborough University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_University) (2008), [Sheffield Hallam University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Hallam_University) (2011)[^o27] and [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) (2019).[^o28][^o26]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Her first marriage was to Martin Hoyles: their marriage ended in divorce.[^o31] In 1996, she married Richard Noss, Professor of Mathematics Education at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London).[^o31][^o30] Her second marriage brought two [step children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Step_children).[^o31]
