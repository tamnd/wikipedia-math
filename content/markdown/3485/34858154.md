# Anatole Beck

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Anatole Beck"}

**Anatole Beck** (19 March 1930 – 21 December 2014)[^o3] was an American mathematician.

:::

## Education and academic career {#Education_and_academic_career}

Beck graduated from [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) in 1947,[^o5] studied at [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College) (Bachelor's degree 1951) and in 1956 received his PhD from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) under [Shizuo Kakutani](wikipedia://en/2572542).[^o6] In 1958 he became Assistant Professor and in 1966 Professor in the [Department of Mathematics](http://math.wisc.edu) at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). He married Jewish feminist writer [Evelyn Torton Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Torton_Beck) in 1954; they had two children before divorcing in 1974.[^o7][^o8] He met his second wife Eve-Lynn Siegel Beck in 1998, she was cousins with Michael Bleicher, one of Anatole’s longtime friends.[^o9]

He was a visiting professor at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) and a visiting scholar at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), and the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University).

Beck's work dealt with [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713), [Probability](wikipedia://en/22934) in [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces), [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), [search theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_theory), [linear search problem](wikipedia://en/22727353), and mathematics in the social sciences.

## Union leadership, political activism, and social commentator {#Union_leadership,_political_activism,_and_social_commentator}

Beck was an ardent supporter of [unionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union) and [cooperative economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_economics), helping to found the faculty union chapter at the University of Wisconsin-Madison and serving as the Vice President of the [Wisconsin Federation of Teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Teachers). He spoke out strongly in favor of academic freedom on campus, and was an early supporter of the free speech rights of the movement against the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War) in the 1960s. He witnessed the “[Dow Riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protests_against_the_Vietnam_War)” of October 18, 1967 in the Commerce Building (now Ingraham Hall), condemning the police violence against students at a faculty meeting.[^o15] Beck made headlines in 1977 when he offered a \$100 grant to a "white Quaker female student with financial need" to highlight the University of Wisconsin's policy of accepting gifts with discriminatory conditions attached.[^o11][^o12] Around 1980 he and Jim Donnelly founded [Wisconsin University Union](http://www.wuu.info/) (WUU) which focused on promoting shared governance and academic freedom.[^o9]

Beck was interested in solving social problems. In 1996 he wrote “[The Knowledge Business](https://www.math.wisc.edu/beck/2k.htm)” advocating for investment in education, universal public preschools with adequate stimulation and nutrition.[^o13] Ahead of his time, he argued for free public transportation, advocated for greater leisure, and a universal basic income from what he called the “knowledge dividend” from all past knowledge and innovation.[^o9][^o13] Arguing against overtime for some and unemployment for other, he predicted that, unchecked, companies would offer little stability, longer hours and create a global economy of “exoslavery,” where the conditions of slavery could be moved to other countries.[^o9][^o13] He condemned economists for calling this rational and free trade, predicting increases in underemployment, homelessness, a rise in TB, and disinvestment in basic research, among other things. He also called for a tax on security transactions, now called a [Tobin tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobin_tax).[^o9][^o13] Beginning in 2008 he was a commentator on the Insurgent Radio Kiosk on Madison's community radio station WORT.[^o14]

In 2009 Beck was interviewed extensively by Robert Lange for the UW-Madison Oral History Program.[^o15]
