# Tangential trapezoid

*Image: Tangential trapezoid.svg.* A tangential trapezoid.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tangential trapezoid"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **tangential trapezoid**, also called a **circumscribed trapezoid**, is a [trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826) whose four sides are all [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) within the trapezoid: the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) or *inscribed circle*. It is the special case of a [tangential quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/9176412) in which at least one pair of opposite sides are [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497). As for other trapezoids, the parallel sides are called the [bases](wikipedia://en/9579379) and the other two sides the *legs*. The legs can be equal (see [isosceles tangential trapezoid](wikipedia://en/34853912#Isosceles_tangential_trapezoid) below), but they don't have to be.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

Examples of tangential trapezoids are [rhombi](wikipedia://en/183884) and [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29).

## Characterization {#Characterization}

If the incircle is tangent to the sides $AB$ and $CD$ at $W$ and $Y$ respectively, then a tangential quadrilateral $ABCD$ is also a [trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826) with parallel sides $AB$ and $CD$ if and only if[^o10]

$$\overline{AW} \cdot \overline{DY} = \overline{BW} \cdot \overline{CY}$$

and $AD$ and $BC$ are the parallel sides of a trapezoid if and only if

$$\overline{AW} \cdot \overline{BW} = \overline{CY} \cdot \overline{DY}.$$

:::

## Area {#Area}

*Image: visual\_proof\_tangential\_polygon\_area\_is\_inradius\_times\_semiperimeter.svg.* As for a general tangential quadrilateral, the area of a tangential trapezoid equals the product of its inradius and its semiperimeter – the dashed edges also show that the total length of even-numbered edges equal that of odd-numbered ones

The formula for the [area of a trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826#Area) can be simplified using [Pitot's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitot's_theorem) to get a formula for the area of a tangential trapezoid. If the bases have lengths $a, b$, and any one of the other two sides has length $c$, then the area $K$ is given by the formula[^o7] (This formula can be used only in cases where the bases are parallel.)

$$K=\frac{a+b}{|b-a|}\sqrt{ab(a-c)(c-b)}.$$

The area can be expressed in terms of the tangent lengths $e, f, g, h$ as[^o8]

$$K=\sqrt[4]{efgh}(e+f+g+h).$$

## Inradius {#Inradius}

Using the same notations as for the area, the radius in the incircle is[^o7]

$$r=\frac{K}{a+b}=\frac{\sqrt{ab(a-c)(c-b)}}{|b-a|}.$$

The [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of the incircle is equal to the height of the tangential trapezoid.

The inradius can also be expressed in terms of the [tangent lengths](wikipedia://en/25278#Special_line_segments) as[^o8]

$$r=\sqrt[4]{efgh}.$$

Moreover, if the tangent lengths $e, f, g, h$ emanate respectively from vertices $A, B, C, D$ and $AB$ is parallel to $DC$, then[^o10]

$$r=\sqrt{eh}=\sqrt{fg}.$$

## Properties of the incenter {#Properties_of_the_incenter}

If the incircle is tangent to the bases at $P, Q$, then $P, I, Q$ are [collinear](wikipedia://en/946975#Collinear_points), where $I$ is the incenter.[^o12]

The angles $\angle AID$ and $\angle BIC$ in a tangential trapezoid $ABCD$, with bases $AB$ and $DC$, are [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956).[^o12]

The incenter lies on the median (also called the midsegment; that is, the segment connecting the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the legs).[^o12]

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

The [median](wikipedia://en/201826#Midsegment_and_height) (midsegment) of a tangential trapezoid equals one fourth of the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the trapezoid. It also equals half the sum of the bases, as in all trapezoids.

If two circles are drawn, each with a diameter coinciding with the legs of a tangential trapezoid, then these two circles are [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to each other.[^o14]

## Right tangential trapezoid {#Right_tangential_trapezoid}

*Image: Right tangential trapezoid 001.svg.* A right tangential trapezoid.

A **right tangential trapezoid** is a tangential trapezoid where two adjacent angles are [right angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_angles). If the bases have lengths $a, b$, then the inradius is[^o17]

$$r=\frac{ab}{a+b}.$$

Thus the [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of the incircle is the [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) of the bases.

The right tangential trapezoid has the [area](wikipedia://en/1209)[^o17]

$$\displaystyle K=ab$$

and its [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) $P$ is[^o17]

$$\displaystyle P=2(a+b).$$

:::

## Isosceles tangential trapezoid {#Isosceles_tangential_trapezoid}

*Image: Bicentric isosceles trapezoid 001.svg.* Every [isosceles](wikipedia://en/511691) tangential trapezoid is [bicentric](wikipedia://en/23335118).

An **isosceles tangential trapezoid** is a tangential trapezoid where the legs are equal. Since an [isosceles trapezoid](wikipedia://en/511691) is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/194131), an isosceles tangential trapezoid is a [bicentric quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/23335118). That is, it has both an incircle and a [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432).

If the bases are $a, b$, then the inradius is given by[^o19]

$$r=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{ab}.$$

To derive this formula was a simple [Sangaku](wikipedia://en/1055365) problem from [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan). From [Pitot's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitot's_theorem) it follows that the lengths of the legs are half the sum of the bases. Since the diameter of the incircle is the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of the product of the bases, an isosceles tangential trapezoid gives a nice geometric interpretation of the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) and [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of the bases as the length of a leg and the diameter of the incircle respectively.

The area $K$ of an isosceles tangential trapezoid with bases $a, b$ is given by[^o20]

$$K=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{ab}(a+b).$$
