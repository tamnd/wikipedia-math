# Section (category theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Section"}

*Image: Section\_retract.svg.* $f$ is a retraction of $g$. $g$ is a section of $f$. $1_Y$ is the identity morphism on an object $Y$.

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **section** is a [right inverse](wikipedia://en/14907#Left_and_right_inverses) of some [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891). [Dually](wikipedia://en/310885), a **retraction** is a [left inverse](wikipedia://en/14907#Left_and_right_inverses) of some [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891). In other words, if $f: X\to Y$ and $g: Y\to X$ are morphisms whose composition $f \circ g: Y\to Y$ is the [identity morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_morphism) on $Y$, then $g$ is a section of $f$, and $f$ is a retraction of $g$.[^o2]

:::

Every section is a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) (every morphism with a left inverse is [left-cancellative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-cancellative)), and every retraction is an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) (every morphism with a right inverse is [right-cancellative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-cancellative)).

If there exists a section from $Y$ to $X$, then we say that $Y$ is a **retract** of $X$.

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), sections are also called **split monomorphisms** and retractions are also called **split epimorphisms**. In an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063), if $f: X\to Y$ is a split epimorphism with split monomorphism $g: Y\to X$, then $X$ is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of $Y$ and the [kernel](wikipedia://en/142616) of $f$. The synonym **coretraction** for section is sometimes seen in the literature, although rarely in recent work.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A section that is also an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828). Dually a retraction that is also a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) is an isomorphism.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

The concept of a retraction in category theory comes from the essentially similar notion of a [retraction](wikipedia://en/2120001) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954): $f:X \to Y$ where $Y$ is a subspace of $X$ is a retraction in the topological sense, if it's a retraction of the inclusion map $i:Y\hookrightarrow X$ in the category theory sense. The concept in topology was defined by [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk) in 1931.[^o7]

Borsuk's student, [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501), was with [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) the founder of category theory, and (as the earliest publications on category theory concerned various topological spaces) one might have expected this term to have initially be used. In fact, their earlier publications, up to, e.g., Mac Lane (1963)'s *Homology*, used the term right inverse. It was not until 1965 when Eilenberg and [John Coleman Moore](wikipedia://en/13433862) coined the dual term 'coretraction' that Borsuk's term was lifted to category theory in general.[^o8] The term coretraction gave way to the term section by the end of the 1960s.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

In the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), every monomorphism ([injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) [function](wikipedia://en/185427)) with a [non-empty](wikipedia://en/9566) [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) is a section, and every epimorphism ([surjective function](wikipedia://en/27873)) is a retraction; the latter statement is equivalent to the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840).

In the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K*, every monomorphism and every epimorphism splits; this follows from the fact that [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) can be uniquely defined by specifying their values on a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420).

In the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748), the epimorphism **Z** → **Z**/2**Z** which sends every [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) to its remainder [modulo 2](wikipedia://en/20087) does not split; in fact the only morphism **Z**/2**Z** → **Z** is the [zero map](wikipedia://en/320638). Similarly, the natural monomorphism **Z**/2**Z** → **Z**/4**Z** doesn't split even though there is a non-trivial morphism **Z**/4**Z** → **Z**/2**Z**.

The categorical concept of a section is important in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), and is also closely related to the notion of a [section](wikipedia://en/367621) of a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954): in the latter case, a section of a fiber bundle is a section of the bundle projection map of the fiber bundle.

Given a [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) $\bar X$ with quotient map $\pi\colon X \to \bar X$, a section of $\pi$ is called a [transversal](wikipedia://en/897733).
