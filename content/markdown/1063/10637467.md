# Patrick du Val

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Patrick du Val"}

**Patrick du Val** (March 26, 1903 – January 22, 1987) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). The concept of [Du Val singularity](wikipedia://en/22921783) of an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) is named after him.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Du Val was born in [Cheadle Hulme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheadle_Hulme), [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire). He was the son of a [cabinet maker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_maker), but his parents' marriage broke up. As a child, he suffered ill-health, in particular [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma), and was educated mostly by his mother. He was awarded a first class honours degree from the [University of London External Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London_External_Programme) in 1926, which he took by correspondence course.

He was a talented linguist, for example teaching himself [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language) so that he might read [Peer Gynt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_Gynt). He also had a strong interest in history but his love of mathematics led him to pursue that as a career. His earliest publications show a leaning towards applied mathematics.

His mother moved to a village near [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) and he became acquainted with [Henry Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._F._Baker), [Lowndean Professor of Astronomy and Geometry](wikipedia://en/648990). Baker turned his interest towards [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and he entered [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1927.

## Research in geometry {#Research_in_geometry}

Du Val's early work before becoming a research student was on relativity, including a paper on the [De Sitter model](wikipedia://en/301666) of the universe and [Grassmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassmann)'s tensor calculus. His doctorate was on algebraic geometry and in his thesis he generalised a result of [Schoute](wikipedia://en/6022750). He worked on [algebraic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_surfaces) and later in his career became interested in [elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_functions).

He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) with a thesis entitled 'On Certain Configurations of Algebraic Geometry Having Groups of Self-Transformations Representable by Symmetry Groups of Certain Polygons' under Baker's supervision in 1930. While a research student he had many famous geometers including [Hodge](wikipedia://en/384430) as fellow research students, and he formed a particular friendship with [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) and [Semple](wikipedia://en/35081307). He was elected a fellow of Trinity in 1930 for four years. During that time he travelled extensively, visiting [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) and working with [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661), then in 1934 [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he attended lectures by [James W. Alexander](wikipedia://en/9848523), [Luther P. Eisenhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_P._Eisenhart), [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384), [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen), [Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494), and [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544).

In 1936, Du Val took up an assistant lectureship in the [Mathematics Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester) at [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester), where he stayed for five years. He was then funded by a [British Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Council) scheme to go to [Istanbul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_University) as a professor of pure mathematics. There he learnt [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) and even wrote a book on coordinate geometry in that language.

After a spell in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia), he returned to the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom), first taking up a post in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol), then at the [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) in 1954, where he remained until he retired in 1970. Together with Semple he led the London Geometry Seminar during the time he spent in London.

Du Val had three children.

## Later life {#Later_life}

After retirement, Du Val returned to Istanbul. For three years he held the same post as before, and then as if reversing history, settled down to a retirement in Cambridge.

He is remembered as an interesting character. For example, in Manchester during the war he was remembered as a cloaked figure striding the parapets, as he carried out his duties as a fire warden. He was also known for startling the travelling public by carrying around a large string bag filled with garishly coloured [stellated icosahedra](wikipedia://en/3029898).
