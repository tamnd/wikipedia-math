# Crossing number (knot theory)

*Image: Crossing numbers trefoil.svg.* Trefoil knot without 3-fold symmetry with crossings labeled.

*Image: Knot table.svg.* A table of all [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) with seven crossing numbers or fewer (not including mirror images).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crossing number"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) area of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **crossing number** of a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) is the smallest number of crossings of any diagram of the knot. It is a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

By way of example, the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) has crossing number [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29), the [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773) three and the [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565) four. There are no other knots with a crossing number this low, and just two knots have crossing number five, but the number of knots with a particular crossing number increases rapidly as the crossing number increases.

:::

## Tabulation {#Tabulation}

Tables of [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) are traditionally indexed by crossing number, with a subscript to indicate which particular knot out of those with this many crossings is meant (this sub-ordering is not based on anything in particular, except that [torus knots](wikipedia://en/1165182) then [twist knots](wikipedia://en/26701261) are listed first). The listing goes 3<sub>1</sub> (the trefoil knot), 4<sub>1</sub> (the figure-eight knot), 5<sub>1</sub>, 5<sub>2</sub>, 6<sub>1</sub>, etc. This order has not changed significantly since [P. G. Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._G._Tait) published a tabulation of knots in 1877.[^o5]

## Additivity {#Additivity}

*Image: Square knot sum of trefoils stick number.png.* Square knot (cr(6)) \= trefoil (cr(3)) + trefoil reflection (cr(3)).

There has been very little progress on understanding the behavior of crossing number under rudimentary operations on knots. A big [open question](wikipedia://en/1333335) asks if the crossing number is additive when taking [knot sums](wikipedia://en/490134#Connected_sum_of_knots). It is also expected that a [satellite](wikipedia://en/9100897) of a knot *K* should have larger crossing number than *K*, but this has not been [proven](wikipedia://en/82285).

Additivity of crossing number under knot sum has been proven for special cases, for example if the summands are [alternating knots](wikipedia://en/1236143)[^o7] (or more generally, [adequate knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adequate_knots)), or if the summands are [torus knots](wikipedia://en/1165182).[^o8][^o9] [Marc Lackenby](wikipedia://en/49182345) has also given a proof that there is a constant *N* > 1 such that $\tfrac{1}{N}$(cr(*K*<sub>1</sub>) + cr(*K*<sub>2</sub>)) ≤ cr(*K*<sub>1</sub> + *K*<sub>2</sub>), but his method, which utilizes [normal surfaces](wikipedia://en/3141418), cannot improve *N* to 1.[^o10]

## Applications in bioinformatics {#Applications_in_bioinformatics}

There are connections between the crossing number of a knot and the physical behavior of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) knots. For prime DNA knots, crossing number is a good predictor of the relative velocity of the DNA knot in agarose [gel electrophoresis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gel_electrophoresis). Basically, the higher the crossing number, the faster the relative velocity. For [composite knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_knot), this does not appear to be the case, although experimental conditions can drastically change the results.[^o12]

## Related invariants {#Related_invariants}

There are related concepts of [average crossing number](wikipedia://en/17695525) and [asymptotic crossing number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_crossing_number). Both of these quantities bound the standard crossing number. Asymptotic crossing number is [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) to be equal to crossing number.

Other numerical knot invariants include the [bridge number](wikipedia://en/10041975), [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999), [stick number](wikipedia://en/12527733), and [unknotting number](wikipedia://en/26711383).
