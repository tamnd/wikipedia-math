# Čech complex

*Image: Cech-example.png.* Constructing the Čech complex of a set of points sampled from a circle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Čech complex"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009), the **Čech complex** is an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) constructed from a point cloud in any [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) which is meant to capture [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) information about the point cloud or the distribution it is drawn from. Given a finite point cloud *X* and an *ε* > 0, we construct the Čech complex $\check C_\varepsilon(X)$ as follows: Take the elements of *X* as the vertex set of $\check C_\varepsilon(X)$. Then, for each $\sigma\subset X$, let $\sigma\in \check C_\varepsilon(X)$ if the set of *ε*-balls centered at points of σ has a [nonempty](wikipedia://en/9566) [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429). In other words, the Čech complex is the [nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_of_a_covering) of the set of *ε*-balls centered at points of *X*. By the [nerve lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_of_a_covering), the Čech complex is homotopy equivalent to the union of the balls, also known as the [offset filtration](wikipedia://en/73134451).[^o2]

:::

## Relation to Vietoris–Rips complex {#Relation_to_Vietoris–Rips_complex}

The Čech complex is a subcomplex of the [Vietoris–Rips complex](wikipedia://en/13056778). While the Čech complex is more computationally expensive than the Vietoris–Rips complex, since we must check for higher order intersections of the balls in the complex, the nerve theorem provides a guarantee that the Čech complex is homotopy equivalent to union of the balls in the complex. The Vietoris–Rips complex may not be.[^o2]
