# Luc Illusie

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luc Illusie"}

**Luc Illusie** (; born 1940)[^o3] is a French mathematician, specializing in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). His most important work concerns the theory of the cotangent complex and deformations, [crystalline cohomology](wikipedia://en/10220067) and the De Rham–Witt complex, and logarithmic geometry.[^o3] In 2012, he was awarded the [Émile Picard Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Picard_Medal) of the French Academy of Sciences.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Luc Illusie entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1959. At first a student of the mathematician [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536), he participated in the Cartan–Schwartz seminar of 1963–1964. In 1964, following Cartan's advice, he began to work with [Alexandre Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Grothendieck), collaborating with him on two volumes of the latter's [Séminaire de Géométrie Algébrique du Bois Marie](wikipedia://en/394508). In 1970, Illusie introduced the concept of the [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860).

A researcher in the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) from 1964 to 1976, Illusie then became a professor at the [University of Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud), retiring as emeritus professor in 2005.[^o5] Between 1984 and 1995, he was the director of the arithmetic and algebraic geometry group in the department of mathematics of that university. Torsten Ekedahl and [Gérard Laumon](wikipedia://en/22565856) are among his students.

## Thesis {#Thesis}

In May 1971, Illusie defended a [state doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) ( Thèse d’État) entitled "Cotangent complex; application to the theory of deformations" at the [University of Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud), in front of a jury composed of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), [Michel Demazure](wikipedia://en/32535595) and [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and presided by [Henri Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Paul_Cartan).[^o7]

The thesis was published in French by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media) as a two-volume book (in 1971[^o8] & 1972[^o9]). The main results of the thesis are summarized in a paper in English (entitled "Cotangent complex and Deformations of torsors and group schemes") presented in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia), at [Dalhousie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalhousie_University), in January 1971 as part of a colloquium on algebraic geometry.[^o7] This paper, originally published by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media) in 1972,[^o10] also exists in a slightly extended version.[^o7]

Illusie's construction of the [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860) generalizes that of Michel André[^o11] and [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519)[^o12] to morphisms of [ringed topoi](wikipedia://en/21391464#Ringed_topoi). The generality of the framework makes it possible to apply the formalism to various first-order [deformation problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory): [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754), [morphisms of schemes](wikipedia://en/7482797), [group schemes](wikipedia://en/510523) and [torsors](wikipedia://en/41910974) under group schemes. Results concerning commutative group schemes in particular were the key tool in Grothendieck's proof of his existence and structure theorem for infinitesimal deformations of [Barsotti–Tate groups](wikipedia://en/29750969),[^o13] an ingredient in [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226)' proof of the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture). In Chapter VIII of the second volume of the thesis, Illusie introduces and studies derived [de Rham](wikipedia://en/221530) complexes.

## Awards {#Awards}

Illusie has received the Langevin Prize of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1977 and, in 2012, the [Émile Picard Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Picard_Medal) of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) for "his fundamental work on the [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860), the [Picard–Lefschetz formula](wikipedia://en/34794974), [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) and [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/37518039) geometry".[^o15]
