# List of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras"}

In mathematics, a [Nichols algebra](wikipedia://en/32370851) is a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) in a [braided category](wikipedia://en/1470466) assigned to an object *V* in this category (e.g. a [braided vector space](wikipedia://en/32518704)). The Nichols algebra is a quotient of the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) of *V* enjoying a certain [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) and is typically infinite-dimensional. Nichols algebras appear naturally in any pointed Hopf algebra and enabled their classification in important cases.[^o2] The most well known examples for Nichols algebras are the [Borel parts](wikipedia://en/588356#Lie_algebra) $U_q(\mathfrak{g})^+$ of the infinite-dimensional [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups) when *q* is no [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950), and the first examples of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras are the [Borel parts](wikipedia://en/588356#Lie_algebra) $u_q(\mathfrak{g})^+$ of the Frobenius–Lusztig kernel (*small quantum group*) when *q* is a root of unity.

:::

The following article lists all known finite-dimensional Nichols algebras $\mathfrak{B}(V)$ where $V$ is a [Yetter–Drinfel'd module](wikipedia://en/10497038) over a finite group $G$, where the group is generated by the support of $V$. For more details on Nichols algebras see [Nichols algebra](wikipedia://en/32370851).

- There are two major cases:
  - $G$ **abelian**, which implies $V$ is diagonally braided $x_i\otimes x_j\mapsto q_{ij}x_j\otimes x_i$.
  - $G$ **nonabelian**.
- The **rank** is the number of irreducible summands $V=\bigoplus_{i\in I} V_i$ in the semisimple Yetter–Drinfel'd module $V$.

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="Conjugacy class"}

- The **irreducible summands** $V_i=\mathcal{O}_{[g]}^\chi$ are each associated to a [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) $[g]\subset G$ and an irreducible representation $\chi$ of the centralizer $\operatorname{Cent}(g)$.

:::

- To any Nichols algebra there is by [^o36] attached
  - a generalized **root system** and a Weyl groupoid. These are classified in.[^o37]
  - In particular **several Dynkin diagrams** (for inequivalent types of Weyl chambers). Each [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109) has one vertex per irreducible $V_i$ and edges depending on their braided commutators in the Nichols algebra.

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hilbert series"}

- The **[Hilbert series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_series)** of the graded algebra $\mathfrak{B}(V)$ is given. An observation is that it factorizes in each case into polynomials $(n)_t:=1+t+t^2+\cdots + t^{n-1}$. We only give the Hilbert series and dimension of the Nichols algebra in characteristic $0$.

:::

Note that a Nichols algebra only depends on the braided vector space $V$ and can therefore be realized over many different groups. Sometimes there are two or three Nichols algebras with different $V$ and non-isomorphic Nichols algebra, which are closely related (e.g. cocycle twists of each other). These are given by different conjugacy classes in the same column.

## State of classification {#State_of_classification}

(as of 2015)

### Established classification results {#Established_classification_results}

- Finite-dimensional diagonal Nichols algebras over the complex numbers were classified by Heckenberger in.[^o38] The case of arbitrary characteristic is ongoing work of Heckenberger, Wang.[^o39]
- Finite-dimensional Nichols algebras of semisimple Yetter–Drinfel'd modules of rank >1 over finite nonabelian groups (generated by the support) were classified by Heckenberger and Vendramin in.[^o40]

### Negative criteria {#Negative_criteria}

The case of rank 1 (irreducible Yetter–Drinfel'd module) over a [nonabelian group](wikipedia://en/4791400) is still largely open, with few examples known.

Much progress has been made by Andruskiewitsch and others by finding subracks (for example diagonal ones) that would lead to infinite-dimensional Nichols algebras. As of 2015, known groups **not** admitting finite-dimensional Nichols algebras are [^o52][^o41]

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alternating group"}

- for [alternating groups](wikipedia://en/49726) $\mathbb{A}_{n\geq 5}$ [^o42]

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Symmetric group"}

- for [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) $\mathbb{S}_{n\geq 6}$ except a short list of examples[^o42]

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Group of Lie type"}

- some [group of Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490) such as most $PSL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$[^o53] and most unipotent classes in $Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_q)$[^o54]

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sporadic group"}

- all [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806) except a short list of possibilities (resp. conjugacy classes in ATLAS notation) that are all real or *j* = 3-quasireal:

:::

  - ...for the [Fischer group](wikipedia://en/327206) $Fi_{22}\;$ the classes $22A,22B\;$
  - ...for the [baby monster group](wikipedia://en/324799) *B* the classes $16C,\;16D,\;32A,\;32B,\;32C,\;32D,\;34A,\;46A,\;46B\;$
  - ...for the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422) *M* the classes $32A,\;32B,\;46A,\;46B,\;92A,\;92B,\;94A,\;94B\;$

Usually a large amount of conjugacy classes ae of type D ("not commutative enough"), while the others tend to possess sufficient abelian subracks and can be excluded by their consideration. Several cases have to be done by-hand. Note that the open cases tend to have very small centralizers (usually cyclic) and representations χ (usually the 1-dimensional sign representation). Significant exceptions are the conjugacy classes of order 16, 32 having as centralizers [p-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) of order 2048 resp. 128 and currently no restrictions on χ.

## Over abelian groups {#Over_abelian_groups}

Finite-dimensional diagonal Nichols algebras over the complex numbers were classified by Heckenberger in [^o38] in terms of the braiding matrix $q_{ij}$, more precisely the data $q_{ii},q_{ij}q_{ji}$. The small quantum groups $u_q(\mathfrak{g})^+$ are a special case $q_{ij}=q^{(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)}$, but there are several exceptional examples involving the primes 2,3,4,5,7.

Recently there has been progress understanding the other examples as exceptional Lie algebras and super-Lie algebras in finite characteristic.

### Nichols algebras from diagram folding {#Nichols_algebras_from_diagram_folding}

The following families Nichols algebras were constructed by Lentner using diagram folding,[^o47] the fourth example appearing only in characteristic 3 was discovered during the classification of Heckenberger and Vendramin.[^o40]

The construction start with a known Nichols algebra (here diagonal ones related to quantum groups) and an additional automorphism of the Dynkin diagram. Hence the two major cases are whether this automorphism exchanges two disconnected copies or is a proper diagram automorphism of a connected Dynkin diagram. The resulting root system is folding / restriction of the original root system.[^o51] By construction, generators and relations are known from the diagonal case.
