# Dorothy Brady

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dorothy Brady"}

**Dorothy Elizabeth Stahl Brady** (June 14, 1903 – April 17, 1977) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist). She was a professor of economics at [Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania) from 1958 to 1970.[^o3]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Born in [Elk River, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk_River,_Minnesota), she grew up in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon), attending [Lincoln High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_High_School_%28Portland,_Oregon%29) and later [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College) studying mathematics and physics. She graduated from Reed in 1924.[^o5] She was married to fellow Reed student [Robert A. Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Brady_%28economist%29) from 1924 to 1936, they had a son in 1933.[^o6]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Brady earned a master's degree in mathematics from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1926, then worked as an instructor at [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College), as a research assistant at the [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Economic_Research), and a studied and taught at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). She went on to complete a Ph.D. in mathematics from [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1933, and was the 6th woman ever to do so.[^o6]

Brady went on to a long career in government working for the [U.S. Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Agriculture) and the [U.S. Department of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Labor) where she was the chief of the cost of living division from 1944 to 1948, and the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](wikipedia://en/72528) where she was the chief of division of prices and cost of living in 1953.

She returned to academia as a professor in 1956 at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), was chairman of the graduate group in economic history from 1964 to 1970 at the [Wharton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania) of the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). She continued to consult to the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](wikipedia://en/72528) and the [Social Security Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_Administration) during her academic tenure.[^o6]

## Publications {#Publications}

- "Variations in Family Living Expenditures" (1938)[^o9]
- "Savings and the Income Distribution" (1947, with [Rose D. Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Friedman))[^o10]
- "Equal Pay for Women Workers" (1947)[^o11]
- "Family Budgets: A Historical Survey" (1948)[^o12]
- "The Pattern of Food Expenditures" (1948, with Helen A. Barber)[^o13]
- "The Use of Statistical Procedures in the Derivation of Family Budgets" (1949)[^o14]
- "Family Savings in Relation to Changes in the Level and Distribution of Income" (1952)[^o15]
- "Individual Incomes and the Structure of Consumer Units" (1958)[^o16]
- "Relative Prices in the Nineteenth Century" (1964)[^o17]

## Honors {#Honors}

Brady received the [National Women's Press Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Woman's_Press_Association) Award in Economics, and was book review editor of the [Journal of Economic History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_of_Economic_History) from 1969 to 1974.[^o6] Brady became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1949[^o19] and Fellow of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society) in 1950.[^o20]
