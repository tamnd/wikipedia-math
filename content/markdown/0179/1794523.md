# Richard Friederich Arens

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Friederich Arens"}

**Richard Friederich Arens** (24 April 1919 – 3 May 2000) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was born in [Iserlohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iserlohn), Germany. He [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigrated) to the United States in 1925.

:::

Arens received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1945 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). He was several times a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (1945–46, 1946–47, and 1953–54).[^o6] He was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1950 in Cambridge, Massachusetts.[^o7]

Arens worked in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and was a professor at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) for more than 40 years. He served on the editorial board of the *Pacific Journal of Mathematics* for 14 years 1965–1979. There are three [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) named for Arens in the book *[Counterexamples in Topology](wikipedia://en/915449)*, including [Arens–Fort space](wikipedia://en/8588347).

Arens died in [Los Angeles, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California).
