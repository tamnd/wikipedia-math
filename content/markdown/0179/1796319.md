# Montel space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Montel space"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Montel space**, named after [Paul Montel](wikipedia://en/1796347), is any [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) in which an analog of [Montel's theorem](wikipedia://en/1484228) holds. Specifically, a Montel space is a [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733592) topological vector space in which every [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) and [bounded subset](wikipedia://en/1789812) is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) has the **[Heine–Borel property](wikipedia://en/59595#In_the_theory_of_topological_vector_spaces)** if every [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) and [bounded subset](wikipedia://en/1789812) is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042). A **Montel space** is a [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733592) topological vector space with the Heine–Borel property. Equivalently, it is an [infrabarrelled](wikipedia://en/63950387) semi-Montel space where a [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) is called a **semi-Montel space** or **perfect** if every [bounded subset](wikipedia://en/1789812) is [relatively compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_compact).[^o4] A subset of a TVS is compact if and only if it is [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856#complete_subset) and [totally bounded](wikipedia://en/1528346). A **Fréchet–Montel space** is a [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) that is also a Montel space.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterizations"}

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

A [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) is a Montel space if and only if each [weak-\* convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak-*_topology) sequence in its continuous dual is [strongly convergent](wikipedia://en/63951372).[^n1]

A [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) $X$ is a Montel space if and only if every bounded continuous function $X \to c_0$ sends closed bounded absolutely convex subsets of $X$ to relatively compact subsets of $c_0.$ Moreover, if $C^b(X)$ denotes the vector space of all bounded continuous functions on a [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) $X,$ then $X$ is Montel if and only if every sequence in $C^b(X)$ that converges to zero in the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335) also converges uniformly to zero on all closed bounded absolutely convex subsets of $X.$ [^n2]

:::

## Sufficient conditions {#Sufficient_conditions}

**Semi-Montel spaces**

A closed vector subspace of a semi-Montel space is again a semi-Montel space. The locally convex [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of any family of semi-Montel spaces is again a semi-Montel space. The [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of an inverse system consisting of semi-Montel spaces is again a semi-Montel space. The [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of any family of semi-Montel spaces (resp. Montel spaces) is again a semi-Montel space (resp. a Montel space).

**Montel spaces**

The strong dual of a Montel space is Montel. A [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733592) [quasi-complete](wikipedia://en/63948433) [nuclear space](wikipedia://en/2702957) is a Montel space.[^n1] Every product and locally convex direct sum of a family of Montel spaces is a Montel space.[^n1] The strict [inductive limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_limit) of a sequence of Montel spaces is a Montel space.[^n1] In contrast, closed subspaces and separated quotients of Montel spaces are in general not even [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091).[^n1] Every [Fréchet](wikipedia://en/233801) [Schwartz](wikipedia://en/64266300) space is a Montel space.[^n3]

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Montel spaces are [paracompact](wikipedia://en/48631) and [normal](wikipedia://en/48629).[^o17] Semi-Montel spaces are [quasi-complete](wikipedia://en/63948433) and [semi-reflexive](wikipedia://en/22124711) while Montel spaces are [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091).

No infinite-dimensional [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is a Montel space. This is because a Banach space cannot satisfy the [Heine–Borel property](wikipedia://en/59595#In_the_theory_of_topological_vector_spaces): the closed unit ball is closed and bounded, but not compact. [Fréchet](wikipedia://en/233801) Montel spaces are separable and have a [bornological](wikipedia://en/1804457) strong dual. A metrizable Montel space is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855).[^n1]

Fréchet–Montel spaces are [distinguished spaces](wikipedia://en/64266856).

:::

::: {#o20 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In classical [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), Montel's theorem asserts that the space of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) on an [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) subset of the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) has this property.

Many Montel spaces of contemporary interest arise as spaces of test functions for a space of [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29). The space $C^{\infty}(\Omega)$ of [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) on an open set $\Omega$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a Montel space equipped with the topology induced by the family of [seminorms](wikipedia://en/292067)[^o21]

$$\|f\|_{K,n} = \sup_{|\alpha| \leq n}\sup_{x \in K}\left|\partial^\alpha f(x)\right|$$

for $n = 1, 2, \ldots$ and $K$ ranges over compact subsets of $\Omega,$ and $\alpha$ is a [multi-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-index). Similarly, the space of [compactly supported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support) functions in an open set with the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736) of the family of inclusions $\scriptstyle{C^\infty_0(K)\subset C^\infty_0(\Omega)}$ as $K$ ranges over all compact subsets of $\Omega.$ The [Schwartz space](wikipedia://en/4277567) is also a Montel space.

### Counter-examples {#Counter-examples}

Every infinite-dimensional [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) is a [barrelled space](wikipedia://en/1733592) that is not a Montel space.[^n4] In particular, every infinite-dimensional [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is not a Montel space.[^n4] There exist Montel spaces that are not [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) and there exist Montel spaces that are not [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856).[^n4] There exist Montel spaces having closed vector subspaces that are not Montel spaces.[^n5]
