# Invariant polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Invariant polynomial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **invariant polynomial** is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) $P$ that is [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) under a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $\Gamma$ [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $V$. Therefore, $P$ is a $\Gamma$-invariant polynomial if

:::

$$P(\gamma x) = P(x)$$

for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$ and $x \in V$.[^o2]

Cases of particular importance are for Γ a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) (in the theory of [Molien series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molien_series), in particular), a [compact group](wikipedia://en/750326), a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) or [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924). For a basis-independent definition of 'polynomial' nothing is lost by referring to the [symmetric powers](wikipedia://en/57047243) of the given [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of Γ.[^o3]
