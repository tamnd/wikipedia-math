# Dual topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dual topology"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) a **dual topology** is a [locally convex topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_topology) on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) that is induced by the [continuous dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual) of the vector space, by means of the [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) (also called *pairing*) associated with the [dual pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_pair).

:::

The different dual topologies for a given dual pair are characterized by the Mackey–Arens theorem. All locally convex topologies with their continuous dual are trivially a dual pair and the locally convex topology is a dual topology.

Several topological properties depend only on the [dual pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_pair) and not on the chosen dual topology and thus it is often possible to substitute a complicated dual topology by a simpler one.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a [dual pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_pair) $(X, Y, \langle , \rangle)$, a **dual topology** on $X$ is a [locally convex topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_topology) $\tau$ so that

$$(X, \tau)' \simeq Y.$$

Here $(X, \tau)'$ denotes the [continuous dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual) of $(X,\tau)$ and $(X, \tau)' \simeq Y$ means that there is a [linear isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_isomorphism)

$$\Psi : Y \to (X, \tau)',\quad y \mapsto (x \mapsto \langle x, y\rangle).$$

(If a locally convex topology $\tau$ on $X$ is not a dual topology, then either $\Psi$ is not surjective or it is ill-defined since the linear functional $x \mapsto \langle x, y\rangle$ is not continuous on $X$ for some $y$.)

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- **Theorem** (by [Mackey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mackey)): Given a dual pair, the [bounded sets](wikipedia://en/1789812) under any dual topology are identical.
- Under any dual topology the same sets are [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733629).

:::

## Characterization of dual topologies {#Characterization_of_dual_topologies}

The **Mackey–Arens theorem**, named after [George Mackey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mackey) and [Richard Arens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Friedrich_Arens), characterizes all possible dual topologies on a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space).

The theorem shows that the [coarsest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarser_topology) dual topology is the [weak topology](wikipedia://en/33662), the topology of uniform convergence on all finite subsets of $X'$, and the [finest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finer_topology) is the [Mackey topology](wikipedia://en/1790627), the topology of uniform convergence on all [absolutely convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_convex) weakly compact subsets of $X'$.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Mackey–Arens theorem"}

### Mackey–Arens theorem {#Mackey–Arens_theorem}

Given a [dual pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_pair) $(X, X')$ with $X$ a locally convex space and $X'$ its [continuous dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual), then $\tau$ is a dual topology on $X$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is a [topology of uniform convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_of_uniform_convergence) on a family of [absolutely convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_convex) and [weakly compact](wikipedia://en/33662) subsets of $X'$

:::
