# Kent Mathematics Project

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kent Mathematics Project"}

*Image: Kent\_Mathematics\_Project,\_Test\_Book,\_Level\_1.jpg.* Front cover of K.M.P. Test Book level 1.

The **Kent Mathematics Project** (K.M.P.) was an educational system for teaching mathematics to 9-16 year olds. The system comprised task worksheets, booklets, audio [compact cassettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_cassette) and tests. Through the 1970s and 1980s, it was widely adopted in [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent) schools, as well as being exported internationally.[^o2]

:::

The system was based on the idea that:

mathematical learning should be in terms of levels of concept development and that chronological age should not be the basis of course design.[^o3]

K.M.P. providing materials and structure for non-specialist teachers to teach math classes while sharing the effort required in producing suitable teaching material.

## History {#History}

Started in 1966, the project originated at Ridgewaye School, [Southborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southborough,_Kent), [Tunbridge Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunbridge_Wells) which closed its doors in 1991. K.M.P went through several titles including "An Auto-Instructional Course in Mathematics" and "The Ridgewaye Individualized Course". The system was inspired by the self-directed learning of the [Dalton Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_Plan) while attempting to avoid its pitfalls.[^o5] K.M.P was gradually extended over time, involving trials at a number of schools before being more widely distributed.[^o6] K.M.P. was adopted by the education authority in 1970[^o7] and used in over seventy schools around Kent.[^o13]

In 1988, the project's director objected to the [National Curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Curriculum_%28England,_Wales_and_Northern_Ireland%29) which emphasised goals to be achieved by particular ages.[^o8]

## Usage {#Usage}

To teach a specific concept, the teacher selected a set of twelve tasks called a "matrix" from a material bank for the pupil to complete. The teacher was expected to be available to mentor pupils if they encountered difficulty. The tasks were completed in any order, then self-corrected by the pupil and checked by the teacher. When the entire task matrix is completed, the pupil performed a test to check their understanding. The tests results were then used by the teacher to construct the next task matrix.[^o7] This allowed children to progress at their own rate, either ahead[^o10] or behind the rest of the class, while allowing the teacher to customize the course to the pupil's needs. Tasks were assigned a level of 1 to 9, depending on their difficulty. The difficulty of tasks assigned was based on the child's ability, rather than their age.[^o11]

KMP materials were published by [Ward Lock & Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Lock_&_Co).

## Impact {#Impact}

Ideas from K.M.P. were adopted in later teaching tools, including Graded Assessment in Mathematics (GAIM) and Secondary Mathematics Individualised Learning Experiment (SMILE).[^o13]
