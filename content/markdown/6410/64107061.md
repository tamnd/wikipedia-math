# Conway circle theorem

*Image: Conway circle theorem.svg.* A [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654)'s [Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) with its six [concentric](wikipedia://en/19049087) points (solid black), the triangle's [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle_and_excircles_of_a_triangle) (dashed gray), and the centre of both circles (white); solid and dashed [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) of the same colour are equal in length

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conway circle theorem"}

In [plane geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **Conway circle theorem** states that when the sides meeting at each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) are extended by the length of the opposite side, the six endpoints of the three resulting [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) lie on a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) whose centre is the [incentre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incentre) of the triangle. The circle on which these six points lie is called the **Conway circle** of the triangle.[^o2][^o4] The theorem and circle are named after mathematician [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807).

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

*Image: Conway circle theorem proof.svg.* segments of equal color are of equal length $\begin{aligned}\triangle IF_cP_a &\cong \triangle IF_cQ_b \cong \triangle IF_aP_b \\ &\cong \triangle IF_aQ_c \cong \triangle IF_bP_c \\ &\cong \triangle IF_bQ_a \\ \Rightarrow \, |IP_a|&=|IQ_a|=|IP_b|=|IQ_b|\\ &=|IP_c|=|IQ_c| \end{aligned}$

Let *I* be the center of the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) of triangle *ABC*, *r* its radius and *F<sub>a</sub>*, *F<sub>b</sub>* and *F<sub>c</sub>* the three points where the incircle touches the triangle sides *a*, *b* and *c*. Since the (extended) triangle sides are [tangents](wikipedia://en/19491492) of the incircle it follows that *IF<sub>a</sub>*, *IF<sub>b</sub>* and *IF<sub>c</sub>* are perpendicular to *a*, *b* and *c*. Furthermore, the following equalities for line segments hold. |AF<sub>c</sub>|=|AF<sub>b</sub>|, |BF<sub>c</sub>|=|BF<sub>a</sub>|, |CF<sub>a</sub>|=|CF<sub>b</sub>|. With that the six triangles *IF<sub>c</sub>P<sub>a</sub>*, *IF<sub>c</sub>Q<sub>b</sub>*, *IF<sub>a</sub>P<sub>b</sub>*, *IF<sub>a</sub>Q<sub>c</sub>*, *IF<sub>b</sub>Q<sub>a</sub>* and *IF<sub>b</sub>P<sub>c</sub>* all have a side of length |*AF<sub>c</sub>*|+|*BF<sub>c</sub>*|+|*CF<sub>a</sub>*| and a side of length *r* with a right angle between them. This means that due [SAS congruence theorem for triangles](wikipedia://en/39330#Congruence_of_triangles) all six triangles are congruent, which yields |*IP<sub>a</sub>*|=|*IQ<sub>a</sub>*|=|*IP<sub>b</sub>*|=|*IQ<sub>b</sub>*|=|*IP<sub>c</sub>*|=|*IQ<sub>c</sub>*|. So the six points *P<sub>a</sub>*, *Q<sub>a</sub>*, *P<sub>b</sub>*, *Q<sub>b</sub>*, *P<sub>c</sub>* and *Q<sub>c</sub>* have all the same distance from the triangle incenter *I*, that is they lie on a common circle with center *I*.

:::

## Additional properties {#Additional_properties}

The radius of the Conway circle is

$$\sqrt{r^2 + s^2}=\sqrt{\frac{a^2b+ab^2+b^2c+bc^2+a^2c+ac^2+abc}{a+b+c}}$$

where $r$ and $s$ are the inradius and semiperimeter of the triangle.[^o4]

## Generalisation {#Generalisation}

*Image: Side divider theorem2a.svg.* Conway's circle theorem as a special case of the generalisation, called "side divider theorem" (Villiers) or "windscreen wiper theorem" (Polster))

Conway's circle is a special case of a more general circle for a triangle that can be obtained as follows: Given any △ABC with an arbitrary point P on line AB. Construct BQ = BP, CR = CQ, AS = AR, BT = BS, CU = CT. Then AU = AP, and PQRSTU is cyclic.[^o9]

If you place P on the extended triangle side AB such that BP=b and BP being completely outside the triangle then the above constructions yield Conway's circle theorem.
