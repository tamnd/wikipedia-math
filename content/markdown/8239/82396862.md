# Tryphon T. Georgiou

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tryphon T. Georgiou"}

**Tryphon Thomas Georgiou** (, Τρύφων Θωμάς Γεωργίου ; born 18 October 1956) is a Greek and American [electrical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineer) and [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969). He has made contributions to [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). Since 2016, he has been a distinguished professor of mechanical and aerospace engineering at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine) (UCI).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Georgiou was born in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens) in 1956. He earned a diploma in Mechanical and Electrical Engineering from the [National Technical University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technical_University_of_Athens) in 1979 and a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in electrical engineering from the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida) in 1983. He was advised by [Rudolf E. Kálmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_E._Kálmán) and wrote a dissertation on [covariance](wikipedia://en/157059) sequences in [stochastic control](wikipedia://en/17058216).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

After completing his doctorate, Georgiou joined the faculty of [Florida Atlantic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_University) in 1983 and [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) in 1986. He joined the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) in 1989, where he was professor for nearly three decades and director of the Control Science and Dynamical Systems Center beginning in 1990.[^o6] He also served as Vincentine Hermes-Luh Chair in Electrical and Computer Engineering from 2002 to 2016.[^o7]

His research specializes in control theory, with an emphasis on stochastic and [robust control](wikipedia://en/3282143). He made contributions to the development of the [gap metric](wikipedia://en/80262847)[^o8][^o9] for measuring distances between [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) and analyzing feedback robustness, including extensions to nonlinear systems. In 1997, he developed the [large-gain theorem](wikipedia://en/16696745), providing quantitative conditions for stability in both linear and nonlinear settings, and worked on the [separation principle in stochastic control](wikipedia://en/58383744).[^o10][^o11] Along with [Malcolm C. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_C._Smith), [Christopher I. Byrnes](wikipedia://en/82397501), and [Anders Lindquist](wikipedia://en/34505584), Georgiou also works on stochastic control problems, including state [covariance steering](wikipedia://en/2007748) and [Linear-quadratic-Gaussian control](wikipedia://en/5347179). He has also made contributions to network models for disease spread in [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997),[^o12][^o13] market fragility in financial systems,[^o14] and neural connectivity in the brain.[^o15][^o16]

In 2016, he became a distinguished professor at the [Samueli School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Irvine_Samueli_School_of_Engineering) at UC Irvine.[^o6] He has been on the board of governors for the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) since 2002.[^o6] He is a fellow of the [International Federation of Automatic Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Automatic_Control) (IFAC) since 2016 and the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) since 2021.[^o6]
