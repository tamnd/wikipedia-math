# Michael Hoskin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Hoskin"}

**Michael Anthony Hoskin** (27 February 1930 – 5 December 2021) was a British [pure mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_mathematician), [historian of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_science), and astronomer who served as the head of the [Department of History and Philosophy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_History_and_Philosophy_of_Science,_University_of_Cambridge) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) from 1975 to 1986 and the president of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge), from 1981 to 1991. He was the founder of the *[Journal for the History of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_for_the_History_of_Astronomy)* and was its longtime editor-in-chief. He was also a leading authority on the [Herschel family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschel_family).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hoskin was born in London on 27 February 1930.[^o7] His father was a tax official and his mother was a schoolteacher.[^o6] He was educated in the [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics), then graduated from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) in 1952 with [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) (BSc) and [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) (MSc) degrees in [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210). He then earned his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (PhD) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) in 1956 from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he was a postgraduate student at [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge).[^o7] At Cambridge, Hoskins's fellow students included mathematicians [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193).[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

After receiving his doctorate, Hoskin was elected a [research fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_fellowship) of [Jesus College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge), in 1956.[^o11] He then was a lecturer in the history of science at the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester) from 1957 to 1959, before moving to the University of Cambridge, where he served as a university lecturer in the history of science from 1959 to 1988. During this period, he also held senior collegiate roles, including fellow, vice-master, and senior tutor of [St Edmund’s House, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Edmund's_House,_Cambridge), from 1965 to 1969, and from 1969 onward was a fellow of [Churchill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge), where he oversaw the construction of the Archives Centre as librarian and later served as president from 1981 to 1991. From 1975 to 1986, he was head of the [Department of History and Philosophy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_History_and_Philosophy_of_Science,_University_of_Cambridge) at Cambridge.[^o6]

Hoskin delivered an invited discourse at the International Astronomical Union General Assembly in 1982, had the minor planet [12223 Hoskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12223_Hoskin) named in his honor in 2001, and became an emeritus fellow of St Edmund’s College, Cambridge, in 2002. He was subsequently awarded major prizes and honors, including the Leroy E. Doggett Prize in 2004, honorary fellowship of the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society) in 2007, the Jaschek Medal in 2008, Spain’s [Gold Medal of Merit in the Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Medal_of_Merit_in_the_Fine_Arts) in 2015, the Menga Medal in 2017, and the Agnes Mary Clerke Medal in 2020, alongside numerous honorary memberships and commemorations, particularly in Spain.[^o6]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hoskin married his wife, Jean Margaret Small, in 1956. They had five children.[^o11] He died on 5 December 2021 at his home in Cambridge.[^o12]
