# Central line (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Central line"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **central lines** are certain special [straight lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) that lie in the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). The special property that distinguishes a straight line as a central line is manifested via the equation of the line in [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790). This special property is related to the concept of [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) also. The concept of a central line was introduced by [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421) in a paper published in 1994.[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\triangle ABC$ be a plane triangle and let $x : y : z$ be the [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of an arbitrary point in the plane of triangle $\triangle ABC$.

A straight line in the plane of $\triangle ABC$ whose equation in trilinear coordinates has the form

$$f(a,b,c)\,x + g(a,b,c)\,y + h(a,b,c)\,z = 0$$

where the point with trilinear coordinates

$$f(a,b,c) : g(a,b,c) : h(a,b,c)$$

is a triangle center, is a central line in the plane of $\triangle ABC$ relative to $\triangle ABC$.[^o3][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Central lines as trilinear polars {#Central_lines_as_trilinear_polars}

The geometric relation between a central line and its associated triangle center can be expressed using the concepts of trilinear polars and [isogonal conjugates](wikipedia://en/2083100).

Let $X = u(a,b,c) : v(a,b,c) : w(a,b,c)$ be a triangle center. The line whose equation is

$$\frac{x}{u (a,b,c)} + \frac{y}{v(a,b,c)} + \frac{z}{w(a,b,c)} = 0$$

is the *[trilinear polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilinear_polar)* of the triangle center $X$.[^o3][^o9] Also the point

$$Y = \frac{1}{u(a,b,c)} : \frac{1}{v(a,b,c)} : \frac{1}{w(a,b,c)}$$

is the [isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) of the triangle center $X$.

Thus the central line given by the equation

$$f(a,b,c)\,x + g(a,b,c)\,y + h(a,b,c)\,z = 0$$

is the trilinear polar of the isogonal conjugate of the triangle center $f(a,b,c) : g(a,b,c) : h(a,b,c).$

The associated triangle center is known as the *crossdifference* of any two points on the central line.[^o7]

## Construction of central lines {#Construction_of_central_lines}

*Image: Construction of central lines.svg.*

Let $X$ be any triangle center of $\triangle ABC$.

- Draw the lines $AX, BX, CX$ and their reflections in the [internal bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_bisector) of the angles at the vertices $A, B, C$ respectively.
- The reflected lines are concurrent and the point of concurrence is the isogonal conjugate $Y$ of $X$.
- Let the [cevians](wikipedia://en/6135790) $AY, BY, CY$ meet the opposite sidelines of $\triangle ABC$ at $A', B', C'$ respectively. The triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ is the cevian triangle of $Y$.
- The $\triangle ABC$ and the [cevian triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cevian_triangle) $\triangle A'B'C'$ are in perspective and let $DEF$ be the [axis of perspectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_perspectivity) of the two triangles. The line $DEF$ is the trilinear polar of the point $Y$. $DEF$ is the central line associated with the triangle center $X$.

## Some named central lines {#Some_named_central_lines}

Let $X_{n}$ be the $n$th triangle center in [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593). The central line associated with $X_{n}$ is denoted by $L_{n}$. Some of the named central lines are given below.

*Image: Antiorthic Axis.svg.* Antiorthic axis as the axis of perspectivity of $\triangle ABC$ and its excentral triangle.

### Central line associated with *X*<sub>1</sub>, the incenter: Antiorthic axis {#Central_line_associated_with_X1,_the_incenter:_Antiorthic_axis}

The central line associated with the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) $X_{1} = 1 : 1 : 1$ (also denoted by $I$) is

$$x + y + z = 0.$$

This line is the *antiorthic axis* of $\triangle ABC$.[^o13]

- The isogonal conjugate of the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) of $\triangle ABC$ is the incenter itself. So the antiorthic axis, which is the central line associated with the incenter, is the axis of perspectivity of $\triangle ABC$ and its [incentral triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incentral_triangle) (the cevian triangle of the incenter of $\triangle ABC$).
- The antiorthic axis of $\triangle ABC$ is the axis of [perspectivity](wikipedia://en/14909067) of $\triangle ABC$ and the [excentral triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excentral_triangle) $\triangle I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}$ of $\triangle ABC$.[^o14]
- The triangle whose sidelines are externally tangent to the [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) of $\triangle ABC$ is the *extangents triangle* of $\triangle ABC$. $\triangle ABC$ and its extangents triangle are in perspective and the axis of perspectivity is the antiorthic axis of $\triangle ABC$.

*Image: Lemoine Axis.svg.*

::: {#o16 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Lemoine line"}

### Central line associated with *X*<sub>2</sub>, the centroid: Lemoine axis {#Central_line_associated_with_X2,_the_centroid:_Lemoine_axis}

The trilinear coordinates of the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) $X_{2}$ (also denoted by $G$) of $\triangle ABC$ are:

$$\frac{1}{a} : \frac{1}{b} : \frac{1}{c}$$

So the central line associated with the centroid is the line whose trilinear equation is

$$\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} + \frac{z}{c} = 0.$$

This line is the *Lemoine axis*, also called the *Lemoine line*, of $\triangle ABC$.

- The isogonal conjugate of the centroid $X_{2}$ is the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) $X_{6}$ (also denoted by $K$) having trilinear coordinates $a : b : c$. So the Lemoine axis of $\triangle ABC$ is the trilinear polar of the symmedian point of $\triangle ABC$.
- The [tangential triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_triangle) of $\triangle ABC$ is the triangle $\triangle T_{A}T_{B}T_{C}$ formed by the tangents to the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ at its vertices. $\triangle ABC$ and its tangential triangle are in perspective and the axis of perspectivity is the Lemoine axis of $\triangle ABC$.

:::

### Central line associated with *X*<sub>3</sub>, the circumcenter: Orthic axis {#Central_line_associated_with_X3,_the_circumcenter:_Orthic_axis}

*Image: Orthic Axis.svg.*

The trilinear coordinates of the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) $X_{3}$ (also denoted by $O$) of $\triangle ABC$ are:

$$\cos A : \cos B : \cos C$$

So the central line associated with the circumcenter is the line whose trilinear equation is

$$x \cos A + y \cos B + z \cos C = 0.$$

This line is the *orthic axis* of $\triangle ABC$.[^o18]

- The isogonal conjugate of the circumcenter $X_{3}$ is the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) $X_{4}$ (also denoted by $H$) having trilinear coordinates $\sec A : \sec B : \sec C$. So the orthic axis of $\triangle ABC$ is the trilinear polar of the orthocenter of $\triangle ABC$. The orthic axis of $\triangle ABC$ is the axis of perspectivity of $\triangle ABC$ and its orthic triangle $\triangle H_{A}H_{B}H_{C}$. It is also the radical axis of the triangle's circumcircle and nine-point-circle.

### Central line associated with *X*<sub>4</sub>, the orthocenter {#Central_line_associated_with_X4,_the_orthocenter}

*Image: Central line of orhocenter.svg.*

The trilinear coordinates of the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) $X_{4}$ (also denoted by $H$) of $\triangle ABC$ are:

$$\sec A : \sec B : \sec C$$

So the central line associated with the circumcenter is the line whose trilinear equation is

$$x \sec A + y \sec B + z \sec C = 0.$$

- The isogonal conjugate of the orthocenter of a triangle is the circumcenter of the triangle. So the central line associated with the orthocenter is the trilinear polar of the circumcenter.

### Central line associated with *X*<sub>5</sub>, the nine-point center {#Central_line_associated_with_X5,_the_nine-point_center}

*Image: Kosnita point.svg.*

The trilinear coordinates of the [nine-point center](wikipedia://en/4517570) $X_{5}$ (also denoted by $N$) of $\triangle ABC$ are:[^o21]

$$\cos(B-C) : \cos(C-A) : \cos(A-B).$$

So the central line associated with the nine-point center is the line whose trilinear equation is

$$x \cos(B-C) + y \cos(C-A) + z \cos(A-B) = 0.$$

- The isogonal conjugate of the nine-point center of $\triangle ABC$ is the *Kosnita point* $X_{54}$ of $\triangle ABC$.[^o22][^o23] So the central line associated with the nine-point center is the trilinear polar of the Kosnita point.
- The Kosnita point is constructed as follows. Let $O$ be the circumcenter of $\triangle ABC$. Let $O_{A}, O_{B}, O_{C}$ be the circumcenters of the triangles $\triangle BOC, \triangle COA, \triangle AOB$ respectively. The lines $AO_{A}, BO_{B}, CO_{C}$ are concurrent and the point of concurrence is the Kosnita point of $\triangle ABC$. The name is due to J Rigby.[^o24]

### Central line associated with *X*<sub>6</sub>, the symmedian point : Line at infinity {#Central_line_associated_with_X6,_the_symmedian_point_:_Line_at_infinity}

*Image: Line at infinity.svg.*

The trilinear coordinates of the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) $X_{6}$ (also denoted by $K$) of $\triangle ABC$ are:

$$a : b : c$$

So the central line associated with the symmedian point is the line whose trilinear equation is

$$ax + by + cz = 0.$$

- This line is the line at infinity in the plane of $\triangle ABC$.
- The isogonal conjugate of the symmedian point of $\triangle ABC$ is the centroid of $\triangle ABC$. Hence the central line associated with the symmedian point is the trilinear polar of the centroid. This is the axis of perspectivity of the $\triangle ABC$ and its [medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743).

## Some more named central lines {#Some_more_named_central_lines}

### Euler line {#Euler_line}

The *[Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622)* of $\triangle ABC$ is the line passing through the centroid, the circumcenter, the orthocenter and the nine-point center of $\triangle ABC$. The trilinear equation of the Euler line is

$$x \sin 2A \sin(B-C) + y \sin 2B \sin(C-A) + z \sin 2C \sin(A-B) = 0.$$

This is the central line associated with the triangle center $X_{647}$.

### Nagel line {#Nagel_line}

The *Nagel line* of $\triangle ABC$ is the line passing through the centroid, the incenter, the [Spieker center](wikipedia://en/35728942) and the [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) of $\triangle ABC$. The trilinear equation of the Nagel line is

$$xa(b-c) + yb(c-a) + zc(a-b) = 0.$$

This is the central line associated with the triangle center $X_{649}$.

::: {#o30 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Brocard axis"}

### Brocard axis {#Brocard_axis}

The *Brocard axis* of $\triangle ABC$ is the line through the circumcenter and the symmedian point of $\triangle ABC$. Its trilinear equation is

$$x \sin(B-C) + y \sin(C-A) + z \sin(A-B) = 0.$$

This is the central line associated with the triangle center $X_{523}$.

:::

### Gergonne line {#Gergonne_line}

The *Gergonne line* of $\triangle ABC$ is the trilinear polar of the [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point). It is perpendicular to the [Soddy line](wikipedia://en/72046251) of $\triangle ABC$. Its trilinear equation is

$$x a(s-a) + y b(s-b) + z c(s-c) = 0,$$

where *s* is the [semiperimeter](wikipedia://en/886617) of $\triangle ABC$. This is the central line associated with the triangle center $X_{55}$.
