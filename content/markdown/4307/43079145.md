# Tensor product of representations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tensor product of representations"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **tensor product of representations** is a [tensor product of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_vector_spaces) underlying [representations](wikipedia://en/19378200) together with the factor-wise [group action](wikipedia://en/12781) on the product. This construction, together with the Clebsch–Gordan procedure, can be used to generate additional [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) if one already knows a few.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Group representations {#Group_representations}

If $V_1, V_2$ are [linear representations of a group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_a_group) $G$, then their tensor product is the tensor product of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) $V_1 \otimes V_2$ with the linear action of $G$ uniquely determined by the condition that

$g \cdot (v_1 \otimes v_2) = (g\cdot v_1) \otimes (g\cdot v_2)$[^n1][^n2]

for all $v_1\in V_1$ and $v_2\in V_2$. Although not every element of $V_1 \otimes V_2$ is expressible in the form $v_1\otimes v_2$, the [universal property of the tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844#Universal_property) guarantees that this action is well-defined.

In the language of [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396), if the actions of $G$ on $V_1$ and $V_2$ are given by homomorphisms $\Pi_1: G\to\operatorname{GL}(V_1)$ and $\Pi_2: G\to\operatorname{GL}(V_2)$, then the tensor product representation is given by the homomorphism $\Pi_1\otimes\Pi_2: G\to\operatorname{GL}(V_1 \otimes V_2)$ given by

$$\Pi_1\otimes\Pi_2(g) = \Pi_1(g)\otimes\Pi_2(g),$$

where $\Pi_1(g)\otimes\Pi_2(g)$ is the [tensor product of linear maps](wikipedia://en/30844#Tensor_product_of_linear_maps).[^o7]

One can extend the notion of tensor products to any finite number of representations. If *V* is a linear representation of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G*, then with the above linear action, the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) $T(V)$ is an [algebraic representation](wikipedia://en/56608952) of *G*; i.e., each element of *G* acts as an [algebra automorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_automorphism).

### Lie algebra representations {#Lie_algebra_representations}

If $(V_1,\pi_1)$ and $(V_2,\pi_2)$ are [representations](wikipedia://en/318712) of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak g$, then the tensor product of these representations is the map $\pi_1\otimes\pi_2:\mathfrak g\to\operatorname{End}(V_1 \otimes V_2)$ given by[^o9]

$$\pi_1\otimes\pi_2(X)=\pi_1(X)\otimes I+I\otimes\pi_2(X),$$

where $I$ is the [identity](wikipedia://en/15069) [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569). This is called the Kronecker sum, defined in [Matrix addition#Kronecker sum](wikipedia://en/125276#Kronecker_sum) and [Kronecker product#Properties](wikipedia://en/712430#Properties). The motivation for the use of the Kronecker sum in this definition comes from the case in which $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ come from [representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group_representation) $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$. In that case, a simple computation shows that the Lie algebra representation associated to $\Pi_1\otimes\Pi_2$ is given by the preceding formula.[^o10]

### Quantum groups {#Quantum_groups}

For [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups), the [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) is no longer co-commutative. As a result, the natural permutation map $V \otimes W \rightarrow W \otimes V$ is no longer an isomorphism of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410). However, the permutation map remains an isomorphism of vector spaces.

:::

## Action on linear maps {#Action_on_linear_maps}

If $(V_1, \Pi_1)$ and $(V_2, \Pi_2)$ are representations of a group $G$, let $\operatorname{Hom}(V_1,V_2)$ denote the [space](wikipedia://en/32370) of all [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) from $V_1$ to $V_2$. Then $\operatorname{Hom}(V_1,V_2)$ can be given the structure of a representation by defining

$$g\cdot A=\Pi_2(g)A\Pi_1(g)^{-1}$$

for all $A\in\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$. Now, there is [a natural isomorphism](wikipedia://en/30844#Adjoint_representation)

$$\operatorname{Hom}(V, W)\cong V^* \otimes W$$

as vector spaces;[^n2] this vector space [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) is in fact an isomorphism of representations.[^o14]

The [trivial subrepresentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_subrepresentation) $\operatorname{Hom}(V, W)^G$ consists of [*G*-linear maps](wikipedia://en/422711); i.e.,

$$\operatorname{Hom}_G(V, W) = \operatorname{Hom}(V, W)^G.$$

Let $E = \operatorname{End}(V)$ denote the [endomorphism algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endomorphism_algebra) of *V* and let *A* denote the subalgebra of $E^{\otimes m}$ consisting of symmetric tensors. The [main theorem of invariant theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_theorem_of_invariant_theory) states that *A* is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/3876151) when the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of the base [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is zero.

## Clebsch–Gordan theory {#Clebsch–Gordan_theory}

### The general problem {#The_general_problem}

The tensor product of two irreducible representations $V_1,V_2$ of a group or Lie algebra is usually not irreducible. It is therefore of interest to attempt to decompose $V_1\otimes V_2$ into irreducible pieces. This decomposition problem is known as the Clebsch–Gordan problem.

### The SU(2) case {#The_SU(2)_case}

The [prototypical example](wikipedia://en/1074990) of this problem is the case of the [rotation group SO(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_group_SO%283%29)—or its double cover, the [special unitary group SU(2)](wikipedia://en/173997#The_group_SU%282%29). The irreducible representations of SU(2) are described by a parameter $\ell$, whose possible values are

$$\ell=0, 1/2, 1, 3/2, \ldots.$$

(The dimension of the representation is then $2\ell+1$.) Let us take two parameters $\ell$ and $m$ with $\ell\geq m$. Then the tensor product representation $V_\ell\otimes V_m$ decomposes as follows:[^o18]

$$V_\ell\otimes V_m\cong V_{\ell+m}\oplus V_{\ell+m-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{\ell-m+1}\oplus V_{\ell-m}.$$

Consider, as an example, the tensor product of the four-dimensional representation $V_{3/2}$ and the three-dimensional representation $V_1$. The tensor product representation $V_{3/2}\otimes V_1$ has dimension 12 and decomposes as

$$V_{3/2}\otimes V_1\cong V_{5/2}\oplus V_{3/2}\oplus V_{1/2},$$

where the representations on the right-hand side have dimension 6, 4, and 2, respectively. We may summarize this result arithmetically as $4 \times 3 = 6+4+2$.

### The SU(3) case {#The_SU(3)_case}

In the case of the group SU(3), all [the irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/318712#The_case_of_sl%283,C%29) can be generated from the standard 3-dimensional representation and its dual, as follows. To generate the representation with label $(m_1,m_2)$, one takes the tensor product of $m_1$ copies of the standard representation and $m_2$ copies of the dual of the standard representation, and then takes the [invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826) generated by the tensor product of the highest weight vectors.[^o20]

In contrast to the situation for SU(2), in the Clebsch–Gordan decomposition for SU(3), a given irreducible representation $W$ may occur more than once in the decomposition of $U\otimes V$.

## Tensor power {#Tensor_power}

As with vector spaces, one can define the **$k$<sup>th</sup> [tensor power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_power) of a representation** $V$ to be the vector space $V^{\otimes k}$ with the action given above.

### The symmetric and alternating square {#The_symmetric_and_alternating_square}

Over a field of characteristic zero, the symmetric and alternating squares are [subrepresentations](wikipedia://en/55943952) of the second tensor power. They can be used to define the [Frobenius–Schur indicator](wikipedia://en/882686), which indicates whether a given [irreducible character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_character) is [real](wikipedia://en/695101), [complex](wikipedia://en/695060), or [quaternionic](wikipedia://en/695046). They are examples of [Schur functors](wikipedia://en/11597641). They are defined as follows.

Let $V$ be a vector space. Define an endomorphism $T$ of $V \otimes V$ as follows:

$\begin{aligned} T: V \otimes V &\longrightarrow V \otimes V \\ v \otimes w &\longmapsto w \otimes v. \end{aligned}$[^o23]

It is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) (its own inverse), and so is an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of $V \otimes V$.

Define two subsets of the second [tensor power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_power) of $V$,

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Sym}^2(V) &:= \{v \in V \otimes V \mid T(v)=v \} \\ \operatorname{Alt}^2(V) &:= \{v \in V \otimes V \mid T(v)=-v \} \end{aligned}$$

These are the **symmetric square of $V$**, $V \odot V$, and the **alternating square of $V$**, $V\wedge V$, respectively.[^n3] The symmetric and alternating squares are also known as the **symmetric part** and **antisymmetric part** of the tensor product.[^n4]

::: {#o27 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

The second [tensor power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_power) of a linear representation $V$ of a group $G$ decomposes as the direct sum of the symmetric and alternating squares:

$$V^{\otimes 2} = V \otimes V \cong \operatorname{Sym}^2(V) \oplus \operatorname{Alt}^2(V)$$

as representations. In particular, both are [subrepresentations](wikipedia://en/55943952) of the second tensor power. In the language of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223), the symmetric and alternating squares are $\mathbb{C}[G]$-[submodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of $V \otimes V$.[^n5]

If $V$ has a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) $\{v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\}$, then the symmetric square has a basis $\{v_i\otimes v_j+v_j\otimes v_i\mid 1\leq i\leq j\leq n\}$ and the alternating square has a basis $\{v_i\otimes v_j-v_j\otimes v_i\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}$. Accordingly,

$\begin{aligned} \dim\operatorname{Sym}^2(V) &= \frac{\dim V(\dim V + 1)}{2}, \\ \dim\operatorname{Alt}^2(V) &= \frac{\dim V(\dim V - 1)}{2}. \end{aligned}$[^n6][^n3]

Let $\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}$ be the [character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_%28representation_theory%29) of $V$. Then we can calculate the characters of the symmetric and alternating squares as follows: for all $g$ in $G$,

$\begin{aligned} \chi_{\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)}(g) &= \frac{1}{2}(\chi(g)^2+\chi(g^2)), \\ \chi_{\operatorname{Alt}^2(V)}(g) &= \frac{1}{2}(\chi(g)^2-\chi(g^2)). \end{aligned}$[^n7]

:::

### The symmetric and exterior powers {#The_symmetric_and_exterior_powers}

As in [multilinear algebra](wikipedia://en/243946), over a field of characteristic zero, one can more generally define the $k$<sup>th</sup> [symmetric power](wikipedia://en/57047243) $\operatorname{Sym}^k(V)$ and $k$<sup>th</sup> [exterior power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power) $\Lambda^k(V)$, which are [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) of the $k$<sup>th</sup> tensor power (see those pages for more detail on this construction). They are also subrepresentations, but higher tensor powers no longer decompose as their direct sum.

The [Schur–Weyl duality](wikipedia://en/15291970) computes the irreducible representations occurring in tensor powers of representations of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $G = \operatorname{GL}(V)$. Precisely, as an $S_n \times G$-module

$$V^{\otimes n} \simeq \bigoplus_\lambda M_{\lambda} \otimes S^{\lambda}(V)$$

where

- $M_{\lambda}$ is an irreducible representation of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $\mathrm{S}_n$ corresponding to a partition $\lambda$ of *n* (in decreasing order),
- $S^{\lambda}(V)$ is the image of the [Young symmetrizer](wikipedia://en/1585226) $c_{\lambda}: V^{\otimes n} \to V^{\otimes n}$.

The mapping $V \mapsto S^{\lambda}(V)$ is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) called the [Schur functor](wikipedia://en/11597641). It generalizes the constructions of symmetric and exterior powers:

$$S^{(n)}(V) = \operatorname{Sym}^n V, \,\, S^{(1, 1, \dots, 1)}(V) = \wedge^n V.$$

In particular, as a *G*-module, the above simplifies to

$$V^{\otimes n} \simeq \bigoplus_{\lambda} S^{\lambda}(V)^{\oplus m_{\lambda}}$$

where $m_\lambda = \dim M_\lambda$. Moreover, the multiplicity $m_{\lambda}$ may be computed by the [Frobenius formula](wikipedia://en/55887304) (or the [hook length formula](wikipedia://en/41497810)). For example, take $n = 3$. Then there are exactly three partitions: $3 = 3 = 2 + 1 = 1 + 1 + 1$ and, as it turns out, $m_{(3)} = m_{(1, 1, 1)} = 1, \, m_{(2, 1)} = 2$. Hence,

$$V^{\otimes 3} \simeq \operatorname{Sym}^3 V \bigoplus \wedge^3 V \bigoplus S^{(2, 1)}(V)^{\oplus 2}.$$

## Tensor products involving Schur functors {#Tensor_products_involving_Schur_functors}

Let $S^{\lambda}$ denote the [Schur functor](wikipedia://en/11597641) defined according to a partition $\lambda$. Then there is the following decomposition:[^o34]

$$S^{\lambda} V \otimes S^{\mu} V \simeq \bigoplus_{\nu} (S^{\nu} V)^{\oplus N_{\lambda \mu \nu}}$$

where the multiplicities $N_{\lambda \mu \nu}$ are given by the [Littlewood–Richardson rule](wikipedia://en/16004359).

Given finite-dimensional vector spaces *V*, *W*, the [Schur functors](wikipedia://en/11597641) *S*<sup>λ</sup> give the decomposition

$$\operatorname{Sym}(W^* \otimes V) \simeq \bigoplus_{\lambda} S^{\lambda}(W^*) \otimes S^{\lambda}(V)$$

The left-hand side can be identified with the [ring of polynomial functions](wikipedia://en/38608533) on Hom(*V*, *W*), *k*\[Hom(*V*, *W*)\] = *k*\[*V*<sup>\*</sup> ⊗ *W*\], and so the above also gives the decomposition of *k*\[Hom(*V*, *W*)\].

## Tensor products representations as representations of product groups {#Tensor_products_representations_as_representations_of_product_groups}

Let *G*, *H* be two groups and let $(\pi,V)$ and $(\rho,W)$ be representations of *G* and *H*, respectively. Then we can let the [direct product group](wikipedia://en/3706015) $G \times H$ act on the tensor product space $V \otimes W$ by the formula

$$(g, h) \cdot (v \otimes w) = \pi(g) v \otimes \rho(h) w.$$

Even if $G=H$, we can still perform this construction, so that the tensor product of two representations of $G$ could, alternatively, be viewed as a representation of $G \times G$ rather than a representation of $G$. It is therefore important to clarify whether the tensor product of two representations of $G$ is being viewed as a representation of $G$ or as a representation of $G \times G$.

In contrast to the Clebsch–Gordan problem discussed above, the tensor product of two irreducible representations of $G$ is irreducible when viewed as a representation of the product group $G \times G$.
