# Stephanie B. Alexander

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stephanie B. Alexander"}

*Image: Stephanie Alexander.jpg.* Stephanie Alexander at [Oberwolfach.](wikipedia://en/1368669)

**Stephanie Brewster Brewer Taylor Alexander** (September 1, 1941 – November 20, 2023)[^o2] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of mathematics at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign).[^o3] Her research concerned [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Alexander was born in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles) and raised in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver), British Columbia, and [London, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Ontario). She was a graduate of [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College).[^o8]

She earned her Ph.D. from UIUC in 1967, under the supervision of [Richard L. Bishop](wikipedia://en/43076291), with a thesis entitled *Reducibility of Euclidean Immersions of Low Codimensions*. After joining the UIUC faculty as a half-time instructor, she became a regular faculty member in 1972.[^o4] She retired in 2009[^o7] and died in 2023.[^o8]

## Mathematical Work {#Mathematical_Work}

Alexander's most significant achievements were in relation to the study of metric spaces with curvature bounds. Of particular importance was her work with Bishop to establish a concept of curvature bounds in the style of Alexandrov geometry for [semi-Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/287555) and [Lorentzian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifolds), an early step towards the development of [synthetic geometry](wikipedia://en/267484) in a Lorentzian setting.[^o10]

## Books {#Books}

- With Vitali Kapovitch and Anton Petrunin, Alexander authored the book *An Invitation to Alexandrov Geometry: CAT(0) Spaces* (Springer, 2019).
- S. Alexander, V. Kapovitch, A. Petrunin. Alexandrov geometry—foundations. — AMS, 2024. — (Grad. Stud. Math. 236).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

- At Illinois, Alexander won the Luckman Distinguished Undergraduate Teaching Award and the William Prokasy Award for Excellence in Undergraduate Teaching in 1993.[^o7]
- In 2014 she was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to geometry, for high-quality exposition, and for exceptional teaching of mathematics."[^o13]
