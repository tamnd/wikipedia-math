# Richard L. Bishop

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Richard L. Bishop"}

**Richard Lawrence Bishop** (August 12, 1931 – December 18, 2019) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and taught at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign).

:::

Bishop went to [Case Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Institute_of_Technology) as an undergraduate, earning a B.S. in 1954. Next he earned his Ph.D. from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1959, and immediately joined the UIUC faculty,[^o4] where he stayed until his retirement in 1997.[^o2] His thesis, *On Imbeddings and Holonomy*, was supervised by [Isadore Singer](wikipedia://en/324564). At UIUC, his doctoral students included future UIUC colleague [Stephanie B. Alexander](wikipedia://en/43075724).[^o5] He is the author of *Geometry of Manifolds* (with Richard J. Crittenden, AMS Chelsea Publishing, 1964,[^o6] translated into Russian 1967 and reprinted 2001[^o8]) and *Tensor Analysis on Manifolds* (with Samuel I. Goldberg, Macmillan, 1968,[^o9] reprinted by Dover Books on Mathematics, 1980).

In 2013, Bishop became one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11]

The [Bishop–Gromov inequality](wikipedia://en/929870) in [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), one form of which appeared in his book with Crittenden, is named after him and [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Leonidovich_Gromov), who gave an improved formulation of Bishop's result. He introduced the "Bishop frame" of curves in Euclidean space, an alternative to the better-known [Frenet frame](wikipedia://en/666987). With [Barrett O'Neill](wikipedia://en/53631967) he made foundational contributions to the study of convex functions and convex sets in Riemannian geometry and their applications in the study of negative [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618), including to the geometry of [warped products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warped_geometry).
