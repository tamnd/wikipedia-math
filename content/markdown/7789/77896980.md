# R. Keith Dennis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="R. Keith Dennis"}

**Roger Keith Dennis** (March 10, 1944 – December 12, 2024), known as **R. Keith Dennis** or **Keith Dennis**, was an American mathematician who worked in [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890). His career was spent as a professor in the Department of Mathematics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). Dennis was the executive editor of *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)* as it transitioned from a printed resource to a mainly-digital one.

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Dennis was born in [Vernon, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon,_Texas), on March 10, 1944, and grew up on a farm 8 miles north of [Quanah, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanah,_Texas). He graduated from [Quanah High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanah_High_School) in 1962, and attended [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University), earning his bachelor's in 1966 and his doctorate in 1970.[^o8][^o9] His thesis on "Presentation for the Elementary Group and the Functor $K_2$" was done under [Stephen M. Gersten](wikipedia://en/61897288).[^o12] He spent the academic year 1970–1971 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton as the first assistant of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632).[^o8][^o3]

In 1971, Dennis joined the Department of Mathematics at Cornell University, and he was promoted to full professor in 1981. From 1987 to 1993 he served as the department chair. He retired to emeritus status in 2019.[^o8]

From January 1995 through June 1998, Dennis was the executive editor of the American Mathematical Society’s *Mathematical Reviews*.[^o10] During this period *Mathematical Reviews* designed and began the development of [MathSciNet](wikipedia://en/2580683)[^o8][^o7] which went online in January 1996.

He was on the Board of Advisors of the [American Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3248005) since its inception.[^o13][^o14]

In 2013, Dennis was in the inaugural class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Dennis had four children. He died of metastatic [prostate cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_cancer) on December 12, 2024, at the age of 80.[^o8][^o9]
