# János Pach

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="János Pach"}

**János Pach** (born May 3, 1954)[^o9] is a mathematician and computer scientist working in the fields of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [discrete and computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Pach was born and grew up in [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary). He comes from a noted academic family: his father, Zsigmond Pál Pach (1919–2001) was a well-known historian, and his mother Klára (née Sós, 1925–2020) was a university mathematics teacher;[^o4] his maternal aunt [Vera T. Sós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_T._Sós) and her husband [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) are two of the best-known Hungarian mathematicians.[^o5]

Pach received his [Candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) degree from the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences), in 1983, where his advisor was [Miklós Simonovits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miklós_Simonovits).

Since 1977, he has been affiliated with the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.[^o7]

He was Research Professor at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University)[^o8] (since 1986), Distinguished Professor of Computer Science at the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) (1992-2011), and Neilson Professor at [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) (2008-2009).

Between 2008 and 2019, he was Professor of the Chair of Combinatorial Geometry at [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne).[^o9][^o10]

He was the program chair for the [International Symposium on Graph Drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Symposium_on_Graph_Drawing) in 2004 and [Symposium on Computational Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_on_Computational_Geometry) in 2015. He is co-editor-in-chief of the journal *[Discrete and Computational Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_and_Computational_Geometry)*, and he serves on the editorial boards of several other journals including *[Combinatorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorica)*, *[SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics](wikipedia://en/30666823)*, *Computational Geometry*, *[Graphs and Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphs_and_Combinatorics)*, *Central European Journal of Mathematics*, and *Moscow Journal of Combinatorics and Number Theory*.

He was an invited speaker at the Combinatorics session of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), in Seoul, 2014.[^o11] He was a plenary speaker at the [European Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13505846) (Portorož), 2021.[^o12]

## Research {#Research}

Pach has authored several books and over 300 research papers. He was one of the most frequent collaborators of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), authoring over 20 papers with him and thus has an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of one.[^o14]

Pach's research is focused in the areas of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468). In 1981, he solved [Ulam's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam) problem, showing that there exists no [universal planar graph](wikipedia://en/1452141).[^o15] In the early 90s[^o16] together with [Micha Perles](wikipedia://en/35294296), he initiated the systematic study of extremal problems on [topological and geometric graphs](wikipedia://en/37439184).

Some of Pach's most-cited research work[^o17] concerns the combinatorial complexity of families of curves in the plane and their applications to [motion planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_planning) problems[^o18][^o19] the maximum number of [k-sets and halving lines](wikipedia://en/8075001) that a planar point set may have,[^o20] [crossing numbers of graphs](wikipedia://en/18298594),[^o21][^o22] embedding of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) onto fixed sets of points,[^o23][^o24] and lower bounds for [epsilon-nets](wikipedia://en/28807941).[^o25][^o26]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Pach received the Grünwald Medal of the [János Bolyai Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/22777615) (1982), the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_R._Halmos_–_Lester_R._Ford_Award) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (1990), and the [Alfréd Rényi Prize](wikipedia://en/26466805) from the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) (1992).[^o28][^o29][^o30] He was an [Erdős Lecturer](wikipedia://en/56645465) at [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) in 2005. In 2011 he was listed as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) for his research in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927).[^o31] In 2014 he was elected as a member of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea),[^o30] and in 2015 as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to discrete and combinatorial geometry and to convexity and combinatorics."[^o32] In 2022 he was elected corresponding member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.[^o33]
