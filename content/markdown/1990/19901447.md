# Aryeh Dvoretzky

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aryeh Dvoretzky"}

**Aryeh** (**Arie**) **Dvoretzky** (אריה דבורצקי, Арье Дворецкий; May 3, 1916 – May 8, 2008) was a Ukrainian-born [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), the winner of the 1973 [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize) in Mathematics.[^o3][^o4] He is best known for his work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). He was the eighth president of the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Aryeh Dvoretzky was born in [Khorol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorol,_Ukraine), [Imperial Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (now [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)). His family [immigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah) to [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) in 1922.[^o6] He graduated from the [Hebrew Reali School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Reali_School) in Haifa in 1933, and received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) in 1941. His advisor was [Michael Fekete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fekete). He continued working in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem), becoming a full professor in 1951, the first graduate of the Hebrew University to achieve this distinction.[^o7] Dvoretzky's son Gideon was killed in the 1973 [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Dvoretzky had visiting appointments at a number of universities, including [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). He also visited twice the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) (in 1948–1950 and in 1957–1958).[^o9] In 1975, he founded the [Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Institutes_for_Advanced_Study) of Jerusalem based on the Princeton IAS model.[^o10] Dvoretzky was the [Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) of the Faculty of Sciences (1955–1956) and Vice President of the Hebrew University (1959–1961). He was elected president of the [Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) (1974–1980) and became the eighth president of the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science) (1986–1989). He was awarded an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) in 1996.[^o11] Dvoretzky's students included [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) and [Joram Lindenstrauss](wikipedia://en/20653945).

## Business and civic career {#Business_and_civic_career}

In 1960, he became the head of [Rafael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Advanced_Defense_Systems_Ltd.), the weapons development authority. He later became the chief scientist for the [Israel Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_%28Israel%29).[^o13]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

In 1998, received the [Solomon Bublick Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Bublick_Award) of the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem). In 1973, he was awarded [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize) in Mathematics.[^o15][^o16]
