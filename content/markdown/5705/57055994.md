# Profinite word

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Profinite word"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more precisely in [formal language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language_theory), the **profinite words** are a generalization of the notion of [finite words](wikipedia://en/10939#Words_over_an_alphabet) into a [complete topological space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_completion). This notion allows the use of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) to study [languages](wikipedia://en/10939) and finite [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799). For example, profinite words are used to give an alternative characterization of the algebraic notion of a [variety of finite semigroups](wikipedia://en/57151064).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* be an [alphabet](wikipedia://en/4292269). The set of profinite words over *A* consists of the [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28topology%29) of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) whose domain is the set $A^*$ of words over *A*. The distance used to define the metric is given using a notion of separation of words. Those notions are now defined.

### Separation {#Separation}

Let *M* and *N* be [monoids](wikipedia://en/19652), and let *p* and *q* be elements of the monoid *M*. Let *φ* be a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) of monoids from *M* to *N*. It is said that the morphism *φ* separates *p* and *q* if $\phi(p)\ne\phi(q)$. For example, the morphism $\phi:A^*\to \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z, w\mapsto |w| (\operatorname{mod} 2)$ sending a word to the parity of its length separates the words *ababa* and *abaa*. Indeed $\phi(ababa)=1\ne0=\phi(abaa)$.

It is said that *N* separates *p* and *q* if there exists a morphism of monoids *φ* from *M* to *N* that separates *p* and *q*. Using the previous example, $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ separates *ababa* and *abaa*. More generally, $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ separates any words whose size are not congruent modulo *n*. In general, any two distinct words can be separated, using the monoid whose elements are the factors of *p* plus a fresh element 0. The morphism sends prefixes of *p* to themselves and everything else to 0.

### Distance {#Distance}

The distance between two distinct words *p* and *q* is defined as the inverse of the size of the smallest monoid *N* separating *p* and *q*. Thus, the distance of *ababa* and *abaa* is $\frac12$. The distance of *p* to itself is defined as 0.

This distance *d* is an [ultrametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrametric), that is, $d(x,z)\leq\max\left\{d(x,y),d(y,z)\right\}$. Furthermore it satisfies $d(uw,vw)\le d(u,v)$ and $d(wu,wv)\le d(u,v)$. Since any word *p* can be separated from any other word using a monoid with *\|p|+1* elements, where *\|p|* is the length of *p*, it follows that the distance between *p* and any other word is at least $\frac{1}{|p|}$. Thus the topology defined by this metric is [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology).

### Profinite topology {#Profinite_topology}

The profinite completion of $A^*$, denoted $\widehat {A^*}$, is the [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28topology%29) of the set of finite words under the distance defined above. The completion preserves the monoid structure.

The topology on $\widehat {A^*}$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

Any monoid morphism $\phi:A^*\to M$, with *M* finite can be extended uniquely into a monoid morphism $\widehat \phi:\widehat {A^*}\to M$, and this morphism is [uniformly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_continuous) (using any metric on $M$ compatible with the discrete topology). Furthermore, $\widehat {A^*}$ is the least topological space with this property.

### Profinite word {#Profinite_word}

A profinite word is an element of $\widehat {A^*}$. And a profinite language is a set of profinite words. Every finite word is a profinite word. A few examples of profinite words that are not finite are now given.

For *m* any word, let $m^\omega$ denote $\lim_{i\to\infty}m^{i!}$, which exists because $m^{i!}$ is a [Cauchy sequence](wikipedia://en/6085). Intuitively, to separate $m^{i!}$ and $m^{i'!}$, a monoid should count at least up to $\min(i,i')$, and hence requires at least $\min(i,i')$ elements. Since $m^{i!}$ is a [Cauchy sequence](wikipedia://en/6085), $m^\omega$ is indeed a profinite word.

Furthermore, the word $m^\omega$ is [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent). This is due to the fact that, for any morphism $\phi:A^*\to N$ with *N* finite, $\phi(m^{i!})=\phi(m)^{i!}$. Since *N* is finite, for *i* great enough, $\phi(m)^{i!}$ is idempotent, and the sequence is constant.

Similarly, $m^{\omega+1}$ and $m^{\omega-1}$ are defined as $\lim_{n\to\infty}m^{n!+ 1}$ and $\lim_{n\to\infty}m^{n!-1}$ respectively.

## Profinite languages {#Profinite_languages}

The notion of profinite languages allows one to relate notions of [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory) to notions of topology. More precisely, given *P* a profinite language, the following statements are equivalent:

- *P* is [clopen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clopen).
- *P* is [recognizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognizable_set),
- The [syntactic congruence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntactic_congruence) of *P* is clopen, as a subset of $\widehat {A^*}\times\widehat {A^*}$.

Similar statements also hold for languages *P* of finite words. The following conditions are equivalent.

- $P$ is recognisable (as a subset of $A^*$),
- the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of *P*, $\overline P$, is recognisable (as a subset of $\widehat {A^*}$)
- $P=K\cap A^*$, for some clopen *K*,
- $\overline P$ is clopen.

Those characterisations are due to the more general fact that, taking the closure of a language of finite words, and restricting a profinite language to finite words are inverse operations, when they are applied to recognisable languages.
