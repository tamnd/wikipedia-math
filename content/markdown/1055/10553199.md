# Jamshid al-Kashi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jamshid al-Kashi"}

**Ghiyāth al-Dīn Jamshīd Masʿūd al-Kāshī** (or **al-Kāshānī**)[^o3] (غیاث‌الدین جمشید کاشانی *Ghiyās-ud-dīn Jamshīd Kāshānī*; c. 1380 – 22 June 1429) was a Persian [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy_in_the_medieval_Islamic_world) and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_in_medieval_Islam) during the reign of [Tamerlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamerlane).

:::

Much of al-Kāshī's work was not brought to [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) and still, even the extant work, remains unpublished in any form.[^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Jamshid bin Mas'ud bin Mahmud known as Ghiyath (d. 1429); al-Risala al-Kamaliya, Safavid Iran, dated 26 June 1520.jpg.* Manuscript of al-Kashi's *al-Risala al-Kamaliya*. Copy created in [Safavid Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran), dated 26 June 1520

*Image: Resaleye mohitiye end.jpg.* Last page of a copy of *The Key to Arithmetic*

Al-Kashi was born in 1380, in [Kashan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashan), in central Iran, to a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) family.[^o6][^o7] This region was controlled by [Tamerlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur), better known as Timur.

The situation changed for the better when Timur died in 1405, and his son, [Shah Rokh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh_%28Timurid_dynasty%29), ascended into power. Shah Rokh and his wife, [Goharshad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goharshad), a Turkish princess, were very interested in the [sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences), and they encouraged their court to study the various fields in great depth. Consequently, the period of their power became one of many scholarly accomplishments. This was the perfect environment for al-Kashi to begin his career as one of the world's greatest mathematicians.

Eight years after he came into power in 1409, their son, [Ulugh Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg), founded an institute in [Samarkand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand) which soon became a prominent university. Students from all over the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East) and beyond, flocked to this academy in the capital city of Ulugh Beg's empire. Consequently, Ulugh Beg gathered many great mathematicians and scientists of the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East). In 1414, al-Kashi took this opportunity to contribute vast amounts of knowledge to his people. His best work was done in the court of Ulugh Beg.

Al-Kashi was still working on his book, called “Risala al-watar wa’l-jaib” meaning “The Treatise on the Chord and Sine”, when he died, in 1429. Some state that he was murdered and say that Ulugh Beg probably ordered this, whereas others suggest he died a natural death.[^o8][^o9] Regardless, after his death, Ulugh Beg described him as "a remarkable scientist" who "could solve the most difficult problems".[^o10][^o11]

## Astronomy {#Astronomy}

### *Khaqani Zij* {#Khaqani_Zij}

Al-Kashi produced a *[Zij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zij)* entitled the *Khaqani Zij*, which was based on [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/33642424)'s earlier *[Zij-i Ilkhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zij-i_Ilkhani)*. In his *Khaqani Zij*, al-Kashi thanks the [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty) sultan and mathematician-astronomer [Ulugh Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg), who invited al-Kashi to work at his [observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory) (see [Islamic astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy)) and his [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) (see [Madrasah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah)) which taught [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalam). Al-Kashi produced [sine](wikipedia://en/30367) tables to four [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) digits (equivalent to eight [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) places) of accuracy for each degree and includes differences for each minute. He also produced tables dealing with transformations between [coordinate systems](wikipedia://en/81931) on the [celestial sphere](wikipedia://en/48239), such as the transformation from the [ecliptic coordinate system](wikipedia://en/48387) to the [equatorial coordinate system](wikipedia://en/48384).[^o29]

### Sizes and distances of heavenly bodies {#Sizes_and_distances_of_heavenly_bodies}

He wrote the book *[Sullam al-sama'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullam_al-sama')* (*Stairway to the Heavens*, 1407) on the resolution of difficulties met by predecessors in the determination of sizes and distances of [heavenly bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavenly_bodies) such as the [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228), the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon), the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun), and the [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stars).

### Astronomical instruments {#Astronomical_instruments}

In 1416, al-Kashi wrote the *Treatise on Astronomical Observational Instruments*, which described a variety of different instruments, including the [triquetrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triquetrum_%28astronomy%29) and [armillary sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armillary_sphere), the [equinoctial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinox) armillary and [solsticial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solstice) armillary of [Mo'ayyeduddin Urdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo'ayyeduddin_Urdi), the [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) and [versine](wikipedia://en/555782) instrument of Urdi, the [sextant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextant_%28astronomical%29) of [al-Khujandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khujandi), the Fakhri sextant at the [Samarqand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarqand) observatory, a double quadrant [Azimuth](wikipedia://en/47487)-[altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude) instrument he invented, and a small armillary sphere incorporating an [alhidade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhidade) which he invented.[^o16]

#### Plate of conjunctions {#Plate_of_conjunctions}

Al-Kashi invented the plate of conjunctions, an [analog computing](wikipedia://en/2428) instrument used to determine the time of day at which [planetary conjunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_conjunction) will occur,[^o18] and for performing [linear interpolation](wikipedia://en/160010).[^o20]

#### Planetary computer {#Planetary_computer}

Al-Kashi also invented a mechanical planetary [computer](wikipedia://en/2428) which he called the Plate of Zones, which could graphically solve a number of planetary problems, including the prediction of the true positions in [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) of the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) and [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon),[^o20] and the [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) in terms of [elliptical orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_orbit);[^o21] the [latitudes](wikipedia://en/17616) of the Sun, Moon, and planets; and the [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264) of the Sun. The instrument also incorporated an [alhidade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhidade) and [ruler](wikipedia://en/65593).[^o22]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

::: {#o25 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computation of and"}

### Computation of $\pi$ and $2\pi$ {#Computation_of_and}

Al-Kashi made the most accurate [approximation of $\pi$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximations_of_π) to date in his *al-Risāla al-muhītīyya* (*Treatise on the Circumference*).[^o26] He correctly computed [$2\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) to 9 [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) digits[^o27] in 1424,[^o29] and he converted this estimate of 2$\pi$ to 16 [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) places of accuracy.[^o28] This was far more accurate than the estimates earlier given in [Greek mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mathematics) (3 decimal places by [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979), AD 150), [Chinese mathematics](wikipedia://en/1524543) (7 decimal places by [Zu Chongzhi](wikipedia://en/151762), AD 480) or [Indian mathematics](wikipedia://en/1848052) (11 decimal places by [Madhava](wikipedia://en/2793007) of [Kerala School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_School_of_Astronomy_and_Mathematics), *c.* 14th Century). The accuracy of al-Kashi's estimate was not surpassed until [Ludolph van Ceulen](wikipedia://en/362612) computed 20 decimal places of $\pi$ 180 years later. Al-Kashi's goal was to compute the circle constant so precisely that the circumference of the largest possible circle (ecliptica) could be computed with the highest desirable precision (the diameter of a hair).

:::

### *Treatise on the Chord and Sine* {#Treatise_on_the_Chord_and_Sine}

In Al-Kashi's *Risālah al-watar waʾl-jaib* (*Treatise on the Chord and Sine*), he computed sin 1° to nearly as much accuracy as his value for $\pi$, which was the most accurate approximation of sin 1° in his time and was not surpassed until [Taqi al-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taqi_ad-Din_Muhammad_ibn_Ma'ruf) in the sixteenth century. In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), he developed an [iterative method](wikipedia://en/15237) for solving [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787), which was not discovered in Europe until centuries later.[^o29]

A method algebraically equivalent to [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) was known to his predecessor [Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/1768580). Al-Kāshī improved on this by using a form of Newton's method to solve $x^P - N = 0$ to find roots of *N*. In [western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe), a similar method was later described by [Henry Briggs](wikipedia://en/210399) in his *Trigonometria Britannica*, published in 1633.[^o31]

In order to determine sin 1°, al-Kashi discovered the following formula, often attributed to [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238) in the sixteenth century:[^o32]

$$\sin 3 \phi = 3 \sin \phi - 4 \sin^3 \phi\,\!$$

### *The Key to Arithmetic* {#The_Key_to_Arithmetic}

::: {#o35 .statement cue=named-heading name="Law of cosines"}

#### Law of cosines {#Law_of_cosines}

*Image: Law of cosines following al-Kashi.png.* Al-Kashi's version of the law of cosines (case where $\gamma$ is obtuse), expressed with modern algebraic notation.

Al-Kashi's *Miftāḥ al-ḥisāb* (*Key of Arithmetic*, 1427) explained how to [solve triangles](wikipedia://en/22228915) from various combinations of given data. The method used when two sides and their included angle were given was essentially the same method used by 13th century Persian mathematician [Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī](wikipedia://en/33642424) in his Kitāb al-Shakl al-qattāʴ (*Book on the Complete Quadrilateral*, c. 1250),[^o36] but Al-Kashi presented all of the steps instead of leaving details to the reader:

Using modern algebraic notation and conventions this might be written

$$c = \sqrt{(b - a \cos \gamma)^2 + (a \sin \gamma)^2}$$

After applying the Pythagorean trigonometric identity $\cos^2\gamma + \sin^2\gamma = 1,$ this is algebraically equivalent to the modern [law of cosines](wikipedia://en/19480890):

$$\begin{aligned} c^2 &= b^2 - 2ba\cos \gamma + a^2\cos^2 \gamma + a^2\sin^2\gamma \\[5mu] &= a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos \gamma. \end{aligned}$$

In [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), the law of cosines is sometimes referred to as the *théorème d'Al-Kashi*.[^o38][^o39]

:::

#### Decimal fractions {#Decimal_fractions}

In discussing [decimal fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_fractions), [Struik](wikipedia://en/932453) states that (p. 7):[^o41]

> "The introduction of decimal fractions as a common computational practice can be dated back to the [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_Region) pamphlet *De Thiende*, published at [Leyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden) in 1585, together with a French translation, *La Disme*, by the Flemish mathematician [Simon Stevin](wikipedia://en/72586) (1548-1620), then settled in the Northern [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands). It is true that decimal fractions were used by the [Chinese](wikipedia://en/1524543) many centuries before Stevin and that the Persian astronomer Al-Kāshī used both decimal and [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) fractions with great ease in his *Key to arithmetic* (Samarkand, early fifteenth century).[^o42]"

#### Khayyam's triangle {#Khayyam's_triangle}

The Persian mathematician [Al-Karaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karaji) (953–1029) wrote a now-lost book which contained the first description of Pascal's triangle.[^o44][^o45][^o46] In considering [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497), known in Persia as "Khayyam's triangle" (named after [Omar Khayyám](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Khayyám)), Struik notes that (p. 21):[^o41]

> "The Pascal triangle appears for the first time (so far as we know at present) in a book of 1261 written by [Yang Hui](wikipedia://en/1716898), one of the mathematicians of the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China).[^o47] The properties of [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) were discussed by the Persian mathematician Jamshid Al-Kāshī in his *Key to arithmetic* of c. 1425.[^o48] Both in China and Persia the knowledge of these properties may be much older. This knowledge was shared by some of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) mathematicians, and we see [Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068)'s triangle on the title page of [Peter Apian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Apian)'s [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) arithmetic of 1527. After this, we find the triangle and the properties of binomial coefficients in several other authors.[^o49]"

## Biographical film {#Biographical_film}

In 2009, [IRIB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIB) produced and broadcast (through Channel 1 of IRIB) a biographical-historical film series on the life and times of Jamshid Al-Kāshi, with the title *[The Ladder of the Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ladder_of_the_Sky)*[^o51][^o52] (*Nardebām-e Āsmān*[^o53]). The series, which consists of 15 parts, with each part being 45 minutes long, is directed by [Mohammad Hossein Latifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hossein_Latifi) and produced by Mohsen Ali-Akbari. In this production, the role of the adult Jamshid Al-Kāshi is played by Vahid Jalilvand.[^o54][^o55][^o56]
