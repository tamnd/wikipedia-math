# Don Zagier

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Don Zagier"}

**Don Bernard Zagier** (born 29 June 1951) is an American-German mathematician whose main area of work is [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is currently one of the directors of the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Mathematik) in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn), Germany. He was a professor at the *[Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France)* in Paris from 2006 to 2014. Since October 2014, he is also a Distinguished Staff Associate at the International Centre for Theoretical Physics ([ICTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICTP)).[^o4] Among his doctoral students are [Fields medalists](wikipedia://en/10859) [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509) and [Maryna Viazovska](wikipedia://en/50004967).

:::

## Background {#Background}

Zagier was born in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany). His mother was a psychiatrist, and his father was the dean of instruction at the [American College of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Switzerland). His father held five different citizenships, and he spent his youth living in many different countries. After finishing high school (at age 13) and attending [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College) for a year, he studied for three years at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), completing his bachelor's and master's degrees and being named a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1967 at the age of 16.[^o6] He then wrote a doctoral dissertation on [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530) under [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) at [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), receiving his PhD at 20. He received his Habilitation at the age of 23, and was named professor at the age of 24.[^o7]

## Work {#Work}

Zagier collaborated with Hirzebruch in work on [Hilbert modular surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_surface). Hirzebruch and Zagier coauthored *Intersection numbers of curves on Hilbert modular surfaces and modular forms of Nebentypus,*[^o9] where they proved that intersection numbers of algebraic cycles on a [Hilbert modular surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_surface) occur as Fourier coefficients of a [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000). [Stephen Kudla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Kudla), John Millson and others generalized this result to intersection numbers of algebraic cycles on arithmetic quotients of symmetric spaces.[^o10]

One of his results is a joint work with [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863) (the so-called [Gross–Zagier formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross–Zagier_formula)). This formula relates the first derivative of the [complex L-series](wikipedia://en/245283) of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) evaluated at 1 to the height of a certain [Heegner point](wikipedia://en/5828654). This theorem has some applications, including implying cases of the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161), along with being an ingredient to [Dorian Goldfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_Goldfeld)'s solution of the [class number problem](wikipedia://en/596282). As a part of their work, Gross and Zagier found a formula for norms of differences of singular moduli.[^o11] Zagier later found a formula for traces of singular moduli as Fourier coefficients of a weight 3/2 [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000).[^o12]

Zagier collaborated with John Harer to calculate the [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) [Euler characteristics](wikipedia://en/148420) of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) of [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260), relating them to special values of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).[^o11]

Zagier found a formula for the value of the [Dedekind zeta function](wikipedia://en/1131243) of an arbitrary number field at *s* = 2 in terms of the dilogarithm function, by studying [arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/15239331).[^o13] He later formulated a general conjecture giving formulas for special values of Dedekind zeta functions in terms of polylogarithm functions.[^o14]

He discovered a short and elementary proof of [Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares](wikipedia://en/1850216).[^o15][^o16]

Zagier won the [Cole Prize in Number Theory](wikipedia://en/1372177) in 1987,[^o17] the Chauvenet Prize in 2000,[^o3] the [von Staudt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Karl_Georg_Christian_von_Staudt_Prize) in 2001,[^o18] the [Heinz Gumin Prize](wikipedia://en/78691910) in 2024[^o19] and the [Gauss Lectureship](wikipedia://en/48440954) of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973) in 2007. He became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1997[^o20] and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (NAS) of the United States in 2017.
