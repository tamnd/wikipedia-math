# Chen's theorem

*Image: Chen Jing-run.JPG.* The statue of Chen Jingrun at [Xiamen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen_University).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chen's theorem"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Chen's theorem** states that every sufficiently large [even](wikipedia://en/143135) number can be written as the sum of either two [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) or a prime and a [semiprime](wikipedia://en/339542) (the product of two primes).

:::

It is a weakened form of [Goldbach's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758), which states that every even number is the sum of two primes.

## History {#History}

The [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) was first stated by [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Chen Jingrun](wikipedia://en/352985) in 1966,[^o3] with further details of the [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) in 1973.[^o4] His original proof was much simplified by P. M. Ross in 1975.[^o5] Chen's theorem is a significant step towards [Goldbach's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758), and a celebrated application of [sieve methods](wikipedia://en/1035915).

Chen's theorem represents the strengthening of a previous result due to [Alfréd Rényi](wikipedia://en/614988), who in 1947 had shown there exists a finite *K* such that any even number can be written as the sum of a prime number and the product of at most *K* primes.[^o6][^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variations"}

## Variations {#Variations}

Chen's 1973 paper stated two results with nearly identical proofs.[^o4] His Theorem I, on the Goldbach conjecture, was stated above. His Theorem II is a result on the [twin prime conjecture](wikipedia://en/41997). It states that if *h* is a positive even [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), there are infinitely many primes *p* such that *p* + *h* is either prime or the product of two primes.

In 2019, Huixi Li gave a version of Chen's theorem for odd numbers. In particular, Li proved that every sufficiently large odd integer $N$ can be represented as[^o14]

$$N=p+2a,$$

where $p$ is prime and $a$ has at most 2 prime factors. Here, the factor of 2 is necessitated since every prime (except for 2) is odd, causing $N-p$ to be even. Li's result can be viewed as an approximation to [Lemoine's conjecture](wikipedia://en/12552062).
