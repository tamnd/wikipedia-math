# CCR and CAR algebras

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CCR and CAR algebras"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) **CCR algebras** (after [canonical commutation relations](wikipedia://en/706295)) and **CAR algebras** (after canonical anticommutation relations) arise from the [quantum mechanical](wikipedia://en/25202) study of [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) and [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion), respectively. They play a prominent role in [quantum statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/712450)[^o2] and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).

:::

## CCR and CAR as \*-algebras {#CCR_and_CAR_as_*-algebras}

Let $V$ be a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) equipped with a [nonsingular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) real [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/174055#Skew-symmetric_and_alternating_forms) [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) $(\cdot,\cdot)$ (i.e. a [symplectic vector space](wikipedia://en/292852)). The [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) [\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/293426) generated by elements of $V$ subject to the relations

$$fg-gf=i(f,g) \,$$

$$f^*=f, \,$$

for any $f,~g$ in $V$ is called the **canonical commutation relations (CCR) algebra**. The uniqueness of the representations of this algebra when $V$ is [finite dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_dimensional) is discussed in the [Stone–von Neumann theorem](wikipedia://en/744569).

If $V$ is equipped with a [nonsingular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) real [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) $(\cdot,\cdot)$ instead, the unital \*-algebra generated by the elements of $V$ subject to the relations

$$fg+gf=(f,g), \,$$

$$f^*=f, \,$$

for any $f,~g$ in $V$ is called the **canonical anticommutation relations (CAR) algebra**.

## The C\*-algebra of CCR {#The_C*-algebra_of_CCR}

There is a distinct, but closely related meaning of CCR algebra, called the CCR C\*-algebra. Let $H$ be a real symplectic vector space with nonsingular symplectic form $(\cdot,\cdot)$. In the theory of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987), the CCR algebra over $H$ is the unital [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) generated by elements $\{W(f):~f\in H\}$ subject to

$$W(f)W(g)=e^{-i(f,g)}W(f+g), \,$$

$$W(f)^*=W(-f). \,$$

These are called the Weyl form of the canonical commutation relations and, in particular, they imply that each $W(f)$ is [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999) and $W(0)=1$. It is well known that the CCR algebra is a simple (unless the symplectic form is degenerate) non-separable algebra and is unique up to isomorphism.[^o5]

When $H$ is a complex [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) and $(\cdot,\cdot)$ is given by the imaginary part of the inner-product, the CCR algebra is [faithfully represented](wikipedia://en/19378200) on the [symmetric Fock space](wikipedia://en/230491) over $H$ by setting

$$W(f)\left(1,g,\frac{g^{\otimes 2}}{2!},\frac{g^{\otimes 3}}{3!},\ldots\right)= e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|f\|^2-\langle f,g\rangle }\left(1,f+g,\frac{(f+g)^{\otimes 2}}{2!}, \frac{(f+g)^{\otimes 3}}{3!}, \ldots\right),$$

for any $f,g \in H$. The field operators $B(f)$ are defined for each $f\in H$ as the [generator](wikipedia://en/893698) of the one-parameter unitary group $(W(tf))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}$ on the symmetric Fock space. These are [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408) [unbounded operators](wikipedia://en/1422584), however they formally satisfy

$$B(f)B(g)-B(g)B(f) = 2i\operatorname{Im}\langle f,g\rangle.$$

As the assignment $f\mapsto B(f)$ is real-linear, so the operators $B(f)$ define a CCR algebra over $(H,2\operatorname{Im}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ in the sense of Section 1.

## The C\*-algebra of CAR {#The_C*-algebra_of_CAR}

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space. In the theory of operator algebras the CAR algebra is the unique [C\*-completion](wikipedia://en/7832) of the complex unital \*-algebra generated by elements $\{b(f),b^*(f):~f\in H\}$ subject to the relations

$$b(f)b^*(g)+b^*(g)b(f)=\langle f,g\rangle, \,$$

$$b(f)b(g)+b(g)b(f)=0, \,$$

$$\lambda b^*(f)=b^*(\lambda f), \,$$

$$b(f)^*=b^*(f), \,$$

for any $f,g\in H$, $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$. When $H$ is separable the CAR algebra is an [AF algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximately_finite_dimensional_C*-algebra) and in the special case $H$ is infinite dimensional it is often written as ${M_{2^\infty}(\mathbb{C})}$.[^o7]

Let $F_a(H)$ be the [antisymmetric Fock space](wikipedia://en/230491) over $H$ and let $P_a$ be the orthogonal projection onto antisymmetric vectors:

$$P_a: \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty H^{\otimes n} \to F_a(H). \,$$

The CAR algebra is faithfully represented on $F_a(H)$ by setting

$$b^*(f)P_a(g_1\otimes g_2\otimes\cdots\otimes g_n)=\sqrt{n+1}P_a(f\otimes g_1\otimes g_2\otimes\cdots\otimes g_n) \,$$

for all $f,g_1,\ldots,g_n\in H$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$. The fact that these form a C\*-algebra is due to the fact that creation and annihilation operators on antisymmetric Fock space are bona-fide [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961). Moreover, the field operators $B(f):=b^*(f)+b(f)$ satisfy

$$B(f)B(g)+B(g)B(f)=2\mathrm{Re}\langle f,g\rangle, \,$$

giving the relationship with Section 1.

## Superalgebra generalization {#Superalgebra_generalization}

Let $V$ be a real $\mathbb{Z}_2$-[graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734) equipped with a nonsingular antisymmetric bilinear superform $(\cdot,\cdot)$ (i.e. $(g,f)=-(-1)^{|f||g|}(f,g)$ ) such that $(f,g)$ is real if either $f$ or $g$ is an even element and [imaginary](wikipedia://en/14554) if both of them are odd. The unital \*-algebra generated by the elements of $V$ subject to the relations

$$fg-(-1)^{|f||g|}gf=i(f,g) \,$$

$$f^*=f,~g^*=g\,$$

for any two pure elements $f,~g$ in $V$ is the obvious [superalgebra](wikipedia://en/469950) generalization which unifies CCRs with CARs: if all pure elements are even, one obtains a CCR, while if all pure elements are odd, one obtains a CAR.

In mathematics, the abstract structure of the CCR and CAR algebras, over any field, not just the complex numbers, is studied by the name of [Weyl](wikipedia://en/491095) and [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305), where many significant results have accrued. One of these is that the [graded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) generalizations of [Weyl](wikipedia://en/491095) and [Clifford](wikipedia://en/45305) algebras allow the basis-free formulation of the canonical commutation and anticommutation relations in terms of a symplectic and a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form. In addition, the binary elements in this graded Weyl algebra give a basis-free version of the commutation relations of the [symplectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_Lie_algebra) and [indefinite orthogonal Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/293609).[^o9]
