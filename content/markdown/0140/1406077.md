# AA postulate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="AA postulate"}

*Image: AA Postulate Helper.png.*

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **AA postulate** states that two [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) are similar if they have two corresponding [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330).[^o2]

:::

The AA postulate follows from the fact that the sum of the [interior angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_angle) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is [always equal to 180°](wikipedia://en/5407025). By knowing two angles, such as 32° and 64° degrees, we know that the next angle is 84°, because 180-(32+64)=84. ( This is sometimes referred to as the AAA Postulate—which is true in all respects, but two angles are entirely sufficient.[^o2]

The postulate can be better understood by working in reverse order. The two triangles on grids A and B are [similar](wikipedia://en/39377), by a 1.5 [dilation](wikipedia://en/651752) from A to B. If they are aligned, as in grid C, it is apparent that the angle on the origin is congruent with the other (D). We also know that the pair of sides opposite the origin are parallel. We know this because the pairs of sides around them are similar, stem from the same point, and line up with each other. We can then look at the sides around the parallels as [transversals](wikipedia://en/13295107), and therefore the corresponding angles are congruent. Using this reasoning we can tell that similar triangles have congruent angles.

AA postulate can be used to prove the [Angle Bisector Theorem](wikipedia://en/2401128), and it is a method of proving triangle [similarity](wikipedia://en/39377).
