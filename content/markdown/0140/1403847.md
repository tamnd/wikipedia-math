# Pandiagonal magic cube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pandiagonal magic cube"}

In [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446), a **pandiagonal magic cube** is a [magic cube](wikipedia://en/372356) with the additional property that all [broken diagonals](wikipedia://en/3231123) (parallel to exactly two of the three coordinate axes) have the same sum as each other. Pandiagonal magic cubes are extensions of [diagonal magic cubes](wikipedia://en/1403664) (in which only the unbroken diagonals need to have the same sum as the rows of the cube) and generalize [pandiagonal magic squares](wikipedia://en/491524) to three dimensions.

:::

In a pandiagonal magic cube, all 3*m* planar arrays must be [panmagic squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panmagic_square). The 6 oblique squares are always [magic](wikipedia://en/52202). Several of them may be panmagic squares. A **proper pandiagonal magic cube** has exactly 9*m*<sup>2</sup> lines plus the 4 main [space diagonals](wikipedia://en/1337505) summing correctly (no broken space diagonals have the correct sum.)

The smallest pandiagonal magic cube has order 7.
