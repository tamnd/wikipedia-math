# Diagonal magic cube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diagonal magic cube"}

The class of **diagonal magic cubes** is the second of the six [magic cube classes](wikipedia://en/1369521) (when ranked by the number of lines summing correctly), coming after the [simple magic cubes](wikipedia://en/13268241).

:::

In a diagonal magic cube of order *m*,[^o2] all 6*m* of the diagonals in the *m* planes parallel to the top, front, and sides of the cube must sum correctly. This means that the cube contains 3*m* [simple magic squares](wikipedia://en/52202#Classification_of_magic_squares) of order *m*. Because the cube contains so many magic squares, it was considered for many years to be "perfect" (although other types of cubes were also sometimes called a "perfect magic cube").

It is now known that there are three higher classes of cubes.

The **(proper)** **diagonal magic cube** has a total of 3m<sup>2</sup> + 6*m* + 4 correctly summing lines and 3*m* + 6 simple magic squares.

The new definition **perfect magic cube** has a total of 13*m*<sup>2</sup> correct lines and 9*m* [pandiagonal magic squares](wikipedia://en/491524).
