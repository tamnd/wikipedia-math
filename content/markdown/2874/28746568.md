# Spectral abscissa

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spectral abscissa"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **spectral abscissa** of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) or a [bounded linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) is the greatest real part of the matrix's [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206136) (its set of eigenvalues).[^o2] It is sometimes denoted $\alpha(A)$. As a transformation $\alpha: \mathrm{M}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, the spectral abscissa maps a square matrix onto its largest real eigenvalue.[^o3]

:::

## Matrices {#Matrices}

Let λ<sub>1</sub>, ..., λ<sub>*s*</sub> be the ([real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)) eigenvalues of a matrix *A* ∈ **C**<sup>*n* × *n*</sup>. Then its spectral abscissa is defined as:

$$\alpha(A) = \max_i\{ \operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) \} \,$$

In [stability theory](wikipedia://en/5493795), a continuous system represented by matrix $A$ is said to be [stable](wikipedia://en/363360) if all real parts of its eigenvalues are negative, i.e. $\alpha(A)<0$.[^o5] Analogously, in [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), the solution to the differential equation $\dot{x}=Ax$ is stable under the same condition $\alpha(A)<0$.[^o3]
