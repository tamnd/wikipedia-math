# Giorgi Nikoladze

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giorgi Nikoladze"}

**Giorgi Nikoladze** (11 August 1888 – 22 September 1931) was a Georgian mathematician, metallurgist, translator, gymnast, and alpinist. He worked as a professor at [Tbilisi State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi_State_University) and as a translator for technical language into Georgian. He was also a cofounder of the [Georgian Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/77639699), founder of the first Georgian gymnastic society *Shevardeni*, and led a summit to [Mount Kazbegi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kazbegi).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

The son of writer [Niko Nikoladze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niko_Nikoladze) and biologist [Olga Guramishvili-Nikoladze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Guramishvili-Nikoladze), and a member of the [Nikoladze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikoladze) noble family, Giorgi Nikoladze was born on 11 August 1888 at Didi Jikhaishi in [Georgia, then part of the Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_within_the_Russian_Empire). He first studied at the [First Tbilisi Classical Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Tbilisi_Classical_Gymnasium) from 1898 to 1906, then the [Saint Petersburg State Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_Institute_of_Technology) until 1913, where he was granted a Diploma of Technological Engineer in Metallurgy.[^n1][^o5][^o6] He then began working at the Tula Metallurgical Plant, later moving to the [Donetsk Metallurgical Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk_Metallurgical_Plant) in the engineering department, and finally returning to Georgia in 1918.[^n1][^o8]

From 1918 to 1928, he worked as a lecturer and then a professor at [Tbilisi State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi_State_University), and was awarded a doctorate from the Faculty of sciences of Paris at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in 1928[^o5][^o6] for his dissertation in geometry.[^n2] Nikoladze was also an avid gymnast who was an adherent of the [Sokol movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokol_movement), founding the first Georgian gymnastic society *Shevardeni* in 1918.[^n3] In 1920, he jointly published a "Russian-Georgian and Georgian-Russian dictionary of mathematical terms" with Archil Kharadze and [Nikoloz Muskhelishvili](wikipedia://en/977804).[^o5][^o6] With this and other translation projects, Nikoladze sought to establish a uniform technical language in [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language).[^o11]

Alongside Kharadze, Muskhelishvli, and [Andrea Razmadze](wikipedia://en/51120930), he founded the [Georgian Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/77639699) in 1923.[^o12][^o13] Nikoladze also had a long-time interest in alpinism since his childhood,[^n4] and in August that same year, he successfully led an expedition to the summit of the 5000-meter high [Mount Kazbegi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kazbek).[^n5] He was married to Georgetta Gambashidze.[^o16] He died on 22 September 1931 in [Tbilisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi).[^o5]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Although Nikoladze was little-known outside the Soviet Union, he was described as "one of the founders of the mathematics section at Tiflis \[Tbilisi\], and a pioneer in the technical and scientific development of the Caucasus."[^o11] A monumental bust dedicated to him stands in the Giorgi Nikoladze Memorial Square in [Zestaponi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zestaponi).[^o18]

In 2016, Georgian historian Paata Natsvlishvili hypothesized that Nikoladze was actually the "[unknown French boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unknown_French_boy)" who won gold at the [1900 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics) in Paris. According to Natsvlishvili, Nikoladze's sister had told him in an interview in the 1980s that her brother had won a boat race in France, and that the family had been touring Europe at the time of the Olympics.[^o19][^o20] However, some historians from the International Society of Olympic Historians remained unconvinced that Nikoladze was really the unknown boy owing to the circumstantial evidence. Historian [Bill Mallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Mallon) said that the unknown boy only looked "a little" like Nikoladze.[^o21]
