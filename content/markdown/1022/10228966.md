# Jabir ibn Aflah

the 8th/9th-century Arabic alchemist

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jabir ibn Aflah"}

**Abū Muḥammad Jābir ibn Aflaḥ** (أبو محمد جابر بن أفلح, Geber/Gebir; 1100–1150) was an [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab)[^o3][^o4] [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy) and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics) from [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville), who was active in 12th century [al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus). His work *Iṣlāḥ al-Majisṭi* (Correction of the *Almagest*) influenced [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic), [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish), and [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians) [astronomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomers).[^n1]

:::

## *Iṣlāḥ al-Majisṭi (Correction of the Almagest)* {#Iṣlāḥ_al-Majisṭi_(Correction_of_the_Almagest)}

This work is a commentary and reworking of [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979)'s *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)* and is the first criticism of it in the Islamic West. He particularly criticized the mathematical basis of the work. For example, he replaced the use of [Menelaus' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus'_theorem) with ones based on [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405), in what seems to be an attempt to increase the mathematical precision of the work. These theorems had been developed by a group of 10th century Islamic mathematicians who included [Abū al-Wafā' Būzjānī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_al-Wafā'_Būzjānī) and then also by [Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Muadh Al-Jayyani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Jayyani) who worked in al-Andalus during the 11th century. Jābir does not credit any of these authors and does not refer to a single Islamic author in this work.[^n1]

One substantial change Jābir made to Ptolemy's account is that he placed the orbits of [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus) and [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_%28planet%29), the [inferior planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferior_planets), outside that of the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun), rather than between the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) and the Sun as had been the case in the original work.[^n1]

## Inventor {#Inventor}

*Image: GNM - Torquetum.jpg.* The [Torquetum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquetum) may have been invented by Jabir ibn Aflah.

He may have invented an observational instrument known as the [torquetum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquetum), a mechanical device to transform between spherical coordinate systems.[^o10]

## Influence {#Influence}

Several later Muslim authors were influenced by Jābir, including [Ibn Rushd](wikipedia://en/47836) (Averroes) and [Nur ad-Din al-Betrugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_ad-Din_al-Betrugi), both of whom worked in al-Andalus. The work was transmitted to [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt) in the 12th century by [Maimonides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimonides) and further east by the end of the 13th century.[^n1]

The work was translated from the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) into both [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew) and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), the latter by [Gerard of Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_of_Cremona), who [Latinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Latinised_names) his name as "Geber". Through this translation, it had a wide influence on later [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) mathematicians and astronomers and helped to promote [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) in Europe.[^n1]

Much of the material on spherical trigonometry in [Regiomontanus](wikipedia://en/25929)' *On Triangles* (c.1463) was taken directly and without credit from Jābir's work, as noted in the 16th century by [Gerolamo Cardano](wikipedia://en/13145).[^o14]
