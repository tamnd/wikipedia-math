# Thyrsa Frazier Svager

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thyrsa Frazier Svager"}

**Thyrsa Anne Frazier Svager** (June 16, 1930 – July 23, 1999) was an American academic who was the first African-American woman to earn a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University). She was also the tenth African-American woman in the United States to earn this degree.[^o3][^o4] Born in Ohio, she graduated from high school at the age of 16, going to [Antioch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_College) in Ohio and then doing her postgraduate degrees at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University).[^o7] Frazier Svager was the head of the Department of Mathematics at [Central State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_State_University) (CSU) in Ohio for decades, ending her academic career as provost and dean for academic affairs.[^o8] She and her husband, physics professor Aleksandar Svager, invested one of their salaries during their careers to build a legacy for scholarships.[^o5][^o25] After her death, the Thyrsa Frazier Svager Fund was established to provide scholarships for African-American women majoring in mathematics.[^o26]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Frazier Svager was born Thyrsa Anne Frazier on June 16, 1930, in [Wilberforce, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilberforce,_Ohio).[^o7] Her mother, Elizabeth Anne Frazier, taught speech at [Central State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_State_University) (CSU), a historically black university in [Wilberforce, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilberforce,_Ohio).[^o8] Her father, G. Thuton Frazier, headed the Logistics Department at the [Wright-Patterson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright-Patterson_Air_Force_Base) in [Dayton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio).[^o8] He was a member of the [Kappa Alpha Psi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa_Alpha_Psi) fraternity, holding the position of Provence Polemarch.[^o8][^o9] Frazier Svager had three sisters, Gail, Constance and Jane, and a brother, William Lafayette.[^o8][^o7]

Frazier Svager graduated from Wilberforce University Preparatory Academy in Ohio at the age of 16 in 1947, as class [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian).[^o7][^o11] She attended [Antioch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_College), a private liberal arts college in [Yellow Springs, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Springs,_Ohio), majoring in mathematics, with a minor in chemistry, and placed in the 99th percentile in the Princeton Senior Student Examination.[^o10] Frazier Svager was one of only four black students at Antioch: one of the others was [Coretta Scott King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coretta_Scott_King), with whom she was friends.[^o11]

She gained a Bachelor of Arts degree from Antioch in 1951, going on to gain a master's (1952) and PhD from [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) (OSU) in Columbus in 1965, where Paul Reichelderfer was her doctoral advisor.[^o12] Her dissertation was titled "On the product of absolutely continuous transformations of measure spaces".[^o14]

## Career {#Career}

Frazier Svager worked for a year at [Wright-Patterson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright-Patterson_Air_Force_Base) in Dayton, before teaching at [Texas Southern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Southern_University) in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston).[^o12] In 1954, she joined the faculty of CSU in Wilberforce.[^o12]

In 1967, Frazier Svager was appointed chairman of the department of mathematics.[^o16] She was awarded tenure in 1970.[^o17][^o12] She spent a summer in DC in 1966 as a systems analyst at [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA), as visiting faculty at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) in 1969, and in 1985, she undertook postdoctoral study at OSU during the summer.[^o7] She was provost and vice president for academic affairs when she retired in 1993.[^o11][^o7] In March 1995, she returned for a short time to CSU as Interim President.[^o7]

Frazier Svager was active on the issue of scholarships, serving as the president of the local chapter of MOLES, a national association that provided scholarships for college students.[^o18] She was also a member of [Beta Kappa Chi](wikipedia://en/44516824), the [National Association of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/58449356), and the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), and was involved with [Jack and Jill of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_and_Jill_of_America).[^o18][^o7] Frazier Svager participated in the meeting that founded the National Association of Mathematics in 1969.[^o7]

She wrote two books, CSU's *Modern Elementary Algebra Workbook* (1969),[^o19] and *Essential Mathematics for College Freshmen* (1976).[^o20]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

While on the CSU faculty, Frazier met Aleksandar Svager, a Holocaust survivor from Yugoslavia and physics professor at CSU.[^o5][^o25] They married in June 1968 at her parents' home.[^o22]

Thyrsa Frazier Svager died on July 23, 1999.[^o23]

## Philanthropy {#Philanthropy}

Both university professors with a strong commitment to furthering education opportunities, the Svagers lived on one income, investing the other to build a scholarship fund.[^o25] After her death, her husband established the Thyrsa Frazier Svager Fund at the Dayton Foundation, for African-American women who major in mathematics at one of six universities, with a legacy contribution planned.[^o26][^o25] As of February 2017, 33 women had received support from the Fund.[^o25] An annual contribution is also being made to the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society)'s Minority Scholarship.[^o5] Alongside her professional career, Thyrsa was active as a member of various civil and social organizations. She was a member of, among others, [Alpha Kappa Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Kappa_Alpha) sorority, Twentieth Century Club, Wilberforce Chapter of Moles, and Volunteers of the African American Museum.[^o27]

## Honors {#Honors}

Frazier Svager was honored with an Honorary [Doctor of Humane Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Humane_Letters) by CSU on her retirement, and she was inducted into the Hall of Fame in [Greene County, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_County,_Ohio).[^o7]
