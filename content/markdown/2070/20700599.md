# Hill tetrahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hill tetrahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Hill tetrahedra** are a family of [space-filling](wikipedia://en/58607583) [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606). They were discovered in 1896 by [M. J. M. Hill](wikipedia://en/36580568), a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), who showed that they are [scissor-congruent](wikipedia://en/152703) to a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

For every $\alpha \in (0,2\pi/3)$, let $v_1,v_2,v_3 \in \mathbb R^3$ be three unit vectors with angle $\alpha$ between every two of them. Define the Hill tetrahedron $Q(\alpha)$ as follows:

$$Q(\alpha) \, = \, \{c_1 v_1+c_2 v_2+c_3 v_3 \mid 0 \le c_1 \le c_2 \le c_3 \le 1\}.$$

A special case $Q=Q(\pi/2)$ is the tetrahedron having all sides right triangles, two with sides $(1,1,\sqrt{2})$ and two with sides $(1,\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})$. [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) studied $Q$ as a special case of the [orthoscheme](wikipedia://en/20740340), and [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) called it the characteristic tetrahedron of the cubic spacefilling.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A cube can be tiled with six copies of $Q$.[^o6]
- Every $Q(\alpha)$ can be [dissected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) into three polytopes which can be reassembled into a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In 1951, [Hugo Hadwiger](wikipedia://en/630451) found the following $n$-dimensional generalization of Hill tetrahedra:

$$Q(w) = \{c_1 v_1+\cdots +c_n v_n \mid 0 \le c_1 \le \cdots \le c_n \le 1\},$$

where vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ satisfy $(v_i,v_j) = w$ for all $1\le i< j\le n$, and where $-1/(n-1)< w < 1$. Hadwiger showed that all such [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) are scissor congruent to a [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783).

:::
