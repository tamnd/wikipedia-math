# Van der Corput inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Van der Corput inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **van der Corput inequality** is a [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) of the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](wikipedia://en/38128) that is useful in the study of [correlations](wikipedia://en/157057) among vectors, and hence [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685). It is also useful in the study of [equidistributed sequences](wikipedia://en/1030420), for example in the [Weyl equidistribution estimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_equidistribution_estimate). Loosely stated, the van der Corput inequality asserts that if a [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) $v$ in an [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856) $V$ is strongly correlated with many unit vectors $u_{1}, \dots, u_{n} \in V$, then many of the pairs $u_{i}, u_{j}$ must be strongly correlated with each other. Here, the notion of correlation is made precise by the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) of the space $V$: when the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of $\langle u, v \rangle$ is close to $1$, then $u$ and $v$ are considered to be strongly correlated. (More generally, if the vectors involved are not unit vectors, then strong correlation means that $| \langle u, v \rangle | \approx \| u \| \| v \|$.)

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Statement of the inequality"}

## Statement of the inequality {#Statement_of_the_inequality}

Let $V$ be a real or complex inner product space with inner product $\langle \cdot , \cdot \rangle$ and induced [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) $\| \cdot \|$. Suppose that $v, u_1, \dots, u_n \in V$ and that $\| v \| = 1$. Then

$$\displaystyle \left( \sum_{i = 1}^{n} | \langle v, u_{i} \rangle | \right)^{2} \leq \sum_{i, j = 1}^{n} | \langle u_{i}, u_{j} \rangle | .$$

In terms of the correlation heuristic mentioned above, if $v$ is strongly correlated with many unit vectors $u_1, \dots, u_n \in V$, then the left-hand side of the inequality will be large, which then forces a significant proportion of the vectors $u_{i}$ to be strongly correlated with one another.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of the inequality"}

### Proof of the inequality {#Proof_of_the_inequality}

We start by noticing that for any $i\in 1,\dots,n$ there exists $\epsilon_i$ (real or complex) such that $|\epsilon_i|=1$ and $|\langle v, u_{i} \rangle| = \epsilon_i \langle v, u_{i} \rangle$. Then,

$$\left(\sum_{i = 1}^{n} \left| \langle v, u_{i} \rangle \right| \right)^{2}$$

$$=\left( \sum_{i = 1}^{n} \epsilon_{i} \langle v, u_{i} \rangle \right)^{2}$$

$= \left( \left\langle v, \sum_{i = 1}^{n} \epsilon_{i} u_{i} \right\rangle \right)^{2}$ since the inner product is [bilinear](wikipedia://en/4365)

$\leq \| v \|^{2} \left\| \sum_{i = 1}^{n} \epsilon_{i} u_{i} \right\|^{2}$ by the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](wikipedia://en/38128)

$= \| v \|^{2} \left\langle \sum_{i = 1}^{n} \epsilon_{i} u_{i}, \sum_{j = 1}^{n} \epsilon_{i} u_{j} \right\rangle$ by the definition of the induced norm

$= \sum_{i, j = 1}^{n}\epsilon_{i} \epsilon_{j} \langle u_{i}, u_{j} \rangle$ since $v$ is a unit vector and the inner product is bilinear

$\leq \sum_{i, j = 1}^{n} | \langle u_{i}, u_{j} \rangle |$ since $|\epsilon_i|=1$ for all $i$.

:::
