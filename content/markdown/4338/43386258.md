# Gelfand–Raikov theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gelfand–Raikov theorem"}

The **Gel'fand–Raikov (Гельфанд–Райков)** theorem is a theorem in the [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) of [locally compact topological groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_topological_group). It states that a [locally compact group](wikipedia://en/1651204) is completely determined by its (possibly infinite dimensional) [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788). The theorem was first published in 1943.[^o2] [^o3]

:::

A unitary representation $\rho: G \to U(H)$ of a locally compact group $G$ on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $H = (H, \langle\,,\rangle)$ defines for each pair of vectors $h,k \in H$ a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) on $G$, the [matrix coefficient](wikipedia://en/11017946), by

$$g \mapsto \langle h, \rho(g)k \rangle.$$

The set of all matrix coefficients for all unitary representations is closed under [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373) (because we can replace $k \to \lambda k$), addition (because of [direct sum representations](wikipedia://en/2084687)), multiplication (because of [tensor representations](wikipedia://en/55631153)) and complex conjugation (because of the [complex conjugate representations](wikipedia://en/1442618)).

The Gel'fand–Raikov theorem now states that the points of $G$ are separated by its irreducible unitary representations, i.e. for any two group elements $g, h \in G$ there exist a Hilbert space $H$ and an irreducible unitary representation $\rho: G \to U(H)$ such that $\rho(g) \ne \rho(h)$. The matrix elements thus separate points, and it then follows from the [Stone–Weierstrass theorem](wikipedia://en/28858) that on every compact subset of the group, the matrix elements are dense in the space of continuous functions, which determine the group completely.
