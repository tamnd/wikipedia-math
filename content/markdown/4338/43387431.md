# Inverted Dirichlet distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inverted Dirichlet distribution"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **inverted Dirichlet distribution** is a multivariate generalization of the [beta prime distribution](wikipedia://en/3126406), and is related to the [Dirichlet distribution](wikipedia://en/1117833). It was first described by Tiao and Cuttman in 1965.[^o2]

:::

The distribution has a density function given by

$$p\left(x_1,\ldots, x_k\right) = \frac{\Gamma\left(\nu_1+\cdots+\nu_{k+1}\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\Gamma\left(\nu_j\right)} x_1^{\nu_1-1}\cdots x_k^{\nu_k-1}\times\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\right)^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\nu_j},\qquad x_i>0.$$

The distribution has applications in [statistical regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_regression) and arises naturally when considering the [multivariate Student distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_Student_distribution). It can be characterized[^o3] by its [mixed moments](wikipedia://en/368684#Mixed_moments):

$$E\left[\prod_{i=1}^kx_i^{q_i}\right] = \frac{\Gamma\left(\nu_{k+1}-\sum_{j=1}^k q_j\right)}{\Gamma\left(\nu_{k+1}\right)}\prod_{j=1}^k\frac{\Gamma\left(\nu_j+q_j\right)}{\Gamma\left(\nu_j\right)}$$

provided that $q_j>-\nu_j, 1\leqslant j\leqslant k$ and $\nu_{k+1}>q_1+\ldots+q_k$.

The inverted Dirichlet distribution is conjugate to the [negative multinomial distribution](wikipedia://en/24993596) if a generalized form of [odds ratio](wikipedia://en/406880) is used instead of the categories' probabilities- if the negative multinomial parameter vector is given by $p$, by changing parameters of the negative multinomial to $x_i = \frac{p_i}{p_0}, i = 1\ldots k$ where $p_0 = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{k} p_i$.

T. Bdiri et al. have developed several models that use the inverted Dirichlet distribution to represent and model non-Gaussian data. They have introduced finite [^o4][^o5] and infinite [^o6] [mixture models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixture_models) of inverted Dirichlet distributions using the [Newton–Raphson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton–Raphson) technique to estimate the parameters and the [Dirichlet process](wikipedia://en/8330403) to model infinite mixtures. T. Bdiri et al. have also used the inverted Dirichlet distribution to propose an approach to generate [Support Vector Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Support_Vector_Machine) kernels [^o7] basing on [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571) and another approach to establish [hierarchical clustering](wikipedia://en/477573).[^o8][^o9]
