# Tim Chartier

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tim Chartier"}

**Timothy P. Chartier** (born 1969)[^o2] is an American mathematician known for his expertise in [sports analytics](wikipedia://en/51589225) and [bracketology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracketology),[^o3][^o4][^o5] for his [popular mathematics](wikipedia://en/2069923) books, and for the "mime-matics" shows combining [mime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mime) and mathematics that he and his wife Tanya have staged.[^o6]

:::

He is Joseph R. Morton Professor of Mathematics and Computer Science at [Davidson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson_College).[^o7] The [National Museum of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mathematics) announced him as 2022-23 Distinguished Visiting Professor for the Public Dissemination of Mathematics, in June 2021.

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Chartier majored in applied mathematics at [Western Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Michigan_University), graduating in 1993, and stayed at Western Michigan for a master's degree in computational mathematics in 1996.[^o9] He completed a PhD at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder) in 2001, with the dissertation *Algebraic Multigrid Based on Element Interpolation (AMGe) and Spectral AMGe* supervised by Steve McCormick. He has also studied [mime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mime), at the Centre du Silence in Colorado, at the [Dell'Arte International School of Physical Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell'Arte_International_School_of_Physical_Theatre) in California, and with [Marcel Marceau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Marceau).[^o11]

After postdoctoral research at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), he joined the Davidson College faculty in 2003.[^o9] As well as his academic work, he is also a frequent consultant on sports analytics for [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN), [NASCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR), the [National Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association), and other groups.[^o12]

## Books {#Books}

Chartier is the author of *Math Bytes: Google Bombs, Chocolate-Covered Pi, and Other Cool Bits in Computing* (2014),[^o14] which won the [Euler Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Book_Prize) in 2020,[^o15] and of *When Life is Linear: From Computer Graphics to Bracketology* (2015),[^o16] which won the [Beckenbach Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckenbach_Book_Prize) in 2017.[^o17]

He is also the author of *X Games In Mathematics: Sports Training That Counts!* (2020) and the coauthor, with [Anne Greenbaum](wikipedia://en/56288604), of *Numerical Methods: Design, Analysis, and Computer Implementation of Algorithms* (2012).[^o18]
