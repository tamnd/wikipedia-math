# Graded structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graded structure"}

graded In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the term "**graded**" has a number of meanings, mostly related:

:::

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), it refers to a family of concepts:

- An [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $X$ is said to be $I$-**graded** for an [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) $I$ if it has a **gradation** or **grading**, i.e. a decomposition into a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) $X = \bigoplus_{i \in I} X_i$ of structures; the elements of $X_i$ are said to be "**homogeneous** of **degree** *i*.
  - The index set $I$ is most commonly $\mathbb{N}$ or $\mathbb{Z}$, and may be required to have extra structure depending on the type of $X$.
  - Grading by $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (i.e. $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$) is also important; see e.g. [signed set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_set) (the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded sets).
  - The **trivial** ($\mathbb{Z}$- or $\mathbb{N}$-) gradation has $X_0 = X, X_i = 0$ for $i \neq 0$ and a suitable trivial structure $0$.
  - An algebraic structure is said to be [doubly graded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_graded) if the index set is a direct product of sets; the pairs may be called "**bidegrees**" (e.g. see [Spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894)).
- A $I$-[graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734) or **graded linear space** is thus a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) with a decomposition into a [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_vector_spaces) $V = \bigoplus_{i \in I} V_i$ of spaces.
  - A [graded linear map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_linear_map) is a map between graded vector spaces respecting their gradations.
- A [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094) is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) that is a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of additive [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) $R_i$ such that $R_i R_j \subseteq R_{i+j}$, with $i$ taken from some [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652), usually $\mathbb{N}$ or $\mathbb{Z}$, or [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) (for a [ring without identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_without_identity)).
  - The [associated graded ring](wikipedia://en/38279783) of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$ with respect to a proper [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $I$ is $\operatorname{gr}_I R = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} I^n/I^{n+1}$.
- A [graded module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_module) is left [module](wikipedia://en/276410) $M$ over a graded ring that is a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) $\bigoplus_{i \in I} M_i$ of modules satisfying $R_i M_j \subseteq M_{i+j}$.
  - The [associated graded module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_graded_module) of an $R$-module $M$ with respect to a proper ideal $I$ is $\operatorname{gr}_I M = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} I^n M/ I^{n+1} M$.
  - A [differential graded module](wikipedia://en/46781249), **differential graded $\mathbb{Z}$-module** or **DG-module** is a graded module $M$ with a **differential** $d \colon M \to M \colon M_i \to M_{i+1}$ making $M$ a **chain complex**, i.e. $d \circ d = 0$ .
- A [graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) is an [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring) $A$ over a ring $R$ that is graded as a ring; if $R$ is graded we also require $A_i R_j \subseteq A_{i+j} \supseteq R_iA_j$.
  - The graded [Leibniz rule](wikipedia://en/248988) for a map $d\colon A \to A$ on a graded algebra $A$ specifies that $d(a \cdot b) = (da) \cdot b + (-1)^{|a|}a \cdot (db)$.
  - A [differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863), **DG-algebra** or **DGAlgebra** is a graded algebra that is a differential graded module whose differential obeys the graded Leibniz rule.
  - A [homogeneous derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_derivation) on a graded algebra *A* is a homogeneous linear map of grade *d* = |*D*| on *A* such that $D(ab) = D(a)b + \varepsilon^{|a||D|}aD(b), \varepsilon = \pm 1$ acting on homogeneous elements of *A*.
  - A [graded derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29#Graded_derivations) is a sum of homogeneous derivations with the same $\varepsilon$.
  - A **DGA** is an augmented DG-algebra, or **[differential graded augmented algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_graded_augmented_algebra)**, (see [Differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863)).
  - A [superalgebra](wikipedia://en/469950) is a $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded algebra.
    - A [graded-commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded-commutative) superalgebra satisfies the "supercommutative" law $yx = (-1)^{|x| |y|}xy.$ for homogeneous *x*,*y*, where $|a|$ represents the "parity" of $a$, i.e. 0 or 1 depending on the component in which it lies.
  - **CDGA** may refer to the category of augmented differential graded commutative algebras.
- A [graded Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/5475402) is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) that is graded as a vector space by a gradation compatible with its Lie bracket.
  - A [graded Lie superalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_Lie_superalgebra) is a graded Lie algebra with the requirement for anticommutativity of its Lie bracket relaxed.
  - A [supergraded Lie superalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergraded_Lie_superalgebra) is a graded Lie superalgebra with an additional super $\mathbb{Z}_2$-gradation.
  - A [differential graded Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/39908921) is a graded vector space over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero together with a bilinear map $[\ , ]\colon L_i \otimes L_j \to L_{i+j}$ and a differential $d\colon L_i \to L_{i-1}$ satisfying $[x,y] = (-1)^{|x||y|+1}[y,x],$ for any homogeneous elements *x*, *y* in *L*, the "graded [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370)" and the graded Leibniz rule.
- The **Graded Brauer group** is a synonym for the [Brauer–Wall group](wikipedia://en/36842500) $BW(F)$ classifying finite-dimensional graded central [division algebras](wikipedia://en/26549625) over the field *F*.
- An $\mathcal{A}$-[graded category](wikipedia://en/1155685) for a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\mathcal{A}$ is a category $\mathcal{C}$ together with a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $F\colon \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{A}$.
  - A [differential graded category](wikipedia://en/9492439) or **DG category** is a category whose morphism sets form differential graded $\mathbb{Z}$-modules.

In other areas of mathematics:

- [Functionally graded elements](wikipedia://en/17156700) are used in [finite element analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_element_analysis).
- A [graded poset](wikipedia://en/2514970) is a [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) $P$ with a **rank function** $\rho\colon P \to \mathbb{N}$ compatible with the ordering (i.e. $\rho(x) < \rho(y) \implies x < y$) such that $y$ [covers](wikipedia://en/14835049) $x \implies \rho(y) = \rho(x)+1$ .
