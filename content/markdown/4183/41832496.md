# Zero Knowledge Systems

zero-knowledge service

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Zero Knowledge Systems"}

**Zero-Knowledge Systems** (also known as **ZKS**) was a Canadian privacy technology software and services company, best known for the Freedom Network, its [privacy network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy_network). It was founded by brothers Austin Hill & Hamnett Hill and their father Hamnett Hill Sr. (aka Hammie Hill) in 1997. Its headquarters were in [Montreal, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal,_Quebec). Early investors and board members were Mike Santer and Alex Hern co-founder [Inktomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inktomi). The company rebranded under the new name Radialpoint though was no longer a developer of privacy-enhancing technologies.[^o3] Most recently, it was acquired by AppDirect and rebranded as AppHelp.

:::

Zero-Knowledge Systems was one of the first large-scale commercial attempts to combine [cypherpunk](wikipedia://en/37314) principles with a for-profit technology. During its heyday, ZKS captured the media's imagination and successfully drew attention to the privacy risks of unsuspecting internet users.

Being based in Canada allowed it to circumvent the US ban on strong cryptography, considered "munitions" at the time. ZKS was featured in *Wired* magazine as early as 1999.[^o4]

The ZKS Freedom Network was a pioneer of anonymous networking technology, predating the [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_%28anonymity_network%29) network. Some of the enterprise privacy research was also inherited by the IBM Tivoli digital rights management suite.[^o5]

Several of the company's employees were from an academic privacy-enhanced technology background: [Stefan Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Brands) (senior cryptographer) and cypherpunk background [Ian Goldberg](wikipedia://en/829135) (chief scientist) and [Adam Back](wikipedia://en/10348688) (architect & cryptographer). Other employees included [Mike Shaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Shaver) (chief software officer), [Adam Shostack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Shostack), [Anton Stiglic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Stiglic), [Christian Paquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Paquin), Jonathan Wilkins and [Ulf Moller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulf_Moller).[^o6] [Stephanie Perrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Perrin) served as [Chief Privacy Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Privacy_Officer) for the firm before she became Director of Research and Policy at the [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Privacy_Commissioner_of_Canada), and helped to draft [PIPEDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIPEDA), the Canadian privacy law.[^o7]
