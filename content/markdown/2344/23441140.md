# Léon Bottou

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Léon Bottou"}

**Léon-Yves Bottou[^o3]** (; born 1965) is a researcher best known for his work in [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) and [data compression](wikipedia://en/8013). His work presents [stochastic gradient descent](wikipedia://en/1180641) as a fundamental learning algorithm.[^o4] He is also one of the main creators of the [DjVu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DjVu) image compression technology (together with [Yann LeCun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_LeCun) and Patrick Haffner), and the maintainer of [DjVuLibre](https://djvu.sf.net), the open source implementation of DjVu. He is the original developer of the [Lush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lush_%28programming_language%29) programming language.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Léon Bottou was born in France in 1965. He obtained the [Diplôme d'Ingénieur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplôme_d'Ingénieur) from [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in 1987, a Magistère de Mathématiques Fondamentales et Appliquées et d’Informatique from [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1988, a Diplôme d'Études Approndies in Computer Science in 1988, in 1988, and a PhD from [Université Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Paris-Sud) in 1991.[^o7]

In 1988, in collaboration with [Yann LeCun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_LeCun), he published SN, a software package for simulating artificial neural networks.[^o8] His master's thesis[^o9] concerned using [Time Delay Neural Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_delay_neural_network) for [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition).[^o10] He then joined the Adaptive Systems Research Department at [AT&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T) [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Laboratories) in [Holmdel, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmdel,_New_Jersey), where he collaborated with [Vladimir Vapnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vapnik) on local learning algorithms.[^o11]

in 1992, he returned to France and founded Neuristique S.A., a company that produced machine learning tools and one of the first data mining software packages, including Lush, an object-oriented programming language based on [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) and [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29) designed for training and using large-scale neural networks.[^o12] In 1995, he returned to Bell Laboratories, where he developed a number of new machine learning methods, such as Graph Transformer Networks (similar to [conditional random field](wikipedia://en/4118276)), and applied them to handwriting recognition and OCR.[^o13] The bank check recognition system that he helped develop was widely deployed by NCR and other companies, reading over 10% of all the checks in the US in the late 1990s and early 2000s.

In 1996, he joined [AT&T Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Labs) and worked primarily on the [DjVu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DjVu) image compression technology,[^o14] that is used by some websites, notably the [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive), to distribute scanned documents. Between 2002 and 2010, he was a research scientist at NEC Laboratories in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), where he focused on the theory and practice of machine learning with large-scale datasets,[^o15] on-line learning, and stochastic optimization methods.[^o16] He developed the open source software [LaSVM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaSVM) for fast large-scale [support vector machine](wikipedia://en/65309), and [stochastic gradient descent](wikipedia://en/1180641) software for training linear SVM and Conditional Random Fields. In 2010 he joined the Microsoft adCenter in [Redmond, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redmond,_Washington), and in 2012 became a Principal Researcher at [Microsoft Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research) in New York City. In March 2015 he joined [Facebook Artificial Intelligence Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_AI), also in New York City, as a research lead.

His work in gradient descent argued that both stochastic gradient descent and batch gradient descent reach similar levels of loss with the same number of training samples, but SGD is faster when running on large datasets. He also argued that second-order gradient descent methods, such as [quasi-Newton methods](wikipedia://en/7883127), can be beneficial compared to plain SGD. See (Bottou et al 2018)[^o17] for a review.

He was program chair of the 2013 [Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Neural_Information_Processing_Systems) and the 2009 [International Conference on Machine Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Conference_on_Machine_Learning). In 2007, he was received one of the first [Blavatnik Awards for Young Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blavatnik_Awards_for_Young_Scientists) from the [Blavatnik Family Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blavatnik_Family_Foundation) and the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences).
