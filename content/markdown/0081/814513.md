# Theoretical definition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theoretical definition"}

A **theoretical definition** defines a term in an academic discipline, functioning as a proposal to see a phenomenon in a certain way. A theoretical definition is a proposed way of thinking about potentially related events.[^o2][^o18] Theoretical definitions contain *built-in* theories; they cannot be simply reduced to describing a set of observations. The definition may contain implicit [inductions](wikipedia://en/393736) and [deductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive) consequences that are part of the theory.[^o3] A theoretical definition of a term can change, over time, based on the methods in the field that created it.

:::

Without a [falsifiable](wikipedia://en/11283) [operational definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_definition), conceptual definitions assume both knowledge and acceptance of the theories that it depends on.[^o2] A [hypothetical construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothetical_construct) may serve as a theoretical definition, as can a [stipulative definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stipulative_definitions).

## In different fields {#In_different_fields}

### Sciences {#Sciences}

The term [scientific theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_theory) is reserved for concepts that are widely accepted. A [scientific law](wikipedia://en/244629) often refers to regularities that can be expressed by a mathematical statement. However, there is no consensus about the distinction between these terms.[^o6] Every scientific concept must have an [operational definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_definition), however the operational definition can use both direct observations and [latent variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_variables).[^o7]

#### Natural sciences {#Natural_sciences}

In the natural sciences, a concept is an abstract conclusion drawn from observations.[^o7]

#### Social and health sciences {#Social_and_health_sciences}

Social and health sciences interact with non-empirical fields and use both observation based and pre-existing concepts such as intelligence, race, and gender.

- In psychology the term "conceptual definition" is used for a concept variable.[^o10]

### Interdisciplinary {#Interdisciplinary}

Most [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary) fields are designed to address specific real world concerns and the status of theoretical definitions in interdisciplinary fields is still evolving.[^o12]

### In natural science {#In_natural_science}

The definitions of substances as various configurations of atoms are theoretical definitions, as are definitions of [colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color) as specific [wavelengths](wikipedia://en/33125) of reflected light.

#### Physics {#Physics}

The first [postulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postulate) of [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) theory that the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light) in vacuum is the same to all inertial observers (i.e. it is a constant, and therefore a good measure of length). Of interest, this theoretical concept is the basis of an [operational definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_definition) for the length of a [metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) is *"the distance traveled by [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light) in a [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) during a time interval of 1/299,792,458 of a [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second)"*. Thus we have defined 'metre' according to other ideas contained in modern scientific theory. Rejection of the theory underlying a theoretical definition leaves the definition invalid for use in argument with those who reject it — neither side will advance its position by using terms the others do not accept .

[Heat](wikipedia://en/19593167) explains a collection of various laws of nature and that predict certain results.[^o18]

#### Union {#Union}

In psychology, the concept of intelligence is meant to explain correlations in performance on certain cognitive tasks.[^o22] Recent models suggest several cognitive processes may be involved in tasks that have been associated with intelligence.[^o23] However, overall the ["g" or general intelligence factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_intelligence_factor) is relatively supported by research, though there are [challenges](wikipedia://en/374298#Challenges).

### Philosophy {#Philosophy}

Differing theoretical definitions of "thinking" have caused conflict amongst [artificial intelligence philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_artificial_intelligence), illustrated for example by the different responses to the [Chinese room](wikipedia://en/6216) experiment. Some philosophers might call "thought" merely *"having the ability to convince another person that you can think"*. An [operational definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_definition) corresponding to this theoretical definition could be a simple conversation test (e.g. [Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test)). Others believe that better theoretical and operational definitions are required.
