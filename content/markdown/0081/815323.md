# Ted Hill (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ted Hill"}

**Theodore Preston Hill** (born December 28, 1943), [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech), is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing mainly in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). He is an Elected Member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) (1993), and an Elected Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) (1999).

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Hill discovered what many consider to be the definitive proof of [Benford's law](wikipedia://en/36782).[^o4][^o5] He is also known for his research in the theories of [optimal stopping](wikipedia://en/7189886) (including the [secretary problem](wikipedia://en/1971750) and [prophet inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophet_inequality)) and of [fair division](wikipedia://en/669899), in particular the [Hill–Beck land division problem](wikipedia://en/44171936).

Hill has attracted widespread attention for a paper on the [variability hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variability_hypothesis), the theory that men exhibit greater variability than women in genetically controlled traits that he wrote with [Sergei Tabachnikov](wikipedia://en/45658812).[^o6] A later version authored by Hill alone was peer reviewed and accepted by *[The New York Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Journal_of_Mathematics)* and retracted after publication. A revised version, again authored by Hill alone, was subsequently peer reviewed again and published in the *Journal of Interdisciplinary Mathematics*.[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born in [Flatbush, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbush,_New_York), he studied at the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy), and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (M.S. in [Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research)). After graduating from the [U.S. Army Ranger School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Ranger_School) and serving as an Army Captain in the Combat Engineers of the [25th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Infantry_Division_%28United_States%29) in Vietnam, he returned to study mathematics at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) ([Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Scholar)), the [UC Berkeley College of Letters and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley_College_of_Letters_and_Science) (M.A., Ph.D. under advisor [Lester Dubins](wikipedia://en/36027272)), and as NATO/NSF Postdoctoral Fellow at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University).

He spent most of his career as a professor in the [Georgia Institute of Technology College of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology_College_of_Sciences), with temporary appointments at [Arts and Sciences at Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_and_Sciences_at_Washington_University_in_St._Louis), [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University), the [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi_at_Mānoa), the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) (Fulbright Professor), the [University of Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Costa_Rica), the [Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit_Amsterdam), the Mexican Centre for Mathematical Research ([CIMAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIMAT)), and as Gauss Professor in the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities).
