# Tombstone (typography)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tombstone"}

*Image: Halmos symbols.svg.* Various forms of the end-of-proof symbol

In mathematics, the **tombstone**, **halmos**, **end-of-proof**, or **Q.E.D.** symbol "∎" (or "□") is a symbol used to denote the end of a [proof](wikipedia://en/82285), in place of the traditional abbreviation "Q.E.D." for the Latin phrase "*[quod erat demonstrandum](wikipedia://en/25228)*". It is inspired by the [typographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) practice of *end marks*, an element that marks the end of an article.[^o2]

:::

In [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode), it is represented as character 220E. Its graphic form varies, as it may be a hollow or filled rectangle or square.

In [AMS-LaTeX](wikipedia://en/6571457), the symbol is automatically appended at the end of a proof environment `\begin{proof}` ... `\end{proof}`. It can also be obtained from the commands `\qedsymbol`, `\qedhere` or `\qed` (the latter causes the symbol to be right aligned).[^o3]

It is sometimes called a "Halmos finality symbol" or "halmos" after the mathematician [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598), who first used it in a mathematical context in 1950.[^o4] He got the idea of using it from seeing end marks in [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine), that is, [typographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) signs that indicate the end of an article. In his memoir *I Want to Be a Mathematician*, he wrote the following:[^o5] The symbol is definitely not my invention – it appeared in popular magazines (not mathematical ones) before I adopted it, but, once again, I seem to have introduced it into mathematics. It is the symbol that sometimes looks like ▯, and is used to indicate an end, usually the end of a proof. It is most frequently called the 'tombstone', but at least one generous author referred to it as the 'halmos'.
