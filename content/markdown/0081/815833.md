# Fano

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fano"}

**Fano** () is a coastal city and *[comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune)* of the [province of Pesaro and Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pesaro_and_Urbino) in the [Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche) region of [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) located 12 km southeast of [Pesaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesaro) at the point where the *[Via Flaminia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Flaminia)* reaches the [Adriatic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Sea). As of 2021, it has a population of approximately 59,000, smaller than [Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancona) and Pesaro.

:::

## History {#History}

Originally part of ancient [Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche), the settlement was known as **Fanum Fortunae** (‘Temple of Fortune’)[^o4] after a prominent local sanctuary dedicated to the goddess [Fortuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortuna_%28mythology%29). Its first mention in history dates from 49 BC, when [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar) held it, along with [Pisaurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisaurum) and Ancona. [Caesar Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar_Augustus) established a *[colonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonia_%28Roman%29)*, and built a wall, some parts of which remain. In 2 AD Augustus also built an arch (which is still standing) at the entrance to the town.

*Image: Fano Castle.jpg.* The Castle of Fano in a 19th-century etching. The high watchtower was destroyed during World War II.

*Image: Fano malattestianische Burg.JPG.* The Castle of Fano July 2011

In January 271, the [Roman Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Army) defeated the [Alamanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamanni) in the [Battle of Fano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fano) that took place on the banks of the [Metauro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metauro) river just inland of Fano.

Fano was destroyed by [Vitiges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitiges)' [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths) in AD 538. It was rebuilt by the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), becoming the capital of the maritime [Pentapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentapolis) ("Five Cities") that included also [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini), Pesaro, [Senigallia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senigallia) and Ancona. In 754 it was donated to the [Holy See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_See) by the Frankish kings.

The [Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Malatesta) became lords of the city in 1356 with [Galeotto I Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeotto_I_Malatesta), who was nominally only a vicar of the Popes. Among the others, [Pandolfo III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandolfo_III_Malatesta) resided in the city. Under his son, the [condottiero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condottiero) [Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismondo_Pandolfo_Malatesta), Fano was besieged by Papal troops under [Federico III da Montefeltro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_III_da_Montefeltro), and returned to the Papal administration. It was later part of the short-lived state of [Cesare Borgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Borgia), and then part of the [duchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy) of the [della Roveres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Della_Rovere) in the Marche.

During the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) it suffered heavy spoliations; the city had an active role in the [Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risorgimento). In [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) Fano was several times bombed by the [Austro-Hungarian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Navy). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) it was massively bombed by [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) aeroplanes due to hit the strategic railway and street bridges crossing the Metauro river. When the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) withdrew from the town they destroyed all of the bell towers in the town.

During the [1916 Rimini earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_Rimini_earthquakes), two spires of [Fano Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_Cathedral) collapsed, a large stucco angel fell in the Sant'Agostino, Fano, and part of the belltower wall of the Santa Maria del Gonfalone collapsed. Part of a wall of the Church of San Paterniano collapsed.[^o5] The earthquake of 16 August caused an exodus from the town.[^o6]

In 2026, archaeological excavations in the piazza at Fano found the remains of the civic basilica described by [Vitruvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitruvius) in his treatise *[De architectura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_architectura)*.[^o7]

## Main sights {#Main_sights}

### Religious structures {#Religious_structures}

- [Fano Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_Cathedral): (12th century), which was erected over a pre-existing cathedral destroyed by a fire in 1111. The current façade is from the 1920s restoration, but is similar to the original. The interior has a nave and two aisles.
- The town's namesake temple of Fortuna is identified in the remains of a monumental [podium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podium) located beneath the medieval convent of Saint Augustine.
- [San Domenico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Domenico,_Fano)
- [San Pietro in Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pietro_in_Valle,_Fano):
- [San Paterniano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Paterniano,_Fano): (16th century) with a Renaissance cloister.
- [San Francesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francesco,_Fano): church housing the tombs of Pandolfo III Malatesta (designed by [Leon Battista Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Battista_Alberti)) and his first wife Paola Bianca Malatesta.
- [Santa Maria Nuova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Nuova,_Fano): (1521) Church has an ancient portal and two works by [Perugino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Perugino) (*[Annunciation of Fano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation_of_Fano)* and [Fano Altarpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_Altarpiece), the latter including perhaps an intervention by [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael)).

Outside the city, in the place called Bellocchi, is the church of [St. Sebastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Sebastian) (16th century), for the construction of which parts of the ancient cathedral were used.

### Secular structures {#Secular_structures}

*Image: Aerial image of Fano (view from the east).jpg.* Aerial view of Fano

- [Arco d'Augusto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_of_Augustus_%28Fano%29): The upper story of this Roman gate was destroyed in a siege conducted on the order of [Pope Pius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_II) in 1463, although a bas-relief of it was made by Bernardino di Pietro da Carona in 1513[^o11] on an adjacent wall of the annexed church and the [loggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggia) of [St. Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael), the former having a noteworthy Renaissance portal.
- *Corte Malatestiana*: built after 1357 by Galeotto I Malatesta. The 14th-century section includes a great vaulted hall (probably part of the first residence of the Malatesta in the city) and a small turret. The modern part was built under Pandolfo III in 1413–23. The current edifice was heavily restored in the 20th century, but the original [mullioned windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullioned_window) in [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) style, as well as the staircase and the loggia from a 16th-century restoration. Also noteworthy is the Borgia-Cybo Arch (late 15th century). The palace is connected to the *Palazzo del Podestà* by a modern bridge, probably present also in the original structure.
- *Rocca Malatestiana*: (Malatesta Castle) was partially destroyed in 1944. The most ancient part dates probably from pre-existing Roman and medieval fortifications. The castle in its current form was begun in 1433 or 1438 by [Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismondo_Pandolfo_Malatesta). The now missing *[mastio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep)* was erected in 1452. Here, Sigismondo's son, [Roberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Malatesta), was besieged by Papal Troops in 1463 and signed the peace treaty that ended the Malatesta domination of Fano.
- *Museo Civico of Fano*: (Archeological Museum and Art Gallery), located inside the *Palazzo Malatestiano*, is a museum with archaeological findings, coins, medals, and an art gallery with works by [Guido Reni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Reni), [Domenichino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenichino), [Guercino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guercino), [Michele Giambono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Giambono), and [Giovanni Santi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Santi).[^o12]
- *Palazzo del [Podestà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podestà)* or *della Ragione* (built from 1229 in Romanesque-Gothic style). The interiors are in Neoclassicist style, and it houses the [Teatro della Fortuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_della_Fortuna).[^o13]
- *Fontana della Fortuna* (Fountain of Fortune) (17th century).

## Culture {#Culture}

- *Fano dei Cesari* is an annual week-long festival held in July or August,[^o15] featuring cultural events ending with a parade in Roman costumes and chariot races.
- The [Fano Jazz by The Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_Jazz_by_The_Sea) festival is held annually for one week.
- The library, the [Biblioteca Federiciana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Federiciana), was established on 17 November 1720.[^o16]

## Sports {#Sports}

The Fano Ultimate Frisbee Association, established in 2001, fields four teams—Croccali (mixed), Mirine (women), Spaccamadoni (men) and Angry Gulls (juniors)—and has won eight Italian championships.

[Alma Juventus Fano 1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_Juventus_Fano_1906) is the local [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) team who play at the Stadio Raffaele Mancini.
