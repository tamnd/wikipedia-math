# Monkey saddle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monkey saddle"}

*Image: Monkey Saddle Surface (Shaded).png.* The monkey saddle

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **monkey saddle** is the [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) defined by the equation

:::

$$z = x^3 - 3xy^2, \,$$

or in [cylindrical coordinates](wikipedia://en/176733)

$$z = \rho^3 \cos(3\varphi).$$

It belongs to the class of [saddle surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_surface), and its name derives from the observation that a [saddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle) used by a [monkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey) would require two depressions for its legs and one for its tail. The point $(0,0,0)$ on the monkey saddle corresponds to a [degenerate critical point](wikipedia://en/1951424) of the function $z(x,y)$ at $(0, 0)$. The monkey saddle has an isolated [umbilical point](wikipedia://en/9757480) with zero [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) at the origin, while the curvature is strictly negative at all other points.

One can relate the rectangular and cylindrical equations using [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $x+iy = r e^{i\varphi}:$

$$z = x^3 - 3xy^2 = \operatorname{Re} [(x+iy)^3] = \operatorname{Re}[r^3 e^{3i\varphi}] = r^3\cos(3\varphi).$$

By replacing 3 in the cylindrical equation with any integer $k \geq 1,$ one can create a saddle with $k$ depressions. [^o2]

Another orientation of the monkey saddle is the *Smelt petal* defined by $x+y+z+xyz=0,$ so that the *z-*axis of the monkey saddle corresponds to the direction $(1,1,1)$ in the Smelt petal.[^o3][^o4]

Another function, which has not three but four areas - in each quadrant of the $\mathbb R^2$, in which the function goes to minus infinity, is given by $z = x^4 - 6x^2y^2 + y^4$.

*Image: Shape\_petal.svg.* Smelt petal: $x + y + z + xyz = 0$

## Horse saddle {#Horse_saddle}

The term *horse saddle* may be used in contrast to monkey saddle, to designate an ordinary saddle surface in which *z*(*x*,*y*) has a [saddle point](wikipedia://en/640249), a local minimum or maximum in every direction of the *xy*-plane. In contrast, the monkey saddle has a stationary [point of inflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_inflection) in every direction.
