# Van Wijngaarden transformation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Van Wijngaarden transformation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), the van Wijngaarden transformation is a variant on the [Euler transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_transform) used to accelerate the convergence of an [alternating series](wikipedia://en/494308).

:::

One algorithm to compute [Euler's transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_transform) runs as follows:

> Compute a row of partial sums
>
> $$s_{0,k} = \sum_{n=0}^k(-1)^n a_n$$
>
> and form rows of averages between neighbors
>
> $$s_{j+1,k} = \frac{s_{j,k}+s_{j,k+1}}2$$
>
> The first column $s_{j,0}$ then contains the partial sums of the Euler transform.

[Adriaan van Wijngaarden](wikipedia://en/943404)'s contribution was to point out that it is better not to carry this procedure through to the very end, but to stop two-thirds of the way.[^o2] If $a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_{12}$ are available, then $s_{8,4}$ is almost always a better approximation to the sum than $s_{12,0}$. In many cases the diagonal terms do not converge in one cycle so process of averaging is to be repeated with diagonal terms by bringing them in a row. (For example, this will be needed in a geometric series with ratio $-4$.) This process of successive averaging of the average of partial sum can be replaced by using the formula to calculate the diagonal term.

For a simple-but-concrete example, recall the [Leibniz formula for π](wikipedia://en/1301559):

::: {#o3 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$1 - \frac 1 3 + \frac 1 5 - \frac 1 7 + \cdots = \frac \pi 4 = 0.7853981\ldots \tag{1}$$

:::
