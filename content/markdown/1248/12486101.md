# Global game

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Global game"}

In [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), **global games** are games of incomplete information where players receive possibly-correlated signals of the underlying state of the world. Global games were originally defined by Carlsson and [van Damme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_van_Damme) (1993).[^o2]

:::

The most important practical application of global games has been the study of [crises in financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_crisis) such as [bank runs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_crisis), [currency crises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_crisis), and [bubbles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_%28economics%29). However, they have other relevant applications such as investments with payoff complementarities, [beauty contests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_contests), [political riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_riots) and [revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions), and any other economic situation which displays [strategic complementarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_complementarity).

## Global games in models of currency crises {#Global_games_in_models_of_currency_crises}

[Stephen Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Morris_%28game_theorist%29) and [Hyun Song Shin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyun_Song_Shin) (1998) considered a stylized currency crises model, in which traders observe the relevant fundamentals with small noise, and show that this leads to the selection of a unique equilibrium.[^o4] This result overturns the result in models of [complete information](wikipedia://en/1603001), which feature multiple equilibria.

One concern with the robustness of this result is that the introduction of a theory of prices in global coordination games may reintroduce multiplicity of equilibria.[^o5] This concern was addressed in [Angeletos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George-Marios_Angeletos) and [Werning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iván_Werning) (2006) as well as [Hellwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Hellwig) and coauthors (2006).[^o6][^o7] They show that equilibrium multiplicity may be restored by the existence of prices acting as an endogenous public signal, provided that [private information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy) is sufficiently precise.
