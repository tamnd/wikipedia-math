# Eutactic star

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eutactic star"}

*Image: Eutactic star 3D.png.* A eutactic star consisting of 5 pairs of vectors in three-dimensional space (*n* = 3, *s* = 5)

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **eutactic star** is a [geometrical figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrical_figure) in a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). A star is a figure consisting of any number of opposing pairs of [vectors](wikipedia://en/32533) (or arms) issuing from a central origin. A star is eutactic if it is the [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) of plus and minus the set of [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005) vectors (i.e., the vertices of a [cross-polytope](wikipedia://en/716401)) from a higher-dimensional space onto a [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_subspace). Such stars were called "eutactic" – meaning "well-situated" or "well-arranged" – by (Schläfli 1901, p. 134) because, for a common [scalar multiple](wikipedia://en/3588331), their vectors are projections of an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Eutactic star.png.* A eutactic star in the plane (*n* = 2, *s* = 4)

A *star* is here defined as a set of 2*s* vectors *A* = ±**a**<sub>1</sub>, ..., ±**a**<sub>*s*</sub> issuing from a particular origin in a Euclidean space of dimension *n* ≤ *s*. A star is eutactic if the **a**<sub>*i*</sub> are the projections onto *n* dimensions of a set of mutually [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) equal vectors **b**<sub>1</sub>, ..., **b**<sub>*s*</sub> issuing from a particular origin in Euclidean *s*-dimensional space.[^o6] The configuration of 2*s* vectors in the *s*-dimensional space *B* = ±**b**<sub>1</sub>, ... , ±**b**<sub>*s*</sub> is known as a *cross*. Given these definitions, a eutactic star is, concisely, a star produced by the orthogonal projection of a cross.

An equivalent definition, first mentioned by [Schläfli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli),[^o7] stipulates that a star is eutactic if a constant *ζ* exists such that

$$\sum_{k=1}^s(\mathbf{a}_k \cdot \mathbf{v})^2 = \zeta |\mathbf{v}|^2$$

for every vector **v**. The existence of such a constant requires that the sum of the squares of the orthogonal projections of *A* on a line be equal in all directions.[^o8] In general,

$$\zeta = \sum_{k=1}^s |\mathbf{a}_k|^2/n.$$

A *normalised* eutactic star is a projected cross composed of [unit vectors](wikipedia://en/167053).[^o6][^o9] Eutactic stars are often considered in *n* = 3 dimensions because of their connection with the study of [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra).

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Hadwiger's principal theorem"}

## Hadwiger's principal theorem {#Hadwiger's_principal_theorem}

Let *T* be the [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) defined for vectors **x** by

$$T\mathbf{x} = \sum_{k=1}^s \mathbf{a}_k (\mathbf{a}_k\cdot\mathbf{x}),$$

where the **a**<sub>*j*</sub> form any collection of *s* vectors in the *n*-dimensional Euclidean space. [Hadwiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadwiger)'s principal theorem states that the vectors ±**a**<sub>1</sub>, ..., ±**a**<sub>*s*</sub> form a eutactic star [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) there is a constant *ζ* such that *T***x** = *ζ***x** for every **x**.[^o6][^o12] The vectors form a normalized eutactic star precisely when *T* is the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator) – when *ζ* = 1.

Equivalently, the star is normalized eutactic if and only if the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) **A** = \[**a**<sub>1</sub> ... **a**<sub>*s*</sub>\], whose columns are the vectors **a**<sub>*k*</sub>, has [orthonormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal) rows. A proof may be given in one direction by completing the rows of this matrix to an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) of $\mathbb{R}^s$, and in the other by orthogonally projecting onto the *n*-dimensional subspace spanned by the first *n* Cartesian coordinate vectors.

[Hadwiger's theorem](wikipedia://en/629554) implies the equivalence of Schläfli's stipulation and the geometrical definition of a eutactic star, by the [polarization identity](wikipedia://en/2047965). Furthermore, both Schläfli's identity and Hadwiger's theorem give the same value of the constant *ζ*.

:::

::: {#o14 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Eutactic stars are useful largely because of their relationship with the geometry of [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471) and [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of [orthogonal transformations](wikipedia://en/1382381). Schläfli showed early on that the vectors from the center of any [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) to its vertices form a eutactic star. Brauer and Coxeter proved the following generalization:[^o15]

> A star is eutactic if it is transformed to itself by some irreducible group of orthogonal transformations that acts transitively on pairs of opposite vectors.

An irreducible group here means a group that does not leave any nontrivial proper subspace invariant (see [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982)). Since the set theoretic union of two eutactic stars is itself eutactic (a consequence of Hadwiger's principal theorem), it can be concluded that, in general:[^o8]

> A star is eutactic if it is transformed to itself by some irreducible group of orthogonal transformations.

Eutactic stars may be used to validate the eutaxy of any form in general. According to [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328): "A form is eutactic if and only if its minimal vectors are [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) to the vectors of a eutactic star."[^o8]
