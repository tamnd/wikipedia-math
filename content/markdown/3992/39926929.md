# Marginal structural model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marginal structural model"}

**Marginal structural models** are a class of [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576) used for [causal inference](wikipedia://en/37103476) in [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997).[^o2][^o3] Such models handle the issue of time-dependent confounding in evaluation of the efficacy of interventions by [inverse probability weighting](wikipedia://en/33350282) for receipt of treatment, they allow us to estimate the average causal effects. For instance, in the study of the effect of [zidovudine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zidovudine) in [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS)-related mortality, [CD4 lymphocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD4) is used both for treatment indication, is influenced by treatment, and affects survival. Time-dependent confounders are typically highly prognostic of health outcomes and applied in dosing or indication for certain therapies, such as body weight or lab values such as [alanine aminotransferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanine_aminotransferase) or [bilirubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilirubin).

:::

The first marginal structural models were introduced in 2000. The works of [James Robins](wikipedia://en/16311183), [Babette Brumback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babette_Brumback), and [Miguel Hernán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Hernán) provided an intuitive theory and an easy-to-implement software which made them popular for the analysis of longitudinal data.[^o4]
