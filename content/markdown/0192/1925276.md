# Carl Gustav Axel Harnack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carl Gustav Axel Harnack"}

*Image: Axel harnack.jpg.* Axel Harnack

**Carl Gustav Axel Harnack** (7 May, [Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorpat) (now Tartu) – 3 April 1888, [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden)) was a [Baltic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_German) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who contributed to [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342). [Harnack's inequality](wikipedia://en/2976061) applied to [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762). He also worked on the [real algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/7252198) of [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691), proving [Harnack's curve theorem](wikipedia://en/5209604) for real [plane algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curves).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was the son of the [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) [Theodosius Harnack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_Harnack)[^o3] and the twin brother of theologian [Adolf von Harnack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_von_Harnack) (who long outlived him) - all of them from [Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorpat), now known as Tartu, in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). After his studies at the [Imperial University of Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_University_of_Dorpat) (where his father was a professor). In 1873 he moved to [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen) to become a student of [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887). He published his Ph.D. thesis in 1875 and received the right to teach (venia legendi) at the University of [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) the same year. One year later he accepted a position at the Technical University of [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt). In 1877 he married Elisabeth von Öttingen, and they moved to [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden), where he acquired a professorship in the Polytechnikum, which became a technical university in 1890. Harnack suffered from health problems from 1882 onwards, forcing him to spend long periods in a [sanatorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanatorium). He published 29 scientific articles and was a well-known mathematician at the time of his death.

The various [Harnack inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harnack_inequality) in harmonic analysis and in related discrete and probabilistic contexts are named after him, as are [Harnack's curve theorem](wikipedia://en/5209604) and [Harnack's principle](wikipedia://en/1433747). The [Harnack medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harnack_medal) of the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society) is named after his brother, [Adolf von Harnack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_von_Harnack).
