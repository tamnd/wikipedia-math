# Bruno Buchberger

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Bruno Buchberger"}

**Bruno Buchberger** (born 22 October 1942) is Professor of Computer Mathematics at [Johannes Kepler University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler_University_Linz) in [Linz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linz), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria). In his 1965 [Ph.D. thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D._thesis), he created the theory of [Gröbner bases](wikipedia://en/357328),[^o4] and has developed this theory throughout his career. He named these objects after his advisor [Wolfgang Gröbner](wikipedia://en/1049506). Since 1995, he has been active in the Theorema project at the [University of Linz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Linz).

:::

## Career {#Career}

In 1987, Buchberger founded and chaired the [Research Institute for Symbolic Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Institute_for_Symbolic_Computation) (RISC) at Johannes Kepler University. In 1985, he started the *[Journal of Symbolic Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Symbolic_Computation)*, which has now become the premier publication in the field of [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805).

Buchberger also conceived [Softwarepark Hagenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softwarepark_Hagenberg) in 1989 and, since then, has been directing the expansion of this Austrian technology park for software.

In 2014 he became a member of the *Global Digital Mathematical Library Working Group* [^o6] of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868).

Beyond the applications of Gröbner bases in computational algebra, Buchberger has also applied them to problems of automated theorem proving in [systems theory](wikipedia://en/29238),[^o7] [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927),[^o8] and the [mathematics of origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_origami).[^o9]

## Awards {#Awards}

- [Wilhelm Exner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Exner_Medal) (1995).[^o11]
- [Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Theory_and_Practice_Award) (2007). For theory of Gröbner bases.[^o12]
- *Golden Medal of Honor* by the Upper Austrian Government
- Honorary doctorates from the Universities of [Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nijmegen) (1993), [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_University_of_Timișoara) (2000), [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) (2005), [Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) (2011), and [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Innsbruck) (2012).
- [Herbrand Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbrand_Award) for Distinguished Contributions to Automated Reasoning (2018)[^o13]
