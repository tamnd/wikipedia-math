# Von Mangoldt function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Von Mangoldt function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **von Mangoldt function** is an [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170) named after [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) mathematician [Hans von Mangoldt](wikipedia://en/1928734). It is an example of an important arithmetic function that is neither [multiplicative](wikipedia://en/20059) nor [additive](wikipedia://en/87372).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The von Mangoldt function, denoted by $\Lambda(n)$, is defined as

$$\Lambda(n) = \begin{cases} \log p & \text{if }n=p^k \text{ for some prime } p \text{ and integer } k \ge 1, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

The first few values of $\Lambda(n)$ are

$$0 , \log 2 , \log 3 , \log 2 , \log 5 , 0 , \log 7 , \log 2 , \log 3, 0, \log 11, 0,\dots$$

## Properties {#Properties}

The von Mangoldt function satisfies the identity[^o6][^o7]

$$\log(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \Lambda(d).$$

The sum is taken over all [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $d$ that [divide](wikipedia://en/53696) $n$. This is proved by the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556), since the terms that are not powers of primes are equal to $0$. For example, consider the case $n = 12 = 2^{2} \times 3$. Then

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{d \mid 12} \Lambda(d) &= \Lambda(1) + \Lambda(2) + \Lambda(3) + \Lambda(4) + \Lambda(6) + \Lambda(12) \\ &= \Lambda(1) + \Lambda(2) + \Lambda(3) + \Lambda \left (2^2 \right ) + \Lambda(2 \times 3) + \Lambda \left (2^2 \times 3 \right) \\ &= 0 + \log(2) + \log(3) + \log(2) + 0 + 0 \\ &=\log (2 \times 3 \times 2) \\ &= \log(12). \end{aligned}$$

By [Möbius inversion](wikipedia://en/20963), we have

$$\Lambda (n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d) \log\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)$$

where $\mu(d)$ is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961). Then by using the [product rule](wikipedia://en/248988) for the logarithm we get[^o7][^o8][^o9]

$$\Lambda (n) = - \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d) \log(d) \ .$$

For all $x\ge 1$, we have[^o10]

$$\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}=\log x+O(1).$$

Also, there exist positive constants $c_{1}$ and $c_{2}$ such that

$$\psi(x)\le c_1x,$$

for all $x\ge 1$, and

$$\psi(x)\ge c_2x,$$

for all sufficiently large $x$.

:::

## Dirichlet series {#Dirichlet_series}

The von Mangoldt function plays an important role in the theory of [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258), and in particular, the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). For example, one has

$$\log \zeta(s)=\sum_{n=2}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{\log(n)}\,\frac{1}{n^s}, \qquad \text{Re}(s) > 1.$$

The [logarithmic derivative](wikipedia://en/470327) is then[^o12]

$$\frac {\zeta^\prime(s)}{\zeta(s)} = -\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}.$$

These are special cases of a more general relation on Dirichlet series. If one has

$$F(s) =\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{f(n)}{n^s}$$

for a [completely multiplicative function](wikipedia://en/1055940) $f(n)$, and the series converges for $\operatorname{Re}(s) > \sigma_{0}$, then

$$\frac {F^\prime(s)}{F(s)} = - \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{f(n)\Lambda(n)}{n^s}$$

converges for $\operatorname{Re}(s) > \sigma_{0}$.

## Chebyshev function {#Chebyshev_function}

The second [Chebyshev function](wikipedia://en/5793598) $\psi(x)$ is the [summatory function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summatory_function) of the von Mangoldt function:[^o14]

$$\psi(x) = \sum_{p^k\le x}\log p=\sum_{n \leq x} \Lambda(n) \ .$$

It was introduced by [Pafnuty Chebyshev](wikipedia://en/149568) who used it to show that the true order of the prime counting function $\pi(x)$ is $x/\log x$. Von Mangoldt provided a rigorous proof of an explicit formula for $\psi(x)$ involving a sum over the non-trivial zeros of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). This was an important part of the first proof of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).

The [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) of the Chebyshev function can be found by applying [Perron's formula](wikipedia://en/4928530):

$$\frac{\zeta^\prime(s)}{\zeta(s)} = - s\int_1^\infty \frac{\psi(x)}{x^{s+1}}\,dx$$

which holds for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$.

## Exponential series {#Exponential_series}

*Image: Mangoldt-series.svg.*

[Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Littlewood) examined the series[^o16]

$$F(y)=\sum_{n=2}^\infty \left(\Lambda(n)-1\right) e^{-ny}$$

in the limit $y \to 0^{+}$. Assuming the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125), they demonstrate that

$$F(y)=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}\right)\quad \text{and}\quad F(y)=\Omega_\pm\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}\right)$$

In particular this function is oscillatory with diverging [oscillations](wikipedia://en/475449): there exists a value $K > 0$ such that both inequalities

$$F(y)< -\frac{K}{\sqrt{y}}, \quad \text{ and } \quad F(z)> \frac{K}{\sqrt{z}}$$

hold infinitely often in any neighbourhood of 0. The graphic to the right indicates that this behaviour is not at first numerically obvious: the oscillations are not clearly seen until the series is summed in excess of 100 million terms, and are only readily visible when $y < 10^{-5}$.

## Riesz mean {#Riesz_mean}

The [Riesz mean](wikipedia://en/5811855) of the von Mangoldt function is given by

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{n\le \lambda} \left(1-\frac{n}{\lambda}\right)^\delta \Lambda(n) &= -\frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty} \frac{\Gamma(1+\delta)\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(1+\delta+s)} \frac{\zeta^\prime(s)}{\zeta(s)} \lambda^s ds \\ &= \frac{\lambda}{1+\delta} + \sum_\rho \frac{\Gamma(1+\delta)\Gamma(\rho)}{\Gamma(1+\delta+\rho)} + \sum_n c_n \lambda^{-n}. \end{aligned}$$

Here, $\lambda$ and $\delta$ are numbers characterizing the Riesz mean. One must take $c > 1$. The sum over $\rho$ is the sum over the zeroes of the Riemann zeta function, and

$$\sum_n c_n \lambda^{-n}\,$$

can be shown to be a convergent series for $\lambda > 1$.

## Approximation by Riemann zeta zeros {#Approximation_by_Riemann_zeta_zeros}

*Image: Real part of n raised to first zeta zero.svg.* The first Riemann zeta zero wave in the sum that approximates the von Mangoldt function

There is an explicit formula for the summatory Mangoldt function $\psi(x)$ given by[^o19]

$$\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\zeta(\rho)=0}\frac{x^\rho}\rho -\log(2\pi).$$

If we separate out the trivial zeros of the zeta function, which are the negative even integers, we obtain

$$\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\zeta(\rho)=0,\ 0<\Re(\rho)<1}\frac{x^\rho}\rho -\log(2\pi)-\frac12\log(1-x^{-2}).$$

(The sum is not absolutely convergent, so we take the zeros in order of the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of their imaginary part.)

In the opposite direction, in 1911 E. Landau proved that for any fixed t > 1[^o20]

$$\sum_{0<\gamma \leq T} t^\rho=\frac{-T}{2 \pi} \Lambda(t)+\mathcal{O}(\log T)$$

(We use the notation ρ = β + iγ for the non-trivial zeros of the zeta function.)

*Image: Von Mangoldt function Fourier transform zeta zero duality.PNG.* (Left) The von Mangoldt function, approximated by zeta zero waves.(Right) The Fourier transform of the von Mangoldt function gives a spectrum with imaginary parts of Riemann zeta zeros as spikes.

Therefore, if we use Riemann notation α = −i(ρ − 1/2) we have that the sum over nontrivial zeta zeros expressed as

$$\lim _{T \rightarrow+\infty} \frac{1}{T} \sum_{0<\gamma \leq T} \cos (\alpha \log t)=-\frac{\Lambda(t)}{2 \pi \sqrt{t}}$$

peaks at primes and powers of primes.

The [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the von Mangoldt function gives a spectrum with spikes at ordinates equal to the imaginary parts of the Riemann zeta function zeros. This is sometimes called a duality.

## Generalized von Mangoldt function {#Generalized_von_Mangoldt_function}

The functions

$$\Lambda_k(n)=\sum\limits_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\log^k(n/d),$$

where $\mu$ denotes the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) and $k$ denotes a positive integer, generalize the von Mangoldt function.[^o22] The function $\Lambda_1$ is the ordinary von Mangoldt function $\Lambda$.
