# Ben Green (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ben Green"}

**Ben Joseph Green** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (born 27 February 1977) is a British mathematician specialising in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is the [Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professor_of_Pure_Mathematics) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Ben Green was born on 27 February 1977 in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol), England. He studied at local schools in Bristol, [Bishop Road Primary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Road_Primary_School) and [Fairfield Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_Grammar_School), competing in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in 1994 and 1995, winning a silver medal both times. He entered [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1995 and completed his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1998, winning the [Senior Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) title. He stayed on for [Part III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos) and earned his doctorate under the supervision of [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715), with a thesis entitled *Topics in arithmetic combinatorics* (2003). During his PhD he spent a year as a visiting student at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He was a research Fellow at Trinity College, Cambridge between 2001 and 2005, before becoming a Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) from January 2005 to September 2006 and then the first [Herchel Smith Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herchel_Smith_Professor_of_Pure_Mathematics) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) from September 2006 to August 2013. He became the [Waynflete Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professorship) of Pure Mathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) on 1 August 2013. He was also a Research Fellow of the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) and held various positions at institutes such as [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), and [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

The majority of Green's research is in the fields of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and [additive combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_Combinatorics), but he also has results in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) and in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890). His best known theorem, proved jointly with his frequent collaborator [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498), states that there exist arbitrarily long [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389) in the [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666): this is now known as the [Green–Tao theorem](wikipedia://en/7006166).[^o6]

Amongst Green's early results in additive combinatorics are an improvement of a result of [Jean Bourgain](wikipedia://en/1024131) of the size of arithmetic progressions in [sumsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumset),[^o7] as well as a proof of the [Cameron–Erdős conjecture](wikipedia://en/13587617) on sum-free sets of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474).[^o8] He also proved an arithmetic regularity lemma[^o9] for functions defined on the first $N$ natural numbers, somewhat analogous to the [Szemerédi regularity lemma](wikipedia://en/1962417) for graphs.

From 2004–2010, in joint work with [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) and [Tamar Ziegler](wikipedia://en/47407832), he developed so-called [higher order Fourier analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_order_Fourier_analysis). This theory relates [Gowers norms](wikipedia://en/38041703) with objects known as [nilsequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilsequences). The theory derives its name from these nilsequences, which play an analogous role to the role that [characters](wikipedia://en/1384568) play in classical [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659). Green and Tao used higher order Fourier analysis to present a new method for counting the number of solutions to simultaneous equations in certain sets of integers, including in the primes.[^o10] This generalises the classical approach using [Hardy–Littlewood circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_circle_method). Many aspects of this theory, including the quantitative aspects of the inverse theorem for the Gowers norms,[^o11] are still the subject of ongoing research.

Green has also collaborated with [Emmanuel Breuillard](wikipedia://en/36434056) on topics in group theory. In particular, jointly with [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498), they proved a structure theorem[^o12] for [approximate groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximate_group), generalising the [Freiman-Ruzsa](wikipedia://en/2240299) theorem on sets of integers with small doubling. Green also has worked, jointly with [Kevin Ford](wikipedia://en/55700462) and [Sean Eberhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Eberhard), on the theory of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901), in particular on what proportion of its elements fix a set of size $k$.[^o13]

Green and Tao also have a paper[^o14] on algebraic [combinatorial geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), resolving the Dirac-Motzkin conjecture (see [Sylvester–Gallai theorem](wikipedia://en/1052632#The_number_of_ordinary_lines)). In particular they prove that, given any collection of $n$ points in the plane that are not all [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear), if $n$ is large enough then there must exist at least $n/2$ lines in the plane containing exactly two of the points.

[Kevin Ford](wikipedia://en/55700462), Ben Green, [Sergei Konyagin](wikipedia://en/29007447), [James Maynard](wikipedia://en/42512102) and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498), initially in two separate research groups and then in combination, improved the lower bound for the size of the longest gap between two consecutive primes of size at most $X$.[^o15] The form of the previously best-known bound, essentially due to [Rankin](wikipedia://en/5884314), had not been improved for 76 years.

More recently Green has considered questions in arithmetic [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175). Together with [Tom Sanders](wikipedia://en/36766438) he proved that, if a sufficiently large [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) of prime order is coloured with a fixed number of colours, then the field has elements $x,y$ such that $x, y, x{+}y, xy$ all have the same colour.[^o16]

Green has also been involved with the new developments of Croot-Lev-Pach-Ellenberg-Gijswijt on applying the [polynomial method](wikipedia://en/62461655) to bound the size of subsets of a finite [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) without solutions to [linear equations](wikipedia://en/17570). He adapted these methods to prove, in function fields, a strong version of [Sárközy's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furstenberg–Sárközy_theorem).[^o17]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Green has been a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) since 2010,[^o19] and a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) since 2012.[^o20] Green was chosen by the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973) to deliver a [Gauss Lectureship](wikipedia://en/48440954) in 2013. He has received several awards:

- 2004: [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167)
- 2005: [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize)
- 2005: [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913)[^o21]
- 2007: [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize)
- 2008: [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) prize recipient
- 2014: [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal), awarded by the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).
- 2019: [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097)
