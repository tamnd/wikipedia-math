# Per Martin-Löf

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Per Martin-Löf"}

**Per Erik Rutger Martin-Löf** (;[^o4] ;[^o5] born 8 May 1942) is a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), and [mathematical statistician](wikipedia://en/888711). He is internationally renowned for his work on the foundations of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), statistics, mathematical logic, and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). Since the late 1970s, Martin-Löf's publications have been mainly in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065). In [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), Martin-Löf has wrestled with the philosophy of [logical consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624) and [judgment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl#Philosophy_of_logic_and_mathematics), partly inspired by the work of [Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano), [Frege](wikipedia://en/48416), and [Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl). In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), Martin-Löf has been active in developing [intuitionistic type theory](wikipedia://en/345023) as a [constructive](wikipedia://en/21059) [foundation of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358); Martin-Löf's work on type theory has influenced [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science).[^o6]

:::

Until his retirement in 2009,[^o7] Per Martin-Löf held a joint chair for Mathematics and Philosophy at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University).[^o8]

His brother [Anders Martin-Löf](wikipedia://en/5751250) was emeritus professor of mathematical statistics at Stockholm University; the two brothers have collaborated in research in probability and statistics. The research of Anders and Per Martin-Löf has influenced statistical theory, especially concerning [exponential families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_families), the [expectation–maximization method](wikipedia://en/470752) for [missing data](wikipedia://en/7859676), and [model selection](wikipedia://en/3664073).[^o9]

Per Martin-Löf received his PhD in 1970 from [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University), under [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161).[^o10]

Martin-Löf is an enthusiastic [bird-watcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird-watcher); his first scientific publication was on the mortality rates of [ringed birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_ringing).[^o11]

## Randomness and Kolmogorov complexity {#Randomness_and_Kolmogorov_complexity}

In 1964 and 1965, Martin-Löf studied in Moscow under the supervision of [Andrei N. Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_N._Kolmogorov). He wrote a 1966 article *The definition of random sequences* that gave the first suitable definition of a random sequence.[^o13]

Earlier researchers such as [Richard von Mises](wikipedia://en/821517) had attempted to formalize the notion of a [test for randomness](wikipedia://en/9296426) in order to define a random sequence as one that passed all tests for randomness; however, the precise notion of a randomness test was left vague. Martin-Löf's key insight was to use the [theory of computation](wikipedia://en/30402) to define formally the notion of a test for randomness. This contrasts with the idea of randomness in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934); in that theory, no particular element of a sample space can be said to be random.

Martin-Löf randomness has since been shown to admit many equivalent characterizations — in terms of [compression](wikipedia://en/8013), randomness tests, and [gambling](wikipedia://en/11921) — that bear little outward resemblance to the original definition, but each of which satisfies our intuitive notion of properties that random sequences ought to have: random sequences should be incompressible, they should pass statistical tests for randomness, and it should be impossible to make money [betting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betting) on them. The existence of these multiple definitions of Martin-Löf randomness, and the stability of these definitions under different models of computation, give evidence that Martin-Löf randomness is a fundamental property of mathematics and not an accident of Martin-Löf's particular model. The thesis that the definition of Martin-Löf randomness "correctly" captures the intuitive notion of randomness has been called the "Martin-Löf–[Chaitin](wikipedia://en/12308) Thesis"; it is somewhat similar to the [Church–Turing thesis](wikipedia://en/6854).[^o14]

Following Martin-Löf's work, [algorithmic information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_information_theory) defines a random string as one that cannot be produced from any computer program that is shorter than the string ([Chaitin–Kolmogorov randomness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitin–Kolmogorov_randomness)); i.e. a string whose [Kolmogorov complexity](wikipedia://en/1635) is at least the length of the string. This is a different meaning from the usage of the term in statistics. Whereas statistical randomness refers to the *process* that produces the string (e.g. flipping a coin to produce each bit will randomly produce a string), algorithmic randomness refers to the *string itself*. Algorithmic information theory separates random from nonrandom strings in a way that is relatively invariant to the [model of computation](wikipedia://en/1773278) being used.

An [algorithmically random sequence](wikipedia://en/4257548) is an *infinite* sequence of characters, all of whose prefixes (except possibly a finite number of exceptions) are strings that are "close to" algorithmically random (their length is within a constant of their Kolmogorov complexity).

## Mathematical statistics {#Mathematical_statistics}

Per Martin-Löf has done important research in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), which (in the Swedish tradition) includes [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).

### Bird-watching and sex determination {#Bird-watching_and_sex_determination}

*Image: Calidris-alpina-001 edit.jpg.* The [Dunlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunlin) *(Calidris alpina)*

Per Martin-Löf began [bird watching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_watching) in his youth and remains an enthusiastic bird-watcher.[^o17] As a teenager, he published an article on estimating the [mortality rates](wikipedia://en/191305) of birds, using data from [bird ringing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_ringing), in a Swedish zoological journal: This paper was soon cited in leading international journals, and this paper continues to be cited.[^o11][^o18]

In the [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_biology) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/8919339) of [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithology), there are several problems of [missing data](wikipedia://en/7859676). Martin-Löf's first paper discussed the problem of estimating the mortality rates of the [Dunlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunlin) species, using [capture-recapture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture-recapture) methods. The problem of determining the [sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sex) of a bird, which, for some species, is extremely difficult for humans, is one of the first examples in Martin-Löf's lectures on [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576).

### Probability on algebraic structures {#Probability_on_algebraic_structures}

Martin-Löf wrote a licenciate thesis on probability on algebraic structures, particularly semigroups, while a student of [Ulf Grenander](wikipedia://en/27178142) at Stockholm University.[^o20][^o21][^o22]

### Statistical models {#Statistical_models}

Martin-Löf developed innovative approaches to [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579). In his paper "On Tables of Random Numbers", [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_N._Kolmogorov) observed that the [frequency probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_probability) notion of the [limiting](wikipedia://en/26551602) properties of infinite sequences failed to provide a foundation for statistics, which considers only finite samples.[^o24] Much of Martin-Löf's work in statistics was to provide a finite-sample foundation for statistics.

::: {#o26 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Model selection and hypothesis testing"}

#### Model selection and hypothesis testing {#Model_selection_and_hypothesis_testing}

*Image: Em old faithful.gif.* The steps of the EM algorithm on a two component Gaussian [mixture model](wikipedia://en/871681) on the [Old Faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Faithful) dataset

In the 1970s, Per Martin-Löf made important contributions to statistical theory and inspired further research, especially by Scandinavian statisticians including Rolf Sundberg, Thomas Höglund, and Steffan Lauritzen. In this work, Martin-Löf's previous research on probability measures on semigroups led to a notion of "repetitive structure" and a novel treatment of sufficient statistics, in which one-parameter [exponential families](wikipedia://en/339174) were characterized. He provided a [category-theoretic](wikipedia://en/5869) approach to [nested statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576#Nested_models), using finite-sample principles. Before (and after) Martin-Löf, such nested models have often been tested using chi-square hypothesis tests, whose justifications are only asymptotic (and so irrelevant to real problems, which always have finite samples).[^o24]

:::

#### Expectation–maximization method for exponential families {#Expectation–maximization_method_for_exponential_families}

Martin-Löf's student, Rolf Sundberg, developed a detailed analysis of the [expectation–maximization (EM) method](wikipedia://en/470752) for estimation using data from exponential families, especially with [missing data](wikipedia://en/7859676). Sundberg credits a formula, later known as the Sundberg formula, to previous manuscripts of the Martin-Löf brothers, Per and [Anders](wikipedia://en/5751250).[^o28][^o29][^o30][^o31] Many of these results reached the international scientific community through the 1976 paper on the [expectation–maximization (EM) method](wikipedia://en/470752) by [Arthur P. Dempster](wikipedia://en/7040761), [Nan Laird](wikipedia://en/18434623), and [Donald Rubin](wikipedia://en/7790659), which was published in a leading international journal, sponsored by the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664).[^o32]

## Logic {#Logic}

*Image: Franz Brentano.jpeg.* [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano)

### Philosophical logic {#Philosophical_logic}

In [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), Per Martin-Löf has published papers on the theory of [logical consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624), on [judgments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl#Philosophy_of_logic_and_mathematics), etc. He has been interested in [Central-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Europe) philosophical traditions, especially of the German-language writings of [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano), [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416), and of [Edmund Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl).

### Type theory {#Type_theory}

Martin-Löf has worked in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) for many decades.

From 1968 to '69 he worked as an assistant professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) where he met [William Alvin Howard](wikipedia://en/1978177) with whom he discussed issues related to the [Curry–Howard correspondence](wikipedia://en/254299). Martin-Löf's first draft article on type theory dates back to 1971. This [impredicative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impredicative) theory generalized [Girard's](wikipedia://en/579673) [System F](wikipedia://en/767637). However, this system turned out to be [inconsistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_proof) due to [Girard's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girard's_paradox) which was discovered by Girard when studying System U, an inconsistent extension of System F. This experience led Per Martin-Löf to develop the philosophical foundations of [type theory](wikipedia://en/345023), his *meaning explanation*, a form of [proof-theoretic semantics](wikipedia://en/910505), which justifies [predicative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impredicative) type theory as presented in his 1984 Bibliopolis book, and extended in a number of increasingly philosophical texts, such as his influential *On the Meanings of the Logical Constants and the Justifications of the Logical Laws*.

The 1984 type theory was extensional while the type theory presented in the book by Nordström *et al.* in 1990, which was heavily influenced by his later ideas, intensional, and more amenable to being implemented on a computer.

Martin-Löf's intuitionistic type theory developed the notion of [dependent types](wikipedia://en/1949487) and directly influenced the development of the [calculus of constructions](wikipedia://en/613557) and the [logical framework LF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LF_%28logical_framework%29). A number of popular computer-based proof systems are based on type theory, for example [NuPRL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NuPRL), LEGO, [Rocq](wikipedia://en/581974), [Lean](wikipedia://en/62889984), ALF, [Agda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agda_theorem_prover), [Twelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelf), [Epigram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigram_%28programming_language%29), and [Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_%28programming_language%29).
