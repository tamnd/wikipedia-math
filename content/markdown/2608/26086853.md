# P-adic Hodge theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="P-adic Hodge theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), ***p*-adic Hodge theory** is a theory that provides a way to classify and study [*p*-adic Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) of [characteristic 0](wikipedia://en/1066621) [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375)[^o2] with residual characteristic *p* (such as [**Q**<sub>*p*</sub>](wikipedia://en/51423)). The theory has its beginnings in [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933)'s study of [Tate modules](wikipedia://en/3094921) of [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) and the notion of [Hodge–Tate representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge–Tate_representation). Hodge–Tate representations are related to certain decompositions of *p*-adic [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) theories analogous to the [Hodge decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_decomposition), hence the name *p*-adic Hodge theory. Further developments were inspired by properties of *p*-adic Galois representations arising from the [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) of [varieties](wikipedia://en/248808). [Jean-Marc Fontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marc_Fontaine) introduced many of the basic concepts of the field.

:::

## General classification of *p*-adic representations {#General_classification_of_p-adic_representations}

Let $K$ be a local field with residue field $k$ of characteristic $p$. In this article, a $p$*\-adic representation* of $K$ (or of $G_K$, the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of $K$) will be a [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28mathematics%29) representation $\rho:G_K\to\text{GL}(V)$, where $V$ is a finite-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The collection of all $p$-adic representations of $K$ form an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) denoted $\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(K)$ in this article. $p$-adic Hodge theory provides subcollections of $p$-adic representations based on how nice they are, and also provides [faithful functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor) to categories of [linear algebraic](wikipedia://en/18422) objects that are easier to study. The basic classification is as follows:[^o4]

$$\operatorname{Rep}_\mathrm{crys}(K)\subsetneq\operatorname{Rep}_{ss}(K) \subsetneq \operatorname{Rep}_{dR}(K)\subsetneq \operatorname{Rep}_{HT}(K) \subsetneq \operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(K)$$

where each collection is a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) properly contained in the next. In order, these are the categories of [crystalline representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystalline_representation), [semistable representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistable_representation), [de Rham representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_representation), Hodge–Tate representations, and all *p*-adic representations. In addition, two other categories of representations can be introduced, the [potentially crystalline representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentially_crystalline_representation) $\operatorname{Rep}_\mathrm{pcrys}(K)$ and the [potentially semistable representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentially_semistable_representation) $\operatorname{Rep}_\mathrm{pss}(K)$. The latter strictly contains the former which in turn generally strictly contains $\operatorname{Rep}_\mathrm{crys}(K)$; additionally, $\operatorname{Rep}_\mathrm{pss}(K)$ generally strictly contains $\operatorname{Rep}_\mathrm{ss}(K)$, and is contained in $\operatorname{Rep}_{dR}(K)$ (with equality when the residue field of $K$ is finite, a statement called the [*p*-adic monodromy theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_monodromy_theorem)).

## Period rings and comparison isomorphisms in arithmetic geometry {#Period_rings_and_comparison_isomorphisms_in_arithmetic_geometry}

The general strategy of *p*-adic Hodge theory, introduced by Fontaine, is to construct certain so-called **period rings**[^o6] such as [*B*<sub>dR</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_p-adic_periods), [*B*<sub>st</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_semistable_periods), [*B*<sub>cris</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_cristalline_periods), and [*B*<sub>HT</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Hodge–Tate_periods) which have both an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) by *G<sub>K</sub>* and some linear algebraic structure and to consider so-called [Dieudonné modules](wikipedia://en/27127074)

$$D_B(V)=(B\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_p}V)^{G_K}$$

(where *B* is a period ring, and *V* is a *p*-adic representation) which no longer have a *G<sub>K</sub>*-action, but are endowed with linear algebraic structures inherited from the ring *B*. In particular, they are vector spaces over the fixed field $E:=B^{G_K}$.[^o7] This construction fits into the formalism of [*B*-admissible representations](wikipedia://en/26110709) introduced by Fontaine. For a period ring like the aforementioned ones *B*<sub>∗</sub> (for ∗ = HT, dR, st, cris), the category of *p*-adic representations Rep<sub>∗</sub>(*K*) mentioned above is the category of [*B*<sub>∗</sub>-admissible](wikipedia://en/26110709) ones, i.e. those *p*-adic representations *V* for which

$$\dim_ED_{B_\ast}(V)=\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_p}V$$

or, equivalently, the [comparison morphism](wikipedia://en/26110709)

$$\alpha_V:B_\ast\otimes_ED_{B_\ast}(V)\longrightarrow B_\ast \otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_p}V$$

is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828).

This formalism (and the name period ring) grew out of a few results and conjectures regarding comparison isomorphisms in [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/1973177) and [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101):

- If *X* is a [proper](wikipedia://en/603782) [smooth](wikipedia://en/7837041) [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) over **[C](wikipedia://en/5826)**, there is a classical comparison isomorphism between the [algebraic de Rham cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_de_Rham_cohomology) of *X* over **C** and the [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology) of *X*(**C**)

$$H^\ast_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathbf{C})\cong H^\ast(X(\mathbf{C}),\mathbf{Q})\otimes_\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{C}.$$

This isomorphism can be obtained by considering a [pairing](wikipedia://en/1191490) obtained by [integrating](wikipedia://en/15532) [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) in the algebraic de Rham cohomology over [cycles](wikipedia://en/9520954) in the singular cohomology. The result of such an integration is called a [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_periods) and is generally a complex number. This explains why the singular cohomology must be [tensored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_of_scalars) to **C**, and from this point of view, **C** can be said to contain all the periods necessary to compare algebraic de Rham cohomology with singular cohomology, and could hence be called a period ring in this situation.

- In the mid sixties, Tate conjectured[^o8] that a similar isomorphism should hold for proper smooth schemes *X* over *K* between algebraic de Rham cohomology and *p*-adic étale cohomology (the [Hodge–Tate conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge–Tate_conjecture), also called C<sub>HT</sub>). Specifically, let **C**<sub>*K*</sub> be the [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_%28topology%29) of an [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of *K*, let **C**<sub>*K*</sub>(*i*) denote **C**<sub>*K*</sub> where the action of *G<sub>K</sub>* is via *g*·*z* = χ(*g*)<sup>*i*</sup>*g*·*z* (where χ is the [*p*-adic cyclotomic character](wikipedia://en/18846136), and *i* is an integer), and let $B_{\mathrm{HT}}:=\oplus_{i\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{C}_K(i)$. Then there is a functorial isomorphism

$$B_{\mathrm{HT}}\otimes_K\mathrm{gr}H^\ast_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/K)\cong B_{\mathrm{HT}}\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_p}H^\ast_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X\times_K\overline{K},\mathbf{Q}_p)$$

of [graded vector spaces](wikipedia://en/1155734) with *G<sub>K</sub>*-action (the de Rham cohomology is equipped with the [Hodge filtration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_filtration), and $\mathrm{gr}H^\ast_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is its associated graded). This conjecture was proved by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) in the late eighties[^o9] after partial results by several other mathematicians (including Tate himself).

- For an abelian variety *X* with good reduction over a *p*-adic field *K*, [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) reformulated a theorem of Tate's to say that the [crystalline cohomology](wikipedia://en/10220067) *H*<sup>1</sup>(*X*/*W*(*k*)) ⊗ **Q**<sub>*p*</sub> of the special fiber (with the Frobenius endomorphism on this group and the Hodge filtration on this group tensored with *K*) and the *p*-adic étale cohomology *H*<sup>1</sup>(*X*,**Q**<sub>*p*</sub>) (with the action of the Galois group of *K*) contained the same information. Both are equivalent to the [*p*-divisible group](wikipedia://en/29750969) associated to *X*, up to isogeny. Grothendieck conjectured that there should be a way to go directly from *p*-adic étale cohomology to crystalline cohomology (and back), for all varieties with good reduction over *p*-adic fields.[^o10] This suggested relation became known as the **mysterious functor**.

To improve the Hodge–Tate conjecture to one involving the de Rham cohomology (not just its associated graded), Fontaine constructed[^o11] a *[filtered](wikipedia://en/726014)* ring *B*<sub>dR</sub> whose associated graded is *B*<sub>HT</sub> and conjectured[^o12] the following (called C<sub>dR</sub>) for any smooth proper scheme *X* over *K*

$$B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_KH^\ast_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/K)\cong B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_p}H^\ast_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X\times_K\overline{K},\mathbf{Q}_p)$$

as filtered vector spaces with *G<sub>K</sub>*-action. In this way, *B*<sub>dR</sub> could be said to contain all (*p*-adic) periods required to compare algebraic de Rham cohomology with *p*-adic étale cohomology, just as the complex numbers above were used with the comparison with singular cohomology. This is where *B*<sub>dR</sub> obtains its name of *ring of p-adic periods*.

Similarly, to formulate a conjecture explaining Grothendieck's mysterious functor, Fontaine introduced a ring *B*<sub>cris</sub> with *G<sub>K</sub>*-action, a "Frobenius" φ, and a filtration after extending scalars from *K*<sub>0</sub> to *K*. He conjectured[^o13] the following (called C<sub>cris</sub>) for any smooth proper scheme *X* over *K* with good reduction

$$B_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{K_0}H^\ast_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/K)\cong B_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_p}H^\ast_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X\times_K\overline{K},\mathbf{Q}_p)$$

as vector spaces with φ-action, *G<sub>K</sub>*-action, and filtration after extending scalars to *K* (here $H^\ast_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/K)$ is given its structure as a *K*<sub>0</sub>-vector space with φ-action given by its comparison with crystalline cohomology). Both the C<sub>dR</sub> and the C<sub>cris</sub> conjectures were proved by Faltings.[^o14]

Upon comparing these two conjectures with the notion of *B*<sub>∗</sub>-admissible representations above, it is seen that if *X* is a proper smooth scheme over *K* (with good reduction) and *V* is the *p*-adic Galois representation obtained as is its *i*th *p*-adic étale cohomology group, then

$$D_{B_\ast}(V)=H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/K).$$

In other words, the Dieudonné modules should be thought of as giving the other cohomologies related to *V*.

In the late eighties, Fontaine and Uwe Jannsen formulated another comparison isomorphism conjecture, C<sub>st</sub>, this time allowing *X* to have [semi-stable reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-stable_reduction). Fontaine constructed[^o15] a ring *B*<sub>st</sub> with *G<sub>K</sub>*-action, a "[Frobenius](wikipedia://en/1088270)" φ, a filtration after extending scalars from *K*<sub>0</sub> to *K* (and fixing an extension of the [*p*-adic logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_logarithm)), and a "monodromy operator" *N*. When *X* has semi-stable reduction, the de Rham cohomology can be equipped with the φ-action and a monodromy operator by its comparison with the [log-crystalline cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-crystalline_cohomology) first introduced by Osamu Hyodo.[^o16] The conjecture then states that

$$B_{\mathrm{st}}\otimes_{K_0}H^\ast_{\mathrm{log-cris}}(X/K)\cong B_{\mathrm{st}}\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_p}H^\ast_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X\times_K\overline{K},\mathbf{Q}_p)$$

as vector spaces with φ-action, *G<sub>K</sub>*-action, filtration after extending scalars to *K*, and monodromy operator *N*. This conjecture was proved in the late nineties by Takeshi Tsuji.[^o17]
