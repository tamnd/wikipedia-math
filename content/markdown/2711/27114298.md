# Isoclinism of groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isoclinism of groups"}

In mathematics, specifically [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), **isoclinism** is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) which generalizes [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828). Isoclinism was introduced by (Hall 1940) to help classify and understand [p-groups](wikipedia://en/24664), although it is applicable to all groups. Isoclinism also has consequences for the [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) and the associated aspects of [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741), as described in (Suzuki 1982, 256) and (Conway Curtis Norton Parker 1985, xxiii). The word "isoclinism" comes from the Greek ισοκλινης meaning equal slope.

:::

Some textbooks discussing isoclinism include (Berkovich 2008, §29) and (Blackburn Neumann Venkataraman 2007, §21.2) and (Suzuki 1986, 92–95).

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The isoclinism class of a group *G* is determined by the groups *G*/*Z*(*G*) (the [inner automorphism group](wikipedia://en/61839)) and *G* (the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847)) and the commutator map from *G*/*Z*(*G*) × *G*/*Z*(*G*) to *G* (taking *a*, *b* to *aba<sup>−1</sup>b<sup>−1</sup>*).

In other words, two groups *G*<sub>1</sub> and *G*<sub>2</sub> are isoclinic if there are isomorphisms from *G*<sub>1</sub>/*Z*(*G*<sub>1</sub>) to *G*<sub>2</sub>/*Z*(*G*<sub>2</sub>) and from *G*<sub>1</sub> to *G*<sub>2</sub> commuting with the commutator map.

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

All [Abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) are isoclinic since they are equal to their centers and their commutator subgroups are always the identity subgroup. Indeed, a group is isoclinic to an abelian group if and only if it is itself abelian, and *G* is isoclinic with *G*×*A* if and only if *A* is abelian. The [dihedral](wikipedia://en/100267), [quasidihedral](wikipedia://en/1050057), and [quaternion groups](wikipedia://en/153130) of order 2<sup>*n*</sup> are isoclinic for *n*≥3, (Berkovich 2008, 285) in more detail.

Isoclinism divides *p*-groups into families, and the smallest members of each family are called **stem groups**. A group is a stem group if and only if Z(*G*) ≤ \[*G*,*G*\], that is, if and only if every element of the center of the group is contained in the [derived subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_subgroup) (also called the commutator subgroup), (Berkovich 2008, 287). Some enumeration results on isoclinism families are given in (Blackburn Neumann Venkataraman 2007, 226).

Isoclinism is used in theory of [projective representations](wikipedia://en/348860) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707), as all [Schur covering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_cover) groups of a group are isoclinic, a fact already hinted at by Hall according to (Suzuki 1982, 256). This is used in describing the character tables of the [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) (Conway Curtis Norton Parker 1985, xxiii).

:::
