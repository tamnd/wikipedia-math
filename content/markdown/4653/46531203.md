# Profinite integer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Profinite integer"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **profinite integer** is an element of the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) (sometimes pronounced as zee-hat or zed-hat)

:::

$$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \varprojlim \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},$$

where the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of the [quotient rings](wikipedia://en/184635) $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ runs through all [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$, [partially ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) by [divisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility). By definition, this ring is the [profinite completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profinite_completion) of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $\mathbb{Z}$. By the [Chinese remainder theorem](wikipedia://en/7713), $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ can also be understood as the [direct product of rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_product_of_rings)

$$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p,$$

where the index $p$ runs over all [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666), and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the ring of [*p*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer). This group is important because of its relation to [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), [étale homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_homotopy_theory), and the ring of [adeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_adeles). In addition, it provides a basic tractable example of a [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266).

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The profinite integers $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ can be constructed as the set of sequences $\upsilon$ of residues represented as

$$\upsilon = (\upsilon_1 \bmod 1, ~ \upsilon_2 \bmod 2, ~ \upsilon_3 \bmod 3, ~ \ldots)$$

such that $m \ |\ n \implies \upsilon_m \equiv \upsilon_n \!\!\!\!\!\pmod{m}$. Pointwise addition and multiplication make it a commutative ring.

The ring of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) embeds into the ring of profinite integers by the canonical injection

$$\eta: \mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \widehat{\mathbb{Z}},$$

where $n \mapsto (n \bmod 1, n \bmod 2, \dots).$ It is canonical since it satisfies the [universal property of profinite groups](wikipedia://en/24266#Profinite_completion) that, given any profinite group $H$ and any [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $f : \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow H$, there exists a unique [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) group homomorphism $g : \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \rightarrow H$ with $f = g \eta$.

### Using the factorial number system {#Using_the_factorial_number_system}

Every integer $n \ge 0$ has a unique representation in the [factorial number system](wikipedia://en/1113108) as

$$n = \sum_{i=1}^\infty c_i i! \quad \text{with } c_i \in \mathbb{Z},$$

where $0 \le c_i \le i$ for every $i$, and only finitely many of $c_1,c_2,c_3,\ldots$ are nonzero. This can be written as $(\cdots c_3 c_2 c_1)_!$.

In the same way, a profinite integer can be uniquely represented in the factorial number system as an infinite string $(\cdots c_3 c_2 c_1)_!$, where each $c_i$ is an integer satisfying $0 \le c_i \le i$.[^o5] The digits $c_1, c_2, c_3, \ldots, c_{k-1}$ determine the value of the profinite integer modulo $k!$. More specifically, there is a ring homomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\to \mathbb{Z} / k! \, \mathbb{Z}$ sending

$$(\cdots c_3 c_2 c_1)_! \mapsto \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} c_i i! \bmod k!$$

The difference of a profinite integer from an integer is that the "finitely many nonzero digits" condition is dropped, allowing for its factorial number representation to have infinitely many nonzero digits.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Using the Chinese remainder theorem"}

### Using the Chinese remainder theorem {#Using_the_Chinese_remainder_theorem}

Another way to understand the construction of the profinite integers is by using the [Chinese remainder theorem](wikipedia://en/7713). Recall that for an integer $n$ with [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization)

$$n = p_1^{a_1}\cdots p_k^{a_k}$$

of non-repeating primes, there is a [ring isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_isomorphism)

$$\mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/p_1^{a_1}\times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}/p_k^{a_k}$$

from the theorem. Moreover, any [surjection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjection)

$$\mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/m$$

will just be a map on the underlying decompositions where there are induced surjections

$$\mathbb{Z}/p_i^{a_i} \to \mathbb{Z}/p_i^{b_i}$$

since we must have $a_i \geq b_i$. Under the inverse limit definition of the profinite integers, we have the isomorphism

$$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p$$

with the direct product of *p*-adic integers. Explicitly, the isomorphism is $\phi: \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p \to \widehat\mathbb{Z}$ by

$$\phi((n_2, n_3, n_5, \cdots))(k) = \prod_{q} n_q \bmod k,$$

where $q$ ranges over all prime-power factors $p_i^{d_i}$ of $k$; that is, $k = \prod_{i=1}^l p_i^{d_i}$ for some different prime numbers $p_1, ..., p_l$.

## Relations {#Relations}

### Topological properties {#Topological_properties}

The set of profinite integers has an induced topology in which it is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) (in fact, a [Stone space](wikipedia://en/380219)) arising from the fact that it can be seen as a closed subset of the infinite [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887)

$$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \subset \prod_{n=1}^\infty \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},$$

which is compact with its [product topology](wikipedia://en/8887#Topological_space_direct_product) by [Tychonoff's theorem](wikipedia://en/82739). The topology on each finite group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is given as the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology).

The topology on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ can be defined by the metric[^o5]

$$d(x,y) = \frac1{ \min\{ k \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0} : x \not\equiv y \bmod{(k+1)!} \} }.$$

Since addition of profinite integers is continuous, $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is a compact Hausdorff [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974), and thus its [Pontryagin dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_dual) must be a discrete abelian group. In fact, the Pontryagin dual of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the abelian group $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ equipped with the discrete topology (note that it is not the subset topology inherited from $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, which is not discrete). The Pontryagin dual is explicitly constructed by the function[^o10]

$$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to U(1), \, (q, a) \mapsto \chi(qa),$$

where $\chi$ is the character of the adele (introduced below) $\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}, f}$ induced by $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \to U(1), \, \alpha \mapsto e^{2\pi i\alpha}$.[^o11]

### Relation with adeles {#Relation_with_adeles}

The tensor product $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$ is the [ring of finite adeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_finite_adeles)

$$\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}, f} = {\prod_p}' \mathbb{Q}_p$$

of $\mathbb{Q}$, where the symbol $'$ indicates a [restricted product](wikipedia://en/247982). That is, an element is a sequence that is integral except at a finite number of places.[^o13] There is an isomorphism

$$\mathbf{A}_\mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{R}\times(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Q}).$$

### Applications in Galois theory and étale homotopy theory {#Applications_in_Galois_theory_and_étale_homotopy_theory}

For the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_q$ of a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbf{F}_q$ of order *q,* the Galois group can be computed explicitly. From the fact $\text{Gal}(\mathbf{F}_{q^n}/\mathbf{F}_q) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ where the automorphisms are given by the [Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270), the Galois group of the algebraic closure of $\mathbf{F}_q$ is given by the inverse limit of the groups $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, so its Galois group is isomorphic to the group of profinite integers[^o15]

$$\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_q/\mathbf{F}_q) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}},$$

which gives a computation of the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of a finite field.

#### Relation with étale fundamental groups of algebraic tori {#Relation_with_étale_fundamental_groups_of_algebraic_tori}

This construction can be reinterpreted in many ways. One of them is from [étale homotopy type](wikipedia://en/39583234), which defines the [étale fundamental group](wikipedia://en/3022906) $\pi_1^{et}(X)$ as the profinite completion of automorphisms

$$\pi_1^{et}(X) = \lim_{i \in I} \text{Aut}(X_i/X),$$

where $X_i \to X$ is an [étale cover](wikipedia://en/3022771). Then, the profinite integers are isomorphic to the group

$$\pi_1^{et}(\text{Spec}(\mathbf{F}_q)) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$$

from the earlier computation of the profinite Galois group. In addition, there is an embedding of the profinite integers inside the étale fundamental group of the [algebraic torus](wikipedia://en/504697)

$$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \hookrightarrow \pi_1^{et}(\mathbb{G}_m),$$

since the covering maps come from the [polynomial maps](wikipedia://en/7482797)

$$(\cdot)^n:\mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m$$

from the map of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346)

$$f:\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}] \to \mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]$$

sending $x \mapsto x^n$ since $\mathbb{G}_m = \text{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}])$. If the algebraic torus is considered over a field $k$, then the étale fundamental group $\pi_1^{et}(\mathbb{G}_m/\text{Spec(k)})$ contains an action of $\text{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)$ as well from the [fundamental exact sequence](wikipedia://en/3022906) in étale homotopy theory.

### Class field theory and the profinite integers {#Class_field_theory_and_the_profinite_integers}

[Class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) is a branch of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) studying the abelian field extensions of a field. Given the [global field](wikipedia://en/288358) $\mathbb{Q}$, the [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) of its absolute Galois group

$$\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})^{ab}$$

is intimately related to the associated ring of adeles $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}$ and the group of profinite integers. In particular, there is a map, called the [Artin map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_map)[^o18]

$$\Psi_\mathbb{Q}:\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times / \mathbb{Q}^\times \to \text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})^{ab},$$

which is an isomorphism. This quotient can be determined explicitly as

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times/\mathbb{Q}^\times &\cong (\mathbb{R}\times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}})/\mathbb{Z} \\ &= \underset{\leftarrow}{\lim} \mathbb({\mathbb{R}}/m\mathbb{Z}) \\ &= \underset{x \mapsto x^m}{\lim} S^1 \\ &= \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}, \end{aligned}$$

giving the desired relation. There is an analogous statement for [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532) since every finite [abelian extension](wikipedia://en/174448) of $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is induced from a finite [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$.
