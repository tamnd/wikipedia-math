# Baruch Lindau

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Baruch Lindau"}

**Baruch ben Jehuda Löb Lindau** (בָּרוּךְ בֶּן יְהוּדָה לֵייבּ לינדא; 1759, [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) – 5 December 1849, [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia)) was a [Jewish-German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Germany) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [science writer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_writer), and translator.

:::

Lindau became a member of the [maskilim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskalah) circle in Berlin, publishing articles on science and scientific instruments in *[ha-Me'assef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me'assefim)*. He was a counselor of the maskilic association *Chevrat shocharai Ha'tov ve'hatushiya* and translated several *[haftarot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haftarah)* into German for [Mendelssohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Mendelssohn)'s *[Bi'ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biurists)* project.p. 287

In 1789, he published *Reshit Limmudim*, his most successful work. It was a Hebrew scientific textbook containing sections on astronomy, physics, biology, and geography. The second part, *Reshit Limmudim*, was published in 1810 and devoted to physics, chemistry, and mechanics.p. 286 The work remained a popular scientific encyclopedia among European Jews for nearly a century.[^o2]
