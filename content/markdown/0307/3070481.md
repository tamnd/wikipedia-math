# Nat (unit)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nat"}

*Image: Edinicy3.JPG.* Units of information measurement.

The **natural unit of information** (symbol: **nat**), sometimes also **nit** or **nepit**, is a unit of [information](wikipedia://en/18985062) or [information entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_entropy), based on [natural logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_logarithms) and powers of [*e*](wikipedia://en/9633), rather than the powers of 2 and [base 2 logarithms](wikipedia://en/249992), which define the [shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_%28unit%29). This unit is also known by its unit symbol, the nat. One nat is the information content of an event when the probability of that event occurring is 1/[*e*](wikipedia://en/9633).

:::

One nat is equal to $\tfrac{1}{\ln 2}$ [shannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_%28unit%29) ≈ 1.44 Sh or, equivalently, $\tfrac{1}{\ln 10}$ [hartleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartley_%28unit%29) ≈ 0.434 Hart.

## History {#History}

Boulton and [Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wallace_%28computer_scientist%29) used the term *nit* in conjunction with [minimum message length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_message_length), which was subsequently changed by the [minimum description length](wikipedia://en/331325) community to *nat* to avoid confusion with the [nit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nit_%28unit%29) used as a unit of [luminance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminance).

[Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) used the term *natural [ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_%28unit%29)* with the same meaning.

## Entropy {#Entropy}

[Shannon entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_entropy) (information entropy), being the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of the information of an event, is inherently a quantity of the same type and with a unit of information. The [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units), by assigning the same unit ([joule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule) per [kelvin](wikipedia://en/19593121)) both to [heat capacity](wikipedia://en/194227) and to [thermodynamic entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) implicitly treats information entropy as a [quantity of dimension one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantity_of_dimension_one), with 1 nat = 1. Systems of natural units that normalize the [Boltzmann constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_constant) to 1 are effectively measuring thermodynamic entropy with the nat as unit.

When the Shannon entropy is written using a natural logarithm,

$$\mathrm{H} = - \sum_i p_i \ln p_i$$

it is implicitly giving a quantity with nat as the unit.
