# Vladimir Levenshtein

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Vladimir Levenshtein"}

**Vladimir Iosifovich Levenshtein** (Влади́мир Ио́сифович Левенште́йн; 20 May 1935 – 6 September 2017) was a Russian and Soviet scientist who did research in [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [error-correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error-correcting_code), and [combinatorial design](wikipedia://en/2761968).[^o3] Among other contributions, he is known for the [Levenshtein distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levenshtein_distance) and a Levenshtein algorithm, which he developed in 1965.

:::

He graduated from the *Department of Mathematics and Mechanics* of [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1958 and worked at the [Keldysh Institute of Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984473) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) ever since. He was a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) [Information Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Theory) Society.

He received the [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal) in 2006, for "contributions to the theory of error-correcting codes and information theory, including the Levenshtein distance".[^o4]

## Life {#Life}

Levenshtein graduated from Moscow State University in 1958, where he studied in the faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics. He was a member of the student [Komsomol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komsomol) organization and was in charge of university preparations for the [6th World Festival of Youth and Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_World_Festival_of_Youth_and_Students).[^o6]

After graduation, he worked at the M.V Keldysh Institute of Applied Mathematics.
