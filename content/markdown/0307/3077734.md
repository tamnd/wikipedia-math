# Fenchel's duality theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fenchel's duality theorem"}

In mathematics, **Fenchel's duality theorem** is a result in the theory of convex functions named after [Werner Fenchel](wikipedia://en/4784468).

:::

Let $f$ be a [proper convex function](wikipedia://en/630360) on $\mathbb R^n$ and let $g$ be a proper concave function on $\mathbb R^n$. Then, if regularity conditions are satisfied,

$$\inf_x ( f(x)-g(x) ) = \sup_p ( g_*(p)-f^*(p)).$$

where $f^*$ is the [convex conjugate](wikipedia://en/1001490) of $f$ (also referred to as the Fenchel–Legendre transform) and $g_*$ is the [concave conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concave_conjugate) of $g$. That is,

$$f^{*} \left( x^{*} \right) := \sup \left \{ \left. \left\langle x^{*} , x \right\rangle - f \left( x \right) \right| x \in \mathbb{R}^n \right\}$$

$$g_{*} \left( x^{*} \right) := \inf \left \{ \left. \left\langle x^{*} , x \right\rangle - g \left( x \right) \right| x \in \mathbb{R}^n \right\}$$

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Mathematical theorem"}

## Mathematical theorem {#Mathematical_theorem}

Let *X* and *Y* be [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces), $f: X \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ and $g: Y \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ be convex functions and $A: X \to Y$ be a [bounded](wikipedia://en/455961) [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102). Then the Fenchel problems:

$$p^* = \inf_{x \in X} \{f(x) + g(Ax)\}$$

$$d^* = \sup_{y^* \in Y^*} \{-f^*(A^*y^*) - g^*(-y^*)\}$$

satisfy [weak duality](wikipedia://en/34390896), i.e. $p^* \geq d^*$. Note that $f^*$ and $g^*$ are the convex conjugates of $f$ and $g$, respectively, and $A^*$ is the [adjoint operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_operator). The [perturbation function](wikipedia://en/33430345) for this [dual problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_problem) is given by $F(x,y) = f(x) + g(Ax - y)$.

Suppose that $f$, $g$, and $A$ satisfy either

1. $f$ and $g$ are [lower semi-continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semi-continuous) and $0 \in \operatorname{core}(\operatorname{dom}g - A \operatorname{dom}f)$ where $\operatorname{core}$ is the [algebraic interior](wikipedia://en/30823252) and $\operatorname{dom}h$, where $h$ is some function, is the set $\{z: h(z) < +\infty\}$, or
1. $A \operatorname{dom}f \cap \operatorname{cont}g \neq \emptyset$ where $\operatorname{cont}$ are the points where the function is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122).

Then [strong duality](wikipedia://en/34390810) holds, i.e. $p^* = d^*$. If $d^* \in \mathbb{R}$ then [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) is attained.[^o4]

:::

## One-dimensional illustration {#One-dimensional_illustration}

In the following figure, the minimization problem on the left side of the equation is illustrated. One seeks to vary *x* such that the vertical distance between the convex and concave curves at *x* is as small as possible. The position of the vertical line in the figure is the (approximate) optimum.

*Image: FencheDual02.png.*

The next figure illustrates the maximization problem on the right hand side of the above equation. Tangents are drawn to each of the two curves such that both tangents have the same slope *p*. The problem is to adjust *p* in such a way that the two tangents are as far away from each other as possible (more precisely, such that the points where they intersect the y-axis are as far from each other as possible). Imagine the two tangents as metal bars with vertical springs between them that push them apart and against the two parabolas that are fixed in place.

*Image: FenchelDual01.png.*

Fenchel's theorem states that the two problems have the same solution. The points having the minimum vertical separation are also the tangency points for the maximally separated parallel tangents.
