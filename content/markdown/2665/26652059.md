# Stevedore knot (mathematics)

*Image: Double eight -1.JPG.* The common [stevedore knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevedore_knot). If the ends were joined together, the result would be equivalent to the mathematical knot.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stevedore knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **stevedore knot** is one of three [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) with [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598) six, the others being the [6<sub>2</sub> knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6₂_knot) and the [6<sub>3</sub> knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6₃_knot). The stevedore knot is listed as the **6<sub>1</sub> knot** in the [Alexander–Briggs notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander–Briggs_notation), and it can also be described as a [twist knot](wikipedia://en/26701261) with four half twists, or as the (5,−1,−1) [pretzel knot](wikipedia://en/1286130).

:::

The mathematical stevedore knot is named after the common [stevedore knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevedore_knot), which is often used as a [stopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stopper_knot) at the end of a [rope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rope). The mathematical version of the knot can be obtained from the common version by joining together the two loose ends of the rope, forming a knotted [loop](wikipedia://en/1189560).

The stevedore knot is [invertible](wikipedia://en/17351839) but not [amphichiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphichiral_knot). Its [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) is

$$\Delta(t) = -2t+5-2t^{-1}, \,$$

its [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander–Conway_polynomial) is

$$\nabla(z) = 1-2z^2, \,$$

and its [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) is

$V(q) = q^2-q+2-2q^{-1}+q^{-2}-q^{-3}+q^{-4}. \,$[^o3]

The Alexander polynomial and Conway polynomial are the same as those for the knot 9<sub>46</sub>, but the Jones polynomials for these two knots are different. Because the Alexander polynomial is not [monic](wikipedia://en/314730), the stevedore knot is not [fibered](wikipedia://en/1358592).

The stevedore knot is a [ribbon knot](wikipedia://en/9809292), and is therefore also a [slice knot](wikipedia://en/4158126).

The stevedore knot is a [hyperbolic knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_knot), with its complement having a [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_volume_%28knot%29) of approximately 3.16396.
