# Cyclic polytope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cyclic polytope"}

In mathematics, a **cyclic polytope**, denoted *C*(*n*, *d*), is a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) formed as a convex hull of *n* distinct points on a [rational normal curve](wikipedia://en/2380117) in **R**<sup>*d*</sup>, where *n* is greater than *d*. These polytopes were studied by [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625), [David Gale](wikipedia://en/1227675), [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055), [Victor Klee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Klee), and others. They play an important role in [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519): according to the [upper bound theorem](wikipedia://en/40964551), proved by Peter McMullen and [Richard Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397), the boundary *Δ*(*n*,*d*) of the cyclic polytope *C*(*n*,*d*) maximizes the number *f*<sub>*i*</sub> of *i*-dimensional faces among all [simplicial spheres](wikipedia://en/22359636) of dimension *d* − 1 with *n* vertices.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The [moment curve](wikipedia://en/29536414) in $\mathbb{R}^d$ is defined by

$\mathbf{x}:\mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d, \mathbf{x}(t) := \begin{bmatrix}t,t^2,\ldots,t^d\end{bmatrix}^T$.[^o4]

The $d$-dimensional cyclic polytope with $n$ vertices is the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634)

$$C(n,d) := \mathbf{conv}\{\mathbf{x}(t_1),\mathbf{x}(t_2),\ldots,\mathbf{x}(t_n) \}$$

of $n > d \ge 2$ distinct points $\mathbf{x}(t_i)$ with $t_1 < t_2 < \ldots < t_n$ on the moment curve.[^o4]

The combinatorial structure of this polytope is independent of the points chosen, and the resulting polytope has dimension *d* and *n* vertices.[^o4] Its boundary is a (*d* − 1)-dimensional [simplicial polytope](wikipedia://en/8853373) denoted *Δ*(*n*,*d*).

:::

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Gale evenness condition"}

## Gale evenness condition {#Gale_evenness_condition}

The Gale evenness condition[^o7] provides a necessary and sufficient condition to determine a facet on a cyclic polytope.

Let $T := \{t_1,t_2,\ldots,t_n\}$. Then, a $d$-subset $T_d \subseteq T$ forms a facet of $C(n,d)$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) any two elements in $T \setminus T_d$ are separated by an even number of elements from $T_d$ in the sequence $(t_1,t_2,\ldots,t_n)$.

:::

## Neighborliness {#Neighborliness}

Cyclic polytopes are examples of [neighborly polytopes](wikipedia://en/26212072), in that every set of at most *d*/2 vertices forms a face. They were the first neighborly polytopes known, and [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055) conjectured that all neighborly polytopes are combinatorially equivalent to cyclic polytopes, but this is now known to be false.[^o9][^o10]

## Number of faces {#Number_of_faces}

The number of *i*-dimensional faces of the cyclic polytope *Δ*(*n*,*d*) is given by the formula

$$f_i(\Delta(n,d)) = \binom{n}{i+1} \quad \textrm{for} \quad 0 \leq i < \left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor$$

and $(f_0,\ldots,f_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor-1})$ completely determine $(f_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor},\ldots,f_{d-1})$ via the [Dehn–Sommerville equations](wikipedia://en/8276156).

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Upper bound theorem"}

## Upper bound theorem {#Upper_bound_theorem}

The **upper bound theorem** states that cyclic polytopes have the maximum possible number of faces for a given dimension and number of vertices: if *Δ* is a simplicial sphere of dimension *d* − 1 with *n* vertices, then

$$f_i(\Delta) \leq f_i(\Delta(n,d)) \quad \textrm{for}\quad i=0,1,\ldots,d-1.$$

The upper bound conjecture for simplicial polytopes was proposed by [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055) in 1957 and proved by [Peter McMullen](wikipedia://en/40964822) in 1970. [Victor Klee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Klee) suggested that the same statement should hold for all simplicial spheres and this was indeed established in 1975 by [Richard P. Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397)[^o14] using the notion of a [Stanley–Reisner ring](wikipedia://en/24394535) and homological methods.

:::
