# Edwin F. Beckenbach

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edwin F. Beckenbach"}

**Edwin Ford Beckenbach** (July 18, 1906 – September 5, 1982) was an American mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Beckenbach was born July 18, 1906, in [Oak Cliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Cliff), [Dallas County, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_County,_Texas), the son of a leather worker and on his father's side the grandson of immigrants from Germany. In 1924, he began study at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University), where in 1929 he earned a master's degree and in 1931 a PhD under the direction of [Lester R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford). As a postdoc, he was a National Research Fellow at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), and the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). In 1933, he was an instructor at Rice University and from 1940 an assistant professor at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). In 1942, Beckenbach became an associate professor at the University of Texas and was from 1945 a professor at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles). At UCLA, he led the development of the graduate program in mathematics. The first mathematics PhD was granted under his direction as thesis advisor.

Beckenbach was also a leader in the founding (in 1948) of the Institute of Numerical Analysis, which was then a branch of the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards). His institute developed in 1948 and 1949 a vacuum-tube computer ([SWAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAC_%28computer%29)), which began operation in July 1950 and was for a short time the fastest computer in the world. In 1974 he retired from UCLA as professor emeritus. From 1949 to 1963, he was a consultant for the [Rand Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Corporation) and in the academic year 1951/1952 he was a visiting professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o4]

In the academic year 1958/59 he was a Guggenheim Fellow at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidgenössische_Technische_Hochschule_Zürich). With [František Wolf](wikipedia://en/6095350), Beckenbach founded in 1951 the *[Pacific Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Journal_of_Mathematics)*, of which he was the first editor. In 1983, he received the Distinguished Service Award from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). The [Beckenbach Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckenbach_Book_Prize), first awarded in January 1985, is named in his honor.

Beckenbach was famous for his work on inequalities and for this subject organized three [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberwolfach) seminars (in 1976, 1978, and 1981). He was a co-author of several college textbooks in mathematics, including algebra, trigonometry, and analytic geometry.[^o5]

From 1933 to 1960, he was married to Madelene Shelby Simons and had from this marriage a son and two daughters. In 1960, the marriage ended in divorce and in the same year he married his second wife Alice Judson Curtiss.[^o6]

He died on September 5, 1982, in [Syracuse, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York).

- Editor: *Modern Mathematics for the Engineer*, McGraw Hill 1957
- Editor: *Applied combinatorial mathematics*, Wiley 1964
