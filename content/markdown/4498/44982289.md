# Number theoretic Hilbert transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Number theoretic Hilbert transform"}

The **number theoretic Hilbert transform** is an extension[^o2] of the discrete [Hilbert transform](wikipedia://en/574024) to [integers modulo a prime $p$](wikipedia://en/20087). The transformation operator is a [circulant matrix](wikipedia://en/889936).

:::

The number theoretic transform is meaningful in the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $\mathbb{Z}_m$, when the modulus $m$ is not prime, provided a principal root of order *n* exists. The $n\times n$ NHT matrix, where $n =2m$, has the form

$$NHT= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & a_{m} & \dots & 0 & a_{1} \\ a_{1} & 0 & a_{m} & & 0 \\ \vdots & a_{1}& 0 & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & & \ddots & \ddots & a_{m} \\ a_{m} & 0& \dots & a_{1} & 0 \\ \end{bmatrix}.$$

The rows are the cyclic permutations of the first row, or the columns may be seen as the cyclic permutations of the first column. The NHT is its own inverse:$NHT^\mathrm{T} NHT = NHT NHT^\mathrm{T} = I \bmod\ p, \,$ where *I* is the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718).

The number theoretic Hilbert transform can be used to generate sets of orthogonal discrete sequences that have applications in [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), [wireless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless) systems, and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432).[^o3] Other ways to generate constrained orthogonal sequences also exist.[^o4][^o5]
