# Robert MacPherson (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert MacPherson"}

**Robert Duncan MacPherson** (born May 25, 1944) is an American mathematician at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Robert Duncan MacPherson was born in [Lakewood, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakewood,_Ohio) on May 25, 1944.[^o6] He received his bachelor's degree from [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) in 1966.[^o6] MacPherson then received his PhD from [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1970 with a thesis, written under the direction of [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457), entitled *Singularities of Maps and Characteristic Classes*.

## Career {#Career}

MacPherson was at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) as a J. D. Tamarkin Instructor from 1970 to 1972, an assistant professor from 1972 to 1974, an associate professor from 1974 to 1977, and then a professor from 1977 to 1987.[^o6] He was a professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1987 to 1994.[^o6] He became a professor of mathematics at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1994, later becoming named a Hermann Weyl Professor.[^o6] He retired and became a professor emeritus 2018.[^o6]

His notable doctoral students include [Mark Goresky](wikipedia://en/10238162), [David Nadler](wikipedia://en/63381002), [Julianna Tymoczko](wikipedia://en/62237204), [Kari Vilonen](wikipedia://en/56918693), and [Zhiwei Yun](wikipedia://en/51494005).[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

MacPherson and Goresky introduced [intersection homology](wikipedia://en/705749).[^o8] He also worked on [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741), in particular on [Siegel modular threefolds](wikipedia://en/61144542) with Mark McConnell and on [compactifications](wikipedia://en/6617) of [symmetric spaces](wikipedia://en/1642877) with [Lizhen Ji](wikipedia://en/22299446).[^o9]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 1983, MacPherson gave a [plenary address](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).

In 1992, MacPherson was awarded the [NAS Award in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Award_in_Mathematics) from the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o11] In 2002, he and Goresky were awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o12][^o13] In 2009 he received the [Heinz Hopf Prize](wikipedia://en/24560211) from [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich).[^o6]

In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14] He is also a fellow of the National Academy of Sciences and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o6]

He received honorary doctorates from the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille) in 1993 and [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1994.[^o6]

## Personal {#Personal}

MacPherson's PhD advisee, Mark Goresky, later became his life partner.[^o8] After the collapse of the Soviet Union, they were instrumental in channeling aid to Russian mathematicians, especially many who had to hide their sexuality.[^o8]
