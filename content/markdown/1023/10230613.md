# Alexander Beilinson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Beilinson"}

**Alexander A. Beilinson** (born 1957) is the David and Mary Winton Green University professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and works on mathematics. His research has spanned [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). In 1999, Beilinson was awarded the [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411) with [Helmut Hofer](wikipedia://en/34920327). In 2017, he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o3] In 2018, he received the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278)[^o4] and in 2020 the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematics.[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Beilinson was born in Moscow of mostly [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians) descent while his paternal grandfather was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews). Nevertheless he was discriminated against because of his Jewish surname, and was not admitted to [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University). He went to the [Pedagogical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Pedagogical_State_University) instead and transferred to Moscow State University when he was a third year student.[^o7]

## Work {#Work}

In 1978, Beilinson published a paper on [coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaves) and several problems in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). His two-page note in the journal *Functional Analysis and Its Applications* was one of the papers on the study of [derived categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_categories) of coherent [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466).

In 1981, Beilinson announced a proof of the [Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazhdan–Lusztig_conjectures) and [Jantzen conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantzen_conjectures) with [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383). Independent of Beilinson and Bernstein, [Brylinski](wikipedia://en/10306904) and [Kashiwara](wikipedia://en/4376209) obtained a proof of the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures.[^o9] However, the proof of Beilinson–Bernstein introduced a method of [localization](wikipedia://en/611460). This established a geometric description of the entire [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of representations of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), by "spreading out" representations as geometric objects living on the [flag variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_variety). These geometric objects naturally have an [intrinsic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrinsic) notion of [parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595): they are [D-modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-modules).

In 1982, Beilinson published his own conjectures about the existence of [motivic cohomology](wikipedia://en/809314) groups for [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754), provided as [hypercohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercohomology_group) of a complex of [abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_groups) and related to [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) by a [motivic spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivic_spectral_sequence), analogous to the [Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/6795342) in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). These conjectures have since been dubbed the [Beilinson-Soulé conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beilinson-Soulé_conjectures); they are intertwined with [Vladimir Voevodsky](wikipedia://en/104482)'s program to develop a [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) for [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754).

In 1984, Beilinson published the paper *Higher Regulators and values of L-functions*, in which he related [higher regulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_regulator) for K-theory and their relationship to [L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-functions). The paper also provided a generalization to [arithmetic varieties](wikipedia://en/23038858) of the [Lichtenbaum conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichtenbaum_conjecture) for [K-groups](wikipedia://en/598500) of number [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), the [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518), the [Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/3124804) about [algebraic cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_cycles), the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) about [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves), and [Bloch's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloch's_conjecture) about *K*<sub>2</sub> of elliptic curves.

Beilinson continued to work on [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) throughout the mid-1980s. He collaborated with [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) on the developing a [motivic](wikipedia://en/430680) interpretation of [Don Zagier](wikipedia://en/1408557)'s [polylogarithm](wikipedia://en/482471) conjectures.

From the early 1990s onwards, Beilinson worked with [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747) to rebuild the theory of [vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra). After some informal circulation, this research was published in 2004 in a form of a monograph on [chiral algebras](wikipedia://en/31824847). This has led to new advances in [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774), [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and the geometric [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905). He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2008.[^o10] He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in the fall of 1994 and again from 1996 to 1998.[^o11]
