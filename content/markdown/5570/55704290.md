# Ingrid C. Kildegaard

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ingrid C. Kildegaard"}

**Ingrid Christensen Kildegaard** (died September 2, 1984 in [Haines Falls, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haines_Falls,_New_York)) was an American statistician and [market researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_research) who worked at the [Advertising Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_Research_Foundation) as a vice president and technical director.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Kildegaard graduated from [Central Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Michigan_University) (Michigan State Teachers College) in 1936. She did her graduate studies at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), completing a master's degree in statistics there in 1950.

## Career {#Career}

She joined the Advertising Research Foundation in 1956 and was promoted to director of technical services in 1965. At around that time, she became involved in discussions with the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070) over whether they should survey ownership of advanced television sets capable of color and of receiving [UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UHF) signals. In 1970 she served as chair of the Subsection on Statistics in Marketing of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988). She retired in 1982.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1967, she was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association). The Market Research Council added her to their Hall of Fame in 2007.
