# Kevin Ford (mathematician)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Kevin Ford"}

**Kevin B. Ford** (born 22 December 1967) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Ford received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in Computer Science and Mathematics in 1990 from the [California State University, Chico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Chico).[^o6] He then attended the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign) (UIUC), where he completed his doctoral studies in 1994 under the supervision of [Heini Halberstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heini_Halberstam).[^o6][^o2] His dissertation was titled *The representation of numbers as sums of unlike powers*.[^o2]

### Early career (1994–2001) {#Early_career_(1994–2001)}

From September 1994 to June 1995 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o6][^o8] He was then a postdoc at [UT Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UT_Austin) until 1998, while also doing software development at the NASA [Ames Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_Research_Center) during the summers of 1997 and 1998.[^o6] From 1998 to 2001, Ford was an assistant professor at the [University of South Carolina, Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina,_Columbia).[^o6]

### Professorship (2001–present) {#Professorship_(2001–present)}

He has been a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in the department of mathematics of UIUC since 2001.[^o6] In addition, he returned to IAS from September 2009 to June 2010,[^o6][^o8] was a research member at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in 2017,[^o6] and was a visiting fellow at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford) in 2019.[^o6]

As of 2025, Ford has supervised eight PhD students, all at UIUC.[^o2]

## Research {#Research}

Ford's early work focused on the distribution of [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452). In 1998, he published a paper that studied in detail the range of this function and established that [Carmichael's totient function conjecture](wikipedia://en/19103379) is true for all integers up to $10^{10^{10}}$.[^o11] In 1999, he settled [Sierpinski’s conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski’s_conjecture) on [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452).[^o12]

In August 2014, Kevin Ford, in collaboration with [Green](wikipedia://en/1927952), [Konyagin](wikipedia://en/29007447) and [Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498),[^o13] resolved a [longstanding conjecture](wikipedia://en/5075259#Lower_bounds) of [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) on large gaps between primes, also proven independently by [James Maynard](wikipedia://en/42512102).[^o14] The five mathematicians were awarded for their work the largest Erdős prize (\$10,000) ever offered. [^o15] In 2017, they improved their results in a joint paper. [^o16]

He is one of the namesakes of the [Erdős–Tenenbaum–Ford constant](wikipedia://en/56804318),[^o17] named for his work using it in estimating the number of small integers that have divisors in a given interval.[^o18]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2013, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o20]
