# Comodule

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Comodule"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **comodule** or corepresentation is a concept [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) to a [module](wikipedia://en/276410). The definition of a comodule over a [coalgebra](wikipedia://en/310886) is formed by dualizing the definition of a module over an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *K* be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and *C* be a [coalgebra](wikipedia://en/310886) over *K*. A (right) **comodule** over *C* is a *K*-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *M* together with a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102)

$$\rho\colon M \to M \otimes C$$

such that

1. $$(\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta) \circ \rho = (\rho \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \rho$$
1. $$(\mathrm{id} \otimes \varepsilon) \circ \rho = \mathrm{id},$$

where Δ is the comultiplication for *C*, and ε is the counit.

Note that in the second rule we have identified $M \otimes K$ with $M\,$.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A coalgebra is a comodule over itself.
- If *M* is a finite-dimensional module over a finite-dimensional *K*-algebra *A*, then the set of [linear functions](wikipedia://en/152111) from *A* to *K* forms a coalgebra, and the set of linear functions from *M* to *K* forms a comodule over that coalgebra.
- A [graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734) *V* can be made into a comodule. Let *I* be the [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) for the graded vector space, and let $C_I$ be the vector space with basis $e_i$ for $i \in I$. We turn $C_I$ into a coalgebra and *V* into a $C_I$-comodule, as follows:
  1. Let the comultiplication on $C_I$ be given by $\Delta(e_i) = e_i \otimes e_i$.
  1. Let the counit on $C_I$ be given by $\varepsilon(e_i) = 1\$.
  1. Let the map $\rho$ on *V* be given by $\rho(v) = \sum v_i \otimes e_i$, where $v_i$ is the *i*-th homogeneous piece of $v$.

### In algebraic topology {#In_algebraic_topology}

One important result in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) is the fact that homology $H_*(X)$ over the dual [Steenrod algebra](wikipedia://en/2146220) $\mathcal{A}^*$ forms a comodule.[^o7] This comes from the fact the Steenrod algebra $\mathcal{A}$ has a canonical action on the cohomology

> $$\mu: \mathcal{A}\otimes H^*(X) \to H^*(X)$$

When we dualize to the [dual Steenrod algebra](wikipedia://en/66300206), this gives a comodule structure

> $$\mu^*:H_*(X) \to \mathcal{A}^*\otimes H_*(X)$$

This result extends to other cohomology theories as well, such as [complex cobordism](wikipedia://en/1033666) and is instrumental in computing its cohomology ring $\Omega_U^*(\{pt\})$.[^o8] The main reason for considering the comodule structure on homology instead of the module structure on cohomology lies in the fact the dual Steenrod algebra $\mathcal{A}^*$ is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), and the setting of commutative algebra provides more tools for studying its structure.

:::

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Rational comodule"}

## Rational comodule {#Rational_comodule}

If *M* is a (right) comodule over the coalgebra *C*, then *M* is a (left) module over the dual algebra *C*<sup>∗</sup>, but the converse is not true in general: a module over *C*<sup>∗</sup> is not necessarily a comodule over *C*. A **rational comodule** is a module over *C*<sup>∗</sup> which becomes a comodule over *C* in the natural way.

:::

::: {#o12 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Colinear map"}

## Comodule morphisms {#Comodule_morphisms}

Let *R* be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), *M*, *N*, and *C* be *R*-modules, and

$$\rho_M: M \rightarrow M \otimes C,\ \rho_N: N \rightarrow N \otimes C$$

be right *C*-comodules. Then an *R*-linear map $f: M \rightarrow N$ is called a **(right) comodule morphism**, or **(right) C-colinear**, if

$$\rho_N \circ f = (f \otimes 1) \circ \rho_M.$$

This notion is dual to the notion of a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) between [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), or, more generally, of a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/474118) between [*R*-modules](wikipedia://en/276410).[^o13]

:::
