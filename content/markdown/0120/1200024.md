# Gyroelongated pentagonal cupolarotunda

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyroelongated pentagonal cupolarotunda"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyroelongated pentagonal cupolarotunda** is one of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) ($J_{47}$). As the name suggests, it can be constructed by gyroelongating a pentagonal cupolarotunda ($J_{32}$ or $J_{33}$) by inserting a [decagonal](wikipedia://en/327393) [antiprism](wikipedia://en/1851) between its two halves.

:::

The gyroelongated pentagonal cupolarotunda is one of five Johnson solids which are [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160), meaning that they have a "left-handed" and a "right-handed" form. In the illustration to the right, each pentagonal face on the bottom half of the figure is connected by a path of two triangular faces to a square face above it and to the left. In the figure of opposite chirality (the mirror image of the illustrated figure), each bottom pentagon would be connected to a square face above it and to the right. The two chiral forms of $J_{47}$ are not considered different Johnson solids.

## Area and volume {#Area_and_volume}

With edge length a, the surface area is

$$A=\frac{1}{4}\left(20+35\sqrt{3}+7\sqrt{25+10\sqrt{5}}\right)a^2\approx32.198786370...a^2,$$

and the volume is

$V=\left(\frac{55}{12}+\frac{25}{12}\sqrt{5}+ \frac{5}{6}\sqrt{2\sqrt{650+290\sqrt{5}}-2\sqrt{5}-2}\right) a^3$ $\approx15.991096162...a^3.$
