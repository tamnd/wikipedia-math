# Homotopy sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Homotopy sphere"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **homotopy sphere** is an *n*-[manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) that is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to the *n*-[sphere](wikipedia://en/27859#Topology). It thus has the same [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) and the same [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) groups as the *n*-sphere, and so every homotopy sphere is necessarily a [homology sphere](wikipedia://en/585388).[^o2]

:::

The topological [generalized Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/8536216) is that any *n*-dimensional homotopy sphere is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the *n*-sphere; it was solved by [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294) in dimensions five and higher, by [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970) in dimension 4, and for dimension 3 (the original [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798)) by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) in 2005.

In the case of [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifolds), in certain dimensions there exist homotopy spheres that are not [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to the sphere, called [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931). The smooth Poincaré conjecture, whether such non-trivial smooth homotopy spheres exist, is open in dimension 4.

Homotopy spheres form an abelian group known as [Kervaire–Milnor group](wikipedia://en/79124653). Its composition is the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) and its [neutral element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_element) is the sphere, while inversion is given by opposite [orientation](wikipedia://en/187446).
