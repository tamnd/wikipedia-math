# Expansion (geometry)

*Image: Expanding pentagon.png.* An example of expanding [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014) into a [decagon](wikipedia://en/327393) by moving edges away from the center and inserting new edges in the gaps. The *expansion* is *uniform* if all the edges are the same length.

*Image: P2-A5-P3.gif.* Animation showing an expanded [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) (and [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635))

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Expansion"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **expansion** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) operation where [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) are separated and moved radially apart, and new facets are formed at separated elements ([vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), etc.). Equivalently this operation can be imagined by keeping facets in the same position but reducing their size.

:::

The expansion of a [regular](wikipedia://en/518693) [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) creates a [uniform](wikipedia://en/6842392) convex polytope.

For polyhedra, an expanded polyhedron has all the [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) of the original polyhedron, all the faces of the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815), and new square faces in place of the original edges.

## Expansion of regular polytopes {#Expansion_of_regular_polytopes}

According to [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter), this multidimensional term was defined by [Alicia Boole Stott](wikipedia://en/3325459)[^o3] for creating new polytopes, specifically starting from [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693) to construct new [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392).

The *expansion* operation is symmetric with respect to a regular polytope and its [dual](wikipedia://en/8815#Dual_polytopes_and_tessellations). The resulting figure contains the [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) of both the regular and its dual, along with various prismatic facets filling the gaps created between intermediate dimensional elements.

It has somewhat different meanings by [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398). In a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777), an expansion is generated by reflections from the first and last mirrors. In higher dimensions, lower dimensional expansions can be written with a subscript, so e<sub>2</sub> is the same as t<sub>0,2</sub> in any dimension.

By dimension:

- A regular {p} [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) expands into a regular 2n-gon.
  - The operation is identical to [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) for polygons, e{p} = e<sub>1</sub>{p} = t<sub>0,1</sub>{p} = t{p} and has [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) .
- A regular {p,q} [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) (3-polytope) expands into a polyhedron with [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) *p.4.q.4*.
  - This operation for polyhedra is also called [cantellation](wikipedia://en/6317514), e{p,q} = e<sub>2</sub>{p,q} = t<sub>0,2</sub>{p,q} = rr{p,q}, and has Coxeter diagram .

*Image: Cube cantellation sequence.svg.*

For example, a rhombicuboctahedron can be called an *expanded cube*, *expanded octahedron*, as well as a *cantellated cube* or *cantellated octahedron*.

- A regular {p,q,r} [4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979) (4-polytope) expands into a new 4-polytope with the original {p,q} cells, new cells {r,q} in place of the old vertices, p-gonal prisms in place of the old faces, and r-gonal prisms in place of the old edges.
  - This operation for 4-polytopes is also called [runcination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runcination_%28geometry%29), e{p,q,r} = e<sub>3</sub>{p,q,r} = t<sub>0,3</sub>{p,q,r}, and has Coxeter diagram .
- Similarly a regular {p,q,r,s} [5-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821670) expands into a new 5-polytope with facets {p,q,r}, {s,r,q}, {p,q}×{ } [prisms](wikipedia://en/1645899#Prismatic_uniform_s), {s,r}×{ } prisms, and {p}*×*{s} [duoprisms](wikipedia://en/2114155).
  - This operation is called [sterication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterication), e{p,q,r,s} = e<sub>4</sub>{p,q,r,s} = t<sub>0,4</sub>{p,q,r,s} = 2r2r{p,q,r,s} and has Coxeter diagram .

The general operator for expansion of a regular n-polytope is t<sub>0,n−1</sub>{p,q,r,...}. New regular facets are added at each vertex, and new prismatic polytopes are added at each divided edge, face, ... [ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_%28geometry%29), etc.
