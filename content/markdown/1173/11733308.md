# Friedel's law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Friedel's law"}

**Friedel's law**, named after [Georges Friedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Friedel), is a property of [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247) of real functions.[^o2]

:::

Given a real function $f(x)$, its [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247)

$$F(k)=\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}f(x)e^{i k \cdot x }dx$$

has the following properties.

- $$F(k)=F^*(-k) \,$$

where $F^*$ is the complex conjugate of $F$.

[Centrosymmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrosymmetric) points $(k,-k)$ are called [Friedel's pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedel's_pairs).

The squared amplitude ($|F|^2$) is centrosymmetric:

- $$|F(k)|^2=|F(-k)|^2 \,$$

The phase $\phi$ of $F$ is [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/592151):

- $$\phi(k) = -\phi(-k) \,.$$

Friedel's law is used in [X-ray diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_diffraction), [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) and scattering from real potentials within the [Born approximation](wikipedia://en/2460228). Note that a [twin operation](http://reference.iucr.org/dictionary/Twin_operation) ( *Opération de maclage*) is equivalent to an inversion centre and the intensities from the individual reflections are equal under Friedel's law.[^o3][^o4][^o5]
