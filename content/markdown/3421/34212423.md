# Kasch ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kasch ring"}

In [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a subfield of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **right Kasch ring** is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* for which every [simple](wikipedia://en/29430) right *R*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a right [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of *R*.[^o2] Analogously the notion of a **left Kasch ring** is defined, and the two properties are independent of each other.

:::

Kasch rings are named in honor of mathematician [Friedrich Kasch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Kasch). Kasch originally called [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273) whose proper ideals have nonzero [annihilators](wikipedia://en/328684) *S-rings*.[^o3][^o4] The characterizations below show that Kasch rings generalize S-rings.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Equivalent definitions will be introduced only for the right-hand version, with the understanding that the left-hand analogues are also true. The Kasch conditions have a few equivalent statements using the concept of [annihilators](wikipedia://en/328684), and this article uses the same notation appearing in the annihilator article.

In addition to the definition given in the introduction, the following properties are equivalent definitions for a ring *R* to be right Kasch. They appear in (Lam 1999, p. 281):

1. For every simple right *R*-module *M*, there is a nonzero [module homomorphism](wikipedia://en/474118) from *M* into *R*.
1. The [maximal](wikipedia://en/48164) right ideals of *R* are right annihilators of ring elements, that is, each one is of the form $\mathrm{r.ann}(x)$ where *x* is in *R*.
1. For any maximal right ideal *T* of *R*, $\mathrm{\ell.ann}(T) \neq \{0\}$.
1. For any proper right ideal *T* of *R*, $\mathrm{\ell.ann}(T) \neq \{0\}$.
1. For any maximal right ideal *T* of *R*, $\mathrm{r.ann}(\mathrm{\ell.ann}(T)) = T$.
1. *R* has no [dense](wikipedia://en/32315625) right ideals except *R* itself.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The content below can be found in references such as (Faith 1999, p. 109), (Lam 1999, §§8C,19B), (Nicholson Yousif 2003, p.51).

- Let *R* be a [semiprimary ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiprimary_ring) with [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) *J*. If *R* is [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346), or if *R*/*J* is a [simple ring](wikipedia://en/443387), then *R* is right (and left) Kasch. In particular, commutative [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273) are right and left Kasch.
- For a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) *k*, consider a certain [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) *R* of the 4-by-4 [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) with entries from *k*. The subring *R* consists of matrices of the following form:

$$\begin{bmatrix} a & 0 & b & c \\ 0 & a & 0 & d \\ 0 & 0 & a & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & e \end{bmatrix}$$

This is a right and left Artinian ring which is right Kasch, but *not* left Kasch.

- Let *S* be the ring of [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) on two noncommuting variables *X* and *Y* with coefficients from a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F*. Let the ideal *A* be the ideal generated by the two elements *YX* and *Y*<sup>2</sup>. The [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) *S*/*A* is a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) which is right Kasch but *not* left Kasch.
- Suppose *R* is a [direct product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_product_of_rings) of infinitely many nonzero rings labeled *A*<sub>k</sub>. The [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of the *A*<sub>k</sub> forms a proper ideal of *R*. It is easily checked that the left and right annihilators of this ideal are zero, and so *R* is not right or left Kasch.
- The 2-by-2 upper (or lower) [triangular matrix](wikipedia://en/374222) ring is not right or left Kasch.
- A ring with right [socle](wikipedia://en/3571350#Socle_of_a_module) zero (i.e. $\mathrm{soc}(R_R)=\{0\}$) cannot be right Kasch, since the ring contains no [minimal](wikipedia://en/34233472) right ideals. So, for example, [domains](wikipedia://en/487627) which are not division rings are not right or left Kasch.

:::
