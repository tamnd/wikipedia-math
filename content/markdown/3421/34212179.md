# Victor Schlegel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Victor Schlegel"}

*Image: Das Fotoalbum für Weierstraß 2 18 (Victor Schlegel).jpg.* Victor Schlegel

**Victor Schlegel** (4 March 1843 – 22 November 1905)[^o2] was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is remembered for promoting the geometric algebra of [Hermann Grassmann](wikipedia://en/99242) and for a method of visualizing [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471) called [Schlegel diagrams](wikipedia://en/7201595).

:::

In the nineteenth century there were various expansions of the traditional field of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) through the innovations of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). Hermann Grassmann was one of the more advanced innovators with his anticipation of [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and [multilinear algebra](wikipedia://en/243946) that he called "Extension theory" (*Ausdehnungslehre*). As recounted by [David E. Rowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Rowe) in 2010:

The most important new convert was Victor Schlegel, Grassmann’s colleague at Stettin Gymnasium from 1866 to 1868. Afterward Schlegel accepted a position as Oberlehrer at the Gymnasium in [Waren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waren_%28Müritz%29), a small town in [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg).

In 1872 Schlegel published the first part of his *System der Raumlehre* which used Grassmann’s methods to develop plane geometry. Schlegel used his book to put forth Grassmann’s case, arguing that "Grassmann’s ideas had been neglected because he had not held a university chair." Continuing his criticism of academics, Schlegel expressed the reactionary view that

Rather than developing a sound basis for their analytic methods, contemporary mathematicians tended to invent new symbolisms on an ad hoc basis, creating a Babel-like cacophony of unintelligible languages.

Schlegel’s attitude was that no basis for [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method) was shown in mathematics, and "neglect of foundations had led to the widely acknowledged lack of interest in mathematics in the schools."

The mathematician [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) addressed Schlegel’s book in a review[^o3] criticizing him for neglect of [cross ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_ratio) and failure to contextualize Grassmann in the flow of mathematical developments. Rowe indicates that Klein was most interested in developing his [Erlangen program](wikipedia://en/243382). In 1875 Schlegel countered with the second part of his *System der Raumlehre*, answering Klein in the preface. This part developed [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673), [harmonic ranges](wikipedia://en/16094600), [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), and [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468).

Schlegel published a [biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biography) of Hermann Grassmann in 1878. Both parts of his textbook, and the biography, are now available at the [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive); see External links section below.

At the Summer meeting of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) on August 15, 1894, Schlegel presented an essay on the problem of finding the place which is at a minimum total distance from given points.[^o4]

In 1899 Schlegel became German national secretary for the international [Quaternion Association](wikipedia://en/16416512) and reported on it in [Monatshefte für Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monatshefte_für_Mathematik).[^o5]
