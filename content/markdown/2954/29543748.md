# Oppermann's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oppermann's conjecture"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

Is every pair of a square number and a pronic number (both greater than one) separated by at least one prime?

:::

**Oppermann's conjecture** is an unsolved problem in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) on the distribution of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).[^n1] It is closely related to but stronger than [Legendre's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2620450), [Andrica's conjecture](wikipedia://en/7878893), and [Brocard's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2620525). It is named after Danish mathematician [Ludvig Oppermann](wikipedia://en/34849134), who announced it in an unpublished lecture in March 1877.[^n2]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The conjecture states that, for every integer $n>1$, there is at least one prime number between

$n(n-1)$ and $n^2$,

and at least another prime between

$n^2$ and $n(n+1)$.

It can also be phrased equivalently as stating that the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826) must take unequal values at the endpoints of each range.[^n3] That is:

$\pi(n^2-n)<\pi(n^2)<\pi(n^2+n)$ for every $n>1$

with $\pi(x)$ being the number of prime numbers less than or equal to $x$. The end points of these two ranges are a [square](wikipedia://en/659939) between two [pronic numbers](wikipedia://en/652733), with each of the pronic numbers being twice a pair [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915). The sum of the pair of triangular numbers is the square.

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

If the conjecture is true, then the [prime gap](wikipedia://en/5075259) would be on the order of

$$g_n < \sqrt{p_n}.\,$$

This also means there would be at least two primes between $n^2$ and $(n+1)^2$ (one in the range from $n^2$ to $n(n+1)$ and the second in the range from $n(n+1)$ to $(n+1)^2$, strengthening [Legendre's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2620450) that there is at least one prime in this range. Because there is at least one non-prime between any two odd primes it would also imply [Brocard's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2620525) that there are at least four primes between the squares of consecutive odd primes.[^n1] Additionally, it would imply that the largest possible [gaps](wikipedia://en/5075259) between two consecutive prime numbers could be at most proportional to twice the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of the numbers, as [Andrica's conjecture](wikipedia://en/7878893) states.

The conjecture also implies that at least one prime can be found in every quarter revolution of the [Ulam spiral](wikipedia://en/67378).
