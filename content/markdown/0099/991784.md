# Circumscribed sphere

*Image: Вписанный куб.gif.* Circumscribed sphere of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Circumscribed sphere"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **circumscribed sphere** of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) is a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) that contains the polyhedron and touches each of the polyhedron's [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).[^o2] The word **circumsphere** is sometimes used to mean the same thing, by analogy with the term *[circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432)*.[^o3] As in the case of two-dimensional circumscribed circles (circumcircles), the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of a sphere circumscribed around a polyhedron $P$ is called the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) of $P$,[^o4] and the center point of this sphere is called the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of $P$.[^o5]

:::

## Existence and optimality {#Existence_and_optimality}

When it exists, a circumscribed sphere need not be the [smallest sphere containing the polyhedron](wikipedia://en/14355284); for instance, the tetrahedron formed by a vertex of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and its three neighbors has the same circumsphere as the cube itself, but can be contained within a smaller sphere having the three neighboring vertices on its equator. However, the smallest sphere containing a given polyhedron is always the circumsphere of the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of a subset of the vertices of the polyhedron.[^o7]

In *De solidorum elementis* (circa 1630), [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) observed that, for a polyhedron with a circumscribed sphere, all faces have circumscribed circles, the circles where the plane of the face meets the circumscribed sphere. Descartes suggested that this necessary condition for the existence of a circumscribed sphere is sufficient, but it is not true: some [bipyramids](wikipedia://en/4153), for instance, can have circumscribed circles for their faces (all of which are triangles) but still have no circumscribed sphere for the whole polyhedron. However, whenever a [simple polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_polyhedron) has a circumscribed circle for each of its faces, it also has a circumscribed sphere.[^o8]

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

The circumscribed sphere is the three-dimensional analogue of the [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262). All [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra) have circumscribed spheres, but most irregular polyhedra do not have one, since in general not all vertices lie on a common sphere. The circumscribed sphere (when it exists) is an example of a [bounding sphere](wikipedia://en/779188), a sphere that contains a given shape. It is possible to define the smallest bounding sphere for any polyhedron, and compute it in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time).[^o7]

Other spheres defined for some but not all polyhedra include a [midsphere](wikipedia://en/4709281), a sphere tangent to all edges of a polyhedron, and an [inscribed sphere](wikipedia://en/991786), a sphere tangent to all faces of a polyhedron. In the [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra), the inscribed sphere, midsphere, and circumscribed sphere all exist and are [concentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric_spheres).[^o10]

When the circumscribed sphere is the set of infinite limiting points of [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742), a polyhedron that it circumscribes is known as an [ideal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/64419430).

## Point on the circumscribed sphere {#Point_on_the_circumscribed_sphere}

There are five convex [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra), known as the [Platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solids). All Platonic solids have circumscribed spheres. For an arbitrary point $M$ on the circumscribed sphere of each Platonic solid with number of the vertices $n$, if $MA_i$ are the distances to the vertices $A_i$, then[^o12]

$$4(MA_1^{2}+MA_2^{2}+...+MA_n^{2})^2=3n(MA_1^{4}+MA_2^{4}+...+MA_n^{4}).$$
