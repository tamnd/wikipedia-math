# Interior algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Interior algebra"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), an **interior algebra** is a certain type of [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) that encodes the idea of the topological [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of a set. Interior algebras are to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and the [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) **S4** what [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959) are to [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and ordinary [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154). Interior algebras form a [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) of [modal algebras](wikipedia://en/17257316).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

An **interior algebra** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) with the [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156)

⟨*S*, ·, +, ′, 0, 1, <sup>I</sup>⟩

where

⟨*S*, ·, +, ′, 0, 1⟩

is a [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959) and postfix <sup>I</sup> designates a [unary operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unary_operator), the **interior operator**, satisfying the identities:

1. *x*<sup>I</sup> ≤ *x*
1. *x*<sup>II</sup> = *x*<sup>I</sup>
1. (*xy*)<sup>I</sup> = *x*<sup>I</sup>*y*<sup>I</sup>
1. 1<sup>I</sup> = 1

*x*<sup>I</sup> is called the **interior** of *x*.

The [dual](wikipedia://en/600618) of the interior operator is the **[closure operator](wikipedia://en/483120)** <sup>C</sup> defined by *x*<sup>C</sup> = ((*x*′)<sup>I</sup>)′. *x*<sup>C</sup> is called the **closure** of *x*. By the [principle of duality](wikipedia://en/600618), the closure operator satisfies the identities:

1. *x*<sup>C</sup> ≥ *x*
1. *x*<sup>CC</sup> = *x*<sup>C</sup>
1. (*x* + *y*)<sup>C</sup> = *x*<sup>C</sup> + *y*<sup>C</sup>
1. 0<sup>C</sup> = 0

If the closure operator is taken as primitive, the interior operator can be defined as *x*<sup>I</sup> = ((*x*′)<sup>C</sup>)′. Thus the theory of interior algebras may be formulated using the closure operator instead of the interior operator, in which case one considers **closure algebras** of the form ⟨*S*, ·, +, ′, 0, 1, <sup>C</sup>⟩, where ⟨*S*, ·, +, ′, 0, 1⟩ is again a Boolean algebra and <sup>C</sup> satisfies the above identities for the closure operator. Closure and interior algebras form [dual](wikipedia://en/600618) pairs, and are paradigmatic instances of "Boolean algebras with operators." The early literature on this subject (mainly Polish topology) invoked closure operators, but the interior operator formulation eventually became the norm following the work of [Wim Blok](wikipedia://en/51156457).

:::

## Open and closed elements {#Open_and_closed_elements}

Elements of an interior algebra satisfying the condition *x*<sup>I</sup> = *x* are called **[open](wikipedia://en/39358)**. The [complements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complement_%28order_theory%29) of open elements are called **[closed](wikipedia://en/47279)** and are characterized by the condition *x*<sup>C</sup> = *x*. An interior of an element is always open and the closure of an element is always closed. Interiors of closed elements are called **[regular open](wikipedia://en/47306)** and closures of open elements are called **regular closed**. Elements that are both open and closed are called **[clopen](wikipedia://en/233398)**. 0 and 1 are clopen.

An interior algebra is called **Boolean** if all its elements are open (and hence clopen). Boolean interior algebras can be identified with ordinary Boolean algebras as their interior and closure operators provide no meaningful additional structure. A special case is the class of **trivial** interior algebras, which are the single element interior algebras characterized by the identity 0 = 1.

## Morphisms of interior algebras {#Morphisms_of_interior_algebras}

### Homomorphisms {#Homomorphisms}

Interior algebras, by virtue of being [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364), have [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564). Given two interior algebras *A* and *B*, a map *f* : *A* → *B* is an **interior algebra homomorphism** [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *f* is a homomorphism between the underlying Boolean algebras of *A* and *B*, that also preserves interiors and closures. Hence:

- *f*(*x*<sup>I</sup>) = *f*(*x*)<sup>I</sup>;
- *f*(*x*<sup>C</sup>) = *f*(*x*)<sup>C</sup>.

### Topomorphisms {#Topomorphisms}

Topomorphisms are another important, and more general, class of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) between interior algebras. A map *f* : *A* → *B* is a topomorphism if and only if *f* is a homomorphism between the Boolean algebras underlying *A* and *B*, that also preserves the open and closed elements of *A*. Hence:

- If *x* is open in *A*, then *f*(*x*) is open in *B*;
- If *x* is closed in *A*, then *f*(*x*) is closed in *B*.

(Such morphisms have also been called *stable homomorphisms* and *closure algebra semi-homomorphisms*.) Every interior algebra homomorphism is a topomorphism, but not every topomorphism is an interior algebra homomorphism.

### Boolean homomorphisms {#Boolean_homomorphisms}

Early research often considered mappings between interior algebras that were homomorphisms of the underlying Boolean algebras but that did not necessarily preserve the interior or closure operator. Such mappings were called **Boolean homomorphisms**. (The terms *closure homomorphism* or *topological homomorphism* were used in the case where these were preserved, but this terminology is now redundant as the standard definition of a homomorphism in [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) requires that it preserves all operations.) Applications involving countably complete interior algebras (in which countable [meets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_%28order_theory%29) and [joins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_%28order_theory%29) always exist, also called *σ-complete*) typically made use of countably complete Boolean homomorphisms also called **Boolean** *σ*-**homomorphisms**—these preserve countable meets and joins.

### Continuous morphisms {#Continuous_morphisms}

The earliest generalization of continuity to interior algebras was [Sikorski](wikipedia://en/1983335)'s, based on the [inverse image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_image) map of a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map). This is a Boolean homomorphism, preserves unions of sequences and includes the closure of an inverse image in the inverse image of the closure. Sikorski thus defined a *continuous homomorphism* as a Boolean *σ*-homomorphism *f* between two *σ*-complete interior algebras such that *f*(*x*)<sup>C</sup> ≤ *f*(*x*<sup>C</sup>). This definition had several difficulties: The construction acts [contravariantly](wikipedia://en/10987#Covariance_and_contravariance) producing a dual of a continuous map rather than a generalization. On the one hand *σ*-completeness is too weak to characterize inverse image maps (completeness is required), on the other hand it is too restrictive for a generalization. (Sikorski remarked on using non-*σ*-complete homomorphisms but included *σ*-completeness in his axioms for *closure algebras*.) Later J. Schmid defined a **continuous homomorphism** or **continuous morphism** for interior algebras as a Boolean homomorphism *f* between two interior algebras satisfying *f*(*x*<sup>C</sup>) ≤ *f*(*x*)<sup>C</sup>. This generalizes the forward image map of a continuous map—the image of a closure is contained in the closure of the image. This construction is [covariant](wikipedia://en/10987#Covariance_and_contravariance) but not suitable for [category theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_theoretic) applications as it only allows construction of continuous morphisms from continuous maps in the case of bijections. (C. Naturman returned to Sikorski's approach while dropping *σ*-completeness to produce topomorphisms as defined above. In this terminology, Sikorski's original "continuous homomorphisms" are *σ*-complete topomorphisms between *σ*-complete interior algebras.)

## Relationships to other areas of mathematics {#Relationships_to_other_areas_of_mathematics}

### Topology {#Topology}

Given a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) ***X*** = ⟨*X*, *T*⟩ one can form the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) Boolean algebra of *X*:

$$\langle P(X), \cap, \cup,', \text{ø}, X\rangle$$

and extend it to an interior algebra

$$\boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \langle P(X), \cap, \cup,', \text{ø}, X,^{I}\rangle,$$

where <sup>I</sup> is the usual topological interior operator. For all *S* ⊆ *X* it is defined by

$$S^{I} = \cup \{O\mid O \subseteq S\text{ and } O\text{ is } \operatorname{open}\text{ in } \boldsymbol{X}\}$$

For all *S* ⊆ *X* the corresponding closure operator is given by

$$S^{C} = \cap \{C\mid S \subseteq C\text{ and } C\text{ is } \operatorname{closed}\text{ in } \boldsymbol{X}\}$$

*S*<sup>I</sup> is the largest open subset of *S* and *S*<sup>C</sup> is the smallest closed superset of *S* in ***X***. The open, closed, regular open, regular closed and clopen elements of the interior algebra ***A***(***X***) are just the open, closed, regular open, regular closed and clopen subsets of ***X*** respectively in the usual topological sense.

Every [complete](wikipedia://en/549445) [atomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_%28order_theory%29) interior algebra is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to an interior algebra of the form ***A***(***X***) for some [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) ***X***. Moreover, every interior algebra can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in such an interior algebra giving a representation of an interior algebra as a **[topological field of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676#Topological_fields_of_sets)**. The properties of the structure ***A***(***X***) are the very motivation for the definition of interior algebras. Because of this intimate connection with topology, interior algebras have also been called **topo-Boolean algebras** or **topological Boolean algebras**.

Given a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) between two topological spaces

$$f : \boldsymbol{X} \to \boldsymbol{Y}$$

we can define a [complete](wikipedia://en/549445) topomorphism

$$\boldsymbol{A}(f) : \boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{Y}) \to \boldsymbol{A}(\boldsymbol{X})$$

by

***A***(*f*)(*S*) = *f*<sup>−1</sup>\[*S*\]

for all subsets *S* of ***Y***. Every complete topomorphism between two complete atomic interior algebras can be derived in this way. If **Top** is the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) and continuous maps and **Cit** is the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) of complete atomic interior algebras and complete topomorphisms then **Top** and **Cit** are [dually isomorphic](wikipedia://en/310885) and $\boldsymbol{A} : \mathbf{Top} \to \mathbf{Cit}$ is a [contravariant functor](wikipedia://en/10987) that is a dual isomorphism of categories. ***A***(*f*) is a homomorphism if and only if *f* is a continuous [open map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_map).

Under this dual isomorphism of categories many natural topological properties correspond to algebraic properties, in particular connectedness properties correspond to irreducibility properties:

- ***X*** is [empty](wikipedia://en/9566) if and only if ***A***(***X***) is trivial
- ***X*** is [indiscrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_space) if and only if ***A***(***X***) is [simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_algebra)
- ***X*** is [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) if and only if ***A***(***X***) is Boolean
- ***X*** is [almost discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_discrete_space) if and only if ***A***(***X***) is [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group)
- ***X*** is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/542198) (Alexandrov) if and only if ***A***(***X***) is **operator complete** i.e. its interior and closure operators distribute over arbitrary meets and joins respectively
- ***X*** is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) if and only if ***A***(***X***) is [directly indecomposable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directly_indecomposable)
- ***X*** is [ultraconnected](wikipedia://en/8101374) if and only if ***A***(***X***) is [finitely subdirectly irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_subdirectly_irreducible)
- ***X*** is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) ultra-connected if and only if ***A***(***X***) is [subdirectly irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdirectly_irreducible)

#### Generalized topology {#Generalized_topology}

The modern formulation of topological spaces in terms of [topologies](wikipedia://en/30450) of open subsets, motivates an alternative formulation of interior algebras: A **generalized topological space** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) of the form

⟨*B*, ·, +, ′, 0, 1, *T*⟩

where ⟨*B*, ·, +, ′, 0, 1⟩ is a Boolean algebra as usual, and *T* is a unary relation on *B* (subset of *B*) such that:

1. $$0,1 \in T$$
1. *T* is closed under arbitrary joins (i.e. if a join of an arbitrary subset of *T* exists then it will be in *T*)
1. *T* is closed under finite meets
1. For every element *b* of *B*, the join $\Sigma\{a \in T\mid a \le b\}$ exists

*T* is said to be a **generalized topology** in the Boolean algebra.

Given an interior algebra its open elements form a generalized topology. Conversely given a generalized topological space

⟨*B*, ·, +, ′, 0, 1, *T*⟩

we can define an interior operator on *B* by $b^{I} = \Sigma\{a \in T\mid a \le b\}$ thereby producing an interior algebra whose open elements are precisely *T*. Thus generalized topological spaces are equivalent to interior algebras.

Considering interior algebras to be generalized topological spaces, topomorphisms are then the standard homomorphisms of Boolean algebras with added relations, so that standard results from [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) apply.

#### Neighbourhood functions and neighbourhood lattices {#Neighbourhood_functions_and_neighbourhood_lattices}

The topological concept of [neighbourhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) can be generalized to interior algebras: An element *y* of an interior algebra is said to be a **neighbourhood** of an element *x* if $x \le y^{I}$. The set of neighbourhoods of *x* is denoted by *N*(*x*) and forms a [filter](wikipedia://en/19719). This leads to another formulation of interior algebras:

A **neighbourhood function** on a Boolean algebra is a mapping *N* from its underlying set *B* to its set of filters, such that:

1. For all $x \in B, \max\{y \in B\mid x \in N(y)\}$ exists
1. For all $x,y \in B, x \in N(y)$ if and only if there is a $z \in B$ such that $y \le z \le x$ and $z \in N(z)$.

The mapping *N* of elements of an interior algebra to their filters of neighbourhoods is a neighbourhood function on the underlying Boolean algebra of the interior algebra. Moreover, given a neighbourhood function *N* on a Boolean algebra with underlying set *B*, we can define an interior operator by $x^{I} = \max\{y \in B\mid x \in N(y)\}$ thereby obtaining an interior algebra. $N(x)$ will then be precisely the filter of neighbourhoods of *x* in this interior algebra. Thus interior algebras are equivalent to Boolean algebras with specified neighbourhood functions.

In terms of neighbourhood functions, the open elements are precisely those elements *x* such that $x \in N(x)$. In terms of open elements $x \in N(y)$ if and only if there is an open element *z* such that $y \le z \le x$.

Neighbourhood functions may be defined more generally on [(meet)-semilattices](wikipedia://en/755647) producing the structures known as [neighbourhood (semi)lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_lattice). Interior algebras may thus be viewed as precisely the **Boolean neighbourhood lattices** i.e. those neighbourhood lattices whose underlying semilattice forms a Boolean algebra.

### Modal logic {#Modal_logic}

Given a [theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_%28logic%29) (set of formal sentences) *M* in the modal logic **S4**, we can form its [Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra](wikipedia://en/990343):

***L***(*M*) = ⟨*M* / ~, ∧, ∨, ¬, *F*, *T*, □⟩

where ~ is the equivalence relation on sentences in *M* given by *p* ~ *q* if and only if *p* and *q* are [logically equivalent](wikipedia://en/169324) in *M*, and *M* / ~ is the set of equivalence classes under this relation. Then ***L***(*M*) is an interior algebra. The interior operator in this case corresponds to the [modal operator](wikipedia://en/333365) □ (**necessarily**), while the closure operator corresponds to ◊ (**possibly**). This construction is a special case of a more general result for [modal algebras](wikipedia://en/17257316) and modal logic.

The open elements of ***L***(*M*) correspond to sentences that are only true if they are **necessarily** true, while the closed elements correspond to those that are only false if they are **necessarily** false.

Because of their relation to **S4**, interior algebras are sometimes called **S4 algebras** or **Lewis algebras**, after the [logician](wikipedia://en/304939) [C. I. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Irving_Lewis), who first proposed the modal logics **S4** and **S5**.

:::

### Preorders {#Preorders}

Since interior algebras are (normal) [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959) with [operators](wikipedia://en/161006), they can be represented by [fields of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676) on appropriate relational structures. In particular, since they are [modal algebras](wikipedia://en/17257316), they can be represented as [fields of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676) on a set with a single [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931), called a [Kripke frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kripke_frame). The Kripke frames corresponding to interior algebras are precisely the [preordered sets](wikipedia://en/23582). [Preordered sets](wikipedia://en/23582) (also called *S4-frames*) provide the [Kripke semantics](wikipedia://en/911960) of the modal logic **S4**, and the connection between interior algebras and preorders is deeply related to their connection with modal logic.

Given a [preordered set](wikipedia://en/23582) ***X*** = ⟨*X*, «⟩ we can construct an interior algebra

$$\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \langle P(X), \cap, \cup,', \text{ø}, X,^{I}\rangle$$

from the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959) of *X* where the interior operator <sup>I</sup> is given by

$S^{I} = \{x \in X\mid \text{ for }\text{ all } y \in X, x \text{«} y \operatorname{implies} y \in S\}$ for all *S* ⊆ *X*.

The corresponding closure operator is given by

$S^{C} = \{x \in X\mid \text{ there }\text{ exists } a y \in S\text{ with } y \text{«} x\}$ for all *S* ⊆ *X*.

*S*<sup>I</sup> is the set of all *worlds* inaccessible from *worlds* outside *S*, and *S*<sup>C</sup> is the set of all *worlds* accessible from some *world* in *S*. Every interior algebra can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in an interior algebra of the form ***B***(***X***) for some [preordered set](wikipedia://en/23582) ***X*** giving the above-mentioned representation as a [field of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676) (a **preorder field**).

This construction and representation theorem is a special case of the more general result for [modal algebras](wikipedia://en/17257316) and Kripke frames. In this regard, interior algebras are particularly interesting because of their connection to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). The construction provides the [preordered set](wikipedia://en/23582) ***X*** with a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450), the [Alexandrov topology](wikipedia://en/542198), producing a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) ***T***(***X***) whose open sets are:

$$\{O \subseteq X\mid \text{ for }\text{ all } x \in O\text{ and }\text{ all } y \in X, x \text{«} y \operatorname{implies} y \in O\}.$$

The corresponding closed sets are:

$$\{C \subseteq X\mid \text{ for }\text{ all } x \in C\text{ and }\text{ all } y \in X, y \text{«} x \operatorname{implies} y \in C\}.$$

In other words, the open sets are the ones whose *worlds* are inaccessible from outside (the **up-sets**), and the closed sets are the ones for which every outside *world* is inaccessible from inside (the **down-sets**). Moreover, ***B***(***X***) = ***A***(***T***(***X***)).

### Monadic Boolean algebras {#Monadic_Boolean_algebras}

Any [monadic Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/1018197) can be considered to be an interior algebra where the interior operator is the universal quantifier and the closure operator is the existential quantifier. The monadic Boolean algebras are then precisely the [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) of interior algebras satisfying the identity *x*<sup>IC</sup> = *x*<sup>I</sup>. In other words, they are precisely the interior algebras in which every open element is closed or equivalently, in which every closed element is open. Moreover, such interior algebras are precisely the [semisimple](wikipedia://en/3876151) interior algebras. They are also the interior algebras corresponding to the modal logic **S5**, and so have also been called **S5 algebras**.

In the relationship between preordered sets and interior algebras they correspond to the case where the preorder is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259), reflecting the fact that such preordered sets provide the Kripke semantics for **S5**. This also reflects the relationship between the [monadic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monadic_logic) of quantification (for which monadic Boolean algebras provide an [algebraic description](wikipedia://en/990343)) and **S5** where the modal operators □ (**necessarily**) and ◊ (**possibly**) can be interpreted in the Kripke semantics using monadic universal and existential quantification, respectively, without reference to an accessibility relation.

### Heyting algebras {#Heyting_algebras}

The open elements of an interior algebra form a [Heyting algebra](wikipedia://en/309343) and the closed elements form a [dual](wikipedia://en/600618) Heyting algebra. The regular open elements and regular closed elements correspond to the [pseudo-complemented](wikipedia://en/43705090) elements and [dual](wikipedia://en/600618) pseudo-complemented elements of these algebras respectively and thus form Boolean algebras. The clopen elements correspond to the complemented elements and form a common subalgebra of these Boolean algebras as well as of the interior algebra itself. Every [Heyting algebra](wikipedia://en/309343) can be represented as the open elements of an interior algebra and the latter may be chosen to be an interior algebra generated by its open elements—such interior algebras correspond one-to-one with Heyting algebras (up to isomorphism) being the free Boolean extensions of the latter.

Heyting algebras [play the same role](wikipedia://en/990343) for [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) that interior algebras play for the modal logic **S4** and [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959) play for [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154). The relation between Heyting algebras and interior algebras reflects the relationship between intuitionistic logic and **S4**, in which one can interpret theories of intuitionistic logic as **S4** theories [closed](wikipedia://en/7750539) under [necessity](wikipedia://en/3986852). The one-to-one correspondence between Heyting algebras and interior algebras generated by their open elements reflects the correspondence between extensions of intuitionistic logic and [normal](wikipedia://en/911820) extensions of the modal logic **S4.Grz**.

### Derivative algebras {#Derivative_algebras}

Given an interior algebra ***A***, the closure operator obeys the axioms of the [derivative operator](wikipedia://en/1022286), <sup>D</sup>. Hence we can form a [derivative algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) ***D***(***A***) with the same underlying Boolean algebra as ***A*** by using the closure operator as a derivative operator.

Thus interior algebras are [derivative algebras](wikipedia://en/1022286). From this perspective, they are precisely the [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) of derivative algebras satisfying the identity *x*<sup>D</sup> ≥ *x*. Derivative algebras provide the appropriate [algebraic semantics](wikipedia://en/990343) for the modal logic **wK4**. Hence derivative algebras stand to topological [derived sets](wikipedia://en/1567335) and **wK4** as interior/closure algebras stand to topological interiors/closures and **S4**.

Given a derivative algebra ***V*** with derivative operator <sup>D</sup>, we can form an interior algebra $\boldsymbol{I}(\boldsymbol{V})$ with the same underlying Boolean algebra as ***V***, with interior and closure operators defined by $x^{I} = x\cdot x'^{D}{}'$ and $x^{C} = x + x^{D}$, respectively. Thus every derivative algebra can be regarded as an interior algebra. Moreover, given an interior algebra ***A***, we have $\boldsymbol{I}(\boldsymbol{D}(\boldsymbol{A})) = \boldsymbol{A}$. However, $\boldsymbol{D}(\boldsymbol{I}(\boldsymbol{V})) = \boldsymbol{V}$ does *not* necessarily hold for every derivative algebra ***V***.

## Stone duality and representation for interior algebras {#Stone_duality_and_representation_for_interior_algebras}

[Stone duality](wikipedia://en/379619) provides a category theoretic duality between Boolean algebras and a class of topological spaces known as [Boolean spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_space). Building on nascent ideas of relational semantics (later formalized by [Kripke](wikipedia://en/48039)) and a result of R. S. Pierce, [Jónsson](wikipedia://en/3893573), [Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) and G. Hansoul extended Stone duality to [Boolean algebras with operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_algebras_with_operators) by equipping Boolean spaces with relations that correspond to the operators via a [power set construction](wikipedia://en/1018676#Complex_algebras_and_fields_of_sets_on_relational_structures). In the case of interior algebras the interior (or closure) operator corresponds to a pre-order on the Boolean space. Homomorphisms between interior algebras correspond to a class of continuous maps between the Boolean spaces known as **pseudo-epimorphisms** or **p-morphisms** for short. This generalization of Stone duality to interior algebras based on the Jónsson–Tarski representation was investigated by Leo Esakia and is also known as the *Esakia duality for S4-algebras (interior algebras)* and is closely related to the [Esakia duality](wikipedia://en/26600432) for Heyting algebras.

Whereas the Jónsson–Tarski generalization of Stone duality applies to Boolean algebras with operators in general, the connection between interior algebras and topology allows for another method of generalizing Stone duality that is unique to interior algebras. An intermediate step in the development of Stone duality is [Stone's representation theorem](wikipedia://en/604111), which represents a Boolean algebra as a [field of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676). The Stone topology of the corresponding Boolean space is then generated using the field of sets as a [topological basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_basis). Building on the [topological semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_semantics) introduced by Tang Tsao-Chen for Lewis's modal logic, [McKinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.C.C._McKinsey) and Tarski showed that by generating a topology equivalent to using only the complexes that correspond to open elements as a basis, a representation of an interior algebra is obtained as a [topological field of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676#topological_field_of_sets)—a field of sets on a topological space that is closed with respect to taking interiors or closures. By equipping topological fields of sets with appropriate morphisms known as **field maps**, C. Naturman showed that this approach can be formalized as a category theoretic Stone duality in which the usual Stone duality for Boolean algebras corresponds to the case of interior algebras having redundant interior operator (Boolean interior algebras).

The pre-order obtained in the Jónsson–Tarski approach corresponds to the accessibility relation in the Kripke semantics for an S4 theory, while the intermediate field of sets corresponds to a representation of the Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra for the theory using the sets of possible worlds in the Kripke semantics in which sentences of the theory hold. Moving from the field of sets to a Boolean space somewhat obfuscates this connection. By treating fields of sets on pre-orders as a category in its own right this deep connection can be formulated as a category theoretic duality that generalizes Stone representation without topology. R. Goldblatt had shown that with restrictions to appropriate homomorphisms such a duality can be formulated for arbitrary modal algebras and Kripke frames. Naturman showed that in the case of interior algebras this duality applies to more general topomorphisms and can be factored via a category theoretic functor through the duality with topological fields of sets. The latter represent the Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra using sets of points satisfying sentences of the S4 theory in the topological semantics. The pre-order can be obtained as the specialization pre-order of the McKinsey–Tarski topology. The Esakia duality can be recovered via a functor that replaces the field of sets with the Boolean space it generates. Via a functor that instead replaces the pre-order with its corresponding Alexandrov topology, an alternative representation of the interior algebra as a field of sets is obtained where the topology is the Alexandrov bico-reflection of the McKinsey–Tarski topology. The approach of formulating a topological duality for interior algebras using both the Stone topology of the Jónsson–Tarski approach and the Alexandrov topology of the pre-order to form a bi-topological space has been investigated by G. Bezhanishvili, R.Mines, and P.J. Morandi. The McKinsey–Tarski topology of an interior algebra is the intersection of the former two topologies.

## Metamathematics {#Metamathematics}

Grzegorczyk proved the [first-order theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theory) of closure algebras [undecidable](wikipedia://en/8336).[^o22][^o23] Naturman demonstrated that the theory is [hereditarily undecidable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditarily_undecidable) (all its subtheories are undecidable) and demonstrated an infinite [chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_%28order_theory%29) of elementary classes of interior algebras with hereditarily undecidable theories.
