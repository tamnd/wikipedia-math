# Louis J. Mordell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Louis J. Mordell"}

**Louis Joel Mordell** (28 January 1888 – 12 March 1972) was an American-born British mathematician, known for his research in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He was born in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), in a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) family of [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania) extraction.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Mordell was educated at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) where he completed the [Cambridge Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Tripos) as a student of [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), starting in 1906 after successfully passing the scholarship examination.[^o5] He graduated as [third wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1909.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

After graduating Mordell began independent research into particular [diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109): the question of [integer points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_point) on the [cubic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_curve), and special case of what is now called a [Thue equation](wikipedia://en/1151323), the [Mordell equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_equation)

*y*<sup>2</sup> = *x*<sup>3</sup> + *k*.

He took an appointment at [Birkbeck College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck,_University_of_London) in 1913. During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) he was involved in war work, but also produced one of his major results, proving in 1917 the multiplicative property of [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717)'s [tau-function](wikipedia://en/1231708). The proof was by means, in effect, of the [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837), which had not yet been named after [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990); it was, in retrospect, one of the major advances in [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000) theory, beyond its status as an odd corner of the theory of [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function).

In 1920, he took a teaching position in [UMIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology). At a General Meeting of the [Manchester Literary & Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_&_Philosophical_Society) on 2 November 1920, Mr. L. J. Morpett, B.A., Lecturer in Mathematics, The College of Technology, Manchester; was elected to membership. .[^o10] He became the holder of the [Fielden Chair of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielden_Chair_of_Pure_Mathematics) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) in 1922 and Professor in 1923. There he developed a third area of interest within number theory, the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153). His basic work on [Mordell's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell's_theorem) is from 1921 to 1922, as is the formulation of the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture). He was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in 1928 in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna) and in 1932 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) and a Plenary Speaker of the ICM in 1936 in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo).[^o11]

He took British citizenship in 1929. In Manchester he also built up the department, offering posts to a number of outstanding mathematicians who had been forced from posts on the continent of Europe. He brought in [Reinhold Baer](wikipedia://en/1612114), G. Billing, [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), [Chao Ko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Ko), [Kurt Mahler](wikipedia://en/4421496), and [Beniamino Segre](wikipedia://en/3534031). He also recruited [J. A. Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._A._Todd), [Patrick du Val](wikipedia://en/10637467), [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111) and [Laurence Chisholm Young](wikipedia://en/9620408), and invited distinguished visitors.

In 1945, he returned to Cambridge as a Fellow of [St. John's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), when elected to the [Sadleirian Chair](wikipedia://en/291556), and became Head of Department. He officially retired in 1953. It was at this time that he had his only formal research students, of whom [J. W. S. Cassels](wikipedia://en/1709206) was one. His idea of supervising research was said to involve the suggestion that a proof of the [transcendence](wikipedia://en/30325) of the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant) was probably worth a doctorate. His book *Diophantine Equations* (1969) is based on lectures, and gives an idea of his discursive style. Mordell is said to have hated administrative duties.[^o12]

## Anecdote {#Anecdote}

While visiting the [University of Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calgary), the elderly Mordell attended the Number Theory seminars and would frequently fall asleep during them. According to a story by number theorist [Richard K. Guy](wikipedia://en/816308), the department head at the time, after Mordell had fallen asleep, someone in the audience asked "Isn't that [Stickelberger's theorem](wikipedia://en/2202290)?" The speaker said "No it isn't." A few minutes later the person interrupted again and said "I'm positive that's Stickelberger's theorem!" The speaker again said no it wasn't. The lecture ended, and the applause woke up Mordell, and he looked up and pointed at the board, saying "There's old [Stickelberger](wikipedia://en/18870377)'s result!"
