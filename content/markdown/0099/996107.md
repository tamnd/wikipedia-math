# Universal coefficient theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Universal coefficient theorem"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), **universal coefficient theorems** (UCT) establish relationships between [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) (or [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group)) with different coefficients. For instance, for every [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$, its *integral homology groups*:

:::

$$H_i(X,\mathbb{Z})$$

completely determine its *homology groups with coefficients in* $A$, for any [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) $A$:

$$H_i(X,A)$$

Here $H_i$ might be the [simplicial homology](wikipedia://en/1060236), or more generally the [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041). The usual proof of this result is a pure piece of [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) about [chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404) of [free abelian groups](wikipedia://en/247296). The form of the result is that other coefficients $A$ may be used, at the cost of using a [Tor functor](wikipedia://en/856347).

For example, it is common to take $A$ to be $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, so that coefficients are modulo 2. This becomes straightforward in the absence of 2-[torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330) in the homology. Quite generally, the result indicates the relationship that holds between the [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) $b_i$ of $X$ and the Betti numbers $b_{i,F}$ with coefficients in a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $F$. These can differ, but only when the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of $F$ is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ for which there is some $p$-torsion in the homology.

## Statement of the homology case {#Statement_of_the_homology_case}

Consider the [tensor product of modules](wikipedia://en/2993836) $H_i(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes A$. The theorem states there is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) involving the [Tor functor](wikipedia://en/856347)

$$0 \to H_i(X, \mathbb{Z})\otimes A \, \overset{\mu}\to \, H_i(X,A) \to \operatorname{Tor}_1(H_{i-1}(X, \mathbb{Z}),A)\to 0.$$

Furthermore, this sequence [splits](wikipedia://en/138378), though not naturally. Here $\mu$ is the map induced by the bilinear map $H_i(X,\mathbb{Z})\times A\to H_i(X,A)$.

If the coefficient ring $A$ is $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, this is a special case of the [Bockstein spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/31328492).

## Universal coefficient theorem for cohomology {#Universal_coefficient_theorem_for_cohomology}

Let $G$ be a module over a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) domain $R$ (for example $\mathbb{Z}$, or any field.)

There is a **universal coefficient theorem for [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060)** involving the [Ext functor](wikipedia://en/840758), which asserts that there is a natural short exact sequence

$$0 \to \operatorname{Ext}_R^1(H_{i-1}(X; R), G) \to H^i(X; G) \, \overset{h} \to \, \operatorname{Hom}_R(H_i(X; R), G)\to 0.$$

As in the homology case, the sequence splits, though not naturally. In fact, suppose

$$H_i(X;G) = \ker \partial_i \otimes G / \operatorname{im}\partial_{i+1} \otimes G,$$

and define

$$H^*(X; G) = \ker(\operatorname{Hom}(\partial, G)) / \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Hom}(\partial, G)).$$

Then $h$ above is the canonical map:

$$h([f])([x]) = f(x).$$

An alternative point of view can be based on representing cohomology via [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230), where the map $h$ takes a [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) class of maps $X\to K(G,i)$ to the corresponding homomorphism induced in homology. Thus, the Eilenberg–MacLane space is a *weak right [adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor)* to the homology [functor](wikipedia://en/10987).[^o5]

:::

## Example: mod 2 cohomology of the real projective space {#Example:_mod_2_cohomology_of_the_real_projective_space}

Let $X=\mathbb{RP}^n$, the [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694). We compute the singular cohomology of $X$ with coefficients in $G=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ using integral homology, i.e., $R=\mathbb{Z}$.

Knowing that the integer homology is given by:

$$H_i(X; \mathbb{Z}) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & i = 0 \text{ or } i = n \text{ odd,}\\ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} & 0<i<n,\ i\ \text{odd,}\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

We have $\operatorname{Ext}(G,G)=G$ and $\operatorname{Ext}(R,G)=0$, so that the above exact sequences yield

$$H^i (X; G) = G$$

for all $i=0,\dots,n$. In fact the total [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) structure is

$$H^*(X; G) = G [w] / \left \langle w^{n+1} \right \rangle.$$

## Corollaries {#Corollaries}

A special case of the theorem is computing integral cohomology. For a finite [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) $X$, $H_i(X,\mathbb{Z})$ is finitely generated, and so we have the following [decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_theorem_of_finitely_generated_abelian_groups#Classification).

$$H_i(X; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{\beta_i(X)}\oplus T_{i},$$

where $\beta_i(X)$ are the [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) of $X$ and $T_i$ is the torsion part of $H_i$. One may check that

$$\operatorname{Hom}(H_i(X),\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{\beta_i(X)},\mathbb{Z}) \oplus \operatorname{Hom}(T_i, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{\beta_i(X)},$$

and

$$\operatorname{Ext}(H_i(X),\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}^{\beta_i(X)},\mathbb{Z}) \oplus \operatorname{Ext}(T_i, \mathbb{Z}) \cong T_i.$$

This gives the following statement for integral cohomology:

$$H^i(X;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{\beta_i(X)} \oplus T_{i-1}.$$

For $X$ an [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446), [closed](wikipedia://en/669475), and [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) $n$-[manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), this corollary coupled with [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) gives that $\beta_i(X)=\beta_{n-i}(X)$.

## Universal coefficient spectral sequence {#Universal_coefficient_spectral_sequence}

There is a generalization of the universal coefficient theorem for (co)homology with [twisted coefficients](wikipedia://en/32182870).

For cohomology we have

$$E^{p,q}_2=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^q(H_p(C_*),G)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(C_*;G),$$

where $R$ is a ring with unit, $C_*$ is a chain complex of free modules over $R$, $G$ is any $(R,S)$-bimodule for some ring with a unit $S$, and $\operatorname{Ext}$ is the [Ext group](wikipedia://en/840758). The differential $d^r$ has degree $(1-r,r)$.

Similarly for homology,

$$E_{p,q}^2=\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_q(H_p(C_*),G)\Rightarrow H_*(C_*;G),$$

for $\operatorname{Tor}$ the [Tor group](wikipedia://en/856347) and the differential $d_r$ having degree $(r-1,-r)$.
