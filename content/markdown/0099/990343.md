# Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), the **Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra** (or **Lindenbaum algebra**) of a [logical theory](wikipedia://en/19858#Definition) *T* consists of the [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of [sentences](wikipedia://en/2129527) of the theory (i.e., the [quotient](wikipedia://en/9260)) under the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) ~ defined such that *p* ~ *q* exactly when *p* and *q* are provably equivalent in *T*. That is, two sentences are equivalent if the theory *T* proves that each implies the other. The Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra is thus the [quotient algebra](wikipedia://en/310953) obtained by factoring the algebra of formulas by this [congruence relation](wikipedia://en/48167).

:::

The algebra is named for [logicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) [Adolf Lindenbaum](wikipedia://en/1029330) and [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920). Starting in the academic year 1926–1927, Lindenbaum pioneered his method in [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064)'s mathematical logic seminar,[^o2][^o3] and the method was popularized and generalized in subsequent decades through work by Tarski.[^o4]

The Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra is considered the origin of the modern [algebraic logic](wikipedia://en/7870034).[^o5]

## Operations {#Operations}

The operations in a Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra *A* are inherited from those in the underlying theory *T*. These typically include [conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152) and [disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjunction), which are [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined) on the equivalence classes. When [negation](wikipedia://en/161019) is also present in *T*, then *A* is a [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959), provided the logic is [classical](wikipedia://en/55868). If the theory *T* consists of the [propositional tautologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus), the Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra is the [free Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3058220) generated by the [propositional variables](wikipedia://en/1557562).

If *T* is closed for deduction, then the embedding of *T/~* in *A* is a [filter](wikipedia://en/19719). Moreover, an [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911) in *A* corresponds to a [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335) consistent theory, establishing the equivalence between [Lindenbaum's lemma](wikipedia://en/6625296) and the [ultrafilter lemma](wikipedia://en/67416519).

## Related algebras {#Related_algebras}

[Heyting algebras](wikipedia://en/309343) and [interior algebras](wikipedia://en/990454) are the Lindenbaum–Tarski algebras for [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) and the [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) **S4**, respectively.

A logic for which Tarski's method is applicable, is called *algebraizable*. There are however a number of logics where this is not the case, for instance the modal logics **S1**, **S2**, or **S3**, which lack the [rule of necessitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_necessitation) (⊢φ implying ⊢□φ), so ~ (defined above) is not a congruence (because ⊢φ→ψ does not imply ⊢□φ→□ψ). Another type of logic where Tarski's method is inapplicable is [relevance logics](wikipedia://en/185076), because given two theorems an implication from one to the other may not itself be a theorem in a relevance logic.[^o5] The study of the algebraization process (and notion) as topic of interest by itself, not necessarily by Tarski's method, has led to the development of [abstract algebraic logic](wikipedia://en/4613861).
