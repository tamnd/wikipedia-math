# Area (graph drawing)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Area"}

In [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813), the **area** used by a drawing is a commonly used way of measuring its quality.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a drawing style in which the vertices are placed on the [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402), the area of the drawing may be defined as the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of the smallest axis-aligned [bounding box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounding_box) of the drawing: that is, it the product of the largest difference in *x*-coordinates of two vertices with the largest difference in *y*-coordinates. For other drawing styles, in which vertices are placed more freely, the drawing may be scaled so that the [closest pair of vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closest_pair_of_points_problem) have distance one from each other, after which the area can again be defined as the area of a smallest bounding box of a drawing. Alternatively, the area can be defined as the area of the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the drawing, again after appropriate scaling.[^o4]

:::

## Polynomial bounds {#Polynomial_bounds}

For straight-line drawings of [planar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_graphs) with *n* vertices, the optimal worst-case bound on the area of a drawing is Θ(*n*<sup>2</sup>). The [nested triangles graph](wikipedia://en/39338633) requires this much area no matter how it is embedded,[^o6] and several methods are known that can draw planar graphs with at most quadratic area.[^o7][^o8] [Binary trees](wikipedia://en/4321), and trees of bounded degree more generally, have drawings with linear or near-linear area, depending on the drawing style.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13] Every [outerplanar graph](wikipedia://en/352343) has a straight-line outerplanar drawing with area subquadratic in its number of vertices,[^o14][^o15] If [bends](wikipedia://en/43176911) or [crossings](wikipedia://en/18298594) are allowed, then outerplanar graphs have drawings with near-linear area.[^o16][^o17] However, drawing [series–parallel graphs](wikipedia://en/9938559) requires an area larger than *n* multiplied by a superpolylogarithmic factor, even if edges can be drawn as [polylines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyline).[^o18]

## Exponential bounds {#Exponential_bounds}

In contrast to these polynomial bounds, some drawing styles may exhibit [exponential growth](wikipedia://en/191933) in their areas, implying that these styles may be suitable only for small graphs. An example is [upward planar drawing](wikipedia://en/40148474) of planar [directed acyclic graphs](wikipedia://en/204002), where the area of an *n*-vertex drawing may be proportional to 2<sup>*n*</sup> in the worst case.[^o20] Even [plane trees](wikipedia://en/48560#Ordered_tree) may require exponential area, if they are to be drawn with straight edges that preserve a fixed [cyclic order](wikipedia://en/434673) around each vertex and must be equally spaced around the vertex.[^o21]
