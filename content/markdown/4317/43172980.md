# Generic matrix ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generic matrix ring"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **generic matrix ring** is a sort of a universal [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

We denote by $F_n$ a generic matrix ring of size *n* with variables $X_1, \dots X_m$. It is characterized by the universal property: given a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* and *n*-by-*n* [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) $A_1, \dots, A_m$ over *R*, there exists a **unique** ring homomorphism (called the evaluation map) $F_n \to M_n(R)$ extending the assignment $X_i \mapsto A_i$.

Explicitly, given a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k*, it is the [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239) $F_n$ of the matrix ring $M_n(k[(X_l)_{ij} \mid 1 \le l \le m,\ 1 \le i, j \le n])$ generated by *n*-by-*n* matrices $X_1, \dots, X_m$, where $(X_l)_{ij}$ are matrix entries and commute by definition. For example, if *m* = 1 then $F_1$ is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) in one variable.

For example, a [central polynomial](wikipedia://en/43161091) is an element of the ring $F_n$ that will map to a central element under an evaluation. (In fact, it is in the [invariant ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_ring) $k[(X_l)_{ij}]^{\operatorname{GL}_n(k)}$ since it is central and invariant.[^o4])

By definition, $F_n$ is a [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635) of the [free ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_ring) $k\langle t_1, \dots, t_m \rangle$ with $t_i \mapsto X_i$ by the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) consisting of all *p* that vanish identically on all *n*-by-*n* matrices over *k*.

:::

## Geometric perspective {#Geometric_perspective}

The universal property means that any ring homomorphism from $k\langle t_1, \dots, t_m \rangle$ to a matrix ring factors through $F_n$. This has a following geometric meaning. In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the polynomial ring $k[t, \dots, t_m]$ is the [coordinate ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) of the affine space $k^m$, and to give a point of $k^m$ is to give a ring homomorphism (evaluation) $k[t, \dots, t_m] \to k$ (either by [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215) or by the [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory)). The free ring $k\langle t_1, \dots, t_m \rangle$ plays the role of the coordinate ring of the affine space in the [noncommutative algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/25493347) (i.e., we don't demand free variables to commute) and thus a generic matrix ring of size *n* is the coordinate ring of a noncommutative affine variety whose points are the Spec's of matrix rings of size *n* (see below for a more concrete discussion.)

### The maximal spectrum of a generic matrix ring {#The_maximal_spectrum_of_a_generic_matrix_ring}

For simplicity, assume *k* is [algebraically closed](wikipedia://en/1018). Let *A* be an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) over *k* and let $\operatorname{Spec}_n(A)$ denote the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all [maximal ideals](wikipedia://en/48164) $\mathfrak{m}$ in *A* such that $A/\mathfrak{m} \approx M_n(k)$. If *A* is commutative, then $\operatorname{Spec}_1(A)$ is the [maximal spectrum](wikipedia://en/38256214#S) of *A* and $\operatorname{Spec}_n(A)$ is [empty](wikipedia://en/9566) for any $n > 1$.
