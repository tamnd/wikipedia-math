# Open coloring axiom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Open coloring axiom"}

The **open coloring axiom** (abbreviated **OCA**) is an [axiom](wikipedia://en/928) about coloring edges of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) are a subset of the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438): two different versions were introduced by (Abraham Rubin Shelah 1985) and by (Todorčević 1989).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that *X* is a subset of the reals, and each pair of elements of *X* is colored either black or white, with the set of white pairs being open in the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) on *X*. The open coloring axiom states that either:

1. *X* has an [uncountable](wikipedia://en/32248) subset such that any pair from this subset is white; or
1. *X* can be partitioned into a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable_infinity) number of subsets such that any pair from the same subset is black.

A weaker version, **OCA<sub>P</sub>**, replaces the uncountability condition in the first case with being a [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) [perfect set](wikipedia://en/5075551) in *X*. Both OCA and OCA<sub>P</sub> can be stated equivalently for arbitrary [separable spaces](wikipedia://en/27855).

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Relation to other axioms"}

## Relation to other axioms {#Relation_to_other_axioms}

OCA<sub>P</sub> can be proved in [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) for [analytic subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_subset) of a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622), and from the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300). The full OCA is consistent with (but independent of) ZFC, and follows from the [proper forcing axiom](wikipedia://en/6250799).

OCA implies that the smallest [unbounded set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbounded_set) of [Baire space](wikipedia://en/2078245) has cardinality $\aleph_2$. Moreover, assuming OCA, Baire space contains few "gaps" between sets of sequences — more specifically, that the only possible gaps are (ω<sub>1</sub>,ω<sub>1</sub>)-gaps and (κ,ω)-gaps where κ is an [initial ordinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_ordinal) not less than ω<sub>2</sub>.

:::
