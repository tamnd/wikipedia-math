# Ronald Fintushel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ronald Fintushel"}

**Ronald Alan Fintushel** (born 1945) is an American mathematician, specializing in low-dimensional [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732) (specifically of 4-[manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470)) and the mathematics of [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Fintushel studied mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) with a bachelor's degree in 1967 and at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign) with a master's degree in 1969.[^o3] In 1975 he received his Ph.D. from the [State University of New York at Binghamton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_at_Binghamton) with thesis *Orbit maps of local $S^1$-actions on manifolds of dimension less than five* under the supervision of Louis McAuley. Fintushel was a professor at [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University) and is a professor at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University).

His research deals with geometric topology, in particular of 4-manifolds (including the computation of Donaldson and Seiberg-Witten invariants) with links to gauge theory, [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), and [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298). He works closely with [Ronald J. Stern](wikipedia://en/46991782).

In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker, with Ronald J. Stern, with talk *Construction of smooth 4-manifolds* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o5] In 1997 Fintushel received the Distinguished Faculty Award from Michigan State University. In 2016 a conference was held in his honor at Tulane University.[^o6]

He was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). Fintushel is a member of the editorial boards of *[Geometry & Topology](wikipedia://en/2862181)* and the *[Michigan Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Mathematical_Journal)*.
