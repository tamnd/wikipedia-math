# Willerton's fish

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Willerton's fish"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), **Willerton's fish** is an unexplained relationship between the first two [Vassiliev invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassiliev_invariant) of a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808). These invariants are $c_{2}$, the quadratic [coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203) of the [Alexander–Conway polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander–Conway_polynomial), and $j_{3}$, an order-three invariant derived from the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642).[^o2][^o3]

:::

When the values of $c_{2}$ and $j_{3}$, for knots of a given fixed [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598), are used as the $x$ and $y$ coordinates of a [scatter plot](wikipedia://en/412544), the points of the plot appear to fill a fish-shaped region of the plane, with a lobed body and two sharp tail fins. The region appears to be bounded by [cubic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_curve),[^o3] suggesting that the crossing number, $c_{2}$, and $j_{3}$ may be related to each other by not-yet-proven [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489).[^o2]

This shape is named after Simon Willerton,[^o2] who first observed this phenomenon and described the shape of the scatterplots as "fish-like".[^o4]
