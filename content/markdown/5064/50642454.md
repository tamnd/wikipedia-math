# Ailles rectangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ailles rectangle"}

*Image: Ailles rectangle.png.* The Ailles rectangle

The **Ailles rectangle** is a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) constructed from four [right-angled triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-angled_triangle) which is commonly used in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) classes to find the values of [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) of 15° and 75°.[^o2] It is named after Douglas S. Ailles who was a high school teacher at [Kipling Collegiate Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipling_Collegiate_Institute) in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto).[^o3][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

A [30°–60°–90° triangle](wikipedia://en/2278116#30°–60°–90°_triangle) has sides of length 1, 2, and $\sqrt{3}$. When two such triangles are placed in the positions shown in the illustration, the smallest rectangle that can enclose them has width $1+\sqrt{3}$ and height $\sqrt{3}$. Drawing a line connecting the original triangles' top corners creates a [45°–45°–90° triangle](wikipedia://en/2278116#45°–45°–90°_triangle) between the two, with sides of lengths 2, 2, and (by the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034)) $2\sqrt{2}$. The remaining space at the top of the rectangle is a right triangle with acute angles of 15° and 75° and sides of $\sqrt{3}-1$, $\sqrt{3}+1$, and $2\sqrt{2}$.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement cue=named-heading name="Derived trigonometric formulas"}

## Derived trigonometric formulas {#Derived_trigonometric_formulas}

From the construction of the rectangle, it follows that

$$\sin 15^\circ = \cos 75^\circ = \frac{\sqrt3 - 1}{2\sqrt2} = \frac{\sqrt6 - \sqrt2} 4,$$

$$\sin 75^\circ = \cos 15^\circ = \frac{\sqrt3 + 1}{2\sqrt2} = \frac{\sqrt6 + \sqrt2} 4,$$

$$\tan 15^\circ = \cot 75^\circ = \frac{\sqrt3 - 1}{\sqrt3 + 1} = \frac{(\sqrt3 - 1)^2}{3 - 1} = 2 - \sqrt3,$$

and

$$\tan 75^\circ = \cot 15^\circ = \frac{\sqrt3 + 1}{\sqrt3 - 1} = \frac{(\sqrt3 + 1)^2}{3 - 1} = 2 + \sqrt3.$$

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variant"}

## Variant {#Variant}

An alternative construction (also by Ailles) places a 30°–60°–90° triangle in the middle with sidelengths of $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{6}$, and $2\sqrt{2}$. Its legs are each the hypotenuse of a 45°–45°–90° triangle, one with legs of length $1$ and one with legs of length $\sqrt{3}$.[^o11][^o12] The 15°–75°–90° triangle is the same as above.

:::
