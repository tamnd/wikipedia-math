# Order-4 hexagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Order-4 hexagonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **order-4 hexagonal tiling** is a [regular](wikipedia://en/505253#Hyperbolic_tilings) tiling of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/241291). It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of {6,4}.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

This tiling represents a hyperbolic [kaleidoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaleidoscope) of 6 mirrors defining a regular hexagon fundamental domain. This symmetry by [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678) is called \*222222 with six order-2 mirror intersections. In [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502) can be represented as \[6<sup>\*</sup>,4\], removing two of three mirrors (passing through the hexagon center). Adding a bisecting mirror through two vertices of a hexagonal fundamental domain defines a trapezohedral [\*4422 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4422_symmetry). Adding three bisecting mirrors through the vertices defines [\*443 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/443_symmetry). Adding three bisecting mirrors through the edge defines [\*3222 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3222_symmetry). Adding all six bisectors leads to full [\*642 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/642_symmetry).

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

There are seven distinct [uniform colorings](wikipedia://en/18624814) for the order-4 hexagonal tiling. They are similar to seven of the [uniform colorings of the square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809#Uniform_colorings), but exclude two cases with order-2 gyrational symmetry. Four of them have reflective constructions and [Coxeter diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) while three of them are undercolorings.

## Regular maps {#Regular_maps}

The [regular map](wikipedia://en/21533914) {6,4}<sub>3</sub> or {6,4}<sub>(4,0)</sub> can be seen as a 4-coloring on the {6,4} tiling. It also has a representation as a [petrial octahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrial_octahedron), {3,4}<sup>$\pi$</sup>, an abstract polyhedron with vertices and edges of an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), but instead connected by 4 [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) faces.

## Related polyhedra and tiling {#Related_polyhedra_and_tiling}

This tiling is topologically related as a part of sequence of regular tilings with [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) faces, starting with the [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719), with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {6,n}, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , progressing to infinity.

This tiling is also topologically related as a part of sequence of regular polyhedra and tilings with four faces per vertex, starting with the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {n,4}, and Coxeter diagram , with n progressing to infinity.
