# Wald's martingale

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wald's martingale"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Wald's martingale** is the name sometimes given to a [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536) used to study sums of [i.i.d. random variables](wikipedia://en/30876902). It is named after the mathematician [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430), who used these ideas in a series of influential publications.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

Wald's martingale can be seen as discrete-time equivalent of the [Doléans-Dade exponential](wikipedia://en/16986027).

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Let $(X_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables whose [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) $M: \theta \mapsto \mathbb{E}(e^{\theta X_1})$ is finite for some $\theta > 0$, and let $S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$, with $S_0 = 0$. Then, the process $(W_n)_{n \geq 0}$ defined by

$$W_n = \frac{e^{\theta S_n}}{M(\theta)^n}$$

is a martingale known as *Wald's martingale*.[^o7] In particular, $\mathbb{E}(W_n) = 1$ for all $n \geq 0$.

:::
