# Arnold's spectral sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arnold's spectral sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Arnold's spectral sequence** (also spelled **Arnol'd**) is a [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) used in [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763) and [normal form theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_form_%28mathematics%29) as an efficient computational tool for reducing a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) to [canonical form](wikipedia://en/515096) near [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424). It was introduced by [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490) in 1975.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

It is a spectral sequence for the filtered de Rham complex with

- The filtration coming from increasing order of poles along discriminant loci (Diagonals)
- E1-page has differential forms that have logarithmic singularities along the diagonals
- The differential encodes the relationships among the singular forms[^o7]
