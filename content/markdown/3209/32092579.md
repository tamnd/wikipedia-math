# Iwahori subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Iwahori subgroup"}

In algebra, an **Iwahori subgroup** is a subgroup of a [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) over a nonarchimedean [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) that is analogous to a [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of an algebraic group. A **parahoric subgroup** is a [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_subset) subgroup that is a finite union of double cosets of an Iwahori subgroup, so is analogous to a [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of an algebraic group. Iwahori subgroups are named after [Nagayoshi Iwahori](wikipedia://en/32092783), and "parahoric" is a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) of "parabolic" and "Iwahori". (Iwahori Matsumoto 1965) studied Iwahori subgroups for Chevalley groups over *p*-adic fields, and (Bruhat Tits 1972) extended their work to more general groups.

:::

Roughly speaking, an Iwahori subgroup of an algebraic group *G*(*K*), for a local field *K* with integers *O* and residue field *k*, is the inverse image in *G*(*O*) of a Borel subgroup of *G*(*k*).

A reductive group over a local field has a [Tits system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tits_system) (*B*,*N*), where *B* is a parahoric group, and the Weyl group of the Tits system is an [affine Coxeter group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_Coxeter_group).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

More precisely, Iwahori and parahoric subgroups can be described using the theory of affine [Tits buildings](wikipedia://en/275015). The (reduced) building *B*(*G*) of *G* admits a decomposition into **facets**. When *G* is [quasisimple](wikipedia://en/1642729) the facets are [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) and the facet decomposition gives *B*(*G*) the structure of a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686); in general, the facets are polysimplices, that is, products of simplices. The facets of maximal dimension are called the **alcoves** of the building.

When *G* is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1088386#Definitions) and [simply connected](wikipedia://en/1088386#Classification_of_split_reductive_groups), the parahoric subgroups are by definition the [stabilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) in *G* of a facet, and the Iwahori subgroups are by definition the stabilizers of an alcove. If *G* does not satisfy these hypotheses then similar definitions can be made, but with technical complications.

When *G* is semisimple but not necessarily simply connected, the stabilizer of a facet is too large and one defines a parahoric as a certain finite index subgroup of the stabilizer. The stabilizer can be endowed with a canonical structure of an *O*-group, and the finite index subgroup, that is, the parahoric, is by definition the *O*-points of the [algebraic connected component](wikipedia://en/35457783) of this *O*-group. It is important here to work with the algebraic connected component instead of the [topological connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29) because a nonarchimedean local field is [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected).

When *G* is an arbitrary reductive group, one uses the previous construction but instead takes the stabilizer in the subgroup of *G* consisting of elements whose image under any [character](wikipedia://en/19414433) of *G* is integral.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- The maximal parahoric subgroups of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*K*) are the stabilizers of O-[lattices](wikipedia://en/36565179) in *K*<sup>*n*</sup>. In particular, GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*O*) is a maximal parahoric. Every maximal parahoric of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*K*) is conjugate to GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*O*). The Iwahori subgroups are conjugated to the subgroup *I* of matrices in GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*O*) which reduce to an upper triangular matrix in GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*k*) where *k* is the residue field of *O*; parahoric subgroups are all groups between *I* and GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*O*), which map one-to-one to parabolic subgroups of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*k*) containing the upper triangular matrices.
- Similarly, the maximal parahoric subgroups of SL<sub>*n*</sub>(*K*) are the stabilizers of O-lattices in *K*<sup>*n*</sup>, and SL<sub>*n*</sub>(*O*) is a maximal parahoric. Unlike for GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*K*), however, SL<sub>*n*</sub>(*K*) has *n* conjugacy classes of maximal parahorics.
- When *G* is [commutative](wikipedia://en/504697), it has a unique maximal compact subgroup and a unique Iwahori subgroup, which is contained in the former. These groups do not always agree. For example, let *L* be a [finite separable extension](wikipedia://en/32769983) of *K* of ramification degree *e*. The torus *L<sup>×</sup>/K<sup>×</sup>* is compact. However, its Iwahori subgroup is *O<sub>L</sub><sup>×</sup>/O<sub>K</sub><sup>×</sup>*, a subgroup of index *e* whose cokernel is generated by a uniformizer of *L*.

:::
