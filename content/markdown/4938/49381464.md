# Wolfgang Walter

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Wolfgang Walter"}

**Wolfgang Ludwig Walter** (2 May 1927 – 26 June 2010) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who specialized in the theory of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). His textbook[^o4] on [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) became a standard graduate text on the subject at many institutions.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Wolfgang Walter was born in 1927 in [Schwäbisch Gmünd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwäbisch_Gmünd), [Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Württemberg). His school studies were interrupted in 1943 when he was drafted into the [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht). He served as a soldier on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_%28World_War_II%29), was subsequently wounded and later interned as a prisoner of war by US troops. In 1946 after his release he completed his school education and in the years 1947–1952 studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen), where he stayed on to study for his PhD under [Erich Kamke](wikipedia://en/22079478) and [Hellmuth Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368525), defending his thesis in 1956.[^o6] In 1986–1992 Walter held the post of president of [GAMM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesellschaft_für_Angewandte_Mathematik_und_Mechanik), the German society of applied mathematics and mechanics.[^o7]

He died in [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe) in 2010 at the age of 83.
