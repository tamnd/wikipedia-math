# Fedor Nazarov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fedor Nazarov"}

L'vovich **Fedor (Fedya) L'vovich Nazarov** (Фёдор (Фе́дя) Льво́вич Наза́ров; born 1967) is a Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). He has done research in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and its applications, in particular in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [classical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_analysis) (including [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), and [complex](wikipedia://en/5759) [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478)).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Fedor Nazarov received his Ph.D. from [St Petersburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) in 1993, with Victor Petrovich Havin[^o3] as advisor.[^o4] Before his Ph.D. studies, Nazarov received the Gold Medal and Special prize at the [International Mathematics Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in 1984.[^o5]

Nazarov worked at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) in [East Lansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Lansing,_Michigan) from 1995 to 2007 and at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) from 2007 to 2011. Since 2011, he has been a full professor of Mathematics at [Kent State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_University).[^o6]

## Awards {#Awards}

Nazarov was awarded the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) in 1999 "for his work in harmonic analysis, in particular, the [uncertainty principle](wikipedia://en/52247#Uncertainty_principle), and his contribution to the development of [Bellman function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellman_function) methods".[^o8]

He gave an [invited talk at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2010, on the topic of "Analysis".[^o9]
