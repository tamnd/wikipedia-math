# Correspondence (algebraic geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Correspondence"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **correspondence** between [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* and *W* is a subset *R* of *V*×*W*, that is closed in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744). In set theory, a subset of a Cartesian product of two sets is called a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) or correspondence; thus, a correspondence here is a relation that is defined by algebraic equations. There are some important examples, even when *V* and *W* are [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260): for example the [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837) of [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000) theory may be considered as correspondences of [modular curves](wikipedia://en/669552).

:::

However, the definition of a correspondence in algebraic geometry is not completely standard. For instance, Fulton, in his book on [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602),[^o2] uses the definition above. In literature, however, a correspondence from a variety *X* to a variety *Y* is often taken to be a subset *Z* of *X*×*Y* such that *Z* is finite and surjective over each component of *X*. Note the asymmetry in this latter definition; which talks about a correspondence from *X* to *Y* rather than a correspondence between *X* and *Y*. The typical example of the latter kind of correspondence is the graph of a function *f*:*X*→*Y*. Correspondences also play an important role in the construction of [motives](wikipedia://en/430680) (cf. [presheaf with transfers](wikipedia://en/57389784)).[^o3]
