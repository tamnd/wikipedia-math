# Harry Coonce

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Harry Coonce"}

**Harry Bernard Coonce** (March 19, 1938 – February 14, 2025) was an American mathematician notable for being the originator of the now-popular [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), launched in 1996, a web-based catalog of mathematics doctoral advisors and students.[^o3]

:::

Coonce conceived of the idea while reading the unsigned thesis of his [academic advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_advisor) Malcolm Robertson, in the [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) library, and wondering who his advisor's advisor was. The amount of time it took Coonce, without the existence of a central database of such information, to find out that Robertson's advisor was [C. Einar Hille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Einar_Hille), gave him the idea for the project. In a 2000 interview, Coonce estimated that the project would top out at about 80,000 entries.[^o4] In June 2016, the number of entries surpassed 200,000,[^o5] and had just over 300,000 as of December 2023.

## Background {#Background}

Coonce completed his PhD in 1969 at the University of Delaware with a dissertation on *A Variational Method for Functions of Bounded Boundary Rotation*.[^o7] Coonce was a mathematics professor of [Minnesota State University, Mankato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_University,_Mankato).

Coonce married Susan Schilling, a computer scientist who died in 2016.[^o8] He died on February 14, 2025, at the age of 86.[^o9]
