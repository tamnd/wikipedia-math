# Hyman Levy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyman Levy"}

**Hyman Levy** (28 February 1889 – 27 February 1975)[^o2] was a Scottish-Jewish mathematician, philosopher, author, political activist, and fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh).[^o3] For many years he taught mathematics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London), eventually retiring as [Emeritus Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_Professor).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Hyman Levy was born into an [orthodox Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) family in Scotland in 1889.[^o5] He was the third oldest of eight children of Minna Cohen and Marcus Levy. His father was a picture-framer and occasional art dealer in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). The family lived at 70 Bristo Street in Edinburgh's South Side.[^o6]

Levy attended [George Heriot's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot's_School)[^o7] a short distance from his family home, and was named the school *[Dux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dux)* in 1907.[^o8] He studied mathematics at [Edinburgh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_University). His leftist politics were evident early when he declined a chance for further study at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University)—on account of what he considered its complicity in Britain's class system—and instead chose to pursue mathematical research at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in Germany.[^o2][^o5] At the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), Levy returned to Britain. In 1916 he joined the [Royal Flying Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Flying_Corps).[^o9] It is unclear if he saw active service, but the experience inspired an interest in aerodynamics.

In 1916 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [Cargill Gilston Knott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill_Gilston_Knott), [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [James Robert Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Robert_Milne) and [George Alexander Carse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Carse).[^o9]

In 1918 he married Marion Aitken Fraser, a devout [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian),[^o7] which led to a rupture with his family who disapproved of the interfaith marriage.[^o5] The couple had a daughter and two sons.

Levy researched [aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics) at the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory_%28United_Kingdom%29) (NPL). He published papers and books on the subject, including *Aeronautics in Theory and Experiment* (1918), which was called "perhaps the earliest text covering, at advanced level, the whole theory of aeroplane design and operation."[^o10] After leaving NPL, he became a professor of mathematics at the [Royal College of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Science), [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London), where he later served as head of the mathematics department, and subsequently became dean of the Royal College itself.[^o5]

Levy's main areas of mathematical specialization were in numerical methods, numerical solutions of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), finite difference equations and statistics.[^o5] Among his important books were *Numerical Studies in Differential Equations* (1934), *Elements of Probability* (1936), and *Finite Difference Equations* (1959). He also made contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), including a 1963 paper comparing [Lemoine's conjecture](wikipedia://en/12552062) to [Goldbach's weak conjecture](wikipedia://en/147164).[^o11][^o12]

Levy was in the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_%28UK%29) from 1920 to 1931. He then joined the [Communist Party of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Communist_Party) (CPGB), and was a loyal member for 25 years. In 1956 he travelled to the Soviet Union and was appalled by what he regarded as the mistreatment of Russian-Jewish writers, artists and intellectuals.[^o13] He published a scathing article on the subject in January 1957, and then followed that with a book, *Jews and the National Question*. He was condemned by CPGB leadership for his "departure from Marxism",[^o13] culminating in his expulsion from the Party in 1958.[^o14]

Hyman Levy died in [Wimbledon, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon,_London) on 27 February 1975,[^o9] one day before his 86th birthday.
