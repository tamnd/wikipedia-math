# Double tangent bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double tangent bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), the **double tangent bundle** or the **second tangent bundle** refers to the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) (*TTM*,*π*<sub>*TTM*</sub>,*TM*) of the total space *TM* of the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) (*TM*,*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>,*M*) of a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) *M* .[^o2] A note on notation: in this article, we denote projection maps by their domains, e.g., *π*<sub>*TTM*</sub> : *TTM* → *TM*. Some authors index these maps by their ranges instead, so for them, that map would be written *π*<sub>*TM*</sub>.

:::

The second tangent bundle arises in the study of [connections](wikipedia://en/391816) and second order ordinary differential equations, i.e., [(semi)spray structures](wikipedia://en/1425185) on smooth manifolds, and it is not to be confused with the [second order jet bundle](wikipedia://en/928060).

## Secondary vector bundle structure and canonical flip {#Secondary_vector_bundle_structure_and_canonical_flip}

Since (*TM*,*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>,*M*) is a vector bundle in its own right, its tangent bundle has the [secondary vector bundle structure](wikipedia://en/26221731) (*TTM*,(*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>)<sub>\*</sub>,*TM*), where (*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>)<sub>\*</sub>:*TTM*→*TM* is the push-forward of the canonical projection *π*<sub>*TM*</sub>:*TM*→*M*. In the following we denote

$$\xi = \xi^k\frac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\Big|_x\in T_xM, \qquad X = X^k\frac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\Big|_x\in T_xM$$

and apply the associated coordinate system

$$\xi \mapsto (x^1,\ldots,x^n,\xi^1,\ldots,\xi^n)$$

on *TM*. Then the fibre of the secondary vector bundle structure at *X*∈*T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M* takes the form

$$(\pi_{TM})^{-1}_*(X) = \Big\{ \ X^k\frac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\Big|_\xi + Y^k\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^k}\Big|_\xi \ \Big| \ \xi\in T_xM \ , \ Y^1,\ldots,Y^n\in\mathbb{R} \ \Big\}.$$

The double tangent bundle is a [double vector bundle](wikipedia://en/45482173).

The **canonical flip**[^o4] is a smooth involution *j*:*TTM*→*TTM* that exchanges these [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) structures in the sense that it is a vector bundle isomorphism between (*TTM*,*π*<sub>*TTM*</sub>,*TM*) and (*TTM*,(*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>)<sub>\*</sub>,*TM*). In the associated coordinates on *TM* it reads as

$$j\Big(X^k\frac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\Big|_\xi + Y^k\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi^k}\Big|_\xi\Big) = \xi^k\frac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\Big|_X + Y^k\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi^k}\Big|_X.$$

The canonical flip has the property that for any *f*: **R**<sup>2</sup> → *M*,

$$\frac {\partial f} {{\partial t} {\partial s}} = j \circ \frac {\partial f} {{\partial s} {\partial t}}$$

where *s* and *t* are coordinates of the standard basis of **R** <sup>2</sup>. Note that both partial derivatives are functions from **R**<sup>2</sup> to *TTM*.

This property can, in fact, be used to give an intrinsic definition of the canonical flip.[^o5] Indeed, there is a submersion *p*: J<sup>2</sup><sub>0</sub> (**R**<sup>2</sup>,M) → *TTM* given by

$$p([f])=\frac {\partial f} {{\partial t} {\partial s}} (0,0)$$

where *p* can be defined in the space of two-jets at zero because only depends on *f* up to order two at zero. We consider the application:

$$J: J^2_0(\mathbb{R}^2,M) \to J^2_0(\mathbb{R}^2,M) \quad / \quad J([f])=[f \circ \alpha]$$

where α(*s*,*t*)= (*t*,*s*). Then *J* is compatible with the projection *p* and induces the canonical flip on the quotient *TTM*.

## Canonical tensor fields on the tangent bundle {#Canonical_tensor_fields_on_the_tangent_bundle}

As for any [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524), the tangent spaces *T*<sub>ξ</sub>(*T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M*) of the fibres *T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M* of the tangent bundle (*TM*,*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>,*M*) can be identified with the fibres *T*<sub>x</sub>*M* themselves. Formally this is achieved through the **vertical lift**, which is a natural vector space isomorphism vl<sub>ξ</sub>:*T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M*→*V*<sub>ξ</sub>(*T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M*) defined as

$$(\operatorname{vl}_\xi X)[f]:=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}f(x,\xi+tX), \qquad f\in C^\infty(TM).$$

The vertical lift can also be seen as a natural vector bundle isomorphism vl:(π<sub>*TM*</sub>)<sup>\*</sup>*TM*→*VTM* from the [pullback bundle](wikipedia://en/1665392) of (*TM*,*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>,*M*) over *π*<sub>*TM*</sub>:*TM*→*M* onto the vertical tangent bundle

$$VTM:=\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{TM})_* \subset TTM.$$

The vertical lift lets us define the **canonical vector field**

$$V:TM\to TTM; \qquad V_\xi := \operatorname{vl}_\xi\xi,$$

which is smooth in the slit tangent bundle *TM*\\0. The canonical vector field can be also defined as the infinitesimal generator of the Lie-group action

$$\mathbb R\times (TM\setminus 0) \to TM\setminus 0; \qquad (t,\xi) \mapsto e^t\xi.$$

Unlike the canonical vector field, which can be defined for any vector bundle, the **canonical endomorphism**

$$J:TTM\to TTM; \qquad J_\xi X := \operatorname{vl}_\xi(\pi_{TM})_*X, \qquad X\in T_\xi TM$$

is special to the tangent bundle. The canonical endomorphism *J* satisfies

$$\operatorname{Ran}(J)=\operatorname{Ker}(J)=VTM, \qquad \mathcal L_VJ= -J, \qquad [JX,JY]=J[JX,Y]+J[X,JY],$$

and it is also known as the **tangent structure** for the following reason. If (*E*,*p*,*M*) is any vector bundle with the canonical vector field *V* and a (1,1)-tensor field *J* that satisfies the properties listed above, with *VE* in place of *VTM*, then the vector bundle (*E*,*p*,*M*) is isomorphic to the tangent bundle (*TM*,*π*<sub>*TM*</sub>,*M*) of the base manifold, and *J* corresponds to the tangent structure of *TM* in this isomorphism.

There is also a stronger result of this kind [^o7] which states that if *N* is a 2*n*-dimensional manifold and if there exists a (1,1)-tensor field *J* on *N* that satisfies

$$\operatorname{Ran}(J)=\operatorname{Ker}(J), \qquad [JX,JY]=J[JX,Y]+J[X,JY],$$

then *N* is diffeomorphic to an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) of the total space of a tangent bundle of some *n*-dimensional manifold *M*, and *J* corresponds to the tangent structure of *TM* in this diffeomorphism.

In any associated coordinate system on *TM* the canonical vector field and the canonical endomorphism have the coordinate representations

$$V = \xi^k\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi^k}, \qquad J = dx^k\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi^k}.$$

## (Semi)spray structures {#(Semi)spray_structures}

A [Semispray structure](wikipedia://en/1425185) on a smooth manifold *M* is by definition a smooth vector field *H* on *TM* \\0 such that *JH*=*V*. An equivalent definition is that *j*(*H*)=*H*, where *j*:*TTM*→*TTM* is the canonical flip. A semispray *H* is a [spray](wikipedia://en/1425185), if in addition, \[*V*,*H*\]=*H*.

Spray and semispray structures are invariant versions of second order ordinary differential equations on *M*. The difference between spray and semispray structures is that the solution curves of sprays are invariant in positive [reparametrizations](wikipedia://en/453198) as point sets on *M*, whereas solution curves of semisprays typically are not.

## Nonlinear covariant derivatives on smooth manifolds {#Nonlinear_covariant_derivatives_on_smooth_manifolds}

The canonical flip makes it possible to define nonlinear covariant derivatives on smooth manifolds as follows. Let

$$T(TM\setminus 0) = H(TM\setminus 0) \oplus V(TM\setminus 0)$$

be an [Ehresmann connection](wikipedia://en/5731754) on the slit tangent bundle *TM*\\0 and consider the mapping

$$D:(TM\setminus 0)\times \Gamma(TM) \to TM; \quad D_XY := (\kappa\circ j)(Y_*X),$$

where *Y*<sub>\*</sub>:*TM*→*TTM* is the push-forward, *j*:*TTM*→*TTM* is the canonical flip and κ:*T*(*TM*/0)→*TM*/0 is the connector map. The mapping *D*<sub>*X*</sub> is a derivation in the module Γ (*TM*) of smooth vector fields on *M* in the sense that

- $$D_X(\alpha Y + \beta Z) = \alpha D_XY + \beta D_XZ, \qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R.$$
- $$D_X(fY) = X[f]Y + f D_XY, \qquad \qquad \qquad f\in C^\infty(M).$$

Any mapping *D*<sub>*X*</sub> with these properties is called a **(nonlinear) covariant derivative** [^o10] on *M*. The term *nonlinear* refers to the fact that this kind of covariant derivative *D*<sub>*X*</sub> on is not necessarily linear with respect to the direction *X*∈*TM*/0 of the differentiation.

Looking at the local representations one can confirm that the Ehresmann connections on (*TM*/0,π<sub>*TM*/0</sub>,*M*) and nonlinear covariant derivatives on *M* are in one-to-one correspondence. Furthermore, if *D*<sub>*X*</sub> is linear in *X*, then the Ehresmann connection is linear in the [secondary vector bundle structure](wikipedia://en/26221731), and *D*<sub>*X*</sub> coincides with its linear covariant derivative.
