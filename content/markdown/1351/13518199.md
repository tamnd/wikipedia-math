# Modular product of graphs

*Image: modular\_product.png.* The modular product of graphs.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modular product of graphs"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **modular product** of [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) $G$ and $H$ is a graph formed by combining $G$ and $H$ that has applications to [subgraph isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgraph_isomorphism). It is one of several different kinds of [graph products](wikipedia://en/11492935) that have been studied, generally using the same [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) set (the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of the [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) of vertices of the two graphs $G$ and $H$) but with different rules for determining which edges to include.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The vertex set of the modular product of $G$ and $H$ is the [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $V(G) \times V(H)$. Any two vertices $(u, v)$ and $(u' , v' )$ are adjacent in the modular product of $G$ and $H$ if and only if $u$ is distinct from $u'$, $v$ is distinct from $v'$, and either

- $u$ is adjacent with $u'$ and $v$ is adjacent with $v'$, **or**
- $u$ is not adjacent with $u'$ and $v$ is not adjacent with $v'$.

:::

## Application to subgraph isomorphism {#Application_to_subgraph_isomorphism}

Cliques in the modular product graph correspond to [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/247577) of [induced subgraphs](wikipedia://en/1655188) of $G$ and $H$. Therefore, the modular product graph can be used to reduce problems of induced subgraph isomorphism to problems of finding [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466) in graphs. Specifically, the [maximum common induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/4288963) of both $G$ and $H$ corresponds to the [maximum clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_clique) in their modular product. Although the problems of finding largest common induced subgraphs and of finding maximum cliques are both [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete), this reduction allows [clique-finding algorithms](wikipedia://en/249254) to be applied to the common subgraph problem.
