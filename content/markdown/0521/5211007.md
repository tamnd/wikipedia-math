# Lemniscate of Gerono

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lemniscate of Gerono"}

*Image: Lemniscate-of-Gerono.svg.* The lemniscate of Gerono

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **lemniscate of Gerono**, or **lemniscate of Huygens**, or **figure-eight curve**, is a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of degree four and [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) zero and is a [lemniscate](wikipedia://en/5210590) curve shaped like an [$\infty$](wikipedia://en/20110824) symbol, or figure eight. It has equation

:::

$$x^4-x^2+y^2 = 0.$$

It was studied by [Camille-Christophe Gerono](wikipedia://en/8885159).

## Parameterization {#Parameterization}

Because the curve is of genus zero, it can be parametrized by rational functions; one means of doing that is

$$x = \frac{t^2-1}{t^2+1},\ y = \frac{2t(t^2-1)}{(t^2+1)^2}.$$

Another representation is

$$x = \cos \varphi,\ y = \sin\varphi\,\cos\varphi = \sin(2\varphi)/2$$

which reveals that this lemniscate is a special case of a [Lissajous figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lissajous_figure).

## Dual curve {#Dual_curve}

The [dual curve](wikipedia://en/3127321) (see [Plücker formula](wikipedia://en/3127299)), pictured below, has therefore a somewhat different character. Its equation is

$$(x^2-y^2)^3 + 8y^4+20x^2y^2-x^4-16y^2=0.$$
