# Lemniscate

*Image: Lemniscate of Bernoulli.svg.* The [lemniscate of Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/250908) and its two [foci](wikipedia://en/1032607)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lemniscate"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **lemniscate** ( or ) is any of several figure-eight or $\infty$-shaped [curves](wikipedia://en/89246).[^o11][^o4] The word comes from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) la, meaning "decorated with ribbons",[^o3] from the Greek grc (λημνίσκος), meaning "ribbon",[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7] or which alternatively may refer to the [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool) from which the [ribbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbons) were made.[^o11]

:::

Curves that have been called a lemniscate include three [quartic plane curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve): the [hippopede](wikipedia://en/2737959) or [lemniscate of Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemniscate_of_Booth), the [lemniscate of Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/250908), and the [lemniscate of Gerono](wikipedia://en/5211007). The hippopede was studied by [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus) (5th century), but the term "lemniscate" was not used until the work of [Jacob Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/64740) in the late 17th century.

## History and examples {#History_and_examples}

### Lemniscate of Booth {#Lemniscate_of_Booth}

*Image: Lemniscate of Booth.png.* [Lemniscate of Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemniscate_of_Booth)

The consideration of curves with a figure-eight shape can be traced back to [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus), a Greek [Neoplatonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplatonist) philosopher and mathematician who lived in the 5th century AD. Proclus considered the [cross-sections](wikipedia://en/5350369) of a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) by a plane parallel to the axis of the torus. As he observed, for most such sections the cross section consists of either one or two ovals; however, when the plane is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the inner surface of the torus, the cross-section takes on a figure-eight shape, which Proclus called a [horse fetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobble_%28device%29) (a device for holding two feet of a horse together), or "hippopede" in Greek.[^o10] The name "lemniscate of Booth" for this curve dates to its study by the 19th-century mathematician [James Booth](wikipedia://en/44180309).[^o11]

The lemniscate may be defined as an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260), the zero set of the [quartic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_polynomial) $(x^2 + y^2)^2 - cx^2 - dy^2$ when the parameter *d* is negative (or zero for the special case where the lemniscate becomes a pair of externally tangent circles). For positive values of *d* one instead obtains the [oval of Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_of_Booth).

### Lemniscate of Bernoulli {#Lemniscate_of_Bernoulli}

*Image: Lemniskate bernoulli2.svg.* Lemniscate of Bernoulli

In 1680, [Cassini](wikipedia://en/5215428) studied a family of curves, now called the [Cassini oval](wikipedia://en/2040906), defined as follows: the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of all points, the product of whose distances from two fixed points, the curves' [foci](wikipedia://en/1032607), is a constant. Under very particular circumstances (when the half-distance between the points is equal to the square root of the constant) this gives rise to a lemniscate.

In 1694, [Johann Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/268886) studied the lemniscate case of the Cassini oval, now known as the [lemniscate of Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/250908) (shown above), in connection with a problem of "[isochrones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isochrone_curve)" that had been posed earlier by [Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz). Like the hippopede, it is an algebraic curve, the zero set of the polynomial $(x^2 + y^2)^2 - a^2 (x^2 - y^2)$. Bernoulli's brother [Jacob Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/64740) also studied the same curve in the same year, and gave it its name, the lemniscate.[^o13] It may also be defined geometrically as the locus of points whose product of distances from two foci equals the square of half the interfocal distance.[^o14] It is a special case of the hippopede (lemniscate of Booth), with $d=-c$, and may be formed as a cross-section of a torus whose inner hole and circular cross-sections have the same diameter as each other.[^o11] The [lemniscatic elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemniscatic_elliptic_function) are analogues of trigonometric functions for the lemniscate of Bernoulli, and the [lemniscate constants](wikipedia://en/2079065) arise in evaluating the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of this lemniscate.

### Lemniscate of Gerono {#Lemniscate_of_Gerono}

*Image: Lemniscate-of-Gerono2.svg.* Lemniscate of Gerono: solution set of $x^{4} - x^{2} + y^{2} = 0$[^o16]

Another lemniscate, the [lemniscate of Gerono](wikipedia://en/5211007) or lemniscate of Huygens, is the zero set of the quartic polynomial $y^2-x^2(a^2-x^2)$.[^o17][^o18] [Viviani's curve](wikipedia://en/2738100), a three-dimensional curve formed by intersecting a sphere with a cylinder, also has a figure eight shape, and has the lemniscate of Gerono as its planar projection.[^o19]

### Others {#Others}

Other figure-eight shaped algebraic curves include

- The [Devil's curve](wikipedia://en/12226619), a curve defined by the quartic equation $y^2 (y^2 - a^2) = x^2 (x^2 - b^2)$ in which one connected component has a figure-eight shape,[^o21]
- [Watt's curve](wikipedia://en/2738105), a figure-eight shaped curve formed by a mechanical linkage. Watt's curve is the zero set of the degree-six polynomial equation $(x^2+y^2)(x^2+y^2-d^2)^2+4a^2y^2(x^2+y^2-b^2)=0$ and has the lemniscate of Bernoulli as a special case.
