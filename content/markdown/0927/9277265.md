# Nina Snaith

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nina Snaith"}

**Nina Claire Snaith** is a British mathematician at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) working in [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory) and [quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Snaith was educated at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) where she received her PhD in 2000[^o4] for research supervised by [Jonathan Keating](wikipedia://en/46796537).[^o8]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

In 1998, Snaith and her then adviser [Jonathan Keating](wikipedia://en/46796537) conjectured a value for the leading coefficient of the asymptotics of the moments of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). Keating and Snaith's guessed value for the constant was based on random-matrix theory, following a trend that started with [Montgomery's pair correlation conjecture](wikipedia://en/21627970). Keating's and Snaith's work extended works[^o6] by [Brian Conrey](wikipedia://en/15375152), Ghosh, and Gonek, also conjectural, based on number theoretic [heuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic); Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein, and Snaith later conjectured the lower terms in the asymptotics of the moments.[^o7] Snaith's work appeared in her doctoral thesis *Random Matrix Theory and zeta functions*.

Snaith is currently Professor of Mathematical Physics at the University of Bristol.[^o9][^o10]

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 2008, Snaith was awarded the London Mathematical Society's [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913).

In 2014, she delivered the annual [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308) Lecture to honour the achievements of women in mathematics.[^o12]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Snaith is the daughter of mathematician Victor Snaith and sister of mathematician and musician [Dan Snaith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Snaith), mostly known by his artistic names Manitoba, Caribou, and Daphni.[^o14]
