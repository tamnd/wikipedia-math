# Kevin Buzzard

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kevin Buzzard"}

**Kevin Mark Buzzard** (born 21 September 1968) is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and currently a professor of pure mathematics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London). He specialises in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

While attending the [Royal Grammar School, High Wycombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Grammar_School,_High_Wycombe) he competed in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), where he won a bronze medal in 1986 and a gold medal with a perfect score in 1987.[^o5]

He obtained a B.A. degree in Mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he was [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) in 1990, and a [C.A.S.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certificate_of_Advanced_Study_in_Mathematics) in 1991.[^o7] He then received his Ph.D. under the supervision of [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) with a thesis titled *The levels of modular representations* in 1995.[^o7]

He took a lectureship at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) in 1998, a readership in 2002, and was appointed to a professorship in 2004. From October to December 2002 he held a visiting professorship at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), having previously worked at the [Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study,_Princeton) (1995), the [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_Berkeley) (1996-7), and the [Institute Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_Henri_Poincaré) in Paris (2000).[^o7]

He was awarded a Whitehead Prize by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2002 for "his distinguished work in number theory",[^o8] and the [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prizes) in 2008.[^o9]

In 2017, he launched an ongoing [formalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_verification) project and blog involving the [Lean theorem prover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_theorem_prover)[^o10] and has since promoted the use of computer [proof assistants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_assistants) in future mathematics research. He gave a plenary lecture at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 2022 on the topic.[^o11]

He was the PhD supervisor to musician [Dan Snaith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Snaith),[^o12] also known as Caribou, who received a PhD in mathematics from [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) for his work on *[Overconvergent Siegel Modular Symbols](wikipedia://en/7721927)*.[^o13]

In 2024, Buzzard and collaborators were awarded a five-year [EPSRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_and_Physical_Sciences_Research_Council) grant to begin formalising [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) in Lean.[^o14]
