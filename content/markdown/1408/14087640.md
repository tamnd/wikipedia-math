# Centroidal Voronoi tessellation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centroidal Voronoi tessellation"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **centroidal Voronoi tessellation** (**CVT**) is a special type of [Voronoi tessellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_tessellation) in which the generating point of each Voronoi cell is also its [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) (center of mass). It can be viewed as an optimal partition corresponding to an optimal distribution of generators. A number of algorithms can be used to generate centroidal Voronoi tessellations, including [Lloyd's algorithm](wikipedia://en/2607912) for [K-means clustering](wikipedia://en/1860407) or [Quasi-Newton methods](wikipedia://en/7883127) like [BFGS](wikipedia://en/1926409). [^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

Gersho's conjecture, proven for one and two dimensions, says that "asymptotically speaking, all cells of the optimal CVT, while forming a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671), are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to a basic cell which depends on the dimension."[^o5]

In two dimensions, the basic cell for the optimal CVT is a regular [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) as it is proven to be the most dense [packing of circles](wikipedia://en/16879665) in 2D Euclidean space. Its three dimensional equivalent is the [rhombic dodecahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombic_dodecahedral_honeycomb), derived from the most dense [packing of spheres](wikipedia://en/901260) in 3D Euclidean space.

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Centroidal Voronoi tessellations are useful in [data compression](wikipedia://en/8013), optimal [quadrature](wikipedia://en/170089), optimal [quantization](wikipedia://en/317018), [clustering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_clustering), and optimal mesh generation.[^o8]

A weighted centroidal Voronoi diagrams is a CVT in which each centroid is weighted according to a certain function. For example, a [grayscale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grayscale) image can be used as a density function to weight the points of a CVT, as a way to create digital [stippling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stippling).[^o9]

:::

## Occurrence in nature {#Occurrence_in_nature}

Many [patterns seen in nature](wikipedia://en/35659147) are closely approximated by a centroidal Voronoi tessellation. Examples of this include the [Giant's Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant's_Causeway), the cells of the [cornea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornea),[^o11] and the breeding pits of the male [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia).[^o8]
