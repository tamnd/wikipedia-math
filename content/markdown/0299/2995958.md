# Potential isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Potential isomorphism"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and in particular in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a **potential isomorphism** is a collection of finite [partial isomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_isomorphism) between two models which satisfies certain closure conditions. Existence of a partial isomorphism entails [elementary equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementarily_equivalent), however the converse is not generally true, but it holds for [ω-saturated models](wikipedia://en/535836).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **potential isomorphism** between two models *M* and *N* is a non-empty collection *F* of finite partial isomorphisms between *M* and *N* which satisfy the following two properties:

- for all finite partial isomorphisms *Z* ∈ *F* and for all *x* ∈ *M* there is a *y* ∈ *N* such that *Z* ∪ {(*x*,*y*)} ∈ *F*
- for all finite partial isomorphisms *Z* ∈ *F* and for all *y* ∈ *N* there is a *x* ∈ *M* such that *Z* ∪ {(*x*,*y*)} ∈ *F*

A notion of [Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé_game) is an exact characterisation of elementary equivalence and potential isomorphism can be seen as an approximation of it. Another notion that is similar to potential isomorphism is that of [local isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_isomorphism).

:::
