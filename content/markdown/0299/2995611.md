# Theta correspondence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theta correspondence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **theta correspondence** or **Howe correspondence** is a mathematical relation between [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) of two [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of a [reductive dual pair](wikipedia://en/11802934). The local theta correspondence relates irreducible [admissible representations](wikipedia://en/11055227) over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375), while the global theta correspondence relates irreducible [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation) over a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358).

:::

The theta correspondence was introduced by [Roger Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Evans_Howe) in (Howe 1979). Its name arose due to its origin in [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019)'s representation theoretical formulation of the theory of [theta series](wikipedia://en/449745) in (Weil 1964). The [Shimura correspondence](wikipedia://en/28754333) as constructed by [Jean-Loup Waldspurger](wikipedia://en/31862139) in (Waldspurger 1980) and (Waldspurger 1991) may be viewed as an instance of the theta correspondence.

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

### Setup {#Setup}

Let $F$ be a local or a global field, not of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) $2$. Let $W$ be a [symplectic vector space](wikipedia://en/292852) over $F$, and $Sp(W)$ the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977).

Fix a [reductive dual pair](wikipedia://en/11802934) $(G,H)$ in $Sp(W)$. There is a classification of reductive dual pairs.[^n1] [^n2]

### Local theta correspondence {#Local_theta_correspondence}

$F$ is now a local field. Fix a non-trivial additive [character](wikipedia://en/293418) $\psi$ of $F$. There exists a [Weil representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_representation) of the [metaplectic group](wikipedia://en/2981795) $Mp(W)$ associated to $\psi$, which we write as $\omega_{\psi}$.

Given the reductive dual pair $(G,H)$ in $Sp(W)$, one obtains a pair of [commuting](wikipedia://en/294390) subgroups $(\widetilde{G}, \widetilde{H})$ in $Mp(W)$ by pulling back the projection map from $Mp(W)$ to $Sp(W)$.

The local theta correspondence is a 1-1 correspondence between certain irreducible admissible representations of $\widetilde{G}$ and certain irreducible admissible representations of $\widetilde{H}$, obtained by restricting the Weil representation $\omega_{\psi}$ of $Mp(W)$ to the subgroup $\widetilde{G}\cdot\widetilde{H}$. The correspondence was defined by [Roger Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Evans_Howe) in (Howe 1979). The assertion that this is a 1-1 correspondence is called the **Howe duality conjecture**.

Key properties of local theta correspondence include its compatibility with Bernstein-Zelevinsky induction [^n3] and conservation relations concerning the first occurrence indices along Witt towers .[^n4]

### Global theta correspondence {#Global_theta_correspondence}

[Stephen Rallis](wikipedia://en/52065873) showed a version of the global Howe duality conjecture for [cuspidal automorphic representations](wikipedia://en/6043458) over a global field, assuming the validity of the Howe duality conjecture for all local places. [^n5]

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Howe duality conjecture"}

## Howe duality conjecture {#Howe_duality_conjecture}

Define $\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G},\omega_{\psi})$ the set of irreducible admissible representations of $\widetilde{G}$, which can be realized as quotients of $\omega_{\psi}$. Define $\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{H},\omega_{\psi})$ and $\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}\cdot\widetilde{H},\omega_{\psi})$, likewise.

The **Howe duality conjecture** asserts that $\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}\cdot\widetilde{H},\omega_{\psi})$ is the graph of a bijection between $\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G},\omega_{\psi})$ and $\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{H},\omega_{\psi})$.

The Howe duality conjecture for [archimedean](wikipedia://en/264158) local fields was proved by [Roger Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Evans_Howe).[^n6] For $p$-adic local fields with $p$ odd it was proved by [Jean-Loup Waldspurger](wikipedia://en/31862139).[^n7] Alberto Mínguez later gave a proof for dual pairs of [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564), that works for arbitrary residue characteristic. [^n8] For orthogonal-symplectic or unitary dual pairs, it was proved by [Wee Teck Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wee_Teck_Gan) and Shuichiro Takeda. [^n9] The final case of quaternionic dual pairs was completed by [Wee Teck Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wee_Teck_Gan) and [Binyong Sun](wikipedia://en/62414012).[^n10]

:::
