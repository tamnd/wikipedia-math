# Bauer–Fike theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bauer–Fike theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Bauer–Fike theorem** is a standard result in the [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/102140) of the [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of a complex-valued [diagonalizable matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonalizable). In its substance, it states an absolute upper bound for the deviation of one perturbed matrix eigenvalue from a properly chosen eigenvalue of the exact matrix. Informally speaking, what it says is that *the sensitivity of the eigenvalues is estimated by the condition number of the matrix of eigenvectors*.

:::

The theorem was proved by [Friedrich L. Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_L._Bauer) and C. T. Fike in 1960.

## The setup {#The_setup}

In what follows we assume that:

- $A \in \mathbf{C}^{n,n}$ is a [diagonalizable matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonalizable);
- $V \in \mathbf{C}^{n,n}$ is the non-singular [eigenvector matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector_matrix) such that $A = V\Lambda V^{-1}$, where $\Lambda$ is a diagonal matrix.
- If $X \in \mathbf{C}^{n,n}$ is invertible, its [condition number](wikipedia://en/6934) in [$p$-norm](wikipedia://en/1543735) is denoted by $\kappa_{p}(X)$ and defined by:

$$\kappa_p(X)=\|X\|_p \left \|X^{-1} \right \|_p.$$

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Bauer–Fike Theorem"}

## The Bauer–Fike Theorem {#The_Bauer–Fike_Theorem}

**Bauer–Fike Theorem.** Let $\mu$ be an eigenvalue of $A + \delta A$. Then there exists $\lambda \in \Lambda(A)$ such that:

$$|\lambda-\mu| \leq \kappa_p (V) \| \delta A \|_p$$

::: {#o5 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof.** We can suppose $\mu \notin \Lambda(A)$, otherwise take $\lambda = \mu$ and the result is trivially true since $\kappa_{p}(V) \ge 1$. Since $\mu$ is an eigenvalue of $A + \delta A$, we have $\det(A + \delta A - \mu I) = 0$ and so

$$\begin{aligned} 0 &= \det(A+\delta A-\mu I) \\ &= \det(V^{-1})\det(A+\delta A-\mu I)\det(V) \\ &= \det \left ( V^{-1} (A+\delta A-\mu I) V \right ) \\ &= \det \left ( V^{-1}AV + V^{-1}\delta AV- V^{-1} \mu I V \right ) \\ &= \det \left (\Lambda+V^{-1}\delta AV-\mu I \right ) \\ &= \det(\Lambda-\mu I)\det \left ((\Lambda-\mu I)^{-1}V^{-1}\delta AV +I \right ) \\ \end{aligned}$$

However our assumption, $\mu \notin \Lambda(A)$, implies that: $\det(\Lambda - \mu I) \ne 0$ and therefore we can write:

$$\det \left ((\Lambda-\mu I)^{-1}V^{-1}\delta AV +I \right ) = 0.$$

This reveals $-1$ to be an eigenvalue of

$$(\Lambda-\mu I)^{-1}V^{-1}\delta AV.$$

Since all $p$-norms are [consistent matrix norms](wikipedia://en/1543735) we have $|\lambda| \le ||A||_{p}$ where $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$. In this instance this gives us:

$$1 = |-1| \leq \left \|(\Lambda-\mu I)^{-1}V^{-1}\delta AV \right \|_p \leq \left \|(\Lambda-\mu I)^{-1} \right \|_p \left \|V^{-1}\right \|_p \|V\|_p\|\delta A\|_p = \left \|(\Lambda-\mu I)^{-1} \right \|_p\ \kappa_p(V)\|\delta A\|_p$$

But $(\Lambda - \mu I)^{-1}$ is a diagonal matrix, the $p$-norm of which is easily computed:

$$\left \| \left (\Lambda-\mu I \right )^{-1} \right \|_p\ =\max_{\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_p\ne 0} \frac{\left \|\left (\Lambda-\mu I \right )^{-1} \boldsymbol{x} \right \|_p}{\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_p} =\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)}\frac{1}{|\lambda -\mu|}\ = \frac{1}{\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)}|\lambda-\mu|}$$

whence:

$$\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)}|\lambda-\mu|\leq\ \kappa_p(V)\|\delta A\|_p.$$

:::

:::

## An Alternate Formulation {#An_Alternate_Formulation}

The theorem can also be reformulated to better suit numerical methods. In fact, dealing with real eigensystem problems, one often has an exact matrix $A$, but knows only an approximate eigenvalue-eigenvector couple, $(\lambda^{a}, \boldsymbol{v}^{a})$ and needs to bound the error. The following version comes in help.

**Bauer–Fike Theorem (Alternate Formulation).** Let $(\lambda^{a}, \boldsymbol{v}^{a})$ be an approximate eigenvalue-eigenvector couple, and $\boldsymbol{r} = A\boldsymbol{v}^{a} - \lambda^{a}\boldsymbol{v}^{a}$. Then there exists $\lambda \in \Lambda(A)$ such that:

$$\left |\lambda-\lambda^a \right |\leq \kappa_p (V)\frac{\|\boldsymbol{r}\|_p}{\left \|\boldsymbol{v}^a \right \|_p}$$

::: {#o7 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof.** We can suppose $\lambda^{a} \notin \Lambda(A)$, otherwise take $\lambda = \lambda^{a}$ and the result is trivially true since $\kappa_{p}(V) \ge 1$. So $(A - \lambda^{a}I)^{-1}$ exists, so we can write:

$$\boldsymbol{v}^a = \left (A-\lambda^a I \right )^{-1} \boldsymbol{r}= V \left (D-\lambda^a I \right )^{-1}V^{-1}\boldsymbol{r}$$

since $A$ is diagonalizable; taking the $p$-norm of both sides, we obtain:

$$\left \| \boldsymbol{v}^a \right \|_p= \left \|V \left (D-\lambda^a I \right )^{-1}V^{-1}\boldsymbol{r} \right \|_p \leq \|V\|_p \left \| \left (D-\lambda^a I \right )^{-1} \right \|_p \left \|V^{-1} \right \|_p \|\boldsymbol{r}\|_p =\kappa_p(V) \left \| \left (D-\lambda^a I \right )^{-1} \right \|_p \|\boldsymbol{r}\|_p.$$

However

$$\left (D- \lambda^a I \right )^{-1}$$

is a diagonal matrix and its $p$-norm is easily computed:

$$\left \|\left (D-\lambda^a I \right )^{-1} \right \|_p = \max_{\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_p \ne 0}\frac{\left \|\left (D-\lambda^a I \right )^{-1}\boldsymbol{x} \right \|_p}{\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_p} =\max_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)} \frac{1}{\left |\lambda-\lambda^a \right |}=\frac{1}{\min_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)} \left |\lambda- \lambda^a \right |}$$

whence:

$$\min_{\lambda\in\lambda(A)} \left |\lambda-\lambda^a \right | \leq\kappa_p(V) \frac{\|\boldsymbol{r}\|_p}{\left \|\boldsymbol{v}^a \right \|_p}.$$

:::

## A Relative Bound {#A_Relative_Bound}

Both formulations of Bauer–Fike theorem yield an absolute bound. The following corollary is useful whenever a relative bound is needed:

**Corollary.** Suppose $A$ is invertible and that $\mu$ is an eigenvalue of $A + \delta A$. Then there exists $\lambda \in \Lambda(A)$ such that:

$$\frac{|\lambda-\mu|}{|\lambda|}\leq\kappa_p (V) \left \|A^{-1}\delta A \right \|_p$$

**Note.** $||A^{-1}\delta A||$ can be formally viewed as the *relative variation of* $A$, just as $\tfrac{|\lambda - \mu|}{|\lambda|}$ is the relative variation of $\lambda$.

::: {#o9 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof.** Since $\mu$ is an eigenvalue of $A + \delta A$ and $\det(A) \ne 0$, by multiplying by $-A^{-1}$ from left we have:

$$-A^{-1}(A+\delta A)\boldsymbol{v}=-\mu A^{-1}\boldsymbol{v}.$$

If we set:

$$A^a =\mu A^{-1}, \qquad (\delta A)^a = -A^{-1}\delta A$$

then we have:

$$\left (A^a + (\delta A)^a - I \right )\boldsymbol{v} = \boldsymbol{0}$$

which means that $1$ is an eigenvalue of $A^{a} + (\delta A)^{a}$, with $\boldsymbol{v}$ as an eigenvector. Now, the eigenvalues of $A^{a}$ are $\tfrac{\mu}{\lambda_{i}}$, while it has the same [eigenvector matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector_matrix) as $A$. Applying the Bauer–Fike theorem to $A^{a} + (\delta A)^{a}$ with eigenvalue $1$, gives us:

$$\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)} \left|\frac{\mu}{\lambda}-1\right| = \min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(A)}\frac{|\lambda-\mu|}{|\lambda|} \leq \kappa_p (V) \left \|A^{-1}\delta A \right \|_p$$

:::

## The Case of Normal Matrices {#The_Case_of_Normal_Matrices}

If $A$ is [normal](wikipedia://en/170366), $V$ is a [unitary matrix](wikipedia://en/129222), therefore:

$$\|V\|_2= \left \|V^{-1} \right \|_2 = 1,$$

so that $\kappa_{2}(V) = 1$. The Bauer–Fike theorem then becomes:

$$\exists\lambda\in\Lambda(A): \quad |\lambda-\mu|\leq\|\delta A\|_2$$

Or in alternate formulation:

$$\exists\lambda\in\Lambda(A): \quad \left |\lambda-\lambda^a \right |\leq\frac{\|\boldsymbol{r}\|_2}{\left \|\boldsymbol{v}^a\right \|_2}$$

which obviously remains true if $A$ is a [Hermitian matrix](wikipedia://en/189682). In this case, however, a much stronger result holds, known as the [Weyl's theorem on eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/4908214). In the hermitian case one can also restate the Bauer–Fike theorem in the form that the map $A \mapsto \Lambda(A)$ that maps a matrix to its [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum) is a [non-expansive function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-expansive_function) with respect to the [Hausdorff distance](wikipedia://en/429296) on the set of compact subsets of $\mathbf{C}$.
