# Zolotarev's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zolotarev's lemma"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Zolotarev's lemma** states that the [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567)

:::

$$\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)$$

for an integer *a* [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) an odd [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*, where *p* does not divide *a*, can be computed as the sign of a permutation:

$$\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = \varepsilon(\pi_a)$$

where ε denotes the [signature of a permutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_of_a_permutation) and π<sub>*a*</sub> is the [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) of the nonzero [residue classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_class) mod *p* induced by [multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_multiplication) by *a*.

For example, take *a* = 2 and *p* = 7. The nonzero squares mod 7 are 1, 2, and 4, so (2|7) = 1 and (6|7) = −1. Multiplication by 2 on the nonzero numbers mod 7 has the cycle decomposition (1,2,4)(3,6,5), so the sign of this permutation is 1, which is (2|7). Multiplication by 6 on the nonzero numbers mod 7 has cycle decomposition (1,6)(2,5)(3,4), whose sign is −1, which is (6|7).

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

In general, for any [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) *G* of order *n*, it is straightforward to determine the signature of the permutation π<sub>*g*</sub> made by left-multiplication by the element *g* of *G*. Considering the group action of the subgroup generated by g (*\<g>*) on G by π<sub>*g*</sub>, this permutation will be even unless there are an odd number of [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) of even size. Note that each orbit must have length equal to the order of *\<g>*. Assuming *n* even, therefore, the condition for π<sub>*g*</sub> to be an odd permutation, when *g* has order *k*, is that *n*/*k* should be odd, or that the subgroup \<*g*> generated by *g* should have odd [index](wikipedia://en/230581).

We will apply this to the group of nonzero numbers mod *p*, which is a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *p* − 1. The *j*th power of a [primitive root modulo p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_root_modulo_p) will have index the [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354)

*i* = (*j*, *p* − 1).

The condition for a nonzero number mod *p* to be a [quadratic non-residue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_non-residue) is to be an odd power of a primitive root. The lemma therefore comes down to saying that *i* is odd when *j* is odd, which is true *a fortiori*, and *j* is odd when *i* is odd, which is true because *p* − 1 is even (*p* is odd).

:::

## Another proof {#Another_proof}

Zolotarev's lemma can be deduced easily from [Gauss's lemma](wikipedia://en/2310978) and *vice versa*. The example

$$\left(\frac{3}{11}\right),$$

i.e. the Legendre symbol (*a*/*p*) with *a* = 3 and *p* = 11, will illustrate how the proof goes. Start with the set {1, 2, . . . , *p* − 1} arranged as a matrix of two rows such that the sum of the two elements in any column is zero mod *p*, say:

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 |

:::

Apply the permutation $U: x\mapsto ax\pmod p$:

::: {#o6 .table cue=table}

| 3 | 6 | 9 | 1 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 5 | 2 | 10 | 7 |

:::

The columns still have the property that the sum of two elements in one column is zero mod *p*. Now apply a permutation *V* which swaps any pairs in which the upper member was originally a lower member:

::: {#o7 .table cue=table}

| 3 | 5 | 2 | 1 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 6 | 9 | 10 | 7 |

:::

Finally, apply a permutation W which gets back the original matrix:

::: {#o8 .table cue=table}

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 |

:::

We have *W*<sup>−1</sup> = *VU*. Zolotarev's lemma says (*a*/*p*) = 1 if and only if the permutation *U* is even. Gauss's lemma says (*a/p*) = 1 iff *V* is even. But *W* is even, so the two lemmas are equivalent for the given (but arbitrary) *a* and *p*.

## Jacobi symbol {#Jacobi_symbol}

This interpretation of the Legendre symbol as the sign of a permutation can be extended to the [Jacobi symbol](wikipedia://en/97313)

$$\left(\frac{a}{n}\right),$$

where *a* and *n* are relatively prime integers with odd *n* > 0: *a* is invertible mod *n*, so multiplication by *a* on **Z**/*n***Z** is a permutation and a generalization of Zolotarev's lemma is that the Jacobi symbol above is the sign of this permutation.

For example, multiplication by 2 on **Z**/21**Z** has cycle decomposition (0)(1,2,4,8,16,11)(3,6,12)(5,10,20,19,17,13)(7,14)(9,18,15), so the sign of this permutation is (1)(−1)(1)(−1)(−1)(1) = −1 and the Jacobi symbol (2|21) is −1. (Note that multiplication by 2 on the units mod 21 is a product of two 6-cycles, so its sign is 1. Thus it's important to use *all* integers mod *n* and not just the units mod *n* to define the right permutation.)

When *n* = *p* is an odd prime and *a* is not divisible by *p*, multiplication by *a* fixes 0 mod *p*, so the sign of multiplication by *a* on all numbers mod *p* and on the units mod *p* have the same sign. But for composite *n* that is not the case, as we see in the example above.

## History {#History}

This lemma was introduced by [Yegor Ivanovich Zolotarev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yegor_Ivanovich_Zolotarev) in an 1872 proof of [quadratic reciprocity](wikipedia://en/25272).
