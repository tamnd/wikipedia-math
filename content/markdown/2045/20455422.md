# Karen Vogtmann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karen Vogtmann"}

**Karen Vogtmann** (born July 13, 1949 in [Pittsburg, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg,_California)[^o3]) is an American mathematician working primarily in the area of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164). She introduced, in a 1986 paper with [Marc Culler](wikipedia://en/29258614),[^o4] an object now known as the [Culler–Vogtmann Outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culler–Vogtmann_Outer_space). The Outer space is a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) analog of the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) of a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) and is particularly useful in the study of the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [outer automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_automorphism) of the free group on *n* generators, [Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)](wikipedia://en/12747184). Vogtmann is a professor of mathematics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick).

:::

## Biographical data {#Biographical_data}

Vogtmann was inspired to pursue mathematics by a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) summer program for high school students at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o7]

She received a B.A. from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1971. Vogtmann then obtained a PhD in mathematics, also from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1977.[^o6] Her PhD advisor was John Wagoner and her doctoral thesis was on [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500).[^o7]

She then held positions at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o12] Vogtmann has been a faculty member at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) since 1984, and she became a full professor at Cornell in 1994.[^o12] In September 2013, she also joined the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick). She is married to the mathematician [John Smillie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_David_Smillie). The couple moved in 2013 to England and settled in [Kenilworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenilworth).[^o8] She is currently a professor of mathematics at Warwick, and a Goldwin Smith Professor of Mathematics Emeritus at Cornell.[^o12]

Vogtmann has been the vice-president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (2003–2006).[^o6][^o9] She has been elected to serve as a member of the board of trustees of the American Mathematical Society for the period February 2008 – January 2018.[^o10][^o11]

Vogtmann is a former editorial board member (2006–2016) of the journal *[Algebraic and Geometric Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_and_Geometric_Topology)* and a former associate editor of *[Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2668304)*.[^o12] She is currently an associate editor of the *[Journal of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)*,[^o13] an editorial board member *Geometry & Topology Monographs* book series,[^o14] and a consulting editor for the *[Proceedings of the Edinburgh Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_Edinburgh_Mathematical_Society)*.[^o15]

She is also a member of the [ArXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) advisory board.[^o16]

Since 1986 Vogtmann has been a co-organizer of the annual conference called the *Cornell Topology Festival*[^o17] that usually takes places at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) each May.

### Awards, honors and other recognition {#Awards,_honors_and_other_recognition}

Vogtmann gave an [invited lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Madrid, Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid,_Spain), in August 2006.[^o19][^o20]

She gave the 2007 annual [AWM](wikipedia://en/9858698) [Noether Lecture](wikipedia://en/11566873) titled "Automorphisms of Free Groups, Outer Space and Beyond" at the annual meeting of [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans) in January 2007.[^o7][^o21] Vogtmann was selected to deliver the [Noether Lecture](wikipedia://en/11566873) for "her fundamental contributions to geometric group theory; in particular, to the study of the automorphism group of a free group".[^o22]

On June 21–25, 2010 a 'VOGTMANNFEST' Geometric Group Theory conference in honor of Vogtmann's birthday was held in [Luminy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculté_des_Sciences_de_Luminy), France.[^o23]

In 2012 she became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o24] She became a member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2020.[^o25] She was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2023.[^o26]

Vogtmann received the [Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Wolfson_Research_Merit_Award) in 2014.[^o27] She also received the [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award) from the [Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Foundation) in 2014.[^o28][^o29] She was named [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) Clay Senior Scholar in 2016 and Simons Professor for 2016-2017.[^o30][^o31]

Vogtmann gave a plenary talk at the 2016 [European Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13505846) in Berlin.[^o32][^o33]

In 2018 she won the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) "for her profound and pioneering work in geometric group theory, particularly the study of automorphism groups of free groups".[^o34]

In May 2021 she was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).[^o35]

In 2022 she was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (NAS).[^o36]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Vogtmann's early work concerned [homological](wikipedia://en/142432) properties of [orthogonal groups](wikipedia://en/173954) associated to [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) over various [fields](wikipedia://en/10603).[^o38][^o39]

Vogtmann's most important contribution came in a 1986 paper with Marc Culler called "Moduli of graphs and automorphisms of free groups".[^o4] The paper introduced an object that came to be known as [Culler–Vogtmann Outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culler–Vogtmann_Outer_space). The Outer space *X<sub>n</sub>*, associated to a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) *F*<sub>*n*</sub>, is a free group analog[^o40] of the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) of a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181). Instead of marked [conformal structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_structure) (or, in an equivalent model, hyperbolic structures) on a surface, points of the Outer space are represented by volume-one *marked metric graphs*. A *marked metric graph* consists of a [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) between a [wedge](wikipedia://en/684143) of *n* circles and a finite connected graph *Γ* without degree-one and degree-two vertices, where *Γ* is equipped with a volume-one metric structure, that is, assignment of positive real lengths to edges of *Γ* so that the sum of the lengths of all edges is equal to one. Points of *X<sub>n</sub>* can also be thought of as free and discrete minimal isometric actions *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914) where the quotient graph has volume one.

By construction the Outer space *X<sub>n</sub>* is a finite-dimensional [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) equipped with a natural action of [Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)](wikipedia://en/12747184) which is properly discontinuous and has finite simplex stabilizers. The main result of Culler–Vogtmann 1986 paper,[^o4] obtained via Morse-theoretic methods, was that the Outer space *X<sub>n</sub>* is contractible. Thus the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) *X<sub>n</sub>* /Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) is "almost" a [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) for [Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)](wikipedia://en/12747184) and it can be thought of as a classifying space over **[Q](wikipedia://en/19727024)**. Moreover, Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) is known to be [virtually](wikipedia://en/10377971) torsion-free, so for any torsion-free [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) the action of *H* on *X<sub>n</sub>* is discrete and free, so that *X<sub>n</sub>*/*H* is a classifying space for *H*. For these reasons the Outer space is a particularly useful object in obtaining [homological](wikipedia://en/142432) and [cohomological](wikipedia://en/194060) information about Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>). In particular, Culler and Vogtmann proved[^o4] that Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) has virtual cohomological dimension 2*n* − 3.

In their 1986 paper Culler and Vogtmann do not assign *X<sub>n</sub>* a specific name. According to Vogtmann,[^o41] the term *Outer space* for the complex *X<sub>n</sub>* was later coined by [Peter Shalen](wikipedia://en/3520910). In subsequent years the Outer space became a central object in the study of [Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)](wikipedia://en/12747184). In particular, the Outer space has a natural compactification, similar to [Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s compactification of the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661), and studying the action of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on this compactification yields interesting information about dynamical properties of [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735).[^o42][^o43][^o44][^o45]

Much of Vogtmann's subsequent work concerned the study of the Outer space *X<sub>n</sub>*, particularly its homotopy, homological and cohomological properties, and related questions for Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>). For example, Hatcher and Vogtmann[^o46][^o47] obtained a number of homological stability results for Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) and Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>).

In her papers with Conant,[^o48][^o49][^o50] Vogtmann explored the connection found by [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509) between the cohomology of certain infinite-dimensional [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) and the homology of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>).

A 2001 paper of Vogtmann, joint with [Louis Billera](wikipedia://en/22444348) and [Susan P. Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_P._Holmes), used the ideas of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and [CAT(0) geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) to study the space of [phylogenetic trees](wikipedia://en/149326), that is trees showing possible evolutionary relationships between different species.[^o51] Identifying precise evolutionary trees is an important basic problem in [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology) and one also needs to have good quantitative tools for estimating how accurate a particular evolutionary tree is. The paper of Billera, Vogtmann and Holmes produced a method for quantifying the difference between two evolutionary trees, effectively determining the distance between them.[^o52] The fact that the space of [phylogenetic trees](wikipedia://en/149326) has "non-positively curved geometry", particularly the uniqueness of shortest paths or *geodesics* in [CAT(0) spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space), allows using these results for practical statistical computations of estimating the confidence level of how accurate particular evolutionary tree is. A free software package implementing these algorithms has been developed and is actively used by biologists.[^o52]
