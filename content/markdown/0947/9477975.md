# Completion of a ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Completion of a ring"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **completion** is any of several related [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) on [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) and [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) that result in complete [topological rings](wikipedia://en/60097) and [modules](wikipedia://en/1616141). Completion is similar to [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring), and together they are among the most basic tools in analysing [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346). Complete commutative rings have a simpler structure than general ones, and [Hensel's lemma](wikipedia://en/1633368) applies to them. In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a completion of a ring of functions *R* on a space *X* concentrates on a **formal neighborhood** of a point of *X*: heuristically, this is a neighborhood so small that *all* [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) centered at the point are convergent. An algebraic completion is constructed in a manner analogous to [completion](wikipedia://en/7832) of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) with [Cauchy sequences](wikipedia://en/6085), and agrees with it in the case when *R* has a metric given by a [non-Archimedean](wikipedia://en/333996) [absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667).

:::

## General construction {#General_construction}

Suppose that *E* is an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) with a descending [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014)

$$E = F^0 E \supset F^1 E \supset F^2 E \supset \cdots \,$$

of subgroups. One then defines the completion (with respect to the filtration) as the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109):

$$\widehat{E} = \varprojlim (E/F^n E)=\left\{\left.(\overline{a_n})_{n\geq0} \in \prod_{n\geq0}(E/F^nE) \;\right|\; a_i \equiv a_j\pmod{F^iE} \text{ for all } i \leq j\right\}. \,$$

This is again an abelian group. Usually *E* is an *additive* abelian group. If *E* has additional algebraic structure compatible with the filtration, for instance *E* is a [filtered ring](wikipedia://en/2954244), a filtered [module](wikipedia://en/276410), or a filtered [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), then its completion is again an object with the same structure that is complete in the topology determined by the filtration. This construction may be applied both to [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) and [noncommutative rings](wikipedia://en/24062516). As may be expected, when the intersection of the $F^i E$ equals zero, this produces a [complete topological ring](wikipedia://en/60097#Completion).

## Krull topology {#Krull_topology}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), the filtration on a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* by the powers of a proper [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* determines the Krull (after [Wolfgang Krull](wikipedia://en/964328)) or *I*-[adic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adic_topology) on *R*. The case of a [*maximal* ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) $I=\mathfrak{m}$ is especially important, for example the distinguished maximal ideal of a [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856). The [basis of open neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_of_open_neighbourhoods) of 0 in *R* is given by the powers *I*<sup>*n*</sup>, which are *nested* and form a descending filtration on *R*:

$$F^0 R = R\supset I\supset I^2\supset\cdots, \quad F^n R = I^n.$$

(Open neighborhoods of any *r* ∈ *R* are given by cosets *r* + *I*<sup>*n*</sup>.) The (*I*-adic) completion is the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of the [factor rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_ring),

$$\widehat{R}_I=\varprojlim (R/I^n)$$

pronounced "R I hat". The kernel of the canonical map $\pi$ from the ring to its completion is the intersection of the powers of *I*. Thus $\pi$ is injective if and only if this intersection reduces to the zero element of the ring; by the [Krull intersection theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krull_intersection_theorem), this is the case for any commutative [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) which is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) or a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391).

There is a related topology on *R*-[modules](wikipedia://en/276410), also called Krull or *I*-adic topology. A basis of open neighborhoods of a module *M* is given by the sets of the form

$$x + I^n M \quad\text{for }x \in M.$$

The *I*-adic completion of an *R*-module *M* is the inverse limit of the quotients

$$\widehat{M}_I=\varprojlim (M/I^n M).$$

This procedure converts any module over *R* into a complete [topological module](wikipedia://en/1616141) over $\widehat{R}_I$ if *I* is finitely generated.[^o5]

## Examples {#Examples}

- The ring of [*p*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integers) $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is obtained by completing the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ of integers at the ideal (*p*).
- Let *R* = *K*\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>\] be the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) in *n* variables over a field *K* and $\mathfrak{m}=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ be the maximal ideal generated by the variables. Then the completion $\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is the ring *K*\[\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>\]\] of [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) in *n* variables over *K*.
- Given a noetherian ring $R$ and an ideal $I = (f_1,\ldots, f_n),$ the $I$-adic completion of $R$ is an image of a formal power series ring, specifically, the image of the surjection[^o8]

$$\begin{cases} R[[x_1, \ldots, x_n]] \to \widehat{R}_I \\ x_i \mapsto f_i \end{cases}$$

The kernel is the ideal $(x_1 - f_1, \ldots, x_n - f_n).$

Completions can also be used to analyze the local structure of [singularities](wikipedia://en/581763) of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754). For example, the affine schemes associated to $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy)$ and the nodal cubic [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y^2 - x^2(1+x))$ have similar looking singularities at the origin when viewing their graphs (both look like a plus sign). Notice that in the second case, any [Zariski neighborhood](wikipedia://en/241744) of the origin is still an irreducible curve. If we use completions, then we are looking at a "small enough" neighborhood where the node has two components. Taking the localizations of these rings along the ideal $(x,y)$ and completing gives $\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/(xy)$ and $\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/((y+u)(y-u))$ respectively, where $u$ is the formal square root of $x^2(1+x)$ in $\mathbb{C}[[x,y]].$ More explicitly, the power series:

$$u = x\sqrt{1+x} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n(2n)!}{(1-2n)(n!)^2(4^n)}x^{n+1}.$$

Since both rings are given by the intersection of two ideals generated by a homogeneous degree 1 polynomial, we can see algebraically that the singularities "look" the same. This is because such a scheme is the union of two non-equal linear subspaces of the affine plane.

## Properties {#Properties}

- The completion of a Noetherian ring with respect to some ideal is a Noetherian ring.[^o11]
- The completion of a Noetherian local ring with respect to the unique maximal ideal is a Noetherian local ring.[^o12]
- The completion is a functorial operation: a continuous map $f\colon R\to S$ of topological rings gives rise to a map of their completions, $$\widehat{f}: \widehat{R}\to\widehat{S}.$$

Moreover, if $M$ and $N$ are two modules over the same topological ring $R$ and $f\colon M\to N$ is a continuous module map, then $f$ uniquely extends to the map of the completions:

$$\widehat{f}: \widehat{M}\to\widehat{N},$$

where $\widehat{M},\widehat{N}$ are modules over $\widehat{R}.$

- The completion of a [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) $R$ is a [flat module](wikipedia://en/492616) over $R$.[^o13]
- The completion of a finitely generated module $M$ over a Noetherian ring $R$ can be obtained by *extension of scalars*:

$$\widehat{M}=M\otimes_R \widehat{R}.$$

Together with the previous property, this implies that the functor of completion on finitely generated $R$-modules is [exact](wikipedia://en/453945): it preserves [short exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence). In particular, taking quotients of rings commutes with completion, meaning that for any quotient $R$-algebra $R / I$, there is an isomorphism

$$\widehat{R / I} \cong \widehat R / \widehat I.$$

- **[Cohen structure theorem](wikipedia://en/27443678)** (equicharacteristic case). Let $R$ be a complete [local](wikipedia://en/153391) Noetherian commutative ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ and [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) $\mathbb k$. If $R$ contains a field, then

$$R\simeq \mathbb k[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]/I$$

for some $n$ and some ideal $I$ (Eisenbud, Theorem 7.7).
