# Erdős–Dushnik–Miller theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Dushnik–Miller theorem"}

In the mathematical theory of [infinite graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_graph), the **Erdős–Dushnik–Miller theorem** is a form of [Ramsey's theorem](wikipedia://en/184898) stating that every infinite graph contains either a [countably infinite](wikipedia://en/6026) [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501), or a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) with the same [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) as the whole graph.

:::

The theorem was first published by (txt), in both the form stated above and an equivalent [complementary form](wikipedia://en/1175649): every infinite graph contains either a countably infinite clique or an independent set with equal cardinality to the whole graph. In their paper, they credited [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) with assistance in its proof. They applied these results to the [comparability graphs](wikipedia://en/6978672) of [partially ordered sets](wikipedia://en/23572) to show that each partial order contains either a countably infinite [antichain](wikipedia://en/348973) or a [chain](wikipedia://en/30330) of cardinality equal to the whole order, and that each partial order contains either a countably infinite chain or an antichain of cardinality equal to the whole order.

The same theorem can also be stated as a result in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), using the [arrow notation](wikipedia://en/15959032) of (Erdős Rado 1956), as $\kappa\rightarrow(\kappa,\aleph_0)^2$. This means that, for every set $S$ of cardinality $\kappa$, and every partition of the ordered pairs of elements of $S$ into two subsets $P_1$ and $P_2$, there exists either a subset $S_1\subset S$ of cardinality $\kappa$ or a subset $S_2\subset S$ of cardinality $\aleph_0$, such that all pairs of elements of $S_i$ belong to $P_i$. Here, $P_1$ can be interpreted as the edges of a graph having $S$ as its vertex set, in which $S_1$ (if it exists) is a clique of cardinality $\kappa$, and $S_2$ (if it exists) is a countably infinite independent set.

If $S$ is taken to be the [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) $\kappa$ itself, the theorem can be formulated in terms of [ordinal numbers](wikipedia://en/26547932) with the notation $\kappa\rightarrow(\kappa,\omega)^2$, meaning that $S_2$ (when it exists) has [order type](wikipedia://en/4499021) $\omega$. For uncountable [regular cardinals](wikipedia://en/374128) $\kappa$ (and some other cardinals) this can be strengthened to $\kappa\rightarrow(\kappa,\omega+1)^2$; however, it is [consistent](wikipedia://en/75802) that this strengthening does not hold for the [cardinality of the continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901).

The Erdős–Dushnik–Miller theorem has been called the first "unbalanced" generalization of Ramsey's theorem, and Paul Erdős's first significant result in set theory.
