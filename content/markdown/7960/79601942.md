# Ooguri–Vafa metric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ooguri–Vafa metric"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Ooguri–Vafa metric** is a four-dimensional [Hyperkähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperkähler_metric). The Ooguri–Vafa metric is named after [Hirosi Ooguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirosi_Ooguri) and [Cumrun Vafa](wikipedia://en/644848), who [first described](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_description) it in 1996 using the [Gibbons–Hawking ansatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbons–Hawking_ansatz). Another construction was given by [Davide Gaiotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Gaiotto), [Gregory Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Moore_%28physicist%29) and [Andrew Neitzke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Neitzke) in 2008.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Ooguri–Vafa metric is defined on the four-dimensional total spaces of principal U(1)-bundles over open subsets of the three-dimensional [euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^3$.[^o4] In particular the whole space results in $\mathbb{R}^3\times S^1$.[^o5]

Define the elliptical fibers $\tau(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log(z)$ with $\tau_1=\operatorname{Re}(\tau(z))$ and $\tau_2=\operatorname{Im}(\tau(z))$ and let $\lambda$ be the [string coupling constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_constant#String_theory). Further define the scaled spatial coordinate

$\mathbf{y}=\left(x,\frac{z}{\lambda},\frac{\bar{z}}{\lambda}\right)$.[^o6]

The metric of Ooguri and Vafa has the form[^o7]

$$ds^2=\lambda^2[V^{-1}(dt-\mathbf{A}\cdot d\mathbf{y})^2+V d\mathbf{y}^2]$$

where $\mathbf{A}=(A_x,A_z,A_{\bar{z}})$ and

$$A_x=-\tau_1=\frac{i}{4\pi}\log\left(\frac{z}{\bar{z}}\right),\quad A_z=0,\quad A_{\bar{z}}=0$$

and $V$ is a potential which gets modified from the form $V=\tau_2=\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\left(\frac{1}{z\bar{z}}\right)$.

### Requirements for the potential {#Requirements_for_the_potential}

There are 5 requirements for the potential $V$:[^o9]

- $V$ should be a function of only $x$ and $|z|$, i.e. $V(x,|z|)$ for $|z|=\sqrt{z\bar{z}}$.
- For the metric to be a hyperkähler metric, the following conditions must be met:

$$V^{-1}\Delta V = 0,\quad\text{and}\quad \nabla V = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$$

where the [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) $\Delta$ is defined as follows:

$$\Delta :=\partial_x^2+4\lambda^2\partial_z\bar{\partial}_{\bar{z}}.$$

- If $|z|\to \infty$ then one obtains the classical potential defined above:

$$V(x,|z|)\to \frac{1}{4\pi}\log\left(\frac{1}{z\bar{z}}\right)$$

- The metric should be periodic, but not translation-invariant, in the $x$-direction with period $1$, i.e. $V(x,|z|)=V(x+1,|z|)$.
- The singularities of $V$ can be removed by a suitable coordinate transformation.

There exists a unique solution which satisfies all these conditions[^o10]

$$V(x,|z|)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x-n)^2+z\bar{z}/\lambda^2}}-\frac{1}{|n|}\right)+C$$

for a constant $C$. Using Poisson's formula on gets

$$V(x,|z|)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\left(\frac{\mu^2}{z\bar{z}}\right)+\sum\limits_{m\neq 0}\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{2\pi i mx}K_0\left(2\pi\frac{|mz|}{\lambda}\right)$$

where $\mu$ is a constant and $K_0$ is the modified [Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700).
