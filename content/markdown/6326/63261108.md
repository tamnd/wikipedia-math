# Algebra and Tiling

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Algebra and Tiling"}

***Algebra and Tiling: Homomorphisms in the Service of Geometry*** is a mathematics textbook on the use of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) to answer questions about [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671) and higher dimensional [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654), partitions of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) or higher-dimensional spaces into congruent tiles. It was written by [Sherman K. Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_K._Stein) and Sándor Szabó, and published by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) as volume 25 of their [Carus Mathematical Monographs](wikipedia://en/2941952) series in 1994. It won the 1998 [Beckenbach Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckenbach_Book_Prize), and was reprinted in paperback in 2008.

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

The seven chapters of the book are largely self-contained, and consider different problems combining tessellations and algebra. Throughout the book, the history of the subject as well as the state of the art is discussed, and there are many illustrations.

The first chapter concerns a conjecture of [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) that, in any lattice tiling of a Euclidean space by unit [hypercubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercubes) (a tiling in which a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) of translational symmetries takes any hypercube to any other hypercube) some two cubes must meet face-to-face. This result was resolved positively by [Hajós's theorem](wikipedia://en/12200213) in group theory, but a generalization of this question to non-lattice tilings ([Keller's conjecture](wikipedia://en/33297462)) was disproved shortly before the publication of the book, in part by using similar group-theoretic methods.

Following this, three chapters concern lattice tilings by [polycubes](wikipedia://en/710426). The question here is to determine, from the shape of the polycube, whether all cubes in the tiling meet face-to-face or, equivalently, whether the lattice of symmetries must be a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of the [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402). After a chapter on the general version of this problem, two chapters consider special classes of cross and "semicross"-shaped polycubes, both with regard to tiling and then, when these shapes do not tile, with regard to how densely they can be packed. In three dimensions, this is the notorious [tripod packing](wikipedia://en/60000338) problem.

Chapter five considers [Monsky's theorem](wikipedia://en/35098575) on the impossibility of partitioning a square into an odd number of equal-area triangles, and its proof using the [2-adic valuation](wikipedia://en/16047618), and chapter six applies [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) to more general problems of tiling polygons by congruent triangles, such as the impossibility of tiling a square with [30-60-90 right triangles](wikipedia://en/2278116).

The final chapter returns to the topic of the first, with material on [László Rédei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/László_Rédei)'s generalization of Hajós's theorem. Appendices cover background material on lattice theory, [exact sequences](wikipedia://en/138215), [free abelian groups](wikipedia://en/247296), and the theory of [cyclotomic polynomials](wikipedia://en/171992).

## Audience and reception {#Audience_and_reception}

*Algebra and Tiling* can be read by undergraduate or graduate mathematics students who have some background in abstract algebra, and provides a source of applications for this topic. It can be used as a textbook, with exercises scattered throughout its chapters.

Reviewer William J. Walton writes that "The student or mathematician whose area of interest is algebra should enjoy this text". In 1998, the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) gave it their [Beckenbach Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beckenbach_Book_Prize) as one of the best of their book publications. The award citation called it "a simultaneously erudite and inviting ex- position of this substantial and timeless area of mathematics".
