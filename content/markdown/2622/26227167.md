# MathOverflow

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="MathOverflow"}

**MathOverflow** is a [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) [question-and-answer (Q&A) website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_Q&A_sites), which serves as an [online community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_community) of [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902). It allows users to ask questions, submit answers, and rate both, all while getting merit points for their activities.[^o2] It is a part of the [Stack Exchange Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_Exchange_Network), but distinct from [math.stackexchange.com](https://math.stackexchange.com).

:::

It is primarily for asking questions on mathematics research – i.e. related to [unsolved problems](wikipedia://en/183091) and the extension of knowledge of mathematics into areas that are not yet known – and does not welcome requests from non-mathematicians for instruction, for example [homework exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homework). It does welcome various questions on other topics that might normally be discussed among mathematicians, for example about [publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_publishing), [refereeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review), [advising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor), getting [tenure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenure_%28academic%29), etc. It is generally inhospitable to questions perceived as tendentious or argumentative.

## Origin and history {#Origin_and_history}

The website was started by [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) graduate students and postdocs Anton Geraschenko, David Zureick-Brown, and Kim Morrison on 28 September 2009 based on the idea of Sándor Kovács's NSF CAREER Grant.[^o19] The hosting was supported by [Ravi Vakil](wikipedia://en/5981037).[^o4] The site originally ran on a separate installation of the StackExchange 1.0 software engine; on June 25, 2013, it was integrated in the regular [Stack Exchange Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_Exchange_Network), running SE 2.0.[^o5]

### Naming {#Naming}

According to MathOverflow FAQ, the proper spelling is "MathOverflow" rather than "Math Overflow".

::: {#o8 .statement cue=named-heading name="Use of mathematical formulas"}

## Use of mathematical formulas {#Use_of_mathematical_formulas}

The original version of the website did not support [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) markup for mathematical formulas. To support most of the functionality of LaTeX, [MathJax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MathJax) was added in order for the site to transform math equations into their appropriate forms. In its current state, any post including "Math Mode" (text between \$s) will translate into proper [mathematical notation](wikipedia://en/277184).

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

As of April 4, 2012, there were 16,496 registered users on MathOverflow, most of whom were located in the United States (35%), India (12%), and the United Kingdom (6%). By December 11, 2018, the number of registered users had grown to 87,850.[^o11] As of June 2019, 123,448 questions have been posted.[^o12]

In 2011, questions were answered an average of 3.9 hours after they were posted, and "Acceptable" answers took an average of 5.01 hours.[^o13]

## Reception {#Reception}

- [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) compared it to "the venerable newsgroup *sci.math*, but with more modern, '[Web 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0)' features".[^o15]
- [John C. Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Baez) writes that "website 'Math Overflow' has become a universal clearinghouse for math questions".[^o16]
- According to [Gil Kalai](wikipedia://en/19709056), MathOverflow "is ran by an energetic and impressive group of very (very very) young people".[^o17]
- [Jordan Ellenberg](wikipedia://en/19765796) comments that the website "offers a constantly changing array of new questions" and is "addictive" in a "particularly pure form", as he compares it to the [Polymath Project](wikipedia://en/34858344).[^o18]
- Jared Keller in *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)* writes: "Math Overflow is almost an anti-social network, focused solely on productively addressing the problems posed by its users." He quotes Kim Morrison saying: "Mathematicians as a whole are surprisingly skeptical of many aspects of the modern Internet... In particular, things like [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook), [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter), etc. are viewed as enormous wastes of time."[^o19]
