# Gaussian logarithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaussian logarithm"}

In mathematics, **addition and subtraction logarithms** or **Gaussian logarithms** can be utilized to find the [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860) of the [sum](wikipedia://en/61338) and [difference](wikipedia://en/74141) of a pair of values whose logarithms are known, without knowing the values themselves.[^o8]

:::

Their mathematical foundations trace back to [Zecchini Leonelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zecchini_Leonelli)[^o4][^o5] and [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)[^o6][^o8][^o7] in the early 1800s.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o8][^o7]

*Image: Gaussian logarithm.svg.* The $s_{b}(z)$ and $d_{b}(z)$ functions for $b = e$.

The operations of addition and subtraction can be calculated by the formulas

$$\log_b\big(|X| + |Y|\big) = x + s_b(y - x),$$

$$\log_b\big(\big||X| - |Y|\big|\big) = x + d_b(y - x),$$

where

- $$x = \log_b|X|,$$
- $$y = \log_b|Y|,$$
- $s_b(z) = \log_b(1 + b^z)$, and
- $$d_b(z) = \log_b|1 - b^z|.$$

The "sum" function $s_b(z)$ and the "difference" function $d_b(z)$ are also known as *Gaussian logarithms*.

For [natural logarithms](wikipedia://en/21476) with $b = e$ the following identities with [hyperbolic functions](wikipedia://en/56567) exist:

$$s_e(z) = \ln 2 + \frac{z}{2} + \ln \left(\cosh\frac{z}{2}\right).$$

$$d_e(z) = \ln 2 + \frac{z}{2} + \ln \left|\sinh\frac{z}{2}\right|.$$

This shows that $s_e$ has a [Taylor expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_expansion) where all but the first term are [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) and all odd terms except the linear term are zero.

The simplification of multiplication, division, [roots](wikipedia://en/235029), and powers is counterbalanced by the cost of evaluating these functions for addition and subtraction.
