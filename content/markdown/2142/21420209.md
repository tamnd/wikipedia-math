# Integrally closed domain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Integrally closed domain"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), an **integrally closed domain** *A* is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) whose [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) in its [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) is *A* itself. Spelled out, this means that if *x* is an element of the field of fractions of *A* that is a root of a [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) with [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) in *A,* then *x* is itself an element of *A.* Many well-studied domains are integrally closed, as shown by the following chain of [class inclusions](wikipedia://en/1828474):

:::

An explicit example is the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) **Z**, a [Euclidean domain](wikipedia://en/10376). All [regular local rings](wikipedia://en/506959) are integrally closed as well.

A ring whose [localizations](wikipedia://en/252216) at all [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) are integrally closed domains is a **normal ring**.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

Let *A* be an integrally closed domain with field of fractions *K* and let *L* be a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of *K*. Then *x*∈*L* is [integral](wikipedia://en/9478630) over *A* if and only if it is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/186291) over *K* and its [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) over *K* has coefficients in *A*.[^o4] In particular, this means that any element of *L* integral over *A* is root of a monic polynomial in *A*\[*X*\] that is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) in *K*\[*X*\].

If *A* is a domain contained in a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K,* we can consider the [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) of *A* in *K* (i.e. the set of all elements of *K* that are integral over *A*). This integral closure is an integrally closed domain.

Integrally closed domains also play a role in the hypothesis of the [Going-down theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going-down_theorem). The theorem states that if *A*⊆*B* is an [integral extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_extension) of domains and *A* is an integrally closed domain, then the [going-down property](wikipedia://en/2083445) holds for the extension *A*⊆*B*.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The following are integrally closed domains.

- A [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039) (in particular: the integers and any field).
- A [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546) (in particular, any polynomial ring over a field, over the integers, or over any unique factorization domain).
- A [GCD domain](wikipedia://en/2932361) (in particular, any [Bézout domain](wikipedia://en/3131407) or [valuation domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_domain)).
- A [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) (in particular, any [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of a [number field](wikipedia://en/28730822)).
- A [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) over a field (since every symmetric algebra is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in several variables over a field).
- Let $k$ be a field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) not 2 and $S = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ a polynomial ring over it. If $f$ is a [square-free](wikipedia://en/3816655) nonconstant polynomial in $S$, then $S[y]/(y^2 - f)$ is an integrally closed domain.[^o7] In particular, $k[x_0, \dots, x_r]/(x_0^2 + \dots + x_r^2)$ is an integrally closed domain if $r \ge 2$.[^o8]

To give a non-example,[^o9] let *k* be a field and $A = k[t^2, t^3] \subset k[t]$, the subalgebra generated by *t*<sup>2</sup> and *t*<sup>3</sup>. Then *A* is not integrally closed: it has the field of fractions $k(t)$, and the monic polynomial $X^2 - t^2$ in the variable *X* has root *t* which is in the field of fractions but not in *A.* This is related to the fact that the [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) $Y^2 = X^3$ has a [singularity](wikipedia://en/4230456) at the origin.

Another domain that is not integrally closed is $A = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{5}\,]$; its field of fractions contains the element $\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}$, which is not in *A* but satisfies the monic polynomial $X^2-X-1 = 0$.

:::

## Noetherian integrally closed domain {#Noetherian_integrally_closed_domain}

For a [noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [local](wikipedia://en/153391) domain *A* of dimension one, the following are equivalent.

- *A* is integrally closed.
- The [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) of *A* is principal.
- *A* is a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) (equivalently *A* is Dedekind.)
- *A* is a regular local ring.

Let *A* be a noetherian integral domain. Then *A* is integrally closed if and only if (i) *A* is the intersection of all localizations $A_\mathfrak{p}$ over prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}$ of height 1 and (ii) the localization $A_\mathfrak{p}$ at a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of height 1 is a discrete valuation ring.

A noetherian ring is a [Krull domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krull_domain) if and only if it is an integrally closed domain.

In the non-noetherian setting, one has the following: an integral domain is integrally closed if and only if it is the intersection of all [valuation rings](wikipedia://en/966856) containing it.

## Normal rings {#Normal_rings}

Authors including [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), and [Matsumura](wikipedia://en/74536110) define a **normal ring** to be a ring whose [localizations](wikipedia://en/252216) at prime ideals are integrally closed domains. Such a ring is necessarily a [reduced ring](wikipedia://en/539749),[^o13] and this is sometimes included in the definition. In general, if *A* is a [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) ring whose localizations at maximal ideals are all domains, then *A* is a finite product of domains.[^o14] In particular, if *A* is a Noetherian and normal ring, then the domains in the product are integrally closed domains.[^o15] Conversely, any finite product of integrally closed domains is normal. In particular, if $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is Noetherian, normal, and connected, then *A* is an integrally closed domain. (Cf. [smooth variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety).)

Let *A* be a noetherian ring. Then ([Serre's criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_criterion_on_normality)) *A* is normal if and only if it satisfies the following: for any prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$,

If $\mathfrak{p}$ has height $\le 1$, then $A_\mathfrak{p}$ is [regular](wikipedia://en/506959) (i.e., $A_\mathfrak{p}$ is a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633).) If $\mathfrak{p}$ has height $\ge 2$, then $A_\mathfrak{p}$ has depth $\ge 2$.[^o16]

Item (i) is often phrased as "regular in codimension 1". Note that (i) implies that the set of [associated primes](wikipedia://en/17286442) $Ass(A)$ has no [embedded primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_prime), and, when (i) is the case, (ii) means that $Ass(A/fA)$ has no embedded prime for any non-zerodivisor *f*. In particular, a [Cohen-Macaulay ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen-Macaulay_ring) satisfies (ii). Geometrically, we have the following: if *X* is a [local complete intersection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_complete_intersection) in a nonsingular variety;[^o17] e.g., *X* itself is nonsingular, then *X* is Cohen-Macaulay; i.e., the stalks $\mathcal{O}_p$ of the structure sheaf are Cohen-Macaulay for all prime ideals p. Then we can say: *X* is [normal](wikipedia://en/2629423) (i.e., the stalks of its structure sheaf are all normal) if and only if it is regular in codimension 1.

## Completely integrally closed domains {#Completely_integrally_closed_domains}

Let *A* be a domain and *K* its field of fractions. An element *x* in *K* is said to be **almost integral over *A*** if the subring *A*\[*x*\] of *K* generated by *A* and *x* is a [fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) of *A*; that is, if there is a nonzero $d \in A$ such that $d x^n \in A$ for all $n \ge 0$. Then *A* is said to be **completely integrally closed** if every almost integral element of *K* is contained in *A*. A completely integrally closed domain is integrally closed. Conversely, a noetherian integrally closed domain is completely integrally closed.

Assume *A* is completely integrally closed. Then the formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) ring $A[[X]]$ is completely integrally closed.[^o20] This is significant since the analog is false for an integrally closed domain: let *R* be a valuation domain of height at least 2 (which is integrally closed). Then $R[[X]]$ is not integrally closed.[^o21] Let *L* be a field extension of *K*. Then the integral closure of *A* in *L* is completely integrally closed.[^o22]

An integral domain is completely integrally closed if and only if the monoid of divisors of *A* is a group.[^o23]

:::

## "Integrally closed" under constructions {#"Integrally_closed"_under_constructions}

The following conditions are equivalent for an integral domain *A*:

1. *A* is integrally closed;
1. *A*<sub>*p*</sub> (the localization of *A* with respect to *p*) is integrally closed for every [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) *p*;
1. *A*<sub>*m*</sub> is integrally closed for every [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) *m*.

1 → 2 results immediately from the preservation of integral closure under localization; 2 → 3 is trivial; 3 → 1 results from the preservation of integral closure under localization, the [exactness of localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_module#Flatness), and the property that an *A*-module *M* is zero if and only if its localization with respect to every maximal ideal is zero.

In contrast, the "integrally closed" does not pass over quotient, for **Z**\[t\]/(t<sup>2</sup>+4) is not integrally closed.

The localization of a completely integrally closed domain need not be completely integrally closed.[^o25]

A direct limit of integrally closed domains is an integrally closed domain.

## Modules over an integrally closed domain {#Modules_over_an_integrally_closed_domain}

Let *A* be a Noetherian integrally closed domain.

An ideal *I* of *A* is [divisorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisorial_fraction_ideal) if and only if every [associated prime](wikipedia://en/17286442) of *A*/*I* has height one.[^o27]

Let *P* denote the set of all prime ideals in *A* of height one. If *T* is a finitely generated torsion module, one puts:

$$\chi(T) = \sum_{p \in P} \operatorname{length}_p(T) p,$$

which makes sense as a formal sum; i.e., a divisor. We write $c(d)$ for the divisor class of *d*. If $F, F'$ are maximal submodules of *M*, then $c(\chi(M/F)) = c(\chi(M/F'))$[^o28] and $c(\chi(M/F))$ is denoted (in Bourbaki) by $c(M)$.
