# Orbit method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orbit method"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **orbit method** (also known as the **Kirillov theory**, **the method of coadjoint orbits** and by a few similar names) establishes a correspondence between irreducible [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788) of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and its [coadjoint orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coadjoint_orbit): orbits of the [action of the group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on the dual space of its [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944). The theory was introduced by (txt) for [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569) and later extended by [Bertram Kostant](wikipedia://en/3520590), [Louis Auslander](wikipedia://en/22378651), [Lajos Pukánszky](wikipedia://en/22378202) and others to the case of [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012). [Roger Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Evans_Howe) found a version of the orbit method that applies to *p*-adic Lie groups.[^o3] [David Vogan](wikipedia://en/10229758) proposed that the orbit method should serve as a unifying principle in the description of the unitary duals of real reductive Lie groups.[^o4]

:::

## Relation with symplectic geometry {#Relation_with_symplectic_geometry}

One of the key observations of Kirillov was that coadjoint orbits of a Lie group *G* have natural structure of [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356) whose symplectic structure is invariant under *G*. If an orbit is the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) of a *G*-invariant [classical mechanical system](wikipedia://en/198319) then the corresponding quantum mechanical system ought to be described via an irreducible unitary representation of *G*. Geometric invariants of the orbit translate into algebraic invariants of the corresponding representation. In this way the orbit method may be viewed as a precise mathematical manifestation of a vague physical principle of quantization. In the case of a nilpotent group *G* the correspondence involves all orbits, but for a general *G* additional restrictions on the orbit are necessary (polarizability, integrality, Pukánszky condition). This point of view has been significantly advanced by Kostant in his theory of [geometric quantization](wikipedia://en/1146267) of coadjoint orbits.

## Kirillov character formula {#Kirillov_character_formula}

For a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$, the [Kirillov orbit method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirillov_orbit_method) gives a heuristic method in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200). It connects the [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247) of [coadjoint orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coadjoint_orbit), which lie in the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of *G*, to the [infinitesimal characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_character) of the [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982). The method got its name after the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician [Alexandre Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515).

At its simplest, it states that a character of a Lie group may be given by the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021) [supported](wikipedia://en/381013) on the coadjoint orbits, weighted by the square-root of the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/44612290), denoted by $j$. It does not apply to all Lie groups, but works for a number of classes of [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) Lie groups, including [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235), some [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) groups, and [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326).

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

### Nilpotent group case {#Nilpotent_group_case}

Let *G* be a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945). Kirillov proved that the equivalence classes of [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788) of *G* are parametrized by the *coadjoint orbits* of *G*, that is the orbits of the action *G* on the dual space $\mathfrak{g}^*$ of its Lie algebra. The [Kirillov character formula](wikipedia://en/3769761) expresses the [Harish-Chandra character](wikipedia://en/11277623) of the representation as a certain integral over the corresponding orbit.

### Compact Lie group case {#Compact_Lie_group_case}

Complex irreducible representations of [compact Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) have been completely classified. They are always finite-dimensional, unitarizable (i.e. admit an invariant positive definite [Hermitian form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form)) and are parametrized by their [highest weights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight), which are precisely the dominant integral weights for the group. If *G* is a compact [semisimple Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) with a [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) *h* then its coadjoint orbits are [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) and each of them intersects the positive Weyl chamber *h*<sup>\*</sup><sub>\+</sub> in a single point. An orbit is **integral** if this point belongs to the weight lattice of *G*. The highest weight theory can be restated in the form of a bijection between the set of integral coadjoint orbits and the set of equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations of *G*: the highest weight representation *L*(*λ*) with highest weight *λ*∈*h*<sup>\*</sup><sub>\+</sub> corresponds to the integral coadjoint orbit *G*·*λ*. The [Kirillov character formula](wikipedia://en/3769761) amounts to the character formula earlier proved by [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190).
