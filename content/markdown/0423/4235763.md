# Victor Zalgaller

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Victor Zalgaller"}

**Victor** (**Viktor**) **Abramovich Zalgaller** (ויקטור אבּרמוביץ' זלגלר; Виктор Абрамович Залгаллер; 25 December 1920 – 2 October 2020) was a Russian-Israeli mathematician in the fields of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033). He is best known for the results he achieved on [convex polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470), [linear](wikipedia://en/43730) and [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297), [isoperimetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoperimetry), and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Zalgaller was born in [Parfino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parfino), [Novgorod Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Governorate) on 25 December 1920.[^o4] In 1936, he was one of the winners of the Leningrad Mathematics Olympiads for high school students. He started his studies at the [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University), however, [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) intervened in 1941, and Zalgaller joined the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army). He took part in the defence of [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad), and in 1945 marched into Germany.[^o5]

He worked as a teacher at the [Saint Petersburg Lyceum 239](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Lyceum_239),[^o7] and received his 1963[^o6] doctoral dissertation on polyhedra with the aid of his high school students who wrote the computer programs for the calculation.[^o7]

Zalgaller did his early work under direction of [Aleksandr Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Aleksandrov_%28mathematician%29) and [Leonid Kantorovich](wikipedia://en/194660). He wrote joint [monographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) with both of them. His later monograph *Geometric Inequalities* (joint with [Yuri Burago](wikipedia://en/8688682)) is still the main reference in the field.

Zalgaller lived in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) most of his life, having studied and worked at the [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) and the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) (Saint Petersburg branch).

In 1999, he immigrated to [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel). Zalgaller died on 2 October 2020 at the age of 99.[^o7]
