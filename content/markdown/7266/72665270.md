# Uncertain geographic context problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uncertain geographic context problem"}

In [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography), [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health), and other fields that study [spatial relationships](wikipedia://en/3190431), the **uncertain geographic context problem** (**UGCoP**) is a methodological problem in which the geographic areas used in research to represent people's environments—such as [neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood), [census tracts](wikipedia://en/105254), [administrative areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division), or [activity spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activity_space)—may differ from the places and periods that actually shape the phenomena being studied, potentially leading to misleading conclusions.[^o6]

:::

For example, a study that measures the effect of a person's residential neighborhood on health outcomes may overlook environmental influences encountered while working, traveling, or engaging in activities elsewhere.[^o2] The term was coined by geographer [Mei-Po Kwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mei-Po_Kwan) in 2012.[^o4][^o5]

## Introduction {#Introduction}

The uncertain geographic context problem is a source of [statistical bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_bias) that can affect the results of [spatial analysis](wikipedia://en/3190431) when data is grouped by geographic areas such as neighbourhoods or districts.[^o4][^o5]

The core difficulty is that the geographic boundaries used to group data such as a [census tract](wikipedia://en/105254) are often arbitrary and may not reflect where people actually spend their time. A person may live within one boundary but regularly travel outside it to work, shop, or go to school, meaning that the area used to represent them in a study may not capture the environment that actually influences their behaviour.[^o6][^o4][^o7] The UGCoP is closely related to the [modifiable areal unit problem](wikipedia://en/3561094) (MAUP) and the [ecological fallacy](wikipedia://en/475598).[^o8] It is particularly relevant in research on [time geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_geography), food access, and human mobility.[^o9][^o10]

## Implications {#Implications}

The UGCoP has further implications when considering the area outside of a study area. [Tobler's second law of geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobler's_second_law_of_geography) states, "the phenomenon external to a geographic area of interest affects what goes on inside."[^o13][^o14] As a study area is often a subset of the planet, data on the edges of the study area will be excluded.[^o15] If the boundary demarcating the study area is permeable to travel, then the phenomena under investigation within it may extend beyond, and be impacted by, forces excluded from the analysis.[^o16][^o17] This uncertainty contributes to the UGCoP.[^o4][^o5]

Maps are inherently simplified representations of reality, and cartographers must ensure that a map's limitations are clearly documented in order to avoid misleading readers.[^o18] With modern technology, there is an emphasis on individual-level data and understanding how individuals interact with their environment.[^o8][^o10] When making maps with this individual-level data, the UGCoP is one source of bias that can impact the results of an analysis.[^o4] When these results inform policy, they can have real world ramifications.[^o18]

The UGCoP is particularly important when understanding food access and human mobility.[^o16][^o9]

## Suggested solutions {#Suggested_solutions}

[Geographic information systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_systems), along with technologies that can monitor the position of individuals in real time, are possible methods for addressing the UGCoP.[^o5] These technologies allow scientists to analyze and visualize the 3D space-time path of people moving through a study area, and better understand their actual activity space.[^o5] [Web GIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_GIS) has also been employed to address the UGCoP by allowing researchers to better contextualize subjects' real and perceived activity space.[^o5][^o20] These technologies have helped to address the problem by moving away from aggregate data and introducing a temporal component to the modeling of subject activity.[^o5][^o20]
