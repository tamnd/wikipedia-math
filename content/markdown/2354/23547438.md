# Method of continuity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Method of continuity"}

In the [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) of [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces), the **method of continuity** provides sufficient conditions for deducing the invertibility of one [bounded linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) from that of another, related operator.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Let *B* be a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), *V* a [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538), and $(L_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ a [norm](wikipedia://en/246722) continuous family of bounded linear operators from *B* into *V*. Assume that there exists a positive constant *C* such that for every $t\in [0,1]$ and every $x\in B$

$$||x||_B \leq C ||L_t(x)||_V.$$

Then $L_0$ is surjective if and only if $L_1$ is surjective as well.

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The method of continuity is used in conjunction with *[a priori estimates](wikipedia://en/30669047)* to prove the existence of suitably regular solutions to [elliptic](wikipedia://en/550137) [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

We assume that $L_0$ is surjective and show that $L_1$ is surjective as well.

Subdividing the interval \[0,1\] we may assume that $||L_0-L_1|| \leq 1/(3C)$. Furthermore, the surjectivity of $L_0$ implies that *V* is isomorphic to *B* and thus a Banach space. The hypothesis implies that $L_1(B) \subseteq V$ is a closed subspace.

Assume that $L_1(B) \subseteq V$ is a proper subspace. [Riesz's lemma](wikipedia://en/5584743) shows that there exists a $y\in V$ such that $||y||_V \leq 1$ and $\mathrm{dist}(y,L_1(B))>2/3$. Now $y=L_0(x)$ for some $x\in B$ and $||x||_B \leq C ||y||_V$ by the hypothesis. Therefore

$$||y-L_1(x)||_V = ||(L_0-L_1)(x)||_V \leq ||L_0-L_1|| ||x||_B \leq 1/3,$$

which is a contradiction since $L_1(x) \in L_1(B)$.

:::
