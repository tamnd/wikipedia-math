# Dustin Clausen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dustin Clausen"}

**Dustin Clausen** is an [American-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American-Canadian)[^o3] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions to [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) and the development of [condensed mathematics](wikipedia://en/68011960), in collaboration with [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306). His research interests include the intersections of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Dustin Clausen completed his undergraduate studies at Harvard University.[^o5] While at Harvard he spent a semester studying at the [Math in Moscow](wikipedia://en/27194034) program.[^o6] He received his PhD in 2013 from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT),[^o7] where he was supervised by [Jacob Lurie](wikipedia://en/26663863). His doctoral thesis was titled "Arithmetic Duality in Algebraic K-Theory."[^o8]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

After earning his PhD, Clausen spent five years as a postdoctoral researcher at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen). He then moved to [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn), Germany, where he was first a postdoctoral researcher at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) and then the head of a research group at the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960). In 2020, Clausen returned to the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) as an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor). Since 2023, he has held a position as a permanent [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) (IHÉS).[^o10]

## Research and contributions {#Research_and_contributions}

Clausen's research has focused on algebraic K-theory and its connections to number theory and homotopy theory. Along with Peter Scholze, he has developed the concept of [condensed mathematics](wikipedia://en/68011960),[^o12] which aims to provide a framework for topological algebraic structures.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- NSF Graduate Research Fellowship (2008)[^o14]
- David Mumford Prize (2008)[^o15]
- Hoopes Prize (2008)[^o16]
- Hartmann Foundation's Diploma Prize (2022)[^o17]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Dustin Clausen is the grandson of mathematician [John T. Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_T._Tate) (1925–2019), and the great grandson of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351).[^o19]
