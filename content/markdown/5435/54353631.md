# Thomas Fantet de Lagny

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Fantet de Lagny"}

**Thomas Fantet de Lagny** (7 November 1660 – 11 April 1734) was a [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), well known for his contributions to computational mathematics, and for calculating [π](wikipedia://en/23601) to 112 correct decimal places.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Thomas Fantet de Lagny was son of Pierre Fantet, a royal official in [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble), and Jeanne d'Azy, the daughter of a physician from [Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier).

He entered a Jesuit College in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon), where he became passionate about [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), as he studied some mathematical texts such as *Euclid* by [Georges Fournier](wikipedia://en/36088025) and an [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) text by [Jacques Pelletier du Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Pelletier_du_Mans). Then he studied three years in the Faculty of Law in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse).

In 1686, he went to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) and became a mathematics tutor to the [Noailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrien_Maurice_de_Noailles) family. He collaborated with [de l'Hospital](wikipedia://en/1728613) under the name of *de Lagny*, and at that time he started publishing his first mathematical papers. He came back to [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon) when, on 11 December 1695, he was named an associate of the [Académie Royale des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences). Then, in 1697, he became professor of [hydrography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography) at [Rochefort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochefort,_Charente-Maritime) for 16 years.

De Lagny returned to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) in 1714, and became a librarian at the *Bibliothèque du roi*, and a deputy director of the *Banque Générale* between 1716 and 1718. On 7 July 1719, he was awarded a pension by the [Académie Royale des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences), finally earning his living from science. In 1723, he became a pensionnaire at the academy, replacing [Pierre Varignon](wikipedia://en/1193511) who died in 1722, but had to retire in 1733.

De Lagny died on 11 April 1734. While he was dying, someone asked him: "What is the square of 12?" and he answered immediately: "144."[^o4]

## Computing π {#Computing_π}

In 1719, de Lagny calculated [π](wikipedia://en/23601) to 127 decimal places, using [Gregory's series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory's_series) for arctangent, but only 112 decimals were correct. This remained the record until 1789, when [Jurij Vega](wikipedia://en/40546) calculated 126 correct digits of π.
