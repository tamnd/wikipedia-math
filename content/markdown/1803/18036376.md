# J. Arthur Seebach Jr.

*Image: Arthur-1986.jpg.* J. Arthur Seebach, Jr. working with a student in his St. Olaf College Math Department Office. Photo from the 1986 St. Olaf College Yearbook.

*Image: Arthur-1987.jpg.* J. Arthur Seebach, Jr. in his St. Olaf College Math Department Office. Photo from the 1987 St. Olaf College Yearbook.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J. Arthur Seebach Jr."}

**J. Arthur Seebach Jr** (May 17, 1938 – December 3, 1996) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Seebach studied [Greek language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) as an undergraduate, making it a second [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_%28academic%29) with mathematics.

Seebach studied with A. I. Weinzweig at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). He earned a Ph.D. with the thesis *Cones and Homotopy in Categories*. Seebach began to teach at [Saint Olaf College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Olaf_College) in [Northfield, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northfield,_Minnesota) in 1965. He, his wife Linda A. Seebach, and [Lynn A. Steen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_A._Steen) wrote an expository article "What is a Sheaf".[^o2] The paper showed that a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) is useful in analysis, algebra, and geometry when considering [germs](wikipedia://en/352714) of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110), [local rings](wikipedia://en/153391), and [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519). J. Arthur also wrote "Injectives and Homotopy".[^o3]

In 1971 Seebach and Steen took over the Book Reviews section of [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly), including [Telegraphic Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraphic_Reviews) which ran for several pages every month. The massive effort was eventually distributed over some 50 mathematicians at Saint Olaf, [Carleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College), and [Macalester Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College). Telegraphic Reviews, in [telegraphic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraphic_style), was started by [Kenneth O. May](wikipedia://en/719577) in 1965 and provided an American posting of new publications before the digital age.

Seebach and Steen conducted research in a 1967 summer school with students investigating the [independence](wikipedia://en/1141208) of conditions on [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450). They summarized their work in *[Counterexamples in Topology](wikipedia://en/915449)* (1978).

In 1975 Seebach and Steen became co-editors of [Mathematics Magazine](wikipedia://en/4230329). Steen wrote:

Arthur’s sense of whimsy, his love of puns, and his proclivity for obscure connections totally transformed the image of *Mathematics Magazine*. [Cover art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_art), viewed as radical at the time, has since been emulated...

Seebach welcomed the rise of computers when he assembled a [Heathkit H8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathkit_H8). In 1986 he became editor of Mathematical Notes in *American Mathematical Monthly*.

Beyond mathematics, Seebach sang with the Bach Society of Minnesota. The craftsmanship of the [Studebaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studebaker) automobile appealed to Seebach and he operated a side-business in Studebaker parts, driving some, and publishing a [newsletter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsletter) for fellow aficionados of the car. His newsletter experience was of value to [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) when they began their own newsletter.

Seebach died in 1996 from complications of [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes).
