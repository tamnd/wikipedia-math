# G-module

*Image: Toroidal coord.png.* The [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) can be made an abelian group isomorphic to the product of the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177). This abelian group is a [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928)-module, where the group acts by reflection in each of the coordinate directions (here depicted by red and blue arrows intersecting at the identity element).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="G-module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), given a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$, a ***G*-module** is an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) $M$ on which $G$ [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) compatibly with the abelian group structure on $M$. This widely applicable notion generalizes that of a [representation of $G$](wikipedia://en/12695). [Group (co)homology](wikipedia://en/248635) provides an important set of tools for studying general $G$-modules.

:::

The term ***G*-module** is also used for the more general notion of an [$R$-module](wikipedia://en/276410) on which $G$ acts linearly (i.e. as a group of $R$-module [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160)).

## Definition and basics {#Definition_and_basics}

Let $G$ be a group. A **left $G$-module** consists of[^o3] an abelian group $M$ together with a [left group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) $\rho:G\times M\to M$ such that

$$g\cdot(a_1+a_2)=g\cdot a_1+g\cdot a_2$$

for all $a_1$ and $a_2$ in $M$ and all $g$ in $G$, where $g\cdot a$ denotes $\rho(g,a)$. A **right $G$-module** is defined similarly. Given a left $G$-module $M$, it can be turned into a right $G$-module by defining $a\cdot g=g^{-1}\cdot a$.

A [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f:M\rightarrow N$ is called a **morphism of $G$-modules** (or a **$G$-linear map**, or a **$G$-homomorphism**) if $f$ is both a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) and $G$-[equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant).

The collection of left (respectively right) $G$-modules and their morphisms form an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) $G\textbf{-Mod}$ (resp. $\textbf{Mod-}G$). The category $G\text{-Mod}$ (resp. $\text{Mod-}G$) can be identified with the category of left (resp. right) **$\mathbb{Z}G$-modules**, i.e. with the [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) $\mathbb{Z}[G]$.

A **submodule** of a $G$-module $M$ is a subgroup $A\subseteq M$ that is stable under the action of $G$, i.e. $g\cdot a\in A$ for all $g\in G$ and $a\in A$. Given a submodule $A$ of $M$, the **quotient module** $M/A$ is the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) with action $g\cdot (m+A)=g\cdot m+A$.

## Examples {#Examples}

- Given a group $G$, the abelian group $\mathbb{Z}$ is a $G$-module with the *trivial action* $g\cdot a=a$.
- Let $M$ be the set of [binary quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/1867129) $f(x,y)=ax^2+2bxy+cy^2$ with $a,b,c$ [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), and let $G=\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ (the 2×2 [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) over $\mathbb{Z}$). Define

$$(g\cdot f)(x,y)=f((x,y)g^t)=f\left((x,y)\cdot\begin{bmatrix} \alpha & \gamma \\ \beta & \delta \end{bmatrix}\right)=f(\alpha x+\beta y,\gamma x+\delta y),$$

where

$$g=\begin{bmatrix} \alpha & \beta \\ \gamma & \delta \end{bmatrix}$$

and $(x,y)g$ is [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280). Then $M$ is a $G$-module studied by [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss).[^o6] Indeed, we have

$$g(h(f(x,y))) = gf((x,y)h^t)=f((x,y)h^tg^t)=f((x,y)(gh)^t)=(gh)f(x,y).$$

- If $V$ is a representation of $G$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$, then $V$ is a $G$-module (it is an abelian group under addition).

:::

## Topological groups {#Topological_groups}

If $G$ is a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) and $M$ is an abelian topological group, then a **topological *G*-module** is a $G$-module where the action map $G\times M\rightarrow M$ is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) (where the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910) is taken on $G\times M$).[^o8]

In other words, a topological $G$-module is an abelian topological group $M$ together with a continuous map $G\times M\rightarrow M$ satisfying the usual relations $g(a+a')=ga+ga'$, $(gg')a=g(g'a)$, and $1a=a$.
