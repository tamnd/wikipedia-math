# Haridatta

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Haridatta"}

**Haridatta** (c. 683 CE) was an [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer)-[mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala), India, who is believed to be the promulgator of the Parahita system of astronomical computations. This system of computations is widely popular in [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala) and [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu). According to legends, Haridatta promulgated the [Parahita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parahita) system on the occasion of the *[Mamankam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamankam)* held in the year 683 CE.[^o3] Mamankam was a 12-yearly festival held in [Thirunnavaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirunnavaya) on the banks of the [Bharathapuzha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharathapuzha) river.

:::

The distinctive contribution of Haridatta, apart from his resolving the [Aryabhatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryabhatiya) calculations and using the [Katapayadi system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katapayadi_system) of [numerals](wikipedia://en/21170) is the corrections he introduced to the values of the mean and true positions, the velocity, etc., of the moon and other planets as obtained from [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204)'s constants. This correction is called the *[Sakabda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka_era)-samskara* since it applied from the date of [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204) in the [Saka era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka_era) 444, at which date his constants gave accurate results.[^o4]

## Parahita system {#Parahita_system}

The Parahita system of computations introduced by Haridatta was a simplification of the system propounded in [Aryabhatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryabhatiya) by [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204). Haridatta introduced the following simplifications.[^o3][^o6] The system was called Parahita meaning *suitable for the common man* because it simplified astronomical computations and made it accessible for practice even for ordinary persons.

- Haridatta dispensed with the numerical symbolism used by Aryabhata and replaced it with the more flexible [Katapayadi system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katapayadi_system). In this system, letters are used to represent digits and these letters are then used to invent meaningful words and sentences to denote specific numbers. These words and sentences could be remembered with much less effort.
- Computations in Indian astronomy involved long numbers representing various parameters associated with the several celestial objects which are applicable for a *[Mahayuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayuga)*, a period of 4,320,000 years. To avoid computations with these large numbers, Haridatta introduced a smaller [Yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga), called a *Dhijagannupura-yuga*, of 576 years or 210,389 days (which 1/7500 th part of a Mahyuga) and accurately determined the zero corrections for this sub-Yuga for the mean motion of the several planets. These corrections were then used to compute the mean planets for any given date.

## Works of Haridatta {#Works_of_Haridatta}

Scholars have been able to identify only two works as authored by Haridatta. One of them, titled *Grahacaranibandhana*, is the basic manual of computations of the Parahita system of astronomy. This was unearthed by [K.V. Sarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K.V._Sarma) and was published in 1954.[^o8] The other work titled *Mahamarganibandhana* is no longer extant.
