# Arthur Milgram

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Milgram"}

**Arthur Norton Milgram** (3 June 1912 – 30 January 1961) was an American mathematician. He made contributions in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), and [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316). Perhaps one of his more famous contributions is the [Lax–Milgram theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax–Milgram_theorem)—a theorem in functional analysis that is particularly applicable in the study of partial differential equations.[^o2] In the third chapter of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351)'s book *Galois Theory*, Milgram also discussed some applications of Galois theory.[^o3] Milgram also contributed to [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), by co-authoring the article *Verallgemeinerung eines graphentheoretischen Satzes von Rédei* with [Tibor Gallai](wikipedia://en/1651618) in 1960.[^o4]

:::

Milgram was born in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), and received his Ph.D. from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in 1937. He worked under the supervision of [John Kline](wikipedia://en/21005350) <http://genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=287> (a student of [Robert Lee Moore](wikipedia://en/1077133)). His dissertation was titled "*Decompositions and Dimension of Closed Sets in* $\mathbf{R}^{n}$".

Milgram advised 2 students at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University) in the 1940s and 1950s ([Robert M. Exner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Exner) <http://genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=361> and [Adnah Kostenbauder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adnah_Kostenbauder) <http://genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=362>).[^o5] In the 1950s, Milgram moved to the [University of Minnesota at Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_at_Minneapolis) and helped found Minnesota's well-known [PDE](wikipedia://en/52564) group (<https://web.archive.org/web/20090216230629/http://www.math.umn.edu/grad/areas/pde.html>). At Minnesota, Milgram was also the Ph.D. advisor for [Robert Duke Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Duke_Adams) <http://genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=15778>. Milgram's son [R. James (Richard) Milgram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._James_Milgram) <http://genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=18610> (Professor Emeritus at [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford) <http://math.stanford.edu/directory/faculty.html>) also studied mathematics and received his Ph.D. from Minnesota.
