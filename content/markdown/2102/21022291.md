# Nelson Dunford

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nelson Dunford"}

**Nelson James Dunford** (December 12, 1906 – September 7, 1986) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), namely [integration of vector valued functions](wikipedia://en/9399072), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), and [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator). The [Dunford decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunford_decomposition), [Dunford–Pettis property](wikipedia://en/16388109), and [Dunford-Schwartz theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunford-Schwartz_theorem) bear his name.

:::

He studied mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and obtained his Ph.D. in 1936 at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) under [Jacob Tamarkin](wikipedia://en/22870348). He moved in 1939 to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), where he remained until his retirement in 1960.

In 1981, he was awarded jointly with [Jacob T. Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_T._Schwartz), his Ph.D. student, the well-known [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for the three-volume work *[Linear Operators](wikipedia://en/77274564)*.

Nelson Dunford was coeditor of [Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937) (1941–1945) and [Mathematical Surveys and Monographs](wikipedia://en/28100142) (1945–1949).
