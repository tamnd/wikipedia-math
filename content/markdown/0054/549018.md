# Welch's method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Welch's method"}

**Welch's method**, named after [Peter D. Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_D._Welch), is an approach for [spectral density estimation](wikipedia://en/13070117). It is used in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), and applied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) for estimating the [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) of a [signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28electrical_engineering%29) at different [frequencies](wikipedia://en/10779). The method is based on the concept of using [periodogram](wikipedia://en/485457) spectrum estimates, which are the result of converting a signal from the time domain to the [frequency domain](wikipedia://en/370346). Welch's method is an improvement on the standard [periodogram](wikipedia://en/485457) spectrum estimating method and on [Bartlett's method](wikipedia://en/17220224), in that it reduces noise in the estimated [power spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectrum) in exchange for reducing the frequency resolution. Due to the noise caused by imperfect and finite data, the noise reduction from Welch's method is often desired.

:::

## Definition and procedure {#Definition_and_procedure}

The Welch method is based on [Bartlett's method](wikipedia://en/17220224) and differs in two ways:

1. The signal is split up into overlapping segments: the original data segment is split up into L data segments of length M, overlapping by D points.
   1. If D = M / 2, the overlap is said to be 50%
   1. If D = 0, the overlap is said to be 0%. This is the same situation as in the [Bartlett's method](wikipedia://en/17220224).
1. The overlapping segments are then windowed: After the data is split up into overlapping segments, the individual L data segments have a window applied to them (in the time domain).
   1. Most [window functions](wikipedia://en/244097) afford more influence to the data at the center of the set than to data at the edges, which represents a loss of information. To mitigate that loss, the individual data sets are commonly overlapped in time (as in the above step).
   1. The windowing of the segments is what makes the Welch method a "modified" [periodogram](wikipedia://en/485457).

After doing the above, the [periodogram](wikipedia://en/485457) is calculated by computing the [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811), and then computing the squared magnitude of the result, yielding power spectrum estimates for each segment. The individual spectrum estimates are then averaged, which reduces the variance of the individual power measurements. The end result is an array of power measurements vs. frequency "bin".
