# Noetherian module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Noetherian module"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **Noetherian module** is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) that satisfies the [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189) on its [submodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule), where the submodules are [partially ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partially_ordered) by [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29).[^o2]

:::

Historically, [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) was the first mathematician to work with the properties of [finitely generated submodules](wikipedia://en/326430). He [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) an important theorem known as [Hilbert's basis theorem](wikipedia://en/13733) which says that any [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in the multivariate [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) of an arbitrary [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/25977#Types_of_ideals). However, the property is named after [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) who was the first one to discover the true importance of the property.

## Characterizations and properties {#Characterizations_and_properties}

In the presence of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840),[^o4] two other characterizations are possible:

- Any [nonempty](wikipedia://en/9566) set *S* of submodules of the module has a [maximal element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) (with respect to [set inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_inclusion)). This is known as the [maximum condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_condition).
- All of the submodules of the module are [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430).[^o5]

If *M* is a module and *K* a submodule, then *M* is Noetherian [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *K* and *M*/*K* are Noetherian. This is in contrast to the general situation with finitely generated modules: a submodule of a finitely generated module need not be finitely generated.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), considered as a module over the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of integers, is a Noetherian module.
- If *R* = M<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) is the full [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over a field, and *M* = M<sub>*n* 1</sub>(*F*) is the set of column vectors over *F*, then *M* can be made into a module using [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280) by elements of *R* on the left of elements of *M*. This is a Noetherian module.
- Any module that is finite as a set is Noetherian.
- Any finitely generated right module over a right [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) is a Noetherian module.

:::

## Use in other structures {#Use_in_other_structures}

A right [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) *R* is, by definition, a Noetherian right *R*-module over itself using multiplication on the right. Likewise a ring is called left Noetherian ring when *R* is Noetherian considered as a left *R*-module. When *R* is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) the left-right adjectives may be dropped as they are unnecessary. Also, if *R* is Noetherian on both sides, it is customary to call it Noetherian and not "left and right Noetherian".

The Noetherian condition can also be defined on [bimodule](wikipedia://en/492612) structures as well: a Noetherian bimodule is a bimodule whose [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) of sub-bimodules satisfies the ascending chain condition. Since a sub-bimodule of an *R*-*S* bimodule *M* is in particular a left *R*-module, if *M* considered as a left *R*-module were Noetherian, then *M* is automatically a Noetherian bimodule. It may happen, however, that a bimodule is Noetherian without its left or right structures being Noetherian.
