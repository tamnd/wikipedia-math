# Clustering illusion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clustering illusion"}

*Image: Plot of random points.gif.* 1,000 points randomly distributed inside a square, showing apparent clusters and empty spaces

The **clustering illusion** is the tendency to erroneously consider the inevitable "streaks" or "clusters" arising in small [samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29) from random distributions to be non-random. The illusion is caused by a human tendency to underpredict the amount of [variability](wikipedia://en/23773247) likely to appear in a small sample of random or [pseudorandom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudorandom) data.[^o2]

:::

*Image: Air Raid Damage Map - East Marylebone.jpg.* Map of air raid damage in [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone), London

[Thomas Gilovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gilovich), an early author on the subject, argued that the effect occurs for different types of random dispersions. Some might perceive patterns in [stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market) price fluctuations over time, or clusters in two-dimensional data such as the locations of impact of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) [V-1 flying bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb) on maps of London.[^o2][^o3] Although Londoners developed specific theories about the pattern of impacts within London, a statistical analysis by R. D. Clarke originally published in 1946 showed that the impacts of [V-2 rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket) on London were a close fit to a random distribution.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8]

## Similar biases {#Similar_biases}

Using this [cognitive bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_bias) in causal reasoning may result in the [Texas sharpshooter fallacy](wikipedia://en/476197), in which differences in data are ignored and similarities are overemphasized. More general forms of erroneous pattern recognition are *[pareidolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareidolia)* and *[apophenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apophenia)*. Related biases are the *[illusion of control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusion_of_control)* which the clustering illusion could contribute to, and *[insensitivity to sample size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insensitivity_to_sample_size)* in which people don't expect greater variation in smaller samples. A different cognitive bias involving misunderstanding of chance streams is the [gambler's fallacy](wikipedia://en/12970).

## Possible causes {#Possible_causes}

[Daniel Kahneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kahneman) and [Amos Tversky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Tversky) explained this kind of misprediction as being caused by the [representativeness heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representativeness_heuristic)[^o3] (which itself they also first proposed).
