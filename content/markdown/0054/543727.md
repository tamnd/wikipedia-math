# Jean-Robert Argand

*Image: Quantites Imaginaires Argand 1806.png.* Quantites Imaginaires Argand 1806

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Robert Argand"}

**Jean-Robert Argand** (, , ; July 18, 1768 – August 13, 1822) was a Genevan [amateur mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_amateur_mathematicians). In 1806, while managing a [bookstore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookstore) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), he published the idea of the geometrical interpretation of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) known as the [Argand diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argand_diagram) and is known for the first rigorous [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Jean-Robert Argand was born in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), then [Republic of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Geneva), to Jacques Argand and Eve Carnac. His background and education are mostly unknown. Since his knowledge of mathematics was self-taught and he did not belong to any mathematical organizations, he likely pursued mathematics as a hobby rather than a profession.

Argand moved to Paris in 1806 with his family and, when managing a bookshop there, privately published his *Essai sur une manière de représenter les quantités imaginaires dans les constructions géométriques* (Essay on a method of representing [imaginary quantities](wikipedia://en/5826)). In 1813, it was republished in the French journal *Annales de Mathématiques*. The essay discussed a method of graphing complex numbers via analytical geometry. It proposed the interpretation of the value $i$ as a rotation of 90 degrees in the Argand plane. In this essay he was also the first to propose the idea of [modulus](wikipedia://en/991) to indicate the magnitude of [vectors](wikipedia://en/32533) and [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), as well as the notation for vectors $\overrightarrow{a b}$. The topic of complex numbers was also being studied by other mathematicians, notably [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) and [Caspar Wessel](wikipedia://en/21418268). Wessel's 1799 paper on a similar graphing technique did not attract attention.

Argand is also renowned for delivering a proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra in his 1814 work *Réflexions sur la nouvelle théorie d'analyse* (Reflections on the new theory of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396)). It was the first complete and [rigorous proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigorous_proof) of the theorem, and was also the first proof to generalize the fundamental theorem of algebra to include [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) with complex [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203).

The first textbook containing a proof of the theorem was [Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy)'s *[Cours d'analyse de l'École Royale Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cours_d'Analyse)* (1821). It contained Argand's proof, although Argand is not credited for it. And the proof was later referenced in [Chrystal's](wikipedia://en/14008803) influential textbook *Algebra*.

Argand died of an unknown cause on August 13, 1822, in Paris. In 1978 his proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra was called by The Mathematical Intelligencer “both ingenious and profound.”
