# Heinz Hopf

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Heinz Hopf"}

**Heinz Hopf** (19 November 1894 – 3 June 1971) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on the fields of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hopf was born in Gräbschen, [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) (now [Grabiszyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grabiszyn-Grabiszynek), part of [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)), the son of Elizabeth (née Kirchner) and Wilhelm Hopf. His father was born Jewish and converted to Protestantism a year after Heinz was born; his mother was from a Protestant family.[^o6][^o7]

Hopf attended [Karl Mittelhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Mittelhaus) higher boys' school from 1901 to 1904, and then entered the König-Wilhelm-[Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in Breslau. He showed mathematical talent from an early age. In 1913 he entered the [Silesian Friedrich Wilhelm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Friedrich_Wilhelm_University) where he attended lectures by [Ernst Steinitz](wikipedia://en/3966685), [Adolf Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368530), [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602), [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191), and [Rudolf Sturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Sturm). When [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) broke out in 1914, Hopf eagerly enlisted. He was wounded twice and received the iron cross (first class) in 1918.

After the war Hopf continued his mathematical education in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) (winter 1919/20 and summer 1920)[^o8] and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) (starting in winter 1920/21). He studied under [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) and received his doctorate in 1925.

## Career {#Career}

In his dissertation, *Connections between topology and metric of manifolds* ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Über Zusammenhänge zwischen Topologie und Metrik von Mannigfaltigkeiten*), he proved that any simply connected complete [Riemannian](wikipedia://en/144652) [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) of constant sectional [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) is globally [isometric](wikipedia://en/254777) to [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697), [spherical](wikipedia://en/236020), or [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742). He also studied the indices of zeros of [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) on [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101), and connected their sum to curvature. Some six months later he gave a new proof that the sum of the indices of the zeros of a vector field on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) is independent of the choice of vector field and equal to the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of the manifold. This theorem is now called the [Poincaré–Hopf theorem](wikipedia://en/1267288).

Hopf spent the year after his doctorate at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), [Carl Runge](wikipedia://en/747636), and [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) were working. While there he met [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245) and began a lifelong friendship.

In 1926 Hopf moved back to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), where he gave a course in [combinatorial topology](wikipedia://en/363400). He spent the academic year 1927/28 at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) on a Rockefeller fellowship with Alexandrov. [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384), [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen) and [J. W. Alexander](wikipedia://en/9848523) were all at Princeton at the time. At this time Hopf discovered the [Hopf invariant](wikipedia://en/4417198) of maps $S^3 \to S^2$ and proved that the [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384) has invariant 1. In the summer of 1928 Hopf returned to Berlin and began working with [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245), at the suggestion of Courant, on a book on [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). Three volumes were planned, but only one was finished. It was published in 1935.

In 1929, he declined a job offer from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). In 1931 Hopf took [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544)'s position at [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich). Hopf received another invitation to Princeton in 1940, but he declined it. Two years later, however, he was forced to file for Swiss citizenship after his property was confiscated by [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany), his father's conversion to Christianity having failed to convince German authorities that he was an "[Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan)".

In 1946/47 and 1955/56 Hopf visited the United States, staying at Princeton and giving lectures at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). He served as president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) from 1955 to 1958.[^o10]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In October 1928 Hopf married Anja von Mickwitz (1891–1967).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

He received honorary doctorates from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg), the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester), the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), the [Free University of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Brussels_%281834–1969%29), and the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne). In 1949 he was elected a corresponding member of the [Heidelberg Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Academy_of_Sciences). He was elected to the United States National Academy of Sciences in 1957, the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1961,[^o13] and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1963.[^o14] He was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) in 1932 and a Plenary Speaker at the ICM in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) in 1950.[^o15]

In memory of Hopf, [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich) awards the [Heinz Hopf Prize](wikipedia://en/24560211) for outstanding scientific work in the field of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210).
