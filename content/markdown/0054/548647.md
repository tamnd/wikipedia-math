# Volterra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Volterra"}

**Volterra** (; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin): *Volaterrae*) is a walled mountaintop town in the [Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany) region of [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy). Its history dates from before the 8th century BC and it has substantial structures from the [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization), [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), and [Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) periods.[^o4]

:::

## History {#History}

Volterra, known to the ancient [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscans) as *Velathri* or *Vlathri*[^o6] and to the Romans as *Volaterrae*,[^o7] is a town and *[comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune)* in the [Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany) region of [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy). The site is believed to have been continuously inhabited as a city since at least the end of the 8th century BC.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

The town was a [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) settlement of the [Proto-Villanovan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Villanovan_culture).[^o11][^o12] It became an important [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization) centre as one of the "twelve cities" of the Etruscan League.[^o13][^o14]

It was allied to Rome at the end of the 3rd century BC and became a [municipium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipium).[^o15][^o16] The wealthy [Caecina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caecinia_gens) family lived here and Gaius Caecina Largus and the eminent [Aulus Caecina Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulus_Caecina_Severus) (consul 2–1 BC) built the theatre and probably other monuments.[^o17] Other important families here were the Persii and the Laelii.[^o18] Aulus Caecina was appointed *[propraetor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propraetor)* of [Moesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moesia) by 4 AD and later in charge of several legions on the lower Rhine after 14 AD where he led them ably, routing the army of [Arminius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminius) who had destroyed three Roman legions. He was eulogised by the chroniclers for his exploits and on his return to Rome he was awarded triumph honours.

The city was a bishop's residence in the 5th century,[^o19] and its episcopal power was affirmed during the 12th century. With the decline of the episcopate and the discovery of local [alum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alum) deposits, Volterra became a place of interest of the [Republic of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Florence), whose forces conquered Volterra.[^o20] Florentine rule was not always popular, and opposition occasionally broke into rebellion. On 18 June 1472, during the so-called Allumiere War between Volterra and Florence in which [Federico da Montefeltro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_da_Montefeltro) allowed his soldiers to pillage Volterra and to commit rape and murder of its citizens, despite the town's surrender on 16 June after a 25-day siege.[^o21] The incident caused the emigration of many wealthy families and the appropriation of their goods.[^o22][^o23]

When the Republic of Florence fell in 1530, Volterra came under the control of the [Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici) family and later followed the history of the [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany).

## Municipal government {#Municipal_government}

*Image: 1208 wurde mit dem Bau des Palazzo dei Priori in Volterra begonnen. 02.jpg.* [Palazzo dei Priori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_dei_Priori,_Volterra) is Volterra's Town Hall.

Volterra is headed by a [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor#Italy) (*sindaco*) assisted by a legislative body, the *consiglio comunale*, and an executive body, the *giunta comunale*. Since 1995 the mayor and members of the *consiglio comunale* are directly elected together by resident citizens, while from 1945 to 1995 the mayor was chosen by the legislative body. The *giunta comunale* is chaired by the mayor, who appoints others members, called *[assessori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assessor_%28Italy%29)*. The offices of the *comune* are housed in a building usually called the *municipio* or *palazzo comunale*.

Since 1995 the mayor of Volterra is directly elected by citizens, originally every four, then every five years. The current mayor is Giacomo Santi ([PD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_%28Italy%29)), elected on 26 May 2019 with 44.4% of votes and re-elected on 9 June 2024 with 50.2% of votes.

## Main sights {#Main_sights}

*Image: Tuscany Landscape In Volterra Pisa Italy Travel Photography (158908959).jpeg.* View from Volterra.

- [Roman Theatre of Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Theatre_at_Volterra), 1st century BC, excavated in the 1950s[^o35][^o36]
- [Roman Amphitheater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_amphitheatre,_Volterra), discovered in 2015 and has been excavated over the succeeding years[^o37][^o38][^o39]
- [Piazza dei Priori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_dei_Priori), the main square, a fine example of medieval Tuscan town squares[^o40][^o41][^o42]
- [Palazzo dei Priori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_dei_Priori,_Volterra), the town hall on main piazza, begun in 1208 and finished in 1257[^o43][^o44][^o45]
- [Pinacoteca e museo civico di Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinacoteca_e_museo_civico_di_Volterra): Art Museum housed in Palazzo Minucci-Solaini.[^o46][^o47] Founded in 1905,[^o48][^o49] the gallery consists mostly of works by Tuscan artists from 14th to 17th centuries.[^o50] Includes a [*Deposition*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposition_from_the_Cross,_Volterra_%28Rosso_Fiorentino%29) by [Rosso Fiorentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosso_Fiorentino).[^o51][^o52]
- Etruscan Acropolis and Roman Cistern. The acropolis on the citadel dates to the 8th century B.C., while the cistern is from the 1st century B.C.[^o53]
- [Volterra Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra_Cathedral). It was enlarged in the 13th century after an earthquake. It houses a [ciborium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciborium_%28architecture%29) and some angels by [Mino da Fiesole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mino_da_Fiesole), a notable wood *Deposition* (1228), a masterwork of Romanesque sculpture and the Sacrament Chapel, with paintings by [Santi di Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santi_di_Tito), [Giovanni Balducci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Balducci) and [Agostino Veracini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agostino_Veracini). In the center of the vault are fragments of an *Eternal Father* by [Niccolò Circignani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Circignani). Also noteworthy is the Addolorata Chapel, with a terracotta group attributed to [Andrea della Robbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_della_Robbia) and a fresco of *Riding Magi* by [Benozzo Gozzoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benozzo_Gozzoli). In the nearby chapel, dedicated to the Most Holy Name of Jesus, is a table with Christ's monogram, allegedly painted by [Bernardino of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_of_Siena). The rectangular bell tower is from 1493.
- [Volterra Baptistery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra_Baptistery) or Baptistery of San Giovanni, built in the second half of the 13th century.
- [Fortezza Medicea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortezza_Medicea_%28Volterra%29) (Medicean Fortress),[^o54] built in the 1470s,[^o55] now a [prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison)[^o56] housing the noted restaurant, [Fortezza Medicea restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortezza_Medicea_restaurant).[^o57][^o58][^o59]
- [Guarnacci Etruscan Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Etrusco_Guarnacci),[^o60][^o61] with thousands of funeral urns dating back to the [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic) and [Archaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_in_Greece) periods.[^o62][^o63] Main attractions are the bronze statuette "[Ombra della sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ombra_della_sera)" ("Shadow of the Evening"),[^o64] and the sculpted effigy, "Urna degli Sposi" ("Urn of the Spouses") of an Etruscan couple in terra cotta.[^o65][^o66][^o67][^o68]
- The Etruscan Walls of Volterra, including the well-preserved Porta all'Arco (3rd-2nd centuries BC),[^o69][^o70] and Porta Diana gates.[^o71][^o72]
- The [Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici) Villa di Spedaletto, outside the city, in direction of [Lajatico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajatico)[^o73]
- There are excavations of Etruscan [tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb) in the Valle Bona area.
- [Sant'Alessandro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Alessandro,_Volterra), Romanesque Roman Catholic church.
- [Volterra Psychiatric Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra_Psychiatric_Hospital), founded in 1888. Closed in 1978, it was reopened for public and will be once more used for psychiatric purposes.[^o74]
- [Palazzo Inghirami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Inghirami,_Volterra)
- [Persio Flacco Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persio_Flacco_Theater)

## Notable people {#Notable_people}

- [Persius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persius) (34–62), the Roman satirist of Etruscan stock
- [Pope Linus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Linus), who, according to the *[Liber Pontificalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis)*, was born in Volterra, and was the successor to [Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter)[^o76]
- [Lucius Petronius Taurus Volusianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Petronius_Taurus_Volusianus), *[consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_consul)* with the Emperor [Gallienus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallienus) in AD 261 and [urban prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_prefect) in AD 267–268
- [Meshullam da Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshullam_of_Volterra) (d. 1508), an Italian-Jewish businessman who traveled to the [Land of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel) and surrounding Jewish communities. His works provide concise and important details about the nature and conditions of [Ottoman Jewry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Jews).
- [Daniele da Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniele_da_Volterra) (1509–1566), Mannerist painter
- The poet [Jacopo da Leona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_da_Leona), a judge at Volterra in the 13th century
- The Maffei family of Volterra produced the apostolic Secretary Gherardo Maffei and his three sons: the eldest [Antonio Maffei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Maffei_da_Volterra), who was one of the assassins in the [Pazzi Conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazzi_Conspiracy) against the [Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici) in 1478; second the humanist [Raffaello Maffei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaello_Maffei) called "Volterrano" who also served in the Curia; and youngest Mario Maffei, who was also a scholar and followed his father in the curia.
- [Giuseppe Bessi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Bessi) (1857–1922), sculptor
- [Emilio Fiaschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Fiaschi) (1858–1941), sculptor

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

- Volterra features in *Horatius*, a poem by [Lord Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Babington_Macaulay,_1st_Baron_Macaulay).[^o78][^o79]
- [Linda Proud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Proud)'s *A Tabernacle for the Sun* (2005), the first volume of *The Botticelli Trilogy*, begins with the sack of Volterra in 1472. Volterra is the ancestral home of the Maffei family and the events of 1472 lead directly to the [Pazzi Conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazzi_Conspiracy) of 1478. The protagonist of the novel is Tommaso de' Maffei, half brother of one of the conspirators.
- Volterra is an important location in [Stephenie Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephenie_Meyer)'s [*Twilight* series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_%28novel_series%29). In the books, Volterra is home to the [Volturi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volturi), a clan of rich, regal, powerful ancient vampires, who essentially act as the rulers of the world's vampire population. (However, the relevant scenes from the [movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Twilight_Saga:_New_Moon) were shot in [Montepulciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montepulciano).)
- Volterra is the site of [Stendhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stendhal)'s famously disastrous encounter in 1819 with his beloved Countess Mathilde Dembowska: she recognised him there, despite his disguise of new clothes and green glasses, and was furious. This is the central incident in his book *On Love*.[^o80][^o81]
- Volterra is mentioned repeatedly in British author [Dudley Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Pope)'s [Captain Nicholas Ramage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Ramage) historical nautical series. Gianna, the Marchesa of Volterra and the fictional ruler of the area, features in the first twelve books of the eighteen-book series. The books chart the progress and career of Ramage during the Napoleonic wars of the late eighteenth and early nineteenth century, providing readers with well-scripted articulate details of life aboard sailing vessels and conditions at sea of that time.[^o82][^o83]
- Volterra is the site where the novel *Chimaira* by the Italian author [Valerio Massimo Manfredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerio_Massimo_Manfredi) takes place.[^o84]
- [Valerio Massimo Manfredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerio_Massimo_Manfredi)'s *The Ancient Curse* is also set in Volterra, where a statue called 'The Shade of Twilight' is stolen from the Volterra museum.
- Volterra is featured in Jhumpa Lahiri's 2008 collection of short stories *Unaccustomed Earth*. It is where Hema and Kaushik, the protagonists of the final short story "Going Ashore," travel before they part.[^o85]
- Volterra is featured in [Luchino Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luchino_Visconti)'s 1965 film *[Vaghe stelle dell'Orsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaghe_stelle_dell'Orsa)*, released as Sandra (Of a Thousand Delights) in the United States and as Of These Thousand Pleasures in the UK.[^o86]
- Volterra's scenery is used for Central City in the 2017 film [Fullmetal Alchemist (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullmetal_Alchemist_%28film%29) directed by Fumihiko Sori.
- The 2016 video game *[The Town of Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Town_of_Light)* is set in a fictionalized version of the notorious [Volterra Psychiatric Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra_Psychiatric_Hospital).[^o87]
- "Volaterrae" is the name given by Dan and Una to their secret place in Far Wood in [Rudyard Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling)'s *Puck of Pook's Hill.* They named it from the verse in Lord Macaulay's *Lays of Ancient Rome*:

From lordly Volaterrae,

Where scowls the far-famed hold

Piled by the hands of giants

For Godlike Kings of old.

- Volterra and its relationship with Medici Florence features in the 2018 second season of *[Medici: Masters of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici:_Masters_of_Florence)*.
