# Spectral space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spectral space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **spectral space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the [spectrum of a commutative ring](wikipedia://en/37291). It is sometimes also called a **coherent space** because of the connection to [coherent topoi](wikipedia://en/64383764).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *X* be a topological space and let *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*) be the set of all [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [open subsets](wikipedia://en/39358) of *X*. Then *X* is said to be *spectral* if it satisfies all of the following conditions:

- *X* is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).
- *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*) is a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) of open subsets of *X*.
- *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*) is [closed under](wikipedia://en/293802) finite intersections.
- *X* is [sober](wikipedia://en/602490), i.e., every nonempty [irreducible](wikipedia://en/7556651) [closed subset](wikipedia://en/47279) of *X* has a unique [generic point](wikipedia://en/3043978).

From that *X* is sober it follows that *X* is [T<sub>0</sub>](wikipedia://en/68121). Indeed the definition of a spectral space can be equivalently reformulated through explicitly assuming that *X* is T<sub>0</sub> and weaking the assumption that *X* is sober to only require it to be **quasi-sober**, i.e. every irreducible closed subspace possesses a (not nececssarily unique) generic point. This is the way the definition was originally formulated in Hochster's 1967 thesis, in which the term "spectral space" was first coined.

:::

## Equivalent descriptions {#Equivalent_descriptions}

Let *X* be a topological space. Each of the following properties are equivalent to the property of *X* being spectral:

1. *X* is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to a [projective limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_limit) of finite T<sub>0</sub> spaces.
1. *X* is homeomorphic to the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/26599926) of a [bounded distributive lattice](wikipedia://en/310883) *L*. In this case, *L* is isomorphic (as a bounded lattice) to the lattice *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*) (this is called **[Stone representation of distributive lattices](wikipedia://en/26599926)**).
1. *X* is homeomorphic to the [spectrum of a commutative ring](wikipedia://en/37291).
1. *X* is the topological space determined by a [Priestley space](wikipedia://en/26599382).
1. *X* is a T<sub>0</sub> space whose [locale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frames_and_locales) of open sets is coherent (and every coherent locale comes from a unique spectral space in this way).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let *X* be a spectral space and let *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*) be as before. Then:

- *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*) is a [bounded sublattice](wikipedia://en/469304) of subsets of *X*.
- Every closed [subspace](wikipedia://en/910926) of *X* is spectral.
- An arbitrary intersection of compact and open subsets of *X* (hence of elements from *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*)) is again spectral.
- *X* is T<sub>0</sub> by definition, but in general not [T<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/148721).[^o7] In fact a spectral space is T<sub>1</sub> if and only if it is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) (i.e. T<sub>2</sub>) if and only if it is a [boolean space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_space) if and only if *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*) is a [boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/54476844).
- *X* can be seen as a [pairwise Stone space](wikipedia://en/26598031).[^o8]

:::

## Spectral maps {#Spectral_maps}

A **spectral map** *f: X → Y* between spectral spaces *X* and *Y* is a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) such that the [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) of every open and compact subset of *Y* under *f* is again compact.

The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of spectral spaces, which has spectral maps as morphisms, is [dually equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of bounded distributive lattices (together with [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) of such lattices).[^n1] In this anti-equivalence, a spectral space *X* corresponds to the lattice *K*<sup>$\circ$</sup>(*X*).
