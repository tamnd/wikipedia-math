# Paolo Ruffini

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paolo Ruffini"}

**Paolo Ruffini** (22 September 1765 – 10 May 1822) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher). Remembered chiefly for what is now known as the [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518), Ruffini also made a major contribution to the [theory of equations](wikipedia://en/583600), developing the so-called theory of substitutions, the forerunner of modern [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Paolo Ruffini was born in 1765 in [Valentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentano) in the Papal States, to a middle-class family. His father, Basilio, was a physician.[^n1] When Paolo was a teenager his family moved to [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia), near Modena.[^n2] As a young boy he intended to enter [Holy orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders) and went so far as to receive the [tonsure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonsure), but he later changed his mind. He enrolled in the University of Modena, where he studied literature, medicine, mathematics, and philosophy. Among his teachers of mathematics at Modena were Luigi Fantini, Paolo Cassiani and [Giovanni Battista Venturi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Venturi).[^n3] In 1788, he was awarded a degree in philosophy, medicine, and surgery, and in 1789, he added a mathematics degree.[^n4] In 1791, he received his license to practice medicine.

### Career {#Career}

When only 23 years of age, Ruffini was elected Professor of Mathematics at the University of Modena. In 1780, he was elected a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_nazionale_delle_scienze), the so-called "Society of the Forty".[^n5] A conservative Catholic, Ruffini lost his position at the University during the French invasion of Italy because he would not take the oath of allegiance to the new government. Barred from teaching, he devoted himself to medicine and mathematical research.

In 1799, he published a revolutionary and highly controversial theory of equations claiming that [quintics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintic_function) could not be solved by radicals, as leading mathematicians had believed at the time. Ruffini was the first to introduce the notion of the order of an element, conjugacy, the cycle decomposition of elements of permutation groups, and the notions of primitive and imprimitive.[^o12]

Like other great minds, ahead of their time, his mathematical contemporaries could not accept his revolutionary idea that a polynomial could not be solved in radicals. [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573) is sometimes incorrectly credited with Ruffini's ideas, since Ruffini was dismissed by peers who did not accept his theories into the mainstream mathematics of the time. [Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793), while finding Ruffini's work impressive, hesitated to accept such a revolutionary concept.[^n6] The first mathematician who recognised the importance of Ruffini's work was [Augustin Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842), who generalised some of his results from 1813 to 1815.[^n7] Ruffini's work became widely recognized at the end of the nineteenth century, thanks to contributions by scholars like [Burkhardt](wikipedia://en/19931824) and [Pierpont](wikipedia://en/2594993).[^n8]

### Later life {#Later_life}

In 1804 Ruffini won a gold medal offered by the Società Italiana delle Scienze for the best method of determining the roots of a numerical equation of any degree, the famous [Ruffini's rule](wikipedia://en/766014).[^o17] After the defeat of Napoleon in 1814, Ruffini returned to the University of Modena as rector, in addition to holding professorships in mathematics and medicine.[^o18] In 1816 he succeeded [Antonio Cagnoli](wikipedia://en/48084397) as president of the National Academy of Sciences. Ruffini contracted severe [typhoid fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid_fever) while aiding victims of the 1817-18 typhus epidemic and never fully recovered.[^n9] He died on 10 May 1822 in Modena and was buried in the Church of [Santa Maria della Pomposa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Pomposa,_Modena), between the tombs of [Sigonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolus_Sigonius) and [Muratori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovico_Antonio_Muratori).[^n4] An accomplished physician, Ruffini also published scientific treatises on [typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus) based on his own experience with the disease in 1820.[^n10] His mathematical works were edited by [Ettore Bortolotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Bortolotti) and published in 1915 by the [Italian Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/11969092).[^o22]

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

Ruffini's works include notable developments in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). His most important accomplishment is the proof that that [quintic (and higher-order) equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintic_function) cannot be solved by [radicals](wikipedia://en/235029) ([Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518)).[^o24] While his initial proof in 1799 was [incomplete](wikipedia://en/14528017), it laid the foundation for the later work of [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573), who provided a complete proof in 1824.[^o25]

Ruffini is also well known for the [Ruffini's rule](wikipedia://en/766014), a quick method to [divide polynomials](wikipedia://en/314205) by linear factors.[^n11] Described by Ruffini in a paper published in 1804, this rule was a significant contribution to the field of algebra and remains a valuable tool in polynomial manipulation. In a second paper published in 1813 Ruffini applies his method to the extraction of roots of numbers and gives in full several illustrations of the process. He explains also how the process may be contracted. Thereupon he proceeds to a derivation of his method of solution by elementary processes, without the use of the differential calculus.[^o27]

Ruffini also marked a major improvement for [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), developing [Joseph-Louis Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793)'s work on permutation theory (*Réflexions sur la théorie algébrique des équations*, 1770–1771).[^o28] Lagrange's work was largely ignored until Ruffini established strong connections between permutations and the solvability of algebraic equations. Ruffini introduced notions like the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of an element, [conjugacy](wikipedia://en/49176), and [cycle decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_decomposition_%28group_theory%29) in [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634), making him a pioneer in this field. He was also the first to distinguish between [transitive](wikipedia://en/12781) and intransitive permutation groups, and among the transitive permutation groups between [primitive](wikipedia://en/2739252) and imprimitive.[^n12]

Ruffini also wrote on [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and the [quadrature of the circle](wikipedia://en/201359). In his later years he wrote several articles and books on epistemological and philosophical topics considered from a Catholic perspective. In his essay *The immateriality of the soul* (Immaterialità dell’anima), published in 1806, he opposed [Erasmus Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Darwin)'s philosophical system, while in his *Riflessioni critiche* (1821) he refuted [Pierre-Simon Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783)’s *Essai philosophique sur les probabilités* (1812).[^o30]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Ruffini's proof was initially received with skepticism by the mathematical community.[^n13] Particularly strong objections against Ruffini's work were raised by [Gian Francesco Malfatti](wikipedia://en/1475987) and [Gregorio Fontana](wikipedia://en/6942423).[^o33] In a number of papers published between 1802 and 1806 Ruffini refined and completed his proof, answering the objections raised by Malfatti and Fontana and adopting some suggestions of [Pietro Abbati Marescotti](wikipedia://en/12894753).[^n14] In his paper *Riflessione intorno alla soluzione delle equazioni algebraiche generali*, published in 1813, Ruffini gave a different demonstration of his theorem that largely coincides with what is now called [Wantzel](wikipedia://en/2610953)'s modification of the theorem of Ruffini–Abel.[^n15]

Only later in Ruffini’s life his proof was recognized as fundamentally correct and built upon by mathematicians like Cauchy, Abel and [Galois](wikipedia://en/9815).[^n16] Ruffini's insights into the structure of permutation groups were crucial for understanding the limitations of algebraic solutions.

## Publications {#Publications}

*Image: Ruffini - Teoria generale delle equazioni, 1799 - 1366896.jpg.* *Teoria generale delle equazioni*, 1799

- 1799: *Teoria Generale delle Equazioni, in cui si dimostra impossibile la soluzione algebraica delle equazioni generali di grado superiore al quarto* ("General Theory of equations, in which the algebraic solution of general equations of degree higher than four is proven impossible")
- 1802: *Riflessioni intorno alla rettificazione ed alla quadratura del circulo* ("Reflections on the rectification and the squaring of the circle")
- 1802: *Della soluzione delle equazioni algebraiche determinate particolari di grado superiore al quarto* ("On the solution of certain determined algebraic equations of degree higher than four")
- 1804: *Sopra la determinazione delle radici nelle equazioni numeriche di qualunque grado* ("About the determination of the roots in the numerical equations of any degree")
- 1806: *Della immortalità dell’anima* ("On the immortality of the soul")
- 1807: *Algebra elementare* ("Elementary algebra")
- 1813: *Riflessioni intorno alla soluzione delle equazioni algebraiche generali* ("Reflections on the algebraic solutions of equations")
- 1820: *Memoria sul tifo contagioso* ("Essay on contagious typhoid")
- 1821: *Riflessioni critiche sopra il saggio filosofico intorno alle probabilità del signor conte Laplace* ("Critical reflections on the philosophical essay about probability by Count Laplace")
