# Eugenio Beltrami

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eugenio Beltrami"}

**Eugenio Beltrami** (16 November 1835 – 18 February 1900) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) notable for his work concerning [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). His work was noted especially for clarity of exposition. He was the first to prove the consistency of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) by modelling it on a surface of [constant curvature](wikipedia://en/3638803), the [pseudosphere](wikipedia://en/60766), and in the interior of an *n*-dimensional [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090), the so-called [Beltrami&ndash;Klein model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltrami&ndash;Klein_model). He also developed [singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207) for [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859), which has been subsequently rediscovered several times. Beltrami's use of [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416) for problems of mathematical physics indirectly influenced the development of [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus) by [Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro](wikipedia://en/499223) and [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Beltrami was born in 1835 in [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona) ([Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy)), then a part of the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire), and now part of Italy. Both parents were artists, Giovanni Beltrami and the Venetian Elisa Barozzi. He began studying mathematics at [University of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia) in 1853, but was expelled from [Ghislieri College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghislieri_College) in 1856 due to his political opinions—he was sympathetic with the *[Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risorgimento)*. During this time, he was taught and influenced by [Francesco Brioschi](wikipedia://en/6421852). He had to discontinue his studies because of financial hardship and spent the next several years as a secretary working for the Lombardy–Venice railroad company. He was appointed to the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) as a professor in 1862, the year he published his first research paper. Throughout his life, Beltrami had various professorial positions at the universities of [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza) and Pavia. From 1891 until the end of his life, Beltrami lived in Rome. He became the president of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) in 1898 and a senator of [the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_%28Napoleonic%29) in 1899.

## Contributions to non-Euclidean geometry {#Contributions_to_non-Euclidean_geometry}

In 1868, Beltrami published two memoirs (written in Italian; French translations by [J. Hoüel](wikipedia://en/17144138) appeared in 1869) dealing with consistency and interpretations of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) of [János Bolyai](wikipedia://en/98912) and [Nikolai Lobachevsky](wikipedia://en/648527). In his "Essay on an interpretation of non-Euclidean geometry", Beltrami proposed that this geometry could be realised on a surface of constant negative [curvature](wikipedia://en/285623), a [pseudosphere](wikipedia://en/60766). For Beltrami's concept, lines of the geometry are represented by [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on the pseudosphere and theorems of non-Euclidean geometry can be proved within ordinary three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), and not derived in an axiomatic fashion, as Lobachevsky and [Bolyai](wikipedia://en/98912) had done previously. In 1840, [Ferdinand Minding](wikipedia://en/22182418) already considered geodesic triangles on the pseudosphere and remarked that the corresponding "trigonometric formulas" are obtained from the corresponding formulas of [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405) by replacing the usual [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) with [hyperbolic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function); this was further developed by [Delfino Codazzi](wikipedia://en/703285) in 1857, but apparently neither of them noticed the association with Lobachevsky's work. In this way, Beltrami attempted to demonstrate that two-dimensional non-Euclidean geometry is as valid as the [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) of the space, and in particular, that [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [parallel postulate](wikipedia://en/33731493) could not be derived from the other axioms of Euclidean geometry. It is often stated that this proof was incomplete due to the singularities of the pseudosphere, which means that geodesics could not be extended indefinitely. However, [John Stillwell](wikipedia://en/15066763) remarks that Beltrami must have been well aware of this difficulty, which is also manifested by the fact that the pseudosphere is topologically a [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29), and not a plane, and he spent a part of his memoir designing a way around it. By a suitable choice of coordinates, Beltrami showed how the [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on the pseudosphere can be transferred to the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) and that the [singularity](wikipedia://en/581763) of the pseudosphere corresponds to a [horocycle](wikipedia://en/11084460) on the non-Euclidean plane. On the other hand, in the introduction to his memoir, Beltrami states that it would be impossible to justify "the rest of Lobachevsky's theory", i.e., the non-Euclidean geometry of space, by this method.

In the second memoir published during the same year (1868), "Fundamental theory of spaces of constant curvature", Beltrami continued this logic and gave an abstract proof of [equiconsistency](wikipedia://en/3435716) of hyperbolic and Euclidean geometry for any dimension. He accomplished this by introducing several models of non-Euclidean geometry that are now known as the [Beltrami–Klein model](wikipedia://en/4792791), the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836), and the [Poincaré half-plane model](wikipedia://en/461942), together with transformations that relate them. For the half-plane model, Beltrami cited a note by [Joseph Liouville](wikipedia://en/341810) in the treatise of [Gaspard Monge](wikipedia://en/77840) on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). Beltrami also showed that *n*-dimensional Euclidean geometry is realised on a [horosphere](wikipedia://en/13487128) of the (*n* + 1)-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742), so the logical relation between consistency of the Euclidean and the non-Euclidean geometries is symmetric. Beltrami acknowledged the influence of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980)'s groundbreaking *Habilitation* lecture "On the hypotheses on which geometry is based" (1854; published posthumously in 1868).

Although today Beltrami's "Essay" is recognised as very important for the development of non-Euclidean geometry, the reception at the time was less enthusiastic. [Luigi Cremona](wikipedia://en/1846002) objected to perceived [circular reasoning](wikipedia://en/313944), which even forced Beltrami to delay the publication of the "Essay" by one year. Subsequently, [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) failed to acknowledge Beltrami's priority in the construction of the projective disk model of non-Euclidean geometry. This reaction can be attributed in part to the novelty of Beltrami's reasoning, which was similar to the ideas of Riemann concerning abstract [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). J. Hoüel published Beltrami's proof in his French translation of the works of Lobachevsky and Bolyai.
