# Joan Birman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Joan Birman"}

**Joan Sylvia Lyttle Birman** (born May 30, 1927, in New York City)[^o3] is an American mathematician, specializing in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). She has made contributions to the study of [knots](wikipedia://en/153008), [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257), [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/31060586) of surfaces, [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), [contact structures](wikipedia://en/476398) and [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087). Birman is research professor emerita at [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University),[^o4] where she has been since 1973.

:::

## Family {#Family}

Her parents were George and Lillian Lyttle, both [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish).[^o6] Her father was from Russia but grew up in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England). At age 17, George emigrated to the US and became a successful dress manufacturer. Her mother was born in New York and her parents were Russian-Polish immigrants.

Birman's father appreciated the opportunities from having a business but he wanted his daughters to focus on education.

Joan Birman married [Joseph Birman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Birman) in 1950, the year she and he gained Masters degrees in their respective fields.[^o6] They have three children, [Kenneth Paul Birman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Birman), Deborah Birman Shlider, and Carl David Birman. Her husband, who died in October 2016, was a physicist and a leading advocate for [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights) for scientists.[^o7]

## Education {#Education}

After high school, Birman entered [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College), a coeducational institution in Swarthmore, Pennsylvania, and majored in mathematics. However, she disliked living in the dorms so she transferred to [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College), a women-only college affiliated with [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), in order to live at home.[^o6]

Birman received her B.A. in mathematics from Barnard College in 1948 and an M.A. in physics from Columbia University in 1950. After working in industry from 1950 to 1960, she studied mathematics at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) under the supervision of [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666), earning her PhD in 1968. Her dissertation was titled "Braid groups and their relationship to mapping class groups".

## Career {#Career}

After she earned her bachelor's degree from Barnard, Birman accepted a position at the Polytechnic Research and Development Co., which was affiliated with [Brooklyn Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Tandon_School_of_Engineering). She later worked for the Technical Research Group and the W. L. Maxson Corporation.[^o11]

Birman's first academic position was at the [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Institute_of_Technology) (1968–1973). When she joined, she was the only female professor of its 160-member faculty.[^o12] In 1969 she published "On Braid Groups", which introduced a way to relate the [mapping class group of a surface](wikipedia://en/31060586) to the mapping class group of a punctured version of the same surface. Known as the Birman Exact Sequence, this has become one of the most important tools in the study of braids and surfaces.[^o13] During the later part of this period, in 1974, she published a [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph), *[Braids, Links, and Mapping Class Groups](wikipedia://en/66274280)*, based on a graduate course she taught as a visiting professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1971–72. It is considered the first comprehensive treatment of braid theory. It introduced the modern theory to the field and offered the first complete proof of the [Markov theorem](wikipedia://en/56349373) on braids.[^o13]

In 1973, she joined the faculty of Barnard College, where she served as chair of the Mathematics Department from 1973 to 1987, 1989 to 1991, and 1995 to 1998. She was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in the summer of 1988.[^o14]

She supervised 21 doctoral students, and has a total of 70 academic descendants. Her doctoral students include [Józef Przytycki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Przytycki) and [Tara E. Brendle](wikipedia://en/62238226).[^o9]

Birman was a founding editor of the journals [Geometry and Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_and_Topology) and [Algebraic and Geometric Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_and_Geometric_Topology).[^o15]

Birman was a co-founder of Mathematical Sciences Publishing, a non-profit publishing house. She was a member of the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences) Human Rights of Scientists Committee.[^o16]

## Work {#Work}

According to her MathSciNet author profile, Birman's scientific work includes 106 research publications and over 300 published reviews in Math Reviews. She is the author of the research monograph *[Braids, Links, and Mapping Class Groups](wikipedia://en/66274280)*.

In 2026, Birman, with Vasudha Bharathram and [Tara E. Brendle](wikipedia://en/62238226) posted a proof of the faithfulness of the [Burau representation](wikipedia://en/19140638) when n = 4 to [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv), whose status had not previously been known.[^o18]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1974, Birman was selected as a [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellows) by the [Alfred P. Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation).[^o20]

In 1994, she was selected as a [Guggenheim Foundation Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) by the [John Simon Guggenheim Memorial Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Simon_Guggenheim_Memorial_Foundation).[^o21] In 1996, the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) awarded Birman the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize), "the highest award for mathematical [expository writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expository_writing)" for her 1993 essay *New Points of View in Knot Theory*.[^o22] In 1997, Birman received an honorary doctorate from the [Technion Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_Israel_Institute_of_Technology).[^o15]

In 2003, Birman was elected to the European Academy of Sciences.[^o23] In 2005, she won the New York City Mayor's Award for Excellence in Science and Technology.[^o3]

In 2012, Birman was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences)[^o24][^o25] In 2015, Birman was named an honorary member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o26] In 2021, Birman was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o27]

### American Mathematical Society {#American_Mathematical_Society}

From 1978 to 1980, and again from 1990 to 1992, Birman was an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) Council member at large.[^o29]

In 1990, Birman donated funds to the AMS to establish the [Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/37554140) in honor of her sister, [Ruth Lyttle Satter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Lyttle_Satter), who was a [plant physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_physiologist).[^o15] In 2017, she endowed the Joan and Joseph Birman Fellowship for Women Scholars at the AMS to support mathematical research by mid-career women.[^o30]

In 2013, she became a fellow of the American Mathematical Society in the inaugural class.[^o31]

### Association of Women in Mathematics {#Association_of_Women_in_Mathematics}

In 1987, she was selected by the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) to be a [Noether Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noether_Lecturer), honoring her fundamental and sustained contributions to the mathematical sciences.[^o33]

In 2015, the Association for Women in Mathematics began awarding the Joan & Joseph Birman Research Prize in Topology and Geometry.[^o34] In 2020, they included her in that year's class of Fellows for "her groundbreaking research connecting diverse fields, and for her award-winning expository writing; for continuously supporting women in mathematics as an active mentor and a research role model; and for sponsoring multiple prize initiatives for women".[^o35] The AWM also included her in a deck of cards featuring notable women mathematicians.[^o36]
