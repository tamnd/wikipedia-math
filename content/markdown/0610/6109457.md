# Dihedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dihedron"}

A **dihedron** (pl. **dihedra**) is a type of [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), made of two [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) faces which share the same set of *n* [edges](wikipedia://en/23470). In three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), it is [degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_degeneracy) if its faces are flat, while in three-dimensional [spherical space](wikipedia://en/1205989#Cyclic_case_.28lens_spaces.29), a dihedron with flat faces can be thought of as a lens, an example of which is the fundamental domain of a [lens space](wikipedia://en/885725) L(*p*,*q*).[^o3] Dihedra have also been called **bihedra**,[^o4] **flat polyhedra**,[^o5] or **doubly covered polygons**.[^o5]

:::

As a [spherical tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_tiling), a **dihedron** can exist as nondegenerate form, with two *n*-sided faces covering the sphere, each face being a [hemisphere](wikipedia://en/27859), and vertices on a [great circle](wikipedia://en/48082). It is **regular** if the vertices are equally spaced.

The [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of an *n*-gonal dihedron is an *n*-gonal [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192), where *n* [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) faces share two vertices.

## As a flat-faced polyhedron {#As_a_flat-faced_polyhedron}

A **dihedron** can be considered a degenerate [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) whose two (planar) *n*-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) bases are connected "back-to-back", so that the resulting object has no depth. The polygons must be congruent, but glued in such a way that one is the mirror image of the other. This applies only if the distance between the two faces is zero; for a distance larger than zero, the faces are infinite polygons (a bit like the [apeirogonal hosohedron](wikipedia://en/42115167)'s digon faces, having a width larger than zero, are infinite stripes).

Dihedra can arise from [Alexandrov's uniqueness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrov's_uniqueness_theorem), which characterizes the distances on the surface of any convex polyhedron as being locally Euclidean except at a finite number of points with positive [angular defect](wikipedia://en/673575) summing to 4$\pi$. This characterization holds also for the distances on the surface of a dihedron, so the statement of Alexandrov's theorem requires that dihedra be considered as convex polyhedra.[^o7]

Some dihedra can arise as lower limit members of other polyhedra families: a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) with [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) bases would be a square dihedron, and a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045) with a digon base would be a triangular dihedron.

A **regular dihedron**, with Schläfli symbol {*n*,2}, is made of two [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306), each with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {*n*}.[^o8]

## As a tiling of the sphere {#As_a_tiling_of_the_sphere}

A **spherical dihedron** is made of two [spherical polygons](wikipedia://en/650405) which share the same set of *n* vertices, on a [great circle](wikipedia://en/48082) equator; each polygon of a spherical dihedron fills a [hemisphere](wikipedia://en/27859#Hemisphere).

A **regular spherical dihedron** is made of two regular spherical polygons which share the same set of *n* vertices, equally spaced on a [great circle](wikipedia://en/48082) equator.

The regular polyhedron {2,2} is self-dual, and is both a [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192) and a dihedron.

## Ditopes {#Ditopes}

A regular *ditope* is an *n*-dimensional analogue of a dihedron, with Schläfli symbol {*p*,...,*q*,*r*,2}. It has two [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647), {*p*,...,*q*,*r*}, which share all [ridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_%28geometry%29), {*p*,...,*q*} in common.[^o13]
