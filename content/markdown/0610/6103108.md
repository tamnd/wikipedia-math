# Charles M. Stein

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Charles M. Stein"}

**Charles Max Stein** (March 22, 1920 – November 24, 2016) was an American mathematical statistician and professor of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).

:::

He received his Ph.D. in 1947 at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) with advisor [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430). He held faculty positions at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley) and the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) before moving permanently to Stanford in 1953. He is known for [Stein's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stein's_paradox) in [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216), which shows that [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) estimates can be uniformly improved when many parameters are estimated;[^o3] for [Stein's lemma](wikipedia://en/744802), giving a formula for the [covariance](wikipedia://en/157059) of one [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with the value of a function of another when the two random variables are [jointly normally distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_normal_distribution); for the [Chernoff-Stein Lemma](wikipedia://en/60531312#Optimal_error_exponent_for_Neyman–Pearson_testing)[^o4] which states that the [Kullback Leibler divergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kullback_Leibler_divergence) describes the optimal [type II error](wikipedia://en/5657877) exponent for the [Neyman-Pearson](wikipedia://en/685158) setting in hypothesis testing, and for [Stein's method](wikipedia://en/12933953), a way of proving theorems such as the [Central Limit Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Limit_Theorem) that does not require the variables to be independent and identically distributed.[^o5] He was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences). He died in November 2016 at the age of 96.[^o6]
