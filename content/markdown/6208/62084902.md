# E. Mark Gold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="E. Mark Gold"}

**E. Mark Gold** (often written "E Mark Gold" without a dot,[^o17][^o4][^o10][^o20][^o21][^o22][^o23][^o26] born 1936 in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles))[^o2] is an American physicist, mathematician, and computer scientist. He became well known for his article *[Language identification in the limit](wikipedia://en/1299933)*[^o3][^o4] which pioneered a formal model for [inductive inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_inference) of [formal languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_languages), mainly by computers. Since 1999, an award of the conference on [algorithmic learning theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_learning_theory) is named after him.[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Academic education {#Academic_education}

In 1956, he got a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) in mathematics from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), in 1958, he got a [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.S.) in physics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o2] In Jan 1965, got his Ph.D. from [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), supervised by [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024).[^o2][^o9][^o10]

## Scientific career {#Scientific_career}

Gold's most influential contribution is the "Language Identification in the Limit" framework, often referred to as Gold's Theorem. This work established the mathematical foundations for how a computational system can learn a formal language from a sequence of examples. He demonstrated that while certain classes of languages (like regular languages) are identifiable in the limit, others, such as the class of all context-free languages, are not learnable from positive examples alone.[^o12] This discovery had a profound impact on [computational linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics) and [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence).

In 1962 and 1963, he worked at Unified Science Associates, [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena), on physics problems.[^o13][^o14][^o15] About in 1963, he turned to mathematics,[^o14] working for [Lear Siegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lear_Siegler),[^o14][^o16][^o10] the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation),[^o3][^o4] [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University),[^o17] the Institute for Formal Studies, [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles),[^o4] and the [Oregon Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Research_Institute).[^o18] About in 1973, he moved to [Montreal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_University)[^o19][^o18][^o20][^o21][^o22][^o23][^o24] and about 1977 to [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester).[^o25][^o23] In 1991, he published from [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland).[^o26]
