# String group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="String group"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **string group** is an infinite-dimensional group $\operatorname{String}(n)$ introduced by (Stolz 1996) as a [$3$-connected](wikipedia://en/6519310) cover of a [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231). A **string manifold** is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with a lifting of its [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) to a string group bundle. This means that in addition to being able to define [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) along paths, one can also define holonomies for surfaces going between strings. There is a short [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) of [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315)

> $$0\rightarrow{\displaystyle K(\mathbb {Z} ,2)}\rightarrow \operatorname{String}(n)\rightarrow \operatorname{Spin}(n)\rightarrow 0$$

where $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$ is an [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) and $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ is a spin group. The string group is an entry in the [Whitehead tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead_tower) (dual to the notion of [Postnikov tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postnikov_tower)) for the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954):

> $$\cdots\rightarrow \operatorname{Fivebrane}(n) \to \operatorname{String}(n)\rightarrow \operatorname{Spin}(n)\rightarrow \operatorname{SO}(n) \rightarrow \operatorname{O}(n)$$

It is obtained by killing the $\pi_3$ [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) for $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$, in the same way that $\operatorname{Spin}(n)$ is obtained from $\operatorname{SO}(n)$ by killing $\pi_1$. The resulting manifold cannot be any finite-dimensional [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), since all finite-dimensional compact Lie groups have a non-vanishing $\pi_3$. The fivebrane group follows, by killing $\pi_7$.

:::

More generally, the construction of the Postnikov tower via short exact sequences starting with Eilenberg–MacLane spaces can be applied to any [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) *G*, giving the string group *String*(*G*).

## Intuition for the string group {#Intuition_for_the_string_group}

The relevance of the Eilenberg–MacLane space $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$ lies in the fact that there are the [homotopy equivalences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence)

> $$K(\mathbb{Z},1) \simeq U(1) \simeq B\mathbb{Z}$$

for the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) $B\mathbb{Z}$, and the fact $K(\mathbb{Z},2) \simeq BU(1)$. Notice that because the complex spin group is a [group extension](wikipedia://en/345807)

> $$0\to K(\mathbb{Z},1) \to \operatorname{Spin}^\mathbb{C}(n) \to \operatorname{Spin}(n) \to 0$$

the String group can be thought of as a "higher" complex spin group extension, in the sense of [higher group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_group) since the space $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$ is an example of a higher group. It can be thought of the topological realization of the [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) $\mathbf{B}U(1)$ whose object is a single point and whose morphisms are the group $U(1)$. Note that the homotopical degree of $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$ is $2$, meaning its homotopy is concentrated in degree $2$, because it comes from the [homotopy fiber](wikipedia://en/19207725) of the map

> $$\operatorname{String}(n) \to \operatorname{Spin}(n)$$

from the Whitehead tower whose homotopy cokernel is $K(\mathbb{Z},3)$. This is because the homotopy fiber lowers the degree by $1$.

### Understanding the geometry {#Understanding_the_geometry}

The geometry of String bundles requires the understanding of multiple constructions in homotopy theory,[^o5] but they essentially boil down to understanding what $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$-bundles are, and how these higher group extensions behave. Namely, $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$-bundles on a space $M$ are represented geometrically as [bundle gerbes](wikipedia://en/1981917) since any $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$-bundle can be realized as the homotopy fiber of a map giving a homotopy square

> $$\begin{matrix} P & \to & * \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ M & \xrightarrow{} & K(\mathbb{Z},3) \end{matrix}$$

where $K(\mathbb{Z},3) = B(K(\mathbb{Z},2))$. Then, a string bundle $S \to M$ must map to a spin bundle $\mathbb{S} \to M$ which is $K(\mathbb{Z},2)$-equivariant, analogously to how spin bundles map equivariantly to the frame bundle.

## Fivebrane group and higher groups {#Fivebrane_group_and_higher_groups}

The fivebrane group can similarly be understood[^o7] by killing the $\pi_7(\operatorname{Spin}(n)) \cong \pi_7(\operatorname{O}(n))$ group of the string group $\operatorname{String}(n)$ using the Whitehead tower. It can then be understood again using an exact sequence of [higher groups](wikipedia://en/21368075)

> $$0 \to K(\mathbb{Z},6) \to \operatorname{Fivebrane}(n) \to \operatorname{String}(n) \to 0$$

giving a presentation of $\operatorname{Fivebrane}(n)$ it terms of an iterated extension, i.e. an extension by $K(\mathbb{Z},6)$ by $\operatorname{String}(n)$. Note map on the right is from the Whitehead tower, and the map on the left is the homotopy fiber.
