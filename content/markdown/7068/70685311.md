# Mean line segment length

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mean line segment length"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **mean line segment length** is the average length of a line segment connecting two points chosen [uniformly at random](wikipedia://en/1699223) in a given shape. In other words, it is the [expected](wikipedia://en/9653) [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) between two random points, where each point in the shape is equally likely to be chosen.

:::

Even for simple shapes such as a square or a triangle, solving for the exact value of their mean line segment lengths can be difficult because their [closed-form expressions](wikipedia://en/585143) can get quite complicated. As an example, consider the following question:

*What is the average distance between two randomly chosen points inside a square with side length 1?*

While the question may seem simple, it has a fairly complicated answer; the exact value for this is $\frac{2 + \sqrt{2} + 5 \ln (1 + \sqrt{2})}{15}$.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

The mean line segment length for an *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) shape *S* may formally be defined as the [expected](wikipedia://en/9653) [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) ||⋅|| between two random points *x* and *y*,[^o4]

$$\mathbb E[\|x-y\|]=\frac1{\lambda(S)^2}\int_S \int_S \|x-y\| \,d\lambda(x) \,d\lambda(y)$$

where *λ* is the *n*-dimensional [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198).

For the [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional) case, this is defined using the [distance formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_formula) for two points (*x*<sub>1</sub>, *y*<sub>1</sub>) and (*x*<sub>2</sub>, *y*<sub>2</sub>)

$$\frac1{\lambda(S)^2}\iint_S \iint_S \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2} \,dx_1 \,dy_1 \,dx_2 \,dy_2.$$

:::

## Approximation methods {#Approximation_methods}

*Image: Mean line segment length Unit square.gif.* [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098) to approximate the mean line segment length of a [unit square](wikipedia://en/285301).

Since computing the mean line segment length involves calculating multidimensional integrals, various methods for [numerical integration](wikipedia://en/170089) can be used to approximate this value for any shape.

One such method is the [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098). To approximate the mean line segment length of a given shape, two points are randomly chosen in its interior and the distance is measured. After several repetitions of these steps, the average of these distances will eventually converge to the true value.

These methods can only give an approximation; they cannot be used to determine its exact value.

## Formulas {#Formulas}

### Line segment {#Line_segment}

For a line segment of length $d$, the average distance between two points is $\tfrac{1}{3}d$.[^o4]

### Triangle {#Triangle}

For a triangle with side lengths $a$, $b$, and $c$, the average distance between two points in its interior is given by the formula

$$\frac{4 s s_a s_b s_c}{15} \left[ \frac{1}{a^3} \ln\left( \frac{s}{s_a} \right) + \frac{1}{b^3} \ln\left( \frac{s}{s_b} \right) + \frac{1}{c^3} \ln\left( \frac{s}{s_c} \right) \right] + \frac{a+b+c}{15} + \frac{(b+c)(b-c)^2}{30a^2} + \frac{(a+c)(a-c)^2}{30b^2} + \frac{(a+b)(a-b)^2}{30c^2},$$

where $s = (a+b+c)/2$ is the [semiperimeter](wikipedia://en/886617), and $s_i$ denotes $s-i$.

For an equilateral triangle with side length *a*, this is equal to

$$\left(\frac{4 + 3 \ln 3}{20}\right)a \approx 0.364791843\ldots a.$$

### Square and rectangles {#Square_and_rectangles}

The average distance between two points inside a square with side length *s* is

$$\left(\frac{2 + \sqrt{2} + 5 \ln (1 + \sqrt{2})}{15}\right) s \approx 0.521405433\ldots s.$$

More generally, the mean line segment length of a rectangle with side lengths *l* and *w* is[^o4]

$$\frac{1}{15}\left[ \frac{l^3}{w^2} + \frac{w^3}{l^2} + d\left(3 - \frac{l^2}{w^2} - \frac{w^2}{l^2}\right) + \frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{w^2}{l} \ln \left(\frac{l+d}{w}\right) + \frac{l^2}{w} \ln \left(\frac{w+d}{l}\right) \right) \right]$$

where $d = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2}$ is the length of the rectangle's diagonal.

If the two points are instead chosen to be on different sides of the square, the average distance is given by[^o12][^o13]

$$\left(\frac{2 + \sqrt{2} + 5 \ln (1 + \sqrt{2})}{9}\right) s \approx 0.869009\ldots s.$$

::: {#o15 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Robbins constant"}

### Cube and hypercubes {#Cube_and_hypercubes}

The average distance between points inside an *n*-dimensional [unit hypercube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_hypercube) is denoted as $\Delta(n)$, and is given as

$$\Delta(n) = \underbrace{\int_0^1 \cdots \int_0^1}_{2n} \sqrt{(x_1 - y_1)^2 + (x_2 - y_2)^2 + \cdots + (x_n - y_n)^2} \,dx_1 \cdots \,dx_n \,dy_1 \cdots \,dy_n$$

The first two values, $\Delta(1)$ and $\Delta(2)$, refer to the unit line segment and unit square respectively.

For the [three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional) case, the mean line segment length of a [unit cube](wikipedia://en/407763) is also known as **Robbins constant**, named after [David P. Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins). This constant has a closed form,[^o17]

$$\Delta(3) = \frac{4+17\sqrt2-6\sqrt3-7\pi}{105} + \frac{\ln(1+\sqrt2)}{5} + \frac{2\ln(2+\sqrt3)}{5}.$$

Its numerical value is approximately $0.661707182...$

Andersson et al. (1976) showed that $\Delta(n)$ satisfies the bounds[^o18]

$$\tfrac{1}{3} n^{1/2} \le \Delta(n) \le (\tfrac{1}{6} n)^{1/2} \sqrt{\frac{1}{3}\left[1 + 2\left(1 - \frac{3}{5n}\right)^{1/2}\right]}.$$

Choosing points from two different faces of the unit cube also gives a result with a closed form, given by,[^o13]

$$\frac{4 + 17\sqrt{2} - 6\sqrt{3} - 7\pi}{75} + \frac{7\ln{(1+\sqrt{2})}}{25} + \frac{14\ln{(2+\sqrt{3})}}{25}.$$

:::

### Circle and sphere {#Circle_and_sphere}

The average chord length between points on the circumference of a circle of radius *r* is

$$\frac{4}{\pi} r \approx 1.273239544\ldots r$$

And picking points on the surface of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) with radius *r* is

$$\frac{4}{3} r$$

### Disks {#Disks}

The average distance between points inside a disk of radius *r* is

$$\frac{128}{45\pi}r \approx 0.905414787\ldots r.$$

The values for a half disk and quarter disk are also known.

For a half disk of radius 1:

$$\frac{64}{135}\frac{12\pi-23}{\pi^2} \approx 0.706053409\ldots$$

For a quarter disk of radius 1:

$$\frac{32}{135\pi^2}(6\ln{(2\sqrt{2}-2)}-94\sqrt{2}+48\pi+3) \approx 0.473877262\ldots$$

### Balls {#Balls}

For a three-dimensional [ball](wikipedia://en/160556), this is

$$\frac{36}{35}r \approx 1.028571428\ldots r.$$

More generally, the mean line segment length of an [*n*-ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-ball) is[^o4]

$$\frac{2n}{2n+1}\beta_n r$$

where $\beta_{n}$ depends on the parity of $n$,

$$\beta_n = \begin{cases}\dfrac{2^{3n+1} \, (n/2)!^2 \, n!}{(n+1)\, (2n)! \, \pi} & (\text{for even } n)\\ \dfrac{2^{n+1}\, n!^3}{(n+1)\, ((n-1)/2)!^2 \, (2n)!} & (\text{for odd } n)\end{cases}$$

### Polyhedra {#Polyhedra}

Beck (2023) found that mean distance between two random points inside any given [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) can be expressed in [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412). The same is true for all [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the distance.[^o27] He also found closed-form expressions for the mean line segment lengths of all regular polyhedra.

The average distance between two points in a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521) with unit volume is

$$\sqrt[3]3\left(\frac{\sqrt2}7-\frac{37\pi}{315}+\frac4{15}\arctan\sqrt2+\frac{113\ln3}{210\sqrt2}\right)\approx0.72946242\ldots$$

The average distance between two points in a [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458) with unit volume is

$$\sqrt[3]{\frac34}\left(\frac4{105}+\frac{13\sqrt2}{105}-\frac{4\pi}{45}+\frac{109\ln3}{630\sqrt2}+\frac{16\operatorname{arccot}\sqrt2}{315}+\frac{158\operatorname{arccoth}\sqrt2}{315}\sqrt2\right)\approx0.65853073\ldots$$

The average distance between two points in a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) with unit volume is approximately 0.64131248551.

The average distance between two points in a [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) with unit volume is approximately 0.65853073.

:::

## General bounds {#General_bounds}

Burgstaller and Pillichshammer (2009) showed that for a [compact subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_subset) of the *n*-dimensional Euclidean space with diameter 1, its mean line segment length *L* satisfies[^o4]

$$L \le \sqrt{\frac{2n}{n+1}} \frac{2^{n-2} \Gamma(n/2)^2}{\Gamma(n - 1/2) \sqrt{\pi}}$$

where $\Gamma$ denotes the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). For *n* = 2, a stronger bound exists.

$$L \le \frac{229}{800} + \frac{44}{75}\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}} + \frac{19}{480}\sqrt{5} = 0.678442\ldots$$
