# The Geometry of the Octonions

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="The Geometry of the Octonions"}

***The Geometry of the Octonions*** is a mathematics book on the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436), a system of numbers generalizing the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), presenting its material at a level suitable for undergraduate mathematics students. It was written by [Tevian Dray](wikipedia://en/22029566) and [Corinne Manogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinne_Manogue), and published in 2015 by [World Scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Scientific). The Basic Library List Committee of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) has suggested its inclusion in undergraduate mathematics libraries.[^o3]

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

The book is subdivided into three parts, with the second part being the most significant. Its contents combine both a survey of past work in this area, and much of its authors' own researches.

The first part explains the [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609), which constructs the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) from the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) from the complex numbers, and the octonions from the quaternions. Related algebras are also discussed, including the [sedenions](wikipedia://en/51434) (a 16-dimensional real algebra formed in the same way by taking one more step past the octonions) and the split real [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) [composition algebras](wikipedia://en/3861635) (also called Hurwitz algebras). A particular focus here is on interpreting the multiplication operation of these algebras in a geometric way. Reviewer Danail Brezov notes with disappointment that [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305), although very relevant to this material, are not covered.

The second part of the book uses the octonions and the other [division algebras](wikipedia://en/26549625) associated with it to provide concrete descriptions of the [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) of geometric symmetries. These include [rotation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_group), [spin groups](wikipedia://en/411231), [symplectic groups](wikipedia://en/173977), and the [exceptional Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exceptional_Lie_groups), which the book interprets as octonionic variants of classical Lie groups.

The third part applies the octonions in geometric constructions including the [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384) and its generalizations, the [Cayley plane](wikipedia://en/9410951), and the [E<sub>8</sub> lattice](wikipedia://en/1800359). It also connects them to problems in physics involving the four-dimensional [Dirac equation](wikipedia://en/39407), the [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) of relativistic [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion), [spinors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinors), and the formulation of quantum mechanics using [Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371). It also includes material on octonionic [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and concludes with a chapter on the [Freudenthal magic square](wikipedia://en/13796065) and related constructions.

## Audience and reception {#Audience_and_reception}

Although presented at an undergraduate level, *The Geometry of the Octonions* is not a textbook: its material is likely too specialized for an undergraduate course, and it lacks exercises or similar material that would be needed to use it as a textbook. Readers should be familiar with [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), and some experience with [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) would also be helpful. The later chapters on applications in physics are heavier going, and require familiarity with quantum mechanics.

The book avoids a proof-heavy formal style of mathematical writing, so much so that reviewer Danail Brezov writes that at points it "seems to lack mathematical rigor".

## Related reading {#Related_reading}

Multiple reviewers suggest that this work would make a good introduction to the octonions, as a stepping stone to the more advanced material presented in other works on the same topic. Their suggestions include the following:

- Baez, John C. (2002). "The octonions". *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society*. 39 (2). p. 145–205. doi:10.1090/S0273-0979-01-00934-X. mr:1886087
- Conway, John H.; Smith, Derek A. (2003). "On Quaternions and Octonions: Their Geometry, Arithmetic, and Symmetry". A K Peters. isbn:1-56881-134-9. mr:1957212
- Kantor, I. L.; Solodovnikov, A. S. (1989). "Hypercomplex Numbers: An Elementary Introduction to Algebras". Springer-Verlag. doi:10.1007/978-1-4612-3650-4. isbn:0-387-96980-2. mr:996029
- Salzmann, Helmut; Betten, Dieter; Grundhöfer, Theo; Hähl, Hermann; Löwen, Rainer; Stroppel, Markus. (1995). "Compact Projective Planes: With an Introduction to Octonion Geometry". 21. Walter de Gruyter & Co., Berlin. doi:10.1515/9783110876833. isbn:3-11-011480-1. mr:1384300
- Springer, Tonny A.; Veldkamp, Ferdinand D. (2000). "Octonions, Jordan Algebras and Exceptional Groups". Springer-Verlag. doi:10.1007/978-3-662-12622-6. isbn:3-540-66337-1. mr:1763974
- Ward, J. P. (1997). "Quaternions and Cayley Numbers: Algebra and Applications". 403. Kluwer Academic Publishers. doi:10.1007/978-94-011-5768-1. isbn:0-7923-4513-4. mr:1458894
