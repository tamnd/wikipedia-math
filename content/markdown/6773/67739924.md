# Lower envelope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lower envelope"}

In mathematics, the **lower envelope** or **pointwise minimum** of a finite set of functions is the [pointwise](wikipedia://en/4786797) minimum of the functions, the function whose value at every point is the minimum of the values of the functions in the given set. The concept of a lower envelope can also be extended to [partial functions](wikipedia://en/23577) by taking the minimum only among functions that have values at the point. The **upper envelope** or **pointwise maximum** is defined symmetrically. For an infinite set of functions, the same notions may be defined using the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) in place of the minimum, and the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) in place of the maximum.

:::

For continuous functions from a given class, the lower or upper envelope is a [piecewise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise) function whose pieces are from the same class. For functions of a single real variable whose graphs have a bounded number of intersection points, the complexity of the lower or upper envelope can be bounded using [Davenport–Schinzel sequences](wikipedia://en/20232529), and these envelopes can be computed efficiently by a [divide-and-conquer algorithm](wikipedia://en/201154) that computes and then merges the envelopes of subsets of the functions.

For [convex functions](wikipedia://en/245568) or [quasiconvex functions](wikipedia://en/5781226), the upper envelope is again convex or quasiconvex. The lower envelope is not, but can be replaced by the [lower convex envelope](wikipedia://en/3165509) to obtain an operation analogous to the lower envelope that maintains convexity. The upper and lower envelopes of [Lipschitz functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function) preserve the property of being Lipschitz. However, the lower and upper envelope operations do not necessarily preserve the property of being a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122).[^n1]
