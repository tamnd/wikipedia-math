# Numberphile

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Numberphile"}

***Numberphile*** is an [educational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_entertainment) [YouTube channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_channel) featuring videos that explore topics from a variety of [fields of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_of_mathematics).[^o3][^o4] In the early days of the channel, each video focused on a specific number, but the channel has since expanded its scope,[^o5] featuring videos on more advanced mathematical concepts such as [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953), the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125)[^o6] and [Kruskal's tree theorem](wikipedia://en/3606300).[^o7] The videos are produced by [Brady Haran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_Haran), a former [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) video journalist and creator of [Periodic Videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_Videos), Sixty Symbols, and several other YouTube channels.[^o8] Videos on the channel feature several university professors, maths communicators and famous mathematicians.[^o9][^o10]

:::

In 2018, Haran released a [spin-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-off_%28media%29) audio [podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast) titled *The Numberphile Podcast*.[^o11]

## YouTube channel {#YouTube_channel}

The *Numberphile* YouTube channel was started on 15 September 2011. Most videos consist of Haran interviewing an expert on a number, [mathematical theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_theorem) or other mathematical concept.[^o13] The expert usually draws out their explanation on a large piece of [brown paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraft_paper) and attempts to make the concepts understandable to the average, non-mathematician viewer.[^o14] It is supported by the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI) and [Math for America](wikipedia://en/2182307).[^o15][^o16] Haran also runs the "Numberphile2" channel, which includes extra footage and further detail than the main channel.[^o17]

### Reception {#Reception}

Numberphile consistently rates among the top YouTube channels in math and education.[^o19][^o20][^o21][^o22] The channel was nominated for a [Shorty Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorty_Award) in Education in 2016.[^o23] *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* said that, "at Numberphile, mathematicians discourse, enthusiastically and winningly, on numbers", and *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* described the channel as "insanely popular".[^o24][^o25] *[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times)* said, "The mathematical stars of social media, such as James Grime and [Matt Parker](wikipedia://en/47409016), entertain legions of fans with striking videos demonstrating how powerful and playful maths can be."[^o26]

*[New Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Scientist)* listed Numberphile as one of the top ten science channels on YouTube in 2019.[^o27]

In 2024, Brady Haran was awarded the [Christopher Zeeman Medal](wikipedia://en/871415#The_Zeeman_Medal) recognising excellence in the communication of mathematics for his work, including *Numberphile*.[^o28]
