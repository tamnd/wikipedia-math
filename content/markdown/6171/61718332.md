# Joanne Elliott

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joanne Elliott"}

**Joanne Elliott** (December 5, 1925 – March 5, 2023) was an American mathematician who specialized in [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), who was described as a "disciple" of her co-author, [probability theorist](wikipedia://en/23542) [William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644). She was also a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of mathematics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Elliott was born on December 5, 1925, in [Providence, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island), and graduated from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1947. She completed her Ph.D. at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1950, as part of a handful of "outstanding graduate students" working at Cornell in the post-World-War-II decade. Her dissertation, *On Some Singular Integral Equations of the Cauchy Type*, was supervised by [Harry Pollard](wikipedia://en/34196465).

## Career and later life {#Career_and_later_life}

After a year at [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College), she worked at [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College) as an assistant professor from 1952 until 1956, when she moved to [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College). In 1958, she was the supervisor of [Doris Stockton](wikipedia://en/61718116)'s doctorate at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). In 1961, as an associate professor at Barnard, she was funded by the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) to visit the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) for postdoctoral research. She also worked at the [Institute for Defense Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses) in Princeton in the early 1960s.

She came to [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in 1964, at a time when Rutgers had a much higher number of female faculty than many mathematics departments then or later. Among her graduate students at Rutgers was [Edward R. Dougherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_R._Dougherty), later a distinguished professor of electrical engineering at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University). She chaired the Rutgers mathematics department from 1974 to 1977. Elliott retired from Rutgers in 1991, in a year in which the university was cutting costs by offering early retirement to its employees.

Elliott died in [Titusville, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titusville,_New_Jersey) on March 5, 2023, at the age of 97.
