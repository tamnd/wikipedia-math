# Doris Stockton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Doris Stockton"}

**Doris G. Skillman Stockton** (1924–2018) was an American mathematician specializing in [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), and known for her many mathematics textbooks. For many years she was a professor of mathematics at the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Stockton entered the [New Jersey College for Women at Rutgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglass_Residential_College) in 1941. Initially planning to study dramatic arts, she was persuaded to switch to mathematics and physics so that she could more directly contribute to the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) efforts.

She completed her Ph.D. at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1958, with the dissertation *Singular Parabolic Partial Differential Equations with Time Dependent Coefficients*, supervised by [Joanne Elliott](wikipedia://en/61718332).

She joined the faculty at the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) but, soon after, moved to the University of Massachusetts. After working for 52 years on the faculty of the University of Massachusetts, Stockton retired in 2006.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Stockton was born on February 9, 1924, in [New Brunswick, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick,_New_Jersey). She was married to Frederick D. Stockton (1920–2015), an associate professor of [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) at the University of Massachusetts. She died on December 13, 2018, in [Avon, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avon,_Connecticut).

## Books {#Books}

Stockton was the author of 11 textbooks and workbooks, including:

- *Elements of Mathematics* (2nd ed., with Helen Murray Roberts, 1956)
- *Essential Mathematics* (Scott & Foresman, 1972)
- *Essential Algebra* (1973)
- *Essential Algebra with Functions* (1973)
- *Essential Algebra and Trigonometry* (Houghton Mifflin, 1978)
- *Essential Precalculus* (Houghton Mifflin, 1978)
- *Essential College Algebra* (Houghton Mifflin, 1979)
