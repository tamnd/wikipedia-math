# Recurrent point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Recurrent point"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **recurrent point** for a function *f* is a point that is in its own [limit set](wikipedia://en/5492198) by *f*. Any [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood) containing the recurrent point will also contain (a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) number of) iterates of it as well.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X$ be a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) and $f\colon X\to X$ a function. A point $x\in X$ is said to be recurrent (for $f$) if $x\in \omega(x)$, *i.e.* if $x$ belongs to its $\omega$-[limit set](wikipedia://en/5492198). This means that for each [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood) $U$ of $x$ and ${\forall N }$ there exists $n>N$ such that $f^n(x)\in U$.[^o4][^o5]

The set of recurrent points of $f$ is often denoted $R(f)$ and is called the **recurrent set** of $f$. Its closure is called the **Birkhoff center** of $f$,[^o6] and appears in the work of [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906) on [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).[^o7][^o8]

Every recurrent point is a [nonwandering point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonwandering_point),[^o4] hence if $f$ is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) and $X$ is [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness), then $R(f)$ is an [invariant subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_set) of the non-wandering set of $f$ (and may be a [proper subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_subset)) and it's already closed.

:::
