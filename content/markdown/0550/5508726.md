# Expansive homeomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Expansive homeomorphism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the notion of expansivity formalizes the notion of points moving away from one another under the action of an [iterated function](wikipedia://en/1448702). The idea of expansivity is fairly [rigid](wikipedia://en/1458875), as the definition of positive expansivity, below, as well as the [Schwarz–Ahlfors–Pick theorem](wikipedia://en/1487910) demonstrate.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If $(X,d)$ is a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) $f\colon X\to X$ is said to be **expansive** if there is a constant

$$\varepsilon_0>0,$$

called the **expansivity constant**, such that for every pair of points $x\neq y$ in $X$ there is an integer $n$ such that

$$d(f^n(x),f^n(y))\geq\varepsilon_0.$$

Note that in this definition, $n$ can be positive or negative, and so $f$ may be expansive in the forward or backward directions.

The space $X$ is often assumed to be [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), since under that assumption expansivity is a [topological property](wikipedia://en/1337282); i.e. if $d'$ is any other metric generating the same topology as $d$, and if $f$ is expansive in $(X,d)$, then $f$ is expansive in $(X,d')$ (possibly with a different expansivity constant).

If

$$f\colon X\to X$$

is a continuous map, we say that $X$ is **positively expansive** (or **forward expansive**) if there is a

$$\varepsilon_0$$

such that, for any $x\neq y$ in $X$, there is an $n\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $d(f^n(x),f^n(y))\geq \varepsilon_0$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Theorem of uniform expansivity"}

## Theorem of uniform expansivity {#Theorem_of_uniform_expansivity}

Given *f* an expansive homeomorphism of a compact metric space, the theorem of uniform expansivity states that for every $\epsilon>0$ and $\delta>0$ there is an $N>0$ such that for each pair $x,y$ of points of $X$ such that $d(x,y)>\epsilon$, there is an $n\in \mathbb{Z}$ with $\vert n\vert\leq N$ such that

$$d(f^n(x),f^n(y)) > c-\delta,$$

where $c$ is the expansivity constant of $f$ ([proof](http://planetmath.org/?op=getobj&amp;from=objects&amp;id=4678)).

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

Positive expansivity is much stronger than expansivity. In fact, one can prove that if $X$ is compact and $f$ is a positively expansive homeomorphism, then $X$ is finite ([proof](http://planetmath.org/?op=getobj&amp;from=objects&amp;id=4677)).
