# Charles Roy Henderson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Roy Henderson"}

**Charles Roy Henderson** (1911-04-01 – 1989) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and a pioneer in [animal breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_breeding) — the application of quantitative methods for the genetic evaluation of domestic livestock. This is critically important because it allows farmers and geneticists to predict whether a crop or animal will have a desired trait, and to what extent the trait will be expressed. He developed [mixed model](wikipedia://en/5431921) equations to obtain [best linear unbiased predictions](wikipedia://en/15915709) of breeding values and, in general, any random effect. He invented three methods for the estimation of [variance components](wikipedia://en/6709643) in unbalanced settings of mixed models, and invented a method for constructing the inverse of [Wright's](wikipedia://en/1700593) numerator [relationship](wikipedia://en/640846) matrix based on a simple list of pedigree information. He, with his Ph.D. student [Shayle R. Searle](wikipedia://en/18409802), greatly extended the use of matrix notation in statistics. His methods are widely used by the domestic livestock industry throughout the world and are a cornerstone of linear model theory.

:::

Henderson obtained his [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.), [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc.)(nutrition) and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.)(breeding) degrees at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University), where he was a student of Professor L. N. Hazel. Henderson joined the faculty of the Department of Animal Science at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1948 and headed the Animal Breeding division for nearly 30 years until he retired in 1976. After retiring from Cornell, he was a visiting professor at the [University of Guelph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Guelph) and [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) until his death. He completed his book in 1984 at the [University of Guelph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Guelph).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- 1955, Senior [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright) Research Scholar (New Zealand)
- 1964, Borden Award, [American Dairy Science Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dairy_Science_Association)
- 1964, Animal Breeding and Genetics Award, [American Society of Animal Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Animal_Science)
- 1969, Fellow, [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988)
- 1971, Morrison Award, American Society of Animal Science
- 1977, National Association of Animal Breeders Award, American Dairy Science Association
- 1980, Member of the [Massey University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_University) Wellington
- 1981, Visiting professor at the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis)
- 1981, [Hermann-von-Nathusius-Medaille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann-von-Nathusius-Medaille) in Gold of the German Society for Animal Production (DGfZ) [^o5]
- 1981, Fellow, American Society of Animal Science
- 1982, Jay L. Lush Animal Breeding and Genetics Award, American Dairy Science Association
- 1984, Henry A. Wallace Award for Service to Agriculture, Iowa State University
- 1985, Elected to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences)
