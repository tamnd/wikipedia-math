# Theorem of Bertini

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theorem of Bertini"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **theorem of Bertini** is an existence and genericity theorem for [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) connected [hyperplane sections](wikipedia://en/3459767) for smooth [projective varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_varieties) over [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018), introduced by [Eugenio Bertini](wikipedia://en/33285389). This is the simplest and broadest of the "Bertini theorems" applying to a [linear system of divisors](wikipedia://en/1186886); simplest because there is no restriction on the [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_field) of the underlying field, while the extensions require characteristic 0.[^o3]

:::

## Statement for hyperplane sections of smooth varieties {#Statement_for_hyperplane_sections_of_smooth_varieties}

*Image: Saddle pt.jpg.* A smooth surface with one singular and two smooth plane sections

Let *X* be a smooth [quasi-projective variety](wikipedia://en/1321785) over an algebraically closed field, embedded in a [projective space](wikipedia://en/31453337) $\mathbf P^n$. Let $|H|$ denote the [complete system](wikipedia://en/1186886) of hyperplane divisors in $\mathbf P^n$. Recall that it is the [dual space](wikipedia://en/539925#Higher_dimensional_duality) $(\mathbf P^n)^{\star}$ of $\mathbf P^n$ and is isomorphic to $\mathbf P^n$.

The theorem of Bertini states that the set of hyperplanes not containing *X* and with smooth intersection with *X* contains an open dense subset of the total system of divisors $|H|$. The set itself is open if *X* is projective. If $\dim(X) \ge 2$, then these intersections (called hyperplane sections of *X*) are connected, hence irreducible.

The theorem hence asserts that a *general* hyperplane section not equal to *X* is smooth, that is: the property of smoothness is generic.

Over an arbitrary field *k*, there is a dense open subset of the dual space $(\mathbf P^n)^{\star}$ whose [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) define smooth hyperplane sections of *X*. When *k* is infinite, this open subset then has infinitely many rational points and there are infinitely many smooth hyperplane sections in *X*.

Over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), the above open subset may not contain rational points and in general there is no hyperplanes with smooth intersection with *X*. However, if we take [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101) of sufficiently big degrees, then the theorem of Bertini holds.[^o5]

## Outline of a proof {#Outline_of_a_proof}

We consider the subfibration of the product variety $X \times |H|$ with fiber above $x\in X$ the linear system of hyperplanes that intersect *X* non-[transversally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversality_%28mathematics%29) at *x*.

The rank of the fibration in the product is one less than the codimension of $X \subset \mathbf P^n$, so that the total space has lesser dimension than $n$ and so its projection is contained in a divisor of the complete system $|H|$.

## General statement {#General_statement}

Over any infinite field $k$ of characteristic 0, if *X* is a smooth quasi-projective $k$-variety, a general member of a [linear system of divisors](wikipedia://en/1186886) on *X* is smooth away from the [base locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_locus) of the system. For clarification, this means that given a linear system $f:X\rightarrow \mathbf{P}^n$, the preimage $f^{-1}(H)$ of a hyperplane *H* is smooth -- outside the base locus of *f* -- for all hyperplanes *H* in some dense open subset of the dual projective space $(\mathbf{P}^n)^\star$. This theorem also holds in characteristic p>0 when the linear system *f* is unramified. [^o8]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The theorem of Bertini has been generalized in various ways. For example, a result due to [Steven Kleiman](wikipedia://en/16169105) asserts the following (cf. [Kleiman's theorem](wikipedia://en/57371589)): for a connected [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) *G*, and any [homogeneous *G*-variety](wikipedia://en/39305653) *X*, and two varieties *Y* and *Z* mapping to *X*, let *Y*<sup>σ</sup> be the variety obtained by letting σ ∈ *G* act on *Y*. Then, there is an open dense subscheme *H* of *G* such that for σ ∈ *H*, $Y^\sigma \times_X Z$ is either empty or purely of the (expected) [dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) dim *Y* + dim *Z* − dim *X*. If, in addition, *Y* and *Z* are [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) and the base field has characteristic zero, then *H* may be taken such that $Y^\sigma \times_X Z$ is smooth for all $\sigma \in H$, as well. The above theorem of Bertini is the special case where $X = \mathbb P^n$ is expressed as the quotient of SL<sub>*n*</sub> by the [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of upper triangular matrices, *Z* is a subvariety and *Y* is a hyperplane.[^o11]

The theorem of Bertini has also been generalized to [discrete valuation domains](wikipedia://en/848633) or finite fields, or for [étale coverings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_covering) of *X*.

The theorem is often used for induction steps.
