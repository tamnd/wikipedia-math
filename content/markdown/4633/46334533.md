# James B. Carrell

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James B. Carrell"}

**James B. Carrell** (born 1940) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_people) and [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who is currently an emeritus professor of mathematics at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada.[^o3] His areas of research are [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681), [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_groups) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

He obtained his Ph.D. at the University of Washington (Seattle) under the supervision of [Allendoefer](wikipedia://en/3466784).[^o4] In 1971, together with [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084), he received the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for the article *Invariant theory, old and new*.[^o5][^o6]

He proved theorems in [Schubert calculus](wikipedia://en/3061740) about singularities of [Schubert varieties](wikipedia://en/10469987). The Carrell–Liebermann theorem on the zero set of a [holomorphic vector field](wikipedia://en/4794668) is used in [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry).

He is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7]
