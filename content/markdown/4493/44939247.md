# Gunther Schmidt

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gunther Schmidt"}

**Gunther Schmidt** (born 1939, [Rüdersdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rüdersdorf)) is a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works also in [informatics](wikipedia://en/23997153).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Schmidt began studying Mathematics in 1957 at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). His academic teachers were in particular Kurt Reidemeister, Wilhelm Klingenberg and Karl Stein. In 1960, he transferred to the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) (LMU) where he studied [functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_of_several_complex_variables) with [Karl Stein](wikipedia://en/7218529). Schmidt wrote a thesis on [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of such functions.

In 1962, Schmidt began work at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) with students of Robert Sauer, in the beginning in labs and tutorials, later in mentoring and administration. Schmidt's interests turned toward programming when he collaborated with Hans Langmaack on [rewriting](wikipedia://en/415847) and the [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138) in 1969. [Friedrich L. Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_L._Bauer) and [Klaus Samelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Samelson) were establishing [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineering) at the university and Schmidt joined their group in 1974. In 1977, he submitted his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) "Programs as partial graphs".[^o4]

He became a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 1980. Shortly after that, he was appointed to hold the chair of the late [Klaus Samelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Samelson) for one and a half years. From 1988 until his retirement in 2004, he held a professorship at the Faculty for Computer Science of the [University of the Bundeswehr Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Bundeswehr_Munich). He was a classroom instructor for beginners courses as well as special courses in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), semantics of [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015), construction of [compilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler), and [algorithmic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_language). Working with Thomas Ströhlein, he authored a [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) on [relations](wikipedia://en/3931) and [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), published in German in 1989 and English in 1993. In 2011, the volume on Relational Mathematics followed in the [Encyclopedia of Mathematics and Its Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Mathematics_and_Its_Applications) of Cambridge University Press.

In 2001, he became involved in a large project (17 nations) with the [European Cooperation in Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Cooperation_in_Science_and_Technology):[^o5] Schmidt was chairman of project COST 274 TARSKI (Theory and Application of Relational Structures as Knowledge Instruments).[^o6]

In 2014, a [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) was organized to celebrate his 75th year.[^o7]

The [calculus of relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_relations) had a relatively low profile among mathematical topics in the twentieth century, but Schmidt and others have raised that profile. The [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) of binary relations can be organized by grouping through [closure](wikipedia://en/293802). In 2018, Schmidt and Michael Winter published *Relational Topology* which reviews classical [mathematical structures](wikipedia://en/1809181), such as [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948) and [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), through the lens of calculus of relations.

## Work {#Work}

In 1981, he participated in the [International Summer School Marktoberdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Summer_School_Marktoberdorf), and edited the lecture notes *Theoretical Foundations of Programming Methodology* with [Manfred Broy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Broy).[^o9]

Gunther Schmidt is mainly known for his work on Relational Mathematics; he was co-founder of the [RAMiCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAMiCS) conference series in 1994.

His textbooks on calculus of relations exhibit applications and potential of [algebraic logic](wikipedia://en/7870034).
