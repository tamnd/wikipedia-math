# Heavy traffic approximation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heavy traffic approximation"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), a **heavy traffic approximation** (sometimes called **heavy traffic limit theorem**[^o2] or **diffusion approximation**) involves the matching of a queueing model with a [diffusion process](wikipedia://en/1667059) under some [limiting](wikipedia://en/26551602) conditions on the model's parameters. The first such result was published by [John Kingman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kingman), who showed that when the utilisation parameter of an [M/M/1 queue](wikipedia://en/14032611) is near 1, a scaled version of the queue length process can be accurately approximated by a [reflected Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/35046943).[^o3]

:::

## Heavy traffic condition {#Heavy_traffic_condition}

Heavy traffic approximations are typically stated for the process *X*(*t*) describing the number of customers in the system at time *t*. They are arrived at by considering the model under the limiting values of some model parameters and therefore for the result to be finite the model must be rescaled by a factor *n*, denoted[^o5]

$$\hat X_n(t) = \frac{X(nt) - \mathbb E(X(nt))}{\sqrt{n}}$$

and the limit of this process is considered as *n* → ∞.

There are three classes of regime under which such approximations are generally considered.

1. The number of servers is fixed and the traffic intensity (utilization) is increased to 1 (from below). The queue length approximation is a [reflected Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/35046943).[^o6][^o7][^o8]
1. Traffic intensity is fixed and the number of servers and arrival rate are increased to infinity. Here the queue length limit converges to the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462).[^o9][^o10][^o11]
1. A quantity *β* is fixed where

$$\beta = (1-\rho)\sqrt{s}$$

with *ρ* representing the traffic intensity and *s* the number of servers. Traffic intensity and the number of servers are increased to infinity and the limiting process is a hybrid of the above results. This case, first published by Halfin and Whitt is often known as the Halfin–Whitt regime[^o2][^o12][^o13] or quality-and-efficiency-driven (QED) regime.[^o14]

## Results for a G/G/1 queue {#Results_for_a_G/G/1_queue}

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem 1"}

**Theorem 1.** [^o17] Consider a sequence of [G/G/1 queues](wikipedia://en/38677129) indexed by $j$.

For queue $j$ let $T_j$ denote the random inter-arrival time, $S_j$ denote the random service time; let $\rho_j=\frac{\lambda_j}{\mu_j}$ denote the traffic intensity with $\frac{1}{\lambda_j}=E(T_j)$ and $\frac{1}{\mu_j}=E(S_j)$; let $W_{q,j}$ denote the waiting time in queue for a customer in steady state; Let $\alpha_j=-E[S_j-T_j]$ and $\beta_j^2=\operatorname{var}[S_j-T_j];$

Suppose that $T_j\xrightarrow{d} T$, $S_j\xrightarrow{d} S$, and $\rho_j \rightarrow 1$. then

$$\frac{2\alpha_j}{\beta_j^2}W_{q,j}\xrightarrow{d} \exp(1)$$

provided that:

(a) $\operatorname{Var}[S-T]>0$

(b) for some $\delta > 0$, $E[S_j^{2+\delta}]$ and $E[T_j^{2+\delta}]$ are both less than some constant $C$ for all $j$.

:::

## Heuristic argument {#Heuristic_argument}

- **Waiting time in queue**

Let $U^{(n)}=S^{(n)}-T^{(n)}$ be the difference between the nth service time and the nth inter-arrival time; Let $W_q^{(n)}$ be the waiting time in queue of the nth customer;

Then by definition:

$$W_q^{(n)}=\max(W_q^{(n-1)}+U^{(n-1)},0)$$

After recursive calculation, we have:

$$W_q^{(n)}=\max(U^{(1)}+\cdots+U^{(n-1)},U^{(2)}+\cdots+U^{(n-1)}, \ldots,U^{(n-1)},0)$$

- **Random walk**

Let $P^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}U^{(n-i)}$, with $U^{(i)}$ are i.i.d; Define $\alpha=-E[U^{(i)}]$ and $\beta^2=\operatorname{var}[U^{(i)}]$;

Then we have

$$E[P^{(k)}]=-k\alpha$$

$$\operatorname{var}[P^{(k)}]=k\beta^2$$

$$W_q^{(n)}=\max_{n-1\geq k \geq 0}P^{(k)};$$

we get $W_q^{(\infty)}=\sup_{k \geq 0} P^{(k)}$ by taking limit over $n$.

Thus the waiting time in queue of the nth customer $W_q^{(n)}$ is the supremum of a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) with a negative drift.

- **Brownian motion approximation**

According to [Donsker's theorem](wikipedia://en/3828419), a [Random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) can be approximated by a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) when the jump sizes approach 0 and the times between the jump approach 0.

We have $P^{(0)}=0$ and $P^{(k)}$ has independent and [stationary increments](wikipedia://en/22497977). When the traffic intensity $\rho$ approaches 1 and $k$ tends to $\infty$, we have $P^{(t)} \ \sim\ \mathbb{N}(-\alpha t, \beta^2 t )$ after replaced $k$ with continuous value $t$ according to [functional central limit theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_central_limit_theorem).[^o19] Thus the waiting time in queue of the $n$th customer can be approximated by the supremum of a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) with a negative drift.

- **Supremum of Brownian motion**

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem 2"}

**Theorem 2.**[^o21] Let $X$ be a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) with drift $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$ starting at the origin, and let $M_t = \sup_{0\leq s\leq t} X(s)$

if $\mu \leq 0$

$$\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty}P(M_t > x)=\exp(2\mu x/\sigma^2 ), x \geq 0;$$

otherwise

$$\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} P(M_t\geq x)=1, x \geq 0.$$

:::

## Conclusion {#Conclusion}

$W_q^{(\infty)}\thicksim \exp\left(\frac{2\alpha}{\beta^2}\right)$ under heavy traffic condition

Thus, the heavy traffic limit theorem (Theorem 1) is heuristically argued. Formal proofs usually follow a different approach which involve [characteristic functions](wikipedia://en/3229401).[^o6][^o23]

::: {#o25 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider an [M/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/33378064) with arrival rate $\lambda$, the mean of the service time $E[S]=\frac{1}{\mu}$, and the variance of the service time $\operatorname{var}[S]=\sigma_{B}^2$. What is average waiting time in queue in the [steady state](wikipedia://en/1326107)?

The exact average waiting time in queue in [steady state](wikipedia://en/1326107) is given by:

$$W_q=\frac{\rho^2+\lambda^2\sigma_B^2}{2\lambda(1-\rho)}$$

The corresponding heavy traffic approximation:

$$W_q^{(H)}=\frac{\lambda(\frac{1}{\lambda^2}+\sigma_B^2)}{2(1-\rho)}.$$

The relative error of the heavy traffic approximation:

$$\frac{W_q^{(H)}-W_q}{W_q}=\frac{1-\rho^2}{\rho^2+\lambda^2\sigma_B^2}$$

Thus when $\rho\rightarrow 1$, we have :

$$\frac{W_q^{(H)}-W_q}{W_q} \rightarrow 0.$$

:::
