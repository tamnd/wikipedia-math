# Morse–Smale system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morse–Smale system"}

In [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632), an area of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210), a **Morse–Smale system** is a smooth dynamical system whose [non-wandering set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-wandering_set) consists of finitely many [hyperbolic equilibrium points](wikipedia://en/3948604) and [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/2658073) [periodic orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit) and satisfying a transversality condition on the [stable](wikipedia://en/5466649) and unstable [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). Morse–Smale systems are [structurally stable](wikipedia://en/2515807) and form one of the simplest and best studied classes of smooth dynamical systems. They are named after [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523), the creator of the [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560), and [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294), who emphasized their importance for smooth dynamics and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider a smooth and [complete vector field](wikipedia://en/62641#Complete_vector_fields) *X* defined on a compact differentiable manifold *M* with dimension *n*. The flow defined by this vector field is a Morse-Smale system if

1. *X* has only a finite number of singular points (i.e. equilibrium points of the flow), and all of them are [hyperbolic equilibrium points](wikipedia://en/3948604).
1. *X* has only a finite number of periodic orbits, and all of them are [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/2658073) [periodic orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit).
1. The [limit sets](wikipedia://en/5492198) of all orbits of X tends to a singular point or a periodic orbit.
1. The stable and unstable manifolds of the singular points and periodic orbits intersect transversely. In other words, if $x_i$ is a singular point (or periodic orbit) and $W_s(x_i)$ (respectively, $W_u(x_i)$) its stable (respectively, unstable) manifold, then $w \in W_s(x_i) \cap W_u(x_j)$ implies that the corresponding tangent spaces of the stable and unstable manifold satisfy $T_w(W_s(x_i)) + T_w(W_u(x_j)) = T_w(M)$.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: UprightTorusFlowLines.png.* Flow lines on an upright torus: the stable and unstable manifolds of the saddle points do not intersect transversely, so the height function does not satisfy the Morse-Smale condition.

*Image: TiltedTorusFlowLines.png.* Flow lines on a tilted torus: the height function satisfies the Morse-Smale condition.

- Any [Morse function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function) *f* on a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) *M* defines a gradient vector field. If one imposes the condition that the [unstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable_manifold) and [stable](wikipedia://en/5466649) [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) of the [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) intersect transversely, then the gradient vector field and the corresponding smooth [flow](wikipedia://en/2619735) form a Morse–Smale system. The finite set of [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) of *f* forms the non-wandering set, which consists entirely of fixed points.
- [Gradient-like dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient-like_dynamical_systems) are a particular case of Morse–Smale systems.
- For Morse–Smale systems on the 2D-sphere all equilibrium points and periodical orbits are [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/3948604); there are no [separatrice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separatrix_%28dynamical_systems%29) loops.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- By [Peixoto's theorem](wikipedia://en/3949356), the vector field on a 2D manifold is structurally stable if and only if this field is Morse-Smale.
- Consider a Morse-Smale system defined on compact differentiable manifold *M* with dimension *n*, and let the index of an equilibrium point (or a periodic orbit) be defined as the dimension of its associated unstable manifold. In Morse-Smale systems, the indices of the equilibrium points (and periodic orbits) are related with the topology of *M* by the Morse-Smale inequalities. Precisely, define *m<sub>i</sub>* as the sum of the number of equilibrium points with index *i* and the number of periodic orbits with indices *i* and *i* + 1, and *b<sub>i</sub>* as the *i*-th [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) of *M*. Then the following inequalities are valid:[^o8]

$$\sum_{j=0}^i (-1)^j m_{i-j} \ge \sum_{j=0}^i (-1)^j b_{i-j}, \quad i = 0, \ldots, n$$
