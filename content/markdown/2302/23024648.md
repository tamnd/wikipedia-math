# Spherical lune

*Image: Regular digon in spherical geometry-2.svg.* The two great circles are shown as thin black lines, whereas the *spherical lune* (shown in green) is outlined in thick black lines. This geometry also defines lunes of greater angles: {2}<sub>π-θ</sub>, and {2}<sub>2π-θ</sub>.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical lune"}

In [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026), a **spherical lune** (or **biangle**) is an area on a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) bounded by two half [great circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_circles) which meet at [antipodal points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipodal_points).[^o2] It is an example of a [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728), {2}<sub>θ</sub>, with [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) θ. The word "lune" derives from *[luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_%28goddess%29)*, the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) word for Moon.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Great circles are the largest possible circles (circumferences) of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859); each one divides the surface of the sphere into two equal halves. Two great circles always intersect at two polar opposite points.

Common examples of great circles are lines of [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) (*meridians*) on a sphere, which meet at the north and south poles.

A spherical lune has two planes of symmetry. It can be bisected into two lunes of half the angle, or it can be bisected by an equatorial line into two right [spherical triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle).

### Surface area {#Surface_area}

*Image: Spherical\_lune\_360°.svg.* A full circle lune, {2}<sub>2π</sub>

The [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) of a spherical lune is 2θ *R*<sup>2</sup>, where *R* is the radius of the sphere and θ is the [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) in radians between the two half great circles.

When this angle equals 2π radians (360°) — i.e., when the second half great circle has moved a full circle, and the lune in between covers the sphere as a spherical [monogon](wikipedia://en/5483767) — the area formula for the spherical lune gives 4π*R*<sup>2</sup>, the [surface area of the sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_area_of_the_sphere).

## Examples {#Examples}

A [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192) is a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of the sphere by lunes. A n-gonal regular hosohedron, {2,n} has *n* equal lunes of π/*n* radians. An *n*-hosohedron has [dihedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2922089#Types) D<sub>*n*h</sub>, \[*n*,2\], (\*22*n*) of order 4*n*. Each lune individually has [cyclic symmetry](wikipedia://en/2926084#Types) *C<sub>2v</sub>*, \[2\], (\*22) of order 4.

Each hosohedra can be divided by an [equatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) bisector into two equal [spherical triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle).

## Astronomy {#Astronomy}

*Image: The Moon Luc Viatour.jpg.* The [phases of the moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_the_moon) make spherical lunes perceived as the intersection of a semicircle and semi-ellipse.

The visibly lighted portion of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) visible from the Earth is a spherical lune. The first of the two intersecting great circles is the [terminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_%28solar%29) between the sunlit half of the Moon and the dark half. The second great circle is a terrestrial terminator that separates the half visible from the Earth from the unseen half. The spherical lune is a lighted [crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescent) shape seen from Earth.

## *n*-sphere lunes {#n-sphere_lunes}

*Image: Hypersphere coord.PNG.* [Stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431) of the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792)'s parallels (red), [meridians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_%28geography%29) (blue) and hypermeridians (green). Lunes exist between pairs of blue meridian arcs.

Lunes can be defined on higher dimensional spheres as well.

In 4-dimensions a [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) is a generalized sphere. It can contain regular [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) lunes as {2}<sub>θ,φ</sub>, where θ and φ are two dihedral angles.

For example, a regular [hosotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosotope) {2,p,q} has digon faces, {2}<sub>2π/p,2π/q</sub>, where its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is a spherical [platonic solid](wikipedia://en/23905), {p,q}. Each vertex of {p,q} defines an edge in the hosotope and adjacent pairs of those edges define lune faces. Or more specifically, the regular hosotope {2,4,3}, has 2 vertices, 8 180° arc edges in a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), {4,3}, [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) between the two vertices, 12 lune faces, {2}<sub>π/4,π/3</sub>, between pairs of adjacent edges, and 6 hosohedral cells, {2,p}<sub>π/3</sub>.
