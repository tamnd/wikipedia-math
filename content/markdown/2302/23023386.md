# Spherical segment

*Image: LaoHaiKugelschicht1.png.* A spherical segment

*Image: 01sphere2planes.pdf.* Pair of parallel planes intersecting a sphere forming a spherical segment (i.e., a spherical frustum)

*Image: 02sph.seg.3D.pdf.* Terminology for spherical segments.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical segment"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **spherical segment** is the [solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_%28geometry%29) defined by cutting a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) or a [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) with a pair of [parallel planes](wikipedia://en/664497). It can be thought of as a [spherical cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_cap) with the top truncated, and so it corresponds to a **spherical [frustum](wikipedia://en/89405)**.

:::

The [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) of the *spherical segment* (excluding the bases) is called **spherical zone**.

*Image: Geometric parameters for spherical segment.pdf.* Geometric parameters for spherical segment.

If the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the sphere is called $R$, the radii of the spherical segment bases are $a$ and $b$, and the height of the segment (the distance from one parallel plane to the other) called $h$, then the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of the spherical segment is

$$V = \frac{\pi}{6} h \left(3 a^2 + 3 b^2 + h^2\right).$$

For the special case of the top plane being tangent to the sphere, we have $b = 0$ and the solid reduces to a [spherical cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_cap).[^o2]

The equation above for volume of the spherical segment can be arranged to

$$V = \biggl [ \pi a^2 \left (\frac{h}{2} \biggr ) \right ] + \biggl [ \pi b^2 \left ( \frac{h}{2} \biggr ) \right ] + \biggl [ \frac{4}{3} \pi \left( \frac{h}{2} \right)^3 \biggr ]$$

Thus, the segment volume equals the sum of three volumes: two right circular cylinders one of radius $a$ and the second of radius $b$ (both of height $h/2$) and a sphere of radius $h/2$.

The curved [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) of the spherical zone—which excludes the top and bottom bases—is given by

$$A = 2 \pi R h.$$

Thus the surface area of the segment depends only on the distance between the cutting planes, and not their absolute heights.
