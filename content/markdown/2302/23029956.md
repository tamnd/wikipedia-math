# Homological integration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Homological integration"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) fields of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694), **homological integration** or **geometric integration** is a method for extending the notion of the [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) to [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). Rather than functions or [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519), the integral is defined over [currents](wikipedia://en/3021207) on a manifold.

:::

The theory is "homological" because currents themselves are defined by duality with differential forms. To wit, the space $D^{k}$ of $k$-currents on a manifold $M$ is defined as the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988), in the sense of [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29), of the space of $k$-forms $\Omega^{k}$ on $M$. Thus there is a pairing between $k$-currents $T$ and $k$-forms $\alpha$, denoted here by

$$\langle T, \alpha\rangle.$$

Under this duality pairing, the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782)

$$d : \Omega^{k-1} \to \Omega^k$$

goes over to a [boundary operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_operator)

$$\partial : D^k \to D^{k-1}$$

defined by

$$\langle\partial T,\alpha\rangle = \langle T, d\alpha\rangle$$

for all $\alpha \in \Omega^{k}$. This is a homological rather than [cohomological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) construction.
