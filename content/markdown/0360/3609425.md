# Research ethics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Research ethics"}

Ética na ciência

**Research ethics** is a discipline within the study of [applied ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics). Its scope ranges from general [scientific integrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_integrity) and [misconduct](wikipedia://en/29537) to the treatment of human and animal subjects. The [social responsibilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_responsibilities) of scientists and researchers are not traditionally included and are less well defined.[^o2]

:::

The discipline is most developed in [medical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_research). Beyond the issues of falsification, fabrication, and plagiarism that arise in every scientific field, [research design](wikipedia://en/3345681), [human subject research](wikipedia://en/447842), and [animal testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_testing) are the areas that most often raise ethical questions.

The [list of historic cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_medical_ethics_cases) includes many large-scale violations and crimes against humanity such as [Nazi human experimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_human_experimentation) and the [Tuskegee syphilis experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuskegee_syphilis_experiment) which led to international codes of research ethics. No approach has been universally accepted,[^o8][^o9][^o10] but typically cited codes are the 1947 [Nuremberg Code](wikipedia://en/21881), the 1964 [Declaration of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Helsinki), and the 1978 [Belmont Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Report).

Today, [research ethics committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_ethics_committees), such as those of the [US](wikipedia://en/1983847), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Ethics_Service), and [EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_committee_%28European_Union%29), govern and oversee the responsible conduct of research. One major goal being to reduce [questionable research practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questionable_research_practices).

Research in other fields such as [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences), [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology), or [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) may generate ethical concerns.[^o8][^o9][^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

## History {#History}

The [list of historic cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_medical_ethics_cases) includes many large scale violations and crimes against humanity such as [Nazi human experimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_human_experimentation) and the [Tuskegee syphilis experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuskegee_syphilis_experiment) which led to international codes of research ethics.[^o8][^o9][^o10] [Medical ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_ethics) developed out of centuries of general malpractice and science motivated only by results. Medical ethics in turn led to today's more broad understanding in [bioethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioethics).[^o11]

### Clinical research ethics {#Clinical_research_ethics}

#### Study participant rights {#Study_participant_rights}

Participants in a [clinical trial](wikipedia://en/241717) in [clinical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_research) have rights which they expect to be honored, including:[^o20]

- [informed consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informed_consent)
- [shared decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_decision-making)
- [privacy for research participants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy_for_research_participants)
- [return of results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_results)
- to [withdraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_withdraw)

#### Vulnerable populations {#Vulnerable_populations}

Study participants are entitled to some degree of autonomy in deciding their participation. One measure for safeguarding this right is the use of [informed consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informed_consent) for clinical research.[^o22] Researchers refer to populations with limited autonomy as "vulnerable populations"; these are subjects who may not be able to fairly decide for themselves whether to participate. Examples of vulnerable populations include [incarcerated persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarceration), children, prisoners, soldiers, people under detention, migrants, persons exhibiting [insanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insanity) or any other condition that precludes their autonomy, and to a lesser extent, any population for which there is reason to believe that the research study could seem particularly or unfairly persuasive or misleading. [Ethical problems particularly encumber using children in clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical_problems_using_children_in_clinical_trials).

## Governance {#Governance}

Ethics committee

- In the United Kingdom, the [National Research Ethics Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Ethics_Service) is the responsible [quango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quango) that forms Research Ethic Committees.
- In the United States, the [Institutional review board](wikipedia://en/1983847) is the relevant ethics committee.
- In Canada, there are different committees for different agencies. The committees are the Research Ethics Board (REB)[^o25] as well as two others that split their committee duties between conduct ([PRCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interagency_Advisory_Panel_on_Responsible_Conduct_of_Research)) and ethics committee ([PRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interagency_Advisory_Panel_on_Research_Ethics)).[^o26]
- The European Union only sets the guidelines for its member's [ethics committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_committee_%28European_Union%29).
- Large international organizations like the [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) have their own ethics committees.

In Canada, mandatory research ethics training is required for students, professors and others who work in research.[^o27][^o28] The US first legislated [institutional review boards](wikipedia://en/1983847) procedures in the 1974 [National Research Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Act).

## Criticism {#Criticism}

Published in *Social Sciences & Medicine (2009)* several authors suggested that research ethics in a medical context is dominated by [principlism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principlism).[^o30]
