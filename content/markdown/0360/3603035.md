# Nielsen theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nielsen theory"}

**Nielsen theory** is a branch of mathematical research with its origins in [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) [fixed-point theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-point_theory). Its central ideas were developed by Danish mathematician [Jakob Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516), and bear his name.

:::

The theory developed in the study of the so-called *minimal number* of a [map](wikipedia://en/516931) *f* from a [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28topology%29) space to itself, denoted *MF*\[*f*\]. This is defined as:

$$\mathit{MF}[f] = \min \{ \# \mathrm{Fix}(g) \, | \, g \sim f \},$$

where *~* indicates [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) of mappings, and #Fix(*g*) indicates the number of fixed points of *g*. The minimal number is very difficult to compute in Nielsen's time, and remains so today. [Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516)'s approach is to group the fixed-point set into classes, which are judged "essential" or "nonessential" according to whether or not they can be "removed" by a homotopy.

[Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516)'s original formulation is equivalent to the following: We define an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on the set of fixed points of a self-map *f* on a space *X*. We say that *x* is equivalent to *y* if and only if there exists a [path](wikipedia://en/1189553) *c* from *x* to *y* with *f*(*c*) homotopic to *c* as paths. The equivalence classes with respect to this relation are called the **[Nielsen classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_classes)** of *f*, and the **[Nielsen number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_number)** *N*(*f*) is defined as the number of Nielsen classes having non-zero [fixed-point index](wikipedia://en/2132356) sum.

[Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516) proved that

$$N(f) \le \mathit{MF}[f],$$

making his invariant a good tool for estimating the much more difficult *MF*\[*f*\]. This leads immediately to what is now known as the **Nielsen fixed-point theorem:** *Any map f has at least N(f) fixed points.*

Because of its definition in terms of the [fixed-point index](wikipedia://en/2132356), the [Nielsen number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_number) is closely related to the [Lefschetz number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_number). Indeed, shortly after [Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516)'s initial work, the two invariants were combined into a single "generalized Lefschetz number" (more recently called the [Reidemeister trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reidemeister_trace)) by [Wecken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wecken) and [Reidemeister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reidemeister).
