# Selberg integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Selberg integral"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Selberg integral** is a generalization of [Euler beta function](wikipedia://en/245660) to *n* dimensions introduced by [Atle Selberg](wikipedia://en/2021).[^o2] It has applications in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), [multivariable orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718), [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory), [Calogero–Moser–Sutherland model](wikipedia://en/80269878), and [Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations](wikipedia://en/16119106).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=named-heading name="Selberg's integral formula"}

## Selberg's integral formula {#Selberg's_integral_formula}

When $Re(\alpha) > 0, Re(\beta) > 0, Re(\gamma) > -\min \left(\frac 1n , \frac{Re(\alpha)}{n-1}, \frac{Re(\beta)}{n-1}\right)$, we have

$$\begin{aligned} S_{n} (\alpha, \beta, \gamma) & = \int_0^1 \cdots \int_0^1 \prod_{i=1}^n t_i^{\alpha-1}(1-t_i)^{\beta-1} \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} |t_i - t_j |^{2 \gamma}\,dt_1 \cdots dt_n \\ & = \prod_{j = 0}^{n-1} \frac {\Gamma(\alpha + j \gamma) \Gamma(\beta + j \gamma) \Gamma (1 + (j+1)\gamma)} {\Gamma(\alpha + \beta + (n+j-1)\gamma) \Gamma(1+\gamma)} \end{aligned}$$

Selberg's formula implies [Dixon's identity](wikipedia://en/19864630) for well poised hypergeometric series, and some special cases of [Dyson's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyson's_conjecture). This is a corollary of Aomoto.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Aomoto's integral formula"}

## Aomoto's integral formula {#Aomoto's_integral_formula}

Aomoto proved a slightly more general integral formula.[^o8] With the same conditions as Selberg's formula,

$$\int_0^1 \cdots \int_0^1 \left(\prod_{i=1}^k t_i\right)\prod_{i=1}^n t_i^{\alpha-1}(1-t_i)^{\beta-1} \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} |t_i - t_j |^{2 \gamma}\,dt_1 \cdots dt_n$$

$$= S_n(\alpha,\beta,\gamma) \prod_{j=1}^k\frac{\alpha+(n-j)\gamma}{\alpha+\beta+(2n-j-1)\gamma}.$$

A proof is found in Chapter 8 of (Andrews Askey Roy 1999).[^o10]

:::

## Mehta's integral {#Mehta's_integral}

When $Re(\gamma) > -1/n$,

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \cdots \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \prod_{i=1}^n e^{-t_i^2/2} \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} |t_i - t_j |^{2 \gamma}\,dt_1 \cdots dt_n = \prod_{j=1}^n\frac{\Gamma(1+j\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)}.$$

It is a corollary of Selberg, by setting $\alpha = \beta$, and change of variables with $t_i = \frac{1+t'_i/\sqrt{2\alpha}}{2}$, then taking $\alpha \to \infty$.

This was conjectured by (Mehta Dyson 1963), who were unaware of Selberg's earlier work.[^o13]

It is the partition function for a gas of point charges moving on a line that are attracted to the origin.[^o14]

In particular, when $\gamma = 1$, the term on the right is $\prod_{j=1}^n j!$.

## Macdonald's integral {#Macdonald's_integral}

(Macdonald 1982) conjectured the following extension of Mehta's integral to all [finite root systems](wikipedia://en/277087), Mehta's original case corresponding to the *A*<sub>*n*−1</sub> root system.[^o17]

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int\cdots\int \left|\prod_r\frac{2(x,r)}{(r,r)}\right|^{\gamma}e^{-(x_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2)/2}dx_1\cdots dx_n =\prod_{j=1}^n\frac{\Gamma(1+d_j\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)}$$

The product is over the roots *r* of the roots system and the numbers *d*<sub>*j*</sub> are the degrees of the generators of the ring of invariants of the reflection group. (Opdam 1989) gave a uniform proof for all crystallographic reflection groups.[^o19] Several years later he proved it in full generality, making use of computer-aided calculations by Garvan.[^o20]
