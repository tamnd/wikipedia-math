# Alfred Kneschke

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Kneschke"}

**Alfred Emil Richard Kneschke** (15 June 1902 in Altlöbau – 24 November 1979 in [Freiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiberg)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), engineer and university lecturer.[^o4] During the World War II, Kneschke managed the Referat IV, Section II of the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) [Signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence) organization [General der Nachrichtenaufklärung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_der_Nachrichtenaufklärung) until November 1944, working on [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) and decoding of British, USA, French and Balkan cipher systems. From Nov 1944, he worked in the [OKW/Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipher_Department_of_the_High_Command_of_the_Wehrmacht) [cipher](wikipedia://en/5244) bureau as a cryptanalyst.[^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Kneschke came from a poor working family. In the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), undertaking study with little money was very difficult and required Kneschke to seek employment as well. In 1922, Kneschke, completed his seminary training as a [primary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_school) [teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher) and at the same time successfully passed the university-preparatory school leaving qualification, [Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur). After a four-year degree at [TU Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dresden), he graduated as an engineer in [technical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_physics). In 1927, he was put forward by advisors Dr. [Georg Wiarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Wiarda) and [Max Otto Lagally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Otto_Lagally) for promotion to [Dr. Phil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in applied mathematics with a thesis titled: *Application of the theory of integral equations to the impact problem of solid insulators* (German:Anwendung der Theorie der Integralgleichungen auf das Durchschlagsproblem von festen Isolatoren). From 1926 to 1930 he was assistant to [Max Otto Lagally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Otto_Lagally). At the age of 27, he qualified as a lecturer at the [Dresden University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dresden) and passed the [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) for the higher school office. In 1930 he went to work in the debt service, but at the same time taught as a lecturer at the TH Dresden, in 1938 he was awarded the title of [professor extraordinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_Germany). Kneschke was a teacher of mathematics and physics at the Higher School of Experiments in Dresden, then a mathematics lecturer at the [Chemnitz University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemnitz_University_of_Technology). He then headed the Oberrealschule in Meerane. In 1939 he was drafted. In 1945 he was released from the public service of Saxony in the course of [denazification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denazification) as a [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) Party member. In 1945, he was dismissed from the debt service agency and the university service due to former [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) membership.[^o7] Between 1945 and 1949 he worked as an electrician FA. Geißler in [Meerane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerane).[^o7] Between 1950 - 1951 Kneschke worked as a mathematical research assistant at VEB [Carl Zeiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Zeiss_AG) in [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena).[^o7] On 1 November 1951 he was appointed as Professor with Chair of Technical Mechanics at the Faculty of Natural Sciences and Supplementary Courses of the [Freiberg University of Mining and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiberg_University_of_Mining_and_Technology) and Director of the [Institute for Applied Mathematics and Technical Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Applied_Mathematics_and_Technical_Mechanics).[^o7]

In November 1933 he signed the [vow of allegiance of the German professors to Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State).[^o8]
