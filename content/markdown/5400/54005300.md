# Gustav S. Christensen

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Gustav S. Christensen"}

**Gustav Strøm Christensen** (1 April 1929 – 9 August 2007) was a Danish-Canadian academic mathematician and engineer. Born in [Vesterø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterø) on the Danish island [Læsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Læsø), he first worked as a radio operator in the Danish merchant marine. Later he became a professor of [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta) in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton), [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia) for 27 years. In 1957 he won the [Engineering Institute of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_Institute_of_Canada)'s prize and the [Henry Birks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Birks) Gold Medal in Engineering. He received a B.Sc. in [engineering physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_physics) from the University of Alberta, Edmonton, in 1958 and a M.A.Sc. from the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver) in 1960. Later he obtained a Ph.D. in 1966, also in electrical engineering, from the University of British Columbia. Christensen wrote over 140 scientific papers, co-authored four books and four chapters.[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Christensen was born 1 April 1929 on a farm on the Danish island of [Læsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Læsø). The eighth of nine children, Gustav completed 9 years of schooling there in a one-room elementary school and a new junior high school. In 1945 he moved to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) to take a radio operator [apprenticeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apprenticeship). He served with the [Danish Merchant Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Danish_navy) for four years and on an American [oil tanker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_tanker) for another two as a radio operator.

He emigrated to Canada and in 1969 he married an English immigrant, Penelope Janet Gardner,[^o2] in Edmonton. They have two children, Lynne [^o3] and Neil.

### Academic life {#Academic_life}

After moving to Canada, Christensen completed his high school diploma in six months, mainly through correspondence school in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton). He enrolled in [engineering physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_physics) at the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta), where he won the top engineering award, the [Henry Birks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Birks) Gold Medal, in 1958 along with several other scholarships. After a summer with the [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_%28Canada%29)[^o9] in [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa), he took his M.A. Sc. in [Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Engineering) at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) (UBC) in 1960 and subsequently obtained practical industrial experience with the BC Energy Board and Chemcell in Edmonton. He disliked the odours associated with chemical engineering left to complete his Ph.D. at UBC. His thesis (1966) was on the [stability](wikipedia://en/5493795) of [non-linear mathematical models of systems](wikipedia://en/146103).

In July 1966, he returned to University of Alberta as [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) and spent 27 years teaching, researching and taking a large load of administrative responsibilities for the [Department of Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Engineering). His research was mainly concentrated on the [optimum economic operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_dispatch) of various types of [power systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_systems), minimizing the energy lost in [transmission lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_lines) while at the same time using, for example, the water available in all the [hydro plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydro_plant) in a system to generate the maximum amount of power.[^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13] This produced the cheapest energy and the best return on investment. Christensen authored over 140 scientific papers, four books and four chapters in books on his research specialities. Christensen retired as full professor in 1993 and moved to [Mission, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission,_British_Columbia).

### Electric power industries {#Electric_power_industries}

Christensen was an active intervener in the [National Energy Board (NEB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Energy_Board) hearings which ultimately resulted in the rejection of the huge SE2 ([Sumas Energy 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumas_2)) [coal-fired plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_power_station), one that would have emitted unacceptable pollution into the [Fraser Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Valley) funnel.

## Final years {#Final_years}

After retirement, Christensen and his wife joined [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) and they both served at the church's Family History Centre in Abbotsford. In co-operation with his wife Penny, a professional genealogist, he wrote his autobiography, the history of his parents' 172 descendants and a volume of translations of historical articles on Læsø. At the age of 75 Christensen took up an [adjunct professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship) in the School of Engineering Science at [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University) in [Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby), BC, where he had an office, a computer and a grad student – but no salary. He spent one or two days there each week until he died. During this time he published a number of papers in the field of [asymptotic stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_stability) of [linear](wikipedia://en/722503) and [nonlinear systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_systems), a continuation of his Ph.D. work. He simplified the solutions to [Lyapunov's](wikipedia://en/363360) stability theorems so that they are now useful to the electric power engineers and other practical engineers. Another development was the solution of the [least absolute value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_absolute_value) [estimation](wikipedia://en/40703875) problem originally posed by [Laplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace) in 1750 by using [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730).

Christensen died 9 August 2007 at the age of 78.[^o5]
