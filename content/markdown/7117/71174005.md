# Tatsujiro Shimizu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tatsujiro Shimizu"}

**Tatsujiro Shimizu** was a Japanese mathematician working in the field of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). He was the founder of the [Japanese Association of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Association_of_Mathematical_Sciences).[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Shimizu graduated from the Department of Mathematics, School of Science, Tokyo Imperial University in 1924, and stayed there working as a staff member. In 1932 he moved to [Osaka Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Imperial_University) and became a professor. He made contributions to the establishment of the Department of Mathematics there. In 1949, Shimizu left Osaka and took up a professorship at [Kobe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_University). After two years, he moved again to [Osaka Prefectural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture_University). From 1961 he was a professor at the [Tokyo University of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University_of_Science).[^o5][^o6]

In 1948, seeing the difficulty in publication of paper in mathematics, Shimizu started a new journal [Mathematica Japonicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica_Japonica), for papers of pure and applied mathematics in general, on his own funds. The journal served as the foundation of the Japanese Association of Mathematical Sciences.[^o5][^o6]

Shimizu remained active in mathematics into old age. He gave talks at the meeting of the Mathematical Society of Japan until 90 years old. He died in Uji City, Kyoto Prefecture, on November 8, 1992, at the age 95.[^o5][^o6]

## Works {#Works}

### Function theory {#Function_theory}

The first works of Shimizu treated topics of function theory, in particular the theory of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857). A new form of the [Nevanlinna characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevanlinna_characteristic) generalised by him (and separately by [Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636)) is now known as the [Ahlfors-Shimizu characteristic](wikipedia://en/393135). Additionally, with the idea of function groups, he attained a profound result on the construction of [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) of meromorphic functions. In 1931, as a pioneer in Japan responding to [Fatou](wikipedia://en/629100)'s study of the theory of iteration of the [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169), Shimizu published two papers introducing the subject in Japanese journals.[^o9]

### Applied mathematics {#Applied_mathematics}

Since he moved to Osaka in 1932, Shimizu has been interested in the application of mathematical methods into science and technology. He broadly worked on the existence conditions of [limit cycles](wikipedia://en/452950), [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and applied analysis (including solving [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753), numerical solutions and non-linear [oscillations](wikipedia://en/475449)), computing machines and devices, as well as artificial intelligence (especially in solving arithmetic problems through electronic computer). His research in these areas was continued through this career. He was also involved in operations research and mathematics in management sciences, as well as probability theory and mathematical statistics.[^o5][^o6]
