# Václav Hlavatý

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Václav Hlavatý"}

*Image: Václav Hlavatý (1894-1969).jpg.* Václav portrait

**Václav Hlavatý** (27 January 1894 – 11 January 1969) was a noted [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_people)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who wrote on the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) and corresponded extensively with [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) on the subject.[^o2] In particular, Hlavatý solved some very difficult equations relating to Einstein's [Unified field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_field_theory), which was featured in the news media as one of the great scientific achievements of 1953.[^o3] Einstein himself was reported to have said that if anyone could solve the equations it would be Professor Hlavatý, which proved to be the case.[^o4]

:::

He was born in [Louny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louny), [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) (now in the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic)) and died in [Bloomington, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Indiana). He obtained his PhD in 1921 at the [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague)[^o5][^o6] and during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) participated in the [Prague uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_uprising),[^o4] but his academic career was mainly at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University), which he joined in 1948, and where he became Professor, later Emeritus, of Mathematics. A special book of mathematical essays was published in his honour.[^o7] In 1958, he became the first President of the [Czechoslovak Society of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Society_of_Arts_and_Sciences)[^o8] established in [Washington DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_DC) by intellectuals of Czech and Slovak origin.

He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1928 in Bologna and in 1936 in Oslo.

In 1931 he married Olga Neumannova, and they had a daughter, Olga.[^o5]
