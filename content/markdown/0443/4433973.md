# Martingale difference sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martingale difference sequence"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **martingale difference sequence** (**MDS**) is related to the concept of the [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536). A [stochastic series](wikipedia://en/47895) *X* is an MDS if its [expectation](wikipedia://en/9653) with respect to the past is zero. Formally, consider an adapted [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $\{X_t, \mathcal{F}_t\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}$ on a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$. $X_t$ is an MDS if it satisfies the following two conditions:

:::

$\mathbb{E} \left|X_t\right| < \infty$, and

$$\mathbb{E} \left[X_t | \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right] = 0, a.s.,$$

for all $t$. By construction, this implies that if $Y_t$ is a martingale, then $X_t=Y_t-Y_{t-1}$ will be an MDS—hence the name.

The MDS is an extremely useful construct in modern probability theory because it implies much milder restrictions on the memory of the sequence than [independence](wikipedia://en/27593), yet most limit theorems that hold for an independent sequence will also hold for an MDS.

A special case of MDS, denoted as {*X*<sub>*t*</sub>,*$\mathcal{F}$*<sub>*t*</sub>}<sub>*0*</sub><sup>${\infty}$</sup> is known as innovative sequence of *S*<sub>*n*</sub>; where *S*<sub>*n*</sub> and $\mathcal{F}_{t}$ are corresponding to [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) and [filtration](wikipedia://en/57854817) of the random processes $\{X_{t}\}_0^\infty$.

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) innovation series is used to emphasize the generality of [Doob representation](wikipedia://en/23360896). In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) the innovation series is used to introduce [Kalman filter](wikipedia://en/180855). The main differences of [innovation](wikipedia://en/21957017) terminologies are in the applications. The later application aims to introduce the nuance of samples to the model by random sampling.
