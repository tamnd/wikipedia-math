# Mathias Lerch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathias Lerch"}

*Image: Matyas Lerch (1860-1922).jpg.* Mathias Lerch

**Mathias Lerch** or **Matyáš Lerch** (; 20 February 1860, [Milínov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milínov) – 3 August 1922, [Sušice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sušice)) was a [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who published about 250 papers, largely on [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He studied in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) ([Czech Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Technical_University_in_Prague)) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin); subsequently held teaching positions at the [University of Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fribourg) in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), the [Brno University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno_University_of_Technology) in [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno), and finally at then newly founded (1920) [Masaryk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaryk_University) in Brno where he became its first mathematics professor.[^o2]

:::

In 1900, he was awarded the Grand Prize of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) for his number-theoretic work.[^o2] The [Lerch zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lerch_zeta_function) is named after him, as is the [Appell&ndash;Lerch sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appell&ndash;Lerch_sum). His doctoral students include [Michel Plancherel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Plancherel) and [Otakar Borůvka](wikipedia://en/2923087).
