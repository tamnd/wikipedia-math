# Link concordance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Link concordance"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), two [links](wikipedia://en/1161784) $L_0 \subset S^n$ and $L_1 \subset S^n$ are **concordant** if there exists an [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) $f : L_0 \times [0,1] \to S^n \times [0,1]$ such that $f(L_0 \times \{0\}) = L_0 \times \{0\}$ and $f(L_0 \times \{1\}) = L_1 \times \{1\}$.

:::

By its nature, **link concordance** is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259). It is weaker than [isotopy](wikipedia://en/212250#Isotopy), and stronger than [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250): isotopy implies concordance implies homotopy. A link is a **slice link** if it is concordant to the [unlink](wikipedia://en/6718898).

## Concordance invariants {#Concordance_invariants}

A function of a link that is invariant under concordance is called a **concordance invariant**.

The [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999) of any two components of a link is one of the most elementary concordance invariants. The [signature of a knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_of_a_knot) is also a concordance invariant. A subtler concordance invariant are the [Milnor invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_invariants), and in fact all rational [finite type](wikipedia://en/3014395) concordance invariants are Milnor invariants and their products,[^o3] though non-finite type concordance invariants exist.

## Higher dimensions {#Higher_dimensions}

One can analogously define concordance for any two submanifolds $M_0, M_1 \subset N$. In this case one considers two submanifolds concordant if there is a [cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) between them in $N \times [0,1],$ i.e., if there is a manifold with boundary $W \subset N \times [0,1]$ whose boundary consists of $M_0 \times \{0\}$ and $M_1 \times \{1\}.$

This higher-dimensional concordance is a [relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_homotopy) form of cobordism – it requires two submanifolds to be not just abstractly cobordant, but "cobordant in *N*".
