# Davis distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Davis distribution"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Davis distributions** are a family of [continuous probability distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_probability_distribution). It is named after [Harold T. Davis](wikipedia://en/42493517) (1892–1974), who in 1941 proposed this distribution to model income sizes. (*The Theory of Econometrics and Analysis of Economic Time Series*). It is a generalization of the [Planck's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck's_law) of radiation from [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the Davis distribution is given by

$$f(x;\mu,b,n)=\frac{ b^n {(x-\mu)}^{-1-n} }{ \left( e^{\frac{b}{x-\mu}} -1 \right) \Gamma(n) \zeta(n) }$$

where $\Gamma(n)$ is the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and $\zeta(n)$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). Here μ, *b*, and *n* are parameters of the distribution, and *n* need not be an integer.

:::

## Background {#Background}

In an attempt to derive an expression that would represent not merely the upper tail of the distribution of income, Davis required an appropriate model with the following properties[^o5]

- $f(\mu)=0 \,$ for some $\mu>0 \,$
- A modal income exists
- For large *x*, the density behaves like a [Pareto distribution](wikipedia://en/53057):

$$f(x) \sim A {(x-\mu)}^{-\alpha-1} \, .$$

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- If $X \sim \mathrm{Davis}(b=1,n=4,\mu=0)\,$ then $\tfrac{1}{X} \sim \mathrm{Planck}$ ([Planck's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck's_law))
