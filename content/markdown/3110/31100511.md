# Bates distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bates distribution"}

In [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and business [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Bates distribution**, named after [Grace Bates](wikipedia://en/40802690), is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) of a number of [statistically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistically_independent) [uniformly distributed](wikipedia://en/1699223) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) on the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324).[^o2] This distribution is related to the [uniform](wikipedia://en/1699223), the [triangular](wikipedia://en/663496), and the [normal Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_distributions), and has applications in [broadcast engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_engineering) for signal enhancement.

:::

The Bates distribution on $[0,1]$ and of parameter $n$ is sometimes confused[^o3] with the [Irwin–Hall distribution](wikipedia://en/15329594) of parameter $n$, which is the distribution of the **sum** (not the **mean**) of $n$ independent random variables uniformly distributed on the unit interval $[0,1]$. More precisely, if $X$ has a Bates distribution on $[0,1]$, then $nX$ has an [Irwin-Hall distribution](wikipedia://en/15329594) of parameter $n$ (and support on $[0,n]$). For $n=1$, both the Bates distribution and the Irwin-Hall distribution coincide with the uniform distribution on the unit interval $[0,1]$.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Bates distribution on the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) $[0,1]$ and with parameter $n \in \mathbb N$ is the continuous [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the [empirical mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_mean) $X$ of $n$ [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) $U_1,...,U_n$ [uniformly distributed](wikipedia://en/1699223) on the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324):

$$X = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n U_k.$$

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is

$f_X(x; n)= \frac n {2(n-1)!} \sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^k {n \choose k} (nx-k)^{n-1} \operatorname{sgn}(nx-k)$[^o6]

for $x$ in the open interval $]0,1[$, and zero elsewhere. Here $\operatorname{sgn}(nx-k)$ denotes the [sign function](wikipedia://en/505449):

$$\operatorname{sgn}(nx-k) = \begin{cases} -1 & nx < k \\ 0 & nx = k \\ 1 & nx > k. \end{cases}$$

More generally, the empirical mean $X^{(a,b)}$ of $n$ [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) random variables $U_1^{(a,b)},...,U_n^{(a,b)}$ [uniformly distributed](wikipedia://en/1699223) on the interval $[a,b]$

$$X^{(a,b)} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n U_k^{(a,b)}$$

has the following the probability density function (PDF) of

$$f_{X^{(a,b)}} (x; n) = \frac{1}{b-a} f_X\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a};n\right)$$

for $x \in ]a,b[$ and zero otherwise.

:::

## Extensions and Applications {#Extensions_and_Applications}

With a few modifications, the Bates distribution encompasses the [uniform](wikipedia://en/1699223), the [triangular](wikipedia://en/663496), and, taking the limit as n goes to infinity, also the [normal Gaussian distribution](wikipedia://en/21462).

Replacing the term $\frac{1}{n}$ when calculating the mean, *X*, with $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ will create a similar distribution with a constant variance, such as unity. Then, by subtracting the mean, the resulting mean of the distribution will be set at zero. Thus the parameter *n* would become a purely [shape-adjusting parameter](wikipedia://en/9210865). By also allowing *n* to be a non-integer, a highly flexible distribution can be created, for example, *U*(0,1) + 0.5*U*(0,1) gives a [trapezoidal distribution](wikipedia://en/40750679).

The [Student-t distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student-t_distribution) provides a natural extension of the normal Gaussian distribution for modeling of [long tail](wikipedia://en/1385393) data. A Bates distribution that has been generalized as previously stated fulfills the same purpose for [short tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-tailed_distribution) data.

The Bates distribution has an application to [beamforming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beamforming) and [pattern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_synthesis) in the field of [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering). The distribution was found to increase the [beamwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beamwidth) of the [main lobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_lobe), representing an increase in the signal of the radiation pattern in a single direction, while simultaneously reducing the usually undesirable[^o8] [sidelobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_lobe) levels.[^o9]
