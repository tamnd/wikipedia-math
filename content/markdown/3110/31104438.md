# K-tree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K-tree"}

*Image: Goldner-Harary graph.svg.* The [Goldner–Harary graph](wikipedia://en/24674758), an example of a planar 3-tree.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a ***k*-tree** is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) formed by starting with a (*k* + 1)-vertex [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) and then repeatedly adding vertices in such a way that each added vertex *v* has exactly *k* neighbors *U* such that, together, the *k* + 1 vertices formed by *v* and *U* form a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466).[^o4][^o8]

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterizations"}

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

The *k*-trees are exactly the maximal graphs with a [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) of *k* ("maximal" means that no more edges can be added without increasing their treewidth).[^o8] They are also exactly the [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165) all of whose [maximal cliques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_clique) are the same size *k* + 1 and all of whose minimal clique separators are also all the same size *k*.[^o4]

:::

## Related graph classes {#Related_graph_classes}

1-trees are the same as [trees](wikipedia://en/48560). 2-trees are maximal [series–parallel graphs](wikipedia://en/9938559),[^o6] and include also the maximal [outerplanar graphs](wikipedia://en/352343). [Planar](wikipedia://en/24314) 3-trees are also known as [Apollonian networks](wikipedia://en/31104610).[^o7]

The graphs that have [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) at most *k* are exactly the [subgraphs](wikipedia://en/325802#subgraph) of *k*-trees, and for this reason they are called [partial *k*-trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_k-tree).[^o8]

The graphs formed by the edges and vertices of *k*-dimensional [stacked polytopes](wikipedia://en/44760854), [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471) formed by starting from a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) and then repeatedly gluing simplices onto the faces of the polytope, are *k*-trees when *k* ≥ 3.[^o9] This gluing process mimics the construction of *k*-trees by adding vertices to a clique.[^o10] A *k*-tree is the graph of a stacked polytope if and only if no three (*k* + 1)-vertex cliques have *k* vertices in common.[^o11]
