# Beta prime distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beta prime distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **beta prime distribution** (also known as **inverted beta distribution** or **beta distribution of the second kind**[^o2]) is an [absolutely continuous probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543#Continuous_probability_distribution). If $p\in[0,1]$ has a [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074), then the [odds](wikipedia://en/172069) $\frac{p}{1-p}$ has a beta prime distribution.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Beta prime distribution is defined for $x > 0$ with two parameters *α* and *β*, having the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487):

$$f(x) = \frac{x^{\alpha-1} (1+x)^{-\alpha -\beta}}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}$$

where *B* is the [Beta function](wikipedia://en/245660).

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) is

$$F(x; \alpha,\beta)=I_{\frac{x}{1+x}}\left(\alpha, \beta \right) ,$$

where *I* is the [regularized incomplete beta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regularized_incomplete_beta_function).

While the related [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074) is the [conjugate prior distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_prior_distribution) of the parameter of a Bernoulli distribution expressed as a probability, the beta prime distribution is the conjugate prior distribution of the parameter of a Bernoulli distribution expressed in [odds](wikipedia://en/172069). The distribution is a [Pearson type VI](wikipedia://en/1804023#The_Pearson_type_VI_distribution) distribution.[^o2]

The mode of a variate *X* distributed as $\beta'(\alpha,\beta)$ is $\hat{X} = \frac{\alpha-1}{\beta+1}$. Its mean is $\frac{\alpha}{\beta-1}$ if $\beta>1$ (if $\beta \leq 1$ the mean is infinite, in other words it has no well defined mean) and its variance is $\frac{\alpha(\alpha+\beta-1)}{(\beta-2)(\beta-1)^2}$ if $\beta>2$.

For $-\alpha <k <\beta$, the *k*-th moment $E[X^k]$ is given by

$$E[X^k]=\frac{\mathrm{B}(\alpha+k,\beta-k)}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}.$$

For $k\in \mathbb{N}$ with $k <\beta,$ this simplifies to

$$E[X^k]=\prod_{i=1}^k \frac{\alpha+i-1}{\beta-i}.$$

The cdf can also be written as

$$\frac{x^\alpha \cdot {}_2F_1(\alpha, \alpha+\beta, \alpha+1, -x)}{\alpha \cdot \mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}$$

where ${}_2F_1$ is the Gauss's [hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/2055223) <sub>2</sub>*F*<sub>1</sub> .

### Alternative parameterization {#Alternative_parameterization}

The beta prime distribution may also be reparameterized in terms of its mean *μ* > 0 and precision *ν* > 0 parameters ([^o6] p. 36).

Consider the parameterization *μ* = *α*/(*β* − 1) and *ν* = *β* − 2, i.e., *α* = *μ*(1 + *ν*) and *β* = 2 + *ν*. Under this parameterization E\[*Y*\] = *μ* and Var\[Y\] = *μ*(1 + *μ*)/*ν*.

### Generalization {#Generalization}

Two more parameters can be added to form the **generalized beta prime distribution** $\beta'(\alpha,\beta,p,q)$:

- $p > 0$ [shape](wikipedia://en/9210865) ([real](wikipedia://en/20646438))
- $q > 0$ scale ([real](wikipedia://en/20646438))

having the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487):

$$f(x;\alpha,\beta,p,q) = \frac{p \left(\frac x q \right)^{\alpha p-1} \left(1+ \left(\frac x q \right)^p\right)^{-\alpha -\beta}}{q \mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}$$

with [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean)

$$\frac{q\Gamma\left(\alpha+\tfrac 1 p\right)\Gamma(\beta-\tfrac 1 p)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)} \quad \text{if } \beta p>1$$

and [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127)

$$q \left({\frac{\alpha p -1}{\beta p +1}}\right)^\tfrac{1}{p} \quad \text{if } \alpha p\ge 1$$

Note that if *p* = *q* = 1 then the generalized beta prime distribution reduces to the **standard beta prime distribution**.

This generalization can be obtained via the following invertible transformation. If $y\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta)$ and $x=qy^{1/p}$ for $q,p>0$, then $x\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta,p,q)$.

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Compound gamma distribution"}

#### Compound gamma distribution {#Compound_gamma_distribution}

The **compound gamma distribution**[^o11] is the generalization of the beta prime when the scale parameter, *q* is added, but where *p* = 1. It is so named because it is formed by [compounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_distribution) two [gamma distributions](wikipedia://en/207079):

$$\beta'(x;\alpha,\beta,1,q) = \int_0^\infty G(x;\alpha,r)G(r;\beta,q) \; dr$$

where $G(x;a,b)$ is the gamma pdf with shape $a$ and inverse scale $b$.

The mode, mean and variance of the compound gamma can be obtained by multiplying the mode and mean in the above infobox by *q* and the variance by *q*<sup>2</sup>.

Another way to express the compounding is if $r\sim G(\beta,q)$ and $x\mid r\sim G(\alpha,r)$, then $x\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta,1,q)$. This gives one way to generate random variates with compound gamma, or beta prime distributions. Another is via the ratio of independent gamma variates, as shown below.

## Properties {#Properties}

- If $X \sim \beta'(\alpha,\beta)$ then $\tfrac{1}{X} \sim \beta'(\beta,\alpha)$.
- If $Y\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta)$, and $X=qY^{1/p}$, then $X\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta,p,q)$.
- If $X \sim \beta'(\alpha,\beta,p,q)$ then $kX \sim \beta'(\alpha,\beta,p,kq)$.
- $$\beta'(\alpha,\beta,1,1) = \beta'(\alpha,\beta)$$

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- If $X \sim \textrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$, then $\frac{X}{1-X} \sim \beta'(\alpha,\beta)$ and $\frac{1}{X}-1 \sim \beta'(\beta,\alpha)$. This property can be used to generate beta prime distributed variates.
- If $X \sim \beta'(\alpha,\beta)$, then $\frac{X}{1+X} \sim \textrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$ and $\frac{1}{X+1} \sim \textrm{Beta}(\beta,\alpha)$. This is a corollary from the property above.
- If $X \sim F(2\alpha,2\beta)$ has an [*F*-distribution](wikipedia://en/332116), then $\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta} X \sim \beta'(\alpha,\beta)$, or equivalently, $X\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta , 1 , \tfrac{\beta}{\alpha})$.
- For [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079) parametrization I:
  - If $X_k \sim \Gamma(\alpha_k,\theta_k)$ are independent, then $\tfrac{X_1}{X_2} \sim \beta'(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,1,\tfrac{\theta_1}{\theta_2})$. Note $\theta_1,\theta_2,\tfrac{\theta_1}{\theta_2}$ are all scale parameters for their respective distributions.
- For gamma distribution parametrization II:
  - If $X_k \sim \Gamma(\alpha_k,\beta_k)$ are independent, then $\tfrac{X_1}{X_2} \sim \beta'(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,1,\tfrac{\beta_2}{\beta_1})$. The $\beta_k$ are rate parameters, while $\tfrac{\beta_2}{\beta_1}$ is a scale parameter.
  - If $\beta_2\sim \Gamma(\alpha_1,\beta_1)$ and $X_2\mid\beta_2\sim\Gamma(\alpha_2,\beta_2)$, then $X_2\sim\beta'(\alpha_2,\alpha_1,1,\beta_1)$. The $\beta_k$ are rate parameters for the gamma distributions, but $\beta_1$ is the scale parameter for the beta prime.
- $\beta'(p,1,a,b) = \textrm{Dagum}(p,a,b)$ the [Dagum distribution](wikipedia://en/31102537)
- $\beta'(1,p,a,b) = \textrm{SinghMaddala}(p,a,b)$ the [Singh–Maddala distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singh–Maddala_distribution).
- $\beta'(1,1,\gamma,\sigma) = \textrm{LL}(\gamma,\sigma)$ the [log logistic distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_logistic_distribution).
- The beta prime distribution is a special case of the type 6 [Pearson distribution](wikipedia://en/1804023).
- If *X* has a [Pareto distribution](wikipedia://en/53057) with minimum $x_m$ and shape parameter $\alpha$, then $\dfrac{X}{x_m}-1\sim\beta^\prime(1,\alpha)$.
- If *X* has a [Lomax distribution](wikipedia://en/32253318), also known as a Pareto Type II distribution, with shape parameter $\alpha$ and scale parameter $\lambda$, then $\frac{X}{\lambda}\sim \beta^\prime(1,\alpha)$.
- If *X* has a standard [Pareto Type IV distribution](wikipedia://en/53057) with shape parameter $\alpha$ and inequality parameter $\gamma$, then $X^{\frac{1}{\gamma}} \sim \beta^\prime(1,\alpha)$, or equivalently, $X \sim \beta^\prime(1,\alpha,\tfrac{1}{\gamma},1)$.
- The [inverted Dirichlet distribution](wikipedia://en/43387431) is a generalization of the beta prime distribution.
- If $X\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta)$, then $\ln X$ has a [generalized logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/15841869). More generally, if $X\sim\beta'(\alpha,\beta,p,q)$, then $\ln X$ has a [scaled and shifted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmoid-beta_distribution#Generalization_with_location_and_scale_parameters) generalized logistic distribution.
- If $X\sim\beta'\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)$, then $\pm\sqrt{X}$ follows a Cauchy distribution, which is equivalent to a student-t distribution with the degrees of freedom of 1.
