# Vector bundles on algebraic curves

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vector bundles on algebraic curves"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **vector bundles on algebraic curves** may be studied as [holomorphic vector bundles](wikipedia://en/4794668) on [compact Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface), which is the classical approach, or as [locally free sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaves) on [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) *C* in a more general, algebraic setting (which can for example admit [singular points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity)).

:::

Some foundational results on classification were known in the 1950s. The result of (Grothendieck 1957), that holomorphic vector bundles on the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) are sums of [line bundles](wikipedia://en/361366), is now often called the **[Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem](wikipedia://en/14675761)**, since it is implicit in much earlier work of (Birkhoff 1909) on the [Riemann–Hilbert problem](wikipedia://en/9710396).

(Atiyah 1957) gave the classification of vector bundles on [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).

The Riemann–Roch theorem for vector bundles was proved by (Weil 1938), before the 'vector bundle' concept had really any official status. Although, associated [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158) were classical objects. See [Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/2892412) for his result. He was seeking a generalization of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734), by passing from [holomorphic line bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle) to higher rank. This idea would prove fruitful, in terms of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) of vector bundles. following on the work in the 1960s on [geometric invariant theory](wikipedia://en/1639003).
