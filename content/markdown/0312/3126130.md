# Wagner's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wagner's theorem"}

*Image: Petersen Wagner minors.svg.* *K*<sub>5</sub> (left) and *K*<sub>3,3</sub> (right) as minors of the nonplanar [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) (small colored circles and solid black edges). The minors may be formed by deleting the red vertex and contracting edges within each yellow circle.

*Image: Clique-sum.svg.* A clique-sum of two planar graphs and the Wagner graph, forming a *K*<sub>5</sub>-free graph.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), **Wagner's theorem** is a mathematical [forbidden graph characterization](wikipedia://en/13547826) of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), named after [Klaus Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Wagner), stating that a finite graph is planar if and only if its [minors](wikipedia://en/353042) include neither *K*<sub>5</sub> (the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) on five [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899)) nor *K*<sub>3,3</sub> (the [utility graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_graph), a [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) on six vertices). This was one of the earliest results in the theory of [graph minors](wikipedia://en/353042) and can be seen as a forerunner of the [Robertson–Seymour theorem](wikipedia://en/351769).

:::

## Definitions and statement {#Definitions_and_statement}

A planar [embedding](wikipedia://en/8149170) of a given [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is a [drawing](wikipedia://en/325813) of the graph in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), with points for its [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and curves for its [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29), in such a way that the only intersections between pairs of edges are at a common endpoint of the two edges. A [minor](wikipedia://en/353042) of a given graph is another graph formed by deleting vertices, deleting edges, and contracting edges. When an edge is contracted, its two endpoints are merged to form a single vertex. In some versions of graph minor theory the graph resulting from a contraction is simplified by removing self-loops and multiple adjacencies, while in other version [multigraphs](wikipedia://en/1774970) are allowed, but this variation makes no difference to Wagner's theorem. The theorem states that every graph has either a planar embedding, or a minor of one of two types, the complete graph *K*<sub>5</sub> or the complete bipartite graph *K*<sub>3,3</sub>.

If a given graph is planar, so are all its minors: vertex and edge deletion obviously preserve planarity, and edge contraction can also be done in a planarity-preserving way, by leaving one of the two endpoints of the contracted edge in place and routing all of the edges that were incident to the other endpoint along the path of the contracted edge. A *minor-minimal* non-planar graph is a graph that is not planar, but in which all proper minors (minors formed by at least one deletion or contraction) are planar. Another way of stating Wagner's theorem is that there are only two minor-minimal non-planar graphs, *K*<sub>5</sub> and *K*<sub>3,3</sub>.

Another result also sometimes known as Wagner's theorem states that a [four-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) graph is planar if and only if it has no *K*<sub>5</sub> minor. That is, by assuming a higher level of connectivity, the graph *K*<sub>3,3</sub> can be made unnecessary in the characterization, leaving only a single forbidden minor, *K*<sub>5</sub>. Correspondingly, the [Kelmans–Seymour conjecture](wikipedia://en/50765752) states that a 5-connected graph is planar if and only if it does not have *K*<sub>5</sub> as a [topological minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_minor).

## History and relation to Kuratowski's theorem {#History_and_relation_to_Kuratowski's_theorem}

Wagner published both theorems in 1937,[^o5] subsequent to the 1930 publication of [Kuratowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/54493),[^o6] according to which a graph is planar if and only if it does not contain as a subgraph a [subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivision_%28graph_theory%29) of one of the same two forbidden graphs *K*<sub>5</sub> and *K*<sub>3,3</sub>. In a sense, Kuratowski's theorem is stronger than Wagner's theorem: a subdivision can be converted into a minor of the same type by contracting all but one edge in each [path](wikipedia://en/638889) formed by the subdivision process, but converting a minor into a subdivision of the same type is not always possible. However, in the case of the two graphs *K*<sub>5</sub> and *K*<sub>3,3</sub>, it is straightforward to prove that a graph that has at least one of these two graphs as a minor also has at least one of them as a subdivision, so the two theorems are equivalent.[^o7]

:::

## Implications {#Implications}

One consequence of the stronger version of Wagner's theorem for four-connected graphs is to characterize the graphs that do not have a *K*<sub>5</sub> minor. The theorem can be rephrased as stating that every such graph is either planar or it can be decomposed into simpler pieces. Using this idea, the *K*<sub>5</sub>-minor-free graphs may be characterized as the graphs that can be formed as combinations of planar graphs and the eight-vertex [Wagner graph](wikipedia://en/17598450), glued together by [clique-sum](wikipedia://en/17598460) operations. For instance, *K*<sub>3,3</sub> can be formed in this way as a clique-sum of three planar graphs, each of which is a copy of the tetrahedral graph *K*<sub>4</sub>.

Wagner's theorem is an important precursor to the theory of graph minors, which culminated in the proofs of two deep and far-reaching results: the [graph structure theorem](wikipedia://en/21318521) (a generalization of Wagner's clique-sum decomposition of *K*<sub>5</sub>-minor-free graphs)[^o9] and the [Robertson–Seymour theorem](wikipedia://en/351769) (a generalization of the forbidden minor characterization of planar graphs, stating that every graph family closed under the operation of taking minors has a characterization by a finite number of forbidden minors).[^o10] Analogues of Wagner's theorem can also be extended to the theory of [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321): in particular, the same two graphs *K*<sub>5</sub> and *K*<sub>3,3</sub> (along with three other forbidden configurations) appear in a characterization of the [graphic matroids](wikipedia://en/745714) by forbidden [matroid minors](wikipedia://en/36749660).[^o11]
