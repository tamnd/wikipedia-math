# Vittorio Fossombroni

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vittorio Fossombroni"}

*Image: Fossombroni - Saggio di ricerche sull'intensità del lume, 1781 - 1523971.jpg.* *Saggio*, 1781

**Vittorio Fossombroni** (15 September 1754 – 13 April 1844) was an Italian [statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) and a distinguished drainage [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Fossombroni was born at [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo).[^o5] He was educated at the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa), where he devoted himself particularly to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [hydraulics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulics). He obtained an official appointment in [Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany) in 1782, and twelve years later was entrusted by the [grand duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_duke) with the direction of the works for the drainage of the marshy [Valdichiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdichiana), one of the four valleys around [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo), on which subject he had published a treatise in 1789.

In 1796 he was made minister for foreign affairs, but on the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) occupation of Tuscany in 1799 he fled to [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily). On the erection of the grand duchy into the ephemeral [Kingdom of Etruria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Etruria), under the queen-regent [Maria Louise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Louise,_Duchess_of_Parma), he was, appointed president of the commission of finance. In 1809 he went to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) as one of the [senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator) for Tuscany to pay homage to [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I_of_France).[^o6]

On the restoration of the [Grand Duke Ferdinand III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_III,_Grand_Duke_of_Tuscany) in 1814, he was made president of the legislative commission and was appointed prime minister, a position he retained under the [Grand Duke Leopold II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II,_Grand_Duke_of_Tuscany).[^o7] He was an important representative of the Italian School of virtual work laws.[^o5] When he was prime minister, he pursued a policy of laissez-faire and international free trade.[^o3]

According to Luigi Villaris, writing in the [*Encyclopædia Britannica* Eleventh Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica_Eleventh_Edition): "His administration, which was only terminated by his death, greatly contributed to promote the well-being of the country. He was the real master of Tuscany, and the bases of his rule were equality of all subjects before the law, honesty in the administration of justice and toleration of opinion, but he totally neglected the moral improvement of the people."[^o6]

Fossombroni married at the age of seventy-eight.[^o6] His son [Enrico Vittorio Fossombroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Vittorio_Fossombroni) was a Senator in the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy).

Vittorio Fossombroni died at [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) in 1844.
