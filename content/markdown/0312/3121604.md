# Planar lamina

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Planar lamina"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **planar lamina** (or **plane lamina**[^o2]) is a [plane figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_figure) representing a thin, usually uniform, [flat layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_surface_%28geometry%29) of a [solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_%28geometry%29). It serves also as an idealized model of a planar [cross section](wikipedia://en/1337370) of a solid body in [integration](wikipedia://en/15532). Planar laminas can be used to determine [moments of inertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moments_of_inertia), or [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) of figures, as well as an aid in corresponding calculations for 3D bodies.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A planar lamina is defined as a figure (a [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279)) $D$ of a finite area in a plane, with some mass $m$.[^o10]

This is useful in calculating [moments of inertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moments_of_inertia) or [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) for a constant density, because the mass of a lamina is proportional to its area. In a case of a variable density, given by some (non-negative) [surface density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_density) function $\rho(x,y),$ the mass $m$ of the planar lamina $D$ is a planar integral of $\rho$ over the figure:[^o11]

$$m = \iint_D\rho(x,y)\,dx\,dy$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The center of mass of the lamina is at the point

$$\left(\frac{M_y}{m},\frac{M_x}{m}\right)$$

where $M_y$ is the moment of the entire lamina about the y-axis and $M_x$ is the moment of the entire lamina about the x-axis:

$$\begin{aligned} M_y &= \lim_{m,n \to \infty} \, \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n x_{ij}^* \,\rho(x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*) \, \Delta D = \iint_D x\, \rho(x,y) \, dx \, dy \\[1ex] M_x &= \lim_{m,n \to \infty} \, \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n y_{ij}^* \,\rho(x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*) \, \Delta D = \iint_D y\, \rho(x,y) \, dx \, dy \end{aligned}$$

with summation and integration taken over a planar domain $D$.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

*Image: Bound region.jpg.*

Find the center of mass of a lamina with edges given by the lines $x=0,$ $y=x$ and $y=4-x$ where the density is given as $\rho(x,y) = 2x + 3y + 2$.

For this the mass $m$ must be found as well as the moments $M_y$ and $M_x$.

Mass is $m = \iint_D\rho(x,y)\,dx\,dy$ which can be equivalently expressed as an [iterated integral](wikipedia://en/349053):

$$m = \int_{x=0}^2 \int_{y=x}^{4-x} \left(2x + 3y + 2\right) dy\,dx$$

The inner integral is:

$$\begin{aligned} & \int_{y=x}^{4-x} \,(2x+3y+2)\,dy \\[1ex] & = \left[2xy + \frac{3}{2} y^2 + 2y\right]_{y=x}^{y=4-x} \\[1ex] & = \left[2x \left(4-x\right) + \frac{3}{2} \left(4-x\right)^2 + 2 \left(4-x\right)\right] - \left[2x^2 + \frac{3}{2} x^2 + 2x\right] \\[1ex] & = -4x^2 - 8x + 32 \end{aligned}$$

Plugging this into the outer integral results in:

$$\begin{aligned} m & = \int_0^2 \left(-4x^2 - 8x + 32\right) dx \\[1ex] & = \left[-\frac{4}{3} x^3 - 4x^2 + 32x\right]_0^2 = \frac{112}{3} \end{aligned}$$

Similarly are calculated both moments:

$$\begin{aligned} M_y &= \iint_D x\,\rho(x,y)\,dx\,dy \\[1ex] &= \int_{x=0}^2 \int_{y=x}^{4-x} x \left(2x + 3y + 2\right) dy\,dx \end{aligned}$$

with the inner integral:

$$\begin{aligned} \int_{y=x}^{4-x} x \left(2x + 3y + 2\right) dy &= \left[2x^2 y + \frac{3x}{2} y^2 + 2xy\right]_{y=x}^{4-x} \\[1ex] &= -4x^3-8x^2+32x \end{aligned}$$

which makes:

$$\begin{aligned} M_y & = \int_0^2 \left(-4x^3 - 8x^2 + 32x\right) dx \\[1ex] & = \left[-x^4 - \frac{8}{3} x^3 + 16x^2\right]_0^2 = \frac{80}{3} \end{aligned}$$

and

$$\begin{aligned} M_x & = \iint_D y\,\rho(x,y)\,dx\,dy \\[1ex] & = \int_{x=0}^2 \int_{y=x}^{4-x} y \left(2x + 3y + 2\right) dy\,dx \\[1ex] & = \int_0^2 \left[xy^2 + y^3 + y^2\right]_{y=x}^{4-x} \, dx \\[1ex] & = \int_0^2 \left(-2x^3 + 4x^2 - 40x + 80\right) dx \\[1ex] & = \left[-\frac{x^4}{2}+\frac{4x^3}{3}-20x^2+80x\right]_0^2 = \frac{248}{3} \end{aligned}$$

Finally, the center of mass is

$$\left( \frac{M_y}m, \frac{M_x}m \right) = \left( \frac{\frac{80}{3}}{\frac{112}{3}}, \frac{\frac{248}{3}}{\frac{112}{3}} \right) = \left( \frac{5}{7}, \frac{31}{14} \right)$$

:::
