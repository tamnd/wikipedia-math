# Join (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Join"}

*Image: Join.svg.* Geometric join of two [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860). The original spaces are shown in green and blue. The join is a three-dimensional solid, a [disphenoid](wikipedia://en/8476166), in gray.

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a field of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **join** of two [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) $A$ and $B$, often denoted by $A\ast B$ or $A\star B$, is a topological space formed by taking the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/1564380) of the two spaces, and attaching line segments joining every point in $A$ to every point in $B$. The join of a space $A$ with itself is denoted by $A^{\star 2} := A\star A$. The join is defined in slightly different ways in different contexts.

:::

## Geometric sets {#Geometric_sets}

If $A$ and $B$ are subsets of the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$, then:[^o3]p. 1

> $$A\star B\ :=\ \{ t\cdot a + (1-t)\cdot b ~|~ a\in A, b\in B, t\in [0,1]\},$$

that is, the set of all line-segments between a point in $A$ and a point in $B$.

Some authors[^o4]p. 5 restrict the definition to subsets that are *joinable*: any two different line-segments, connecting a point of A to a point of B, meet in at most a common endpoint (that is, they do not intersect in their interior). Every two subsets can be made "joinable". For example, if $A$ is in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $B$ is in $\mathbb{R}^m$, then $A\times\{ 0^m \}\times\{0\}$ and $\{0^n \}\times B\times\{1\}$ are joinable in $\mathbb{R}^{n+m+1}$. The figure above shows an example for m=n=1, where $A$ and $B$ are line-segments.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- The join of two [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) is a simplex: the join of an *n*-dimensional and an *m*-dimensional simplex is an (*m*+*n*+1)-dimensional simplex. Some special cases are:
  - The join of two disjoint points is an interval (*m*=*n*=0).
  - The join of a point and an interval is a triangle (m=0, n=1).
  - The join of two line segments is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to a solid [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) or [disphenoid](wikipedia://en/8476166), illustrated in the figure above right (*m*=*n*=1).
  - The join of a point and an (*n*-1)-dimensional [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) is an *n*-dimensional simplex.
- The join of a point and a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) (or any [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471)) is a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045), like the join of a point and square is a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455). The join of a point and a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is a [cubic pyramid](wikipedia://en/36537625).
- The join of a point and a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) is a [cone](wikipedia://en/782427), and the join of a point and a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) is a [hypercone](wikipedia://en/8062003).

:::

## Topological spaces {#Topological_spaces}

If $A$ and $B$ are any topological spaces, then:

$$A\star B\ :=\ A\sqcup_{p_0}(A\times B \times [0,1])\sqcup_{p_1}B,$$

where the cylinder $A\times B \times [0,1]$ is [attached](wikipedia://en/1240842) to the original spaces $A$ and $B$ along the natural projections of the faces of the cylinder:

$${A\times B\times \{0\}} \xrightarrow{p_0} A,$$

$${A\times B\times \{1\}} \xrightarrow{p_1} B.$$

Usually it is implicitly assumed that $A$ and $B$ are non-empty, in which case the definition is often phrased a bit differently: instead of attaching the faces of the cylinder $A\times B \times [0,1]$ to the spaces $A$ and $B$, these faces are simply collapsed in a way suggested by the attachment projections $p_1,p_2$: we form the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213)

$$A\star B\ :=\ (A\times B \times [0,1] )/ \sim,$$

where the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) $\sim$ is generated by

$$(a, b_1, 0) \sim (a, b_2, 0) \quad\mbox{for all } a \in A \mbox{ and } b_1,b_2 \in B,$$

$$(a_1, b, 1) \sim (a_2, b, 1) \quad\mbox{for all } a_1,a_2 \in A \mbox{ and } b \in B.$$

At the endpoints, this collapses $A\times B\times \{0\}$ to $A$ and $A\times B\times \{1\}$ to $B$.

If $A$ and $B$ are bounded subsets of the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$, and $A\subseteq U$ and $B \subseteq V$, where $U, V$ are disjoint subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^n$ such that the dimension of their [affine hull](wikipedia://en/3416209) is $\dim U + \dim V + 1$ (e.g. two non-intersecting non-parallel lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$), then the topological definition reduces to the geometric definition, that is, the "geometric join" is homeomorphic to the "topological join":*[^o22]*p. 75

> $$\big((A\times B \times [0,1] )/ \sim\big) \simeq \{ t\cdot a + (1-t)\cdot b ~|~ a\in A, b\in B, t\in [0,1]\}$$

## Abstract simplicial complexes {#Abstract_simplicial_complexes}

If $A$ and $B$ are any [abstract simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/723105), then their **join** is an abstract simplicial complex defined as follows:*[^o22]*p. 74

- The vertex set $V(A\star B)$ is a [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of $V(A)$ and $V( B)$.
- The simplices of $A\star B$ are all [disjoint unions](wikipedia://en/188946) of a simplex of $A$ with a simplex of $B$: $A\star B := \{ a\sqcup b: a\in A, b\in B \}$ (in the special case in which $V(A)$ and $V( B)$ are disjoint, the join is simply $\{ a\cup b: a\in A, b\in B \}$).

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- Suppose $A = \{ \emptyset, \{a\} \}$ and $B = \{\emptyset, \{b\} \}$, that is, two sets with a single point. Then $A \star B = \{ \emptyset, \{a\}, \{b\}, \{a,b\} \}$, which represents a line-segment. Note that the vertex sets of A and B are disjoint; otherwise, we should have made them disjoint. For example, $A^{\star 2} = A \star A = \{ \emptyset, \{a_1\}, \{a_2\}, \{a_1,a_2\} \}$ where a<sub>1</sub> and a<sub>2</sub> are two copies of the single element in V(A). Topologically, the result is the same as $A \star B$ - a line-segment.
- Suppose $A = \{ \emptyset, \{a\} \}$ and $B = \{\emptyset, \{b\}, \{c\}, \{b,c\} \}$. Then $A \star B = P(\{a,b,c\})$, which represents a triangle.
- Suppose $A = \{ \emptyset, \{a\}, \{b\} \}$ and $B = \{\emptyset, \{c\}, \{d\} \}$, that is, two sets with two discrete points. then $A\star B$ is a complex with facets $\{a,c\}, \{b,c\}, \{a,d\}, \{b,d\}$, which represents a "square".

The combinatorial definition is equivalent to the topological definition in the following sense:*[^o22]*p. 77 for every two abstract simplicial complexes $A$ and $B$, $||A\star B||$ is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to $||A||\star ||B||$, where $||X||$ denotes any [geometric realization](wikipedia://en/723105#Geometric_realization) of the complex $X$.

:::

## Maps {#Maps}

Given two maps $f:A_1\to A_2$ and $g:B_1\to B_2$, their join $f\star g:A_1\star B_1 \to A_2\star B_2$ is defined based on the representation of each point in the join $A_1\star B_1$ as $t\cdot a +(1-t)\cdot b$, for some $a\in A_1, b\in B_1$:*[^o22]*p. 77

> $$f\star g ~(t\cdot a +(1-t)\cdot b) ~~=~~ t\cdot f(a) + (1-t)\cdot g(b)$$

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

The [**cone**](wikipedia://en/782162) of a topological space $X$, denoted $CX$ , is a join of $X$ with a single point.

The [**suspension**](wikipedia://en/1251702) of a topological space $X$, denoted $SX$ , is a join of $X$ with $S^0$ (the 0-dimensional [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), or, the [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061) with two points).

:::

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Commutativity {#Commutativity}

The join of two spaces is commutative [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660), i.e. $A\star B\cong B\star A$.

### Associativity {#Associativity}

It is *not* true that the join operation defined above is associative up to homeomorphism for arbitrary topological spaces. However, for [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) $A, B, C$ we have $(A\star B)\star C \cong A\star(B\star C).$ Therefore, one can define the *k*-times join of a space with itself, $A^{*k} := A * \cdots * A$ (*k* times).

It is possible to define a different join operation $A\; \hat{\star}\;B$ which uses the same underlying set as $A\star B$ but a different topology, and this operation is associative for *all* topological spaces. For locally compact Hausdorff spaces $A$ and $B$, the joins $A\star B$ and $A \;\hat{\star}\;B$ coincide.[^o18]

### Homotopy equivalence {#Homotopy_equivalence}

If $A$ and $A'$ are [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence), then $A\star B$ and $A'\star B$ are homotopy equivalent too.*[^o22]*p. 77

### Reduced join {#Reduced_join}

Given basepointed [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) $(A, a_0)$ and $(B, b_0)$, the "reduced join"

$$\frac{A\star B}{A \star \{b_0\} \cup \{a_0\} \star B}$$

is homeomorphic to the reduced [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702)

> $$\Sigma(A\wedge B)$$

of the [smash product](wikipedia://en/698010). Consequently, since ${A \star \{b_0\} \cup \{a_0\} \star B}$ is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414), there is a [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence)

$$A\star B\simeq \Sigma(A\wedge B).$$

This equivalence establishes the isomorphism $\widetilde{H}_n(A\star B)\cong H_{n-1}(A\wedge B)\ \bigl( =H_{n-1}(A\times B / A\vee B)\bigr)$.

### Homotopical connectivity {#Homotopical_connectivity}

Given two [triangulable spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulable_space) **$A, B$**, the [homotopical connectivity](wikipedia://en/6519310) ($\eta_{\pi}$) of their join is at least the sum of connectivities of its parts:*[^o22]*p. 81

- **$\eta_{\pi}(A*B) \geq \eta_{\pi}(A)+\eta_{\pi}(B)$**.

As an example, let **$A = B = S^0$** be a set of two disconnected points. There is a 1-dimensional hole between the points, so $\eta_{\pi}(A)=\eta_{\pi}(B)=1$. The join **$A * B$** is a square, which is homeomorphic to a circle that has a 2-dimensional hole, so $\eta_{\pi}(A * B)=2$. The join of this square with a third copy of $S^0$ is a [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), which is homeomorphic to **$S^2$**, whose hole is 3-dimensional. In general, the join of *n* copies of **$S^0$** is homeomorphic to **$S^{n-1}$** and $\eta_{\pi}(S^{n-1})=n$.

:::

## Deleted join {#Deleted_join}

The **deleted join** of an abstract complex *A* is an abstract complex containing all [disjoint unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjoint_unions) of *disjoint* faces of *A*:*[^o22]*p. 112

> $$A^{*2}_{\Delta} := \{ a_1\sqcup a_2: a_1,a_2\in A, a_1\cap a_2 = \emptyset \}$$

::: {#o25 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- Suppose $A = \{ \emptyset, \{a\} \}$ (a single point). Then $A^{*2}_{\Delta} := \{ \emptyset, \{a_1\}, \{a_2\} \}$, that is, a discrete space with two disjoint points (recall that $A^{\star 2} =\{ \emptyset, \{a_1\}, \{a_2\}, \{a_1,a_2\} \}$ = an interval).
- Suppose $A = \{ \emptyset, \{a\} ,\{b\}\}$ (two points). Then $A^{*2}_{\Delta}$ is a complex with facets $\{a_1, b_2\}, \{a_2, b_1\}$ (two disjoint edges).
- Suppose $A = \{ \emptyset, \{a\} ,\{b\}, \{a,b\}\}$ (an edge). Then $A^{*2}_{\Delta}$ is a complex with facets $\{a_1,b_1\}, \{a_1, b_2\}, \{a_2, b_1\}, \{a_2,b_2\}$ (a square). Recall that $A^{\star 2}$ represents a solid tetrahedron.
- Suppose *A* represents an (*n*-1)-dimensional simplex (with *n* vertices). Then the join $A^{\star 2}$ is a (*2n-*1)-dimensional simplex (with 2*n* vertices): it is the set of all points (x<sub>1</sub>,...,x<sub>2n</sub>) with non-negative coordinates such that x<sub>1</sub>+...+x<sub>2n</sub>=1. The deleted join $A^{*2}_{\Delta}$ can be regarded as a subset of this simplex: it is the set of all points (x<sub>1</sub>,...,x<sub>2n</sub>) in that simplex, such that the only nonzero coordinates are some *k* coordinates in x<sub>1</sub>,..,x<sub>n</sub>, and the complementary n-k coordinates in x<sub>n+1</sub>,...,x<sub>2n</sub>.

:::

::: {#o27 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

The deleted join operation commutes with the join. That is, for every two abstract complexes *A* and *B*:*[^o22]*

> $$(A*B)^{*2}_{\Delta} = (A^{*2}_{\Delta}) * (B^{*2}_{\Delta})$$

*Proof*. Each simplex in the left-hand-side complex is of the form $(a_1 \sqcup b_1) \sqcup (a_2\sqcup b_2)$, where $a_1,a_2\in A, b_1,b_2\in B$, and $(a_1 \sqcup b_1), (a_2\sqcup b_2)$ are disjoint. Due to the properties of a disjoint union, the latter condition is equivalent to: $a_1,a_2$ are disjoint and $b_1,b_2$ are disjoint.

Each simplex in the right-hand-side complex is of the form $(a_1 \sqcup a_2) \sqcup (b_1\sqcup b_2)$, where $a_1,a_2\in A, b_1,b_2\in B$, and $a_1,a_2$ are disjoint and $b_1,b_2$ are disjoint. So the sets of simplices on both sides are exactly the same. □

In particular, the deleted join of the n-dimensional simplex $\Delta^n$ with itself is the n-dimensional [crosspolytope](wikipedia://en/716401), which is homeomorphic to the n-dimensional sphere $S^n$.*[^o22]*

:::

::: {#o29 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

### Generalization {#Generalization}

The **n-fold k-wise deleted join** of a simplicial complex A is defined as:

> $$A^{*n}_{\Delta(k)} := \{ a_1\sqcup a_2 \sqcup\cdots \sqcup a_n: a_1,\cdots,a_n \text{ are k-wise disjoint faces of } A \},$$
>
> where "k-wise disjoint" means that every subset of *k* have an empty intersection.

In particular, the *n*-fold *n*-wise deleted join contains all disjoint unions of *n* faces whose intersection is empty, and the *n*-fold 2-wise deleted join is smaller: it contains only the disjoint unions of *n* faces that are pairwise-disjoint. The 2-fold 2-wise deleted join is just the simple deleted join defined above.

The *n*-fold 2-wise deleted join of a [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061) with *m* points is called the (*m*,*n*)-[chessboard complex](wikipedia://en/73384654).

:::
