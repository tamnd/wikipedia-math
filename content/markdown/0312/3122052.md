# Schnyder's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schnyder's theorem"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), **Schnyder's theorem** is a characterization of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) in terms of the [order dimension](wikipedia://en/3121852) of their [incidence posets](wikipedia://en/44460457). It is named after Walter Schnyder, who published its proof in 1989.

:::

The incidence poset $P(G)$ of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) $G$ with [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) set $V$ and [edge](wikipedia://en/12401) set $E$ is the [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) of [height](wikipedia://en/348973#Height_and_width) 2 that has $V \cup E$ as its elements. In this partial order, there is an order relation $x < y$ when $x$ is a vertex, $y$ is an edge, and $x$ is one of the two endpoints of $y$.

The order dimension of a partial order is the smallest number of total orderings whose intersection is the given partial order; such a set of orderings is called a *realizer* of the partial order. Schnyder's theorem states that a graph $G$ is planar if and only if the order dimension of $P(G)$ is at most three.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

## Extensions {#Extensions}

This theorem has been generalized by (txt) to a tight bound on the dimension of the height-three partially ordered sets formed analogously from the vertices, edges and faces of a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron), or more generally of an embedded planar graph: in both cases, the order dimension of the poset is at most four. However, this result cannot be generalized to higher-dimensional [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885), as there exist four-dimensional polytopes whose [face lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_lattice) have unbounded order dimension.

Even more generally, for [abstract simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/723105), the order dimension of the face poset of the complex is at most $1 + d$, where $d$ is the minimum dimension of a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) in which the complex has a geometric realization ().

:::

## Other graphs {#Other_graphs}

As Schnyder observes, the incidence poset of a graph *G* has order dimension two if and only if the graph is a path or a subgraph of a path. For, when an incidence poset has order dimension two, its only possible realizer consists of two total orders that (when restricted to the graph's vertices) are the reverse of each other. Any other two orders would have an intersection that includes an order relation between two vertices, which is not allowed for incidence posets. For these two orders on the vertices, an edge between consecutive vertices can be included in the ordering by placing it immediately following the later of the two edge endpoints, but no other edges can be included.

If a graph can be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) with four colors, then its incidence poset has order dimension at most four (Schnyder 1989).

The incidence poset of a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) on *n* vertices has order dimension $\Theta(\log\log n)$ (Spencer 1971).
