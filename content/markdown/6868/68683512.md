# Vladimir Bogachev

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vladimir Bogachev"}

**Vladimir Igorevich Bogachev** (Владимир Игоревич Богачёв; born in 1961) is an eminent Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Full Professor of the Department of Mechanics and Mathematics of the [Lomonosov Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Moscow_State_University). He is an expert in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [infinite-dimensional analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-dimensional_analysis) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) arising in mathematical physics. [^o2][^o3] His research was distinguished by several awards including the medal and the prize of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union) (1990); Award of the [Japan Society for the Promotion of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Society_for_the_Promotion_of_Science) (2000); the Doob Lecture of the [Bernoulli Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_Society_for_Mathematical_Statistics_and_Probability) (2017);[^o4] and the [Kolmogorov Prize](wikipedia://en/73812406) of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) (2018).[^o5]

:::

Vladimir Bogachev is one of the most cited Russian mathematicians. He is the author of more than 200 publications and 12 monographs. His total citation index by [MathSciNet](wikipedia://en/2580683) is 2960, with [h-index](wikipedia://en/3604693)=23 (by September 2021).[^o6]

## Biography {#Biography}

Bogachev graduated with honours from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) (1983). In 1986, he received his PhD ([Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) in Russia) under the supervision of Prof. O. G. Smolyanov.

## Scientific contributions {#Scientific_contributions}

In 1984, V. Bogachev resolved three [Aronszajn](wikipedia://en/13496987)'s problems on [infinite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-dimensional) [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) and answered a famous question of [I. M. Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) posed about 25 years before that. In 1992, Vladimir Bogachev proved T. Pitcher’s conjecture (stated in 1961) on the [differentiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiability) of the [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) of [diffusion processes](wikipedia://en/1667059). In 1995, he proved (with [Michael Röckner](wikipedia://en/31709769)) the famous Shigekawa conjecture on the [absolute continuity](wikipedia://en/252670) of [invariant measures](wikipedia://en/7635201) of diffusion processes. In 1999, in a joint work with [Sergio Albeverio](wikipedia://en/28869024) and Röckner, Professor Bogachev resolved the well-known problem of [S. R. S. Varadhan](wikipedia://en/9638038) on the uniqueness of [stationary distributions](wikipedia://en/2524727), which had remained open for about 20 years.

A remarkable achievement of Vladimir Bogachev is the recently obtained (2021) answer to the question of [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) (posed in 1931) on the uniqueness of the solution to the [Cauchy problem](wikipedia://en/1725027): it is shown that the Cauchy problem with a unit [diffusion coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_coefficient) and locally bounded drift has a unique probabilistic solution on $\mathbb{R}^1$, and in $\mathbb{R}^{>1}$ this is not true even for smooth drift.[^o11]
