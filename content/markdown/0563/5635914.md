# Robert Wald

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Wald"}

**Robert M. Wald** (; born June 29, 1947, in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City)) is an American [theoretical](wikipedia://en/19594028) [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). He studies general relativity, black holes, and quantum gravity and has written textbooks on these subjects.

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

He is the son of the mathematician and statistician [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430) and great-grandson of the chief rabbi [Moshe Shmuel Glasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Shmuel_Glasner). Wald's parents died in a plane crash when he was three years old.[^o4] He earned his bachelor's degree from Columbia University in 1968 and his PhD in physics from Princeton University in 1972,[^o5] under the supervision of [John Archibald Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler). His doctoral dissertation was titled *Nonspherical Gravitational Collapse and Black Hole Uniqueness*.[^o6]

## Career and contributions {#Career_and_contributions}

Between 1972 and 1974, Robert Wald worked as a research associate in physics at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park).[^o6] He then moved to the University of Chicago, spending two years as a postdoctoral fellow before joining the faculty in 1976.[^o23] He wanted to move to Chicago in order to work with [Robert Geroch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Geroch) and other specialists in gravitation.[^o8]

In 1977, Wald published a popular-science book titled *Space, Time, and Gravity: The Theory of the Big Bang and Black Holes* explaining Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity, and its implications in cosmology and astrophysics. The book also gives a survey of what was then ongoing research on [gravitational collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_collapse) and [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650). This book grew out of a series of lectures Wald gave as part of the Compton Lectures at the University of Chicago in the spring of 1976.[^o9] The Compton Lectures, given every Spring and Fall quarter, are intended to explain notable advances in the physical sciences to members of the general public.[^o10]

He published the textbook *[General Relativity](wikipedia://en/9023795)* in 1984. Aimed at beginning graduate students, it covers [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276), the [variational principles](wikipedia://en/491097), the [initial-value formulation](wikipedia://en/13276958), (exact) gravitational waves, singularities, [Penrose diagrams](wikipedia://en/681666), [Hawking radiation](wikipedia://en/173724), and [black-hole thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/339350).[^o11] It is well-received,[^o12][^o13] but has become outdated by the early twenty-first century.[^o14]

Wald has taught first-year graduate courses covering a broad range of topics, including classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, and electromagnetism. He has also taught courses on [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), his specialty, at both introductory and advanced levels. A particularly effective teacher, he received the Graduate Teaching Award from the University of Chicago in 1997.[^o15]

Wald investigates black holes and their thermodynamics, and gravitational radiation-reaction (or self-force).[^o23] During the early 1970s, Wald and a number of other theoretical physicists studied the external [electric field](wikipedia://en/41092) lines of black holes,[^o16][^o17] and subsequently the perturbations of black holes,[^o18] following the publication of the Teukolsky equations by [Saul Teukolsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Teukolsky) in 1972.[^o17] Wald has published over 100 research papers on general relativity and [quantum field theory in curved spacetime](wikipedia://en/1057601), many of which have been cited by hundreds of subsequent papers.[^o19] In 1993, he described the Wald entropy of a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650), which is dependent simply on the area of the event horizon of the black hole.[^o20]

He organized The Symposium on Black Holes and Relativistic Stars in 1996, in honor of the late Nobel Prize-winning theoretical astrophysicist [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](wikipedia://en/145319). Distinguished speakers of this event included [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148), [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193) and [Martin Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Rees). Although the event charged an entrance fee of \$100, Wald made sure all University of Chicago students were admitted free of charge.[^o15] Chandrasekhar founded a research group on general relativity at the University of Chicago, which includes Wald, [James Hartle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hartle) and Robert Geroch.[^o21] Although Wald and Chandrasekhar never collaborated on any particular research projects, the two developed warm relations.[^o8]

He became a fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) (APS) in 1996[^o6] and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2001.[^o22] He received the [Einstein Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_Prize_%28APS%29) from the APS Division of Gravitational Physics in 2017 for "the discovery of the general formula for black hole entropy, and for developing a rigorous formulation of quantum field theory in curved spacetime."[^o23]

Wald delivered a public lecture at the [University of Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alabama) on October 27, 2015, titled "The Formulation of General Relativity," celebrating the centennial of Einstein's theory.[^o24] Wald is a member of the [LIGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO) group at the University of Chicago, headed by astrophysicist [Daniel Holz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Holz). The Laser Interferometry Gravitational-wave Observatory detected [gravitational waves](wikipedia://en/8111079) for the first time in 2015, one century after Einstein predicted their existence.[^o25]

In 2025 he received the [Albert Einstein Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Medal) from the [Albert Einstein Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Society) in Switzerland for his studies of classical and semi-classical gravity.[^o26] In the same year, Wald, [Gary Gibbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Gibbons), [Gary Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Horowitz), and [Roy Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Kerr) were awarded the [Dirac Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_%28ICTP%29) from the [International Center for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in Italy for their landmark contributions to the study of gravity, in both the classical and quantum regimes.[^o27]
