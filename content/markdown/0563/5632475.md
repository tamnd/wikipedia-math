# IA automorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="IA automorphism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), an **IA automorphism** of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) that acts as identity on the [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization).[^o2] The abelianization of a group is its [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) by its [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847). An IA automorphism is thus an automorphism that sends each [coset](wikipedia://en/98759) of the commutator subgroup to itself.

:::

The IA automorphisms of a group form a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of the automorphism group. Every [inner automorphism](wikipedia://en/61839) is an IA automorphism, since inner automorphisms act trivially on the abelianization and the group of inner automorphisms is normal in the full automorphism group of any group.[^o3]

For free groups, the study of IA automorphisms is important in understanding the structure of the automorphism group and its subgroups, as these automorphisms often preserve significant algebraic properties of the group.[^o4] These automorphisms are particularly studied in relation to the lower central series and the behavior of commutator subgroups, providing insight into the intrinsic symmetries of free groups.
