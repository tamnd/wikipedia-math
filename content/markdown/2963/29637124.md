# Walter theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walter theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Walter theorem**, [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by (txt), describes the [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) whose [Sylow 2-subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_subgroup) is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974). (Bender 1970) used [Bender's method](wikipedia://en/28055690) to give a simpler proof.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Walter's theorem states that if *G* is a finite group whose 2-Sylow subgroups are abelian, then *G*/*O*(*G*) has a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of odd [index](wikipedia://en/230581) that is a product of groups each of which is a [2-group](wikipedia://en/24664) or one of the [simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) PSL<sub>2</sub>(*q*) for *q* = 2<sup>*n*</sup> or *q* = 3 or 5 [mod](wikipedia://en/20087) 8, or the [Janko group J1](wikipedia://en/10796779), or [Ree groups](wikipedia://en/1739637) <sup>2</sup>*G*<sub>2</sub>(3<sup>2*n*+1</sup>). (Here *O*(*G*) denotes the unique largest normal subgroup of *G* of odd order.)

The original statement of Walter's theorem did not quite identify the Ree groups, but only stated that the corresponding groups have a similar subgroup structure as Ree groups. (txt) and (Bombieri Odlyzko Hunt 1980) later showed that they are all Ree groups, and (Enguehard 1986) gave a unified exposition of this result.

:::
