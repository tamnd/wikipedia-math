# Clark Barwick

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Clark Barwick"}

**Clark Edward Barwick** (born January 9, 1980) is an American mathematician and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_the_United_Kingdom) of pure mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). His research is centered around [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), [algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/598500), [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013), and related areas.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Barwick grew up in [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina), and in 2001 completed his [BS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill).[^o4][^o5] Barwick was then a graduate student at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), and received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics in 2005 under the direction of Tony Pantev.

## Career {#Career}

Barwick held [postdoctoral fellowships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_Fellow) at the [Mathematisches Institut Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) (2005–2006) and at the [Matematisk Institutt, Universitetet i Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) (2006–2007).[^o5] Barwick spent the year 2007–2008 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), and from 2008–2010 was a [Benjamin Peirce](wikipedia://en/364270) Lecturer at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). In 2010 Barwick became an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), and in 2013 he became the Cecil and Ida Green Career Development Assistant Professor of Mathematics. In 2015 Barwick was a [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) visiting professor at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) and was promoted to Cecil and Ida Green Career Development [Associate Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) of Mathematics at MIT, a position which he held until he became a reader at the University of Edinburgh in 2017.[^o7][^o8] In 2020, Barwick was promoted to a professor at the University of Edinburgh.[^o9]

## Research and notable works {#Research_and_notable_works}

A theme in Barwick's work is the homotopy theory of higher categories. In his early career, he frequently collaborated with [Dan Kan](wikipedia://en/2290155); much of their work was concerned with models for the homotopy theory of homotopy theories. In his joint work with Chris Schommer-Pries, Barwick proved a unicity theorem for the homotopy theory of [(∞,*n*)-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28∞,_n%29-category).

Barwick has also made contributions to algebraic *K*-theory. In particular, Barwick defined higher-categorical generalizations of [Waldhausen categories](wikipedia://en/12727767) and Waldhausen's [*S*-construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-construction) and used these to extend [Waldhausen's](wikipedia://en/20163961) *K*-theory to the setting of [(∞,1)-categories](wikipedia://en/28662219). Using this new theory, he proved the Theorem of the Heart for Waldhausen *K*-theory. In joint work with John Rognes, he generalized [Quillen's](wikipedia://en/435519) [*Q*-construction](wikipedia://en/41403415) to the higher-categorical setting, providing higher-categorical generalizations of [Quillen's Theorem B](wikipedia://en/41445293) as well as Quillen's dévissage argument in the process. Much of his recent work has concerned [equivariant algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/18887838) and [equivariant homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_algebraic_topoloy). Barwick won the 2019 [Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) for his paper "On the algebraic *K*-theory of higher categories" where he "proves that Waldhausen's algebraic K-theory is the universal homology theory for ∞-categories, and uses this universality to reprove the major fundamental theorems of the subject in this new context."[^o11][^o12]

In 2019 Barwick with his student Haine introduced the theory of [pyknotic objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyknotic_object). Which was published coincidentally and is very closely related to that of [condensed sets](wikipedia://en/68011960), with the main differences being set-theoretic in nature: pyknotic theory depends on a choice of [Grothendieck universes](wikipedia://en/1072144), whereas condensed mathematics can be developed strictly within [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC).[^o13]
