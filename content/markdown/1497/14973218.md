# Toroid

*Image: Toroid by Zureks.svg.* A toroid using a square.

*Image: Torus.png.* A [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) is a type of toroid.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toroid"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **toroid** is a [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) with a hole in the middle. The [axis of revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_revolution) passes through the hole and so does not intersect the surface. For example, when a rectangle is rotated around an axis parallel to one of its edges, then a hollow rectangle-section ring is produced. If the revolved figure is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), then the object is called a *[torus](wikipedia://en/74800)*.

:::

The term *toroid* is also used to describe a [toroidal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/24804975). In this context a toroid need not be circular and may have any number of holes. A *g*-holed *toroid* can be seen as approximating the surface of a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) having a [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28Mathematics%29) [genus](wikipedia://en/61891), *g*, of 1 or greater. The [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) χ of a *g* holed toroid is 2(1−*g*).[^o3]

The torus is an example of a toroid, which is the surface of a [doughnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doughnut). Doughnuts are an example of a [solid torus](wikipedia://en/1251473) created by rotating a disk, and are not toroids.

Toroidal structures occur in both natural and synthetic materials.[^o4]

## Equations {#Equations}

A toroid is specified by the radius of revolution *R* measured from the center of the section rotated. For symmetrical sections volume and surface of the body may be computed (with circumference *C* and area *A* of the section):

### Square toroid {#Square_toroid}

The volume (V) and surface area (S) of a toroid are given by the following equations, where A is the area of the square section of side, and R is the radius of revolution.

$$V = 2 \pi R A$$

$$S = 2 \pi R C$$

### Circular toroid {#Circular_toroid}

The volume (V) and surface area (S) of a toroid are given by the following equations, where r is the radius of the circular section, and R is the radius of the overall shape.

$$V = 2 \pi^2 r^2 R$$

$$S = 4 \pi^2 r R$$

[Pappus's centroid theorem](wikipedia://en/509331) generalizes the formulas here to arbitrary surfaces of revolution.
