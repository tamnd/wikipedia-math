# Mandart inellipse

*Image: Mandart inellipse - resized.svg.* solid #333333 solid red solid blue solid lime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mandart inellipse"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Mandart inellipse** of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) that is [inscribed within](wikipedia://en/43943006) the triangle, [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to its sides at the contact points of its [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) (which are also the vertices of the [extouch triangle](wikipedia://en/11125142) and the endpoints of the [splitters](wikipedia://en/14961391)).[^o2] The Mandart inellipse is named after H. Mandart, who studied it in two papers published in the late 19th century.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Parameters {#Parameters}

As an [inconic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inconic), the Mandart inellipse is described by the [parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parameters)

$$x:y:z=\frac{a}{b+c-a}:\frac{b}{a+c-b}:\frac{c}{a+b-c}$$

where *a*, *b*, and *c* are sides of the given triangle.

## Related points {#Related_points}

The center of the Mandart inellipse is the [mittenpunkt](wikipedia://en/14941140) of the triangle. The three lines connecting the triangle vertices to the opposite points of tangency all meet in a single point, the [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) of the triangle.[^o3]
