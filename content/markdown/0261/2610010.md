# Quadratic differential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quadratic differential"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quadratic differential** on a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) is a section of the [symmetric square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_square) of the holomorphic [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600). If the section is [holomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic), then the quadratic differential is said to be holomorphic. The vector space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on a Riemann surface has a natural interpretation as the cotangent space to the Riemann moduli space, or [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661).

:::

## Local form {#Local_form}

Each quadratic differential on a domain $U$ in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) may be written as $f(z) \,dz \otimes dz$, where $z$ is the complex variable, and $f$ is a complex-valued function on $U$. Such a "local" quadratic differential is holomorphic if and only if $f$ is [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110). Given a chart $\mu$ for a general Riemann surface $R$ and a quadratic differential $q$ on $R$, the [pull-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pull-back) $(\mu^{-1})^*(q)$ defines a quadratic differential on a domain in the complex plane.

## Relation to abelian differentials {#Relation_to_abelian_differentials}

If $\omega$ is an [abelian differential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_differential) on a Riemann surface, then $\omega \otimes \omega$ is a quadratic differential.

## Singular Euclidean structure {#Singular_Euclidean_structure}

A holomorphic quadratic differential $q$ determines a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) $|q|$ on the complement of its zeroes. If $q$ is defined on a domain in the complex plane, and $q = f(z) \,dz \otimes dz$, then the associated Riemannian metric is $|f(z)|(dx^2 + dy^2)$, where $z = x + iy$. Since $f$ is holomorphic, the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) of this metric is zero. Thus, a holomorphic quadratic differential defines a flat metric on the complement of the set of $z$ such that $f(z) = 0$.
