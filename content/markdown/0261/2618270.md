# Gravitational instanton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gravitational instanton"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **gravitational instanton** is a four-dimensional [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_metric) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) satisfying the [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) [Einstein equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_equation). They are so named because they are analogues in [quantum theories of gravity](wikipedia://en/25312) of [instantons](wikipedia://en/296077) in [Yang&ndash;Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang&ndash;Mills_theory). In accordance with this analogy with [self-dual Yang–Mills instantons](wikipedia://en/296077#Yang–Mills_theory), gravitational instantons are usually assumed to look like four dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) at large distances, and to have a self-dual [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor). Mathematically, this means that they are asymptotically locally Euclidean (or perhaps asymptotically locally flat) [hyperkähler 4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/646120), and in this sense, they are special examples of [Einstein manifolds](wikipedia://en/644814). From a physical point of view, a gravitational instanton is a non-singular solution of the vacuum [Einstein equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_equation) with *positive-definite*, as opposed to [Lorentzian](wikipedia://en/287555), metric.

:::

There are many possible generalizations of the original conception of a gravitational instanton: for example one can allow gravitational instantons to have a nonzero [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992) or a Riemann tensor which is not self-dual. One can also relax the boundary condition that the metric is asymptotically Euclidean.

There are many methods for constructing gravitational instantons, including the [Gibbons&ndash;Hawking Ansatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbons&ndash;Hawking_Ansatz), [twistor theory](wikipedia://en/260836), and the [hyperkähler quotient](wikipedia://en/35171991) construction.

## Introduction {#Introduction}

Gravitational instantons are interesting, as they offer insights into the quantization of gravity. For example, positive definite asymptotically locally Euclidean metrics are needed as they obey the positive-action conjecture; actions that are unbounded below create divergence in the [quantum path integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation).

- A four-dimensional Ricci-flat [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538) manifold has anti-self-dual [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor) with respect to the complex orientation.
- Consequently, a simply-connected anti-self-dual gravitational instanton is a four-dimensional complete [hyperkähler manifold](wikipedia://en/646120).
- Gravitational instantons are analogous to [self-dual Yang–Mills instantons](wikipedia://en/296077).

Several distinctions can be made with respect to the structure of the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526), pertaining to flatness and self-duality. These include:

- Einstein (non-zero cosmological constant)
- Ricci flatness (vanishing Ricci tensor)
- Conformal flatness (vanishing Weyl tensor)
- Self-duality
- Anti-self-duality
- Conformally self-dual
- Conformally anti-self-dual

## Taxonomy {#Taxonomy}

By specifying the 'boundary conditions', i.e. the asymptotics of the metric 'at infinity' on a noncompact Riemannian manifold, gravitational instantons are divided into a few classes, such as **asymptotically locally Euclidean spaces** (ALE spaces), **asymptotically locally flat spaces** (ALF spaces).

They can be further characterized by whether the [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor) is self-dual, whether the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848) is self-dual, or neither; whether or not they are [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538); and various [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530), such as [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420), the [Hirzebruch signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirzebruch_signature) ([Pontryagin class](wikipedia://en/385944)), the [Rarita–Schwinger index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarita–Schwinger_index) (spin-3/2 index), or generally the [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349). The ability to support a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) (*i.e.* to allow consistent [Dirac spinors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor)) is another appealing feature.

## List of examples {#List_of_examples}

Eguchi *et al.* list a number of examples of gravitational instantons.[^o5] These include, among others:

- Flat space $\mathbb{R}^4$, the torus $\mathbb{T}^4$ and the Euclidean [de Sitter space](wikipedia://en/301666) $\mathbb{S}^4$, *i.e.* the standard metric on the [4-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782).
- The product of spheres $S^2\times S^2$.
- The [Schwarzschild metric](wikipedia://en/264606) $\mathbb{R}^2\times S^2$ and the [Kerr metric](wikipedia://en/456715) $\mathbb{R}^2\times S^2$.
- The Eguchi–Hanson instanton $T^*\mathbb{CP}(1)$, given below.
- The [Taub–NUT solution](wikipedia://en/16009385), given below.
- The [Fubini–Study metric](wikipedia://en/2574486) on the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) $\mathbb{CP}(2).$[^o6] Note that the complex projective plane does not support well-defined [Dirac spinors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor). That is, it is not a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303). It can be given a [spin<sup>c</sup> structure](wikipedia://en/79367906), however.
- The [Page space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_space), which exhibits an explicit Einstein metric on the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of two oppositely oriented [complex projective planes](wikipedia://en/737155) $\mathbb{CP}(2)\# \overline{\mathbb{CP}}(2)$.
- The Gibbons–Hawking multi-center metrics, given below.
- The [Taub-bolt metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taub-bolt_metric) $\mathbb{CP}(2)\setminus \{0\}$ and the rotating Taub-bolt metric. The "bolt" metrics have a cylindrical-type coordinate singularity at the origin, as compared to the "nut" metrics, which have a sphere coordinate singularity. In both cases, the coordinate singularity can be removed by switching to Euclidean coordinates at the origin.
- The [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116).
- The ALE (asymptotically locally Euclidean) anti-self-dual manifolds. Among these, the simply connected ones are all [hyper-Kähler](wikipedia://en/646120), and each one is asymptotic to a flat cone over $S^3=SU(2)$ modulo a finite subgroup. Each finite sub-group of $SU(2)$ actually occurs. The complete list of possibilities consists of the cyclic groups $\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}$ together with the inverse images of the [dihedral groups](wikipedia://en/100267), the [tetrahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_group), the [octahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_group), and the [icosahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_group) under the double cover $SU(2) \to SO(3)$. Note that $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ corresponds to the Eguchi–Hanson instanton, while for higher *k*, the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}$ corresponds to the Gibbons–Hawking multi-center metrics, each of which [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to the space obtained from the disjoint union of *k* copies of $T^*S^2$ by using the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109) $A_k$ as a [plumbing diagram](wikipedia://en/61352041).

This is a very incomplete list; there are many other possibilities, not all of which have been classified.

## Examples {#Examples}

It will be convenient to write the gravitational instanton solutions below using left-invariant 1-forms on the [three-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-sphere) **S**<sup>3</sup> (viewed as the group Sp(1) or SU(2)). These can be defined in terms of [Euler angles](wikipedia://en/411492) by

$$\begin{aligned} \sigma_1 &= \sin \psi \, d \theta - \cos \psi \sin \theta \, d \phi \\ \sigma_2 &= \cos \psi \, d \theta + \sin \psi \sin \theta \, d \phi \\ \sigma_3 &= d \psi + \cos \theta \, d \phi. \\ \end{aligned}$$

Note that $d\sigma_i + \sigma_j \wedge \sigma_k=0$ for $i,j,k=1,2,3$ cyclic.

### Taub–NUT metric {#Taub–NUT_metric}

$$ds^2 = \frac{1}{4} \frac{r+n}{r-n} dr^2 + \frac{r-n}{r+n} n^2 {\sigma_3}^2 + \frac{1}{4}(r^2 - n^2)({\sigma_1}^2 + {\sigma_2}^2)$$

### Eguchi–Hanson metric {#Eguchi–Hanson_metric}

The [Eguchi–Hanson space](wikipedia://en/29321898) is defined by a metric on the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) of the 2-sphere $T^*\mathbb{CP}(1)=T^*S^2$. This metric is

$$ds^2 = \left( 1 - \frac{a}{r^4} \right) ^{-1} dr^2 + \frac{r^2}{4} \left( 1 - \frac{a}{r^4} \right) {\sigma_3}^2 + \frac{r^2}{4} (\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2),$$

where $r \ge a^{1/4}$. This metric is smooth everywhere if it has no [conical singularity](wikipedia://en/54244#Conical) at $r \rightarrow a^{1/4}$, $\theta = 0, \pi$. For $a = 0$ this happens if $\psi$ has a period of $4\pi$, which gives a flat metric on **R**<sup>4</sup>; However, for $a \ne 0$ this happens if $\psi$ has a period of $2\pi$.

Asymptotically (i.e., in the limit $r \rightarrow \infty$) the metric looks like

$$ds^2 = dr^2 + \frac{r^2}{4} \sigma_3^2 + \frac{r^2}{4} (\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)$$

which naively seems as the flat metric on **R**<sup>4</sup>. However, for $a \ne 0$, $\psi$ has only half the usual periodicity, as we have seen. Thus the metric is asymptotically **R**<sup>4</sup> with the identification $\psi\, {\sim}\, \psi + 2\pi$, which is a [Z<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/52327) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of [SO(4)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%284%29), the rotation group of **R**<sup>4</sup>. Therefore, the metric is said to be asymptotically **R**<sup>4</sup>/**Z**<sub>2</sub>.

There is a transformation to another [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931), in which the metric looks like

$$ds^2 = \frac{1}{V(\mathbf{x})} ( d \psi + \boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot d \mathbf{x})^2 + V(\mathbf{x}) d \mathbf{x} \cdot d \mathbf{x},$$

where $\nabla V = \pm \nabla \times \boldsymbol{\omega}, \quad V = \sum_{i=1}^2 \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_i| }.$

(For a = 0, $V = \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|}$, and the new coordinates are defined as follows: one first defines $\rho=r^2/4$ and then parametrizes $\rho$, $\theta$ and $\phi$ by the **R**<sup>3</sup> coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, i.e. $\mathbf{x}=(\rho \sin \theta \cos \phi, \rho \sin \theta \sin \phi,\rho \cos\theta)$).

In the new coordinates, $\psi$ has the usual periodicity $\psi\ {\sim}\ \psi + 4\pi.$

One may replace V by

$$\quad V = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_i|}.$$

For some *n* points $\mathbf{x}_i$, *i* = 1, 2..., *n*. This gives a multi-center Eguchi–Hanson gravitational instanton, which is again smooth everywhere if the angular coordinates have the usual periodicities (to avoid [conical singularities](wikipedia://en/54244#Conical)). The asymptotic limit ($r\rightarrow \infty$) is equivalent to taking all $\mathbf{x}_i$ to zero, and by changing coordinates back to r, $\theta$ and $\phi$, and redefining $r\rightarrow r/\sqrt{n}$, we get the asymptotic metric

$$ds^2 = dr^2 + \frac{r^2}{4} \left({d\psi\over n} + \cos \theta \, d\phi\right)^2 + \frac{r^2}{4} [(\sigma_1^L)^2 + (\sigma_2^L)^2].$$

This is **R**<sup>4</sup>/**Z**<sub>*n*</sub> = **C**<sup>2</sup>/**Z**<sub>n</sub>, because it is **R**<sup>4</sup> with the angular coordinate $\psi$ replaced by $\psi/n$, which has the wrong periodicity ($4\pi/n$ instead of $4\pi$). In other words, it is **R**<sup>4</sup> identified under $\psi\ {\sim}\ \psi + 4\pi k/n$, or, equivalently, **C**<sup>2</sup> identified under *z*<sub>*i*</sub> ~ $e^{2\pi i k/n}$ *z*<sub>*i*</sub> for *i* = 1, 2.

To conclude, the multi-center Eguchi–Hanson geometry is a [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538) Ricci flat geometry which is asymptotically **C**<sup>2</sup>/**Z**<sub>n</sub>. According to [Yau's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabi&ndash;Yau_manifold) this is the only geometry satisfying these properties. Therefore, this is also the geometry of a **C**<sup>2</sup>/**Z**<sub>n</sub> [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) after its [conical singularity](wikipedia://en/54244#Conical) has been smoothed away by its "blow up" (i.e., deformation).[^o11]

### Gibbons–Hawking multi-centre metrics {#Gibbons–Hawking_multi-centre_metrics}

The Gibbons–Hawking multi-center metrics are given by[^o13][^o14]

$$ds^2 = \frac{1}{V(\mathbf{x})} ( d \tau + \boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot d \mathbf{x})^2 + V(\mathbf{x}) d \mathbf{x} \cdot d \mathbf{x},$$

where

$$\nabla V = \pm \nabla \times \boldsymbol{\omega}, \quad V = \varepsilon + 2M \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_i | }.$$

Here, $\epsilon = 1$ corresponds to multi-Taub–NUT, $\epsilon = 0$ and $k = 1$ is flat space, and $\epsilon = 0$ and $k = 2$ is the Eguchi–Hanson solution (in different coordinates).
