# Ernst Witt

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Witt"}

**Ernst Witt** (26 June 1911 – 3 July 1991) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), one of the leading [algebraists](wikipedia://en/18716923) of his time.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Witt was born on the island of [Alsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Als_%28island%29), then a part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). Shortly after his birth, his parents moved the family to China to work as missionaries,[^o5] and he did not return to Europe until he was nine.[^o5]

After his schooling, Witt went to the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). He joined the [NSDAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) (Nazi Party) and was an active party member.[^o6] Witt was awarded a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at the University of Göttingen in 1933 with a thesis titled: "Riemann-Roch theorem and zeta-Function in hypercomplexes"[^o8] (Riemann-Rochscher Satz und Zeta-Funktion im Hyperkomplexen) that was supervised by [Gustav Herglotz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Herglotz), with [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) suggesting the topic for the doctorate.[^o7] He became a qualified lecturer and gave guest lectures in Göttingen and [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg).[^o7] He was also associated with the team led by [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) who led his habilitation. In June 1936, he gave his habilitation lecture.[^o8]

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he joined a group of five mathematicians, recruited by [Wilhelm Fenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Fenner), and which included [Georg Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Aumann), [Alexander Aigner](wikipedia://en/53762405), [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller), Johann Friedrich Schultze and their leader professor [Wolfgang Franz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Franz_%28mathematician%29), to form the backbone of the new mathematical research department in the late 1930s that would eventually be called: Section IVc of [Cipher Department of the High Command of the Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipher_Department_of_the_High_Command_of_the_Wehrmacht) (abbr. OKW/Chi).[^o9][^o10]

From 1937 until 1979, he taught at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg).[^o11] He died in Hamburg in 1991, shortly after his 80th birthday.

## Work {#Work}

Witt's work has been highly influential. His invention of the [Witt vectors](wikipedia://en/5573710) clarifies and generalizes the structure of the [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423).[^o13] It has become fundamental to [*p*-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853).

Witt was the founder of the theory of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) over an arbitrary [field](wikipedia://en/10603).[^o14] He [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) several of the key results, including the [Witt cancellation theorem](wikipedia://en/4222668). He defined the [Witt ring](wikipedia://en/5578523#Ring_structure) of all quadratic forms over a field, now a central object in the theory.[^o15]

The [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](wikipedia://en/762696) is basic to the study of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944). In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the [Hasse–Witt matrix](wikipedia://en/3028181) of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) determines the cyclic [étale](wikipedia://en/3022771) coverings of degree *p* of a curve in [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*.

In the 1970s, Witt claimed that in 1940 he had discovered what would eventually be named the "[Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919)" many years before [John Leech](wikipedia://en/4548516) discovered it in 1965, but Witt did not publish his discovery and the details of exactly what he did are unclear.[^n1][^o17]
