# Faithful representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Faithful representation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in an area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), a **faithful representation** ρ of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $V$ is a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) in which different elements $g$ of $G$ are represented by distinct [linear mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_mapping) $\rho(g)$. In more abstract language, this means that the [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $\rho: G\to GL(V)$ is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) (or [one-to-one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective)).

:::

## Caveat {#Caveat}

While representations of $G$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ are *de facto* the same as $K[G]$-[modules](wikipedia://en/276410) (with $K[G]$ denoting the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223#Group_algebra_over_a_finite_group) of the group $G$), a faithful representation of $G$ is not necessarily a [faithful module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_module) for the group algebra. In fact each faithful $K[G]$-module is a faithful representation of $G$, but the [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) does not hold. Consider for example the natural representation of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_{n}$ in $n$ dimensions by [permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_matrices), which is certainly faithful. Here the [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) of the group is $n!$ while the $n \times n$ [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) form a vector space of [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) $n^{2}$. As soon as $n$ is at least 4, dimension counting means that some [linear dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_dependence) must occur between permutation matrices (since $24 > 16$); this relation means that the module for the group algebra is not faithful.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A representation $V$ of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) $G$ over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) $K$ of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero is faithful (as a representation) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) every [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) of $G$ occurs as a [subrepresentation](wikipedia://en/55943952) of $S^{n}V$ (the $n$-th symmetric power of the representation $V$) for a sufficiently high $n$. Also, $V$ is faithful (as a representation) if and only if every irreducible representation of $G$ occurs as a subrepresentation of

$$V^{\otimes n} = \underbrace{V \otimes V \otimes \cdots \otimes V}_{n\text{ times}}$$

(the $n$-th [tensor power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_power_of_representations) of the representation $V$) for a sufficiently high $n$.[^o5]

:::
