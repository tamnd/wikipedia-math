# Gilbert Strang

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gilbert Strang"}

**William Gilbert "Gil" Strang** (born November 27, 1934[^o34]) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions to [finite element theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_elements), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [wavelet analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelet_analysis) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). He has made many contributions to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) education, including publishing mathematics textbooks. Strang was the [MathWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MathWorks) Professor of Mathematics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o35] He taught [Linear Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_Algebra), Computational Science and Engineering, and Learning from Data. His lectures are freely available on MIT OpenCourseWare.

:::

Strang popularized the designation of the [Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank-nullity_theorem) as such.[^o3][^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

Gilbert Strang was born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) in 1934. His parents William and Mary Catherine Strang migrated to the USA from Scotland. He and his sister Vivian grew up in Washington DC and Cincinnati, and went to high school at Principia in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis).

Strang graduated from MIT in 1955 with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics. He then received a [Rhodes Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholar) to the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), where he received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) and [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts)[^o6] from [Balliol College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford) in 1957.

Strang earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1959 as a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Fellow, under the supervision of [Peter K. Henrici](wikipedia://en/31964476). His dissertation was titled "Difference Methods for [Mixed Boundary Value Problems](wikipedia://en/436779)".[^o7]

While at Oxford, Strang met his future wife Jillian Shannon, and they married in 1958. Following his Ph.D. at UCLA, they have lived in Wellesley, Massachusetts for almost all of his 62 years on the [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) faculty. The Strangs have three sons David, John, and Robert. They describe themselves as a very close-knit family. He retired on May 15, 2023 after giving his final linear algebra [lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecture) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o8]

Strang's teaching has focused on linear algebra which has helped the subject become essential for students of many majors. His video lectures are popular on YouTube and MIT OpenCourseWare. Strang founded Wellesley-Cambridge Press to publish *Introduction to Linear Algebra* (now in 6th edition) and ten other books.

Professor Strang's video lectures at MIT are open to everyone. They are watched worldwide (more than 20 million viewers). They include 3 popular courses and the final lecture in 2023[^o9].

Math 18.06 (Linear Algebra) & Math 18.06SC (with several problems solved by TAs)

Math 18.065 (Linear Algebra and Learning from Data) leading to deep learning and basic AI

Math 18.085 (Computational Science and Engineering)

## University positions {#University_positions}

*Image: HadamardConjectureMIT.png.* Strang demonstrates the [Hadamard conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_conjecture) at MIT, 2005

- [C. L. E. Moore instructor](wikipedia://en/21957255), MIT (1959–1961)
- [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO) Postdoctoral Fellow, [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) (1961–1962)
- Mathematics faculty, MIT (1962–2023, as a full professor from 1970)[^o11]
