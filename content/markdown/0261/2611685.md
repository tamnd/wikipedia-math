# Computable isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computable isomorphism"}

In [computability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computability_theory_%28computer_science%29) two sets $A, B$ of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) are **computably isomorphic** or **recursively isomorphic** if there exists a [total](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_function) [computable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computable) and [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) function $f \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ such that the image of $f$ restricted to $A\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ equals $B\subseteq \mathbb{N}$, i.e. $f(A) = B$.

:::

Further, two [numberings](wikipedia://en/2399097) $\nu$ and $\mu$ (of the same set of objects) are called **computably isomorphic** if there exists a computable bijection $f$ so that $\nu = \mu \circ f$. Computably isomorphic numberings induce the same notion of computability on a set.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Theorems"}

## Theorems {#Theorems}

By the [Myhill isomorphism theorem](wikipedia://en/2611629), the relation of computably isomorphic coincides with the relation of mutual [one-one reducibility](wikipedia://en/362983).[^o4]
