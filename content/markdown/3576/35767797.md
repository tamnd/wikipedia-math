# Nizam al-Din al-Nisaburi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nizam al-Din al-Nisaburi"}

**Nizam al-Din Hasan al-Nisaburi**, whose full name was **Nizam al-Din Hasan ibn Mohammad ibn Hossein Qumi Nishapuri** (d. 1328/29) (نظام الدین حسن نیشاپوری) was a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni)[^n1][^o4][^n2] Islamic [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i), [Ash'ari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash'ari) scholar, mathematician, astronomer, jurist, Qur'an exegete, and poet.

:::

## Family and education {#Family_and_education}

Nizam al-Din Hasan al-Nisaburi, who according to [genealogical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genealogical) information provided in his full name—Nizam al-Din Hasan ibn Mohammad ibn Hossein Qumi Nishapuri—had a grandfather from the city of [Qom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qom), was born in [Nishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishapur).

Little is known about Nīsābūrī's early life and education.[^n3] His early education was in the city of Nishapur, but he later moved to [Tabriz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabriz), the capital of [Il-Khanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il-Khanids) at the time.

Nīsābūrī studied under and worked with [Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_al-Din_al-Shirazi),[^n3] who was himself a student of [Nasir al-Din Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_al-Din_Tusi). He was one of the great scientists of [Maragheh observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh_observatory).

In 1304, Nīsābūrī arrived in Azerbaijan; by 1306 he was in Tabrīz, the largest city in Azerbaijan.[^n3]

Nīsābūrī died in 1329/1330, the year he completed his ar.[^n3]

## Works {#Works}

### Astronomy and mathematics {#Astronomy_and_mathematics}

*Image: Nizam al-Din Hassan bin Husayn Qumi Nishapuri, known as Nizam-i a'raj (fl. 1311); a commentary on Tusi’s al-Tadhkirah Fi 'ilm al-Hay'a, signed ‘Ali bin Sufi bin Ibrahim Aqhari, Timurid Iran, dated December 1490.jpg.* Manuscript of al-Nishaburi's commentary on [Nasir al-Din Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_al-Din_Tusi)'s *Fi 'ilm al-Hay'a*. Copy created in Timurid Iran, dated December 1490

Nīsābūrī started to write ar (تفسیر التحریر, "Commentary on the recension of the Almagest") in 1303., a commentary on a work by [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/33642424). Together with an explanation al-Tusi's text, Nīsābūrī added his own results and ideas. He included data about the [obliquity of the ecliptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obliquity_of_the_ecliptic) and discussed the possibility that the [transits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_transit) of [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus) and [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_%28planet%29) across the Sun had been seen.[^n3]

Nīsābūrī second astronomical work, ar ("Uncovering the Truths of the Īlkhānid Astronomical Handbook") was completed in 1308/1309. A commentary on a ar by Ṭūsī', it focused upon topics discussed in the ar, such as the positions of the planets in the night sky.[^n3]

ar ((توضیح الذکر), "Elucidation of the Tadhkira") was a commentary on Ṭūsī's ar ("Memento on Astronomy") that investigated topics that included alternatives to [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979)'s model of the cosmos, and ideas to explain that accounted the known variations in the obliquity of the ecliptic.[^n3]

The ar and the ar were not written for astronomers, but for students whose [curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum) included astronomy.[^n3]

Nisaburi also wrote a [treatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise) on mathematics.

### Religious works {#Religious_works}

Nīsābūrī's most famous work is his ar (تفسير النيسابوري‎, "A Commentary on the Wonders of Quran in Exegesis"), also known as ar). It is ar of the [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an), which closely follows [al-Fakhr al-Razi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fakhr_al-Razi)'s ar in many places. The work was written to demonstrate the importance of science for religious scholars. The work reflects Nīsābūrī's scientific background, in contrast with Rāzī's bias towards the theologians.[^n3]

Nīsābūrī's other religious works include:

- ar (اوقاف القران);
- fa (کشف حقایق زیج ایلخانی, "Explanation of the Facts"), an explanation of ar by Nasir al-Din Tusi, written in [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language)
- ar (البصائر فی مختصر تنقیح المناظر), a work on the topics in al-Manazer by [Ibn Haytham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Haytham);
- ar (شرح نظام بر الشافیه);
