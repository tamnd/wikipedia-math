# Torsion conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Torsion conjecture"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **torsion conjecture** or **uniform boundedness conjecture for torsion points** for [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties) states that the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of the [torsion group](wikipedia://en/3568695) of an abelian variety over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) can be bounded in terms of the [dimension of the variety](wikipedia://en/253251) and the number field. A stronger version of the conjecture is that the torsion is bounded in terms of the dimension of the variety and the degree of the number field. The torsion conjecture has been completely resolved in the case of [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves).

:::

## Elliptic curves {#Elliptic_curves}

::: {#o3 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Ogg's conjecture"}

**Ogg's conjecture.**

:::

From 1906 to 1911, [Beppo Levi](wikipedia://en/8718312) published a series of papers investigating the possible finite orders of points on elliptic curves over the rationals.[^n1] He showed that there are infinitely many elliptic curves over the rationals with the following torsion groups:

- *C*<sub>*n*</sub> with 1 ≤ *n* ≤ 10, where *C*<sub>*n*</sub> denotes the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *n*;
- *C*<sub>12</sub>;
- *C*<sub>2n</sub> × *C*<sub>2</sub> with 1 ≤ *n* ≤ 4, where × denotes the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/106341).

At the 1908 [International Mathematical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Congress) in Rome, Levi conjectured that this is a complete list of torsion groups for elliptic curves over the rationals.[^n1] The torsion conjecture for elliptic curves over the rationals was independently reformulated by (txt) and again by (txt), with the conjecture becoming commonly known as **Ogg's conjecture**.[^n1]

(txt) drew the connection between the torsion conjecture for elliptic curves over the rationals and the theory of [classical modular curves](wikipedia://en/5249663).[^n1] In the early 1970s, the work of Gérard Ligozat, [Daniel Kubert](wikipedia://en/35199552), [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679), and [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) showed that several small values of *n* do not occur as orders of torsion points on elliptic curves over the rationals.[^n1] (txt) proved the full torsion conjecture for elliptic curves over the rationals. His techniques were generalized by (Kamienny 1992) and (Kamienny Mazur 1995), who obtained uniform boundedness for [quadratic fields](wikipedia://en/454781) and number fields of degree at most 8 respectively. Finally, (txt) proved the conjecture for elliptic curves over any number field.[^n1] He proved for $K$ a number field of degree $d=[K:\mathbb{Q}]$ and an elliptic curve $E/K$ that there is a bound on the order of the torsion group depending only on the degree $|E(K)_{\text{tors}}|\leq B(d)$. Furthermore if $P\in E(K)_{\text{tors}}$ is a point of prime order $p$ we have $p\leq d^{3d^2}.$

An effective bound for the size of the torsion group in terms of the degree of the number field was given by (Parent 1999). Parent proved that for $P\in E(K)_{\text{tors}}$ a point of prime power order $p^n$ we have

$$p^n \leq B(d,p)=\begin{cases} 129(3^d-1)(3d)^6&\text{if }p=2,\\ 65(5^d-1)(2d)^6&\text{if }p=3,\\ 65(3^d-1)(2d)^6&\text{if }p>3. \end{cases}$$

Setting $B_{\text{max}}(d)=129(5^d-1)(3d)^6$ we get from the structure result behind the [Mordell-Weil theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell-Weil_theorem), i.e. there are two integers $n_1,n_2$ such that $E(K)_{\text{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/n_1\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n_2\mathbb{Z}$, a coarse but effective bound $B(d)=\left(B_{\text{max}}(d)^{B_{\text{max}}(d)}\right)^2.$

[Joseph Oesterlé](wikipedia://en/1604631) gave in private notes from 1994 a slightly better bound for points of prime order $p$ of $p\leq (3^{d/2}+1)^2$, which turns out to be useful for computations over fields of small order, but alone is not enough to yield an effective bound for $|E(K)_{\text{tors}}|$. (Derickx Kamienny Stein Stoll 2023) provide a published version of Oesterlé's result.

For number fields of small degree more refined results are known (Sutherland 2012). Complete lists of possible torsion groups have been given for elliptic curves over number fields up to degree 4 (see above for the explicit complete list in degree 1, i.e. over $\mathbb{Q}$). In degree 1, 2 and 4 all groups that arise occur infinitely often. The same holds for cubic fields[^o24] except for the group *C*<sub>21</sub> which occurs only in a single elliptic curve over $K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9)^+$. Additionally all torsion groups that arise infinitely often have been determined up to degree 7 - the state of the art in 2026 is summarized in (Najman 2026). Barry Mazur gave a survey talk on the torsion conjecture[^o24] on the occasion of the establishment of the Ogg Professorship[^o25] at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in October 2022.

## Abelian Varieties {#Abelian_Varieties}

The uniform boundedness conjecture for higher-dimensional abelian varieties is still largely unresolved, even in the setting of abelian surfaces. In the special case of abelian surfaces defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ with geometric endomorphism ring equal to a maximal order in a non-split quaternion algebra, Jef Laga, Ciarna Schembri, Ari Shnidman, and John Voight established an explicit bound of $18$ on the size of the torsion group ((Laga Schembri Shnidman Voight 2024)). A proof assuming a generalized Szpiro conjecture is recently claimed in a preprint by [Ziyang Gao](wikipedia://en/83288827) and Kaiyuan Gu ((Gao Gu 2026)).

In contrast, the situation over characteristic-zero function fields is better understood. Benjamin Bakker and [Jacob Tsimerman](wikipedia://en/48497888) proved the conjecture for abelian surfaces ((Bakker Tsimerman 2018)) via techniques from hyperbolic geometry, a recent preprint by Nicole Looper and Jit Wu Yap claims a proof in full generality (Looper Yap 2026), and an alternative proof with explicit bounds is claimed by [Ziyang Gao](wikipedia://en/83288827) and Kaiyuan Gu ((Gao Gu 2026)).
