# Lithospheric flexure

*Image: Local-regional isostasy - flexure, elastic thickness.jpg.* Cartoon showing the isostatic vertical motions of the lithosphere (grey) in response to a vertical load (in green)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lithospheric flexure"}

In [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology), **lithospheric flexure** (also called **regional isostasy**) is the process by which the [lithosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithosphere) (rigid, thin outer layer of the Earth) [bends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bending) under the action of forces such as the weight of a growing [orogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orogeny) or changes in ice thickness related to [glaciation](wikipedia://en/598434). The lithosphere rests on the [asthenosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthenosphere), a [viscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscous) layer that in [geological time scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_time_scale) behaves like a [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid). Thus, when loaded, the lithosphere progressively reaches an [isostatic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/185153), which represents [Archimedes' principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes'_principle) applied to geological settings.[^o2]

:::

This phenomenon was first described in the late 19th century to explain the shorelines uplifted in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) by the removal of large ice massed during the [last glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). American geologist [G. K. Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grove_Karl_Gilbert) used it to explain the uplifted shorelines of [Lake Bonneville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Bonneville).[^o3] The concept was not retaken until the 1950s by [Vening Meinesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Andries_Vening_Meinesz).

The geometry of the lithospheric bending is often modeled adopting a pure [elastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29) thin plate approach (sometimes by fitting the [gravity anomaly](wikipedia://en/937535) produced by that bending rather than more direct data of it). The thickness of such plate that best fits the observed lithospheric bending is called the *equivalent elastic thickness* of the lithosphere and is related to the stiffness or rigidity of the lithosphere. These lithospheric bending calculations are typically performed following the [Euler-Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/2064935) bending formulation, or alternatively the [Lagrange equation](wikipedia://en/23371726).
