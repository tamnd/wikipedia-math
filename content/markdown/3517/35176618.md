# Denis Miéville

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Denis Miéville"}

**Denis Miéville** (15 September 1946 – 27 October 2018) was a Swiss expert on the logic of [Stanislaw Lesniewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Lesniewski) and natural logic.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Denis Miéville was raised in the towns of [Colombier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombier,_Neuchâtel) ([Canton of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Neuchâtel)) and [Essert-Pittet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essert-Pittet) ([Canton of Vaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Vaud)). After studying [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) at the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel) (Switzerland) and [Bowling Green University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green_University) (Ohio, United States), Denis Miéville developed an interest in the development and formalization of natural logic that led him to study both the theory of collective classes and the foundations of maximal predicates in propositional logic. These interests were integrated in the doctoral thesis that he defended in 1984 ("A Development of [Stanislaw Lesniewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Lesniewski)'s logical systems: Protothetic, [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) and [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380)") at the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel), supervised by the eminent logician Jean-Blaise Grize. Appointed Professor at the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel) in 1987 (he will become its rector from 1999 to 2003), he taught logic and chaired the Semiologic Research Centre.

Professor Miéville has taught at various institutions such as the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva), the [University of Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rennes) in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) and the [University of Iasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iasi) in [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania). The [University of Iasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iasi) awarded him a degree [Honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa) in 2003. He also received the Honorary Certificate of the [Francophony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophony) in 2001.

## Research interests {#Research_interests}

Professor Miéville developed an expertise on [Lesniewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesniewski)'s logic due to his interest in developmental systems, that have the advantage of being dynamic, universal, free and of a higher order. Moreover, by developing a theory of syntactic-semantic categories, he focussed on a methodology able to identify logico-discursive indices in texts, by representing them as argumentative and reasoned structures. His interests became more and more inclined towards understanding the way in which discursive thought creates meaning, by inscribing it into reasoning networks. This is one of the reasons that led Professor Miéville to specifically examine the discursive procedures from which new knowledge is developed, such as those proceeding by analogy, and those structuring creative definitions. Very sensitive to the epistemological dimension of knowledge, he has been interested in how concepts develop gradually and crystallize into stable entities.
