# Dana S. Richards

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dana S. Richards"}

**Dana S. Richards** is a writer, mathematics popularizer and Associate Professor in [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science) at [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University).

:::

His research interests include comparisons of [protein sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_sequences), [Steiner tree algorithms](wikipedia://en/498304),[^o3] information dissemination in networks, parallel heuristics, methodology for computationally intractable problems and parallel algorithms for median filters.[^o4] He is the longtime bibliographer of polymath [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435).[^o20]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Richards received an M.S. from the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia) in 1976[^o10] and a Ph.D. in computer science from the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign) under [Chung Laung Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chung_Laung_Liu) in 1984.[^o6]

He was an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the University of Virginia,[^o10] and a Program Director of [Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_Computing) at the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (June 1993 through May 1994).[^o7]

He has written or edited seven books[^o8] and numerous journal articles.[^o9] In addition, he is a reviewer for many journals, and has received numerous research awards. He became an Associate Professor of Computer Science at George Mason University in 1994.[^o10]

## Martin Gardner {#Martin_Gardner}

Dana Richards was a friend of Martin Gardner going back to the 1970s,[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15] and in his writing and speaking he often memorialized and popularized Gardner's work.[^o16][^o17] In 2006 he edited *The Colossal Book of Short Puzzles and Problems* which collected all of Gardner's short puzzles in one volume.[^o18] He wrote Gardner's obituary in *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29)*[^o19] and in 2023 he published a comprehensive bibliography of Gardner's works.[^o20]

Since Gardner's death in 2010 events called [Celebration of Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebration_of_Mind) are held every October which include games, magic and puzzles in the Gardner tradition, and Richards is frequently featured at these events discussing Gardner's life and work.[^o21]
