# Markov information source

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Markov information source"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Markov information source**, or simply, a **Markov source**, is an [information source](wikipedia://en/18909947) whose underlying dynamics are given by a stationary finite [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

An **information source** is a sequence of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) ranging over a finite alphabet $\Gamma$, having a [stationary distribution](wikipedia://en/2524727).

A Markov information source is then a (stationary) Markov chain $M$, together with a [function](wikipedia://en/185427)

$$f:S\to \Gamma$$

that maps states $S$ in the Markov chain to letters in the alphabet $\Gamma$.

A **unifilar Markov source** is a Markov source for which the values $f(s_k)$ are distinct whenever each of the states $s_k$ are reachable, in one step, from a common prior state. Unifilar sources are notable in that many of their properties are far more easily analyzed, as compared to the general case.

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Markov sources are commonly used in [communication theory](wikipedia://en/248810), as a model of a [transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmitter). Markov sources also occur in [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing), where they are used to represent hidden meaning in a text. Given the output of a Markov source, whose underlying Markov chain is unknown, the task of solving for the underlying chain is undertaken by the techniques of [hidden Markov models](wikipedia://en/98770), such as the [Viterbi algorithm](wikipedia://en/228015).

:::
