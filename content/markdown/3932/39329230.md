# Bipartite half

*Image: Demi-4-cube.svg.* The halved cube graph of order 4, obtained as the bipartite half of an order-4 [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bipartite half"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **bipartite half** or **half-square** of a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) $G = (U,V,E)$ is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) whose [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) set is one of the two sides of the [bipartition](wikipedia://en/11973947) ([without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000), $U$) and in which there is an edge $u_{i}u_{j}$ for each pair of vertices $u_{i}, u_{j}$ in $U$ that are at [distance](wikipedia://en/1020021) two from each other in $G$.[^o2] That is, in a more compact notation, the bipartite half is $G^{2}[U]$ where the superscript 2 denotes the [square of a graph](wikipedia://en/38618878) and the square brackets denote an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188).

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For instance, the bipartite half of the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{n,n}$ is the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{n}$ and the bipartite half of the [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815) is the [halved cube graph](wikipedia://en/39329236). When $G$ is a [distance-regular graph](wikipedia://en/7768943), its two bipartite halves are both distance-regular.[^o5] For instance, the halved [Foster graph](wikipedia://en/11197065) is one of finitely many degree-6 distance-regular [locally linear graphs](wikipedia://en/59938063).[^o6]

:::

## Representation and hardness {#Representation_and_hardness}

Every graph $G$ is the bipartite half of another graph, formed by subdividing the edges of $G$ into two-edge paths. More generally, a representation of $G$ as a bipartite half can be found by taking any [clique edge cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_edge_cover) of $G$ and replacing each clique by a [star](wikipedia://en/19310129).[^o8] Every representation arises in this way. Since finding the smallest clique edge cover is NP-hard, so is finding the graph with the fewest vertices for which $G$ is the bipartite half.[^o9]

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

The [map graphs](wikipedia://en/50947627), that is, the [intersection graphs](wikipedia://en/7726759) of interior-disjoint simply-connected regions in the plane, are exactly the bipartite halves of bipartite [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314).[^o12]

:::
