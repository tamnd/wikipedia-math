# Linda Preiss Rothschild

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Linda Preiss Rothschild"}

**Linda Preiss Rothschild** ( Preiss; born February 28, 1945) is a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) of mathematics at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego).[^o4] Her thesis research concerned [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), but subsequently her interests broadened to include also [polynomial factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_factorization), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), and the theory of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).[^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

The daughter of Philadelphia fur merchants, she was unable to attend the best high school of the city, which were then restricted to boys. She graduated from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in 1966.[^o4][^o5] Rejected from graduate study at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) because it was also male-only,[^o5][^o7] she earned her Ph.D. in 1970 from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) under the supervision of [Isadore Singer](wikipedia://en/324564).[^o4]

She held temporary positions at MIT, [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) before landing an associate professorship at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) in 1976. She moved to San Diego in 1983,[^o4][^o5] and retired in 2011.[^o7]

Rothschild was a Member at Large at the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1977 to 1979.[^o9] She served as president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) from 1983 to 1985, and vice president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1985 to 1987. She has been co-editor-in-chief of the journal *Mathematical Research Letters* since 1994.[^o4][^o7]

The mother of two sons by her first marriage, her second husband, [M. Salah Baouendi](wikipedia://en/44998696), was a distinguished professor of mathematics at UC San Diego; he died in 2011.[^o10]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Rothschild was awarded a [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) in 1976.[^o4][^o5] In 1997, Rothschild gave the [Noether Lecture](wikipedia://en/11566873) of the Association for Women in Mathematics, on the subject "How do Real Manifolds live in Complex Space?",[^o4][^o5] and she was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2006.[^o7] She was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2005,[^o7] and in 2012 she became one of the inaugural fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o12] A conference in her honor was held in 2008 at the [University of Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fribourg) in Switzerland.[^o7] In 2017, she was selected as a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the inaugural class.[^o13]

She and her husband were jointly awarded the [Stefan Bergman Prize](wikipedia://en/9395687#The_Bergman_Prize) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2003.[^o7]
