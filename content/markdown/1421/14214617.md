# Power of three

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Power of three"}

*Image: fewest\_weights\_balance\_puzzle.svg.* 81 (3<sup>4</sup>) combinations of weights of 1 (3<sup>0</sup>), 3 (3<sup>1</sup>), 9 (3<sup>2</sup>) and 27 (3<sup>3</sup>) kg – each weight on the left pan, right pan or unused – allow integer weights from −40 to +40 kg to be balanced; the figure shows the positive values

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **power of three** is a number of the form $3^{n}$ where $n$ is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), that is, the result of [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491) with number [three](wikipedia://en/208157) as the [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_%28exponentiation%29) and integer $n$ as the [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent). The first ten non-negative powers of three are:

:::

[1](wikipedia://en/77591639), [3](wikipedia://en/208157), [9](wikipedia://en/173457), [27](wikipedia://en/369235), [81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81_%28number%29), [243](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/243_%28number%29), [729](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/729_%28number%29), 2187, 6561, 19683, etc. (sequence [A000244](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeis:A000244) in [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OEIS))

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The powers of three give the place values in the [ternary numeral system](wikipedia://en/62950).[^o4]

### Graph theory {#Graph_theory}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), powers of three appear in the Moon–Moser bound $3^{n/3}$ on the number of [maximal independent sets](wikipedia://en/1793590) of an $n$-vertex [graph](wikipedia://en/325806),[^o6] and in the time analysis of the [Bron–Kerbosch algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bron–Kerbosch_algorithm) for finding these sets.[^o7] Several important [strongly regular graphs](wikipedia://en/1399856) also have a number of vertices that is a power of three, including the [Brouwer–Haemers graph](wikipedia://en/28189558) (81 vertices), [Berlekamp–van Lint–Seidel graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlekamp–van_Lint–Seidel_graph) (243 vertices), and [Games graph](wikipedia://en/59961913) (729 vertices).[^o8]

### Enumerative combinatorics {#Enumerative_combinatorics}

In [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533), there are $3^{n}$ [signed subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_set) of a set of $n$ elements. In [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519), the [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) and all other [Hanner polytopes](wikipedia://en/39408400) have a number of faces (not counting the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) as a face) that is a power of three. For example, a 2-cube, or [square](wikipedia://en/659939), has 4 vertices, 4 edges and 1 face, and $4 + 4 + 1 = 3^{2}$. [Kalai's $3^{d}$ conjecture](wikipedia://en/39408412) states that this is the minimum possible number of faces for a [centrally symmetric](wikipedia://en/12487536) polytope.[^o10]

### Inverse power of three lengths {#Inverse_power_of_three_lengths}

In [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446) and [fractal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_geometry), inverse power-of-three lengths occur in the constructions leading to the [Koch snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959),[^o12] [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172),[^o13] [Sierpinski carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_carpet) and [Menger sponge](wikipedia://en/208811), in the number of elements in the construction steps for a [Sierpinski triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_triangle), and in many formulas related to these sets. There are $3^{n}$ possible states in an $n$-disk [Tower of Hanoi](wikipedia://en/56990) puzzle or vertices in its associated [Hanoi graph](wikipedia://en/58619302).[^o14] In a [balance puzzle](wikipedia://en/14429995) with $w$ weighing steps, there are $3^{w}$ possible outcomes (sequences where the scale tilts left or right or stays balanced); powers of three often arise in the solutions to these puzzles, and it has been suggested that (for similar reasons) the powers of three would make an ideal system of [coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin).[^o15]

### Perfect totient numbers {#Perfect_totient_numbers}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), all powers of three are [perfect totient numbers](wikipedia://en/8890014).[^o17] The sums of distinct powers of three form a [Stanley sequence](wikipedia://en/60013689), the lexicographically smallest sequence that does not contain an [arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/168389) of three elements. A [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) states that this sequence contains no [powers of two](wikipedia://en/376948) other than 1, 4, and 256.[^o19]

### Graham's number {#Graham's_number}

[Graham's number](wikipedia://en/234232), an enormous number arising from a [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) in [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175), is (in the version popularized by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435)) a power of three. However, the actual publication of the proof by [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) used a different number which is a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948) and much smaller.[^o21]
