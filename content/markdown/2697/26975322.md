# Khabibullin's conjecture on integral inequalities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Khabibullin's conjecture on integral inequalities"}

**Khabibullin's conjecture** is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) in mathematics related to [Paley](wikipedia://en/2788568)'s problem[^o2] for [plurisubharmonic functions](wikipedia://en/2519085) and to various [extremal problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_value) in the theory of [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372) of several variables. The conjecture was named after its proposer, B. N. Khabibullin.

:::

There are three versions of the conjecture, one in terms of logarithmically convex functions, one in terms of increasing functions, and one in terms of non-negative functions. The conjecture has implications in the study of complex functions and is related to Euler's [Beta function](wikipedia://en/245660). While the conjecture is known to hold for certain conditions, counterexamples have also been found.

## The first statement in terms of logarithmically convex functions {#The_first_statement_in_terms_of_logarithmically_convex_functions}

**Khabibullin's conjecture** (version 1, 1992). *Let $\displaystyle S$ be a non-negative [increasing function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Increasing_function) on the half-line $[0,+\infty)$ such that $\displaystyle S(0)=0$. Assume that $\displaystyle S(e^x)$ is a [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) of $x\in[-\infty,+\infty)$. Let $\lambda\geq 1/2$, $n\geq 2$, and $n\in\mathbb N$. If*

::: {#o4 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\int^1_0 S(tx)\,(1-x^2)^{n-2}\,x\,dx\leq t^\lambda\text{ for all }t\in[0,+\infty), \tag{1}$$

:::

*then*

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\int^{+\infty}_0 S(t)\,\frac{t^{2\lambda-1}}{(1+t^{2\lambda})^2}\,dt\leq \frac{\pi\,(n-1)}{2\lambda}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1} \Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda}{2k}\Bigr). \tag{2}$$

:::

This statement of the Khabibullin's conjecture completes his survey.[^o6]

## Relation to Euler's Beta function {#Relation_to_Euler's_Beta_function}

The product in the right hand side of the inequality ((2)) is related to the Euler's [Beta function](wikipedia://en/245660) $\mathrm{B}$:

$$\frac{\pi\,(n-1)}{2\lambda}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1} \Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda}{2k}\Bigr)=\frac{\pi\,(n-1)}{\lambda^2}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{B}(\lambda/2,n)}$$

## Discussion {#Discussion}

For each fixed $\lambda\geq 1/2$ the function

$$S(t)=2(n-1)\prod_{k=1}^{n-1} \Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda}{2k}\Bigr) \, t^{\lambda},$$

turns the inequalities ((1)) and ((2)) to equalities.

The Khabibullin's conjecture is valid for $\lambda\leq 1$ without the assumption of convexity of $S(e^x)$. Meanwhile, one can show that this conjecture is not valid without some convexity conditions for $S$. In 2010, [R. A. Sharipov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Sharipov) showed that the conjecture fails in the case $n=2$ and for $\lambda=2$.[^o9]

## The second statement in terms of increasing functions {#The_second_statement_in_terms_of_increasing_functions}

**Khabibullin's conjecture** (version 2). *Let $\displaystyle h$ be a non-negative increasing function on the half-line $[0,+\infty)$ and $\alpha>1/2$. If*

$$\int_0^1 \frac{h(tx)}{x} \,(1-x)^{n-1}\,dx \leq t^\alpha\text{ for all }t\in[0,+\infty),$$

*then*

$$\int_0^{+\infty}\frac{h(t)}{t}\,\frac{dt}{1+t^{2\alpha}}\leq \frac{\pi}{2} \prod_{k=1}^{n-1} \Bigl(1+\frac{\alpha}{k}\Bigr)= \frac{\pi}{2\alpha} \cdot \frac{1}{\mathrm B (\alpha, n)}.$$

## The third statement in terms of non-negative functions {#The_third_statement_in_terms_of_non-negative_functions}

**Khabibullin's conjecture** (version 3). *Let $\displaystyle q$ be a non-negative continuous function on the half-line $[0,+\infty)$ and $\alpha>1/2$. If*

$$\int_0^1 \Bigl(\,\int_x^1 (1-y)^{n-1} \frac{dy}{y}\Bigr)q(tx)\,dx \leq t^{\alpha-1}\text{ for all }t\in[0,+\infty),$$

*then*

$$\int_0^{+\infty} q(t)\log \Bigl(1+\frac1{t^{2\alpha}}\Bigr)\,dt\leq \pi \alpha \prod_{k=1}^{n-1} \Bigl(1+\frac{\alpha}{k}\Bigr)= \frac{\pi}{\mathrm B (\alpha, n)}.$$
