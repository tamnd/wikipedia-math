# John Lemmon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Lemmon"}

**Edward John Lemmon** (1 June 1930 – 29 July 1966) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) born in [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England). He is most well known for his work on [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365), particularly his joint text with [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875) published posthumously (Lemmon and Scott, 1977).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Lemmon attended [King Edward VII School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VII_School_%28Sheffield%29)[^o3] in Sheffield until 1947, before reading [Literae humaniores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literae_humaniores) at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford), as an undergraduate, and was appointed Fellow of [Trinity College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford), in 1957. In 1963, following a visiting professorship in [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas), Lemmon emigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) to lecture at the Claremont Graduate School (now [Claremont Graduate University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claremont_Graduate_University)). Lemmon died from [heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_failure) while climbing.

## Modal logic {#Modal_logic}

John Lemmon became interested in modal logic when [Arthur Prior](wikipedia://en/308764) visited [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) in 1956 to give the [John Locke lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke_lectures), later published as his *Time and Modality* (Prior 1957). Prior returned for twelve months soon after, to lead a small group including Lemmon, [Peter Geach](wikipedia://en/302884) and [Ivo Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Thomas) (Copeland 2004). John Lemmon became one of the early champions of Prior's distinctive approach to [tense logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tense_logic), and Lemmon's later work on [alethic modality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alethic_modality) and applications of modal logic to [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics) bear the mark of Prior's influence. At this time, Lemmon published a treatment of [alethic](wikipedia://en/1547308) and [epistemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic) modalities that introduced some systems of non-[normal modal logics](wikipedia://en/911820) that have proven to have had lasting interest, the alethic system S0.5 and the epistemic systems E1–E5 linked to the systems S0.5 and Lewis's systems S2–S5, but which lack the law of necessitation (Lemmon 1957).

Lemmon was a pioneer of the modern approach to the [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107) of modal logic, particularly through his collaboration with [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875), but also became interested in the rival [algebraic semantics](wikipedia://en/2848519) of modal logic that follows more closely the kind of semantics found in the work of [Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) and [Jónsson](wikipedia://en/3893573).
