# Sudan function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sudan function"}

In the [theory of computation](wikipedia://en/30402), the **Sudan function** is an example of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) that is [recursive](wikipedia://en/25407#Functional_recursion), but not [primitive recursive](wikipedia://en/24829). This is also true of the better-known [Ackermann function](wikipedia://en/2925).

:::

In 1926, [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) conjectured that every computable function was primitive recursive. This was refuted by [Gabriel Sudan](wikipedia://en/6749249) and [Wilhelm Ackermann](wikipedia://en/62727) — both his students — using different functions that were published in quick succession: Sudan in 1927,[^n1] Ackermann in 1928.[^n2]

The Sudan function is the earliest published example of a recursive function that is not primitive recursive.[^n3]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

$$\begin{array}{lll} F_0 (x, y) & = x+y \\ F_{n+1} (x, 0) & = x & \text{if } n \ge 0 \\ F_{n+1} (x, y+1) & = F_n (F_{n+1} (x, y), F_{n+1} (x, y) + y + 1) & \text{if } n\ge 0 \\ \end{array}$$

The last equation can be equivalently written as

$$\begin{array}{lll} F_{n+1} (x, y+1) & = F_n(F_{n+1} (x, y), F_0(F_{n+1}(x, y), y + 1)) \\ \end{array}$$

.[^n4]

:::

::: {#o9 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computation"}

## Computation {#Computation}

These equations can be used as rules of a [term rewriting system (TRS)](wikipedia://en/415847#Term_rewriting_systems).

The generalized function $F(x,y,n) \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=} F_n(x,y)$ leads to the rewrite rules

$$\begin{array}{lll} \text{(r1)} & F(x, y, 0) & \rightarrow x+y \\ \text{(r2)} & F(x, 0, n+1) & \rightarrow x \\ \text{(r3)} & F(x, y+1, n+1) & \rightarrow F(F(x, y, n+1), F(F(x, y, n+1), y + 1,0), n) \\ \end{array}$$

At each reduction step the rightmost innermost occurrence of F is rewritten, by application of one of the rules (r1) - (r3).

Calude (1988) gives an **example**: compute $F(2,2,1) \rightarrow_{*} 12$.[^n5]

The reduction sequence is[^o11]

{|

\|- |$\underline{{F(2,2,1)}}$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r3} F(F(2,1,1),F(\underline{{F(2,1,1)}},2,0),0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r3} F(F(2,1,1),F(F(F(2,0,1),F(\underline{{F(2,0,1)}},1,0),0),2,0),0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r2} F(F(2,1,1),F(F(F(2,0,1),\underline{{F(2,1,0)}},0),2,0),0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r1} F(F(2,1,1),F(F(\underline{{F(2,0,1)}},3,0),2,0),0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r2} F(F(2,1,1),F(\underline{{F(2,3,0)}},2,0),0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r1} F(F(2,1,1),\underline{{F(5,2,0)}},0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r1} F(\underline{{F(2,1,1)}},7,0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r3} F(F(F(2,0,1),F(\underline{{F(2,0,1)}},1,0),0),7,0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r2} F(F(F(2,0,1),\underline{{F(2,1,0)}},0),7,0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r1} F(F(\underline{{F(2,0,1)}},3,0),7,0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r2} F(\underline{{F(2,3,0)}},7,0)$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r1} \underline{{F(5,7,0)}}$ |- |4$\rightarrow_{r1} 12$ |}

:::
