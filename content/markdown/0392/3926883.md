# Campanus of Novara

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Campanus of Novara"}

**Campanus of Novara** (c. 1220 – 1296) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), [astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrologer), and [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician)[^o3] who is best known for his work on [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107).[^o4][^o5] In his writings he refers to himself as Campanus Nouariensis; contemporary documents refer to him as [Magister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magister_%28degree%29) Campanus; and the full style of his name is Magister Campanus Nouariensis.[^o4] He is also referred to as **Campano da Novara**, **Giovanni Campano** or similar. Later authors (from the 16th century on) sometimes applied the forename **Johannes Campanus** or Iohannes Campanus.[^o3][^o4][^o12]

:::

His date of birth is uncertain but may have been as early as the first decade of the 13th century and the place of birth was probably [Novara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novara) in [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont).[^o12] He served as chaplain to [Pope Urban IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_IV), [Pope Adrian V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Adrian_V), [Pope Nicholas IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV), and [Pope Boniface VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Boniface_VIII).[^o4] His contemporary [Roger Bacon](wikipedia://en/25879) cited Campanus as one of the two "good" (but not "perfect") mathematicians[^o6] indicating that Bacon considered Campanus as excellent[^o12] or one of the greatest mathematicians of their time.[^o3] A number of [benefices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefice) were conferred upon him, and he was relatively wealthy at the time of his death.[^o4] He died at [Viterbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viterbo) in 1296.[^o12] The [crater Campanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanus_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) is named after him.[^o7]

## Published works {#Published_works}

*Image: Euclid3a.gif.* First page of the Latin edition of Euclid's *Elements* by Campanus (1482 printing)

*Image: Campano - Tetragonismus idest circuli quadratura, 1503 - 106319.jpg.* *Tetragonismus idest circuli quadratura*, 1503

Campanus wrote a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) edition of Euclid's *Elementa* in fifteen books. This work by Campanus was influential and was the most frequently used compilation of Euclid until the 16th century.[^o9] It was based on a compilation by [Robert of Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_of_Chester) and also includes material from: *Arithmetica* by [Jordanus de Nemore](wikipedia://en/4763255), commentary on Euclid by [Anaritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaritius), and additions by Campanus himself.[^o9][^o10] It would later become the first [printed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letterpress_printing) edition of Euclid,[^o9] published by [Erhard Ratdolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhard_Ratdolt) in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) in 1482 as *Preclarissimus liber elementorum Euclidis perspicacissimi*.[^o11]

In the field of astronomy, he wrote a *Theorica Planetarum* in which he geometrically described the motions of the [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) as well as their [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude). He also included instructions on building a planetary [equatorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorium) as well as its geometrical description. Campanus also attempted to determine the time of each planet's [retrograde motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrograde_motion). The data on planets are drawn from the *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)* and the *[Toledan Tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledan_Tables)* of the Arab astronomer [Arzachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arzachel). Campanus gave precise instructions on using the tables, and made detailed calculations of the distances to the planets and their sizes. This work has been called "the first detailed account of the Ptolemaic astronomical system... to be written in the Latin-speaking West."[^o12]

A [house system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_%28astrology%29) for [horoscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horoscope) that divides the [prime vertical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_vertical) into equal 30° arcs, or houses, is often attributed to him but the method is known to have been described by others before his time.[^o13]
