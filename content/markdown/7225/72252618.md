::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Tetrahedral bipyramid"}

In 4-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **tetrahedral bipyramid** is the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of a tetrahedron and a segment, {3,3} + { }. Each face of a central tetrahedron is attached with two tetrahedra, creating 8 tetrahedral cells, 16 triangular faces, 14 edges, and 6 vertices.[^o6] A tetrahedral bipyramid can be seen as two [tetrahedral pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_pyramid) augmented together at their base.

:::

It is the dual of a [tetrahedral prism](wikipedia://en/5977290), , so it can also be given a [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), , and both have [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502) symmetry \[2,3,3\], order 48.

Being convex with all regular cells (tetrahedra) means that it is a [Blind polytope](wikipedia://en/72252379).

This bipyramid exists as the cells of the dual of the uniform [rectified 5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_5-simplex), and [rectified 5-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_5-cube) or the dual of any uniform 5-polytope with a [tetrahedral prism](wikipedia://en/5977290) [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719). And, as well, it exists as the cells of the dual to the [rectified 24-cell honeycomb](wikipedia://en/32023901).
