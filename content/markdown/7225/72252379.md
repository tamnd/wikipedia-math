# Blind polytope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Blind polytope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **Blind polytope** is a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) composed of [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29). The category was named after the German couple Gerd and [Roswitha Blind](wikipedia://en/68449302), who described them in a series of papers beginning in 1979.[^o2] It generalizes the set of [semiregular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_polyhedra) and [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) to higher dimensions.[^o3]

:::

## Uniform cases {#Uniform_cases}

The set of [convex uniform 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_uniform_4-polytope) (also called semiregular 4-polytopes) are completely known cases, nearly all grouped by their [Wythoff constructions](wikipedia://en/2950777), sharing symmetries of the [convex regular 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_regular_4-polytope) and prismatic forms.

Set of convex [uniform 5-polytopes](wikipedia://en/14415338), [uniform 6-polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821728), [uniform 7-polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821719), etc are largely enumerated as Wythoff constructions, but not known to be complete.

## Other cases {#Other_cases}

Pyramidal forms: (4D)

1. (*Tetrahedral pyramid*, ( ) ∨ {3,3}, a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) base, and 4 tetrahedral sides, a lower symmetry name of regular [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821).)
1. [Octahedral pyramid](wikipedia://en/41727746), ( ) ∨ {3,4}, an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) base, and 8 [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) sides meeting at an apex.
1. [Icosahedral pyramid](wikipedia://en/43632532), ( ) ∨ {3,5}, an [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) base, and 20 [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) sides.

Bipyramid forms: (4D)

1. [Tetrahedral bipyramid](wikipedia://en/72252618), { } + {3,3}, a tetrahedron center, and 8 tetrahedral cells on two side.
1. (*Octahedral bipyramid*, { } + {3,4}, an octahedron center, and 8 tetrahedral cells on two side, a lower symmetry name of regular [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422).)
1. [Icosahedral bipyramid](wikipedia://en/72252654), { } + {3,5}, an icosahedron center, and 40 tetrahedral cells on two sides.

Augmented forms: (4D)

- [Rectified 5-cell](wikipedia://en/2720430) augmented with one [octahedral pyramid](wikipedia://en/41727746), adding one vertex for 11 total. It retains 5 tetrahedral cells, reduced to 4 octahedral cells and adds 8 new tetrahedral cells.[^o6]

Diminished forms: (4D)

- Precisely 314,248,344[^o7] "special cuts" of the [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447), all but one of which (the [icositetradiminished 600-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756)) are non-uniform. They are created by diminishing a set of non-adjacent vertices and replacing 20 tetrahedra with an icosahedron for each selected vertex.

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="CRF polytope"}

## Convex regular-faced polytopes {#Convex_regular-faced_polytopes}

Blind polytopes are a subset of convex regular-faced polytopes (CRF).[^o10] This much larger set allows *CRF 4-polytopes* to have [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) as cells, as well as regular and semiregular polyhedral cells.

For example, a [cubic bipyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_bipyramid) has 12 [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) cells.

:::
