# Luttinger's theorem

*Image: Fermi Surface.svg.* Luttinger's theorem relates a Fermi liquid's particle density to the volume of its Fermi surface.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luttinger's theorem"}

In [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics), **Luttinger's theorem**[^o2][^o3] is a result derived by [J. M. Luttinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Mazdak_Luttinger) and [J. C. Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Clive_Ward) in 1960 that has broad implications in the field of [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) transport. It arises frequently in theoretical models of correlated electrons, such as the [high-temperature superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-temperature_superconductivity), and in [photoemission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoemission), where a metal's [Fermi surface](wikipedia://en/986096) can be directly observed.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Luttinger's theorem states that **the volume enclosed by a material's Fermi surface is directly proportional to the particle density**.

While the theorem is an immediate result of the [Pauli exclusion principle](wikipedia://en/24669) in the case of noninteracting particles, it remains true even as interactions between particles are taken into consideration provided that the appropriate definitions of Fermi surface and particle density are adopted. Specifically, in the interacting case the Fermi surface must be defined according to the criteria that

$G(\omega=0,\,p) \to 0$ or $\infty,$

where $G$ is the single-particle [Green function](wikipedia://en/311001) in terms of [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) and [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431). Then Luttinger's theorem can be recast into the form[^o6]

$$n = 2 \int_{\mathcal{Re\,}G(\omega=0,k)>0}\frac{d^D k}{(2\pi)^D},$$

where $\mathcal{Re\,}G$ is the real part of the above [Green function](wikipedia://en/311001) and $d^D k$ is the differential volume of $k$-space in $D$ dimensions.
