# Michel Kervaire

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Kervaire"}

**Michel André Kervaire** (26 April 1927 – 19 November 2007) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made significant contributions to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923).

:::

He introduced the [Kervaire semi-characteristic](wikipedia://en/35176060). He was the first to show the existence of topological *n*-[manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) with no [differentiable structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_structure) (using the [Kervaire invariant](wikipedia://en/13816037)), and (with [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632)) computed the number of [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931) in dimensions greater than four, known as [Kervaire–Milnor groups](wikipedia://en/79124653). He is also well known for fundamental contributions to high-dimensional [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008). The solution of the [Kervaire invariant problem](wikipedia://en/13816037) was announced by [Michael Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 21 April 2009.

## Education {#Education}

He was the son of André Kervaire (a French industrialist) and Nelly Derancourt. After completing high school in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), Kervaire pursued his studies at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) (1947–1952), receiving a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1955. His thesis, entitled *Courbure intégrale généralisée et homotopie*, was written under the direction of [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082) and [Beno Eckmann](wikipedia://en/19613365).

## Career {#Career}

Kervaire was a professor at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University)'s [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) from 1959 to 1971, and then at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) from 1971 to 1997, when he retired.[^o6] He received an honorary doctorate from the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel) in 1986; he was also an honorary member of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society).[^o7]
