# George W. Whitehead

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George W. Whitehead"}

**George William Whitehead, Jr.** (August 2, 1918 – April 12, 2004) was an American professor of mathematics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), a member of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), and a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences). He is known for his work on [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).[^o3] He invented the [J-homomorphism](wikipedia://en/3061995), and was among the first to systematically calculate the [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697). He is also central to the study of [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655), in particular making concrete the connections between [spectra](wikipedia://en/1033697) and [generalized homology/cohomology theories](wikipedia://en/194060#Axioms_and_generalized_cohomology_theories).[^o4]

:::

Whitehead was born in [Bloomington, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Illinois), and received his Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1941, under the supervision of [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod).[^o3] After teaching at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), and [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), he took a position at MIT in 1949, where he remained until his retirement in 1985. He advised 13 Ph.D. students, including [Robert Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aumann), [Edgar Brown, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Brown,_Jr.), and [John Coleman Moore](wikipedia://en/13433862), and has over 1,940 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy) (as of 2025).

He was a member of the [American Academy of Arts & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_&_Sciences) (elected in 1954)[^o6] and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (elected in 1972).[^o7]
