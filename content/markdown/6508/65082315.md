# Robert John Audley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert John Audley"}

**Robert John Audley** was a British psychologist whose research was concerned with choice and decision-making.

:::

## Career {#Career}

**Robert (Bob) Audley** was born in London in 1925. Following [national service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_service), he obtained his BSc from [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) in 1949. Among his lecturers was [E.S. Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.S._Pearson). He then obtained a [Fulbright scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_scholarship) which took him to [Washington State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University). On his return he completed a PhD supervised by [A.R. Jonckheere](wikipedia://en/50408842) at [UCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London).[^o4] He was appointed to the faculty and remained there for the whole of his academic career. He served as head of department.[^o5]

He was active in the British Psychological Society of which he became president in 1981. His Presidential address was on the subject of choice.[^o6] He was Editor of the [British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology](wikipedia://en/23825185) from 1963 to 1969. He was also President of the Experimental Psychology Society about which he was interviewed.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

There were three strands to his research. The first strand, as a mathematical psychologist, he developed a Theory of Choice to explain the process of decision-making (Audley, 1960; Audley & Pike, 1965). His second strand was on reaction time (Audley, Caudrey, Howell and Powell, 1975) and the third was on medical accidents (Audley, Vincents & Ennis, 1993).
