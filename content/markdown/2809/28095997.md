# Julian Besag

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Julian Besag"}

**Julian Ernst Besag** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (26 March 1945 – 6 August 2010) was a British [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) known chiefly for his work in [spatial statistics](wikipedia://en/9467349) (including its applications to [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997), [image analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_analysis) and [agricultural science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_science)), and [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571) (including [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) algorithms).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Besag was born in [Loughborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough) and was educated at [Loughborough Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_Grammar_School). He began studying [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) but moved to the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham) to study statistics, obtaining his [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSc) in 1968.

## Career {#Career}

He then spent a year as a [research assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_assistant) to [Maurice Bartlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Bartlett) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) before obtaining a [lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lectureship) at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool). Inspired by [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974), he visited Princeton for a year.

He moved to the [University of Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Durham) in 1975, where he became a professor in 1986. He was a visiting professor at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in Seattle during 1989–90 and, after a year at [Newcastle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University), returned to Seattle long-term. He officially retired in 2007 but remained an [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) professor. At his death in 2010 he was also a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) at the Universities of [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) and [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol).

Besag was an [ISI highly cited researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISI_highly_cited_researcher); his 1986 paper "On the Statistical Analysis of Dirty Pictures" was the most cited paper by a UK mathematical scientist in the 1980s. The [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) awarded him its [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Silver in 1983 for his contributions to spatial statistics, and he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2004.[^o6]

## Notable contributions {#Notable_contributions}

### Spatial statistics {#Spatial_statistics}

For an array of random variables Y<sub>ij</sub>, [stochastic dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_dependence) was known to be important. Julian initially researched a model to for the correlation between Y<sub>ij</sub> pairs as a function of the distance between the corresponding lattice point pairs. However, this proved to be difficult due to ambiguous conditions for self-consistency.

He therefore suggested using multivariate distributions for such a variable, taking inspiration from statistical physics and unpublished work by Peter Clifford and [John Hammersley](wikipedia://en/656531):[^o6]

$$P[Y_{i,j}|Y_{k,l}:(k,l)\neq(i,j)]$$

He published his findings to the Royal Statistical Society in March 1974.[^o9]

## Death {#Death}

Besag died in Bristol, 2010 following a surgery.[^o6]
