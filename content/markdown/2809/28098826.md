# Denis Sargan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Denis Sargan"}

**John Denis Sargan**, FBA (23 August 1924 – 13 April 1996) was a British econometrician who specialized in the analysis of economic [time-series](wikipedia://en/406624).

:::

Sargan was born in [Doncaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster),[^o3] [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire) in 1924, and was educated at [Doncaster Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster_Grammar_School) and [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge). He made many contributions, notably in [instrumental variables](wikipedia://en/1514405) [estimation](wikipedia://en/10043), [Edgeworth expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgeworth_expansion) for the distributions of econometric estimators, identification conditions in [simultaneous equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_equations) models, [asymptotic tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_test) for [overidentifying restrictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overidentifying_restrictions) in [homoskedastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoskedastic) equations and [exact tests](wikipedia://en/2485378) for [unit roots](wikipedia://en/2398969) in [autoregressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoregressive) and [moving average](wikipedia://en/990809) models. At the [LSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics), Sargan was Professor of Econometrics from 1964 to 1984.[^o5] Sargan was [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_the_Econometric_Society) of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society), a Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy)[^o6] and an (honorary foreign) member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o5][^o7]

His influence on econometric methodology is evident in several fields including in the development of [Generalized Method of Moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Method_of_Moments) estimators.
