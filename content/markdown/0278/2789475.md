# Paul Sally

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Sally"}

**Paul Joseph Sally Jr.** (January 29, 1933 – December 30, 2013) was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago),[^o3] where he was the director of undergraduate studies for 30 years.[^o21][^o4] His research areas were [*p*-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).[^o5]

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Sally was born in the [Roslindale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roslindale,_Boston,_Massachusetts) neighborhood of [Boston, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston) on January 29, 1933.[^o7][^o15] He was a star basketball player at [Boston College High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College_High_School).[^o5][^o15] He received his [BS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) and [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) degrees from [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College) in 1954 and 1956.[^o25]

After a short career in Boston area high schools and at Boston College,[^o8] he entered the first class of mathematics graduate students at [Brandeis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in 1957,[^o5] and earned his PhD in 1965.[^o7] During his graduate career he married [Judith D. Sally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_D._Sally) and had three children in three years. David, the oldest, is a Visiting Associate Professor of Business Administration at [Tuck School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuck_School_of_Business) at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College),[^o5][^o9] Stephen is a partner at [White & Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_&_Case)[^o10] (previously [Ropes & Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ropes_&_Gray)),[^o5][^o11] and Paul, the youngest, is Superintendent at [New Trier High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Trier_High_School).[^o5][^o12]

Sally was diagnosed with [type 1 diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus_type_1) in 1948.[^o13] The condition resulted in his use of an [eye patch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_patch) and two [prosthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosthetic) legs,[^o14] which caused him to be widely referred to as "Professor Pirate," and "The Math Pirate" around the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) campus.[^o15] He was known to detest cell phones in class and destroyed several over the years by inviting students to stomp on them or by throwing them out of a window.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Sally joined the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) faculty in 1965 and taught there until his death.[^o5] He was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1967–68, 1971–72, 1981–82, and 1983–84.[^o17]

While at the IAS he collaborated with [Joseph Shalika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Shalika).[^o18] In 1983, he became the first director of the [University of Chicago School Mathematics Project](wikipedia://en/26837834), which is responsible for the [Everyday Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everyday_Mathematics) program (also called "Chicago math").[^o5]

He founded Seminars for Elementary Specialists and Mathematics Educators (SESAME) in 1992.[^o5] He co-founded the Young Scholars Program with Dr. Diane Herrmann in 1988, providing mathematical enrichment for gifted Chicago-area students in grades 7–12.[^o5][^o19]

## Death {#Death}

*Image: Graves of Paul Joseph Sally Jr. (1933–2013) and Judith Donovan Sally (1937–2024) at Maryhill Catholic Cemetery, Niles, IL.jpg.* Graves of Paul and Judith Sally at Maryhill Catholic Cemetery

Sally died December 30, 2013, aged 80, from [congestive heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestive_heart_failure), at the University of Chicago Hospital, and was buried at Maryhill Catholic Cemetery in Niles, Illinois.[^o21][^o22][^o23]

## Awards {#Awards}

- Amoco Foundation Award for Long-Term Excellence in Undergraduate Teaching, 1995[^o25][^o26]
- [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Distinguished Service Award, 2000[^o27]
- One of the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Awards for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), 2002[^o27]
- Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), 2012.[^o28]
