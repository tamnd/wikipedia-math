# Raymond Paley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raymond Paley"}

*Image: Paley.jpg.* Photograph of Paley's grave in The Old Banff Cemetery. The main gate is visible on the left.

**Raymond Edward Alan Christopher Paley** (7 January 1907 – 7 April 1933) was an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made significant contributions to [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) before dying young in a skiing accident.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Paley was born in [Bournemouth, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth,_England), the son of an artillery officer who died of tuberculosis before Paley was born. He was educated at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College) as a [King's Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Scholar) and at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). He became a [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1928, and with [J. A. Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._A._Todd), he was one of two winners of the 1930 [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) examination.

He was elected a Research Fellow of Trinity College in 1930, edging out Todd for the position, and continued at Cambridge as a postgraduate student, advised by [John Edensor Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371). After the 1931 return of [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) to Cambridge he participated in weekly joint seminars with the other students of Hardy and Littlewood. He traveled to the US in 1932 to work with [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and with [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), and as part of the same trip also planned to work with [Lipót Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357) at a seminar in Chicago organized as part of the [Century of Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_of_Progress) exposition.

He was killed on 7 April 1933 in a skiing trip to the [Canadian Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Rockies), by an [avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalanche) on [Deception Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deception_Pass_%28Alberta%29).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Paley's contributions include the following.

- His mathematical research with Littlewood began in 1929, with his work towards a fellowship at Trinity, and Hardy writes that "Littlewood's influence dominates nearly all his earliest work". Their work became the foundation for [Littlewood–Paley theory](wikipedia://en/24465861), an application of real-variable techniques in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).a
- The Walsh–Paley numeration, a standard method for indexing the [Walsh functions](wikipedia://en/380839), came from a 1932 suggestion of Paley.b
- Paley collaborated with [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477) on [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038), continuing the work on this topic that he had already done with Littlewood. His work in this area also led to the [Paley–Zygmund inequality](wikipedia://en/9414239) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).c
- In a 1933 paper, he published the [Paley construction](wikipedia://en/6899907) for [Hadamard matrices](wikipedia://en/507553).d In the same paper, he first formulated the [Hadamard conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_conjecture) on the sizes of matrices for which Hadamard matrices exist. The [Paley graphs](wikipedia://en/5719307) and Paley [tournaments](wikipedia://en/1427502) in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) are closely related, although they do not appear explicitly in this work. In the context of [compressed sensing](wikipedia://en/11403316), [frames](wikipedia://en/4504771) (partial bases of [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932)) derived from this construction have been called "Paley equiangular tight frames".
- His collaboration with [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185) included the [Paley–Wiener theorem](wikipedia://en/632992) in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). Paley was originally selected as the 1934 [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Colloquium Lecturer; after his death, Wiener replaced him as speaker, and spoke on their joint work, which was published as a book.e
