# Mixed boundary condition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mixed boundary condition"}

*Image: Mixed boundary conditions.svg.* Green: Neumann boundary condition; purple: Dirichlet boundary condition.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **mixed boundary condition** for a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) defines a [boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/436779) in which the solution of the given equation is required to satisfy different [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_condition) on [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) parts of the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of the [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) where the condition is stated. Precisely, in a mixed boundary value problem, the solution is required to satisfy a [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/423504) or a [Neumann boundary condition](wikipedia://en/735443) in a mutually exclusive way on disjoint parts of the boundary.

:::

For example, given a solution $u$ to a partial differential equation on a domain $\Omega$ with boundary $\partial\Omega$, it is said to satisfy a mixed boundary condition if, consisting $\partial\Omega$ of two disjoint parts, $\Gamma_{1}$ and $\Gamma_{2}$, such that $\partial\Omega = \Gamma_{1} \cup \Gamma_{2}$, $u$ verifies the following equations:

$\left. u \right|_{\Gamma_1} = u_0$10and10$\left. \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right|_{\Gamma_2} = g,$

where $u_{0}$ and $g$ are given functions defined on those portions of the boundary.[^o2]

The mixed boundary condition differs from the [Robin boundary condition](wikipedia://en/9945920) in that the latter requires a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632), possibly with [pointwise](wikipedia://en/4786797) variable coefficients, of the Dirichlet and the Neumann boundary value conditions to be satisfied on the whole boundary of a given domain.

## Historical note {#Historical_note}

The first boundary value problem satisfying a mixed boundary condition was solved by [Stanisław Zaremba](wikipedia://en/1028345) for the [Laplace equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace_equation): according to himself, it was [Wilhelm Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921) who suggested him to study this problem.[^o6]
