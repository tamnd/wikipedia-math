# Mostow rigidity theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mostow rigidity theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Mostow's rigidity theorem**, or **strong rigidity theorem**, or **Mostow–Prasad rigidity theorem**, essentially states that the geometry of a complete, finite-volume [hyperbolic manifold](wikipedia://en/1589135) of dimension greater than two is determined by the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) and hence unique. The theorem was proven for [closed manifolds](wikipedia://en/669475) by (txt) and extended to finite volume manifolds by (Marden 1974) in 3 dimensions, and by (txt) in all dimensions at least 3. (Gromov 1981) gave an alternate proof using the [Gromov norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov_norm). (Besson Courtois Gallot 1996) gave the simplest available proof.

:::

While the theorem shows that the deformation space of (complete) hyperbolic structures on a finite volume hyperbolic $n$-manifold (for $n >2$) is a point, for a hyperbolic surface of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) $g>1$ there is a [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of dimension $6g-6$ that parameterizes all metrics of constant curvature (up to [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564)), a fact essential for [Teichmüller theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_theory). There is also a rich theory of deformation spaces of hyperbolic structures on *infinite* volume manifolds in three dimensions.

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="The theorem"}

## The theorem {#The_theorem}

The theorem can be given in a geometric formulation (pertaining to finite-volume, complete manifolds), and in an algebraic formulation (pertaining to lattices in [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945)).

### Geometric form {#Geometric_form}

Let $\mathbb H^n$ be the $n$-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742). A complete hyperbolic manifold can be defined as a quotient of $\mathbb H^n$ by a group of isometries acting freely and [properly discontinuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Types_of_action) (it is equivalent to define it as a [Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature -1](wikipedia://en/1589135) which is [complete](wikipedia://en/144652#Riemannian_manifolds_as_metric_spaces)). It is of finite volume if the integral of a [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811) is finite (which is the case, for example, if it is compact). The Mostow rigidity theorem may be stated as:

*Suppose $M$ and $N$ are complete finite-volume hyperbolic manifolds of dimension $n \ge 3$. If there exists an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) $f\colon \pi_1(M) \to \pi_1(N)$ then it is induced by a unique isometry from $M$ to $N$.*

Here $\pi_1(X)$ is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a manifold $X$. If $X$ is a hyperbolic manifold obtained as the quotient of $\mathbb H^n$ by a group $\Gamma$ then $\pi_1(X) \cong \Gamma$.

An equivalent statement is that any [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) from $M$ to $N$ can be homotoped to a unique isometry. The proof actually shows that if $N$ has greater dimension than $M$ then there can be no homotopy equivalence between them.

### Algebraic form {#Algebraic_form}

The group of isometries of hyperbolic space $\mathbb H^n$ can be identified with the Lie group $\mathrm{PO}(n,1)$ (the [projective orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/4845690) of a [quadratic form of signature](wikipedia://en/251478#Real_quadratic_forms) $(n,1)$. Then the following statement is equivalent to the one above.

*Let $n \ge 3$ and $\Gamma$ and $\Lambda$ be two [lattices](wikipedia://en/15782871) in $\mathrm{PO}(n,1)$ and suppose that there is a group isomorphism $f\colon \Gamma \to \Lambda$. Then $\Gamma$ and $\Lambda$ are conjugate in $\mathrm{PO}(n,1)$. That is, there exists a $g \in \mathrm{PO}(n,1)$ such that $\Lambda = g \Gamma g^{-1}$.*

### In greater generality {#In_greater_generality}

Mostow rigidity holds (in its geometric formulation) more generally for fundamental groups of all complete, finite volume, non-positively curved (without Euclidean factors) [locally symmetric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_symmetric_space) of dimension at least three, or in its algebraic formulation for all lattices in [simple Lie groups](wikipedia://en/292831) not locally isomorphic to $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{R})$.

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

It follows from the Mostow rigidity theorem that the group of isometries of a finite-volume hyperbolic $n$-manifold $M$ (for $n > 2$) is finite and isomorphic to $\operatorname{Out}(\pi_1(M))$.

Mostow rigidity was also used by Thurston to prove the uniqueness of [circle packing representations](wikipedia://en/16648043) of [triangulated planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314).[^n1]

A consequence of Mostow rigidity of interest in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) is that there exist [hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817) which are [quasi-isometric](wikipedia://en/12747259) but not [commensurable](wikipedia://en/29343178) to each other.

:::
