# Constant sheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Constant sheaf"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **constant sheaf** on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ associated to a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $A$ is a [sheaf of sets](wikipedia://en/245466) on $X$ whose [stalks](wikipedia://en/15879621) are all equal to $A$. It is denoted by $\underline{A}$ or $A_X$. The **constant presheaf** with value $A$ is the [presheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf) that assigns to each [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358) of $X$ the value $A$, and all of whose restriction maps are the identity map $A\to A$. The constant sheaf associated to $A$ is the [sheafification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheafification) of the constant presheaf associated to $A$. This sheaf may be identified with the sheaf of locally constant $A$-valued functions on $X$.[^o2]

:::

In certain cases, the set $A$ may be replaced with an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $A$ in some [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\textbf{C}$ (e.g. when $\textbf{C}$ is the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748), or [commutative rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_commutative_rings)).

Constant sheaves of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) appear in particular as coefficients in [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357).

## Basics {#Basics}

Let $X$ be a topological space, and $A$ a set. The sections of the constant sheaf $\underline{A}$ over an open set $U$ may be interpreted as the continuous functions $U\to A$, where $A$ is given the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology). If $U$ is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), then these locally constant functions are constant. If $f:X\to\{\text{pt}\}$ is the unique [map](wikipedia://en/516931) to the one-point space and $A$ is considered as a sheaf on $\{\text{pt}\}$, then the [inverse image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_image_sheaf) $f^{-1}A$ is the constant sheaf $\underline{A}$ on $X$. The [sheaf space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_space) of $\underline{A}$ is the projection map $A$ (where $X\times A\to X$ is given the discrete topology).

## A detailed example {#A_detailed_example}

*Image: Constantpresheaf.png.* Constant presheaf on a two-point discrete space

*Image: 2 point discrete space.png.* Two-point discrete topological space

Let $X$ be the topological space consisting of two points $p$ and $q$ with the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology). $X$ has four open sets: $\varnothing, \{p\}, \{q\}, \{p,q\}$. The five non-trivial inclusions of the open sets of $X$ are shown in the chart.

A presheaf on $X$ chooses a set for each of the four open sets of $X$ and a restriction map for each of the [inclusions](wikipedia://en/492516) (with [identity map](wikipedia://en/15069) for $U\subset U$). The **constant presheaf** with value $\textbf{Z}$, denoted $F$, is the presheaf where all four sets are $\textbf{Z}$, the integers, and all restriction maps are the identity. $F$ is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) on the diagram of inclusions (a presheaf), because it is constant. It satisfies the gluing axiom, but is not a sheaf because it fails the local identity axiom on the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566). This is because the empty set is covered by the empty [family of sets](wikipedia://en/723043), $\varnothing = \bigcup\nolimits_{U\in\{\}} U$, and vacuously, any two sections in $F(\varnothing)$ are equal when restricted to any set in the empty family $\{\}$. The local identity axiom would therefore imply that any two sections in $F(\varnothing)$ are equal, which is false.

To modify this into a presheaf $G$ that satisfies the local identity axiom, let $G(\varnothing)=0$, a one-element set, and give $G$ the value $\textbf{Z}$ on all non-empty sets. For each inclusion of open sets, let the restriction be the unique map to 0 if the smaller set is empty, or the identity map otherwise. Note that $G(\varnothing)=0$ is forced by the local identity axiom.

*Image: Constantsheaf intermediate step.png.* Intermediate step for the constant sheaf

Now $G$ is a separated presheaf (satisfies local identity), but unlike $F$ it fails the gluing axiom. Indeed, $\{p,q\}$ is [disconnected](wikipedia://en/6233), covered by non-intersecting open sets $\{p\}$ and $\{q\}$. Choose distinct sections $m\neq n$ in $\mathbf Z$ over $\{p\}$ and $\{q\}$ respectively. Because $m$ and $n$ restrict to the same element 0 over $\varnothing$, the gluing axiom would guarantee the existence of a unique section $s$ on $G(\{p,q\})$ that restricts to $m$ on $\{p\}$ and $n$ on $\{q\}$; but the restriction maps are the identity, giving $m = s = n$, which is false. Intuitively, $G(\{p,q\})$ is too small to carry information about both connected components $\{p\}$ and $\{q\}$.

*Image: Constant sheaf with product.png.* Constant sheaf on a two-point topological space

Modifying further to satisfy the gluing axiom, let

> $$H(\{p,q\}) = \mathrm{Fun}(\{p,q\},\mathbf{Z})\cong \mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},$$

the $\mathbf Z$-valued functions on $\{p,q\}$, and define the restriction maps of $H$ to be natural restriction of functions to $\{p\}$ and $\{q\}$, with the zero map restricting to $\varnothing$. Then $H$ is a sheaf, called the **constant sheaf** on $X$ with value $\textbf{Z}$. Since all restriction maps are ring homomorphisms, $H$ is a sheaf of commutative rings.
