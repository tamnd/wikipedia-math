# Numerical resistivity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Numerical resistivity"}

**Numerical resistivity** is a problem in [computer simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation) of ideal [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310) (MHD).[^o2] It is a form of [numerical diffusion](wikipedia://en/2785898). In near-ideal MHD systems, the magnetic field diffuses only very slowly through the [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_%28physics%29) or [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid) of the system; its rate-limited by the inverse of the [resistivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistivity) of the fluid. In [Eulerian simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulerian_method) where the field is arbitrarily aligned compared to the simulation grid, the [numerical diffusion](wikipedia://en/2785898) behaves similarly to an additional resistivity, causing non-physical and sometimes bursty [magnetic reconnection](wikipedia://en/1166647) in the simulation. Numerical resistivity is influenced by resolution, the alignment of the magnetic field with the grid, and the numerical method used. In general, numerical resistivity does not behave isotropically, and different parts can exhibit varying effective numerical resistivities. For simulations of the [solar corona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_corona) and inner [heliosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliosphere) as of 2005, this numerical effect can be several orders of magnitude larger than the physical resistivity of the plasma.

:::
