# Function field of an algebraic variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Function field of an algebraic variety"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **function field** of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* consists of objects that are interpreted as [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) on *V*. In classical algebraic geometry they are [ratios of polynomials](wikipedia://en/361210); in [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101) these are [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) and their higher-dimensional analogues; in [modern algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/364754) they are elements of some [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635)'s [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271).

:::

## Definition for complex manifolds {#Definition_for_complex_manifolds}

In complex geometry the objects of study are [complex analytic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analytic_varieties), on which we have a local notion of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), through which we may define meromorphic functions. The function field of a variety is then the set of all meromorphic functions on the variety. (Like all meromorphic functions, these take their values in $\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}$.) Together with the operations of addition and multiplication of functions, this is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) in the sense of algebra.

For the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799), which is the variety $\mathbb{P}^1$ over the complex numbers, the global meromorphic functions are exactly the [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) (that is, the ratios of complex polynomial functions).

## Construction in algebraic geometry {#Construction_in_algebraic_geometry}

In classical algebraic geometry, we generalize the second point of view. For the Riemann sphere, above, the notion of a polynomial is not defined globally, but simply with respect to an [affine](wikipedia://en/298834) coordinate chart, namely that consisting of the complex plane (all but the north pole of the sphere). On a general variety *V*, we say that a rational function on an open affine subset *U* is defined as the ratio of two polynomials in the [affine coordinate ring](wikipedia://en/320468) of *U*, and that a rational function on all of *V* consists of such local data as agree on the intersections of open affines. We may define the function field of *V* to be the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of the affine coordinate ring of any open affine subset, since all such subsets are dense.

## Generalization to arbitrary scheme {#Generalization_to_arbitrary_scheme}

In the most general setting, that of modern [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory), the latter point of view above is taken as a point of departure. Namely, if $X$ is an integral [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754), then for every open affine subset $U$ of $X$ the ring of sections $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ on $U$ is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) and, hence, has a field of fractions. Furthermore, it can be verified that these are all the same, and are all equal to the [stalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalk_of_a_sheaf) of the [generic point](wikipedia://en/3043978) of $X$. Thus the function field of $X$ is just the stalk of its generic point.[^o5]

## Geometry of the function field {#Geometry_of_the_function_field}

If *V* is a variety defined over a field *K*, then the function field *K*(*V*) is a finitely generated [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of the ground field *K*; its [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) is equal to the [dimension](wikipedia://en/253251) of the variety. All extensions of *K* that are finitely generated as fields over *K* arise in this way from some algebraic variety. These field extensions are also known as [algebraic function fields](wikipedia://en/447645) over *K*.

Properties of the variety *V* that depend only on the function field are studied in [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733).

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The function field of a point over *K* is *K*.

The function field of the affine line over *K* is isomorphic to the field *K*(*t*) of [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) in one variable. This is also the function field of the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578).

Consider the [affine algebraic plane curve](wikipedia://en/253260) defined by the equation $y^2 = x^5 + 1$. Its function field is the field *K*(*x*,*y*), generated by elements *x* and *y* that are [transcendental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_element) over *K* and satisfy the algebraic relation $y^2 = x^5 + 1$.
