# Beyond Infinity (mathematics book)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Beyond Infinity"}

***Beyond Infinity : An Expedition to the Outer Limits of Mathematics*** is a [popular mathematics](wikipedia://en/2069923) book by [Eugenia Cheng](wikipedia://en/47503508) centered on concepts of [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824). It was published by [Basic Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Books) and (with a slightly different title) by [Profile Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profile_Books) in 2017, and in a paperback edition in 2018. It was shortlisted for the [2017 Royal Society Insight Investment Science Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Prizes_for_Science_Books).

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

The book is divided into two parts, with the first exploring notions leading to concepts of [actual infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actual_infinity), concrete but infinite mathematical values. After an exploration of [number systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_system), this part discusses [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [cardinal numbers](wikipedia://en/6173), and [ordinal numbers](wikipedia://en/26547932), [transfinite arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfinite_arithmetic), and the existence of different infinite sizes of sets. Topics used to illustrate these concepts include [Hilbert's paradox of the Grand Hotel](wikipedia://en/37797), [Cantor's diagonal argument](wikipedia://en/51426), and the unprovability of the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705).

The second part concerns mathematics related to the idea of [potential infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_infinity), the assignment of finite values to the results of infinite processes including [growth rates](wikipedia://en/641995), [limits](wikipedia://en/26551602), and [infinite series](wikipedia://en/15287). This part also discusses [Zeno's paradoxes](wikipedia://en/34535), [Dedekind cuts](wikipedia://en/45569), the [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398) of spaces, and the possibility of spaces of infinite dimensions, with a mention of [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013), Cheng's research specialty.

The mathematics is frequently lightened and made accessible with personal experiences and stories, involving such subjects as the [Loch Ness Monster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loch_Ness_Monster), [puff pastry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puff_pastry), boating, dance contests, shoes, "Legos, the iPod Shuffle, snorkeling, Battenberg cakes and Winnie-the-Pooh".

## Audience and reception {#Audience_and_reception}

The Royal Society judges called *Beyond Infinity* "a very engaging introduction to a forbidding subject". Similarly, reviewer Anne Haworth calls it "engaging and readable", and *[Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)* reviewer Sam Kean writes that its "chatty tone keeps things fresh". It is aimed at a popular audience, not assumed to have a significant background in mathematics, including "the young or those brimming with curiosity" as well as college or secondary-school students, although it may be "too elementary for mathematicians or mathematics students".

As similar reading material, reviewer Andrew James Simoson suggests placing this book alongside *The Book of Numbers* by [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Richard K. Guy](wikipedia://en/816308) (1996), *[One Two Three... Infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Two_Three..._Infinity)* by [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow) (1947), and *Really Big Numbers* by [Richard Schwartz](wikipedia://en/1213887) (2014).
