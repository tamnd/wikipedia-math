# Zhilan Feng

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zhilan Feng"}

**Zhilan Julie Feng** (born 1959) is a Chinese-American applied mathematician whose research topics include [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology), [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466), and [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997). She is a professor of mathematics at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), and a program director in the Division of Mathematical Sciences at the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Feng studied mathematics at [Jilin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin_University) in China, earning a bachelor's degree in 1982 and a master's degree in 1985. She came to [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) for graduate study, completing her Ph.D. in 1994. Her dissertation, *A Mathematical Model for the Dynamics of Childhood Diseases Under the Impact of Isolation*, was supervised by Horst R. Thieme.

After her postdoctoral study at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), she joined Purdue University as an assistant professor in 1996. She was promoted to full professor in 2005, and became a program director at the National Science Foundation in 2019.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Feng was named a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), in the 2022 class of fellows, "for contributions to applied mathematics, particularly in biology, ecology, and epidemiology".

## Books {#Books}

Feng's books include:

- *Disease Evolution: Models, Concepts, and Data Analyses* (American Mathematical Society, 2006, edited with Ulf Dieckmann and [Simon A. Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_A._Levin))
- *Applications of Epidemiological Models to Public Health Policymaking: The role of heterogeneity in model predictions* (World Scientific, 2014)
- *Mathematical Models of Plant-Herbivore Interactions* (Chapman & Hall / CRC, 2018, with Donald DeAngelis)
- *Mathematical Models in Epidemiology* (Springer, 2019, with Fred Brauer and [Carlos Castillo-Chavez](wikipedia://en/15989869))
