# Valentine Bargmann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Valentine Bargmann"}

**Valentine** "**Valya**" **Bargmann** (April 6, 1908 – July 20, 1989)[^o3] was a German-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and theoretical [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in Berlin, [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), to a [German Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Germany) family, Bargmann studied there from 1925 to 1933. After the National Socialist [Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung), he moved to Switzerland to the [University of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zürich) where he received his Ph.D. under [Gregor Wentzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Wentzel).

He emigrated to the U.S., barely managing immigration acceptance, as his German passport was to be revoked with only two days of validity left.

At the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) (1937–1946) he worked as an assistant to [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736),[^o5] publishing with him and [Peter Bergmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bergmann) on classical five-dimensional [Kaluza–Klein theory](wikipedia://en/17070) (1941). He taught at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1946 for the rest of his career.

He pioneered understanding of the [irreducible unitary representations of SL<sub>2</sub>(**R**)](wikipedia://en/11103925) and the [Lorentz group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representations_of_the_Lorentz_group) (1947). He further formulated the [Bargmann–Wigner equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargmann–Wigner_equations) with [Eugene Wigner](wikipedia://en/10101) (1948), for particles of arbitrary spin, building up on work of several theorists who pioneered [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202).[^o6][^o7]

[Bargmann's theorem](wikipedia://en/348860#Infinite-dimensional_projective_unitary_representations:_Bargmann's_theorem) (1954) on projective unitary representations of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) gives a condition for when a projective unitary representation of a Lie group comes from an ordinary unitary representation of its universal cover.

Bargmann further discovered the [Bargmann–Michel–Telegdi equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larmor_precession#Bargmann–Michel–Telegdi_equation) (1959) describing relativistic precession; [Bargmann's limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargmann's_limit) of the maximum number of QM bound states of a potential (1952); the notion of **Bargmann potentials**[^o8] for the radial Schrödinger equations with bound states but no non-trivial scattering, which play a basic role in the theory of [Solitons](wikipedia://en/51654), and the holomorphic representation in the [Segal–Bargmann space](wikipedia://en/40731477) (1961), including the [Bargmann kernel](wikipedia://en/34205013#Holomorphic_Fock_space).

Bargmann was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1968.[^o9] In 1978, he received the [Wigner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner_Medal), together with Wigner himself, in the founding year of the prize. In 1979, Bargmann was elected to the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o10] In 1988, he received the [Max Planck Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Medal) of the [German Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Physical_Society).

He was also a talented pianist.

He died in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) in 1989.
