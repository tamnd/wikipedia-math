# Arjen Lenstra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arjen Lenstra"}

*Image: Arjen Lenstra-IMG 8901.jpg.* Arjen Klaas Lenstra

**Arjen Klaas Lenstra** (born 2 March 1956, in Groningen) is a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [cryptographer](wikipedia://en/18934432) and computational [number theorist](wikipedia://en/21527). He is a professor emeritus from the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (EPFL) where he headed of the Laboratory for Cryptologic Algorithms.[^o2]

:::

## Career {#Career}

He studied mathematics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam). He is a former professor at the [EPFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPFL) ([Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne)), in the Laboratory for Cryptologic Algorithms, and previously worked for [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank) and [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs).

## Research {#Research}

Lenstra is active in [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [computational number theory](wikipedia://en/511466), especially in areas such as [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491). With [Mark Manasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Manasse), he was the first to seek volunteers over the internet for a large scale [volunteer computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_computing) project.[^o5] Such projects became more common after the [Factorization of RSA-129](wikipedia://en/511379#RSA-129) which was a high publicity distributed factoring success led by Lenstra along with [Derek Atkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Atkins), Michael Graff and [Paul Leyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Leyland). He was also a leader in the successful factorizations of several other [RSA numbers](wikipedia://en/511379).

Lenstra was also involved in the development of the [number field sieve](wikipedia://en/152734).[^o6] With coauthors, he showed the great potential of the algorithm early on by using it to factor the ninth [Fermat number](wikipedia://en/91127), which was far out of reach by other factoring algorithms of the time.[^o7] He has since been involved with several other number field sieve factorizations including [RSA-768](wikipedia://en/511379#RSA-768).

Lenstra's most widely cited scientific result is the first polynomial time algorithm to [factor polynomials with rational coefficients](wikipedia://en/3776351#Uses_of_LLL_algorithm) in the seminal paper that introduced the [LLL lattice reduction algorithm](wikipedia://en/1735228) with [Hendrik Willem Lenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Willem_Lenstra) and [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938).[^o8]

Lenstra is also co-inventor of the [XTR](wikipedia://en/3963209) [cryptosystem](wikipedia://en/506383).

On 1 March 2005, Arjen Lenstra, [Xiaoyun Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaoyun_Wang), and Benne de Weger of [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology) demonstrated[^o9] construction of two [X.509](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X.509) certificates with different public keys and the same [MD5](wikipedia://en/18826) hash, a demonstrably practical [hash collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hash_collision). The construction included private keys for both public keys.

## Distinctions {#Distinctions}

Lenstra is the recipient of the [RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Award_for_Excellence_in_Mathematics) 2008 Award.[^o11]

## Private life {#Private_life}

Lenstra's brother and co-author [Hendrik Lenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lenstra) is a professor in mathematics at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) and his brother [Jan Karel Lenstra](wikipedia://en/33685896) is a former director of [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063) (CWI).
