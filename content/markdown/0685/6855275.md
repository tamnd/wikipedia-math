# Courant bracket

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Courant bracket"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a field of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Courant bracket** is a generalization of the [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/294437) from an operation on the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) to an operation on the [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_vector_bundles) of the tangent bundle and the [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) of [*p*-forms](wikipedia://en/221519).

:::

The case $p = 1$ was introduced by [Theodore James Courant](wikipedia://en/22117952) in his 1990 doctoral dissertation as a structure that bridges [Poisson geometry](wikipedia://en/882902) and [pre-symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), based on work with his advisor [Alan Weinstein](wikipedia://en/7344764). The twisted version of the Courant bracket was introduced in 2001 by [Pavol Severa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavol_Severa), and studied in collaboration with Weinstein.

Today a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) version of the $p = 1$ Courant bracket plays a central role in the field of [generalized complex geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_complex_geometry), introduced by [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164) in 2002. Closure under the Courant bracket is the [integrability condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrability_condition) of a [generalized almost complex structure](wikipedia://en/514258#Generalized_almost_complex_structure).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *$X$* and *$Y$* be [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) on an $N$-dimensional real [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_%28mathematics%29) *$M$* and let *$\xi$* and *$\eta$* be *$p$*-forms. Then *$X + \xi$* and *$Y + \eta$* are [sections](wikipedia://en/276526#Sections) of the direct sum *$TM \oplus \wedge^p T^*M$* of the tangent bundle and the bundle of *$p$*-forms. The Courant bracket of *$X + \xi$* and *$Y + \eta$* is defined to be

$$[X+\xi,Y+\eta]=[X,Y] +\mathcal{L}_X\eta-\mathcal{L}_Y\xi -\frac{1}{2}d(i(X)\eta-i(Y)\xi)$$

where $\mathcal{L}_X$ is the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437) along the vector field *$X$*, *$d$* is the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) and *$\iota$* is the [interior product](wikipedia://en/2154963).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Courant bracket is [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176) but it does not satisfy the [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370) for *$p$* greater than zero.

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="The Jacobi identity"}

### The Jacobi identity {#The_Jacobi_identity}

However, at least in the case *$p = 1$*, the [Jacobiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobiator), which measures a bracket's failure to satisfy the Jacobi identity, is an [exact form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_form). It is the exterior derivative of a form which plays the role of the [Nijenhuis tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijenhuis_tensor) in generalized complex geometry.

The Courant bracket is the antisymmetrization of the [Dorfman bracket](wikipedia://en/6855275#Dorfman_bracket), which does satisfy a kind of Jacobi identity.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetries"}

### Symmetries {#Symmetries}

Like the Lie bracket, the Courant bracket is invariant under diffeomorphisms of the manifold *M*. It also enjoys an additional symmetry under the vector bundle [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160)

$$X+\xi\mapsto X+\xi+i(X)\alpha$$

where *$\alpha$* is a closed *$(p+1)$*-form. In the *$p = 1$* case, which is the relevant case for the geometry of [flux compactifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_%28physics%29#Flux_compactification) in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), this transformation is known in the physics literature as a shift in the [B field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalb–Ramond_field).

:::

:::

## Dirac and generalized complex structures {#Dirac_and_generalized_complex_structures}

The [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600), ${\mathbf T}^*$ of $M$ is the bundle of differential one-forms. In the case $p = 1$ the Courant bracket maps two sections of ${\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*$, the direct sum of the tangent and cotangent bundles, to another section of ${\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*$. The fibers of ${\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*$ admit [inner products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) with [signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_of_a_quadratic_form) $(N,N)$ given by

$$\langle X+\xi,Y+\eta\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\xi(Y)+\eta(X)).$$

A [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of ${\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*$ in which all pairs of vectors have zero inner product is said to be an [isotropic subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropic_subspace). The fibers of ${\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*$ are *$2N$*-dimensional and the maximal dimension of an isotropic subspace is *$N$*. An *$N$*-dimensional isotropic subspace is called a maximal isotropic subspace.

A [Dirac structure](wikipedia://en/41519162) is a maximally isotropic subbundle of ${\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*$ whose sections are closed under the Courant bracket. Dirac structures include as special cases [symplectic structures](wikipedia://en/294298), [Poisson structures](wikipedia://en/882902) and [foliated geometries](wikipedia://en/371227).

A [generalized complex structure](wikipedia://en/6871218) is defined identically, but one [tensors](wikipedia://en/30844) ${\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*$ by the complex numbers and uses the [complex dimension](wikipedia://en/2783513) in the above definitions and one imposes that the direct sum of the subbundle and its [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) be the entire original bundle $({\mathbf T}\oplus{\mathbf{T}}^*)\otimes \mathbf C$. Special cases of generalized complex structures include [complex structure](wikipedia://en/509742) and a version of [Kähler structure](wikipedia://en/390538) which includes the B-field.

::: {#o12 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Dorfman bracket"}

## Dorfman bracket {#Dorfman_bracket}

In 1987 [Irene Dorfman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Dorfman) introduced the Dorfman bracket $[\cdot, \cdot]_D$, which like the Courant bracket provides an integrability condition for Dirac structures. It is defined by

$$[A,B]_D=[A,B]+d\langle A,B\rangle.$$

The Dorfman bracket is not antisymmetric, but it is often easier to calculate with than the Courant bracket because it satisfies a [Leibniz rule](wikipedia://en/248988) which resembles the Jacobi identity

$$[A,[B,C]_D]_D=[[A,B]_D,C]_D+[B,[A,C]_D]_D.$$

:::

## Courant algebroid {#Courant_algebroid}

The Courant bracket does not satisfy the [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370) and so it does not define a [Lie algebroid](wikipedia://en/310921), in addition it fails to satisfy the Lie algebroid condition on the [anchor map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor_map). Instead it defines a more general structure introduced by [Zhang-Ju Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang-Ju_Liu), [Alan Weinstein](wikipedia://en/7344764) and [Ping Xu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_Xu) known as a [Courant algebroid](wikipedia://en/10109649).

## Twisted Courant bracket {#Twisted_Courant_bracket}

::: {#o16 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and properties"}

### Definition and properties {#Definition_and_properties}

The Courant bracket may be twisted by a *$(p+2)$*-form *$H$*, by adding the interior product of the vector fields *$X$* and *$Y$* of *$H$*. It remains antisymmetric and invariant under the addition of the interior product with a *$(p+1)$*-form *$B$*. When *$B$* is not closed then this invariance is still preserved if one adds *$dB$* to the final *$H$*.

If *$H$* is closed then the Jacobiator is exact and so the twisted Courant bracket still defines a Courant algebroid. In [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), *$H$* is interpreted as the [Neveu–Schwarz 3-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalb–Ramond_field).

:::

### *p=0*: Circle-invariant vector fields {#p=0:_Circle-invariant_vector_fields}

When $p=0$ the Courant bracket reduces to the Lie bracket on a [principal](wikipedia://en/310950) [circle bundle](wikipedia://en/2794417) over *$M$* with [curvature](wikipedia://en/130526) given by the 2-form twist *$H$*. The bundle of 0-forms is the trivial bundle, and a section of the direct sum of the tangent bundle and the trivial bundle defines a circle [invariant vector field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_vector_field) on this circle bundle.

Concretely, a section of the sum of the tangent and trivial bundles is given by a vector field *$X$* and a function *$f$* and the Courant bracket is

$$[X+f,Y+g]=[X,Y]+Xg-Yf$$

which is just the Lie bracket of the vector fields

$$[X+f,Y+g]=[X+f\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},Y+g\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}]_{Lie}$$

where *$\theta$* is a coordinate on the circle fiber. Note in particular that the Courant bracket satisfies the Jacobi identity in the case *$p=0$*.

### Integral twists and gerbes {#Integral_twists_and_gerbes}

The curvature of a circle bundle always represents an integral [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) class, the [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) of the circle bundle. Thus the above geometric interpretation of the twisted $p=0$ Courant bracket only exists when *H* represents an integral class. Similarly at higher values of *$p$* the twisted Courant brackets can be geometrically realized as untwisted Courant brackets twisted by [gerbes](wikipedia://en/822043) when *H* is an integral cohomology class.
