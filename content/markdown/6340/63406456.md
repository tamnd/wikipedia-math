# Laver's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laver's theorem"}

**Laver's theorem**, in [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924), states that [order embeddability](wikipedia://en/762203) of countable [total orders](wikipedia://en/30330) is a [well-quasi-ordering](wikipedia://en/739775). That is, for every infinite [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of totally-ordered [countable sets](wikipedia://en/6026), there exists an order embedding from an earlier member of the sequence to a later member. This result was previously known as **Fraïssé's conjecture**, after [Roland Fraïssé](wikipedia://en/4234672), who conjectured it in 1948; [Richard Laver](wikipedia://en/22280145) proved the conjecture in 1971. More generally, Laver proved the same result for order embeddings of countable unions of [scattered orders](wikipedia://en/15922429).

:::

In [reverse mathematics](wikipedia://en/326365), the version of the theorem for countable orders is denoted FRA (for Fraïssé) and the version for countable unions of scattered orders is denoted LAV (for Laver). In terms of the "big five" systems of [second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454), FRA is known to fall in strength somewhere between the strongest two systems, $\Pi_1^1$-CA<sub>0</sub> and ATR<sub>0</sub>, and to be weaker than $\Pi_1^1$-CA<sub>0</sub>. However, it remains open whether it is equivalent to ATR<sub>0</sub> or strictly between these two systems in strength.
