# Chinese monoid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chinese monoid"}

In mathematics, the **Chinese monoid** is a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) generated by a totally ordered alphabet with the relations *cba* = *cab* = *bca* for every *a* ≤ *b* ≤ *c*. An [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) similar to [Schensted's](wikipedia://en/22306020) algorithm yields characterisation of the equivalence classes and a [cross-section theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-section_theorem). It was discovered by (Duchamp Krob 1994) during their classification of monoids with growth similar to that of the [plactic monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plactic_monoid), and studied in detail by Julien Cassaigne, Marc Espie, Daniel Krob, Jean-Christophe Novelli, and Florent Hivert in 2001.[^o3]

:::

The Chinese monoid has a [regular language](wikipedia://en/25723) cross-section

$$a^* \ (ba)^*b^* \ (ca)^*(cb)^* c^* \cdots$$

and hence polynomial growth of dimension $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.[^o4]

The Chinese monoid equivalence class of a [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) is the preimage of an involution under the map $w \mapsto w \circ w^{-1}$ where $\circ$ denotes the product in the [Iwahori-Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/521808) with $q_s = 0$.[^o5]
