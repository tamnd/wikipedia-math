# Motzkin–Taussky theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Motzkin–Taussky theorem"}

The **Motzkin–Taussky theorem** is a result from [operator](wikipedia://en/712675) and [matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_theory) about the representation of a sum of two bounded, [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operators) (resp. matrices). The theorem was proven by [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055) and [Olga Taussky-Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Taussky-Todd).[^o2]

:::

The theorem is used in [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/102140), where e.g. operators of the form

$$T+xT_1$$

are examined.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $X$ be a finite-dimensional complex [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). Furthermore, let $A,B\in B(X)$ be such that all [linear combinations](wikipedia://en/55632)

$$T=\alpha A+\beta B$$

are [diagonalizable](wikipedia://en/246325) for all $\alpha,\beta\in \mathbb{C}$. Then all [eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/2161429) of $T$ are of the form

$$\lambda_{T}=\alpha\lambda_{A} + \beta \lambda_{B}$$

(i.e. they are linear in $\alpha$ und $\beta$) and $\lambda_{A},\lambda_{B}$ are independent of the choice of $\alpha,\beta$.[^o5]

Here $\lambda_{A}$ stands for an eigenvalue of $A$.

### Comments {#Comments}

- Motzkin and Taussky call the above property of the linearity of the eigenvalues in $\alpha,\beta$ *property L*.[^o7]

:::
