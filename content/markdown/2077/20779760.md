# Kakutani's theorem (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kakutani's theorem"}

**Kakutani's theorem** is a result in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) named after [Shizuo Kakutani](wikipedia://en/2572542). It states that every [convex body](wikipedia://en/8652020) in 3-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) space has a circumscribed [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), i.e. a cube all of whose faces touch the body. The result was further generalized by [Yamabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidehiko_Yamabe) and Yujobô to higher dimensions, and by Floyd to other circumscribed [parallelepipeds](wikipedia://en/23975).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Kakutani's theorem on 2-spheres"}

## Kakutani's theorem on 2-spheres {#Kakutani's_theorem_on_2-spheres}

Given a continuous function $f:S^2\to\mathbb{R}$, there exist orthonormal basis $u,v,w$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ such that $f(u)=f(v)=f(w)$.

The proof relies crucially on the fact that the fundamental group of $SO(3)$ is finite: $\mathbb{Z}_2$, while the fundamental group of $S^1$ is infinite cyclic: $\mathbb{Z}$.

This result easily implies the theorem on inscribing convex bodies in cubes.
