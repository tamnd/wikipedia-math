# Solomon Mikhlin

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Solomon Mikhlin"}

**Solomon Grigor'evich Mikhlin** (Соломо́н Григо́рьевич Ми́хлин, real name Zalman Girshevich Mikhlin) (the [family name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_name) is also [transliterated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) as **Mihlin** or **Michlin**) (23 April 1908 – 29 August 1990[^o2]) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of who worked in the fields of [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429), [singular integrals](wikipedia://en/12791220) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506): he is best known for the introduction of the symbol of a [singular integral](wikipedia://en/12791220) operator, which eventually led to the foundation and development of the theory of [pseudodifferential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudodifferential_operator).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in Kholmech, [Rechytsa District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rechytsa_District), [Minsk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk_Governorate) (in present-day [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus)) on 23 April 1908; (Mikhlin 1968) himself states in his [resume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Résumé) that his father was a merchant, but this assertion could be untrue since, in that period, people sometimes lied on the profession of parents in order to overcome political limitations in the access to higher education. According to a different version, his father was a [melamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamed), at a primary religious school ([kheder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheder)), and that the family was of modest means: according to the same source, Zalman was the youngest of five children. His first wife was Victoria Isaevna Libina: Mikhlin's book (Mikhlin 1965) is dedicated to her memory. She died of [peritonitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peritonitis) in 1961 during a boat trip on [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga). In 1940 they adopted a son, Grigory Zalmanovich Mikhlin, who later emigrated to [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel). His second wife was Eugenia Yakovlevna Rubinova, born in 1918, who was his companion for the rest of his life.

### Education and academic career {#Education_and_academic_career}

He graduated from a secondary school in [Gomel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomel) in 1923 and entered the [State Herzen Pedagogical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzen_University) in 1925. In 1927 he was transferred to the Department of Mathematics and Mechanics of [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University) as a second year student, passing all the exams of the first year without attending lectures. Among his university professors there were [Nikolai Maximovich Günther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Maximovich_Günther) and [Vladimir Ivanovich Smirnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Ivanovich_Smirnov_%28mathematician%29). The latter became his master thesis supervisor: the topic of the thesis was the convergence of double [series](wikipedia://en/15287),[^o9] and was defended in 1929. [Sergei Lvovich Sobolev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Lvovich_Sobolev) studied in the same class as Mikhlin. In 1930 he started his teaching career, working in some [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad) institutes for short periods, as Mikhlin himself records on the document (Mikhlin 1968). In 1932 he got a position at the Seismological Institute of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences), where he worked till 1941: in 1935 he got the degree of [Doctor of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences) in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939), without having to earn the [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) degree, and finally in 1937 he was promoted to the rank of professor. During World War II he became professor at the [Kazakh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abai_University) in [Alma Ata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_Ata). Since 1944 S.G. Mikhlin has been professor at the [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University). From 1964 to 1986 he headed the Laboratory of Numerical Methods at the Research Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics of the same university: since 1986 until his death he was a senior researcher at that laboratory.

### Honours {#Honours}

He received the [order of the Badge of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Badge_of_Honour) (Орден Знак Почёта) in 1961:[^o12] the name of the recipients of this prize was usually published in newspapers. He was awarded of the [Laurea honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) by the Karl-Marx-Stadt (now [Chemnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemnitz)) [Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_technology) in 1968 and was elected member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina) in 1970 and of the [Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_dei_Lincei) in 1981. As (Fichera 1994, 51) states, in his country he did not receive honours comparable to his scientific stature, mainly because of the racial policy of the [communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_regime), briefly described in the following section.

#### Influence of communist antisemitism {#Influence_of_communist_antisemitism}

He lived in one of the most difficult periods of contemporary Russian history. The state of mathematical sciences during this period is well described by (Lorentz 2002): [marxist ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist_ideology) rise in the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) universities and Academia was one of the main themes of that period. Local administrators and [communist party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) functionaries interfered with scientists on either [ethnical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) or [ideological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology) grounds. As a matter of fact, during the war and during the creation of a new academic system, Mikhlin did not experience the same difficulties as younger [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) scientists of Jewish origin: for example he was included in the Soviet delegation in 1958, at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Edinburgh.[^o16] However, (Fichera 1994, 56–60), examining the life of Mikhlin, finds it surprisingly similar to the life of [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra) under the [fascist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_regime). He notes that [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) in [communist countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) took different forms compared to his [nazist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) counterpart: the [communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_regime) aimed not to the brutal [homicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homicide) of Jews, but imposed on them a number of constrictions, sometimes very cruel, in order to make their life difficult. During the period from 1963 to 1981, he met Mikhlin attending several conferences in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and realised how he was in a state of isolation, almost marginalized inside his native community: [Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) describes several episodes revealing this fact.[^o18] Perhaps, the most illuminating one is the election of Mikhlin as a member of the [Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_dei_Lincei): in June 1981, Solomon G. Mikhlin was elected Foreign Member of the class of [mathematical](wikipedia://en/25750436) and [physical sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_sciences) of the Lincei. At first time, he was proposed as a winner of the [Antonio Feltrinelli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Feltrinelli_Prize), but the almost sure confiscation of the prize by the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) authorities induced the Lincei members to elect him as a member: they decided *to honour him in a way that no political authority could alienate*.[^o19] However, Mikhlin was not allowed to visit Italy by the Soviet authorities,[^o20] so Fichera and his wife brought the tiny golden [lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynx), the symbol of the Lincei membership, directly to Mikhlin's apartment in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad) on 17 October 1981: the only guests to that "ceremony" were [Vladimir Maz'ya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Maz'ya) and his wife [Tatyana Shaposhnikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatyana_Shaposhnikova).

### Death {#Death}

According to (Fichera 1994, 60–61), which refers a conversation with [Mark Vishik](wikipedia://en/33293921) and [Olga Oleinik](wikipedia://en/3033629), on 29 August 1990 Mikhlin left home to buy medicines for his wife Eugenia. On a public transport, he suffered a lethal stroke. He had no documents with him, therefore he was identified only some time after his death: this may be the cause of the difference in the death date reported on several biographies and obituary notices.[^o24] Fichera also writes that Mikhlin's wife Eugenia survived him only a few months.

## Work {#Work}

### Research activity {#Research_activity}

He was author of [monographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) and [textbooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) which become classics for their style. His research is devoted mainly to the following fields.[^o27]

#### Elasticity theory and boundary value problems {#Elasticity_theory_and_boundary_value_problems}

In [mathematical elasticity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_theory), Mikhlin was concerned by three themes: the [plane problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_elasticity) (mainly from 1932 to 1935), the [theory of shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_shells) (from 1954) and the [Cosserat spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosserat_spectrum) (from 1967 to 1973).[^o29] Dealing with the plane elasticity problem, he proposed two methods for its solution in [multiply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiply_connected) [domains](wikipedia://en/16796643). The first one is based upon the so-called [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001) and the reduction of the related [boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/436779) to [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234). The second method is a certain generalization of the classical [Schwarz algorithm](wikipedia://en/400296) for the solution of the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109) in a given domain by splitting it in simpler problems in smaller domains whose [union](wikipedia://en/47949) is the original one. Mikhlin studied its convergence and gave applications to special applied problems. He proved existence theorems for the fundamental problems of plane elasticity involving [inhomogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneity_%28physics%29) [anisotropic](wikipedia://en/14865) [media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_media): these results are collected in the book (Mikhlin 1957). Concerning the [theory of shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_shells), there are several Mikhlin's articles dealing with it. He studied the error of the approximate solution for shells, similar to plane plates, and found out that this error is small for the so-called [purely rotational state of stress](wikipedia://en/228107). As a result of his study of this problem, Mikhlin also gave a new ([invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638)) form of the basic equations of the theory. He also proved a theorem on [perturbations](wikipedia://en/102140) of [positive operators](wikipedia://en/454936) in a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) which let him to obtain an error estimate for the problem of approximating a sloping shell by a [plane plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_elasticity).[^o31] Mikhlin studied also the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/506713) of the [operator pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_pencil) of the classical [linear elastostatic operator](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics) or [Navier–Cauchy operator](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics)

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)\boldsymbol{u}=\Delta_2\boldsymbol{u}+\omega\nabla\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}\right)$$

where ***$u$*** is the [displacement vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_vector), $\scriptstyle\Delta_2$ is the [vector laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_laplacian), $\scriptstyle\nabla$ is the [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461), $\scriptstyle\nabla\cdot$ is the [divergence](wikipedia://en/8328) and $\omega$ is a [Cosserat eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosserat_spectrum). The full description of the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/506713) and the proof of the [completeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) of the system of [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) are also due to Mikhlin, and partly to [V.G. Maz'ya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Gilelevich_Maz'ya) in their only joint work.[^o32]

#### Singular integrals and Fourier multipliers {#Singular_integrals_and_Fourier_multipliers}

He is one of the founders of the [multi-dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) theory of [singular integrals](wikipedia://en/12791220), jointly with [Francesco Tricomi](wikipedia://en/19830487) and [Georges Giraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Giraud), and also one of the main contributors. By [singular integral](wikipedia://en/12791220) we mean an [integral operator](wikipedia://en/2649421) of the following form

$$Au = v(\boldsymbol{x}) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})}{r^n}u(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y}$$

where $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is a point in the [*n*-dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), $r$=|**$y-x$**| and $\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\theta}=\frac{\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}}{r}$ are the [hyperspherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersphere#Hyperspherical_coordinates) (or the [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates) or the [spherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinates) respectively when $n=2$ or $n=3$) of the [point](wikipedia://en/593693) **$y$** with respect to the point **$x$**. Such [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) are called *singular* since the [singularity](wikipedia://en/54240) of the [kernel of the operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28integral_operator%29) is so strong that the integral does not exist in the ordinary sense, but only in the sense of [Cauchy principal value](wikipedia://en/408195).[^o34] Mikhlin was the first to develop a theory of [singular integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_equation) as a theory of [operator equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_equation) in [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630). In the papers (Mikhlin 1936a) and (Mikhlin 1936b) he found a rule for the composition of double singular integrals (i.e. in [2-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) [euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697)) and introduced the very important notion of [symbol of a singular integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_of_a_singular_integral). This enabled him to show that the [algebra of bounded singular integral operators](wikipedia://en/455987) is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to the [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) of either [scalar](wikipedia://en/161879) or [matrix-valued functions](wikipedia://en/256738). He proved the [Fredholm's theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredholm's_theorems) for [singular integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_equation) and systems of such equations under the hypothesis of non-degeneracy of the [symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_of_a_singular_integral): he also proved that the [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation#Index) of a single singular integral equation in the [euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero). In 1961 Mikhlin developed a theory of [multidimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) [singular integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_equation) on [Lipschitz spaces](wikipedia://en/51731). These spaces are widely used in the theory of one-dimensional singular integral equations: however, the direct extension of the related theory to the multidimensional case meets some technical difficulties, and Mikhlin suggested another approach to this problem. Precisely, he obtained the basic properties of this kind of singular integral equations as a by-product of the [L*<sup>p</sup>*-space](wikipedia://en/45194) theory of these equations. Mikhlin also proved[^o37] a now classical theorem on [multipliers of Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/995746) in the [L*<sup>p</sup>*-space](wikipedia://en/45194), based on an analogous theorem of [Józef Marcinkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Marcinkiewicz) on [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038). A complete collection of his results in this field up to the 1965, as well as the contributions of other mathematicians like [Tricomi](wikipedia://en/19830487), [Giraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Giraud), [Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderón) and [Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477),[^o38] is contained in the monograph (Mikhlin 1965).[^o40]

A synthesis of the theories of singular integrals and [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) [partial differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_operator) was accomplished, in the mid 1960s, by the theory of [pseudodifferential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudodifferential_operator): [Joseph J. Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_J._Kohn), [Louis Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725), [Lars Hörmander](wikipedia://en/500958) and others operated this synthesis, but this theory owe his rise to the discoveries of Mikhlin, as is universally acknowledged.[^o4] This theory has numerous applications to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). [Mikhlin's multiplier theorem](wikipedia://en/995746#Mikhlin_multiplier_theorem) is widely used in different branches of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), particularly to the theory of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). The analysis of [Fourier multipliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_multiplier) was later forwarded by [Lars Hörmander](wikipedia://en/500958), [Walter Littman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Littman), [Elias Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elias_Stein), [Charles Fefferman](wikipedia://en/502831) and others.

#### Partial differential equations {#Partial_differential_equations}

In four papers, published in the period 1940–1942, Mikhlin applies the [potentials method](wikipedia://en/1431342) to the [mixed problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_problem) for the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691). In particular, he solves the mixed problem for the [two-space dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) in the half [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) by reducing it to the planar [Abel integral equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_integral_equation). For [plane domains](wikipedia://en/16796643) with a sufficiently [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [curvilinear](wikipedia://en/971691) [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) he reduces the problem to an [integro-differential equation](wikipedia://en/11095324), which he is also able to solve when the boundary of the given domain is [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478). In 1951 Mikhlin proved the convergence of the [Schwarz alternating method](wikipedia://en/16252015) for second order [elliptic equations](wikipedia://en/662787).[^o42] He also applied the methods of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), at the same time as [Mark Vishik](wikipedia://en/33293921) but independently of him, to the investigation of [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779) for degenerate second order elliptic partial differential equations.

#### Numerical mathematics {#Numerical_mathematics}

His work in this field can be divided into several branches:[^o44] in the following text, four main branches are described, and a sketch of his last researches is also given. The papers within the first branch are summarized in the monograph (Mikhlin 1964), which contain the study of convergence of [variational methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_method) for problems connected with [positive operators](wikipedia://en/454936), in particular, for some problems of [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). Both "a priori" and "a posteriori" [estimates](wikipedia://en/1565926) of the errors concerning the [approximation](wikipedia://en/1092713) given by these methods are proved. The second branch deals with the notion of [stability of a numerical process](wikipedia://en/233807) introduced by Mikhlin himself. When applied to the variational method, this notion enables him to state necessary and sufficient conditions in order to minimize errors in the solution of the given problem when the error arising in the numerical construction of the [algebraic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_equations) resulting from the application of the method itself is sufficiently small, no matter how large is the system's order. The third branch is the study of [variational-difference](wikipedia://en/6054681) and [finite element methods](wikipedia://en/18233581). Mikhlin studied the completeness of the [coordinate functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_function) used in this methods in the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $W^{1,p}$, deriving the [order of approximation](wikipedia://en/346769) as a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of the [smoothness properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) of the functions to be [approximation of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_of_functions) [approximated](wikipedia://en/1092713). He also characterized the class of [coordinate functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_function) which give the best [order of approximation](wikipedia://en/346769), and has studied the [stability](wikipedia://en/233807) of the [variational-difference process](wikipedia://en/6054681) and the growth of the [condition number](wikipedia://en/6934) of the variation-difference [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859). Mikhlin also studied the [finite element](wikipedia://en/18233581) approximation in [weighted](wikipedia://en/245523) [Sobolev spaces](wikipedia://en/611964) related to the numerical solution of degenerate [elliptic equations](wikipedia://en/662787). He found the optimal [order of approximation](wikipedia://en/346769) for some methods of solution of [variational inequalities](wikipedia://en/1468817). The fourth branch of his research in [numerical mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_mathematics) is a method for the solution of [Fredholm integral equations](wikipedia://en/363377) which he called *resolvent method*: its essence rely on the possibility of substituting the [kernel of the integral operator](wikipedia://en/2649421) by its variational-difference approximation, so that the [resolvent](wikipedia://en/3378540) of the new kernel can be expressed by simple [recurrence relations](wikipedia://en/146806). This eliminates the need to construct and solve large [systems of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_equations).[^o46] During his last years, Mikhlin contributed to the [theory of errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_errors) in numerical processes,[^o47] proposing the following classification of [errors](wikipedia://en/12076692).

1. **Approximation error**: is the error due to the replacement of an exact problem by an approximating one.
1. **Perturbation error**: is the error due to the inaccuracies in the computation of the data of the approximating problem.
1. **Algorithm error**: is the intrinsic error of the [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) used for the solution of the approximating problem.
1. **[Rounding error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rounding_error)**: is the error due to the limits of [computer arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_arithmetic).

This classification is useful since enables one to develop computational methods adjusted in order to diminish the errors of each particular type, following the *[divide et impera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divide_et_impera)* (divide and rule) principle.

### Teaching activity {#Teaching_activity}

He was the doctoral advisor of [Tatyana O. Shaposhnikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatyana_Shaposhnikova). He was also [mentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentor) and friend of [Vladimir Maz'ya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Maz'ya): he was never his official [supervisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervisor), but his friendship with the young undergraduate Maz'ya had a great influence on shaping his mathematical style.
