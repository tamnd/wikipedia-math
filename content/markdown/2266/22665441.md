# Completely regular semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Completely regular semigroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **completely regular semigroup** is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) in which every element is in some [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of the semigroup. The [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of completely regular semigroups forms an important subclass of the [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of [regular semigroups](wikipedia://en/6797677), the class of [inverse semigroups](wikipedia://en/1757814) being another such subclass. [Alfred H. Clifford](wikipedia://en/22683347) was the first to publish a major paper on completely regular semigroups though he used the terminology "semigroups admitting relative inverses" to refer to such semigroups.[^o2] The name "completely regular semigroup" stems from Lyapin's book on semigroups.[^o3][^o4] In the Russian literature, completely regular semigroups are often called "Clifford semigroups".[^o5] In the English literature, the name "[Clifford semigroup](wikipedia://en/22665481)" is used synonymously to "inverse Clifford semigroup", and refers to a completely regular [inverse semigroup](wikipedia://en/1757814).[^o6] In a completely regular semigroup, each [Green](wikipedia://en/1192012) *H*-class is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) and the semigroup is the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of these groups.[^o7] Hence completely regular semigroups are also referred to as "unions of groups". [Epigroups](wikipedia://en/37300268) generalize this notion and their class includes all completely regular semigroups.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

"While there is an abundance of natural examples of inverse semigroups, for completely regular semigroups the examples (beyond completely simple semigroups) are mostly artificially constructed: the minimum ideal of a finite semigroup is completely simple, and the various relatively free completely regular semigroups are the other more or less natural examples."[^o10]
