# Gustave Malécot

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gustave Malécot"}

**Gustave Malécot** (28 December 1911 – November 1998) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose work on [heredity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity) had a strong influence on [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Malécot grew up in [L'Horme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Horme), a small village near [St. Étienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Étienne) in the [Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loire) [département](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Département), the son of a mining engineer.

In 1935, Malécot obtained a degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). He then went on to do a PhD under [George Darmois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Darmois) and completed that in 1939. His work focused on [R.A. Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824)'s 1918 article *[The Correlation Between Relatives on the Supposition of Mendelian Inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Correlation_Between_Relatives_on_the_Supposition_of_Mendelian_Inheritance)*.

Between 1940 and 1942, with France under [Nazi German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_German) occupation, Malécot taught mathematics at the Lyceé de Saint-Étienne. In 1942 he was appointed maître de conférence (lecturer) [Université de Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montpellier). In 1945 he joined the [Université de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Lyon), becoming professor of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in 1946, a position he held until his retirement in 1981.

[Malécot's Coancestry Coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malecot's_method_of_coancestry), a measure of genetic similarity, still bears his name.
