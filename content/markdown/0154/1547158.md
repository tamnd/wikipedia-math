# Hypotrochoid

*Image: HypotrochoidOutThreeFifths.gif.* The red curve is a hypotrochoid drawn as the smaller black circle rolls around inside the larger blue circle (parameters are $R = 5, r = 3, d = 5$).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypotrochoid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hypotrochoid** is a [roulette](wikipedia://en/846500) traced by a point attached to a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) of [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) $r$ rolling around the inside of a fixed circle of radius $R$, where the point is a [distance](wikipedia://en/39378) $d$ from the center of the interior circle.

:::

The [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108) for a hypotrochoid are:[^o2]

$$\begin{aligned} & x (\theta) = (R - r)\cos\theta + d\cos\left({R - r \over r}\theta\right) \\ & y (\theta) = (R - r)\sin\theta - d\sin\left({R - r \over r}\theta\right) \end{aligned}$$

where $\theta$ is the angle formed by the horizontal and the center of the rolling circle (these are not polar equations because $\theta$ is not the polar angle). When measured in radians, $\theta$ takes values from 0 to $2 \pi \times \tfrac{\operatorname{LCM}(r, R)}{R}$ (where $\operatorname{LCM}$ is [least common multiple](wikipedia://en/17961)).

Special cases include the [hypocycloid](wikipedia://en/592735) with $d = r$ and the [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) with $R = 2r$ and $d \ne r$.[^o3] The eccentricity of the ellipse is

$$e=\frac{2\sqrt{d/r}}{1+(d/r)}$$

becoming 1 when $d=r$ (see [Tusi couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusi_couple)).

*Image: Ellipse as hypotrochoid.gif.* The [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) (drawn in red) may be expressed as a special case of the hypotrochoid, with $R = 2r$ ([Tusi couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusi_couple)); here $R = 10, r = 5, d = 1$.

The classic [Spirograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirograph) toy traces out hypotrochoid and [epitrochoid](wikipedia://en/1547292) curves.

Hypotrochoids describe the support of the eigenvalues of some random matrices with cyclic correlations.[^o4]
