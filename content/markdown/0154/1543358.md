# Darboux's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Darboux's theorem"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a field in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Darboux's theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) providing a normal form for special classes of [differential 1-forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms), partially generalizing the [Frobenius integration theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_integration_theorem). It is named after [Jean Gaston Darboux](wikipedia://en/1142530)[^o2] who established it as the solution of the [Pfaff](wikipedia://en/1669324) problem.[^o3]

:::

It is a foundational result in several fields, the chief among them being [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298). Indeed, one of its many consequences is that any two [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356) of the same dimension are locally [symplectomorphic](wikipedia://en/312304) to one another. That is, every $2n$-dimensional symplectic manifold can be made to look locally like the [linear symplectic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_symplectic_space) $\mathbb{C}^n$ with its canonical symplectic form.

There is also an analogous consequence of the theorem applied to [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398).

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that $\theta$ is a differential 1-form on an *$n$*-dimensional manifold, such that $\mathrm{d} \theta$ has constant [rank](wikipedia://en/221537#Rank_of_a_k-vector) *$p$*. Then

- if $\theta \wedge \left(\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^p = 0$ everywhere, then there is a local system of coordinates $(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-p},y_1,\ldots, y_p)$ in which $$\theta=x_1\,\mathrm{d}y_1+\ldots + x_p\,\mathrm{d}y_p;$$
- if $\theta \wedge \left( \mathrm{d} \theta \right)^p \ne 0$ everywhere, then there is a local system of coordinates $(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-p},y_1,\ldots, y_p)$ in which $$\theta=x_1\,\mathrm{d}y_1+\ldots + x_p\,\mathrm{d}y_p + \mathrm{d}x_{p+1}.$$

Darboux's original proof used [induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on *$p$* and it can be equivalently presented in terms of [distributions](wikipedia://en/2976052)[^o6] or of [differential ideals](wikipedia://en/8056148).[^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Frobenius' theorem"}

### Frobenius' theorem {#Frobenius'_theorem}

Darboux's theorem for *$p=0$* ensures that any 1-form *$\theta \neq 0$* such that *$\theta \wedge d\theta = 0$* can be written as *$\theta = dx_1$* in some coordinate system $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$.

This recovers one of the formulation of [Frobenius theorem](wikipedia://en/602480) in terms of differential forms: if $\mathcal{I} \subset \Omega^*(M)$ is the differential ideal generated by $\theta$, then *$\theta \wedge d\theta = 0$* implies the existence of a coordinate system $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ where $\mathcal{I} \subset \Omega^*(M)$ is actually generated by $d x_1$.[^o7]

:::

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Darboux's theorem for symplectic manifolds"}

## Darboux's theorem for symplectic manifolds {#Darboux's_theorem_for_symplectic_manifolds}

Suppose that $\omega$ is a [symplectic 2-form](wikipedia://en/28356) on an $n=2m$-dimensional manifold *$M$*. In a neighborhood of each point *$p$* of *$M$*, by the [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030), there is a 1-form $\theta$ with $\mathrm{d} \theta = \omega$. Moreover, $\theta$ satisfies the first set of hypotheses in Darboux's theorem, and so locally there is a [coordinate chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_chart) *$U$* near *$p$* in which

$$\theta=x_1\,\mathrm{d}y_1+\ldots + x_m\,\mathrm{d}y_m.$$

Taking an [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) now shows

$$\omega = \mathrm{d} \theta = \mathrm{d}x_1 \wedge \mathrm{d}y_1 + \ldots + \mathrm{d}x_m \wedge \mathrm{d}y_m.$$

The chart *$U$* is said to be a **Darboux chart** around *$p$*.[^o12] The manifold *$M$* can be [covered](wikipedia://en/317552) by such charts.

To state this differently, identify $\mathbb{R}^{2m}$ with $\mathbb{C}^{m}$ by letting $z_j=x_j+\textit{i}\,y_j$. If $\varphi: U \to \mathbb{C}^n$ is a Darboux chart, then $\omega$ can be written as the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) of the standard symplectic form $\omega_0$ on $\mathbb{C}^{n}$:

$$\omega = \varphi^{*}\omega_0.\,$$

A modern proof of this result, without employing Darboux's general statement on 1-forms, is done using [Moser's trick](wikipedia://en/73989969).[^o12][^o13]

### Comparison with Riemannian geometry {#Comparison_with_Riemannian_geometry}

Darboux's theorem for symplectic manifolds implies that there are no local invariants in symplectic geometry: a [Darboux basis](wikipedia://en/11067444) can always be taken, valid near any given point. This is in marked contrast to the situation in [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) where the [curvature](wikipedia://en/651361) is a local invariant, an obstruction to the [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) being locally a sum of squares of coordinate differentials.

The difference is that Darboux's theorem states that $\omega$ can be made to take the standard form in an *entire neighborhood* around *$p$*. In Riemannian geometry, the metric can always be made to take the standard form *at* any given point, but not always in a neighborhood around that point.

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Darboux's theorem for contact manifolds"}

### Darboux's theorem for contact manifolds {#Darboux's_theorem_for_contact_manifolds}

Another particular case is recovered when $n=2p+1$; if $\theta \wedge \left( \mathrm{d} \theta \right)^p \ne 0$ everywhere, then $\theta$ is a [contact form](wikipedia://en/476398#Contact_forms_and_structures). A simpler proof can be given, as in the case of symplectic structures, by using [Moser's trick](wikipedia://en/73989969).[^o17]

:::

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Darboux-Weinstein theorem"}

## The Darboux-Weinstein theorem {#The_Darboux-Weinstein_theorem}

[Alan Weinstein](wikipedia://en/7344764) showed that the Darboux's theorem for symplectic manifolds can be strengthened to hold on a [neighborhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095):[^o20]

> *Let $M$ be a smooth manifold endowed with two symplectic forms $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, and let $N \subset M$ be a closed submanifold. If $\left.\omega_1\right|_N = \left.\omega_2\right|_N$, then there is a neighborhood $U$ of $N$ in $M$ and a diffeomorphism $f : U \to U$ such that $f^*\omega_2 = \omega_1$.*

The standard Darboux theorem is recovered when $N$ is a point and $\omega_2$ is the standard symplectic structure on a coordinate chart.

This theorem also holds for infinite-dimensional [Banach manifolds](wikipedia://en/2504397).

:::
