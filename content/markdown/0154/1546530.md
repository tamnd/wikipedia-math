# Breather

*Image: Breather-surface.png.* This breather pseudospherical surface corresponds to a solution of a non-linear wave-equation.

*Image: Breather-4-11.png.* Pseudospherical breather surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Breather"}

In physics, a **breather** is a [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear) [wave](wikipedia://en/33516) in which energy concentrates in a localized and oscillatory fashion. This contradicts with the expectations derived from the corresponding linear system for [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) [amplitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude), which tends towards an even distribution of initially localized energy. A **discrete breather** is a breather solution on a nonlinear [lattice](wikipedia://en/291472).

:::

The term breather originates from the characteristic that most breathers are localized in space and oscillate in time, similar to [breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathing) in living beings.[^o2] This also apply in the opposite situation: oscillations in space and localized in time, which are also denoted as breathers.

## Overview {#Overview}

*Image: Sine gordon 5.gif.* [Sine-Gordon](wikipedia://en/306645) standing breather is a swinging in time coupled kink-antikink 2-soliton solution.

*Image: Sine gordon 6.gif.* Large amplitude moving [sine-Gordon](wikipedia://en/306645) breather.

A breather is a localized [periodic](wikipedia://en/76848) solution of either [continuous media](wikipedia://en/5918) equations or discrete [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) equations. The exactly solvable [sine-Gordon equation](wikipedia://en/306645)[^o2] and the focusing [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748)[^o4] are examples of one-[dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensional) [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) that possess breather solutions.[^o5] Discrete nonlinear [Hamiltonian lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_lattices) in many cases support breather solutions.

Breathers are [solitonic](wikipedia://en/51654) structures. There are two types of breathers: [standing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave) or [traveling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_wave) ones.[^o6] Standing breathers correspond to localized solutions whose amplitude vary in time (they are sometimes called [oscillons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillon)). A necessary condition for the existence of breathers in discrete lattices is that the breather main [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) and all its multipliers are located outside of the [phonon](wikipedia://en/85754) [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum) of the lattice.

## Example of breather solutions {#Example_of_breather_solutions}

### Sine-Gordon equation {#Sine-Gordon_equation}

The [sine-Gordon equation](wikipedia://en/306645) is the nonlinear [dispersive partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/8477417)

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} - \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \sin u = 0,$$

with the [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) *u* a function of the spatial coordinate *x* and time *t*.

An exact solution found by using the [inverse scattering transform](wikipedia://en/4916481) is:[^o2]

$$u = 4 \arctan\left(\frac{\sqrt{1-\omega^2}\;\cos(\omega t)}{\omega\;\cosh(\sqrt{1-\omega^2}\; x)}\right),$$

which, for *ω \< 1*, is periodic in time *t* and [decays exponentially](wikipedia://en/330320) when moving away from *x = 0*.

### Nonlinear Schrödinger equation {#Nonlinear_Schrödinger_equation}

The focusing [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748)[^o10] is the dispersive partial differential equation:

$$i\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + |u|^2 u = 0,$$

with *u* a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) field as a function of *x* and *t*. Further *i* denotes the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358).

One of the breather solutions (Kuznetsov-Ma breather) is [^o4]

$$u = \left( \frac{2 b^2 \cosh(\theta) + 2 i b \sqrt{2-b^2} \sinh(\theta)} {2 \cosh(\theta)-\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2-b^2} \cos(a b x)} - 1 \right)a\,e^{i a^2 t}$$

with

$$\theta=a^2\,b\,\sqrt{2-b^2}\;t,$$

which gives breathers periodic in space *x* and approaching the uniform value *a* when moving away from the focus time *t* = 0. These breathers exist for values of the [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation) parameter *b* less than $\sqrt{2}$. Note that a limiting case of the breather solution is the [Peregrine soliton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_soliton).[^o11]
