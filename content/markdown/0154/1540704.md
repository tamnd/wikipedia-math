# Equation of state (cosmology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equation of state"}

In [cosmology](wikipedia://en/5378), the **equation of state** of a [perfect fluid](wikipedia://en/2137509) is characterized by a [dimensionless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless) number $w$, equal to the ratio of its [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) $p$ to its [energy density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density) $\varepsilon$:

$$w \equiv \frac{p}{\varepsilon}.$$

It is closely related to the thermodynamic [equation of state](wikipedia://en/9908) and [ideal gas law](wikipedia://en/59881).

:::

## The equation {#The_equation}

The [perfect gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_gas) equation of state may be written as

$$p = \rho RT = \rho c_s^2$$

where $\rho$ is the mass density, $R$ is the particular gas constant, $T$ is the temperature and $c_s=\sqrt{RT}$ is a characteristic thermal speed of the molecules. Thus

$$w \equiv \frac{p}{\rho c^2} = \frac{\rho c_s^2}{\rho c^2} = \frac{c_s^2}{c^2}\approx 0$$

where $c$ is the speed of light, $\varepsilon = \rho c^2$ and $c_s\ll c$ for a "cold" gas.

### FLRW equations and the equation of state {#FLRW_equations_and_the_equation_of_state}

The equation of state may be used in [Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker) (FLRW) equations to describe the evolution of an isotropic universe filled with a perfect fluid. If $a$ is the [scale factor](wikipedia://en/921168) then

$$\varepsilon \propto a^{-3(1+w)}.$$

If the fluid is the dominant form of matter in a [flat universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe#Flat_universe), then

$$a \propto t^{\frac{2}{3(1+w)}},$$

where $t$ is the proper time.

In general the [Friedmann acceleration equation](wikipedia://en/1147994) is

$$3 \frac{\ddot{a}}{a} = \Lambda - 4 \pi G \left(\varepsilon + 3p\right)$$

where $\Lambda$ is the [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992) and $G$ is [Newton's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_constant), and $\ddot{a}$ is the second [proper time](wikipedia://en/494418) derivative of the scale factor.

If we define (what might be called "effective") energy density and pressure as

$$\begin{aligned} \varepsilon' &\equiv \varepsilon + \frac{\Lambda}{8 \pi G} \\[1ex] p' &\equiv p - \frac{\Lambda}{8 \pi G} \end{aligned}$$

and

$$p' = w'\varepsilon'$$

the acceleration equation may be written as

$$\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4}{3}\pi G\left(\varepsilon' + 3p'\right) = -\frac{4}{3} \pi G \left(1+3w'\right)\varepsilon'$$

### Non-relativistic particles {#Non-relativistic_particles}

The equation of state for ordinary non-[relativistic](wikipedia://en/30001) 'matter' (e.g. cold dust) is $w = 0$, which means that its energy density decreases as $\varepsilon \propto a^{-3} = V^{-1}$, where $V$ is a volume. In an expanding universe, the total energy of non-relativistic matter remains constant, with its density decreasing as the volume increases.

### Radiation-dominated ultra-relativistic case {#Radiation-dominated_ultra-relativistic_case}

The equation of state for ultra-relativistic 'radiation' (including [neutrinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino), and in the very early universe other particles that later became non-relativistic) is $w = 1/3$ which means that its energy density decreases as $\varepsilon \propto a^{-4}$. In an expanding universe, the energy density of radiation decreases more quickly than the volume expansion, because its wavelength is [red-shifted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-shift).

### Stiff matter {#Stiff_matter}

Stiff matter is governed by the [Zeldovich equation of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeldovich_equation_of_state) with $w = 1$ which means that its energy density decreases as $\varepsilon \propto a^{-6}$.

### Acceleration of cosmic inflation {#Acceleration_of_cosmic_inflation}

[Cosmic inflation](wikipedia://en/5382) and the [accelerated expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_universe) of the universe can be characterized by the equation of state of [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy). In the simplest case, the equation of state of the [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992) is $w = -1$. In this case, the above expression for the scale factor is not valid and $a\propto e^{Ht}$, where the constant $H$ is the [Hubble parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_parameter). More generally, the expansion of the universe is accelerating for any equation of state $w < -1/3$. The accelerated expansion of the Universe was indeed observed.[^o8] The observed value of equation of state of cosmological constant is near -1 from three different major studies.[^o9]

Hypothetical [phantom dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_dark_energy) would have an equation of state $w < -1$, and would cause a [Big Rip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Rip). Using the existing data, it is still impossible to distinguish between phantom $w < -1$ and non-phantom $w \ge -1$.

### Fluids {#Fluids}

In an expanding universe, fluids with larger equations of state disappear more quickly than those with smaller equations of state. This is the origin of the [flatness](wikipedia://en/1118171) and [monopole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopole_problem) problems of the [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116): [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) has $w = -1/3$ and monopoles have $w = 0$, so if they were around at the time of the early Big Bang, they should still be visible today. These problems are solved by cosmic inflation which has $w \approx -1$. Measuring the equation of state of dark energy is one of the largest efforts of [observational cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_cosmology). By accurately measuring $w$, it is hoped that the cosmological constant could be distinguished from [quintessence](wikipedia://en/39137) which has $w \ne -1$.

### Scalar modeling {#Scalar_modeling}

A [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879) $\phi$ can be viewed as a sort of perfect fluid with equation of state

$$w = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2-V(\phi)}{\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2+V(\phi)},$$

where $\dot{\phi}$ is the time-derivative of $\phi$ and $V(\phi)$ is the potential energy. A free ($V = 0$) scalar field has $w = 1$, and one with vanishing kinetic energy is equivalent to a cosmological constant: $w = -1$. Any equation of state in between, but not crossing the $w = -1$ barrier known as the Phantom Divide Line (PDL),[^o12] is achievable, which makes scalar fields useful models for many phenomena in cosmology.
