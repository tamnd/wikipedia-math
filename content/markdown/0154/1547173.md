# Abraham Sharp

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Abraham Sharp"}

**Abraham Sharp** (c. 1653 – 18 July 1742) was an English mathematician and astronomer.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Sharp was born in Horton Hall in [Little Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Horton), [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford), the son of well-to-do merchant John Sharp and Mary (née Clarkson) Sharp and was educated at [Bradford Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Grammar_School).[^o4]

*Image: Abraham Sharp Wooden Telescope YORYM 2012 106.jpg.* Abraham Sharp's wooden telescope

In 1669 he became a merchant's apprentice before becoming a schoolmaster in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) and subsequently a bookkeeper in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London). His wide knowledge of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) attracted [Flamsteed's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Flamsteed) attention and it was through Flamsteed that Sharp was invited, in 1688, to enter the [Greenwich Royal Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Royal_Observatory). There he did notable work, improving instruments and showing great skill as a calculator, publishing *Geometry Improved* and [logarithmic tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_table).

Sharp calculated [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) to 72 decimal places using an [arctan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctan) sequence, briefly holding the record until [John Machin](wikipedia://en/274756) calculated 100 digits in 1706.[^o5]

He returned to Little Horton in 1694. When the [Atlas Coelestis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Coelestis) – the largest star map at the time – was published,[^o6] it contained 26 maps of the major [constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) visible from [Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich), and two [planispheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planisphere) designed by Sharp.[^o7]

Sharp died in Little Horton in 1742. He had never married. He was a great-uncle of [Jesse Ramsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Ramsden), the scientific instrument maker.[^o8]

An English translation of a memorial tablet in Latin in [Bradford Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Cathedral) carved by [Peter Scheemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Scheemakers)[^o9] translates as " *He was rightly counted among the most accomplished mathematicians of his day. He enjoyed constant friendship with the very famous men of the same repute, notably Flamsteed and the illustrious Newton. He drew up the description of the heavens made by the former of these (Flamsteed) in (astronomical) tables of the greatest accuracy; he also published anonymously various writings and descriptions of instruments perfected by himself...* .

The crater [Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) is named after him.
