# Jacob Palis

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Jacob Palis"}

**Jacob Palis Jr.** (15 March 1940 – 7 May 2025) was a Brazilian mathematician and academic. Palis's research interests were mainly [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations). Some themes are [global stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_stability) and [hyperbolicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolicity), [bifurcations](wikipedia://en/2326042), [attractors](wikipedia://en/191094) and [chaotic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaotic_system). He proposed the [Palis' conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palis'_conjectures) (which form the [Palis' program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palis'_program)),[^o7] which influenced the development of the theory of dynamical systems, and also of its applications to other sciences.[^o8] He was a world leader in [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295) research.[^o9] Palis was an influential figure in the development of mathematics in Brazil.[^o10]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Jacob Palis was born in [Uberaba, Minas Gerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uberaba,_Minas_Gerais). His father was a [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Brazilians) immigrant, and his mother was of [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Brazilians) ancestry.[^o12] The couple had eight children (five men and three women), and Jacob was the youngest. His father was a merchant, owner of a large store, and supported and funded the studies of his children. Palis said that he already enjoyed mathematics in his childhood.[^o13][^o14]

At 16, Palis moved to Rio de Janeiro to study engineering at the [University of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Brazil) – now [UFRJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFRJ). He was approved in first place in the entrance exam, but was not old enough to enroll; he then had to take the university's entry exam again a year later, where he once again finished in first place. He graduated in 1962 with honours and received the award for the best student.[^o14]

In 1964, he moved to the United States. In 1966, he obtained his master's degree in mathematics under the guidance of [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), and in 1968 his PhD, with the thesis *On Morse-Smale [Diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564)*, again with Smale as advisor.[^o15][^o16]

In 1968, he returned to Brazil and became a researcher at the [Instituto Nacional de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708) (IMPA) in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro), Brazil.[^o15] Beginning in 1973 he held a permanent position as professor at IMPA, where he was director from 1993 until 2003. He was Secretary-General of the [Third World Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World_Academy_of_Sciences) from 2004 to 2006, and elected its president in 2006[^o17] and remained on position till December 2012. He was also president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) from 1999 to 2002.[^o18] He was president of the [Brazilian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Academy_of_Sciences) from 2007 to 2016.[^o19] Palis advised more than forty [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) students so far from more than ten countries, including [Artur Oscar Lopes](wikipedia://en/46769167), [Ricardo Mañé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Mañé), [Welington de Melo](wikipedia://en/26871914), [Carlos Gustavo Moreira](wikipedia://en/46768742), [Enrique Pujals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Pujals) and [Marcelo Viana](wikipedia://en/44610530).[^o20]

Palis died at a hospital in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro), on 7 May 2025, at the age of 85. He had been hospitalized since March.[^o21]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Palis received numerous medals and decorations. He was a foreign member of several academies of sciences, including the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) and [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina).[^o23] In 2005 Palis received the [Legion of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honor).

He was a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o24] In 2010, he was awarded the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize) for his fundamental contributions in the mathematical theory of dynamical systems that has been the basis for many applications in various scientific disciplines, such as in the study of oscillations.[^o25][^o26] He was also a recipient of the 1988 [TWAS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWAS_Prize).[^o27]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- *On Morse-Smale Dynamical Systems*, Topology 19, 1969 (385–405).
- *Structural Stability Theorems*, with [S. Smale](wikipedia://en/40294), Proceedings of the Institute on Global Analysis, American Math. Society, Vol. XIV, 1970 (223–232).
- *Cycles and Bifurcations Theory*, with S. Newhouse, Asterisque 31, Société Mathématique de France, 1976 (44–140).
- *[The Topology of Holomorphic Flows near a Singularity](http://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1978__48__5_0)*, with C. Camacho and [N. Kuiper](wikipedia://en/3344929), Publications Math.Institut Hautes Études Scientifiques 48, 1978 (5–38).
- *Moduli of Stability and Bifurcation Theory*, Proceedings of the International Congress of Mathematicians, Helsinki, 1978 (835–839).
- *Stability of Parameterized Families of Gradient Vector Fields*, with [F. Takens](wikipedia://en/1790146), Annals of Mathematics 118, 1983 (383–421).
- *Cycles and Measure of Bifurcation Sets for Two-Dimensional Diffeomorphisms*, with F. Takens, Inventiones Mathematicae 82, 1985 (397–422).
- *Homoclinic Orbits, Hyperbolic Dynamic and Fractional Dimensions of Cantor Sets* (Lefschetz Centennial Conference) Contemporary Mathematics - American Mathematical Society, 58, 1987 (203–216).
- *Hyperbolicity and Creation of Homoclinic Orbits*, with F.Takens, Annals of Mathematics 125, 1987 (337–374).
- *On the C1 Omega-Stability Conjecture, Publications Math*. Institut Hautes Études Scientifiques, 66, 1988 (210–215).
- *Bifurcations and Global Stability of Two-Parameter Families of Gradient Vector Fields* with M. J. Carneiro, Publications Math. Institut Hautes Études Scientifiques 70, 1990 (103–168).
- "Homoclinic Tangencies for Hyperbolic Sets of Large Hausdorff Dimension", with [J. C. Yoccoz](wikipedia://en/589307), *Acta Mathematica* **172**, 1994, pp. 91–136.
- *High Dimension Diffeomorphisms Displaying Infinitely Many Sinks*, with [M. Viana](wikipedia://en/44610530), Annals of Mathematics 140, 1994 (207–250).
- *A Global View of Dynamics and a [Conjecture on the Denseness of Finitude of Attractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palis_conjecture)*. *Astérisque*. France:, v. 261, pp. 339–351, 2000.
- *Homoclinic tangencies and fractal invariants in arbitrary dimension*, with [C. Moreira](wikipedia://en/46768742) and M. Viana, C R Ac Sc Paris., 2001.
- *Nonuniformily hyperbolic horseshoes unleashed by homoclinic bifurcations and zero density of attractors*, with J.-C. Yoccoz, C R Ac Sc Paris., 2001.

**Books published**

- *Geometric Theory of Dynamical Systems*, with W. de Melo. Springer-Verlag, 1982; also published in Portuguese, Russian and Chinese.
- *Hyperbolicity and Sensitive-Chaotic Dynamics at Homoclinic Bifurcations, Fractal Dimensions and Infinitely Many Attractors*, with F. Takens. Cambridge Univ. Press, 1993; Second Edition, 1994.
