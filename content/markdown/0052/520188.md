# Kurt Reidemeister

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kurt Reidemeister"}

**Kurt Werner Friedrich Reidemeister** (13 October 1893 – 8 July 1971) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) born in [Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig) (Brunswick), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire).

:::

## Life {#Life}

*Image: KurtReidemeister MFO19544cropped.jpg.* Reidemeister in the late 1950s

He was a brother of [Marie Neurath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Neurath). Beginning in 1912, he studied in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg), [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich), [Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg), and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). In 1920, he got the Staatsexamen (master's degree) in mathematics, philosophy, physics, chemistry, and geology. He received his doctorate in 1921 with a [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg) under the supervision of [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990).[^o5][^o6]

He became interested in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625); he edited [Wilhelm Blaschke](wikipedia://en/728998)'s second volume on the topic,[^o7] and both made an acclaimed contribution to the Jena [DMV](wikipedia://en/30864973) conference in September 1921.[^o8][^o9]

In October 1922[^o5][^o6] or 1923[^o4] he was appointed assistant professor at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna). While there he became familiar with the work of [Wilhelm Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921) on [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), and became closely connected to [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212) and the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle). Its 1929 manifesto lists one of Reidemeister's publications[^o10] in a bibliography of closely related authors.

In 1925 he became a full professor at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg); he stayed until 1933, when he was regarded politically unsound by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) and dismissed from his position. Whilst there he organised the [Second Conference on the Epistemology of the Exact Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Conference_on_the_Epistemology_of_the_Exact_Sciences) in conjunction with journal *[Erkenntnis](wikipedia://en/18761111)*.[^o11]

Blaschke managed to get a promise about Reidemeister's reappointment, and in autumn 1934 he got the chair of [Kurt Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565) at the [University of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg). He stayed there, except for a visit to the Princeton [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1948–1950, until he got appointed to Göttingen University in 1955, where he stayed until his [emeritation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus).[^o4][^o5][^o6]

## Works {#Works}

Reidemeister's interests were mainly in [combinatorial group theory](wikipedia://en/979834), [combinatorial topology](wikipedia://en/363400), [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), and the foundations of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). His works include *Knoten und Gruppen* (1926), *Einführung in die kombinatorische Topologie* (1932), and *Knotentheorie* (1932). He co-edited the journal *[Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381)* from 1947 until 1963.[^o13] He was also a philosopher. His book "Das exakte Denken der Griechen" (1949) is not as well known as his mathematical work. In it he remarks that mathematical thought is "just the beginning of thought".
