# Charles-Eugène Delaunay

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Charles-Eugène Delaunay"}

**Charles-Eugène Delaunay** (; 9 April 1816 – 5 August 1872) was a French [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) and mathematician. His [lunar motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_theory) studies were important in advancing both the theory of [planetary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) motion and mathematics.[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Lusigny-sur-Barse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusigny-sur-Barse), France, to Jacques-Hubert Delaunay and Catherine Choiselat,[^o5] Delaunay studied under [Jean-Baptiste Biot](wikipedia://en/75109) at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). He worked on the mechanics of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) as a special case of the [three-body problem](wikipedia://en/23859945#Three-body_problem). He published two volumes on the topic, each of 900 pages in length, in 1860 and 1867. The work hints at chaos in the system, and clearly demonstrates the problem of so-called "small denominators" in [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/102140). His [infinite series](wikipedia://en/102140) expression for finding the position of the Moon converged too slowly to be of practical use but was a catalyst in the development of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805).[^o7]

Delaunay maintained a home in [Ramerupt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramerupt), the ancestral home of his wife's family. He would frequent the commune each summer.[^o8][^o9]

Delaunay became director of the [Paris Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Observatory) in 1870 but drowned in a boating accident near [Cherbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherbourg), France, two years later.[^o6] He was followed by [Jean Claude Bouquet](wikipedia://en/59476782) at the Academy. [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Tait_%28physicist%29) in his book *An Elementary Treatise on Quaternions* edition 1867 on page 244 named Didonia in honour of Delaunay.

Delaunay was buried in Ramerupt.[^o10]

## Honours {#Honours}

- Member of the *[Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences)*, (1855)[^o12]
- [Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Medal_of_the_Royal_Astronomical_Society), (1870)[^o13]
- His name is one of the [72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_72_names_on_the_Eiffel_Tower).
- [Delaunay crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaunay_%28crater%29) on the Moon is named after him.[^o14]
