# E. M. Wright

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="E. M. Wright"}

**Sir Edward Maitland Wright** FRSE (13 February 1906 – 2 February 2005) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), best known for co-authoring *[An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers](wikipedia://en/31962097)* (Hardy Wright 1938)[^o4] with [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599). He served as the [Principal of the University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_of_the_University_of_Aberdeen) from 1962 to 1976.

:::

## Career {#Career}

He was born in Farnley, near Leeds, Yorkshire, where his father was a soap manufacturer. He moved to the south of England with his mother when his parents separated.

After obtaining a first-class mathematics degree as a self-taught external student at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), Wright studied at [Jesus College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford) and [Christ Church, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford). His research career lasted from 1931 until the early 1980s, firstly on a Research Fellowship at Christ Church, which included a year in Göttingen, Germany. He was then appointed a lecturer at Christ Church, teaching there until 1935 followed by his appointment as Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen). He held that chair from 1936 to 1962, except for a break during the war (from 1943 to 1945) when he was seconded to the Air Ministry Intelligence at MI6 headquarters. He became Vice-Principal of the University in 1961 and Principal and Vice-Chancellor from 1962 until he stood down in 1976. He nevertheless continued to work as a Research Fellow at the University until 1983. A building there is named after him in recognition of his service to the university.[^o6]

Wright worked in many different subspecialties, including [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), and published over a hundred papers. Most of his work focused on [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh in 1937 and awarded their [Makdougall Brisbane Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makdougall_Brisbane_Prize) in 1952.

He was elected to the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1929 and awarded their [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prizes) in 1978.

He was knighted in 1977 and awarded the Gold Medal of the Order of Polonia Restituta of Poland in 1978.

## Private life {#Private_life}

He died in Reading shortly before his 99th birthday. He had married Phyllis Harris of North Wales, with whom he had a son, the mathematician [John D. M. Wright](wikipedia://en/75085466).[^o9]
