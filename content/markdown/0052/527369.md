# Lists of mathematics topics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lists of mathematics topics"}

These lists cover a variety of topics related to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). Some link to hundreds of articles, others only a few. They cover, for example: aspects of [basic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_mathematics) and advanced mathematics, methodology, general concepts, reference tables, [mathematical societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_society), and [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

The purpose of this list is not similar to that of the [Mathematics Subject Classification](wikipedia://en/3008091) formulated by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). Many mathematics journals ask authors of research papers and expository articles to list subject codes from the Mathematics Subject Classification in their papers. The subject codes so listed are used by the two major reviewing databases, *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)* and *[Zentralblatt MATH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentralblatt_MATH)*. This list has some items that would not fit in such a classification, such as [list of exponential topics](wikipedia://en/490054) and [list of factorial and binomial topics](wikipedia://en/508022), which may surprise the reader with the diversity of their coverage.

#### Algebra {#Algebra}

[Algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) includes the study of algebraic structures, which are sets and operations defined on these sets satisfying certain axioms. The field of algebra is further divided according to which structure is studied; for instance, group theory concerns an algebraic structure called *group*.

#### Geometry and topology {#Geometry_and_topology}

*Image: Ford circles.svg.* [Ford circles](wikipedia://en/365503)—A circle rests upon each fraction in lowest terms. Each touches its neighbors without crossing.

[Topology](wikipedia://en/29954) developed from geometry; it looks at those properties that do not change even when the figures are deformed by stretching and bending, like dimension.

#### Combinatorics {#Combinatorics}

[Combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) concerns the study of [discrete](wikipedia://en/6026) (and usually [finite](wikipedia://en/11742)) objects. Aspects include "counting" the objects satisfying certain criteria (*[enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533)*), deciding when the criteria can be met, and constructing and analyzing objects meeting the criteria (as in *[combinatorial designs](wikipedia://en/2761968) and [matroid](wikipedia://en/244321) theory*), finding "largest", "smallest", or "optimal" objects (*[extremal combinatorics](wikipedia://en/2675917)* and *[combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555)*), and finding [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) structures these objects may have (*[algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582)*).

#### Number theory {#Number_theory}

The branch of mathematics deals with the properties and relationships of numbers, especially positive integers. [Number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) is a branch of pure mathematics devoted primarily to the study of the integers and integer-valued functions. German mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss said, "Mathematics is the queen of the sciences—and number theory is the queen of mathematics." Number theory also studies the natural, or whole, numbers. One of the central concepts in number theory is that of the [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), and there are many questions about primes that appear simple but whose resolution continues to elude mathematicians.

#### Dynamical systems and differential equations {#Dynamical_systems_and_differential_equations}

*Image: Limitcycle.svg.* Phase portrait of a continuous-time dynamical system, the [Van der Pol oscillator](wikipedia://en/1961786).

A [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) is an equation involving an unknown function and its derivatives.

In a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087), a fixed rule describes the time dependence of a point in a geometrical space. The mathematical models used to describe the swinging of a clock pendulum, the flow of water in a pipe, or the number of fish each spring in a lake are examples of dynamical systems.

#### Information theory and signal processing {#Information_theory_and_signal_processing}

[Information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) is a branch of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science) involving the quantification of [information](wikipedia://en/18985062). Historically, information theory was developed to find fundamental limits on compressing and reliably [communicating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication) data.

[Signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) is the analysis, interpretation, and manipulation of [signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28electrical_engineering%29). Signals of interest include [sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal_processing), [images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing), biological signals such as [ECG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECG), [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar) signals, and many others. Processing of such signals includes [filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_%28signal_processing%29), storage and reconstruction, separation of information from [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise), [compression](wikipedia://en/8013), and [feature extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_extraction).

#### Probability and statistics {#Probability_and_statistics}

*Image: Normal approximation to binomial.svg.* The "bell curve"—the probability density function of the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462).

[Probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) is the formalization and study of the mathematics of uncertain events or knowledge. The related field of [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) develops [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579) with mathematics. [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the science concerned with collecting and analyzing data, is an autonomous discipline (and not a subdiscipline of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969)).

#### Game theory {#Game_theory}

[Game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) is a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) that uses [models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_%28abstract%29) to study interactions with formalized incentive structures ("games"). It has applications in a variety of fields, including [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology), [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science), [social psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_psychology) and [military strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_strategy).
