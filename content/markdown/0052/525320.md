# Complete bipartite graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Complete bipartite graph"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **complete bipartite graph** or **biclique** is a special kind of [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) where every [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) of the first set is connected to every vertex of the second set.[^o3][^o4]

:::

Graph theory itself is typically dated as beginning with [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902)'s 1736 work on the [Seven Bridges of Königsberg](wikipedia://en/192753). However, [drawings](wikipedia://en/325813) of complete bipartite graphs were already printed as early as 1669, in connection with an edition of the works of [Ramon Llull](wikipedia://en/69677) edited by [Athanasius Kircher](wikipedia://en/93815).[^o6][^o5] Llull himself had made similar drawings of [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) three centuries earlier.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **complete bipartite graph** is a graph whose vertices can be partitioned into two subsets $V_{1}$ and $V_{2}$ such that no edge has both endpoints in the same subset, and every possible edge that could connect vertices in different subsets is part of the graph. That is, it is a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) $(V_{1}, V_{2}, E)$ such that for every two vertices $v_{1} \in V_{1}$ and$v_{2} \in V_{2}$, $v_{1}v_{2}$ is an edge in $E$. A complete bipartite graph with partitions of size $\lvert V_{1}\rvert = m$ and $\lvert V_{2}\rvert = n$, is denoted $K_{m,n}$;[^o3][^o4] every two graphs with the same notation are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Star graphs.svg.* The [star graphs](wikipedia://en/19310129) $K_{1,3}$, $K_{1,4}$, $K_{1,5}$, and $K_{1,6}$.

*Image: Zarankiewicz K4 7.svg.* A complete bipartite graph of $K_{4,7}$ showing that [Turán's brick factory problem](wikipedia://en/22039971) with 4 storage sites (yellow spots) and 7 kilns (blue spots) requires 18 crossings (red dots)

- For any $k$, $K_{1,k}$ is called a [star](wikipedia://en/19310129).[^o4] All complete bipartite graphs which are [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) are stars.
  - The graph $K_{1,3}$ is called a [claw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claw_%28graph_theory%29), and is used to define the [claw-free graphs](wikipedia://en/12415907).[^o11]
- The graph $K_{3,3}$ is called the [utility graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water,_gas,_and_electricity). This usage comes from a standard mathematical puzzle in which three utilities must each be connected to three buildings; it is impossible to solve without crossings due to the [nonplanarity](wikipedia://en/24314) of $K_{3,3}$.[^o12]
- The maximal bicliques found as subgraphs of the digraph of a relation are called **concepts**. When a lattice is formed by taking meets and joins of these subgraphs, the relation has an [Induced concept lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterogeneous_relation#Induced_concept_lattice). This type of analysis of relations is called [formal concept analysis](wikipedia://en/313845).

- Given a bipartite graph, testing whether it contains a complete bipartite subgraph $K_{i,i}$ for a parameter $i$ is an [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) problem.[^o18]
- A [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) cannot contain $K_{3,3}$ as a [minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_%28graph_theory%29); an [outerplanar graph](wikipedia://en/352343) cannot contain $K_{3,2}$ as a minor (These are not [sufficient conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition) for planarity and outerplanarity, but necessary). Conversely, every nonplanar graph contains either $K_{3,3}$ or the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{5}$ as a minor; this is [Wagner's theorem](wikipedia://en/3126130).[^o19]
- Every complete bipartite graph. $\mathbf{K}_{n,n}$ is a [Moore graph](wikipedia://en/4112719) and a $(n,4)$-[cage](wikipedia://en/7735022).[^o20]
- The complete bipartite graphs $\mathbf{K}_{n,n}$ and $\mathbf{K}_{n,n+1}$ have the maximum possible number of edges among all [triangle-free graphs](wikipedia://en/7667996) with the same number of vertices; this is [Mantel's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantel's_theorem). Mantel's result was generalized to $k$-partite graphs and graphs that avoid larger [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466) as subgraphs in [Turán's theorem](wikipedia://en/360601), and these two complete bipartite graphs are examples of [Turán graphs](wikipedia://en/360581), the extremal graphs for this more general problem.[^o21]
- The complete bipartite graph $\mathbf{K}_{m,n}$ has a [vertex covering number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_covering_number) of $\mathbf{min}\{m, n\}$ and an [edge covering number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_covering_number) of $\mathbf{max}\{m, n\}.$
- The complete bipartite graph $\mathbf{K}_{m,n}$ has a [maximum independent set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_independent_set) of size $\mathbf{max}\{m, n\}.$
- The [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of a complete bipartite graph $\mathbf{K}_{m,n}$ has eigenvalues $\sqrt{nm}$, $-\sqrt{nm}$ and 0; with multiplicity 1, 1 and $n + m - 2$ respectively.[^o22]
- The [Laplacian matrix](wikipedia://en/1448472) of a complete bipartite graph $\mathbf{K}_{m,n}$ has eigenvalues $n + m$, $n$, $m$, and 0; with multiplicity 1, $m - 1$, $n - 1$ and 1 respectively.
- A complete bipartite graph $\mathbf{K}_{m,n}$ has $m^{n-1} n^{m-1}$ [spanning trees](wikipedia://en/455770).[^o23]
- A complete bipartite graph $\mathbf{K}_{m,n}$ has a [maximum matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_matching) of size $\mathbf{min}\{m,n\}.$
- A complete bipartite graph $\mathbf{K}_{n,n}$ has a proper [$n$-edge-coloring](wikipedia://en/690647) corresponding to a [Latin square](wikipedia://en/37447).[^o24]
- Every complete bipartite graph is a [modular graph](wikipedia://en/51807881): every triple of vertices has a median that belongs to shortest paths between each pair of vertices.[^o25]
