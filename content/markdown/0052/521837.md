# Hecke operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hecke operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular in the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), a **Hecke operator**, studied by (txt), is a certain kind of "averaging" operator that plays a significant role in the structure of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) of modular forms and more general [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation).

:::

## Mathematical description {#Mathematical_description}

Hecke operators can be realized in a number of contexts. The simplest meaning is combinatorial, namely as taking for a given integer $n$ some function $f(\Lambda)$ defined on the [lattices](wikipedia://en/469246) of fixed rank to

$$\sum f(\Lambda')$$

with the sum taken over all the $\Lambda'$ that are [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of $\Lambda$ of index $n$. For example, with $n=2$ and two dimensions, there are three such $\Lambda'$. [Modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) are particular kinds of functions of a lattice, subject to conditions making them [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) and [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/622844) with respect to [homotheties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation), as well as moderate growth at infinity; these conditions are preserved by the summation, and so Hecke operators preserve the space of modular forms of a given weight.

Another way to express Hecke operators is by means of [double cosets](wikipedia://en/982771) in the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019). In the contemporary [adelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelic) approach, this translates to double cosets with respect to some compact subgroups.

::: {#o5 .statement cue=named-heading name="Explicit formula"}

### Explicit formula {#Explicit_formula}

Let $M_m$ be the set of $2\times 2$ integral matrices with [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) $m$ and $\Gamma = M_1$ be the full [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019) $\text{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. Given a [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000) $f(z)$ of weight $k$, the $m$th Hecke operator acts by the formula

$$T_m f(z) = m^{k-1}\sum_{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a & b\\ c & d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma\backslash M_m}(cz+d)^{-k}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right),$$

where $z$ is in the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826) and the normalization constant $m^{k-1}$ assures that the image of a form with integer Fourier coefficients has integer Fourier coefficients. This can be rewritten in the form

$$T_m f(z) = m^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix} ad = m \\ a, d > 0 \end{smallmatrix}}\frac{1}{d^k}\sum_{b \pmod d} f\left(\frac{az+b}{d}\right),$$

which leads to the formula for the Fourier coefficients of $T_m(f(z)) = \sum b(n) q^n$ in terms of the Fourier coefficients of $f(z) = \sum a(n) q^n$:

$$b(n) = \sum_{d|(m,n)}d^{k-1}a\left(\frac{mn}{d^2}\right).$$

One can see from this explicit formula that Hecke operators with different indices commute and that if $a(0) = 0$ then $b(0) = 0$, so the subspace $S_k$ of cusp forms of weight $k$ is preserved by the Hecke operators. If a (non-zero) cusp form $f$ is a [simultaneous eigenform](wikipedia://en/30613786) of all Hecke operators $T_m$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_m$ then $a(m) = \lambda_m a(1)$ and $a(1) \neq 1$. Hecke eigenforms are **normalized** so that $a(1) = 1$, then

$$T_m f = a(m) f, \quad a(m) a(n) = \sum_{d|(m,n)}d^{k-1}a\left(\frac{mn}{d^2}\right),\quad m,n\geq 1.$$

Thus for normalized cuspidal Hecke eigenforms of integer weight, their Fourier coefficients coincide with their Hecke eigenvalues.

:::

## History {#History}

(txt) used Hecke operators on modular forms in a paper on the special [cusp form](wikipedia://en/610165) of [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan), ahead of the general theory given by (txt). Mordell proved the Hecke relations for the [Ramanujan tau function](wikipedia://en/1231708), which implies that it is a [multiplicative function](wikipedia://en/20059), a property conjectured by Ramanujan. The idea goes back to earlier work of [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690), who treated [algebraic correspondences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_correspondence) between [modular curves](wikipedia://en/669552) which realise some individual Hecke operators.

## Hecke algebras {#Hecke_algebras}

Algebras of Hecke operators are called "Hecke algebras", and are [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346). In the classical [elliptic modular form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_modular_form) theory, the Hecke operators $T_n$ with $n$ coprime to the level acting on the space of cusp forms of a given weight are [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408) with respect to the [Petersson inner product](wikipedia://en/4028987). Therefore, the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) implies that there is a basis of modular forms that are [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) for these Hecke operators. Each of these basic forms possesses an [Euler product](wikipedia://en/398540). More precisely, its [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) is the [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) that has [Euler products](wikipedia://en/398540) with the local factor for each prime $p$ is the inverse of the **Hecke polynomial**, a quadratic polynomial in $p^{-s}$. In the case treated by Mordell, the space of cusp forms of weight 12 with respect to the full modular group is one-dimensional. It follows that the Ramanujan form has an Euler product and establishes the multiplicativity of [Ramanujan's tau function](wikipedia://en/1231708) $\tau(n)$.

[Other related mathematical rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_algebra_%28disambiguation%29) are also called "Hecke algebras", although sometimes the link to Hecke operators is not entirely obvious. These algebras include certain quotients of the [group algebras](wikipedia://en/349223) of [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138). The presence of this commutative operator algebra plays a significant role in the [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) of modular forms and generalisations.
