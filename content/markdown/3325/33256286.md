# Demographics of the world

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of the world"}

[Earth](wikipedia://en/9228) has a human population of over 8.2 billion as of 2025, with an overall [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 50 people per km<sup>2</sup> (130 per sq. mile). Nearly 60% of the world's population lives in [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia), with more than 2.8 billion in the countries of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) combined. The percentage shares of China, India and rest of South Asia of the world population have remained at similar levels for the last few thousand years of recorded history.[^o3][^o4]

:::

The world's population is predominantly urban and suburban,[^o5] and there has been significant migration toward cities and urban centers. The urban population jumped from 29% in 1950 to 55.3% in 2018.[^o6][^o7] Interpolating from the United Nations prediction that the world will be 51.3% urban by 2010, Ron Wimberley, Libby Morris and Gregory Fulkerson estimated 23 May 2007 would have been the first time the urban population was more populous than the rural population in history.[^o8] [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) are the most populous countries,[^o9] as the [birth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_rate) has consistently dropped in wealthy countries and until recently remained high in poorer countries. [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta) is the [largest urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_by_population) in the world.[^o7]

As of 2024, the [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) of the world is estimated at 2.25 children per woman,[^o10] which is slightly below the global average for the [replacement fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replacement_fertility_rate) of approximately 2.33 (as of 2003).[^o11] However, world [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) is unevenly distributed, with the total fertility rate ranging from the world's lowest of 0.8 in [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea),[^o12] to the highest of 6.7 in [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger).[^o13] The [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) estimated an annual population increase of 1.14% for the year of 2000.[^o14] The current world population growth is approximately 1.09%.[^o7] People under 15 years of age made up over a quarter of the world population (25.18%), and people age 65 and over made up nearly ten percent (9.69%) in 2021.[^o7] The world's [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) rate has increased dramatically in the last 40 years, from 66.7% in 1979 to 86.3% today.[^o15] Lower [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) levels are mostly attributable to [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty)[^o16] and are found mostly in [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia) and [Sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa).[^o17]

The world population more than tripled during the 20th century from about 1.65 billion in 1900 to 5.97 billion in 1999.[^o18][^o19][^o20] It reached the 2 billion mark in 1927, the 3 billion mark in 1960, 4 billion in 1974, and 5 billion in 1987.[^o21] The overall population of the world is approximately 8 billion as of November 2022. Currently, population growth is fastest among low wealth, [least developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries).[^o22] The UN projects a world population of 9.15 billion in 2050, a 32.7% increase from 6.89 billion in 2010.[^o18]

## History {#History}

*Image: Illustration of contemporary and past human populations Our World in Data.png.* Comparison of all humans living with all previous generations, as of March 2022.[^o25]

Historical migration of human populations begins with the movement of *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus)* out of Africa across [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) about a million years ago. *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human)* appear to have occupied all of Africa about 300,000 years ago, moved out of Africa 50,000 – 60,000 years ago, and had spread across [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Australia), [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Asia) and [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic_Europe) by 30,000 years BC. [Migration to the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Models_of_migration_to_the_New_World) took place 20,000 to 15,000 years ago, and by 2,000 years ago, most of the [Pacific Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands) were colonized.

Until c. 10,000 years ago, humans lived as [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer). They generally lived in small nomadic groups known as [band societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_societies). The advent of agriculture prompted the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution), when access to food surplus led to the formation of permanent [human settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement). About 6,000 years ago, the first proto-states developed in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt)'s [Nile Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Valley) and the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilisation). Early human settlements were dependent on proximity to [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources) and, depending on the [lifestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifestyle_%28sociology%29), other [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resources) used for [subsistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_economy). But humans have a great capacity for altering their [habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_%28ecology%29) by means of technology.

Since 1800, the [human population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_population) has increased from one billion[^o26] to over eight billion.[^o27] In 2004, some 2.5 billion out of 6.3 billion people (39.7%) lived in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area). In February 2008, the U.N. estimated that half the world's population would live in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) by the end of the year.[^o28] Problems for humans living in [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) include various forms of pollution and [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime),[^o29] especially in inner city and suburban [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum). Both overall population numbers and the proportion residing in cities are expected to increase significantly in the coming decades.[^o30]

## Population density {#Population_density}

*Image: Population density map of the world.svg.* Population density (people per km<sup>2</sup>) by country

*Image: Cities with 1M People in 2025.png.* Map showing urban areas with at least one million inhabitants in 2025. Only 3% of the world's population lived in urban areas in 1800; this proportion had risen to 47% by 2000, and reached 56% by 2020.[^o61]

The [world's population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population) is over 8 billion[^o62] and [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228)'s total surface area (including land and water) is 510 e6km2.[^o7] Therefore, the worldwide human population density is 8 billion ÷ 510 e6km2 = 15.7 . If only the Earth's land area of 150 e6km2 is taken into account, then human population density increases to 53.3 .[^o63][^o64]

Several of the most densely populated territories in the world are [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-states), [microstates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstates) or [dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory).[^o65][^o66] These territories share a relatively small area and a high [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) level, with an economically specialized [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) population drawing also on rural resources outside the area, illustrating the difference between high population density and [overpopulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_overpopulation).

## Population growth rate {#Population_growth_rate}

*Image: World population growth rate 1950–2050.svg.* Growth rate of world population (1950–2010) The sharp decline in world population growth in the early 1960s caused primarily by the [Great Chinese Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine)

Globally, the [growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth_rate) of the [human population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population) has been declining since peaking in 1962 and 1963 at 2.20% per annum. In 2009, the estimated annual growth rate was 1.1%.[^o138] The [CIA World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook) gives the world annual birthrate, mortality rate, and growth rate as 1.915%, 0.812%, and 1.092% respectively[^o7] The last one hundred years have seen a rapid increase in population due to [medical advances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_medicine#Modern_medicine) and massive increase in agricultural productivity[^o139] made possible by the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution).[^o140][^o141][^o142]

*Image: 1 world map 2010-2015 population increase rate by country.svg.* 2010–2015 net population increase rate, per 1000 people

The actual annual growth in the number of humans fell from its peak of 88.0 million in 1989, to a low of 73.9 million in 2003, after which it rose again to 75.2 million in 2006. Since then, annual growth has declined. In 2009, the human population increased by 74.6 million, which is projected to fall steadily to about 41 million per annum in 2050, at which time the population will have increased to about 9.2 billion.[^o138] Each region of the globe has seen great reductions in growth rate in recent decades, though growth rates remain above 2% in some countries of the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East) and [Sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa), and also in [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia), and [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America).[^o138]

Some countries experienced [negative population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_decline), especially in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) mainly due to low [fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate), high death rates and [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration). In [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa), growth is slowing due to the high number of [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV)-related deaths. Some [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe) countries might also encounter negative population growth.[^o143] [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan)'s population began decreasing in 2005.[^o144]

Population in the world increased from 1990 to 2008 with 1,423 billion and 27% growth. Measured by persons, the increase was highest in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) (290 million) and China (192 million). Population growth was highest in [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar) (174%) and [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates) (140%).[^o145]

In 2022, the world population reached 8 billion. The latest projections by the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) suggest that the global population could grow to around 8.5 billion in 2030, 9.7 billion in 2050 and 10.4 billion in 2100.[^o146]

According to the [CIA – The World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook), the country with the highest birth rate currently is Niger at 51.26 births per 1000 people. The country with the lowest birth rate is Japan at 7.64 births per 1000 people. Hong Kong, a Special Administrative Region of China, is at 7.42 births per 1000 people. As compared to the 1950s, birth rate was at 36 births per 1000 in the 1950s,[^o154] birth rate has declined by 16 births per 1000 people. In July 2011, the U.S. National Institutes of Health announced that the adolescent birth rate continues to decline.[^o155]

Birth rates vary even within the same geographic areas. In Europe, as of July 2011, Ireland's birth rate is 16.5 percent, which is 3.5 percent higher than the next-ranked country, the UK. France has a birth rate of 12.8 per cent while Sweden is at 12.3 percent.[^o156] In July 2011, the UK's Office for National Statistics (ONS) announced a 2.4% increase in live births in the UK in 2010 alone.[^o157] This is the highest birth rate in the UK in 40 years.[^o157] By contrast, the birth rate in Germany is only 8.3 per 1,000, which is so low that both the UK and France, which have significantly smaller populations, produced more births in 2010.[^o158] Birth rates also vary within the same geographic area, based on different demographic groups. For example, in April 2011, the U.S. CDC announced that the birth rate for women over the age of 40 in the U.S. rose between 2007 and 2009, while it fell among every other age group during the same time span.[^o159] In August 2011, Taiwan's government announced that its birth rate declined in the previous year, despite the fact that it implemented a host of approaches to encourage its citizens to have babies.[^o160]

Birth rates ranging from 10 to 20 births per 1000 are considered low, while rates from 40 to 50 births per 1000 are considered high. There are problems associated with both an extremely high birth rate and an extremely low birth rate. High birth rates can cause stress on the government welfare and family programs to support a youthful population. Additional problems faced by a country with a high birth rate include educating a growing number of children, creating jobs for these children when they enter the workforce, and dealing with the environmental effects that a large population can produce. Low birth rates can put stress on the government to provide adequate senior welfare systems and also the stress on families to support the elders themselves. There will be less children or working age population to support the constantly growing aging population.

## Total fertility rate {#Total_fertility_rate}

The [Total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) is the average number of children born per mother. In 2021, fertility levels high were found in sub-Saharan Africa (4.6 births per woman), Oceania excluding Australia and New Zealand (3.1), Northern Africa and Western Asia (2.8), and Central and Southern Asia (2.3).[^o146]

There is an inverse correlation between [income and fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_and_fertility), wherein [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) usually have a much lower [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate). Various [fertility factors](wikipedia://en/36960506) may be involved, such as education and urbanization. [Mortality rates](wikipedia://en/191305) are low, [birth control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_control) is understood and easily accessible, and costs are often deemed very high because of education, clothing, feeding, and social amenities. With wealth, contraception becomes affordable. However, in countries like Iran where contraception was made artificially affordable before the economy accelerated, birth rate also rapidly declined. Further, longer periods of time spent getting higher education often mean women have children later in life.[^o173] Female labor participation rate also has substantial negative impact on fertility. However, this effect is neutralized among Nordic or liberalist countries.[^o174]

In [undeveloped countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undeveloped_countries) on the other hand, families desire children for their labour and as caregivers for their parents in old age. Fertility rates are also higher due to the lack of access to [contraceptives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraceptives), generally lower levels of [female education](wikipedia://en/9384153), patriarchal culture and lower rates of female employment in industry.

## Sex ratio {#Sex_ratio}

*Image: Sex ratio total population.PNG.* }

The value for the entire [world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population) is 1.02 males/female,[^o7] with 1.07 at birth, 1.06 for those under 15, 1.02 for those between 15 and 64, and 0.78 for those over 65.

The [Northern Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands) have the highest female ratio with 0.77 males per female. [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar) has the highest male ratio, with 2.87 males/female. For the group aged below 15, [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone) has the highest female ratio with 0.96 males/female, and [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_%28country%29) and China are tied for the highest male ratio with 1.13 males/female (according to the 2006 [CIA World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook)).

The "[First World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World)" [G7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G7) members all have a gender ratio in the range of 0.95–0.98 for the total population, of 1.05–1.07 at birth, of 1.05–1.06 for the group below 15, of 1.00–1.04 for the group aged 15–64, and of 0.70–0.75 for those over 65.

Countries on the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula) tend to have a "natural" ratio of about 1.05 at birth but a very high ratio of males for those over 65 ([Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia) 1.13, [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates) 2.73, [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar) 2.84), indicating either an above-average mortality rate for females or a below-average mortality for males, or, more likely in this case, a large population of aging male [guest workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_worker). Conversely, countries of [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) (the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia)) tend to have a "normal" ratio at birth but a very low ratio of males among those over 65 (Russia 0.46, Latvia 0.48, Ukraine 0.52); similarly, [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia) has a far above average male ratio at birth (1.17), and a below-average male ratio above 65 (0.67). This effect may be caused by [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration) and higher male mortality as result of higher post-Soviet era deaths; it may also be related to the enormous (by western standards) rate of alcoholism in the former Soviet states. Another possible contributory factor is an aging population, with a higher than normal proportion of relatively elderly people: we recall that due to higher differential mortality rates the ratio of males to females reduces for each year of age.

## Education {#Education}

Total population: 83.7% over the age of 15 [can read and write](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_literacy_rate), 88.3% male and 79.2% female note: over two-thirds of the world's 793 million illiterate adults are found in only eight countries (Bangladesh, China, Egypt, Ethiopia, India, Indonesia, Nigeria, and Pakistan); of all the illiterate adults in the world, two-thirds are women; extremely low literacy rates are concentrated in three regions, the Arab states, South and West Asia, and Sub-Saharan Africa, where around one-third of the men and half of all women are illiterate (2005–09 est.)

As of 2008, the school life expectancy (primary to tertiary education) for a man or woman is 11 years. [^o188]
