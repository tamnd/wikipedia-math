# Theorem of the cube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theorem of the cube"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **theorem of the cube** is a condition for a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) over a product of three [complete varieties](wikipedia://en/1047627) to be trivial. It was first discovered in the context of [linear equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equivalence) by the [Italian school](wikipedia://en/383424) of algebraic geometry. The modern version of the theorem was first published by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991),[^n1] who credited it to [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019).[^n2] A treatment by means of [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357), and description in terms of the [Picard functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_functor), was given by [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594).[^n3]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The theorem states that for any [complete varieties](wikipedia://en/1047627) $U$, $V$ and $W$ over an algebraically closed field, and given points $u$, $v$ and $w$ on them, any [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) $L$ which has a trivial restriction to each of $U\times V\times\{w\}$, $U\times \{v\}\times W$, and $\{u\}\times V\times W$ is itself trivial.[^n4]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

### Special cases {#Special_cases}

On a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014) $X$, an invertible sheaf $L$ is *trivial* if it is isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}_X$ as an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. If the base $X$ is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742), then an invertible sheaf is (the sheaf of sections of) a [holomorphic line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle), and trivial means holomorphically equivalent to a [trivial bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle), not just topologically equivalent.

:::

### Restatement using biextensions {#Restatement_using_biextensions}

Weil's result has been restated in terms of [biextensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biextension), a concept now generally used in the [duality theory of abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_theory_of_abelian_varieties).[^n5]

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Theorem of the square"}

## Theorem of the square {#Theorem_of_the_square}

The **theorem of the square**[^n1][^n6] is a corollary (also due to Weil) applying to an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) $A$. One version of it states that the function $\phi_L$ taking $x\in A$ to $T_x^*L\otimes L^{-1}$ is a group homomorphism from $A$ to $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$ (where $T_x^*$ is translation by $x$ on line bundles).

:::
