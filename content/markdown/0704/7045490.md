# Dual abelian variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dual abelian variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **dual abelian variety** can be defined from an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) *A*, defined over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k*. A 1-dimensional abelian variety is an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225), and every elliptic curve is isomorphic to its dual, but this fails for higher-dimensional abelian varieties, so the concept of dual becomes more interesting in higher dimensions.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* be an abelian variety over a field *k*. We define $\operatorname{Pic}^0 (A) \subset \operatorname{Pic} (A)$ to be the subgroup of the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) consisting of line bundles *L* such that $m^*L \cong p^*L \otimes q^*L$, where $m, p, q$ are the multiplication and projection maps $A \times_k A \to A$ respectively. An element of $\operatorname{Pic}^0(A)$ is called a **degree 0 line bundle** on *A*.[^o4]

To *A* one then associates a **dual abelian variety** *A*<sup>v</sup> (over the same field), which is the solution to the following [moduli problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_problem). A family of degree 0 line bundles parametrized by a *k*-variety *T* is defined to be a line bundle *L* on *A*×*T* such that

1. for all $t \in T$, the restriction of *L* to *A*×{*t*} is a degree 0 line bundle,
1. the restriction of *L* to {0}×*T* is a trivial line bundle (here 0 is the identity of *A*).

Then there is a variety *A*<sup>v</sup> and a line bundle $P \to A \times A^\vee$, called the Poincaré bundle, which is a family of degree 0 line bundles parametrized by *A*<sup>v</sup> in the sense of the above definition.[^o5] Moreover, this family is universal, that is, to any family *L* parametrized by *T* is associated a unique morphism *f*: *T* → *A*<sup>v</sup> so that *L* is isomorphic to the pullback of *P* along the morphism 1<sub>A</sub>×*f*: *A*×*T* → *A*×*A*<sup>v</sup>. Applying this to the case when *T* is a point, we see that the points of *A*<sup>v</sup> correspond to line bundles of degree 0 on *A*, so there is a natural [group operation](wikipedia://en/19447) on *A*<sup>v</sup> given by [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of line bundles, which makes it into an abelian variety.

In the language of [representable functors](wikipedia://en/844461) one can state the above result as follows. The contravariant functor, which associates to each *k*-variety *T* the set of families of degree 0 line bundles parametrised by *T* and to each *k*-morphism *f*: *T* → *T**the mapping induced by the pullback with*****f*, is representable. The universal element representing this functor is the pair (*A*<sup>v</sup>,*P*).***

This association is a duality in the sense that there is a [natural isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) between the double dual *A*<sup>vv</sup> and *A* (defined via the Poincaré bundle) and that it is [contravariant functorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor), i.e. it associates to all morphisms *f*: *A* → *B* dual morphisms *f*<sup>v</sup>: *B*<sup>v</sup> → *A*<sup>v</sup> in a compatible way. The *n*-torsion of an abelian variety and the *n*-torsion of its dual are [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_dual) to each other when *n* is coprime to the characteristic of the base. In general - for all *n* - the *n*-torsion [group schemes](wikipedia://en/510523) of dual abelian varieties are [Cartier duals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_dual) of each other. This generalizes the [Weil pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_pairing) for elliptic curves.

:::

## History {#History}

The theory was first put into a good form when *K* was the field of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). In that case there is a general form of duality between the [Albanese variety](wikipedia://en/1814828) of a [complete variety](wikipedia://en/1047627) *V*, and its [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety); this was realised, for definitions in terms of [complex tori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_tori), as soon as [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) had given a general definition of Albanese variety. For an abelian variety *A*, the Albanese variety is *A* itself, so the dual should be *Pic*<sup>0</sup>(*A*), the [connected component](wikipedia://en/6233) of the identity element of what in contemporary terminology is the [Picard scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_scheme).

For the case of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) *J* of a [compact Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface) *C*, the choice of a [principal polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_polarization) of *J* gives rise to an identification of *J* with its own Picard variety. This in a sense is just a consequence of [Abel's theorem](wikipedia://en/10756143). For general abelian varieties, still over the complex numbers, *A* is in the same [isogeny](wikipedia://en/1085176) class as its dual. An explicit isogeny can be constructed by use of an [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) *L* on *A* (i.e. in this case a [holomorphic line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle)), when the subgroup

*K*(*L*)

of translations on *L* that take *L* into an isomorphic copy is itself finite. In that case, the quotient

*A*/*K*(*L*)

is isomorphic to the dual abelian variety *A*<sup>v</sup>.

This construction of *A*<sup>v</sup> extends to any field *K* of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero).[^o7] In terms of this definition, the **Poincaré bundle**, a universal line bundle can be defined on

*A* × *A*<sup>v</sup>.

The construction when *K* has characteristic *p* uses [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory). The definition of *K*(*L*) has to be in terms of a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) that is a scheme-theoretic [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29), and the quotient taken is now a quotient by a subgroup scheme.[^o8]

## The Dual Isogeny {#The_Dual_Isogeny}

Let $f: A \to B$ be an [isogeny](wikipedia://en/1085176) of abelian varieties. (That is, $f$ is finite-to-one and surjective.) We will construct an isogeny $f^\vee: B^\vee \to A^\vee$ using the functorial description of $A^\vee$, which says that the data of a map $f^\vee: B^\vee \to A^\vee$ is the same as giving a family of degree zero line bundles on $A$, parametrized by $B^\vee$.

To this end, consider the isogeny $f \times 1_{B^\vee}: A \times B^\vee \to B \times B^\vee$ and $(f \times 1_{B^\vee})^* P_{B}$ where $P_B$ is the Poincare line bundle for $B$. This is then the required family of degree zero line bundles on $A$.

By the aforementioned functorial description, there is then a morphism $f^\vee: B^\vee \to A^\vee$ so that $(f^\vee \times 1_A)^*P_A \cong (f \times 1_{B^\vee})^* P_{B}$. One can show using this description that this map is an isogeny of the same degree as $f$, and that $f^{\vee\vee} = f$.[^o10]

Hence, we obtain a contravariant endofunctor on the category of abelian varieties which squares to the identity. This kind of functor is often called a *dualizing functor*.[^o11]

## Mukai's Theorem {#Mukai's_Theorem}

A celebrated theorem of Mukai[^o14] states that there is an isomorphism of [derived categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_categories) $D^b(A) \cong D^b(A^\vee)$, where $D^b(X)$ denotes the bounded derived category of [coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaves) on *X*. Historically, this was the first use of the [Fourier-Mukai transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier-Mukai_transform) and shows that the bounded derived category cannot necessarily distinguish non-isomorphic varieties.

Recall that if *X* and *Y* are varieties, and $\mathcal{K} \in D^b(X \times Y)$ is a complex of coherent sheaves, we define the **Fourier-Mukai transform** $\Phi^{X \to Y}_{\mathcal{K}}: D^b(X) \to D^b(Y)$ to be the composition $\Phi^{X \to Y}_{\mathcal{K}}(\cdot) = Rq_*(\mathcal{K} \otimes_L Lp^*(\cdot))$, where *p* and *q* are the projections onto *X* and *Y* respectively.

Note that $p$ is flat and hence $p^*$ is exact on the level of coherent sheaves, and in applications $\mathcal{K}$ is often a line bundle so one may usually leave the left derived functors underived in the above expression. Note also that one can analogously define a Fourier-Mukai transform $\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}^{Y \to X}$ using the same kernel, by just interchanging the projection maps in the formula.

The statement of Mukai's theorem is then as follows.

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let *A* be an abelian variety of dimension *g* and $P_A$ the Poincare line bundle on $A \times A^\vee$. Then, $\Phi_{P_A}^{A^\vee \to A} \circ \Phi_{P_A}^{A\to A^\vee} \cong \iota^*[-g]$, where $\iota: A \to A$ is the inversion map, and $[-g]$ is the shift functor. In particular, $\Phi_{P_A}^{A\to A^\vee}$ is an isomorphism.[^o16]

:::
