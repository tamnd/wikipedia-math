# Conchoid of de Sluze

*Image: Conchoid of deSluze.svg.* The Conchoid of de Sluze for several values of $a$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conchoid of de Sluze"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **conchoids of de Sluze** are a [family](wikipedia://en/5726601) of [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691) studied in 1662 by Walloon mathematician [René François Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_François_Walter), baron de Sluze.[^o2][^o3]

:::

The curves are defined by the [polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates) equation

$$r=\sec\theta+a\cos\theta \,.$$

In [cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706), the curves satisfy the [implicit equation](wikipedia://en/250896)

$$(x-1)(x^2+y^2)=ax^2 \,$$

except that for $a = 0$ the implicit form has an [acnode](wikipedia://en/3090256) $(0,0)$ not present in polar form.

They are [rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_curve), [circular](wikipedia://en/24215692), [cubic plane curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_plane_curve).

These expressions have an [asymptote](wikipedia://en/3107) $x = 1$ (for $a \ne 0$). The point most distant from the asymptote is $(1 + a, 0)$. $(0,0)$ is a [crunode](wikipedia://en/3090324) for $a < -1$.

The area between the curve and the [asymptote](wikipedia://en/3107) is, for $a \ge -1$,

$$|a|(1+a/4)\pi \,$$

while for $a < -1$, the area is

$$\left(1-\frac a2\right)\sqrt{-(a+1)}-a\left(2+\frac a2\right)\arcsin\frac1{\sqrt{-a}}.$$

If $a < -1$, the curve will have a loop. The area of the loop is

$$\left(2+\frac a2\right)a\arccos\frac1{\sqrt{-a}} + \left(1-\frac a2\right)\sqrt{-(a+1)}.$$

Four of the family have names of their own:

- $a = 0$, [line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29) (asymptote to the rest of the family)
- $a = -1$, [cissoid of Diocles](wikipedia://en/820041)
- $a = -2$, [right strophoid](wikipedia://en/2738060)
- $a = -4$, [trisectrix of Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/2738114)
