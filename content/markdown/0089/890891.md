# Zero–one law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zero–one law"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **zero–one law** is a result that states that an event must have probability 0 or 1 and no intermediate value. Sometimes, the statement is that the limit of certain probabilities must be 0 or 1.

:::

It may refer to:

- [Borel–Cantelli lemma](wikipedia://en/44987),
- [Blumenthal's zero–one law](wikipedia://en/54262640) for [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process),
- [Engelbert–Schmidt zero–one law](wikipedia://en/56591592) for continuous, nondecreasing additive functionals of Brownian motion,
- [Hewitt–Savage zero–one law](wikipedia://en/6595367) for exchangeable sequences,
- [Kolmogorov's zero–one law](wikipedia://en/191797) for the tail σ-algebra,
- [Lévy's zero–one law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lévy's_zero–one_law), related to martingale convergence,
- Gaussian process.

Outside the area of probability, it may refer to:

- Topological zero–one law, related to [meager sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meager_set),
- [Zero-one law (logic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-one_law_%28logic%29) for sentences valid in finite structures.
