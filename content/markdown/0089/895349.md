# Philipp Furtwängler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philipp Furtwängler"}

**Friederich Pius Philipp Furtwängler** (April 21, 1869 – May 19, 1940) was a German [number theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theorist).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Furtwängler wrote an 1896 doctoral dissertation at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) on [cubic forms](wikipedia://en/6680683) (*Zur Theorie der in Linearfaktoren zerlegbaren ganzzahligen ternären kubischen Formen*), under [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887). Most of his academic life, from 1912 to 1938, was spent at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), where he taught for example [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), who later said that Furtwängler's lectures on number theory were the best mathematical lectures that he ever heard; Gödel had originally intended to become a physicist but turned to mathematics partly as a result of Furtwängler's lectures. From 1916, Furtwängler became increasingly paralysed[^o4] and, without notes, lectured from a wheelchair while his assistant wrote equations on the blackboard.[^o5]

Some of Furtwängler's doctoral students were [Wolfgang Gröbner](wikipedia://en/1049506), [Nikolaus Hofreiter](wikipedia://en/22209837), [Henry Mann](wikipedia://en/30545778), [Otto Schreier](wikipedia://en/4355201), and [Olga Taussky-Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Taussky-Todd). Through these and others, he has over 3000 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy).

He is now best known for his contribution to the [principal ideal theorem](wikipedia://en/3136164) in the form of his *Beweis des Hauptidealsatzes für Klassenkörper algebraischer Zahlkörper* (1929).

Philipp Furtwängler was a grandson of the organ builder Philipp Furtwängler (1800-1867) and a second cousin of the conductor [Wilhelm Furtwängler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Furtwängler).[^o7]
