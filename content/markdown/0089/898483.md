# Semistable abelian variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Semistable abelian variety"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **semistable abelian variety** is an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) defined over a [global](wikipedia://en/288358) or [local field](wikipedia://en/145375), which is characterized by how it reduces at the primes of the field.

:::

For an abelian variety $A$ defined over a field $F$ with [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) $R$, consider the [Néron model](wikipedia://en/9190726) of $A$, which is a 'best possible' model of $A$ defined over $R$. This model may be represented as a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) over $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$ (cf. [spectrum of a ring](wikipedia://en/37291)) for which the [generic fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_fibre) constructed by means of the [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) $\mathrm{Spec}(F) \to \mathrm{Spec}(R)$ gives back $A$. The Néron model is a smooth [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523), so we can consider $A^0$, the connected component of the Néron model which contains the identity for the group law. This is an open subgroup scheme of the Néron model. For a [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) $k$, $A^0_k$ is a [group variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_variety) over $k$, hence an extension of an abelian variety by a linear group. If this linear group is an [algebraic torus](wikipedia://en/504697), so that $A^0_k$ is a [semiabelian variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiabelian_variety), then $A$ has *semistable reduction* at the prime corresponding to $k$. If $F$ is a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358), then $A$ is semistable if it has good or semistable reduction at all primes.

The fundamental **[semistable reduction theorem](wikipedia://en/70859961)** of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) states that an abelian variety acquires semistable reduction over a finite extension of $F$.[^o2]

## Semistable elliptic curve {#Semistable_elliptic_curve}

A **semistable elliptic curve** may be described more concretely as an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) that has [bad reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_reduction) only of [multiplicative type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_bad_reduction).[^o5] Suppose $E$ is an elliptic curve defined over the [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) field $\mathbb{Q}$. It is known that there is a [finite](wikipedia://en/11742), [non-empty set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-empty_set) *S* of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ for which $E$ has *bad reduction* *[modulo](wikipedia://en/20087)* $p$. The latter means that the curve $E_p$ obtained by reduction of $E$ to the [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field) with $p$ elements has a [singular point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity). Roughly speaking, the condition of multiplicative reduction amounts to saying that the singular point is a [double point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_point), rather than a [cusp](wikipedia://en/6851758).[^o6] Deciding whether this condition holds is effectively computable by [Tate's algorithm](wikipedia://en/14795246).[^o7][^o9] Therefore, in a given case it is decidable whether or not the reduction is semistable, namely multiplicative reduction at worst.

The semistable reduction theorem for $E$ may also be made explicit: $E$ acquires semistable reduction over the extension of $F$ generated by the coordinates of the points of order 12.[^o8][^o9]
