# Francis John Welsh Whipple

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Francis John Welsh Whipple"}

**Francis John Welsh Whipple** [ScD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ScD) [FInstP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Institute_of_Physics) (17 March 1876 – 25 September 1943) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [meteorologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorologist) and [seismologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismologist). From 1925 to 1939, he was superintendent of the [Kew Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kew_Observatory).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Whipple was the son of Kew Observatory employees [George Mathews Whipple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mathews_Whipple) and [Elizabeth Beckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Beckley), an astronomical photographer.[^o4][^o5]

Whipple attended [Willington Preparatory School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willington_School) in [Putney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putney), where in 1888 he won a scholarship at [Merchant Taylors' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Taylors'_School,_Northwood).[^o6] From here, he obtained a scholarship at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1895, where he was placed [Second Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Wrangler) in the Mathematical Tripos of 1897.[^o7] In 1899, he showed that [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle) could be [self stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_and_motorcycle_dynamics#Self-stability).[^o8][^o9][^o10]

## Career {#Career}

From 1899–1912, he was an assistant master at Merchant Taylors' School, and then worked at the [Meteorological Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_Office) in [Exhibition Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_Road), [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington) from 1912, living at Addison Road, [Bedford Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Park,_London).[^o13] From 1925, he was Assistant-Director of the Meteorological Office and Superintendent of [Kew Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kew_Observatory), where he succeeded [Charles Chree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Chree). Whipple remained in this post until he retired in 1939.[^o7] He also served as president of the [Royal Meteorological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Meteorological_Society) from 1936 to 1937.[^o14]

In his role as Superintendent of [Kew Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kew_Observatory), Whipple played an important part in the recording of earthquakes, since Kew had been the site of a seismological observatory since 1898.[^o15] During his time as superintendent, Whipple devoted considerable time and energy to improve the quality of the seismological observations. He realised that the sensitivity of the [seismometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismometers) was badly affected by wind, since wind would cause the whole observatory building to move. So he designed and commissioned a new, underground bunker to hold the Kew seismometers.[^o16] Whipple was Chair of the Seismological Investigations Committee of the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Science_Association) from 1931-1939,[^o17] and also served on the National Committee for Geodesy and Geophysics.[^o18] These roles brought him into contact with prominent [seismologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismologists) around the world. Whipple was a [Fellow of the Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Institute_of_Physics), and was awarded the [ScD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ScD) degree from Cambridge in 1929.

## Family and connections {#Family_and_connections}

Francis Whipple had an older brother, [Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Stewart_Whipple), whose collection of [scientific instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_instruments) and books was the basis of the [Whipple Museum of the History of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whipple_Museum_of_the_History_of_Science) in Cambridge.[^o20] Whipple's father, George Whipple, was superintendent of Kew Observatory from 1876-1893; as was George's godfather, [John Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Welsh_%28meteorologist%29), from 1852-1859.[^o21]
