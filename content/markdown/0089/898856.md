# Conchoid (mathematics)

*Image: Conchoid of Nicomedes.png.* **Conchoids of line with common center.** red solid #333333 Each pair of coloured curves is length $d$ from the intersection with the line that a ray through $O$ makes. solid blue solid lime solid red

*Image: Nicomedes.gif.* Conchoid of Nicomedes drawn by an apparatus illustrated in Eutocius' Commentaries on the works of Archimedes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conchoid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **conchoid** is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) derived from a fixed point $O$, another curve, and a length $d$. It was invented by the ancient Greek mathematician [Nicomedes](wikipedia://en/12434756).[^o2]

:::

## Description {#Description}

For every line through $O$ that intersects the given curve at $A$ the two points on the line which are $d$ from $A$ are on the conchoid. The conchoid is, therefore, the [cissoid](wikipedia://en/899085) of the given curve and a circle of radius $d$ and center $O$. They are called *conchoids* because the shape of their outer branches resembles [conch shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conch).

The simplest expression uses polar coordinates with $O$ at the origin. If

$$r=\alpha(\theta)$$

expresses the given curve, then

$$r=\alpha(\theta)\pm d$$

expresses the conchoid.

If the curve is a [line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29), then the conchoid is the *conchoid of [Nicomedes](wikipedia://en/12434756)*.

For instance, if the curve is the line $x = a$, then the line's polar form is $r = a \sec \theta$ and therefore the conchoid can be expressed [parametrically](wikipedia://en/576108) as

$$x=a \pm d \cos \theta,\, y=a \tan \theta \pm d \sin \theta.$$

A [limaçon](wikipedia://en/641715) is a conchoid with a circle as the given curve.

The so-called [conchoid of de Sluze](wikipedia://en/898316) and [conchoid of Dürer](wikipedia://en/7853003) are not actually conchoids. The former is a strict cissoid and the latter a construction more general yet.
