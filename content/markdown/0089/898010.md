# Hodge cycle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hodge cycle"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Hodge cycle** or **Hodge class** is a particular kind of [homology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_class) defined on a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V*, or more generally on a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538). A homology class *x* in a [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group)

:::

$$H_k(V, \mathbb{C}) = H$$

where *V* is a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) complex algebraic variety or Kähler manifold is a **Hodge cycle**, provided it satisfies two conditions. Firstly, *k* is an even integer $2p$, and in the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) decomposition of *H* shown to exist in [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), *x* is purely of type $(p,p)$. Secondly, *x* is a rational class, in the sense that it lies in the image of the abelian group homomorphism

$$H_k(V, \mathbb{Q}) \to H$$

defined in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) (as a special case of the [universal coefficient theorem](wikipedia://en/996107)). The conventional term Hodge *cycle* therefore is slightly inaccurate, in that *x* is considered as a *class* ([modulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulo_%28jargon%29) boundaries); but this is normal usage.

The importance of Hodge cycles lies primarily in the [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518), to the effect that Hodge cycles should always be [algebraic cycles](wikipedia://en/9520954), for *V* a [complete algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_algebraic_variety). This is an unsolved problem, one of the [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234). It is known that being a Hodge cycle is a [necessary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition) to be an algebraic cycle that is rational, and numerous particular cases of the conjecture are known.
