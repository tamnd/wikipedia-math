# Local hidden-variable theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local hidden-variable theory"}

In the [interpretation of quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretations_of_quantum_mechanics), a **local hidden-variable theory** is a [hidden-variable theory](wikipedia://en/211602) that satisfies the [principle of locality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_locality). These models attempt to account for the probabilistic features of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) via the mechanism of underlying but inaccessible variables, with the additional requirement that distant events be statistically independent.

:::

The mathematical implications of a local hidden-variable theory with regard to [quantum entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336) were explored by physicist [John Stewart Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_Bell), who [in 1964 proved](wikipedia://en/56369) that broad classes of local hidden-variable theories cannot reproduce the correlations between measurement outcomes that quantum mechanics predicts, a result since confirmed by a range of detailed [Bell test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_test) experiments.[^o2]

## Models {#Models}

### Single qubit {#Single_qubit}

A [collection of related theorems](wikipedia://en/56369), beginning with Bell's proof in 1964, show that quantum mechanics is incompatible with local hidden variables. However, as Bell pointed out, restricted sets of quantum phenomena *can* be imitated using local hidden-variable models. Bell provided a local hidden-variable model for quantum measurements upon a spin-1/2 particle, or in the terminology of quantum information theory, a single [qubit](wikipedia://en/25284).[^o5] Bell's model was later simplified by [N. David Mermin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._David_Mermin), and a closely related model was presented by [Simon B. Kochen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_B._Kochen) and [Ernst Specker](wikipedia://en/22209158).[^o6][^o7][^o8] The existence of these models is related to the fact that [Gleason's theorem](wikipedia://en/6796998) does not apply to the case of a single qubit.[^o9]

### Bipartite quantum states {#Bipartite_quantum_states}

Bell also pointed out that up until then, discussions of [quantum entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336) focused on cases where the results of measurements upon two particles were either perfectly correlated or perfectly anti-correlated. These special cases can also be explained using local hidden variables.[^o5][^o11][^o12]

For [separable states](wikipedia://en/2833933) of two particles, there is a simple hidden-variable model for any measurements on the two parties. Surprisingly, there are also [entangled states](wikipedia://en/25336) for which all [von Neumann measurements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_in_quantum_mechanics) can be described by a hidden-variable model.[^o13] Such states are entangled, but do not violate any Bell inequality. The so-called [Werner states](wikipedia://en/23606750) are a single-parameter family of states that are invariant under any transformation of the type $U \otimes U,$ where $U$ is a unitary matrix. For two qubits, they are noisy singlets given as

$$\varrho = p \vert \psi^- \rangle \langle \psi^-\vert + (1 - p) \frac{\mathbb{I}}{4},$$

where the singlet is defined as $\vert \psi^-\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\vert 01\rangle - \vert 10\rangle\right)$.

[Reinhard F. Werner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_F._Werner) showed that such states allow for a hidden-variable model for $p \leq 1/2$, while they are entangled if $p > 1/3$. The bound for hidden-variable models could be improved until $p = 2/3$.[^o14] Hidden-variable models have been constructed for Werner states even if positive operator-valued measurements ([POVM](wikipedia://en/3793084)) are allowed, not only von Neumann measurements.[^o15] Hidden variable models were also constructed to noisy maximally entangled states, and even extended to arbitrary pure states mixed with white noise.[^o16] Beside bipartite systems, there are also results for the multipartite case. A hidden-variable model for any von Neumann measurements at the parties has been presented for a three-qubit quantum state.[^o17]

## Time-dependent variables {#Time-dependent_variables}

Previously some new hypotheses were conjectured concerning the role of time in constructing hidden-variables theory. One approach was suggested by K. Hess and W. Philipp and relies upon possible consequences of time dependencies of hidden variables; this hypothesis has been criticized by [Richard D. Gill](wikipedia://en/7716518), Gregor Weihs, [Anton Zeilinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Zeilinger) and [Marek Żukowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marek_Żukowski), as well as D. M. Appleby.[^o19][^o20][^o21]
