# Fermat curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fermat curve"}

*Image: Quartic Fermat curve.png.* With respect to the quartic Fermat curve $x^4 + y^4 = 1$, the hyperbolic lemniscate sine is analogous to the trigonometric tangent function. Unlike $\operatorname{slh}$ and $\operatorname{clh}$, the functions $\sin_4$ and $\cos_4$ cannot be analytically extended to meromorphic functions in the whole complex plane.[^o2]

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Fermat curve** is the [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) defined in [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) (*X*:*Y*:*Z*) by the **Fermat equation:**

:::

$$X^n + Y^n = Z^n.\$$

Therefore, in terms of the [affine plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) its equation is:

$$x^n + y^n = 1.\$$

An integer solution to the Fermat equation would correspond to a nonzero [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) solution to the affine equation, and vice versa. But by [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) it is now known that (for *n* > 2) there are no nontrivial integer solutions to the Fermat equation; therefore, the Fermat curve has no nontrivial rational points.

The Fermat curve is [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) and has [genus](wikipedia://en/61891):

$$(n - 1)(n - 2)/2.\$$

This means genus 0 for the case *n* = 2 (a [conic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic)) and genus 1 only for *n* = 3 (an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225)). The [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of the Fermat curve has been studied in depth. It is isogenous to a product of simple abelian varieties with [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183).

The Fermat curve also has [gonality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonality):

$$n-1.\$$

## Fermat varieties {#Fermat_varieties}

Fermat-style equations in more variables define as [projective varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_varieties) the **Fermat varieties**.
