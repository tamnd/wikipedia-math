# Free presentation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free presentation"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **free presentation** of a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) *M* over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* is an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) of *R*-modules:

:::

$$\bigoplus_{i \in I} R \ \overset{f} \to\ \bigoplus_{j \in J} R \ \overset{g}\to\ M \to 0.$$

Note the image under *g* of the standard basis [generates](wikipedia://en/50718738) *M*. In particular, if *J* is [finite](wikipedia://en/11742), then *M* is a [finitely generated module](wikipedia://en/326430). If *I* and *J* are finite sets, then the presentation is called a **finite presentation**; a module is called [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_module) if it admits a finite presentation.

Since *f* is a [module homomorphism](wikipedia://en/474118) between [free modules](wikipedia://en/326454), it can be visualized as an (infinite) [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) with entries in *R* and *M* as its [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715).

A free presentation always exists: any module is a [quotient](wikipedia://en/364820) of a free module: $F \ \overset{g}\to\ M \to 0$, but then the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of *g* is again a quotient of a free module: $F' \ \overset{f} \to\ \ker g \to 0$. The combination of *f* and *g* is a free presentation of *M*. Now, one can obviously keep "resolving" the kernels in this fashion; the result is called a [free resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_resolution). Thus, a free presentation is the early part of the free resolution.

A presentation is useful for computation. For example, since [tensoring](wikipedia://en/2993836) is [right-exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-exact_functor), tensoring the above presentation with a module, say *N*, gives:

$$\bigoplus_{i \in I} N \ \overset{f \otimes 1} \to\ \bigoplus_{j \in J} N \to M \otimes_R N \to 0.$$

This says that $M \otimes_R N$ is the cokernel of $f \otimes 1$. If *N* is also a ring (and hence an [*R*-algebra](wikipedia://en/2112)), then this is the presentation of the *N*-module $M \otimes_R N$; that is, the presentation extends under base extension.

For left-exact [functors](wikipedia://en/10987), there is for example

::: {#o2 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=math-theorem name="Proposition"}

**Proposition.**

Let *F*, *G* be left-exact contravariant functors from the category of modules over a commutative ring *R* to abelian groups and *θ* a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022) from *F* to *G*. If $\theta: F(R^{\oplus n}) \to G(R^{\oplus n})$ is an isomorphism for each natural number *n*, then $\theta: F(M) \to G(M)$ is an isomorphism for any finitely-presented module *M*.

:::

Proof: Applying *F* to a finite presentation $R^{\oplus n} \to R^{\oplus m} \to M \to 0$ results in

$$0 \to F(M) \to F(R^{\oplus m}) \to F(R^{\oplus n}).$$

This can be trivially extended to

$$0 \to 0 \to F(M) \to F(R^{\oplus m}) \to F(R^{\oplus n}).$$

The same thing holds for $G$. Now apply the [five lemma](wikipedia://en/138214). $\square$
