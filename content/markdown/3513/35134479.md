# Normal-Wishart distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal-Wishart distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **normal-Wishart distribution** (or **Gaussian-Wishart distribution**) is a multivariate four-parameter family of continuous [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543). It is the [conjugate prior](wikipedia://en/846412) of a [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) with unknown [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) and [precision matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_matrix) (the inverse of the [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752)).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose

$$\boldsymbol\mu|\boldsymbol\mu_0,\lambda,\boldsymbol\Lambda \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol\mu_0,(\lambda\boldsymbol\Lambda)^{-1})$$

has a [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) with [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) $\boldsymbol\mu_0$ and [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) $(\lambda\boldsymbol\Lambda)^{-1}$, where

$$\boldsymbol\Lambda|\mathbf{W},\nu \sim \mathcal{W}(\boldsymbol\Lambda|\mathbf{W},\nu)$$

has a [Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/364996). Then $(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\Lambda)$ has a normal-Wishart distribution, denoted as

$$(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\Lambda) \sim \mathrm{NW}(\boldsymbol\mu_0,\lambda,\mathbf{W},\nu) .$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

### Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

$$f(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\Lambda|\boldsymbol\mu_0,\lambda,\mathbf{W},\nu) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol\mu|\boldsymbol\mu_0,(\lambda\boldsymbol\Lambda)^{-1})\ \mathcal{W}(\boldsymbol\Lambda|\mathbf{W},\nu)$$

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Scaling {#Scaling}

### Marginal distributions {#Marginal_distributions}

By construction, the [marginal distribution](wikipedia://en/506077) over $\boldsymbol\Lambda$ is a [Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/364996), and the [conditional distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_distribution) over $\boldsymbol\mu$ given $\boldsymbol\Lambda$ is a [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347). The [marginal distribution](wikipedia://en/506077) over $\boldsymbol\mu$ is a [multivariate *t*-distribution](wikipedia://en/7984768).

:::

## Posterior distribution of the parameters {#Posterior_distribution_of_the_parameters}

After making $n$ observations $\boldsymbol{x}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{x}_n$, the posterior distribution of the parameters is

$$(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\Lambda) \sim \mathrm{NW}(\boldsymbol\mu_n,\lambda_n,\mathbf{W}_n,\nu_n),$$

where

$$\lambda_n = \lambda + n,$$

$$\boldsymbol\mu_n = \frac{\lambda \boldsymbol\mu_0 + n\boldsymbol{\bar{x}}}{\lambda + n},$$

$$\nu_n = \nu + n,$$

$\mathbf{W}_n^{-1} = \mathbf{W}^{-1} + \sum_{i=1}^n (\boldsymbol{x}_i - \boldsymbol{\bar{x}})(\boldsymbol{x}_i - \boldsymbol{\bar{x}})^T + \frac{n \lambda}{n + \lambda} (\boldsymbol{\bar{x}} - \boldsymbol\mu_0)(\boldsymbol{\bar{x}} - \boldsymbol\mu_0)^T.$[^o13]

## Generating normal-Wishart random variates {#Generating_normal-Wishart_random_variates}

Generation of random variates is straightforward:

1. Sample $\boldsymbol\Lambda$ from a [Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/364996) with parameters $\mathbf{W}$ and $\nu$
1. Sample $\boldsymbol\mu$ from a [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) with mean $\boldsymbol\mu_0$ and variance $(\lambda\boldsymbol\Lambda)^{-1}$

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- The [normal-inverse Wishart distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal-inverse_Wishart_distribution) is essentially the same distribution parameterized by variance rather than precision.
- The [normal-gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/10896234) is the one-dimensional equivalent.
- The [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) and [Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/364996) are the component distributions out of which this distribution is made.
