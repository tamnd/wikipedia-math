# Kronheimer–Mrowka basic class

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kronheimer–Mrowka basic class"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kronheimer–Mrowka basic classes** are elements of the second [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) $H^2(M;\mathbb{Z})$ of a [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879), smooth [4-manifold](wikipedia://en/1362795) $M$ of simple type that determine its [Donaldson polynomials](wikipedia://en/7684340). They were introduced by (txt).

:::

## Description {#Description}

For a 4-manifold $M$, its [Donaldson invariants](wikipedia://en/11256383) are an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $\gamma_0(M)\in\mathbb{Z}$ and maps $\gamma_d(M)\colon H_2(M,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$ (into half-integers), which combine into the Donaldson polynomial:[^o4][^o5]

$$\mathcal{D}_M\colon H_2(M,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{D}_M(x) =\sum_{d=0}^\infty\frac{\gamma_d(M)(x)}{d!}.$$

Peter Kronheimer and Tomasz Mrowka introduced a condition known as Kronheimer–Mrowka simple type (KM simple type), which is sufficient to obtain the separate Donaldson invariants from their common Donaldson polynomial. For a KM-simple manifold $M$ there are cohomology classes $K_1,\ldots,K_s\in H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})$, called Kronheimer–Mrowka basic classes (KM basic classes), as well as rational numbers $a_1,\ldots,a_s\in\mathbb{Q}$, called Kronheimer–Mrowka coefficients (KM coefficients), so that:

$$\mathcal{D}_M(x) =\exp(Q_M(x,x)/2)\sum_{r=1}^sa_r\exp(K_r(x))$$

for all $x\in H_2(M,\mathbb{Z})$. Furthermore $w_2(M)=K_r\operatorname{mod}2\in H^2(M,\mathbb{Z}_2)$ for all Kronheimer–Mrowka basic classes.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

Although this reduction of the infinite sum of the Donaldson polynomial to a finite sum in early 1994 brought a significant simplification to Donaldson theory, it was overhauled just a few months later in late 1994 by the development of [Seiberg–Witten theory](wikipedia://en/3526032). [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894), presented in a lecture at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), used a purely physical argument to conjecture that Kronheimer–Mrowka basic classes are exactly the [support](wikipedia://en/381013) of the [Seiberg–Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_invariant) $\operatorname{SW}\colon \operatorname{Spin}^\mathrm{c}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ (hence the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) $c_1\colon \operatorname{Spin}^\mathrm{c}(M)\rightarrow H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})$ of [spin<sup>c</sup> structures](wikipedia://en/79367906) with a non-vanishing Seiberg–Witten invariant) and their Kronheimer–Mrowka coefficients are up to a topological factor exactly their Seiberg–Witten invariants. More concretely, it claims that a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) orientable smooth 4-manifold $M$ with $b_2^+(M)\geq 2$ odd is of Kronheimer–Mrowka simple type if and only if is of Seiberg–Witten simple type (meaning non-vanishing Seiberg–Witten invariants only come from zero-dimensional [Seiberg–Witten moduli spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_moduli_space) by counting its points with a sign determined by their orientation). In this case the Donaldson polynomial is given by:[^o9]

$$\mathcal{D}_M(x) =\exp(Q_M(x,x)/2)\sum_{\mathfrak{s}\in\operatorname{Spin}^\mathrm{c}(M), \dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{s}}^\mathrm{SW}=0}2^{2+\frac{1}{4}(7\chi(M)+11\sigma(M))}\operatorname{SW}(M,\mathfrak{s})\exp(c_1(\mathfrak{s})(x)).$$
