# Haim Gaifman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Haim Gaifman"}

**Haim Gaifman** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): חיים גייפמן; born 1934) is a logician, probability theorist, and philosopher of language who is professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

In 1958 he received his M.Sc. at [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University). Then in 1962, he received his Ph.D. at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) under [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) on the topic of infinite [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/54476844). Since, he has held various permanent and visiting positions in mathematics, philosophy and computer science departments. While he was professor of mathematics at the Hebrew University, he taught courses in philosophy and directed the program in History and Philosophy of Science.

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Gaifman works in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and developed the iterated [ultrapower](wikipedia://en/273642) technique in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and models of [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic). Further, he has results in the foundations of probability, defining probabilities on [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) and on richer languages. He has also worked in [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439), and [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).[^o6]
