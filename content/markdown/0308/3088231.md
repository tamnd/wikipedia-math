# Ralph P. Boas Jr.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ralph P. Boas Jr."}

**Ralph Philip Boas Jr.** (August 8, 1912 – July 25, 1992) was a mathematician, teacher, and journal editor. He wrote over 200 papers, mainly in the fields of [real](wikipedia://en/26478) and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Walla Walla, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walla_Walla,_Washington), the son of an English professor at [Whitman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitman_College), but moved frequently as a child; his younger sister, [Marie Boas Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Boas_Hall), later to become a historian of science, was born in [Springfield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Massachusetts), where his father had become a high school teacher.[^o7] He was home-schooled until the age of eight, began his formal schooling in the sixth grade, and graduated from high school while still only 15.[^o7] After a gap year auditing classes at [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College) (where his father had become a professor) he entered Harvard, intending to major in chemistry and go into medicine, but ended up studying mathematics instead.[^o7] His first mathematics publication was written as an undergraduate, after he discovered an incorrect proof in another paper.[^o7] He got his [A.B. degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1933, received a Sheldon Fellowship for a year of travel, and returned to Harvard for his doctoral studies in 1934.[^o7] He earned his doctorate there in 1937, under the supervision of [David Widder](wikipedia://en/14428679).[^o5][^o7]

After postdoctoral studies at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847), and then the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in England, he began a two-year instructorship at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), where he met his future wife, [Mary Layne](wikipedia://en/27606754), also a mathematics instructor at Duke. They were married in 1941, and when the United States entered World War II later that year, Boas moved to the Navy Pre-flight School in [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina). In 1942, he interviewed for a position in the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project), at the [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory), but ended up returning to Harvard to teach in a Navy instruction program there, while his wife taught at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University).[^o7]

Beginning when he was an instructor at Duke University, Boas had become a prolific reviewer for *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)*, and at the end of the war he took a position as its full-time editor.[^o7] In the academic year 1950–1951 he was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow). In 1950 he became Professor of Mathematics at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University), without ever previously having been an assistant or associate professor; his wife became a professor of physics at nearby [DePaul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePaul_University), due to the anti-nepotism rules then in place at Northwestern.[^o5][^o7] He stayed at Northwestern until his retirement in 1980, and was chair there from 1957 to 1972.[^o5][^o7] He was president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) from 1973 to 1974, and as president launched the *Dolciani Mathematical Expositions* series of books.[^o10] He was also editor of the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* from 1976 to 1981.[^o5] He continued mathematical work after retiring, for instance as co-editor (with [George Leitmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Leitmann)) of the *[Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications](wikipedia://en/54943204)* from 1985 to 1991.[^o5]

Along with his mathematical education, Boas was educated in many languages: Latin in junior high school, French and German in high school, Greek at Mount Holyoke, Sanskrit as a Harvard undergraduate, and later self-taught Russian while at Duke University.[^o7]

Boas' son [Harold P. Boas](wikipedia://en/11754242) is also a noted mathematician.

## The hunting of big game {#The_hunting_of_big_game}

Boas, [Frank Smithies](wikipedia://en/33371416), and colleagues were behind the 1938 paper *A Contribution to the Mathematical Theory of Big Game Hunting* published in the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* under the pseudonym **H. Pétard** (referring to [Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet)'s "[hoist by his own petard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoisted_by_my_own_petard#.22Hoist_with_his_own_petard.22)"). The paper offers short [spoofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parody) of theorems and proofs from [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), in the form of applications to the [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting) of [lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion) in the [Sahara desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_desert). One "proof" [parodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parody) the [Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem](wikipedia://en/31211):

The Bolzano–Weierstrass Method. Bisect the desert by a line running N–S. The lion is either in the E portion or in the W portion; let us suppose him to be in the W portion. Bisect this portion by a line running E–W. The lion is either in the N portion or in the S portion; let us suppose him to be in the N portion. We continue this process indefinitely, constructing a sufficiently strong fence about the chosen portion at each step. The diameter of the chosen portions approaches zero, so that the lion is ultimately surrounded by a fence of arbitrarily small perimeter.

The paper became a classic of [mathematical humor](wikipedia://en/2287691) and spawned various follow-ons over the years with theories or methods from other scientific areas adapted to hunting lions.

The paper and later work is published in *Lion Hunting and Other Mathematical Pursuits : A Collection of Mathematics, Verse, and Stories by the Late Ralph P. Boas Jr.*, edited by [Gerald L. Alexanderson](wikipedia://en/43156665) and Dale H. Mugler, . Various online collections of the lion hunting methods exist too.

### Pondiczery {#Pondiczery}

**E. S. Pondiczery** was another pseudonym invented by Boas and Smithies as the fictional person behind the "H. Pétard" pseudonym,[^o7] and later used again by Boas, this time for a serious paper on [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), *Power problems in abstract spaces*, [Duke Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Mathematical_Journal), 11 (1944), 835–837. This paper and the name became part of the [Hewitt–Marczewski–Pondiczery theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewitt–Marczewski–Pondiczery_theorem).[^o13]

The name, revealed in *Lion Hunting and Other Mathematical Pursuits* cited above, came from [Pondicherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pondicherry_%28city%29) (a place in India disputed by the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people)) and a [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) twist. The initials "E.S." were a plan to write a spoof on [extra-sensory perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra-sensory_perception) (ESP).

## Other {#Other}

His best-known books are the lion-hunting book previously mentioned and the monograph *[A Primer of Real Functions](wikipedia://en/56963959)*.[^o15] The current edition of the primer has been revised and edited by his son, mathematician [Harold P. Boas](wikipedia://en/11754242).

The best-known of his 13 doctoral students is [Philip J. Davis](wikipedia://en/3753768), who is also his only advisee who did not graduate from Northwestern. Boas advised Davis, who was at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), while Boas was visiting at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University).
