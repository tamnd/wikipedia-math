# Burr–Erdős conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Burr–Erdős conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Burr–Erdős conjecture** was a problem concerning the [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number) of [sparse graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_graph). The conjecture is named after [Stefan Burr](wikipedia://en/26467605) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), and is one of many [conjectures named after Erdős](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_conjecture); it states that the Ramsey number of graphs in any sparse family of graphs should [grow linearly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_growth) in the number of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) of the graph.

:::

The conjecture was proven by Choongbum Lee. Thus it is now a theorem.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

If *G* is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph), then the [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816) of *G* is the minimum number *p* such that every subgraph of *G* contains a vertex of degree *p* or smaller. A graph with degeneracy *p* is called *p*-degenerate. Equivalently, a *p*-degenerate graph is a graph that can be reduced to the [empty graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_graph) by repeatedly removing a vertex of degree *p* or smaller.

It follows from [Ramsey's theorem](wikipedia://en/184898) that for any graph *G* there exists a least integer $r(G)$, the *Ramsey number* of *G*, such that any [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) on at least $r(G)$ [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) whose [edges](wikipedia://en/12401) are coloured red or blue contains a monochromatic copy of *G*. For instance, the Ramsey number of a triangle is 6: no matter how the edges of a complete graph on six vertices are colored red or blue, there is always either a red triangle or a blue triangle.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="The conjecture"}

## The conjecture {#The_conjecture}

In 1973, [Stefan Burr](wikipedia://en/26467605) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) made the following conjecture:

For every integer *p* there exists a constant *c<sub>p</sub>* so that any *p*-degenerate graph *G* on *n* vertices has Ramsey number at most *c<sub>p</sub> n*.

That is, if an *n*-vertex graph *G* is *p*-degenerate, then a monochromatic copy of *G* should exist in every two-edge-colored complete graph on *c<sub>p</sub> n* vertices.

:::

## Known results {#Known_results}

Before the full conjecture was proved, it was first settled in some special cases. It was proven for bounded-degree graphs by (Chvátal Rödl Szemerédi Trotter 1983); their proof led to a very high value of *c*<sub>*p*</sub>, and improvements to this constant were made by (Eaton 1998) and (Graham Rödl Rucínski 2000). More generally, the conjecture was known to be true for *p*-arrangeable graphs, which includes graphs with bounded maximum degree, [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) and graphs that do not contain a [subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivision_%28graph_theory%29) of *K*<sub>*p*</sub>.[^o11] It was also known for subdivided graphs, graphs in which no two adjacent vertices have degree greater than two.[^o12]

For arbitrary graphs, the Ramsey number was known to be bounded by a function that grows only slightly superlinearly. Specifically, (Fox Sudakov 2009) showed that there exists a constant *c<sub>p</sub>* such that, for any *p*-degenerate *n*-vertex graph *G*,

$$r(G) \leq 2^{c_p \sqrt{\log n}} n.$$
