# Fujita conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fujita conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Fujita's conjecture** is a problem in the theories of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742). It is named after Takao Fujita, who formulated it in 1985.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

In complex geometry, the conjecture states that for a [positive](wikipedia://en/10592733#Positive_line_bundles) [holomorphic line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle) $L$ on a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) complex manifold $M$, the line bundle $K_M \otimes L^{\otimes m}$ (where $K_M$ is a [canonical line bundle](wikipedia://en/734893) of $M$) is

- [spanned by sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanned_by_sections) when $m\geq n+1$;
- [very ample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_ample) when $m\geq n+2$,

where $n$ is the [complex dimension](wikipedia://en/2783513) of $M$.

Note that for large $m$ the line bundle $K_M \otimes L^{\otimes m}$ is very ample by the standard [Serre's vanishing theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_vanishing_theorem) (and its complex analytic variant). The Fujita conjecture provides an explicit bound on $m$, which is optimal for [projective spaces](wikipedia://en/242135).

:::

## Known cases {#Known_cases}

For surfaces the Fujita conjecture follows from [Reider's theorem](wikipedia://en/23553917). For three-dimensional algebraic varieties, Ein and Lazarsfeld in 1993 proved the first part of the Fujita conjecture, i.e. that $m\geq 4$ implies global generation.
