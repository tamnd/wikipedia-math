# Pasquale del Pezzo

*Image: PasqualeDelPezzo.jpg.* Pasquale Del Pezzo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pasquale del Pezzo"}

**Pasquale del Pezzo**, Duke of [Caianello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caianello) and Marquis of Campodisola (2 May 1859 – 20 June 1936), was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o2]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Del Pezzo was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) (where his father was a representative of the [Neapolitan king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples)) on 2 May 1859. At the [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples), he received first a law degree in 1880 and then in 1882 a mathematics degree.

## Career {#Career}

He became a pre-eminent professor at that university, teaching [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), and remained at that University, as rector, faculty president, etc. Del Pezzo was [mayor of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Naples) from 1914 to 1917. Starting in 1919 he became a [Senator of the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator_of_the_Kingdom_of_Italy) until his death.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

His wife was the [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) writer [Anne Charlotte Leffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Charlotte_Leffler), sister of the great mathematician [Gösta Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/1438792) (1846–1927).

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

He died in [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples) on 20 June 1936. Del Pezzo is remembered particularly for first describing what became known as a [del Pezzo surface](wikipedia://en/732323).
