# Ryan Tibshirani

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ryan Tibshirani"}

**Ryan Joseph Tibshirani** (born December 15, 1985) is a professor and chair of the Department of Statistics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o3] His work spans high-dimensional statistics, nonparametric estimation, distribution-free inference, convex optimization, and epidemic tracking and forecasting.[^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Tibshirani was born on December 15, 1985 in Toronto, Canada. He earned a B.S. in Mathematics from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 2007 and a Ph.D. in Statistics from Stanford in 2011; his dissertation, *The Solution Path of the Generalized Lasso*, was advised by Jonathan Taylor.[^o8][^o9]

## Career {#Career}

From 2011 to 2022, Tibshirani was a faculty member in the Department of Statistics and Department of Machine Learning at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) (CMU).[^o11] He joined UC Berkeley in 2022 and became department chair effective July 1, 2025.[^o3]

Tibshirani is a principal investigator with the Delphi Research Group, which develops epidemic tracking and forecasting systems in collaboration with the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention) (CDC), as well as other partners.[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15]

In 2023, he became Editor-in-Chief of *Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning*, taking over from founding Editor [Michael I. Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_I._Jordan).[^o16] In 2024, he became founding co-Editor-in-Chief of *Foundations and Trends in Statistics* with [Rina Foygel Barber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rina_Foygel_Barber).[^o17]

## Research {#Research}

Tibshirani’s research focuses on methodology and theory for high-dimensional and nonparametric problems, often connecting statistical inference with convex optimization.[^o4][^o5] He has made important contributions to regularization and sparsity methods, including the lasso, generalized lasso, and trend filtering, developing both theoretical guarantees and efficient algorithms.[^o19][^o20] He has also contributed to selective inference, to distribution-free predictive inference (conformal prediction), and to epidemic modeling and forecasting.[^o21][^o22]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- [COPSS Presidents' Award](wikipedia://en/11690483) (2023) [^o24][^o4]
- [Mortimer Spiegelman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortimer_Spiegelman_Award) (2022).[^o25][^o26]
- Fellow, [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) (2022).[^o27][^o28]
- AAPOR Policy Impact Award (2022) and Warren J. Mitofsky Innovators Award (2022), as part of the COVID-19 Trends and Impact Survey Team (Delphi/UMD/Meta) team.[^o29][^o30]
- ASA Statistical Partnerships Among Academe, Industry, and Government (SPAIG) Award (2021), with the Delphi COVIDcast Team.[^o31][^o32]
- Carnegie Mellon University Teaching Innovation Award (2017).[^o33]
- NSF CAREER Award (2016).[^o34]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Ryan Tibshirani is the son of statistician [Robert Tibshirani](wikipedia://en/31404681), with older brother Charlie Tibshirani, and younger sister Julie Tibshirani who is a co-creator generalized random forests.[^o36] He is married to Jessica Tibshirani (née Issler) and they have two children.[^o37]
