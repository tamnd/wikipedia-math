# Joseph L. Ullman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph L. Ullman"}

**Joseph Leonard Ullman** (30 January 1923, in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York) – 11 September 1995, in [Chelsea, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea,_Michigan)) was a mathematician who worked on [classical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) with a focus on [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713).[^o2]

:::

Ullman received his A.B. from the [University of Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buffalo) and his graduate studies were interrupted by service in the U.S. Army in World War II. He was injured, received a [Purple Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Heart), and spent the rest of the war as a mathematics instructor. He received a Ph.D. in 1949 from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) with thesis *Studies on Faber Polynomials* under the direction of [Gábor Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065). Ullman became an instructor at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1949, an assistant professor in 1954, an associate professor in 1962, and a professor in 1966.

He wrote forty-three research papers. During his career at the University of Michigan he supervised eleven doctoral theses.[^o3][^o4]
