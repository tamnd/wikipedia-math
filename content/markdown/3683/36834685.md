# Louis de Beausobre

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Louis de Beausobre"}

*Image: Ludwig von Beausobre 1779 von Daniel Nikolaus Chodowiecki (Inventor, Zeichner), Daniel Berger (Stecher).png.* Louis de Beausobre, 1779, by Daniel Nikolaus Chodowiecki

**Louis Isaac de Beausobre** (19 August 1730 – 3 December 1783) was a German philosopher and political economist of French [Huguenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) descent. He was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), the son of the French Protestant churchman and ecclesiastical historian [Isaac de Beausobre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_de_Beausobre) and his second wife, Charlotte Schwarz. He is not to be confused with his elder half-brother, the pastor and theologian Charles Louis de Beausobre (1690–1753).

:::

Beausobre was educated at the [Collège Français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Französisches_Gymnasium_Berlin) in Berlin, where he was taught and greatly influenced by [Johann Heinrich Samuel Formey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Heinrich_Samuel_Formey). He went on to study philosophy at [Frankfurt an der Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_%28Oder%29), and later in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). On his return to Berlin he was received as a member of the [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1755.

[Frederick the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_the_Great), out of esteem for Isaac de Beausobre, became the guardian of his son Louis de Beausobre, and supported him in his studies.
