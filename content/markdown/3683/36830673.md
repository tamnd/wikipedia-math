# Hurwitz problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hurwitz problem"}

In mathematics, the **Hurwitz problem** (named after [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690)) is the problem of finding multiplicative relations between [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) which generalise those known to exist between sums of squares in certain numbers of variables.

:::

## Description {#Description}

There are well-known multiplicative relationships between sums of squares in two variables

$$(x^2+y^2)(u^2+v^2) = (xu-yv)^2 + (xv+yu)^2 \ ,$$

(known as the [Brahmagupta–Fibonacci identity](wikipedia://en/326483)), and also [Euler's four-square identity](wikipedia://en/222004) and [Degen's eight-square identity](wikipedia://en/4982245). These may be interpreted as multiplicativity for the norms on the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) ($\mathbb{C}$), [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) ($\mathbb{H}$), and [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) ($\mathbb{O}$), respectively.[^o3][^o4]

The Hurwitz problem for the field $K$ is to find general relations of the form

$$(x_1^2+\cdots+x_r^2) \cdot (y_1^2+\cdots+y_s^2) = (z_1^2 + \cdots + z_n^2) \ ,$$

with the $z$ being bilinear forms in the $x$ and $y$: that is, each $z$ is a $K$-linear combination of terms of the form $x_{i} y_{j}$.[^o5]

We call a triple $\; (r, s, n) \;$ *admissible* for $K$ if such an identity exists.[^o3] Trivial cases of admissible triples include $\; (r, s, rs) \;.$ The problem is uninteresting for $K$ of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 2, since over such fields every sum of squares is a square, and we exclude this case. It is believed that otherwise admissibility is independent of the field of definition.[^o3]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Hurwitz–Radon theorem"}

## The Hurwitz–Radon theorem {#The_Hurwitz–Radon_theorem}

Hurwitz posed the problem in 1898 in the special case $\; r = s = n \;$ and showed that, when coefficients are taken in $\mathbb{C}$, the only admissible values $\, (n, n, n) \,$ were $\; n \in \{ 1, 2, 4, 8 \} \;.$[^o5] His proof extends to a field of any [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *except* 2.[^o3]

The "Hurwitz–Radon" problem is that of finding admissible triples of the form $\, (r, n, n) \;.$ Obviously $\; (1, n, n) \;$ is admissible. The **Hurwitz–Radon theorem** states that $\; \left( \rho(n), n, n \right) \;$ is admissible over any field where $\, \rho(n) \,$ is the function defined for $\; n = 2^u v\;,$ $v$ odd, $\; u = 4 a + b \;,$ with $\; 0 \le b \le 3 \;,$ and $\; \rho(n) = 8 a + 2^b\;.$[^o3][^o5]

Other admissible triples include $\, (3, 5, 7) \,$[^o3] and $\, (10, 10, 16) \;.$[^o3]
