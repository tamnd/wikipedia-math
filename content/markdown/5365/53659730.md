# Lifting property

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lifting property"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the **lifting property** is a property of a pair of [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_%28category_theory%29) in a [category](wikipedia://en/53759). It is used in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) within [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) to define properties of morphisms starting from an explicitly given class of morphisms. It appears in a prominent way in the theory of [model categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_categories), an axiomatic framework for [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) introduced by [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519). It is also used in the definition of a [factorization system](wikipedia://en/7149012), and of a [weak factorization system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_factorization_system), notions related to but less restrictive than the notion of a model category. Several elementary notions may also be expressed using the lifting property starting from a list of (counter)examples.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A morphism $i$ in a category has the *left lifting property* with respect to a morphism $p$, and $p$ also has the *right lifting property* with respect to $i$, sometimes denoted $i\perp p$ or $i\downarrow p$, iff the following implication holds for each morphism $f$ and $g$ in the category:

- if the outer square of the following diagram commutes, then there exists $h$ completing the diagram, i.e. for each $f:A\to X$ and $g:B\to Y$ such that $p\circ f = g \circ i$ there exists $h:B\to X$ such that $h\circ i = f$ and $p\circ h = g$.

*Image: Model\_category\_lifting.png.*

This is sometimes also known as the morphism $i$ being *orthogonal to* the morphism $p$; however, this can also refer to the stronger property that whenever $f$ and $g$ are as above, the diagonal morphism $h$ exists and is also required to be unique.

For a class $C$ of morphisms in a category, its *left orthogonal* $C^{\perp \ell}$ or $C^\perp$ with respect to the lifting property, respectively its *right orthogonal* $C^{\perp r}$ or ${}^\perp C$, is the class of all morphisms which have the left, respectively right, lifting property with respect to each morphism in the class $C$. In notation,

$$\begin{aligned} C^{\perp\ell} &:= \{ i \mid \forall p\in C, i\perp p\} \\ C^{\perp r} &:= \{ p \mid \forall i\in C, i\perp p\} \end{aligned}$$

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Taking the orthogonal of a class $C$ is a simple way to define a class of morphisms excluding [non-isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828#Category_theoretic_view) from $C$, in a way which is useful in a [diagram chasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagram_chasing) computation.

In the category **Set** of [sets](wikipedia://en/26691), the right orthogonal $\{\emptyset \to \{*\}\}^{\perp r}$ of the simplest [non-surjection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) $\emptyset\to \{*\}$ is the class of surjections. The left and right orthogonals of $\{x_1,x_2\}\to \{*\},$ the simplest [non-injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective), are both precisely the class of injections,

$$\{\{x_1,x_2\}\to \{*\}\}^{\perp\ell} = \{\{x_1,x_2\}\to \{*\}\}^{\perp r} = \{ f \mid f \text{ is an injection } \}.$$

It is clear that $C^{\perp\ell r} \supset C$ and $C^{\perp r\ell} \supset C$. The class $C^{\perp r}$ is always closed under retracts (that is, if $X$ and $Y$ are objects, $C \perp Y$, and $X$ is a [retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retract_%28category_theory%29) of $Y$, then $C \perp X$), [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/841689), (small) [products](wikipedia://en/237868) (whenever they exist in the category) & composition of morphisms, and contains all isomorphisms (that is, invertible morphisms) of the underlying category. Meanwhile, $C^{\perp \ell}$ is closed under retracts, [pushouts](wikipedia://en/582024), (small) [coproducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coproduct_%28category_theory%29) & transfinite composition ([filtered colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_colimit)) of morphisms (whenever they exist in the category), and also contains all isomorphisms.

Let $i$, $j$, and $k$ be morphisms such that $i \circ j$ exists. Then:

- If $i \circ j \perp k$ and $j$ is an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539), then $i \perp k$.
- If $k \perp i \circ j$ and $i$ is a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538), then $k \perp j$.

These two properties are useful when the category is equipped with a [weak factorisation system](wikipedia://en/7149012) consisting of epimorphisms and monomorphisms.

## Examples {#Examples}

A number of notions can be defined by passing to the left or right orthogonal several times starting from a list of explicit examples, i.e., as $C^{\perp\ell}, C^{\perp r}, C^{\perp\ell r}, C^{\perp\ell\ell}$, etc., where $C$ is a given class of morphisms. A useful intuition is to think that the left and right lifting properties against a class $C$ are a way of expressing a *negation* of some property of the morphisms in $C$. In this vein, performing a "double negation" can be seen as a kind of "closure" or "completion" procedure.

### Elementary examples in various categories {#Elementary_examples_in_various_categories}

#### In **Set** {#In_Set}

Let $1$ denote any fixed [singleton set](wikipedia://en/381798), such as $\{0\}$, and let $2$ denote any fixed set with two elements, such as $\{0,1\}$.

- If $i : 1 \to 2$ denotes either of the two functions from $1$ to $2$, then $\{i\}^{\perp l} = \{i\}^{\perp r}$ is the class of [surjections](wikipedia://en/27873).
- If $j: 2 \to 1$ is the unique function from $2$ to $1$, then $\{j\}^{\perp r} = \{j\}^{\perp l}$ is the class of [injections](wikipedia://en/45196).

#### In the category of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a commutative ring R {#In_the_category_of_modules_over_a_commutative_ring_R}

Let $0$ denote the [zero module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_module) and for each $R$-module $M$, let $0 \to M$ and $M \to 0$ denote the two unique morphisms between $0$ and $M$.

- $\{0\to R\}^{\perp r}$ is the class of surjective module homomorphisms.
- $\{R\to 0\}^{\perp r}$ is the class of injective module homomorphisms.
- A module $M$ is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) if and only if $0\to M$ is in $\{0\to R\}^{\perp rl}$.
- A module $M$ is [injective](wikipedia://en/473775) if and only if $M\to 0$ is in $\{R\to 0\}^{\perp rr}$.

#### In the category of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) {#In_the_category_of_groups}

Let $\mathbb{Z}$ denote the infinite [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) under addition.

- $\{0\to \mathbb{Z}\}^{\perp r}$ is the class of surjective group homomorphisms.
- $\{\mathbb{Z} \to 0\}^{\perp r}$ is the class of injective group homomorphisms.
- A group $F$ is a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) if and only if $0\to F$ is in $\{0\to \mathbb{Z} \}^{\perp rl}$.
- A group $A$ is [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/3039330) if and only if $0\to A$ is in $\{ n \mathbb{Z}\to \mathbb{Z} : n>0 \}^{\perp r}$.
- A [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $A$ of a group $B$ is [pure](wikipedia://en/5739585) if and only if $A \to B$ is in $\{ n\mathbb{Z}\to \mathbb{Z} : n>0 \}^{\perp r}$.

For a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) $G$,

- $\{0\to {\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}\} \perp 1\to G$ iff the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of $G$ is prime to $p$ iff $\{{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}} \to 0\} \perp G\to 1$.
- $G\to 1 \in (0\to {\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{\perp rr}$ iff $G$ is a [$p$-group](wikipedia://en/24664).
- $H$ is nilpotent iff the diagonal map $H\to H\times H$ is in $(1\to *)^{\perp\ell r}$ where $(1\to *)$ denotes the class of maps $\{ 1\to G : G \text{ arbitrary}\}$.
- a finite group $H$ is [soluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble_group) iff $1\to H$ is in $$\{0\to A : A\text{ abelian}\}^{\perp\ell r}=\{[G,G]\to G : G\text{ arbitrary } \}^{\perp\ell r}.$$

#### In the category of topological spaces {#In_the_category_of_topological_spaces}

Let $\{0,1\}$ and $\{0\leftrightarrow 1\}$ denote a two-element set with the [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) topology and the [indiscrete](wikipedia://en/382447) topology, respectively. Let $\{0\to 1\}$ denote the [Sierpinski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_space) of two points, in which the set $\{0\}$ is open (and not closed) and the set $\{1\}$ is closed (and not open), and let $\{0\}\to \{0\to 1\}, \{1\} \to \{0\to 1\}$, etc. denote the obvious embeddings.

- A space $X$ is a [T<sub>0</sub> space](wikipedia://en/68121) if and only if $X\to \{*\}$ is in $(\{0\leftrightarrow 1\} \to \{*\})^{\perp r}$.
- A space $X$ is a [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721) if and only if $\emptyset\to X$ is in $( \{0\to 1\}\to \{*\})^{\perp r}$.
- $(\{1\}\to \{0\to 1\})^{\perp l}$ is the class of maps with [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) [image](wikipedia://en/579311).
- $(\{0\to 1\}\to \{*\})^{\perp\ell}$ is the class of maps $f:X\to Y$ such that the [topology](wikipedia://en/30450#Definitions) on $A$ is the pullback of topology on $B$, i.e. the topology on $A$ is the topology with least number of open sets such that the map is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122#Continuous_functions_between_topological_spaces),
- $(\emptyset\to \{*\})^{\perp r}$ is the class of surjective maps,
- $(\emptyset\to \{*\})^{\perp r\ell}$ is the class of maps of form $A\to A\cup D$ where $D$ is discrete,
- $(\emptyset\to \{*\})^{\perp r\ell\ell} = (\{a\}\to \{a,b\})^{\perp\ell}$ is the class of maps $A\to B$ such that each [connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29) of $B$ intersects $\operatorname{Im} A$,
- $(\{0,1\}\to \{*\})^{\perp r}$ is the class of injective maps,
- $(\{0,1\}\to \{*\})^{\perp\ell}$ is the class of maps $f:X\to Y$ such that the [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) of a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) closed [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358) of $Y$ is a connected closed open [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of $X$, e.g. $X$ is connected iff $X\to \{*\}$ is in $(\{0,1\} \to \{*\})^{\perp\ell}$,
- for a connected space $X$, each continuous function on $X$ is bounded iff $\emptyset\to X \perp \cup_n (-n,n) \to \mathbb{R}$ where $\cup_n (-n,n) \to \mathbb{R}$ is the map from the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of open intervals $(-n,n)$ into the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) $\mathbb{R},$
- a space $X$ is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) iff for any injective map $\{a,b\}\hookrightarrow X$, it holds $\{a,b\}\hookrightarrow X \perp \{a\to x \leftarrow b \}\to\{*\}$ where $\{a\leftarrow x\to b \}$ denotes the three-point space with two [open points](wikipedia://en/390557) $a$ and $b$, and a [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408) $x$,
- a space $X$ is [perfectly normal](wikipedia://en/48629) iff $\emptyset\to X \perp [0,1] \to \{0\leftarrow x\to 1\}$ where the open interval $(0,1)$ goes to $x$, and $0$ maps to the point $0$, and $1$ maps to the point $1$, and $\{0\leftarrow x\to 1\}$ denotes the three-point space with two closed points $0, 1$ and one open point $x$.

#### In the category of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) with [uniformly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_continuous) maps {#In_the_category_of_metric_spaces_with_uniformly_continuous_maps}

- A space $X$ is [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) iff $\{1/n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \to \{0\}\cup \{1/n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \perp X\to \{0\}$ where $\{1/n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \to \{0\}\cup \{1/n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is the obvious inclusion between the two subspaces of the real line with [induced metric](wikipedia://en/1253303), and $\{0\}$ is the metric space consisting of a single point,
- A subspace $i:A\to X$ is closed iff $\{1/n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \to \{0\}\cup \{1/n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \perp A\to X.$

### Examples of lifting properties in algebraic topology {#Examples_of_lifting_properties_in_algebraic_topology}

A map $f:U\to B$ has the *path lifting property* iff $\{0\}\to [0,1] \perp f$ where $\{0\} \to [0,1]$ is the inclusion of one end point of the closed interval into the interval $[0,1]$.

A map $f:U\to B$ has the [homotopy lifting property](wikipedia://en/522786) iff $X \to X\times [0,1] \perp f$ where $X\to X\times [0,1]$ is the map $x \mapsto (x,0)$.

### Examples of lifting properties coming from model categories {#Examples_of_lifting_properties_coming_from_model_categories}

Fibrations and cofibrations.

- Let **Top** be the category of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), and let $C_0$ be the class of maps $S^n\to D^{n+1}$, [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) of the boundary $S^n=\partial D^{n+1}$ of a ball into the ball $D^{n+1}$. Let $WC_0$ be the class of maps embedding the upper semi-sphere into the disk. $WC_0^{\perp\ell}, WC_0^{\perp\ell r}, C_0^{\perp\ell}, C_0^{\perp\ell r}$ are the classes of fibrations, acyclic cofibrations, acyclic fibrations, and cofibrations.[^o16]
- Let **sSet** be the category of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784). Let $C_0$ be the class of boundary inclusions $\partial \Delta[n] \to \Delta[n]$, and let $WC_0$ be the class of horn inclusions $\Lambda^i[n] \to \Delta[n]$. Then the classes of fibrations, acyclic cofibrations, acyclic fibrations, and cofibrations are, respectively, $WC_0^{\perp\ell}, WC_0^{\perp\ell r}, C_0^{\perp\ell}, C_0^{\perp\ell r}$.[^o17]
- Let $\mathbf{Ch}(R)$ be the category of [chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$. Let $C_0$ be the class of maps of form

$$\cdots\to 0\to R \to 0 \to 0 \to \cdots \to \cdots \to R \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}} R \to 0 \to 0 \to \cdots,$$

and $WC_0$ be

$$\cdots \to 0\to 0 \to 0 \to 0 \to \cdots \to \cdots \to R \xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}} R \to 0 \to 0 \to \cdots.$$

Then $WC_0^{\perp\ell}, WC_0^{\perp\ell r}, C_0^{\perp\ell}, C_0^{\perp\ell r}$ are the classes of fibrations, acyclic cofibrations, acyclic fibrations, and cofibrations.[^o18]
