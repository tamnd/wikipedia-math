# Permutation representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Permutation representation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the term **permutation representation** of a (typically finite) [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ can refer to either of two closely related notions: a [representation](wikipedia://en/6355948) of $G$ as a group of [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027), or as a group of [permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_matrices). The term also refers to the combination of the two.

:::

## Abstract permutation representation {#Abstract_permutation_representation}

A **permutation representation** of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $X$ is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) from $G$ to the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of $X$:

$$\rho\colon G \to \operatorname{Sym}(X).$$

The image $\rho(G)\subset \operatorname{Sym}(X)$ is a [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634) and the elements of $G$ are represented as permutations of $X$.[^o3] A permutation representation is equivalent to an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of $G$ on the set $X$:

$$G\times X \to X.$$

See the article on [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) for further details.

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Linear permutation representation"}

## Linear permutation representation {#Linear_permutation_representation}

If $G$ is a [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634) of degree $n$, then the **permutation representation** of $G$ is the [linear representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of $G$

$$\rho\colon G\to \operatorname{GL}_n(K)$$

which maps $g\in G$ to the corresponding [permutation matrix](wikipedia://en/238849) (here $K$ is an arbitrary [field](wikipedia://en/10603)).[^o6] That is, $G$ acts on $K^n$ by permuting the standard basis vectors.

This notion of a permutation representation can, of course, be composed with the previous one to represent an arbitrary abstract group $G$ as a group of permutation matrices. One first represents $G$ as a permutation group and then maps each permutation to the corresponding matrix. Representing $G$ as a permutation group acting on itself by [translation](wikipedia://en/19447#translation), one obtains the [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011).

:::

## Character of the permutation representation {#Character_of_the_permutation_representation}

Given a group $G$ and a finite set $X$ with $G$ acting on the set $X$ then the [character](wikipedia://en/293418) $\chi$ of the permutation representation is exactly the number of fixed points of $X$ under the action of $\rho(g)$ on $X$. That is $\chi(g)=$ the number of points of $X$ fixed by $\rho(g)$.

This follows since, if we represent the map $\rho(g)$ with a matrix with basis defined by the elements of $X$ we get a permutation matrix of $X$. Now the character of this representation is defined as the trace of this permutation matrix. An element on the diagonal of a permutation matrix is 1 if the point in $X$ is fixed, and 0 otherwise. So we can conclude that the trace of the permutation matrix is exactly equal to the number of fixed points of $X$.

For example, if $G=S_3$ and $X=\{1, 2, 3\}$ the character of the permutation representation can be computed with the formula $\chi(g)=$ the number of points of $X$ fixed by $g$. So

$\chi((12))=\operatorname{tr}(\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0\\ 1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix})=1$ as only 3 is fixed

$\chi((123))=\operatorname{tr}(\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\\ 1 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix})=0$ as no elements of $X$ are fixed, and

$\chi(1)=\operatorname{tr}(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 1 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix})=3$ as every element of $X$ is fixed.
