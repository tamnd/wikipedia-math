# Grothendieck's Tôhoku paper

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grothendieck's Tôhoku paper"}

The article "**Sur quelques points d'algèbre homologique**" by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042),[^n1] now often referred to as the **Tôhoku paper**,[^n2] was published in 1957 in the *[Tôhoku Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tôhoku_Mathematical_Journal)*. It revolutionized the subject of [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), a purely algebraic aspect of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).[^n3] It removed the need to distinguish the cases of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) and [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) over a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450).[^n4]

:::

## Background {#Background}

Material in the paper dates from Grothendieck's year at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) in 1955–56.[^n5] Research there allowed him to put homological algebra on an axiomatic basis, by introducing the [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) concept.[^n6]

That same year, a textbook treatment of homological algebra, *Homological Algebra* by [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) and [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501), appeared. Grothendieck's work was largely independent of it; however, his abelian category concept had at least partially been anticipated by others.[^n7] [David Buchsbaum](wikipedia://en/30781131) in his doctoral thesis written under Eilenberg had introduced a notion of "[exact category](wikipedia://en/45063#History)" close to the abelian category concept (needing only [direct sums](wikipedia://en/2084687) to be identical); and had formulated the idea of "[enough injectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_injectives)".[^n8] The Tôhoku paper contains an argument to prove that a [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538) (a particular type of abelian category, the name coming later) has enough injectives; the author indicated that the proof was of a standard type.[^n9] In showing by this means that categories of sheaves of abelian groups admitted [injective resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_resolution), Grothendieck went beyond the theory available in Cartan and Eilenberg's book to prove the existence of a [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) in generality.[^n10]

## Later developments {#Later_developments}

After the [Gabriel–Popescu theorem](wikipedia://en/35884241) of 1964, it was known that every Grothendieck category is a [quotient category](wikipedia://en/48897477) of a [module category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_category).[^n11]

The Tôhoku paper also introduced the [Grothendieck spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3017363) associated to the composition of [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875).[^n12] In further reconsideration of the foundations of homological algebra, Grothendieck introduced and developed with [Jean-Louis Verdier](wikipedia://en/9168893) the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) concept.[^n13] The initial motivation, as announced by Grothendieck at the 1958 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), was to formulate results on [coherent duality](wikipedia://en/1815224), now going under the name "Grothendieck duality".[^n14]
