# Minlos–Sazonov theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minlos–Sazonov theorem"}

The **Minlos–Sasonov theorem** is a result from [measure theory in topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/79849108). It provides a sufficient condition for a [cylindrical measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_measure) to be [σ-additive](wikipedia://en/1073567) on a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space). This is the case when its [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) at [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero) in the *Sazonov topology* and such a topology is called *sufficient*. The theorem is named after the two [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematicians [Robert Adol'fovich Minlos](wikipedia://en/29114990) and [Vyacheslav Vasilievich Sazonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Vasilievich_Sazonov).

:::

The theorem generalizes two classical theorem: the [Minlos theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minlos_theorem) (1963) and the [Sazonov theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sazonov_theorem) (1958). It was then later generalized in the 1970s by the mathematicians [Albert Badrikian](wikipedia://en/79861357) and [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485) to locally convex spaces. Therefore, the theorem is sometimes also called **Minlos-Sasonov-Badrikian theorem**.[^o2][^o3]

## Minlos–Sasonov theorem {#Minlos–Sasonov_theorem}

Let $(X,\tau)$ be a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space), $X^*$ and $X'$ are the corresponding algebraic and topological [dual spaces](wikipedia://en/7988), and $\langle,\rangle:X\times X'\to \mathbb{R}$ is the [dual pair](wikipedia://en/63735167). A topology $\tau^K$ on $X$ is called *compatible* with the dual pair $\langle,\rangle$ if the corresponding topological dual space induced by $\tau^K$ is again $X'$. A [seminorm](wikipedia://en/292067) $p$ on $X$ is called *Hilbertian* or a *Hilbert seminorm* if there exists a positive definite [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) $b\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}$ such that $p(x)=\sqrt{b(x,x)}$ for all $x\in X$.

Let $\mathfrak{A}:=\mathfrak{A}(X,X'):=\bigotimes\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{A}_{f_1,\dots,f_n}$ denote the [cylindrical algebra](wikipedia://en/21236657).[^o6]

### Deriving the Sazonov topology {#Deriving_the_Sazonov_topology}

Let $p$ be a seminorm on $X$ and $X_{p}$ be the [factor space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_space) $X_{p}:=X/p^{-1}(0)$ with canonical mapping $Q_{p}:X\to X_{p}$ defined as $Q_{p}:x\mapsto [x]$. Let $\overline{p}$ be the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534)

$$\overline{p}(y)=p\left(Q^{-1}_{p}(y)\right)$$

on $X_{p}$, denote the corresponding [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) as $\overline{X}_{p}$ and let $i_{p}:X_p\hookrightarrow \overline{X}_p$ be the natural embedding, then define the continuous map

$$\overline{Q}_p(x):=i_p\left(Q_{p}(x)\right)$$

which is a map $\overline{Q}_p:X\to \overline{X}_p$. Let $q$ be a seminorm such that for all $x\in X$

$$p(x)\leq C q(x),$$

then define a continuous linear operator $T_{q,p}: \overline{X}_{q}\to \overline{X}_{p}$ as follows:

- If $z\in i_q(X_q)\subseteq \overline{X}_q$ then $T_{q,p}(z):=\overline{Q}_p\left(\overline{Q}^{-1}_q(z)\right)$, which is well-defined.
- If $z\not\in i_q(X_q)$ and $z\in \overline{X}_q$, then there exists a sequence $(z_n)_n\in i_q(X_q)$ which converges against $z$ and the sequence $\left(T_{q,p}(z_n)\right)_n$ converges in $\overline{X}_p$ therefore $T_{q,p}(z):=\lim\limits_{n\to \infty}\left(T_{q,p}(z_n)\right)_n.$[^o8]

If $p$ it *Hilbertian* then $\overline{X}_p$ is a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

### Sazonov topology {#Sazonov_topology}

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be a family of continuous Hilbert seminorms defined as follows: $p\in \mathcal{P}$ if and only if there exists a Hilbert seminorm $q$ such that for all $x\in X$

$$p(x)\leq C q(x)$$

for some constant $C\in \mathbb{R}$ and if $T_{q,p}$ is a [Hilbert-Schmidt operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert-Schmidt_operator). Then the topology $\tau^S:=\tau^S(X,\tau)$ induced by the family $\mathcal{P}$ is called the **Sazonov topology** or **S-topology**.[^o8] Clearly it depends on the underlying topology $\tau$. If $(X,\tau)$ is a [nuclear](wikipedia://en/2702957) then $\tau^S=\tau$.

::: {#o11 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

### Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $\mu$ be a cylindrical measure on $\mathfrak{A}$ and $\tau$ a locally convex topology that is compatible with the dual pair and let $\tau^S:=\tau^S(X,\tau)$ be the Sazonov topology. Then $\mu$ is σ-additive on $\mathfrak{A}$ if the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) $\hat{\mu}(f):X'\to \mathbb{C}$ is continuous in zero in $\tau^S$.[^o8]
