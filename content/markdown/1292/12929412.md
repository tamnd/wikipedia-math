# Dixmier conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dixmier conjecture"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **Dixmier conjecture**, stated by [Jacques Dixmier](wikipedia://en/9169420) in 1968,[^o2] originally asked whether any [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) of the first [Weyl algebra](wikipedia://en/491095) $A_1$ over a field of characteristic zero is an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160). The analogous statement for the $n$-th Weyl algebra $A_n$ was recorded in 1982 by [Bass](wikipedia://en/7085910), Connell, and Wright, who attributed it to communications from [Leonid Vaserstein](wikipedia://en/16636593) and [Victor Kac](wikipedia://en/4697697),[^o3] and was later referred to as the "generalized Dixmier conjecture".[^o4][^o5]

:::

[Tsuchimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshifumi_Tsuchimoto) in 2005,[^o5] and independently [Belov-Kanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belov-Kanel) and [Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509) in 2007,[^o6] showed that the Dixmier conjecture is stably equivalent to the Jacobian conjecture: the Dixmier conjecture for the *n*-th Weyl algebra $A_n$ implies the Jacobian conjecture for polynomial maps in *n* variables, while conversely the Jacobian conjecture in $2n$ variables implies the Dixmier conjecture for $A_n$.[^o6] In July 2026, a counterexample to the [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823) in three variables was found,[^o7] which by the first of these implications shows that the Dixmier conjecture is false for $A_n$ for all $n \geq 3$.

The conjecture remains open for the first and second Weyl algebras, since the Jacobian conjecture is still open in two variables. The case of the first Weyl algebra $A_1$ was the problem originally posed by Dixmier; a proposed proof of this case was announced by Alexander Zheglov in 2024.[^o8]
