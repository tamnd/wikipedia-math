# Chain (algebraic topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chain"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a $k$-**chain** is a [formal linear combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_linear_combination) of the [$k$-cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-cell_%28mathematics%29) in a [cell complex](wikipedia://en/353022). In [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686) (respectively, [cubical complexes](wikipedia://en/54829672)), $k$-chains are combinations of $k$-simplices (respectively, $k$-cubes),[^o2][^o3][^o4] but not necessarily connected. Chains are used in [homology](wikipedia://en/142432); the elements of a homology group are equivalence classes of chains.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) $X$, the group $C_n(X)$ of $n$-chains of $X$ is given by:

$$C_n(X) = \left\{ \sum\limits_i m_i \sigma_i | m_i \in \mathbb{Z} \right\}$$

where $\sigma_i$ are [singular $n$-simplices](wikipedia://en/431041) of $X$. Note that an element in $C_n(X)$ is not necessarily a connected simplicial complex.

:::

## Integration on chains {#Integration_on_chains}

Integration is defined on chains by taking the linear combination of integrals over the simplices in the chain with coefficients (which are typically integers). The set of all *k*-chains forms a group and the sequence of these groups is called a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404).

## Boundary operator on chains {#Boundary_operator_on_chains}

*Image: Chainline.svg.* The boundary of a [polygonal curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonal_curve) is a linear combination of its nodes; in this case, some linear combination of A<sub>1</sub> through A<sub>6</sub>. Assuming the segments all are oriented left-to-right (in increasing order from A<sub>*k*</sub> to A<sub>*k*+1</sub>), the boundary is A<sub>6</sub> − A<sub>1</sub>.

*Image: Closed polygonal line.svg.* A closed polygonal curve, assuming consistent orientation, has null boundary.

The boundary of a chain is the linear combination of boundaries of the simplices in the chain. The boundary of a *k*-chain is a (*k*−1)-chain. Note that the boundary of a simplex is not a simplex, but a chain with coefficients 1 or −1 – thus chains are the closure of simplices under the boundary operator.

::: {#o9 .example cue=inline-head name="Example 1"}

**Example 1:** The boundary of a [path](wikipedia://en/1189553) is the formal difference of its endpoints: it is a [telescoping sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescoping_sum). To illustrate, if the 1-chain $c = t_1 + t_2 + t_3\,$ is a path from point $v_1\,$ to point $v_4\,$, where $t_1=[v_1, v_2]\,$, $t_2=[v_2, v_3]\,$ and $t_3=[v_3, v_4]\,$ are its constituent 1-simplices, then

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_1 c &= \partial_1(t_1 + t_2 + t_3)\\ &= \partial_1(t_1) + \partial_1(t_2) + \partial_1(t_3)\\ &= \partial_1([v_1, v_2]) + \partial_1([v_2, v_3]) + \partial_1([v_3, v_4]) \\ &= ([v_2]-[v_1]) + ([v_3]-[v_2]) + ([v_4]-[v_3]) \\ &= [v_4]-[v_1]. \end{aligned}$$

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=inline-head name="Example 2"}

**Example 2:** The boundary of the triangle is a formal sum of its edges with signs arranged to make the traversal of the boundary counterclockwise.

A chain is called a **cycle** when its boundary is zero. A chain that is the boundary of another chain is called a **boundary**. Boundaries are cycles, so chains form a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404), whose homology groups (cycles modulo boundaries) are called simplicial [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) groups.

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=inline-head name="Example 3"}

**Example 3:** The plane punctured at the origin has nontrivial 1-homology group since the unit circle is a cycle, but not a boundary.

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the duality between the boundary operator on chains and the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) is expressed by the general [Stokes' theorem](wikipedia://en/25441497).

:::
