# Ad nauseam

la

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ad nauseam"}

***Ad nauseam*** is a Latin term used to describe an argument or a discussion that has been extended to the figurative point of [nausea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nausea).[^o3][^o4] For example, "this has been discussed *ad nauseam*" indicates that the topic has been discussed extensively and those involved have grown sick of it.

:::

The fallacy of dragging the conversation to an *ad nauseam* state in order to then assert one's position as correct due to it not having been contradicted is also called ***argumentum ad infinitum*** ([to infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_infinitum)) and **argument from repetition**.[^o5]

The term is defined by the *[American Heritage Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Heritage_Dictionary)* as "to a disgusting or ridiculous degree; to the point of nausea".[^o5] Colloquially, it is sometimes used as "until nobody cares to discuss it any more".[^o6]

## Origin {#Origin}

*ad nauseam* originates as a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) expression, translating to "to nausea". It combines the preposition *ad* ("to") with *nauseam* the [accusative case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accusative_case) of *nausea* ("nausea" or "seasickness").[^o8] This term *nausea* traces back to [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) *nausía*, denoting "seasickness" and stemming from *naûs* ("ship").[^o9] In [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), the phrase appeared during the mid 16th century. The earliest documented instance occurs in a 1565 translation by [John Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hall_%28physician%29), where it describes continuation to the point of inducing disgust.[^o10] By 1644, usage had become more established, often meaning excessive repetition that leads to weariness.[^o11] During the 17th century, the expression got higher usage in [English literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_literature) and discourse, particularly for critiquing repetitive debates or ideas.[^o12]
