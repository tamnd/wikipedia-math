# Cotangent bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cotangent bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **cotangent bundle** of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) is the [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) of all the [cotangent spaces](wikipedia://en/6620) at every point in the manifold. It may be described also as the [dual bundle](wikipedia://en/7824691) to the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794). This may be generalized to [categories](wikipedia://en/53759) with more structure than smooth manifolds, such as [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742), or (in the form of cotangent sheaf) [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) or [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754). In the smooth case, any Riemannian metric or symplectic form gives an isomorphism between the cotangent bundle and the tangent bundle, but they are not in general isomorphic in other categories.

:::

## Formal definition via [diagonal morphism](wikipedia://en/4606682) {#Formal_definition_via_diagonal_morphism}

There are several equivalent ways to define the cotangent bundle. [One way](wikipedia://en/44901757#Construction_through_a_diagonal_morphism) is through a [diagonal mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_mapping) $\Delta$ and [germs](wikipedia://en/352714).

Let $M$ be a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) and let $M\times M$ be the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of $M$ with itself. The [diagonal mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_mapping) $\Delta$ sends a point $p$ in $M$ to the point $(p,p)$ of $M\times M$. The image of $\Delta$ is called the diagonal. Let $\mathcal{I}$ be the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of [germs](wikipedia://en/352714) of smooth functions on $M\times M$ which vanish on the diagonal. Then the [quotient sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466#Operations) $\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^2$ consists of equivalence classes of functions which vanish on the diagonal modulo higher order terms. The [cotangent sheaf](wikipedia://en/44901757) is defined as the [pullback](wikipedia://en/7086661) of this sheaf to $M$:

$$\Gamma T^*M=\Delta^*\left(\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^2\right).$$

By [Taylor's theorem](wikipedia://en/51714), this is a [locally free sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaf) of modules with respect to the sheaf of germs of smooth functions of $M$. Thus it defines a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) on $M$: the **cotangent bundle**.

[Smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of the cotangent bundle are called (differential) [one-forms](wikipedia://en/314692).

## Contravariance properties {#Contravariance_properties}

A smooth morphism $\phi\colon M\to N$ of manifolds induces a [pullback sheaf](wikipedia://en/494995) $\phi^*T^*N$ on *M*. There is an [induced map](wikipedia://en/494995#Pullback_of_cotangent_vectors_and_1-forms) of vector bundles $\phi^*(T^*N)\to T^*M$.

## Examples {#Examples}

The tangent bundle of the vector space $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $T\,\mathbb{R}^n = \mathbb{R}^n\times \mathbb{R}^n$, and the cotangent bundle is $T^*\mathbb{R}^n = \mathbb{R}^n\times (\mathbb{R}^n)^*$, where $(\mathbb{R}^n)^*$ denotes the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of covectors, linear functions $v^*:\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}$.

Given a smooth manifold $M\subset \mathbb{R}^n$ embedded as a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) represented by the vanishing locus of a function $f\in C^\infty (\mathbb{R}^n),$ with the condition that $\nabla f \neq 0,$ the tangent bundle is

$$TM = \{(x,v) \in T\,\mathbb{R}^n \ :\ f(x) = 0,\ \, df_x(v) = 0\},$$

where $df_x \in T^*_xM$ is the [directional derivative](wikipedia://en/487859) $df_x(v) = \nabla\! f(x)\cdot v$. By definition, the cotangent bundle in this case is

$$T^*M = \bigl\{(x,v^*)\in T^*\mathbb{R}^n \ :\ f(x)=0,\ v^* \in T^*_xM \bigr\},$$

where $T^*_xM=\{v \in T_x\mathbb{R}^n\ :\ df_x(v)=0\}^*.$ Since every covector $v^* \in T^*_xM$ corresponds to a unique vector $v \in T_xM$ for which $v^*(u) = v \cdot u,$ for an arbitrary $u \in T_xM,$

$$T^*M = \bigl\{(x,v^*)\in T^*\mathbb{R}^n\ :\ f(x) = 0,\ v \in T_x\mathbb{R}^n,\ df_x(v)=0 \bigr\}.$$

:::

## The cotangent bundle as phase space {#The_cotangent_bundle_as_phase_space}

Since the cotangent bundle $X=T^*M$ is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524), it can be regarded as a manifold in its own right. Because at each point the tangent directions of $M$ can be paired with their dual covectors in the fiber, $X$ possesses a canonical one-form $\theta$ called the [tautological one-form](wikipedia://en/1855974), discussed below. The [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) of $\theta$ is a [symplectic 2-form](wikipedia://en/28356), out of which a non-degenerate [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811) can be built for $X$. For example, as a result $X$ is always an [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) manifold (the tangent bundle $TX$ is an orientable vector bundle). A special set of [coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinates) can be defined on the cotangent bundle; these are called the [canonical coordinates](wikipedia://en/706311). Because cotangent bundles can be thought of as [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356), any real function on the cotangent bundle can be interpreted to be a [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/292852); thus the cotangent bundle can be understood to be a [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) on which [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319) plays out.

### The tautological one-form {#The_tautological_one-form}

The cotangent bundle carries a canonical one-form $\theta$ also known as the [symplectic potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_potential), *Poincaré* *1*-form, or *Liouville* *1*-form. This means that if we regard $T^*M$ as a manifold in its own right, there is a canonical [section](wikipedia://en/367621) of the vector bundle $T^*(T^*M)$ over $T^*M$.

This section can be constructed in several ways. The most elementary method uses local coordinates. Suppose that $x^i$ are local coordinates on the base manifold $M$. In terms of these base coordinates, there are fibre coordinates $p_i$: a one-form at a particular point of $T^*M$ has the form $p_i\text{d}x^i$ ([Einstein summation convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_summation_convention) implied). So the manifold $T^*M$ itself carries local coordinates $(x^i,p_i)$ where the $x$'s are coordinates on the base and the $p$'s are coordinates in the fibre. The canonical one-form is given in these coordinates by

$$\theta_{(x,p)}=\sum_{i=1}^n p_i \, dx^i.$$

Intrinsically, the value of the canonical one-form in each fixed point of $T^*M$ is given as a [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995). Specifically, suppose that $\pi:T^*M\rightarrow M$ is the [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) of the bundle. Taking a point in $T^*_xM$ is the same as choosing of a point $x$ in $M$ and a one-form $\omega$ at $x$, and the tautological one-form $\theta$ assigns to the point $(x,\omega)$ the value

$$\theta_{(x,\omega)}=\pi^*\omega.$$

That is, for a vector $v$ in the tangent bundle of the cotangent bundle, the application of the tautological one-form $\theta$ to $v$ at $(x,\omega)$ is computed by projecting $v$ into the tangent bundle at $x$ using $\text{d}\pi:T(T^*M)\rightarrow TM$ and applying $\omega$ to this projection. Note that the tautological one-form is not a pullback of a one-form on the base $M$.

### Symplectic form {#Symplectic_form}

The cotangent bundle has a canonical [symplectic 2-form](wikipedia://en/28356) on it, as an [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) of the [tautological one-form](wikipedia://en/1855974), the [symplectic potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_potential). Proving that this form is, indeed, symplectic can be done by noting that being symplectic is a local property: since the cotangent bundle is locally trivial, this definition need only be checked on $\mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n$. But there the one form defined is the sum of $y_i\,dx_i$, and the differential is the canonical symplectic form, the sum of $dy_i \land dx_i$.

### Phase space {#Phase_space}

If the manifold $M$ represents the set of possible positions in a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087), then the cotangent bundle $T^{*}\!M$ can be thought of as the set of possible *positions* and *momenta*. For example, this is a way to describe the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) of a pendulum. The state of the pendulum is determined by its position (an angle) and its momentum (or equivalently, its velocity, since its mass is constant). The entire state space looks like a cylinder, which is the cotangent bundle of the circle. The above symplectic construction, along with an appropriate [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) function, gives a complete determination of the physics of system. See [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319) and the article on [geodesic flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_flow) for an explicit construction of the Hamiltonian equations of motion.
