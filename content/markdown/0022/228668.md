# Suslin's problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Suslin's problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Suslin's problem** is a question about [totally ordered sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_set) posed by (txt) and published posthumously. It has been shown to be [independent](wikipedia://en/1141208) of the standard [axiomatic system](wikipedia://en/188401) of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) known as [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC); (Solovay Tennenbaum 1971) showed that the statement can neither be proven nor disproven from those axioms, assuming ZF is consistent.

:::

(Suslin is also sometimes written with the French transliteration as Souslin, from the Cyrillic Суслин.) right

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Suslin's problem asks: Given a [non-empty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-empty) [totally ordered set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_set) *R* with the four properties

1. *R* does not have a [least nor a greatest element](wikipedia://en/663041);
1. the order on *R* is [dense](wikipedia://en/6320997) (between any two distinct elements there is another);
1. the order on *R* is [complete](wikipedia://en/549445), in the sense that every non-empty bounded subset has a [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) and an [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum); and
1. every collection of mutually [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) non-empty [open intervals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval) in *R* is [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) (this is the [countable chain condition](wikipedia://en/1550771) for the [order topology](wikipedia://en/213650) of *R*),

is *R* necessarily [order-isomorphic](wikipedia://en/23916899) to the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) **R**?

If the requirement for the countable chain condition is replaced with the requirement that *R* contains a countable dense subset (i.e., *R* is a [separable space](wikipedia://en/27855)), then the answer is indeed yes: any such set *R* is necessarily order-isomorphic to **R** (proved by [Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216)).

The condition for a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that every collection of non-empty disjoint [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) is at most countable is called the **Suslin property**.

:::

## Implications {#Implications}

Any totally ordered set that is *not* isomorphic to **R** but satisfies properties 1–4 is known as a **Suslin line**. The **Suslin hypothesis** says that there are no Suslin lines: that every countable-chain-condition dense complete linear order without endpoints is isomorphic to the real line. An equivalent statement is that every [tree](wikipedia://en/704160) of height ω<sub>1</sub> either has a branch of length ω<sub>1</sub> or an [antichain](wikipedia://en/348973) of [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) ℵ<sub>1</sub>.

The **generalized Suslin hypothesis** says that for every infinite [regular cardinal](wikipedia://en/374128) *κ* every tree of height *κ* either has a branch of length *κ* or an antichain of cardinality *κ.* The existence of Suslin lines is equivalent to the existence of [Suslin trees](wikipedia://en/5028195) and to [Suslin algebras](wikipedia://en/43236771).

The Suslin hypothesis is independent of ZFC. (Jech 1967) and (Tennenbaum 1968) independently used [forcing methods](wikipedia://en/152205) to construct models of ZFC in which Suslin lines exist. [Jensen](wikipedia://en/3785996) later proved that Suslin lines exist if the [diamond principle](wikipedia://en/691736), a consequence of the [axiom of constructibility](wikipedia://en/696472) V = L, is assumed. (Jensen's result was a surprise, as it had previously been [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that V = L implies that no Suslin lines exist, on the grounds that V = L implies that there are "few" sets.) On the other hand, (Solovay Tennenbaum 1971) used forcing to construct a model of ZFC without Suslin lines; more precisely, they showed that [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783) plus the negation of the continuum hypothesis implies the Suslin hypothesis.

The Suslin hypothesis is also independent of both the [generalized continuum hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continuum_hypothesis) (proved by [Ronald Jensen](wikipedia://en/3785996)) and of the negation of the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705). It is not known whether the generalized Suslin hypothesis is consistent with the generalized continuum hypothesis; however, since the combination implies the negation of the [square principle](wikipedia://en/2292730) at a singular strong [limit cardinal](wikipedia://en/374002)—in fact, at all [singular cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cardinal) and all regular [successor cardinals](wikipedia://en/375210)—it implies that the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300) holds in L(R) and is believed to imply the existence of an [inner model](wikipedia://en/1118686) with a [superstrong cardinal](wikipedia://en/248114).
