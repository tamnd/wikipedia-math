# Ipso jure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ipso jure"}

**Ipso jure** is a Latin phrase, directly translated as "by the law itself". It is used as an [adverb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverb).[^o2]

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

The phrase is used to describe legal consequences that occur by the act of the law itself. For example, if property is held in a [tenancy by the entirety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenancy_by_the_entirety) by a husband and wife, who then get [divorced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce), the property is converted ipso jure (i.e. by the law itself) into another form of tenancy, usually a [tenancy in common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenancy_in_common), at the very instant the marriage is dissolved. Likewise, [contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract) that establish [partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership) sometimes provide that the partnership is ipso jure dissolved if one partner attempts to sell his or her interest in the partnership. In all of these situations, when one legally significant fact occurs, other relationships are automatically changed by the law.
