# Finite mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Finite mathematics"}

In [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471), **Finite Math** is a [syllabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syllabus) in college and university mathematics that is independent of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176). A course in [precalculus](wikipedia://en/725821) may be a prerequisite for Finite Mathematics.

:::

Contents of the course include an eclectic selection of topics often applied in social science and business, such as finite [probability spaces](wikipedia://en/43325), [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280), [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process), finite [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), or [mathematical models](wikipedia://en/20590). These topics were used in Finite Mathematics courses at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) as developed by [John G. Kemeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Kemeny), [Gerald L. Thompson](wikipedia://en/25301762), and [J. Laurie Snell](wikipedia://en/33962324) and published by [Prentice-Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prentice-Hall). Other publishers followed with their own topics. With the arrival of [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) to facilitate computations, teaching and usage shifted from a broad-spectrum Finite Mathematics with paper and pen, into development and usage of software.
