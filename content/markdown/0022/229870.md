# Hans-Hermann Hoppe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans-Hermann Hoppe"}

**Hans-Hermann Hoppe** (;[^o7] ; born 2 September 1949) is a [German-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) academic associated with [Austrian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_School) economics, [anarcho-capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-capitalism), [right-wing libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-libertarianism), [paleolibertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolibertarianism), and [opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_democracy) to [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy).[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o32] From 1986 until 2008 he was professor of economics at the [University of Nevada, Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Las_Vegas) (UNLV). He is a senior fellow of the [Mises Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Mises_Institute) think tank. In 2006 he emigrated to Turkey and founded the [Property and Freedom Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_and_Freedom_Society).[^o12]

:::

Hoppe has written extensive criticisms of democracy, notably in his 2001 book *[Democracy: The God That Failed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy:_The_God_That_Failed)*.[^o13][^o41][^o32] The book favors exclusionary [covenant communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_%28law%29#Related_to_land) that are "founded for the purpose of protecting family and kin".[^o14][^o52] A section of the book favoring exclusion of democrats and homosexuals from society helped popularize Hoppe on the [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics).[^o13][^o42][^o72][^o15] Some of the speakers invited at his Property and Freedom Society conferences in Turkey have been [white nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism).[^o16][^o35][^o34][^o13]

Hoppe was a protégé of [Murray Rothbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Rothbard), who established him at UNLV, where Hoppe taught from 1986 to 2008.[^o23][^o9][^o8] In 2004, a student's complaint about Hoppe's lecture comments regarding homosexuals and [time preference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_preference) led to an investigation and non-disciplinary letter to Hoppe by UNLV,[^o17] which was subsequently withdrawn after a controversy over [academic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_freedom).[^o72][^o18]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

*Image: MurrayBW.jpg.* [Murray Rothbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Rothbard), whom Hoppe called his [mentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentor) and master

Hoppe was born in 1949 in [Peine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peine), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany).[^o20] His parents had settled there in 1946 after fleeing from [East Elbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Elbia), which after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) came under [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) occupation. He has stated the expropriation of his mother's family who were East Elbean [Junkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junker) has been a lasting trauma.[^o3]

Hoppe completed his undergraduate studies at [Saarland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_University)[^o20] and received his MA and PhD degrees in philosophy from [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt).[^o15] He studied under [Jürgen Habermas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jürgen_Habermas), a leading German intellectual of the post-WWII era, but came to reject Habermas's ideas and European leftism generally.[^o21]

He was a post-doctoral fellow at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), in [Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan), from 1976 to 1978 and earned his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in Foundations of Sociology and Economics from the University of Frankfurt in 1981.

## Career {#Career}

From 1981 to 1985 he taught in West Germany and Italy.[^o8] In 1986 Hoppe came to the United States through [Murray Rothbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Rothbard) on a scholarship from the [Center for Libertarian Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Libertarian_Studies), and Rothbard also established Hoppe at [University of Nevada, Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Las_Vegas).[^o23] From 1986[^o20] until his retirement in 2008,[^o8] Hoppe was a professor in the School of Business at University of Nevada, Las Vegas. He is a Distinguished Fellow of the [Mises Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mises_Institute), a libertarian think tank that is publisher of much of his work, and was editor of various Mises Institute periodicals.[^o24]

Hoppe has said that Rothbard was his "principal teacher, mentor and master".[^o25] Hoppe said he was "working and living side-by-side with him, in constant and immediate personal contact," and said that from 1985 until Rothbard's 1995 death, he considered Rothbard his "dearest fatherly friend".[^o26]

### Mises Institute and John Randolph Club {#Mises_Institute_and_John_Randolph_Club}

The [Mises Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mises_Institute) was founded in 1982 by [Lew Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Rockwell), [Burton Blumert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Blumert), and Murray Rothbard,[^o28] following a split between the [Cato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute) and Rothbard, who had been one of the founders of the Cato Institute.[^o29] After Rothbard's death in 1996, Hoppe was a leading anarcho-capitalist figure at the Mises Institute.[^o9]

Hoppe was active in the [John Randolph Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Randolph_Club), a far-right alliance of [paleolibertarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolibertarianism) and [paleoconservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoconservatism) that was organized by Rothbard and associated with the [Rockford Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockford_Institute).[^o13] The club was known for promoting [secessionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession) and [neo-Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confederates) views in the 1990s.[^o13]

### Property and Freedom Society {#Property_and_Freedom_Society}

In 2006, Hoppe founded The Property and Freedom Society (PFS), with annual conferences in [Bodrum, Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodrum,_Turkey). It and the Mises Institute represent a [paleolibertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolibertarianism) challenge to the [Mont Pelerin Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Pelerin_Society) and [Atlas Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Network) of think tanks.[^o10][^o31][^o32] Figures of the [European New Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_New_Right) and the American [alt-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-right) have attended PFS conferences.[^o32] Quinn Slobodian and Dieter Plehwe describe Hoppe as a "racialist right-wing libertarian", and Slobodian writes that the conferences have included members of the former [John Randolph Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Randolph_Club) along with "new advocates of stateless libertarianism and racial secession".[^o10][^o13]

In 2011, on the fifth anniversary of PFS, Hoppe reflected on its goals: "On the one hand, positively, it was to explain and elucidate the legal, economic, cognitive and cultural requirements and features of a free, state-less natural order. On the other hand, negatively, it was to unmask the State and showcase it for what it really is: an institution run by gangs of murderers, plunderers and thieves, surrounded by willing executioners, propagandists, sycophants, crooks, liars, clowns, charlatans, dupes and useful idiots – an institution that dirties and taints everything it touches."[^o33]

In 2016, Hoppe was criticized for inviting [white nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalist) speakers such as [Jared Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Taylor) and [neo-Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Nazi) [Richard B. Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_B._Spencer) to speak at the PFS, where also venture capitalist [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel) spoke.[^o16][^o57][^o34] Describing the PFS, the Southern Poverty Law Center said in 2016 that "in Hoppe one can see the connection between the ultra-Libertarians and white nationalists".[^o34] *[Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligencer_%28website%29)* in 2017 described the annual PFS meeting as "[Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum), but for racists".[^o35] Slobodian wrote in 2023 that "prophets of racial and social breakdown share the stage with investment advisors and financial consultants" at the conferences.[^o13]

### On democracy {#On_democracy}

Hoppe's 2001 book *[Democracy: The God That Failed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy:_The_God_That_Failed)* argues that democracy is a cause of civilizational decline.[^o39] Passages in the book oppose [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) and favor "natural elites".[^o13] In the book, Hoppe blames democratic forms of government for various social and economic problems, and attributes democracy's failures to pressure groups which seek to increase government expenditures and regulations. Hoppe proposes alternatives and remedies, including [secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession), decentralization of government, and "complete freedom of contract, occupation, trade and migration".[^o40] Hoppe argues that monarchy would preserve individual liberty more effectively than democracy.[^o41] The book helped popularize Hoppe in the [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics), particularly a section of the book that called for the exclusion of political rivals.[^o13][^o42]

Janek Wasserman writes that Hoppe "reimagined the Austrian legacy as one of authoritarianism, conservatism, antidemocracy, and anti-Enlightenment".[^o32] [Steven Horwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Horwitz) called the approaches of Hoppe and his Mises Institute colleague [Joseph Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_T._Salerno) "a fascist fist in a libertarian glove".[^o32] The political scientist George Hawley writes that Hoppe "may be the most important bridge between libertarianism and the Alt-Right".[^o43] Hawley notes that Hoppe has argued "libertarians must actually be radical conservatives", and that libertarians must favor a right to [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination), including on the basis of [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_discrimination).[^o11]

In Hoppe's view, Wasserman writes, "the successes of the [fin-de-siecle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fin-de-siecle) age—and the Austrian school—were not the product of liberal predominance or cosmopolitan virtues but of the ancien régime and its restrictive social order".[^o32] Regarding democracy and the arts, Hoppe argued in 2013 that "democracy leads to the subversion and ultimately disappearance of the notion of beauty and universal standards of beauty. Beauty is swamped and submerged by so-called '[modern art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_art)'."[^o44]

Reviewing *Democracy: The God That Failed*, [Walter Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Block), a colleague of Hoppe's at the [Mises Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mises_Institute), wrote that Hoppe's arguments shed light "on historical occurrences, from wars to poverty to inflation to interest rates to crime". While Hoppe concedes that 21st-century democracies are more prosperous than the monarchies of old, Hoppe argues that if nobles and kings replaced today's political leaders, their ability to take a long-term view of a country's well-being would "improve matters", Block wrote. Block shared what he called minor criticisms of Hoppe's theses regarding [time preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_preference), immigration and the gap between libertarianism and conservatism.[^o45]

Alberto Benegas-Lynch Jr., a professor of economics at the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires) who is associated with the libertarian [Cato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute),[^o46] criticized Hoppe's thesis that monarchy is preferable to democracy.[^o47] Benegas-Lynch provided evidence that modern monarchies tend to be far poorer than modern democracies. In response, Hoppe argued that comparing mostly African monarchies with mostly European democracies led to a distortion.[^o48]

Asked by *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)* in 2021 about his incorporation into far-right [internet memes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_meme) celebrating political murder, Hoppe responded that the question was ignorant, writing, "I have been an intellectual champion of private property right, free markets, freedom of contract and association, and peace", and, "What do I know? There are lots of crazy people out there!"[^o42]

### Covenant communities and discrimination {#Covenant_communities_and_discrimination}

In *Democracy: The God That Failed,* Hoppe argues in favor of property owners' right to establish communities with exclusive criteria for admission and acceptance.[^o53][^o50] Hoppe describes a society of [covenant communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_%28law%29#Related_to_land) made up of residents who have signed an agreement defining the nature of that community. Hoppe believes that these covenant communities should have the right to certain forms of discrimination, including the physical separation of people whose lifestyle is deemed incompatible with the norms of that community. He writes that "There would be little or no 'tolerance' and 'openmindedness' so dear to [left-libertarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-libertarianism). Instead, one would be on the right path toward restoring the freedom of association and exclusion implied in the institution of [private property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property)".[^o51][^o52]

Hoppe writes: "In a covenant concluded among proprietor and community tenants for the purpose of protecting their [private property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property), no such thing as a right to free (unlimited) speech exists, ... naturally no one is permitted to advocate ideas contrary to the very purpose of the covenant of preserving and protecting private property, such as [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) and [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism). There can be no tolerance toward democrats and communists in a [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism) social order. They will have to be physically separated and expelled from society. Likewise, in a covenant founded for the purpose of protecting family and kin, there can be no tolerance toward those habitually promoting lifestyles incompatible with this goal. They – the advocates of alternative, non-family and kin-centered lifestyles such as, for instance, individual [hedonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedonism), parasitism, nature-environment worship, homosexuality, or [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) – will have to be physically removed from society, too, if one is to maintain a [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism) order."[^o53][^o72]

Commenting on this passage, Martin Snyder of the [American Association of University Professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Professors) said Hoppe's words will disturb "\[t\]hose with a better memory than Hoppe for [segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States), [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid), internment facilities and concentration camps, for yellow stars and pink triangles".[^o72] [Walter Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Block) describes Hoppe as calling for homosexuals and others to be banned from polite society, and says of Hoppe's statement: "it is stark, it is well written, it is radical...it is still exceedingly difficult to reconcile with [libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism)."[^o54]

Critics such as Phillip Magness have argued that Hoppe's cultural [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism) views were not derived from [Mises's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Mises) [Austrian economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_school_of_economics), but rather from outside philosophical traditions such as other right-wing political thought and the [Frankfurt School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_School).[^o55]

### Support for immigration restrictions {#Support_for_immigration_restrictions}

Although a self-described [anarcho-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-capitalism) who favors abolishing the nation-state, Hoppe also garners controversy due to his support for governmental enforcement of [immigration laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_law), which critics argue is at odds with [libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism) and [anarcho-capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-capitalism).[^o62][^o57] Hoppe argues that as long as states exist, they should impose some restrictions on immigration. He has equated free immigration to "forced integration" which violates the rights of native peoples, since if land were privately owned, immigration would not be unhindered but would only occur with the consent of private property owners.[^o58]

Hoppe's [Mises Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mises_Institute) colleague [Walter Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Block) has characterized Hoppe as an "anti-open immigration activist" who argues that, though all [public property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_property) is "stolen" by the state from [taxpayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxpayer), "the state compounds the injustice when it allows immigrants to use \[public\] property, thus further "invading" the private property rights of the original owners".[^o59] However, Block rejects Hoppe's views as incompatible with [libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism).[^o60] He argues that Hoppe's logic implies that flagrantly unlibertarian laws such as regulations on prostitution and drug use "could be defended on the basis that many tax-paying property owners would not want such behavior on their own [private property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property)".[^o61] Another libertarian author, Simon Guenzl, writing for *Libertarian Papers,* argues that: "supporting a legitimate role for the state as an immigration gatekeeper is inconsistent with [Rothbardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Rothbard) and Hoppean libertarian anarchism, as well as with the associated strategy of advocating always and in every instance reductions in the state's role in society."[^o62]

In terms of specific immigration restrictions, Hoppe argued that an appropriate policy will require immigrants to the United States to display proficiency in English in addition to "superior (above-average) intellectual performance and character structure as well as a compatible system of values".[^o63] He suggested that these criteria would lead to a "systematic pro-European immigration bias". Jacob Hornberger of the *Future of Freedom Foundation* argued that the immigration test Hoppe advocated would probably be prejudiced against Latin American immigrants to the United States.[^o64]

### Remarks about homosexuals and academic investigation {#Remarks_about_homosexuals_and_academic_investigation}

*Image: Hans-Hermann Hoppe by Gage Skidmore.jpg.* Hoppe at an event in New York City in 2017

Hoppe's statements concerning [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_%28human_categorization%29) and [homosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality) have provoked controversy. In a 4 March 2004, lecture on [time preference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_preference) at the [University of Nevada, Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Las_Vegas) (UNLV), he stated that homosexuals tend to be more shortsighted than heterosexuals in their ability to save and plan economically (in part because they tend not to have children),[^o66] suggested that [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes)'s homosexuality might explain his economic views,[^o67] and that very young, very old, and childless people, are generally less future-oriented. Hoppe later said the comments lasted only about 90 seconds of a 75-minute class and that he had given similar lectures for 18 years without prior complaint. After a student complaint and ensuing request from the university, Hoppe apologized to the class, comparing the remarks to cultural generalizations about food preferences. The student then filed a formal complaint alleging Hoppe did not take it the apology seriously.[^o68]

An investigation followed. In February 2005, UNLV provost [Raymond W. Alden III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_W._Alden_III)issued Hoppe a non-disciplinary letter of instruction finding that he had "created a hostile or intimidating educational environment in violation of the University's policies regarding discrimination as to sexual orientation". The letter also instructed Hoppe to "... cease mischaracterizing opinion as objective fact".[^o69]

Hoppe appealed with assistance from the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union), which threatened legal action and defended academic freedom.[^o70][^o68] Alden's decision was picked up by [Fox News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News) and several blogs and libertarians organized a campaign to contact the university.[^o70] The university received two weeks of bad publicity and the Interim Chancellor ([Nevada System of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_System_of_Higher_Education)) [Jim Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Rogers_%28attorney%29) expressed concerns about "any attempts to thwart free speech".[^o71]

UNLV President [Carol Harter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Harter) acted upon Hoppe's appeal on 18 February 2005, deciding that Hoppe's views, even if non-mainstream or controversial, should not be cause for reprimanding him. She dismissed the discrimination complaint against Hoppe, and the non-disciplinary letter was withdrawn from Hoppe's personnel file.[^o72] She wrote, "In the balance between freedoms and responsibilities, and where there may be ambiguity between the two, academic freedom must, in the end, be foremost."[^o73]

Hoppe later wrote about the incident and the UNLV investigation in an article entitled "My Battle With the Thought Police".[^o74] Martin Snyder of the [American Association of University Professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Professors) wrote that he should not be "punished for freely expressing his opinions".[^o72]

Various controversies about [academic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_freedom), including the Hoppe matter and remarks made by [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) President [Lawrence Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Summers), prompted the University of Nevada, Las Vegas, to hold a conference on academic freedom in October 2005.[^o75] In 2009 UNLV proposed a new policy that included the encouragement of reporting by people who felt that they had encountered bias.[^o76] The proposed policy was criticized by the Nevada ACLU and some faculty members who remembered the Hoppe incident as adverse to academic freedom.[^o76][^o77]

## Argumentation ethics {#Argumentation_ethics}

*Image: Hans-Hermann-Hoppe.jpg.* Hoppe in 2005

In the September 1988 issue of [*Liberty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_%28libertarian_magazine%29),[^o80] Hoppe attempted to establish an *[a priori](wikipedia://en/6679056)* and [value-neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_judgment) justification for libertarian ethics by devising a new theory which he named *argumentation ethics*.[^o81] Hoppe asserted that any argument which in any respect purports to contradict libertarian principles is logically incoherent.[^o82]

Hoppe argued that, in the course of having an argument about politics (or indeed any subject), people assume certain norms of argumentation, including a prohibition on initiating violence. Hoppe then extrapolated this argument to political life in general, arguing that the norms governing argumentation should apply in all political contexts. Hoppe claimed that, of all political philosophies, only anarcho-capitalist libertarianism prohibits the initiation of aggressive violence (the [non-aggression principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-aggression_principle)); therefore, any argument for any political philosophy other than anarcho-capitalist libertarianism is logically incoherent.

In the following issue, *Liberty* published comments by ten libertarians,[^o83] followed by a rejoinder from Hoppe.[^o81] In his comment for *Liberty*, Hoppe's friend and [Mises Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mises_Institute) supervisor [Murray Rothbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Rothbard) wrote that Hoppe's theory was "a dazzling breakthrough for political philosophy in general and for libertarianism in particular" and that Hoppe "has managed to transcend the famous [is/ought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is/ought), [fact/value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact–value_distinction) dichotomy that has plagued philosophy since the days of the [Scholastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism), and that had brought modern [libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism) into a tiresome deadlock".[^o81] However, the majority of Hoppe's colleagues surveyed by *Liberty* rejected his theory.[^o84][^o85] In his response, Hoppe derided his critics as "[utilitarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilitarians)".[^o81]

Mises Institute Senior Fellow Roderick T. Long stated that Hoppe's *a priori* formulation of [libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism) denied the fundamental principle of Misesean [praxeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxeology). On the issue of utilitarianism, Long wrote, "Hoppe's argument, if it worked, would commit us to recognizing and respecting libertarian rights regardless of what our goals are – but as a [praxeologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxeology), I have trouble seeing how any practical requirement can be justified apart from a means-end structure."[^o86] Libertarian philosopher [Jason Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Brennan) rejected Hoppe's argument, saying, "Hoppe's argument illicitly conflates a [liberty right with a claim right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claim_rights_and_liberty_rights), and so fails."[^o87]

J. Mikael Olsson argued that Hoppe had not provided any non-circular reasons why we "have to regard moral values as something that *must* be regarded as being established through (consensual) argument instead of 'mere' subjective preferences for situations turning out in certain ways". In other words, the theory relies "on the existence \[of\] certain intuitions, the acceptance of which cannot itself be the result of 'value-free' reasoning."[^o88][^o89]

According to commentators, his [original appropriation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_appropriation) theory based on argumentation ethics initially tended toward the [labor theory of property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_theory_of_property), but later moved toward the [first possession theory of property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_possession_theory_of_property) perspective.[^o90][^o91]

## Influence {#Influence}

Hoppe was an influence on the [neoreactionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoreactionary) [monarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchism) blogger [Curtis Yarvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_Yarvin), also known by the pen name Mencius Moldbug.[^o93][^o94]

[Javier Milei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Milei), the president of Argentina, has cited Hoppe as one of his major influences and has recommended his bibliography in the past, despite recent grievances between both about the ideologic purity of [Milei's administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Javier_Milei) and the [praxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxis_%28process%29) for a libertarian on a democratic system.[^o95][^o96]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hoppe resides in Turkey with his wife Gülçin Imre Hoppe, an Austrian school economist and hotelier.[^o12][^o98][^o13] According to her, she comes from a family of medical doctors and she studied economic theory in 2003 at University of Istanbul and became a Misesian. In 2006, Hoppe left Las Vegas and they "started a life together". As of 2024, their [patchwork family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepfamily) consisted of four children and seven grandchildren. A daughter, Emily, lives with her husband and children in California, while his son Nick moved to Austria in 2019 with his wife and two daughters.[^o99]

Ther PFS Congress series takes place at their family-owned hotel, the Karia Princess in [Bodrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodrum).[^o99]
