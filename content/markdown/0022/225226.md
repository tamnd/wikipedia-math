# Peter Armitage (statistician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Armitage"}

**Peter Armitage** [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBE) (15 June 1924 – 14 February 2024) was a British [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who specialised in [medical statistics](wikipedia://en/11082896).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Peter Armitage was born in [Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield), and was educated at [Huddersfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_New_College), before going on to read mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge).[^o4] Armitage belonged to the generation of mathematicians who came to maturity in the Second World War. He joined the weapons procurement agency, the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply_%28United_Kingdom%29) where he worked on statistical problems with [George Barnard](wikipedia://en/2792579).

After the war he resumed his studies and then worked as a statistician for the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28UK%29) from 1947 to 1961.[^o4] From 1961 to 1976, he was Professor of Medical Statistics at the [London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_and_Tropical_Medicine) where he succeeded [Austin Bradford Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Bradford_Hill).[^o4] His main work there was on [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530). He moved to Oxford as Professor of Biomathematics and became Professor of Applied Statistics and head of the new Department of Statistics, retiring in 1990.[^o4] He was president of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) in 1982–4.[^o4] He was president of the [International Society for Clinical Biostatistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Clinical_Biostatistics) in 1990–1991, and editor-in-chief of the *Encyclopedia of Biostatistics*.

Armitage lived in [Wallingford, Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallingford,_Oxfordshire), and died on 14 February 2024, at the age of 99.[^o5]
