# Edmund Landau

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edmund Landau"}

Landau

**Edmund Georg Hermann Landau** (14 February 1877 – 19 February 1938) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in the fields of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Edmund Landau was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family in Berlin. His father was [Leopold Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Landau), a [gynecologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynaecology), and his mother was Johanna Jacoby. Landau studied mathematics at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin), receiving his doctorate in 1899 and his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) (the post-doctoral qualification required to teach in German universities) in 1901. His doctoral thesis was 14 pages long.

In 1895, his paper on scoring chess tournaments is the earliest use of [eigenvector centrality](wikipedia://en/3495507).[^o4][^o5]

Landau taught at the University of Berlin from 1899 to 1909, after which he held a chair at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). He married Marianne Ehrlich, the daughter of the Nobel Prize-winning biologist [Paul Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich), in 1905.

At the [1912 International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065#1912,_Cambridge) Landau listed four problems in number theory about primes that he said were particularly hard using current mathematical methods. They remain unsolved to this day and are now known as [Landau's problems](wikipedia://en/5785677).

During the 1920s, Landau was instrumental in establishing the Mathematics Institute at the nascent [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem). Intent on eventually settling in Jerusalem, he taught himself Hebrew and delivered a lecture entitled *Solved and unsolved problems in elementary number theory* in Hebrew on 2 April 1925 during the university's groundbreaking ceremonies. He negotiated with the university's president, [Judah Magnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judah_Magnes), regarding a position at the university and the building that was to house the Mathematics Institute.

Landau and his family emigrated to [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) in 1927 and he began teaching at the Hebrew University. The family had difficulty adjusting to the primitive living standards then available in Jerusalem. In addition, Landau became a pawn in a struggle for control of the university between Magnes and [Chaim Weizmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Weizmann) and [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736). Magnes suggested that Landau be appointed Rector of the university, but Einstein and Weizmann supported [Selig Brodetsky](wikipedia://en/4601432). Landau was disgusted by the dispute and decided to return to Göttingen, remaining there until he was forced out by the Nazi regime after the [Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung) in 1933, in a boycott organized by [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller). Thereafter, he lectured only outside Germany. He moved to Berlin in 1934, where he died in early 1938 of natural causes.

In 1903, Landau gave a much simpler proof than was then known of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) and later presented the first systematic treatment of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) in the *Handbuch der Lehre von der Verteilung der Primzahlen* (the "*Handbuch*").[^o6] He also made important contributions to [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).

[G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [Hans Heilbronn](wikipedia://en/4388848) wrote that "No one was ever more passionately devoted to mathematics than Landau". [^o7]
