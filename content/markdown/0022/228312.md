# Additive inverse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Additive inverse"}

In mathematics, the **additive inverse** of an [element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29) $x$, denoted $-x$,[^o2] is the element that when [added](wikipedia://en/61338) to $x$, yields the [additive identity](wikipedia://en/4178225).[^o3] This additive identity is often the number [0 (zero)](wikipedia://en/34513), but it can also refer to a more generalized [zero element](wikipedia://en/3476379).

:::

In [elementary mathematics](wikipedia://en/1775329), the additive inverse is often referred to as the **opposite** number,[^o4][^o5] or the **negative** of a number.[^o6] The [unary operation](wikipedia://en/161006) of **arithmetic negation**[^o7] is closely related to *[subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141)*[^o8] and is important in [solving algebraic equations](wikipedia://en/9710).[^o9] Not all [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) where addition is defined have an additive inverse, such as the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474).[^o10]

## Common examples {#Common_examples}

When working with [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), and [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), the additive inverse of any number can be found by multiplying it by [−1](wikipedia://en/449568).[^o9]

*Image: NegativeI2Root.svg.* These complex numbers, two of eight values of [$\sqrt[8]{1}$](wikipedia://en/171950), are mutually opposite

*Simple cases of additive inverses*

::: {#o12 .table cue=table name="Simple cases of additive inverses"}

| $n$ | $-n$ |
|---|---|
| $7$ | $-7$ |
| $0.35$ | $-0.35$ |
| $\frac{1}{4}$ | $-\frac{1}{4}$ |
| $\pi$ | $-\pi$ |
| $1 + 2i$ | $-1 - 2i$ |

:::

The concept can also be extended to algebraic expressions, which is often used when balancing [equations](wikipedia://en/9284).

*Additive inverses of algebraic expressions*

::: {#o13 .table cue=table name="Additive inverses of algebraic expressions"}

| $n$ | $-n$ |
|---|---|
| $a - b$ | $-(a - b) = -a + b$ |
| $2x^2 + 5$ | $-(2x^2 + 5) = -2x^2 - 5$ |
| $\frac{1}{x + 2}$ | $-\frac{1}{x+2}$ |
| $\sqrt{2}\sin{\theta} - \sqrt{3}\cos{2\theta}$ | $-(\sqrt{2}\sin{\theta} - \sqrt{3}\cos{2\theta}) = -\sqrt{2}\sin{\theta} + \sqrt{3}\cos{2\theta}$ |

:::

## Relation to subtraction {#Relation_to_subtraction}

The additive inverse is closely related to [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141), which can be viewed as an addition using the inverse:

$$a - b \,=\, a + (-b).$$

Conversely, the additive inverse can be thought of as subtraction from zero:

$$-a\,=\,0 - a.$$

This connection lead to the minus sign being used for both opposite magnitudes and subtraction as far back as the 17th century. While this notation is standard today, it was met with opposition at the time, as some mathematicians felt it could be unclear and lead to errors.[^o15]

::: {#o17 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Given an algebraic structure defined under addition $(S, +)$ with an additive identity $e \in S$, an element $x \in S$ has an additive inverse $y$ if and only if $y \in S$, $x + y = e$, and $y + x = e$.[^o10]

Addition is typically only used to refer to a [commutative](wikipedia://en/294390) operation, but for some systems of numbers, such as [floating point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_point), it might not be [associative](wikipedia://en/1335).[^o18] When it is associative, so $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$, the left and right inverses, if they exist, will agree, and the additive inverse will be unique. In non-associative cases, the left and right inverses may disagree, and in these cases, the inverse is not considered to exist.

The definition requires [closure](wikipedia://en/293802), that the additive element $y$ be found in $S$. However, despite being able to add the natural numbers together, the set of natural numbers does not include the additive inverse values. This is because the additive inverse of a natural number (e.g., $-3$ for $3$) is not a natural number; it is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). Therefore, the natural numbers in set $S$ do have additive inverses and their associated inverses are [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616).

:::

::: {#o20 .example cue=heading name="Further examples"}

## Further examples {#Further_examples}

- In a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), the additive inverse $-\mathbf{v}$ (often called the *[opposite vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_vector)* of $\mathbf{v}$) has the same [magnitude](wikipedia://en/990534) as $\mathbf{v}$ and but the opposite direction.[^o21]
- In [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087), the **modular additive inverse** of $x$ is the number $a$ such that $a + x \equiv 0\pmod{n}$ and always exists. For example, the inverse of 3 modulo 11 is 8, as $3 + 8 \equiv 0\pmod{11}$.[^o22]
- In a [Boolean ring](wikipedia://en/54356), which has elements $\{0, 1\}$ addition is often defined as the [symmetric difference](wikipedia://en/262288). So $0 + 0 = 0$, $0 + 1 = 1$, $1 + 0 = 1$, and $1 + 1 = 0$. Our additive identity is 0, and both elements are their own additive inverse as $0 + 0 = 0$ and $1 + 1 = 0$.[^o23]

:::
