# Math rock

*Image: Albini\_atp.jpg.* [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini) was an influence in the math rock genre.

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Math rock"}

**Math rock** is a style of [alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock) and [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock)[^o4] with roots in bands such as [King Crimson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Crimson), [Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_%28band%29), and [Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_%28band%29).[^o5][^o6] It is characterized by complex, atypical [rhythmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm) structures (including irregular stopping and starting), [counterpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterpoint), odd [time signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_signatures), and [extended chords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_chord). Math rock has been classified as a derivative or subgenre of [experimental rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_rock), sharing its [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde_music) ethos and emphasis on [through-composed structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through-composed_music) rather than traditional song forms.[^o7][^o8][^o9] The genre has additionally been described as bearing similarities to [post-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-rock), as the "opposite side of the same coin". Opting for a "[rockier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music)" approach to songwriting and [timbres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbres), the style is often performed by [smaller ensembles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_ensemble) which emphasize the role of the [guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar).[^o4]

:::

## History and precursors {#History_and_precursors}

The albums *[Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_%28King_Crimson_album%29)* and *[Discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discipline_%28King_Crimson_album%29)* by [King Crimson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Crimson),[^o11][^o12] as well as *[Spiderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiderland)* by [Slint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slint),[^o13] are generally considered seminal influences on the development of math rock. The Canadian [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) group [Nomeansno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomeansno) (founded in 1979 and inactive as of 2016) have been cited by music critics as a "secret influence" on math rock,[^o14] predating much of the genre's development by more than a decade. The American group [Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_%28experimental_band%29), an even more avant-garde group of the same era, featured the guitarist [Fred Frith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Frith) and the bassist [Bill Laswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Laswell). With some influence from the rapid-fire energy of punk, Massacre's influential music used complex rhythmic characteristics. [Black Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Flag_%28band%29)'s 1984 album, *[My War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_War)*, also included unusual [polyrhythms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyrhythm).[^o15]

Two songs on [Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_%28band%29)' album *[Fragile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_%28Yes_album%29)* (1971) have drawn attention – [Paul Lester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Lester) of *[Classic Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Rock_%28magazine%29)* writes that "[Five Per Cent for Nothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Per_Cent_for_Nothing)" finds drummer [Bill Bruford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Bruford) "inventing math rock",[^o16] while "[Heart of the Sunrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_the_Sunrise)" was described by *[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_%28website%29)*'s Chris Dahlen, [Dominique Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Leone) and Joe Tangari as "a deftly constructed proto math-rock epic".[^o17]

[Polvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polvo), [Don Caballero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Caballero), [Slint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slint), [Bitch Magnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitch_Magnet), [Bastro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastro) and [Ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruins_%28Japanese_band%29) are the genre's pioneers.[^o18][^o19]

Examples of modern math rock bands include [Delta Sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Sleep),[^o20] [Covet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covet),[^o21] [Tricot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricot_%28band%29),[^o22] and [TTNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TTNG).[^o23]

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

Math rock is typified by its rhythmic complexity, seen as *[mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical)* in character by listeners and critics. While most rock music uses a time [meter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meter_%28music%29) (however [accented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accent_%28music%29) or [syncopated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncopation)), math rock makes use of more non-standard, frequently changing [time signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_signature) such as time, time, time, or time.[^o6]

As in traditional rock, the sound is most often dominated by guitars and drums. However, drums play a greater role in math rock in providing driving, complex rhythms. Math rock guitarists make use of [tapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapping) techniques and [loop pedals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_looping) to build on these rhythms, as illustrated by songs like those of math rock supergroup [Battles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_%28band%29).[^o25][^o26]

[Lyrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyrics) are generally not the focus of math rock; the voice is treated as just another instrument in the mix. Often, vocals are not [overdubbed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overdub), and are positioned less prominently, as in the recording style of [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini). Many of math rock's best-known groups are entirely [instrumental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental_rock) such as [Don Caballero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Caballero) or [Hella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hella_%28band%29).[^o27][^o28]

A significant intersection exists between math rock and [emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo), exemplified by bands such as [Tiny Moving Parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Moving_Parts)[^o29] or [American Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_%28band%29), whose sound has been described as "twinkly, mathy rock, a sound that became one of the defining traits of the emo scene throughout the 2000s".[^o30]

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The term began as a joke but has since developed into the accepted name for the musical style. According to [Matt Sweeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Sweeney), singer with [Chavez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavez_%28band%29), the term started because his friend would not react at all while listening to his music, then proceed to take a calculator out to figure out how good the song was.[^o32] Not all critics see math rock as a serious sub-genre of rock, and some of the genre's most notable acts have disavowed the term.[^o33][^o19]

## Artists {#Artists}

### Asian {#Asian}

Math rock has a significant presence in Japan; the most prominent Japanese groups include [Toe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toe_%28band%29), [Tricot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricot_%28band%29), The Cabs, and [Lite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lite_%28band%29).[^o36] Other Japanese groups which incorporate math rock in their music include [Ling Tosite Sigure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ling_Tosite_Sigure),[^o37] [Zazen Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazen_Boys)[^o36] and [Mouse on the Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouse_on_the_Keys)[^o38] while the [Japanoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanoise) scene features bands such as [Ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruins_%28Japanese_band%29), [Zeni Geva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeni_Geva), and [Boredoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boredoms).[^o36]

Taiwan has a very small indie music scene, of which math rock is an emergent genre that is quickly gaining in popularity, with well-known math rock bands including [Elephant Gym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_Gym).[^o39]

Math rock also has a presence in South Korea, led by bands such as [Cotoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotoba)[^o40] and [Dabda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabda).

### North American {#North_American}

[Polvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polvo) of [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina) is often considered one of the household names in math rock, although the band members themselves have disavowed the categorization.[^o42]

In California, [power pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_pop) groups [Game Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Theory_%28band%29) and [the Loud Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Loud_Family) were both led by [Scott Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Miller_%28pop_musician%29), who was said to "tinker with [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music) the way a born mathematician tinkers with numbers".[^o43] The origin of Game Theory's name is mathematical, suggesting a "nearly mathy" sound cited as "IQ rock."[^o44]

Although the Seattle [grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge) scene was not widely associated with math rock, some consider [Soundgarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundgarden) to be one of few exceptions, due to the odd time signatures found in many of their songs.[^o45]
