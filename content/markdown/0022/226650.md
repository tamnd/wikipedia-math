# Glenn Shafer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glenn Shafer"}

**Glenn Shafer** (born November 21, 1946) is an American mathematician and statistician. He is the co-creator of [Dempster–Shafer theory](wikipedia://en/226660). He is a university professor and a board of governors professor at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), as well as a former dean of its [business school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_Business_School_–_Newark_and_New_Brunswick).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Shafer grew up on a farm near [Caney, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caney,_Kansas). He earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), then entered the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps), serving in Afghanistan. He returned to Princeton, earning a PhD in mathematical statistics in 1973 under [Geoffrey Watson](wikipedia://en/7688869).[^o3][^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Shafer taught at Princeton and the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), joining the faculty of [Rutgers Business School – Newark and New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_Business_School_–_Newark_and_New_Brunswick) in 1992. From 2011 to 2014, he served as dean of the business school of the university.

During the 1970s and 1980s Shafer expanded a theory first introduced by the Harvard professor, [Arthur P. Dempster](wikipedia://en/7040761), to create [Dempster–Shafer theory](wikipedia://en/226660), also described as, the theory of belief functions or evidence theory. It is a general framework for reasoning with uncertainty, allowing one to combine evidence from different sources and to arrive at a degree of belief (represented by a mathematical object called *belief function*) that takes into account all the available evidence.[^o6] The theory and its extensions have been of particular interest to the [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) community.[^o7]

More recently Shafer worked with [Vladimir Vovk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vovk) to develop a [game-theoretic](wikipedia://en/11924) framework for [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). That work produced a 2001 book, *Probability and Finance: It's Only a Game!*. A joint research group between Rutgers and [Royal Holloway, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway) has produced more than 50 working papers on the subject.[^o8]

### Principal publications {#Principal_publications}

- Shafer, Glenn, *A Mathematical Theory of Evidence*, Princeton University Press, 1976.[^o10]
- Shafer, Glenn, and Vovk, Vladimir, *Probability and Finance: It's Only a Game!*, John Wiley and Sons, 2001.[^o11]
- Shafer, Glenn, and Vovk, Vladimir, *Game‐Theoretic Foundations for Probability and Finance*, John Wiley and Sons, 2019.[^o12]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Shafer is designated as a Board of Governors Professor at Rutgers. The [Prague University of Economics and Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_University_of_Economics_and_Business) recognized him with an honorary doctorate in 2009[^o14]. He has been a [Fulbright Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Fellow) and a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow).[^o3] He was elected a Fellow of the [Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Artificial_Intelligence) in 1992.[^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He is married to retired Princeton history professor, author, and artist [Nell Irvin Painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nell_Irvin_Painter).
