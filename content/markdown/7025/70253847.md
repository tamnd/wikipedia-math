# Bekić's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bekić's theorem"}

In [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), **Bekić's theorem** or **Bekić's lemma** is a [theorem about fixed-points](wikipedia://en/1011848) which allows splitting a mutual recursion into recursions on one variable at a time.[^o2][^o3][^o4] It was created by Austrian Hans Bekić (1936-1982) in 1969,[^o5] and published posthumously in a book by [Cliff Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Jones_%28computer_scientist%29) in 1984.[^o6]

:::

The theorem is set up as follows.[^o2][^o5] Consider two operators $f : P \times Q \to P$ and $g : P \times Q \to Q$ on [pointed directed-complete partial orders](wikipedia://en/572352) $P$ and $Q$, [continuous](wikipedia://en/743106) in each component. Then define the operator $(f, g)(x, y) = (f (x, y), g(x, y))$. This is monotone with respect to the [product order](wikipedia://en/1956306) (componentwise order). By the [Kleene fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/1234125), it has a least fixed point $\mu (x, y).(f, g)(x, y)$, a pair $(x_0, y_0)$ in $P \times Q$ such that $f (x_0, y_0) = x_0$ and $g(x_0, y_0) = y_0$.

Bekić's theorem (called the "bisection lemma" in his notes)[^o5] is that the simultaneous least fixed point $\mu (x, y).(f, g)(x, y)$ can be separated into a series of least fixed points on $P$ and $Q$, in particular the fixed point is equal to $(x_0,y_0)$ as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} x_0 &= \mu x . f ( x , \mu y . g(x,y) ) \\ y_0 &= \mu y . g ( x_0 , y ) \end{aligned}$$

In this presentation $y_0$ is defined in terms of $x_0$. It can instead be defined in a symmetric presentation:[^o2][^o7][^o8]

$$\begin{aligned} x_0 &= \mu x . f ( x , \mu y . g(x,y) ) \\ y_0 &= \mu y . g ( \mu x . f(x,y), y ) \end{aligned}$$

::: {#o9 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof** (Bekić):[^o5]

Taking $y_0 = \mu y . g ( x_0 , y )$, it is clear that $y_0 = g(x_0,y_0)$ since $y_0$ is the fixed point. Similarly letting $x_0 = \mu x . f ( x , \mu y . g(x,y) )$, we have $x_0 = f ( x_0 , \mu y . g(x_0,y) ) = f(x_0,y_0)$. Hence $(x_0,y_0)$ is a fixed point of $(f,g)$.

Furthermore, if there is a pre-fixed point $(x_1,y_1)$ with $(x_1,y_1) \geq (f(x_1,y_1),g(x_1,y_1))$, then $x_1 \geq f ( x_1 , \mu y . g(x_1,y)) \geq x_0$ and $y_1 \geq \mu y . g ( x_1 , y ) \geq \mu y . g ( x_0 , y ) = y_0$; hence $(x_1,y_1) \geq (x_0,y_0)$ and we conclude $(x_0,y_0)$ is the minimal fixed point.

:::

## Variants {#Variants}

### In a complete lattice {#In_a_complete_lattice}

A variant of the theorem strengthens the conditions on $P$ and $Q$ to be that they are [complete lattices](wikipedia://en/44777), and finds the [least fixed point](wikipedia://en/1211913) using the [Knaster–Tarski theorem](wikipedia://en/181417). The requirement for continuity of $f$ and $g$ can then be weakened to only requiring them to be [monotonic functions](wikipedia://en/48260).[^o2][^o4]

### Categorical formulation {#Categorical_formulation}

Bekić's lemma has been generalized to fix-points of endofunctors of categories (initial [$F$-algebras](wikipedia://en/2168700)).[^o14]

Given two functors $F : \mathbf{C} \times \mathbf{D} \to \mathbf{C}$ and $G : \mathbf{C} \times \mathbf{D} \to \mathbf{D}$ such that all $\mu X.F(X,Y)$ and $\mu Y.G(X,Y)$ exist, the fix-point $\mu \langle X,Y\rangle .\langle F(X,Y),G(X,Y)\rangle$ is carried by the pair:

$$\begin{aligned} X_0 &= \mu X . F ( X , \mu Y . G(X,Y) ) \\ Y_0 &= \mu Y . G ( X_0 , Y ) \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

Bekić's theorem can be applied repeatedly to find the least fixed point of a [tuple](wikipedia://en/132729) in terms of least fixed points of single variables. Although the resulting expression might become rather complex, it can be easier to reason about fixed points of single variables when designing an [automated theorem prover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_theorem_prover).[^o16]
