# George J. Minty

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George J. Minty"}

**George James Minty Jr.** (September 16, 1929, [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit) – August 6, 1986,[^o2] [Bloomington, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Indiana)) was an American mathematician, specializing in mathematical analysis and discrete mathematics. He is known for the [Klee–Minty cube](wikipedia://en/31302509), the [Browder–Minty theorem](wikipedia://en/11908534), the introduction of [oriented regular matroids](wikipedia://en/24338041), and the [Minty-Vitaver theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minty-Vitaver_theorem) on [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Minty.png.* The beginning of Minty's article *Monotone (nonlinear) operators in Hilbert space*, published in *[Duke Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Mathematical_Journal)*, 1962

George Minty Jr. grew up in Detroit. His father emigrated, after WW I, from Scotland to work in Detroit's automotive industry as a [tool and die maker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_and_die_maker). George Minty Jr. received his bachelor's degree from Detroit's [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University). After service in the [US Army Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Corps_%28United_States_Army%29) at [Fort Monmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Monmouth),[^o4] he became in 1956 a graduate student in mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan).[^o5] There he received in 1959 his PhD with thesis *Integrability Conditions for Vector Fields in Banach Spaces* supervised by [Erich Rothe](wikipedia://en/67679736). As a postdoc in 1959 he was briefly a visiting researcher at Tokyo's [Waseda University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waseda_University).[^o7]

His first job out of grad school was as an applied mathematician at the General Motors Research Laboratory in Detroit, Michigan, and it was here that he first melded the discrete and the continuous to obtain significant results.[^o4]

Minty joined the University of Michigan faculty and was eventually promoted to associate professor before he resigned in 1965.[^o5] For the academic year 1964–1965 he was on leave to do research at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute).[^o8] In 1965 he was awarded a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship).[^o9] At Indiana University he was a full professor of mathematics from 1965[^o10] until his death in 1986.[^o2]

## Research {#Research}

Minty's 1966 paper *On the axiomatic foundations of the theories of directed linear graphs, electrical networks and network-programming* is important in [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory). In that 1966 paper, according to [Dominic Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Welsh):

The circuit space and cocircuit space of binary matroids were introduced by Minty ... who was also the first to study orientable matriods.[^o12]

According to K.-C. Chang: The theory of [monotone operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_function#Monotonicity_in_functional_analysis) and [pseudo-monotone operators](wikipedia://en/11908435) attracted much attention in the 1960s and 70s. The works of Minty ..., Browder ..., [Hartman](wikipedia://en/35263118) and [Stampacchia](wikipedia://en/4872667) ..., [H. Brezis](wikipedia://en/18329570) ..., etc. constitute the basic content of the theory.[^o13]

In 2012 D. Erdős, A. Frank, and K. Kun published a sharpening of Minty's coloring theorem (published in 1962 in *[The American Mathematical Monthly](wikipedia://en/1179483)*).[^o14]
