# Carl A. Wiley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carl A. Wiley"}

**Carl Atwood Wiley** (December 30, 1918 – April 21, 1985) was an American mathematician and engineer. He is most widely known as the originator of the [solar sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_sail) concept as well as the inventor of [synthetic aperture radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_aperture_radar).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Wiley's research work began at the [Air Force Aircraft Radiation Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Laboratory) at [Wright Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Field) in 1941. In 1942 he discovered the [piezoelectricity](wikipedia://en/24975) of [Barium titanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barium_titanate), for which he later was awarded a patent.[^o4] In 1949 he went to work as the engineer-in-charge of [Goodyear Aerophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Aerospace). It was during this time he invented [synthetic aperture radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_aperture_radar) in 1951, patented as "Pulsed Doppler Radar Methods and Means," #3,196,436. That same year Wiley posited the idea of solar sails in a science fiction story published in *[Astounding Science Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astounding_Science_Fiction)* magazine entitled *Clipper Ships of Space* (originally titled *Are the Clipper Ships gone forever?*). Wiley wrote his story under the [pen name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen_name) of Russel Saunders, an in-group reference to [Russel–Saunders coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russel–Saunders_coupling) not unlike [J.J. Coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.J._Coupling), itself a reference to [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839).[^o4] Seven years later [Richard L. Garwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_L._Garwin) developed the first technical specifications for a solar sail.[^o5] [Robert L. Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_L._Forward) credited Wiley for the genesis of the idea in Forward's 1990 patent.[^o6] Wiley's research and manuscripts for the story are now housed in the [Eaton collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eaton_collection).[^o7] In 1953 he left Goodyear to found his own company, Wiley Electronics in [Phoenix, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona) until it was bought out in 1962. Following that Wiley worked for [North American Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation) and its successor, [Rockwell International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_International) where he worked on various radar projects including LOCO, SINCO, VOLPHASE, and VOLFRE.[^o8] In 1978 he went to work at [Hughes Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Aircraft_Company) where he eventually retired as a chief scientist in the technology division of Hughes' Space and Communications Group.[^o4] In 1985, [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) awarded Wiley their Pioneer Award.[^o8]
