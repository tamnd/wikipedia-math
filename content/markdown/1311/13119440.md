# Michael P. Drazin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael P. Drazin"}

**Michael Peter Drazin** (born 5 June 1929) is a British and American mathematician, working in [noncommutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_algebra).

:::

## Background {#Background}

The Drazins (Дразин) were a [Russian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Jewish) family who moved to the United Kingdom in the years before [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I). Isaac Drazin founded in 1927 a well-known electrical goods shop in [Heath Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Street,_Hampstead), [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead), which existed for over 50 years.[^o4]

Isaac Drazin married Leah Wexler, and had three sons, of whom Michael was the eldest, and [Philip Drazin](wikipedia://en/46526574), also a mathematician, was the youngest, the middle son being David; and died 1 January 1993.[^o5][^o6]

## Life {#Life}

Michael Drazin was born in London on 5 June 1929.[^o8] His younger brother Philip was educated as a boarder at [St Christopher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Christopher_School), [Letchworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letchworth) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).[^o9] The self-published memoirs of Roger Atkinson, a school friend of Michael (Mike), indicate that Drazin attended [King Alfred School, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Alfred_School,_London), located in [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead), retaining contacts at the school when it was evacuated in wartime to [Royston, Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royston,_Hertfordshire); Atkinson was a boarder at St Christopher School, Letchworth from September 1942. In 1946 Atkinson and Drazin visited Paris together.[^o10]

Drazin was a student at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), graduating B.A. in 1950 and M.A. in 1953.[^o8] He was awarded a Ph.D. in 1953 for a dissertation *Contributions to Abstract Algebra* written with advisers [Robert Rankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rankin) and [David Rees](wikipedia://en/15398568).[^o11] He was a Fellow of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) from 1952 to 1956, during that period relocating to the United States.[^o12]

In the academic year 1957–8 Drazin was Visiting Lecturer at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University).[^o13] In 1958 he began a period at RIAS Inc. (the [Research Institute for Advanced Studies](wikipedia://en/25718963)) in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore) as senior scientist, after which he took a position as associate professor at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in 1962.[^o12][^o14][^o15]

## Works {#Works}

Drazin gave his name to a type of [generalized inverse](wikipedia://en/7043631) in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424) and [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory) he introduced in 1958, now known as the [Drazin inverse](wikipedia://en/13079354). It was later extended to contexts in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675).[^o17]

While at RIAS, Drazin worked with [Emilie Virginia Haynsworth](wikipedia://en/61816794), then at the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards), within its [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) program.[^o18] He also worked with the metallurgist Henry Martin Otte of RIAS, and they published a book of crystallographic tables.[^o19][^o20]
