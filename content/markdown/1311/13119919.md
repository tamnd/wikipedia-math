# Jury stability criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jury stability criterion"}

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) and [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), the **Jury stability criterion** is a method of determining the stability of a [discrete-time](wikipedia://en/40240263), [linear system](wikipedia://en/722503) by analysis of the [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) of its [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268). It is the discrete time analogue of the [Routh–Hurwitz stability criterion](wikipedia://en/1705432). The Jury [stability criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stability_criterion) requires that the system poles are located inside the unit circle centered at the origin, while the Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion requires that the poles are in the left half of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). The Jury criterion is named after [Eliahu Ibraham Jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliahu_Ibraham_Jury).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Method"}

## Method {#Method}

If the characteristic polynomial of the system is given by

$$f(z) = a_n + a_{n-1}z^1 + a_{n-2}z^2 + \dots + a_1z^{n-1} + a_0z^n$$

then the table is constructed as follows:[^o4]

$$\begin{array}{lcccccc} \underline\text{row} & \ \underline{z^n} \ & \ \underline{z^{n-1}} \ & \ \underline{z^{n-2}} \ & \ \cdots \ & \ \underline{z^1} \ & \ \underline{z^0} \ \\[8pt] 1 & a_0 & a_1 & a_2 & \cdots & a_{n-1} & a_n \\[4pt] 2 & a_n & a_{n-1} & a_{n-2} & \cdots & a_1 & a_0 \\[10pt] 3 & b_0 & b_1 & \cdots & b_{n-2} & b_{n-1} \\[4pt] 4 & b_{n-1} & b_{n-2} & \cdots & b_1 & b_0 \\[10pt] 5 & c_0 & c_1 & \cdots & c_{n-2} & \\[4pt] 6 & c_{n-2} & c_{n-3} & \cdots & c_0 & & \\[10pt] \ \!\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \\[10pt] 2n-5 \quad & p_0 & p_1 & p_2 & p_3 & & \\[4pt] 2n-4 & p_3 & p_2 & p_1 & p_0 & & \\[10pt] 2n-3 & q_2 & q_1 & q_0 & & & \end{array}$$

That is, the first row is constructed of the polynomial coefficients in order, and the second row is the first row in reverse order and conjugated.

The third row of the table is calculated by subtracting $\tfrac{a_n}{a_0}$ times the second row from the first row, and the fourth row is the third row with the first $n$ elements reversed (as the final element is zero).

$$\begin{array}{ccccc} a_0 & a_1 & \dots & a_{n-1} & \quad a_n \quad \\[4pt] a_n & a_{n-1} & \dots & a_1 & a_0 \\[4pt] a_0 - a_n \frac{a_n}{a_0} & a_1 - a_{n-1} \frac{a_n}{a_0} & \dots & a_{n-1} - a_1 \frac{a_n}{a_0} & 0 \\[4pt] a_{n-1} - a_1 \frac{a_n}{a_0} & \dots & a_1 - a_{n-1} \frac{a_n}{a_0} & a_0 - a_n \frac{a_n}{a_0} & 0 \end{array}$$

The expansion of the table is continued in this manner until a row containing only one non-zero element is reached.

Note the $\tfrac{a_n}{a_0}$ is $a_n$ for the 1st two rows. Then for 3rd and 4th row the coefficient changes (i.e. $\tfrac{b_{n-1}}{b_0}$). This can be viewed as the new polynomial which has one less [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) and then continuing.

:::

## Stability test {#Stability_test}

If ${a_0}>0$ then for every value of $a_0, b_0, c_0$... that is negative, the polynomial has one [root](wikipedia://en/264210) outside of the unit disc. This implies that the method can be stopped after the first negative value is found when checking for stability.

## Sample implementation {#Sample_implementation}

This method is very easy to implement using dynamic arrays on a computer. It also tells whether all the modulus of the roots ([complex](wikipedia://en/5826) and [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)) lie inside the unit disc. The vector $v$ contains the real coefficients of the original polynomial in the order from highest degree to lowest degree.

```cpp
        /* vvd is the jury array */
        vvd.push_back(v); // Store the first row
        reverse(v.begin(),v.end());
        vvd.push_back(v); // Store the second row

        for (i=2;;i+=2)
        {
            v.clear();
            double mult = vvd[i-2][vvd[i-2].size()-1]/vvd[i-2][0]; // This is an/a0 as mentioned in the article.

            for (j=0; j<vvd[i-2].size()-1; j++) // Take the last 2 rows and compute the next row
                   v.push_back(vvd[i-2][j] - vvd[i-1][j] * mult);

            vvd.push_back(v);
            reverse(v.begin(), v.end()); // reverse the next row
            vvd.push_back(v);
            if (v.size() == 1) break;
         }

         // Check is done using
         for (i=0; i<vvd.size(); i+=2)
         {
              if (vvd[i][0]<=0) break;
         }

         if (i == vvd.size())
              "All roots lie inside unit disc "
         else
              "no"
```
