# Level structure (algebraic geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Level structure"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **level structure** on a [space](wikipedia://en/5308894) *X* is an extra structure attached to *X* that shrinks or eliminates the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of *X*, by demanding automorphisms to preserve the level structure; attaching a level structure is often phrased as **rigidifying** the geometry of *X*.[^o2][^o3]

:::

In applications, a level structure is used in the construction of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609); a moduli space is often constructed as a quotient. The presence of automorphisms poses a difficulty to forming a [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_%28algebraic_geometry%29); thus introducing level structures helps overcome this difficulty.

There is no single definition of a level structure; rather, depending on the space *X*, one introduces the notion of a level structure. The classic one is that on an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) (see #Example: an abelian scheme). There is a level structure attached to a [formal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group) called a **Drinfeld level structure**, introduced in (Drinfeld 1974).[^o5]

## Level structures on elliptic curves {#Level_structures_on_elliptic_curves}

Classically, level structures on elliptic curves $E = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ are given by a lattice containing the defining lattice of the variety. From the moduli theory of elliptic curves, all such lattices can be described as the lattice $\mathbb{Z}\oplus \mathbb{Z}\cdot \tau$ for $\tau \in \mathfrak{h}$ in the upper-half plane. Then, the lattice generated by $1/n, \tau/n$ gives a lattice which contains all $n$-torsion points on the elliptic curve denoted $E[n]$. In fact, given such a lattice is invariant under the $\Gamma(n) \subset \text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ action on $\mathfrak{h}$, where

> $$\begin{aligned} \Gamma(n) &= \text{ker}(\text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \to \text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/n)) \\ &= \left\{ M \in \text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) : M \equiv \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \text{ (mod n)} \right\} \end{aligned}$$

hence it gives a point in $\Gamma(n)\backslash\mathfrak{h}$[^o7] called the moduli space of level N structures of elliptic curves $Y(n)$, which is a [modular curve](wikipedia://en/669552). In fact, this moduli space contains slightly more information: the [Weil pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_pairing)

> $$e_n\left(\frac{1}{n}, \frac{\tau}{n}\right) = e^{2\pi i /n}$$

gives a point in the $n$-th roots of unity, hence in $\mathbb{Z}/n$.

## Example: an abelian scheme {#Example:_an_abelian_scheme}

Let $X \to S$ be an [abelian scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_scheme) whose geometric fibers have dimension *g*.

Let *n* be a positive integer that is prime to the residue field of each *s* in *S*. For *n* ≥ 2, a **level *n*-structure** is a set of sections $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{2g}$ such that[^o9]

1. for each geometric point $s : S \to X$, $\sigma_{i}(s)$ form a basis for the group of points of order *n* in $\overline{X}_s$,
1. $m_n \circ \sigma_i$ is the identity section, where $m_n$ is the multiplication by *n*.

See also: [modular curve#Examples](wikipedia://en/669552#Examples), [moduli stack of elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/49905706).
