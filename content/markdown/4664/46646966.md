# Thomas N. Hibbard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas N. Hibbard"}

**Thomas Nathaniel Hibbard** (March 14, 1929 – February 11, 2016) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist).

:::

Thomas N. Hibbard received the B.S. degree in physics from [Pacific University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_University), Forest Grove, OR, in 1951, the M.S. degree in mathematics from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois), Urbana, in 1954, and the Ph.D. degree in mathematics from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), in 1966.

From 1955 to 1958 T. N. Hibbard was a Scientific Programmer at the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation), Santa Monica, CA, programming the [JOHNNIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JOHNNIAC), an early computer built by Rand, and from 1959 to 1965 a member of the research staff of the [System Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Development_Corporation), Santa Monica, CA, where he worked with [Seymour Ginsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Ginsburg) and [Joseph Ullian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ullian) in [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356) and [formal languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_languages). Following a three-year visiting faculty appointment at the Catholic University of [Salta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salta), [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina), he joined the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California), Los Angeles, as an assistant professor of Computer Science in 1970. He conducted research in searching, [sorting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellsort#Gap_sequences), and [data structures](wikipedia://en/22045750), helping to pioneer the field of analysis of algorithms.H62H63 In 1974, he started research with his then faculty colleague [Armin B. Cremers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_B._Cremers), initiating the theory and applications of data spaces.CH In February 1976, he joined the staff of the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory), Pasadena, CA, working on the [Voyager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_program), [IRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRAS) and [Galileo project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_project) until his retirement from JPL in 1986. At that time, he joined the [Information Sciences Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Sciences_Institute) (ISI), Marina del Rey, CA, and did experimental research on [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing) until 1989, when he returned to Salta, Argentina, to teach at the [National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Salta) (UNSA).

## Contributions to Computer Science {#Contributions_to_Computer_Science}

Hibbard was a co-inventor of the [binary search tree](wikipedia://en/4320). He was the first to propose the so-called Hibbard deletion for binary search trees, in which the node to be deleted is replaced by its successor.[^o3] Hibbard proposed one of several widely cited [increment sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellsort#Gap_sequences) for the [Shellsort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellsort) algorithm.
