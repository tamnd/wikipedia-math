# Daniel Slotnick

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Slotnick"}

**Daniel Leonid Slotnick** (November 12, 1931 – October 25, 1985) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer architect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_architecture).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Slotnick studied Mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), where he obtained a Bachelor's in 1951 and a Master's in 1952.

In 1956 he obtained a PhD at the [Courant Institute](wikipedia://en/1617601) of Mathematical Sciences of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), supervised by [Jürgen K. Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jürgen_K._Moser) and [Kurt Otto Friedrichs](wikipedia://en/3626171).

He worked as professor of computer science at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign)

He was awarded the [AFIPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Information_Processing_Societies) Prize in 1962 and was elected an [IEEE Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_fellows_of_the_IEEE) in 1976.

Slotnick died in [Baltimore, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland), on October 25, 1985, age 53, from an apparent heart attack while jogging.[^o4][^o5]

In 1985, when the [Institute for Defense Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses) and [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) formed their supercomputing research facility in the [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) area, Slotnick's widow donated his library to them. In 1987 the first issue of *[The Journal of Supercomputing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_of_Supercomputing)* contained a tribute to Slotnick.

## Research activities {#Research_activities}

Slotnick, in papers published with [John Cocke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cocke_%28computer_scientist%29) in 1958, discussed the use of [parallelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing) in numerical calculations for the first time. He later served as the chief architect of the [ILLIAC IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILLIAC_IV) supercomputer.[^o7]

He was the principal investigator on a [DARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) contract in the early 1970s that produced the ILLIAC IV and the [ARPANET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARPANET). It was a fairly large operation, with its own building on the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) campus, originally called the Center for Advanced Computation but which is now the Astronomy Building. ILLIAC IV was constructed by the [Burroughs Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burroughs_Corporation), using special chips made by [Fairchild Semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Semiconductor). Because of campus unrest due to the [Vietnam war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War), and [the Mansfield amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mansfield#The_Mansfield_Amendments), the ILLIAC IV was completed and installed at [Ames Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_Research_Center) instead of the University of Illinois, and Slotnick's DARPA contract was not renewed.

## ARPANET {#ARPANET}

Most of the development of the ARPANET took place at [MIT Lincoln Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Lincoln_Laboratory) and [BBN Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon_BBN). However, it was planned that ILLIAC IV would be on the network, and some work was funded by Slotnick's DARPA contract. For example, a standard character set was established, and also the [Purdy polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._Purdy#Purdy_polynomial), a secure hash function to protect passwords on ARPANET. Ironically, when the ILLIAC IV project was moved to [Ames Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_Research_Center), the computer could only be accessed by telephone.
