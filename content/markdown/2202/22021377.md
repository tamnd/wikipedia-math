# Theodore Wilbur Anderson

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Theodore Wilbur Anderson"}

**Theodore Wilbur Anderson** (June 5, 1918 – September 17, 2016) was an American mathematician and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who specialized in the analysis of [multivariate data](wikipedia://en/19384).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

He was born in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis), Minnesota.[^o5] He was on the faculty of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) from 1946 until moving to [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1967, becoming emeritus professor in 1988. He served as editor of *[Annals of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/23772805)* from 1950 to 1952. He was elected president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1962.

Anderson's 1958[^o6] textbook,*An Introduction to Multivariate Analysis*,[^o7] educated a generation of theorists and applied statisticians; Anderson's book emphasizes [hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis_testing) via [likelihood ratio tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likelihood_ratio_test) and the properties of [power functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power): [Admissibility](wikipedia://en/1776726), [unbiasedness](wikipedia://en/8450479) and [monotonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonicity).[^o8][^o9]

Anderson is also known for [Anderson–Darling test](wikipedia://en/3479881) of whether there is evidence that a given sample of data did not arise from a given probability distribution.

He also framed the [Anderson–Bahadur algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson–Bahadur_algorithm)[^o10] along with [Raghu Raj Bahadur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghu_Raj_Bahadur), which is used in statistics and engineering for solving binary classification problems when the underlying data have [multivariate normal distributions](wikipedia://en/50347) with different [covariance matrices](wikipedia://en/191752).

He was a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o11]

Anderson died in September 2016 at the age of 98 in Stanford, California after experiencing heart problems.[^o12]
