# Boolean model (probability theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boolean model"}

*Image: Boolean model.svg.* Realization of Boolean model with random-radii discs.

For [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Boolean-Poisson model** or simply **Boolean model** for a random subset of the plane (or higher dimensions, analogously) is one of the simplest and most tractable models in [stochastic geometry](wikipedia://en/22037813). Take a [Poisson point process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) of rate $\lambda$ in the plane and make each point be the center of a random set; the resulting union of overlapping sets is a realization of the Boolean model ${\mathcal B}$. More precisely, the parameters are $\lambda$ and a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on compact sets; for each point $\xi$ of the Poisson point process we pick a set $C_\xi$ from the distribution, and then define ${\mathcal B}$ as the union $\cup_\xi (\xi + C_\xi)$ of translated sets.

:::

To illustrate tractability with one simple formula, the mean density of ${\mathcal B}$ equals $1 - \exp(- \lambda A)$ where $\Gamma$ denotes the area of $C_\xi$ and $A=\operatorname{E} (\Gamma).$ The classical theory of [stochastic geometry](wikipedia://en/22037813) develops many further formulae. [^o2][^o3]

As related topics, the case of constant-sized discs is the basic model of [continuum percolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_percolation_theory)[^o4] and the low-density Boolean models serve as a first-order approximations in the study of extremes in many models.[^o5]
