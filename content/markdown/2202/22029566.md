# Tevian Dray

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tevian Dray"}

**Tevian Dray** (born March 17, 1956) is an American mathematician who has worked in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and both [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_education) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/326471) education. He was elected a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) in 2010.

:::

He has primarily worked in the area of classical general relativity. His research results include confirmation of the existence of solutions of [Einstein's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein's_equation) containing [gravitational radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_radiation), the use of [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805) to classify exact solutions of Einstein's equation, an analysis of a class of gravitational shock waves (including one of the few known exact [2-body solutions](wikipedia://en/277468) in general relativity), and the study of [signature](wikipedia://en/463225) change, a possible model for the [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116). More recently, his work has focused on applications of the [octonions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octonions) to the theory of fundamental particles.

He was a graduate student under [Rainer K. Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_K._Sachs) at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1981, although much of his dissertation research was done in collaboration with [Abhay Ashtekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhay_Ashtekar). The context of his dissertation, titled *The Asymptotic Structure of a Family of Einstein-Maxwell Solutions* focused on families of [spacetimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetimes) which describe accelerating black holes, and which contain gravitational radiation. This demonstrated the existence of exact radiating solutions to the Einstein field equations.[^o4]

He is currently a professor of mathematics at [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University). In addition to his ongoing work in mathematical physics, he has made significant contributions in science education, where he directs the Vector Calculus Bridge Project,[^o5] an attempt to teach vector calculus the way it is used by scientists and engineers, and is part of the development team of the Paradigms Project,[^o6] a complete restructuring of the undergraduate physics major around several core "paradigms". He has written a book[^o7] on special relativity and a sequel on general relativity using differential forms,[^o8] and is coauthor of *[The Geometry of the Octonions](wikipedia://en/70684813)*, released in 2015.[^o9]

In 2017 he received a [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o3]
