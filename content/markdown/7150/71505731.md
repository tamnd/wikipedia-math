# Frederick R. Cohen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frederick R. Cohen"}

**Frederick Ronald Cohen**[^o3] (1945 – January 16, 2022)[^o4][^o5] was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Fred Cohen was born in 1945 in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago). He received a BA from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in 1967 and a PhD from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1972. He taught at the [University of Northern Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Northern_Illinois) until 1979 and then at the [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky). In 1989, he settled at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester), where he spent the rest of his career.[^o4][^o7]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Cohen did influential work in several areas of homotopy theory. His thesis concerned the topology of [configuration spaces](wikipedia://en/54981574), a topic he came back to throughout his life, with connections to [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138) and [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295). This was followed by a series of influential papers on unstable [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697) with [John Moore](wikipedia://en/13433862) and [Joseph Neisendorfer](wikipedia://en/54960470).[^o9] Late in his life, Cohen studied [polyhedral products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_product) in a series of articles with Bahri, Bendersky, and [Gitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Gitler_Hammer).[^o10]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In the mid-1970s, Cohen battled a [spinal tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_tumor). Although he survived with the help of [radiation therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_therapy), he was partially paralyzed for the rest of his life. Starting in 2013, he used a wheelchair.[^o4]

Cohen was survived by his wife Kathleen and two daughters.[^o5]
