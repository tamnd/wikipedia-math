# Roberto Conti (mathematician)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Roberto Conti"}

**Roberto Conti** (23 April 1923 – 30 August 2006) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who contributed to the theory of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753)[^o4] and the development of the [comparison method](wikipedia://en/6626644).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Roberto Conti was born in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) on 29 April 1923. He obtained his M.Sc. and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa), under the supervision, respectively, of [Leonida Tonelli](wikipedia://en/10018991) (replaced, after his premature death, by [Emilio Baiada](wikipedia://en/44228628)) and [Giovanni Sansone](wikipedia://en/34941945). Conti’s M.Sc. and Ph.D. dissertations dealt with [translation surfaces](wikipedia://en/33418730) (possibly a topic suggested by Tonelli after knowing about some Russian works) and particular aspects of the [Cauchy problem](wikipedia://en/1725027). Later he held the position of research assistant to the chair of Sansone at the [University of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florence). Their collaboration was fruitful and resulted in numerous articles, as well as the book (Sansone & Conti 1964),(Sansone Conti 1964) originally published in [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language), which was translated into a number of languages and became one of the standard texts on the subject in the 1960s.[^o4] In 1956 Conti became full professor at the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania), holding the chair of mathematical analysis until 1958, when he returned to [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florence).[^o8] In 1963-1964 he held a visiting professorship at the [Research Institute for Advanced Studies](wikipedia://en/25718963) (RIAS) in Baltimore, Maryland.

His research focused on several topics, which often overlapped in the time and contributed to motivate each other. A leading theme was constituted by [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and its applications to the theory of [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equations), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and [control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_systems).[^o4] An impulse to do research on control systems was most probably given by his discussions with the Russian engineer and mathematician [Nicolas Minorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Minorsky), who first proposed application of control theory to the [automatic steering of ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-steering_gear). Minorsky, after the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution), moved to U.S. and later to southern France: during that period he came frequently to Florence, to give seminars and exchange mathematical ideas with Sansone and Conti. On another hand, Conti’s contributions to the development of the [comparison method](wikipedia://en/6626644) for the qualitative analysis of differential equations were particularly prominent.[^o5] He was a corresponding member of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) since 1983 and a full member since 1994,[^o9] a foreign honorary member of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy) since 1997[^o10] and was also a member of the editorial board of the [Journal of Differential Equations](wikipedia://en/84067337) since its inception in 1964 until his death in 2006.[^o4]
