# Leo Corry

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Leo Corry"}

**Leo Corry** (ליאו קורי; born 1956 in [Santiago de Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Chile))[^o3] is an Israeli [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_mathematics).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Corry migrated with his Jewish family to [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela) when he was two years old. He attended primary and secondary school at [Colegio Moral y Luces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colegio_Moral_y_Luces) of [Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas) and then at the [Universidad Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simón_Bolívar_University_%28Venezuela%29) studied mathematics with licentiate's degree in 1977. He attended graduate school at the [University of Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tel_Aviv). There he received in 1983 his master's degree in mathematics with master's thesis *Splitting data in cohomology classes*, supervised by [Shmuel Rosset](wikipedia://en/30373123), and in 1990 his Ph.D. in the history of science with Ph.D. thesis *The origins of category theory as a mathematical discipline*, supervised by [Sabetai Unguru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabetai_Unguru) and Shmuel Rosset. At the University of Tel Aviv, Corry became in 1985 an instructor, in 1996 a lecturer, in 2004 an associate professor and from 2007 a full professor in the Cohn Institute for the History of Science. He was the Director of the Cohn Institute from 2003 to 2009, Director of the Yavetz School of Historical Studies from 2013 to 2015, and Dean of Humanities at TAU between 2015 and 2020. In the academic year 1994–1995, he was fellow at the [Dibner Institute for the History of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibner_Institute_for_the_History_of_Science_and_Technology) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), for the academic year 1995–1996 at the [Max Planck Institute for the History of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_the_History_of_Science) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) and in 2006 at the [Wissenschaftskolleg zu Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wissenschaftskolleg_zu_Berlin).

Corry has done research on the development of modern [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) (including [computational number theory](wikipedia://en/511466) done by [Harry Vandiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Vandiver), [D. H. Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._H._Lehmer), and [Emma Lehmer](wikipedia://en/7682439)) and the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) (including the [Bourbaki School](wikipedia://en/167394)). His research has also dealt with [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537), [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and his school, the history of Latin American science, and [Jorge Luis Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Borges).[^o6] With [John Stachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stachel) and [Jürgen Renn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jürgen_Renn), Corry discovered new documents concerning the [priority dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity_priority_dispute) between Hilbert and Einstein, and these new documents support Einstein against Hilbert.[^o7][^o8]

In 2006, Corry was an invited speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid) with a talk *On the origin of Hilbert's sixth problem: physics and the empiricist approach to axiomatization*.[^o9]

From 1999 to 2009 and from 2011 to 2013, he was editor of *[Science in Context](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_in_Context)*.
