# Angelo Genocchi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Angelo Genocchi"}

**Angelo Genocchi** (5 March 1817 – 7 March 1889) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He worked with [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043). The [Genocchi numbers](wikipedia://en/4992862) are named after him.

:::

## Life and Works {#Life_and_Works}

Angelo Genocchi was born and grew up and went to school in [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza), Italy. Despite his love of mathematics, he studied law at [Piacenza University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza_University). After practicing law in Piacenza for a number of years, he was invited by the university to become the Chair of Law. He was mostly unsuccessful as a teacher however and disliked by many of his students.

Angelo Genocchi was part of a group who participated in the attempted overthrow of the Austro-Hungarian government in [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza). The revolution was unsuccessful.

After the revolution Genocchi moved to [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), where he took up [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) as the subject of his research and teaching. He became President of the Academy of Sciences of [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin). It was there that he worked with [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043). His biggest discovery was the [Genocchi numbers](wikipedia://en/4992862), named after him.

After years of ill-health, he died in 1889, aged 72.
