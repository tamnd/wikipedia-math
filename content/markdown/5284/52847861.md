# Marc Stevens (cryptology)

Biographies

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marc Stevens"}

**Dr. ir. Marc Stevens** is a [cryptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptology) researcher most known for his work on cryptographic [hash collisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hash_collisions) and for the creation of the chosen-prefix hash collision tool [HashClash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HashClash) as part of his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis).[^o4] He first gained international attention for his work with [Alexander Sotirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Sotirov), [Jacob Appelbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Appelbaum), [Arjen Lenstra](wikipedia://en/6850292), [David Molnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Molnar), [Dag Arne Osvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dag_Arne_Osvik), and [Benne de Weger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benne_de_Weger) in creating a rogue [SSL certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSL_certificate) which was presented in 2008 during the 25th annual [Chaos Communication Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_Communication_Congress) warning of the dangers of using the [MD5](wikipedia://en/18826) hash function in issuing [SSL certificates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSL_certificates).[^o5] Several years later in 2012, according to [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft), the authors of the [Flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flame) malware used similar methodology to that which the researchers warned of by initiating an MD5 collision to forge a Windows code-signing certificate.[^o6] Marc was most recently awarded the [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) Security Privacy and Anti-abuse applied award. Google selected Stevens for this award in recognition of his work in Cryptanalysis, in particular related to the SHA-1 hash function.[^o7]

:::

In February 2017, the first known successful [SHA-1](wikipedia://en/26672) [collision attack](wikipedia://en/969624) in practice (termed "[SHAttered](wikipedia://en/26672#SHAttered_–_first_public_collision)") was recognized. Marc Stevens was first-credited in the subsequent paper[^o8] along with [CWI Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CWI_Amsterdam) colleague Pierre Karpman, and researchers Elie Bursztein, Ange Albertini, Yarik Markov, Alex Petit Bianco, Clement Baisse[^o9] from [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google).

Marc is currently employed as a Cryptology Researcher at [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063).[^o10]
