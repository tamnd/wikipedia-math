# X̅ and s chart

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="X̅ and s chart"}

In [statistical quality control](wikipedia://en/593680), the **$\bar x$ and s chart** is a type of [control chart](wikipedia://en/435754) used to monitor [variables data](wikipedia://en/30423282) when samples are collected at regular intervals from a [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process) or [industrial process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_industrial_processes).[^o3] This is connected to traditional [statistical quality control](wikipedia://en/593680) (SQC) and statistical process control (SPC). However, Woodall[^o4] noted that "I believe that the use of control charts and other monitoring methods should be referred to as “statistical process monitoring,” not “statistical process control (SPC).”"

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

The chart is advantageous in the following situations:[^o7]

1. The sample size is relatively large (say, n > 10—[$\bar x$ and R charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbar_and_R_chart) are typically used for smaller sample sizes)
1. The sample size is variable
1. Computers can be used to ease the burden of calculation

The "chart" actually consists of a pair of charts: One to monitor the process standard deviation and another to monitor the process mean, as is done with the [$\bar x$ and R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbar_and_R_chart) and [individuals control charts](wikipedia://en/11999755). The $\bar x$ and s chart plots the mean value for the quality characteristic across all units in the sample, $\bar x_i$, plus the standard deviation of the quality characteristic across all units in the sample as follows:

$$s = \sqrt{\frac {\sum_{i=1}^n {\left ( x_i - \bar x \right )}^2}{n - 1}}.$$

:::

## Assumptions {#Assumptions}

The [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) is the basis for the charts and requires the following assumptions:

- The quality characteristic to be monitored is adequately modeled by a [normally-distributed](wikipedia://en/21462) [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685)
- The parameters μ and σ for the random variable are the same for each unit and each unit is independent of its predecessors or successors
- The inspection procedure is same for each sample and is carried out consistently from sample to sample

## Control limits {#Control_limits}

The control limits for this chart type are:[^o10]

- $B_3 \bar s$ (lower) and $B_4 \bar s$ (upper) for monitoring the process variability
- $\bar x \pm A_3 \bar s$ for monitoring the process mean

where $\bar x$ and $\bar s = \frac {\sum_{i=1}^m s_i}{m}$ are the estimates of the long-term process mean and range established during control-chart setup and A<sub>3</sub>, B<sub>3</sub>, and B<sub>4</sub> are sample size-specific [anti-biasing](wikipedia://en/10240807) constants. The anti-biasing constants are typically found in the appendices of textbooks on [statistical process control](wikipedia://en/593680). NIST provides guidance on manually calculating these constants "6.3.2. What are Variables Control Charts?". <https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc32.htm#C4>

## Validity {#Validity}

As with the [$\bar x$ and R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbar_and_R_chart) and [individuals control charts](wikipedia://en/11999755), the $\bar x$ chart is only valid if the within-sample variability is constant.[^o13] Thus, the s chart is examined before the $\bar x$ chart; if the s chart indicates the sample variability is in statistical control, then the $\bar x$ chart is examined to determine if the sample mean is also in statistical control. If on the other hand, the sample variability is *not* in statistical control, then the entire process is judged to be not in statistical control regardless of what the $\bar x$ chart indicates.

## Unequal samples {#Unequal_samples}

When samples collected from the process are of unequal sizes (arising from a mistake in collecting them, for example), there are two approaches:

::: {#o15 .table cue=table}

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| Use variable-width control limits[^o16] | Each observation plots against its own control limits as determined by the sample size-specific values, n<sub>i</sub>, of A<sub>3</sub>, B<sub>3</sub>, and B<sub>4</sub> |
| Use control limits based on an average sample size[^o17] | Control limits are fixed at the [modal](wikipedia://en/1432127) (or most common) sample size-specific value of A<sub>3</sub>, B<sub>3</sub>, and B<sub>4</sub> |

:::

## Limitations and improvements {#Limitations_and_improvements}

Effect of estimation of parameters plays a major role. Also a change in variance affects the performance of $\bar{X}$chart while a shift in mean affects the performance of the S chart.

Therefore, several authors recommend using a single chart that can simultaneously monitor $\bar{X}$and S.[^o19] McCracken, Chackrabori and Mukherjee [^o20] developed one of the most modern and efficient approach for jointly monitoring the [Gaussian process](wikipedia://en/302944) parameters, using a set of reference sample in absence of any knowledge of true process parameters.
