# Ruth King (statistician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth King"}

**Ruth King** (née **Langham**[^o9]) [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) [FLSW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_Wales) is the current Thomas Bayes' Chair of Statistics in the School of Mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), having held the position since 2015. Prior to this she held positions at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) and the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews).

:::

Her main research interests are "Capture-recapture; [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571); hidden (semi-)[Markov models](wikipedia://en/22022581); [state-space models](wikipedia://en/548156); missing data; applications in [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) and [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997)".[^o3]

> Her research focuses on the development of novel statistical techniques applied to a range of different application areas, particularly within ecology, epidemiology or medicine. This research is often interdisciplinary in nature, relying on strong collaborative links with other researchers.[^o4]

She was recognised by the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) as one of Scotland's leading female scientists in their exhibition[^o5] and elected as a Fellow in 2018. She was also elected a fellow of the [Learned Society of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_Wales) in 2017,[^o6] and a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 2022.[^o7]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

She graduated with a BSc in Mathematics with Statistics in 1998,[^o9] and completed a PhD in 2001,[^o10] both from the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol).[^o3] In her final year of undergraduate she was awarded the 'Henry Ronald Hasse Prize'.[^o9] Her PhD was supervised by [Steve Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Brooks_%28statistician%29).[^o10]

Following her PhD she worked as a research associate at the University of Cambridge, from 2001 to 2003.[^o3]

From 2003 to 2005, she was an [EPSRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_and_Physical_Sciences_Research_Council) post-doctoral fellow.[^o3]

From 2003 to 2010, she was a lecturer in Statistics at the University of St Andrews, then a reader in Statistics from 2010 to 2015.[^o3]

Currently, she is the Thomas Bayes' Chair of Statistics at the University of Edinburgh, a post she has held since 2015.[^o3]

She has spoken at numerous Conferences, in a number of countries, throughout her career.[^o3]

In 2019, she co-organised the [ICMS](wikipedia://en/8298904) workshop on "Addressing Statistical Challenges of Modern Technological Advances" as well as the "Global Expert Working Group on Multiple Systems Estimation of Modern Slavery" in Edinburgh.[^o3]

She has 69 publications on "Google Scholar" since 2001, with a total of 1370 citations since 2014 (as of June 2019), and 2213 citations overall.[^o11]

Since 2017 she has been part of the team delivering the University of Edinburgh's 'Unlocking the World of Data' course on [edX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EdX)[^o12]
