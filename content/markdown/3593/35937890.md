# Locally compact field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally compact field"}

In algebra, a **locally compact field** is a [topological field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_field) whose topology forms a [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637).[^o2] These kinds of fields were originally introduced in [p-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327) since the fields $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) are locally compact topological spaces constructed from the norm $|\cdot|_p$ on $\mathbb{Q}$. The topology (and [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) structure) is essential because it allows one to construct analogues of [algebraic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822) in the p-adic context.

:::

With the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology), all [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_fields) are [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and therefore locally compact. A locally compact field that is not discrete is known as a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375).

## Structure {#Structure}

### Finite dimensional vector spaces {#Finite_dimensional_vector_spaces}

One of the useful structure theorems for [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over locally compact fields is that the finite dimensional vector spaces have only one [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of norms: the [sup norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sup_norm).[^o5] <sup>pg. 58-59</sup>

### Finite field extensions {#Finite_field_extensions}

Given a [finite field extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field_extension) $K/F$ over a locally compact field $F$, there is at most one unique field norm $|\cdot|_K$ on $K$ extending the field norm $|\cdot|_F$; that is,

> $$|f|_K = |f|_F$$

for all $f\in K$ which is in the image of $F \hookrightarrow K$. Note this follows from the previous theorem and the following trick: if $\|\cdot\|_1,\|\cdot\|_2$ are two equivalent norms, and

> $$\|x\|_1 < \|x\|_2$$

then for a fixed constant $c_1$ there exists an $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

> $$\left(\frac{\|x\|_1}{\|x\|_2} \right)^N < \frac{1}{c_1}$$

for all $N \geq N_0$ since the sequence generated from the powers of $N$ converge to $0$.

#### Finite Galois extensions {#Finite_Galois_extensions}

If the extension is of degree $n = [K:F]$ and $K/F$ is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064), (so all solutions to the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106), or [conjugate elements](wikipedia://en/458467), of any $a \in K$ are also contained in $K$) then the unique field norm $|\cdot|_K$ can be constructed using the [field norm](wikipedia://en/450555)[^o5] <sup>pg. 61</sup>. This is defined as

> $$|a|_K = |N_{K/F}(a)|^{1/n}$$

Note the n-th root is required in order to have a well-defined field norm extending the one over $F$ since given any $f \in K$ in the image of $F \hookrightarrow K$ its norm is

> $$N_{K/F}(f) = \det m_f = f^n$$

since it acts as [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373) on the $F$-vector space $K$.

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Finite fields {#Finite_fields}

All [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) are locally compact since they can be equipped with the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology). In particular, any field with the discrete topology is locally compact since every point is the neighborhood of itself, and also the closure of the neighborhood, hence is compact.

### Local fields {#Local_fields}

The main examples of locally compact fields are the p-adic rationals $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and finite extensions $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$. Each of these are examples of [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375). Note the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ and its completion $\mathbb{C}_p$ are **not** locally compact fields[^o5] <sup>pg. 72</sup> with their standard topology.

#### Field extensions of Q<sub>p</sub> {#Field_extensions_of_Qp}

Field extensions $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ can be found by using [Hensel's lemma](wikipedia://en/1633368). For example, $f(x) = x^2 - 7 = x^2 - (2 + 1\cdot 5 )$ has no solutions in $\mathbb{Q}_5$ since

> $$\frac{d}{dx}(x^2 - 5) = 2x$$

only equals zero mod $p$ if $x \equiv 0 \text{ } (p)$, but $x^2 - 7$ has no solutions mod $5$. Hence $\mathbb{Q}_5(\sqrt{7})/\mathbb{Q}_5$ is a quadratic field extension.
