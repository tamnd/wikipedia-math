# Irrationality sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Irrationality sequence"}

In mathematics, a [sequence of positive integers](wikipedia://en/51421) *a*<sub>*n*</sub> is called an **irrationality sequence** if it has the property that for every sequence *x*<sub>*n*</sub> of positive integers, the sum of the series

:::

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{a_n x_n}$$

exists (that is, it [converges](wikipedia://en/26551602)) and is an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689).[^o2][^o3] The problem of characterizing irrationality sequences was posed by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Ernst G. Straus](wikipedia://en/6920898), who originally called the property of being an irrationality sequence "Property P".[^o4]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [powers of two whose exponents are powers of two](wikipedia://en/376948#Powers_of_two_whose_exponents_are_powers_of_two), $2^{2^n}$, form an irrationality sequence. However, although [Sylvester's sequence](wikipedia://en/4535485)

2, 3, 7, 43, 1807, 3263443, ...

(in which each term is one more than the product of all previous terms) also grows [doubly exponentially](wikipedia://en/8541166), it does not form an irrationality sequence. For, letting $x_n=1$ for all $n$ gives

$$\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{7}+\frac{1}{43}+\cdots = 1,$$

a series converging to a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024). Likewise, the [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606), $n!$, do not form an irrationality sequence because the sequence given by $x_n=n+2$ for all $n$ leads to a series with a rational sum,

$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+2)n!}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{30}+\frac{1}{144}+\cdots=1.$[^o2]

:::

## Growth rate {#Growth_rate}

For any sequence *a*<sub>*n*</sub> to be an irrationality sequence, it must grow at a rate such that

$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{\log\log a_n}{n} \geq \log 2$.[^o8]

This includes sequences that grow at a more than doubly exponential rate as well as some doubly exponential sequences that grow more quickly than the powers of powers of two.[^o2]

Every irrationality sequence must grow quickly enough that

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n^{1/n}=\infty.$$

However, it is not known whether there exists such a sequence in which the [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354) of each pair of terms is 1 (unlike the powers of powers of two) and for which

$\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n^{1/2^n}<\infty.$[^o9]

## Related properties {#Related_properties}

Analogously to irrationality sequences, (Hančl 1996) has defined a transcendental sequence to be an integer sequence *a*<sub>*n*</sub> such that, for every sequence *x*<sub>*n*</sub> of positive integers, the sum of the series

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{a_n x_n}$$

exists and is a [transcendental number](wikipedia://en/30325).[^o12]
