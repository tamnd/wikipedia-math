# Arthur Sard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Sard"}

**Arthur Sard** (28 July 1909, [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) – 31 August 1980, [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel)) was an American mathematician, famous for his work in [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) and in [spline interpolation](wikipedia://en/550633). His fame stems primarily from [Sard's theorem](wikipedia://en/914901), which says that the set of [critical values](wikipedia://en/1951424) of a differential function which has sufficiently many derivatives has measure zero.[^o19]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Arthur Sard was born and grew up in New York City and spent most of his adult life there. He attended the [Friends Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_Seminary), a private school in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan), and went to college at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he received in 1931 his bachelor's degree, in 1932 his master's degree, and in 1936 his PhD under the direction of [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523).[^o19] Sard's PhD thesis has the title *The measure of the critical values of functions*.[^o3] He was a member of the first faculty members at the then newly founded [Queens College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_College,_City_University_of_New_York), where he worked from 1937 to 1970.[^o19]

During WWII Sard worked as a member, under the auspices of the [Applied Mathematics Panel](wikipedia://en/4078986), of the *Applied Mathematics Group* of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (AMG-C), especially in support of [fire control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system) for machine guns mounted on [bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber). [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) wrote concerning Sard: “His judicious judgments kept AMG-C on a straight course, \[…\]”.[^o4]

Sard retired as professor emeritus in 1970 at Queens College and then worked at [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla), where he spent five years as a research associate in the mathematics department of the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego). In 1975 he went to [Binningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binningen,_Switzerland) near [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel) and taught at various European universities and research institutes. In 1978 he accepted an invitation from the Soviet Academy of Sciences to be a guest lecturer. In 1978 and 1979 he was a guest professor at the [University of Siegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Siegen). Arthur Sard died on 31 August 1980 in Basel.[^o19]

From 1938 until his death Sard published almost forty research articles in [refereed mathematical journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_journal).[^o20] Also he wrote two [monographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph): in 1963 the book *Linear Approximation* and in 1971, in collaboration with Sol Weintraub, *A Book of Splines*.[^o20] According to the book review from the [Deutsche Mathematiker-Vereinigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mathematiker-Vereinigung) the content-rich („inhaltsreiche“) *Linear Approximation* is an important contribution to the theory of approximation of integrals, derivatives, function values, and sums („ein wesentlicher Beitrag zur Theorie der Approximation von Integralen, Ableitungen, Funktionswerten und Summen“).[^o5]
