# Singular term

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Singular term"}

**Singular terms** are as expressions that purport to denote or designate particular individual people, places, or other objects. They contrast with *[general terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_term)* (such as "car" or "chair") which can apply to more than one thing.[^o2]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

There are various kinds of singular terms: [proper names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_noun) (e.g. "Matthew"), [definite descriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_description) (e.g. "the second fisherman in the boat"), singular [personal pronouns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_pronoun) (e.g. "she"), [demonstrative pronouns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstrative_pronoun) (e.g. "this"), etc.

Historically, various definitions for "singular term" have been offered:

1. A term that tells us which individual is being talked about. ([John Stuart Mill](wikipedia://en/15626), [Arthur Prior](wikipedia://en/308764), [P. F. Strawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._F._Strawson)) [^o4]
1. A term that is grammatically singular, i.e. a [proper name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_name) (*proprium nomen*), a [demonstrative pronoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstrative_pronoun) (*pronomen demonstrativum*) or a demonstrative pronoun with a common name (*cum termino communi*). ([William of Ockham](wikipedia://en/33617))[^o5]
1. A term that is inherently *about* the object to which it applies or [refers](wikipedia://en/20110874). ([Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416)) [^o6]
1. A term that is true "in the same sense" of only one object. ([Peter of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_of_Spain_%28author%29))[^o7]
