# Rank of an abelian group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rank of an abelian group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **rank**, **Prüfer rank**, or **torsion-free rank** of an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *A* is the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of a maximal [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) subset.[^o2] The rank of *A* determines the size of the largest [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) contained in *A*. If *A* is [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/3039330) then it embeds into a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers) of dimension rank *A*. For [finitely generated abelian groups](wikipedia://en/58527), rank is a strong invariant and every such group is determined up to isomorphism by its rank and [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052). [Torsion-free abelian groups of rank 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_abelian_groups_of_rank_1) have been completely classified. However, the theory of abelian groups of higher rank is more involved.

:::

The term rank has a different meaning in the context of [elementary abelian groups](wikipedia://en/4843202).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A subset {*a*<sub>*α*</sub>} of an abelian group *A* is **[linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent)** (over **Z**) if the only linear combination of these elements that is equal to zero is trivial: if

$$\sum_\alpha n_\alpha a_\alpha = 0, \quad n_\alpha\in\mathbb{Z},$$

where all but finitely many coefficients *n*<sub>*α*</sub> are zero (so that the sum is, in effect, finite), then all coefficients are zero. Any two maximal linearly independent sets in *A* have the same [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174), which is called the **rank** of *A*.

The rank of an abelian group is analogous to the [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_space_dimension) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). The main difference with the case of vector space is a presence of [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330). An element of an abelian group *A* is a torsion element if its [order](wikipedia://en/493527) is finite. The set of all torsion elements is a subgroup, called the [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052) and denoted *T*(*A*). A group is called torsion-free if it has no non-trivial torsion elements. The factor-group *A*/*T*(*A*) is the unique maximal torsion-free quotient of *A* and its rank coincides with the rank of *A*.

The notion of rank with analogous properties can be defined for [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over any [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), the case of abelian groups corresponding to modules over **Z**. For this, see [finitely generated module#Generic rank](wikipedia://en/326430#Generic_rank).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The rank of an abelian group *A* coincides with the dimension of the **Q**-vector space *A* ⊗ **Q**. If *A* is torsion-free then the canonical map *A* → *A* ⊗ **Q** is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) and the rank of *A* is the minimum dimension of **Q**-vector space containing *A* as an abelian subgroup. In particular, any intermediate group **Z**<sup>*n*</sup> \< *A* \< **Q**<sup>*n*</sup> has rank *n*.
- Abelian groups of rank 0 are exactly the [periodic abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_group).
- The group **Q** of rational numbers has rank 1. [Torsion-free abelian groups of rank 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_abelian_groups_of_rank_1) are realized as subgroups of **Q** and there is a satisfactory classification of them up to isomorphism. By contrast, there is no satisfactory classification of torsion-free abelian groups of rank 2.[^o7]
- Rank is additive over [short exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence): if

$$0\to A\to B\to C\to 0\;$$

is a short exact sequence of abelian groups then rk *B* = rk *A* + rk *C*. This follows from the [flatness](wikipedia://en/492616) of **Q** and the corresponding fact for vector spaces.

- Rank is additive over arbitrary [direct sums](wikipedia://en/2084687):

$$\operatorname{rank}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J}A_j\right) = \sum_{j\in J}\operatorname{rank}(A_j),$$

where the sum in the right hand side uses [cardinal arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_arithmetic).

:::

## Groups of higher rank {#Groups_of_higher_rank}

Abelian groups of rank greater than 1 are sources of interesting examples. For instance, for every cardinal *d* there exist torsion-free abelian groups of rank *d* that are [indecomposable](wikipedia://en/810549), i.e. cannot be expressed as a direct sum of a pair of their proper subgroups. These examples demonstrate that torsion-free abelian group of rank greater than 1 cannot be simply built by direct sums from torsion-free abelian groups of rank 1, whose theory is well understood. Moreover, for every integer $n\ge 3$, there is a torsion-free abelian group of rank $2n-2$ that is simultaneously a sum of two indecomposable groups, and a sum of *n* indecomposable groups. Hence even the number of indecomposable summands of a group of an even rank greater or equal than 4 is not well-defined.

Another result about non-uniqueness of direct sum decompositions is due to A.L.S. Corner: given integers $n\ge k\ge 1$, there exists a torsion-free abelian group *A* of rank *n* such that for any partition $n = r_1 + \cdots + r_k$ into *k* natural summands, the group *A* is the direct sum of *k* indecomposable subgroups of ranks $r_1, r_2, \ldots, r_k$. Thus the sequence of ranks of indecomposable summands in a certain direct sum decomposition of a torsion-free abelian group of finite rank is very far from being an invariant of *A*.

Other surprising examples include torsion-free rank 2 groups *A*<sub>*n*,*m*</sub> and *B*<sub>*n*,*m*</sub> such that *A*<sup>*n*</sup> is isomorphic to *B*<sup>*n*</sup> if and only if *n* is divisible by *m*.

For abelian groups of infinite rank, there is an example of a group *K* and a subgroup *G* such that

- *K* is indecomposable;
- *K* is generated by *G* and a single other element; and
- Every nonzero direct summand of *G* is decomposable.

## Generalization {#Generalization}

The notion of rank can be generalized for any module *M* over an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) *R*, as the dimension over *R*<sub>0</sub>, the [quotient field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_field), of the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of the module with the field:

$$\operatorname{rank} (M)=\dim_{R_0}( M\otimes_R R_0 )$$

It makes sense, since *R*<sub>0</sub> is a field, and thus any module (or, to be more specific, [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370)) over it is free.

It is a generalization, since every abelian group is a module over the integers. It easily follows that the dimension of the product over **Q** is the cardinality of maximal linearly independent subset, since for any torsion element *x* and any rational *q*,

$$x\otimes_{\mathbf Z} q = 0.$$

:::
