# Jean Leray

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Leray"}

**Jean Leray** (; 7 November 1906 – 10 November 1998)[^o3] was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who worked on both [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

He was born in Chantenay-sur-Loire (today part of [Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantes)). He studied at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) from 1926 to 1929. He received his Ph.D. in 1933. In 1934 Leray published an important paper that founded the study of [weak solutions](wikipedia://en/3555416) of the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395).[^o5] In the same year, he and [Juliusz Schauder](wikipedia://en/685314) discovered[^o6] a topological invariant, now called the [Leray–Schauder degree](wikipedia://en/70573417), which they applied to prove the existence of solutions for partial differential equations lacking uniqueness.

From 1938 to 1939 he was professor at the [University of Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nancy). He did not join the [Bourbaki group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbaki_group), although he was close with its founders.

His main work in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) was carried out while he was a [prisoner of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war) in a camp in [Edelbach, Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edelbach,_Allentsteig) from 1940 to 1945. He concealed his expertise on differential equations, fearing that its connections with [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) could lead him to be asked to aid the German war effort.

Leray's work of this period proved seminal to the development of [spectral sequences](wikipedia://en/803894) and [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466).[^o7] These were subsequently developed by many others,[^o8] each separately becoming an important tool in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).

He returned to work on partial differential equations from about 1950.

He was professor at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) from 1945 to 1947, and then at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) until 1978.

He was awarded the Malaxa Prize ([Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania), 1938), the Grand Prix in mathematical sciences ([French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences), 1940), the [Feltrinelli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feltrinelli_Prize) ([Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei), 1971), the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) ([Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel), 1979), and the [Lomonosov Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Gold_Medal) ([Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), 1988). He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1959 and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1965.[^o9][^o10]
