# Multilinear algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multilinear algebra"}

**Multilinear algebra** is the study of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) with multiple [vector](wikipedia://en/32370)-valued [arguments](wikipedia://en/32842052), with the functions being [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) with respect to each argument. It involves concepts such as [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859), [tensors](wikipedia://en/29965), [multivectors](wikipedia://en/2727288), [systems of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087), [higher-dimensional spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-dimensional_space), [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468), [inner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) and [outer](wikipedia://en/103109) products, and [dual spaces](wikipedia://en/7988). It is a mathematical tool used in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).[^o2]

:::

## Origin {#Origin}

While many theoretical concepts and applications involve [single vectors](wikipedia://en/32370), mathematicians such as [Hermann Grassmann](wikipedia://en/99242) considered structures involving pairs, triplets, and [multivectors](wikipedia://en/2727288) that generalize [vectors](wikipedia://en/23218956). With multiple combinational possibilities, the space of [multivectors](wikipedia://en/2727288) expands to 2<sup>*n*</sup> dimensions, where *n* is the dimension of the relevant vector space.[^o4] The [determinant can be formulated abstractly](wikipedia://en/8468#Abstract_formulation) using the structures of multilinear algebra.

Multilinear algebra appears in the study of the mechanical response of materials to stress and strain, involving various moduli of [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29). The term "[tensor](wikipedia://en/29965)" describes elements within the multilinear space due to its added structure. Despite Grassmann's early work in 1844 with his *[Ausdehnungslehre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ausdehnungslehre)*, which was also republished in 1862, the subject was initially not widely understood, as even ordinary linear algebra posed many challenges at the time.

The concepts of multilinear algebra find applications in certain studies of [multivariate calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_calculus) and [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), particularly concerning the [Jacobian matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_matrix). [Infinitesimal differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_%28infinitesimal%29) encountered in single-variable calculus are transformed into [differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms) in [multivariate calculus](wikipedia://en/615222), and their manipulation is carried out using [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537).[^o5]

Following Grassmann, developments in multilinear algebra were made by [Victor Schlegel](wikipedia://en/34212179) in 1872 with the publication of the first part of his *System der Raumlehre*[^o6] and by [Elwin Bruno Christoffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Bruno_Christoffel). Notably, significant advancements came through the work of [Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro](wikipedia://en/499223) and [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180),[^o7] particularly in the form of *absolute differential calculus* within multilinear algebra. [Marcel Grossmann](wikipedia://en/1247037) and [Michele Besso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Besso) introduced this form to [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), and in 1915, Einstein's publication on [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), explaining the [precession of Mercury's perihelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precession_of_the_perihelion_of_Mercury), established multilinear algebra and tensors as important mathematical tools in physics.

In 1958, [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) included a chapter on multilinear algebra titled "*Algèbre Multilinéaire*" in their series [Éléments de mathématique](wikipedia://en/38333812), specifically within the algebra book. The chapter covers topics such as bilinear functions, the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of two [modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_%28ring_theory%29), and the properties of tensor products.[^o8]
