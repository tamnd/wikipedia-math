# Plus and minus signs

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Plus and minus signs"}

The **plus sign** (+) and the **minus sign** (−) are [mathematical symbols](wikipedia://en/73634) used to denote [positive](wikipedia://en/7951270) and [negative](wikipedia://en/7951270) [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), respectively. In addition, the symbol + represents the operation of [addition](wikipedia://en/61338), which results in a [sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_%28mathematics%29), while the symbol − represents [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141), resulting in a [difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_%28mathematics%29).[^o3] Their use has been extended to many other meanings, more or less analogous. Plus and minus are [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) terms meaning 'more' and 'less', respectively.

:::

The forms + and − are used in many countries around the world. Other designs include fb29 for plus and 2052 for minus.

## History {#History}

Though the signs now seem as familiar as the [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet) or the [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786), they are not of great antiquity. The [Egyptian hieroglyphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyph) sign for addition, for example, resembles a pair of legs walking in the direction in which the text was written ([Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_language) could be written either from right to left or left to right), with the reverse sign indicating subtraction:[^o5]

|---|

[Nicole Oresme](wikipedia://en/593659)'s [manuscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuscript) from the 14th century show what may be one of the earliest uses of + as a sign for plus.[^o6]

In early 15th century Europe, the letters "P" and "M" were generally used.[^o7][^o8] The symbols (P with overline, p̄, for più (more), i.e., plus, and M with overline, m̄, for meno (less), i.e., minus) appeared for the first time in [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473)'s mathematics [compendium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compendium), [Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalità](wikipedia://en/45351614), first printed and published in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) in 1494.[^o9]

The + sign is a simplification of the et (comparable to the evolution of the [ampersand](wikipedia://en/59153) &).[^o10] The − may be derived from a [macron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macron_%28diacritic%29) ◌̄ written over $\langle m\rangle$ when used to indicate subtraction; or it may come from a shorthand version of the letter $\langle m\rangle$ itself.[^o11]

*Image: Johannes\_Widmann-Mercantile\_Arithmetic\_1489.jpg.* From [Johannes Widmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Widmann)'s book on "handy and pretty [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) for all [merchants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant)"[^o12][^o13]

In his 1489 treatise, [Johannes Widmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Widmann) referred to the symbols − and + as *minus* and *mer* (Modern German mehr; "more"): "\[...\] was − ist das ist minus \[...\] und das + das ist mer das zu addirst".[^o14] They were not used for addition and subtraction in the treatise, but were used to indicate surplus and deficit; usage in the modern sense is attested in a 1518 book by [Henricus Grammateus](wikipedia://en/5787009).[^o15][^o16]

[Robert Recorde](wikipedia://en/310247), the designer of the [equals sign](wikipedia://en/501828), introduced plus and minus to Britain in 1557 in *[The Whetstone of Witte](wikipedia://en/19934039)*:[^o17] "There be other 2 signes in often use of which the first is made thus + and betokeneth more: the other is thus made − and betokeneth lesse."

## Plus sign {#Plus_sign}

The plus sign (+) is a [binary operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_operator) that indicates [addition](wikipedia://en/61338), as in 2 + 3 = 5. It can also serve as a [unary operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unary_operator) that leaves its [operand](wikipedia://en/22656) [unchanged](wikipedia://en/15069) (+*x* means the same as *x*). This notation may be used when it is desired to emphasize the positiveness of a number, especially in contrast with the [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616) (+5 versus −5).

The plus sign can also indicate many other operations, depending on the mathematical system under consideration. Many [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364), such as [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) and [matrix rings](wikipedia://en/487541), have some operation which is called, or is equivalent to, addition. It is though conventional to use the plus sign to only denote [commutative operations](wikipedia://en/294390).[^o20]

The symbol is also used in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). For more, see .

## Minus sign {#Minus_sign}

The minus sign (−) has three main uses in mathematics:[^o23]

1. The [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141) operator: a [binary operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_operator) to indicate the operation of subtraction, as in 5 − 3 = 2. Subtraction is the inverse of addition.[^o3]
1. The [additive inverse](wikipedia://en/228312) operator: a [unary operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unary_operator) to indicate the [function](wikipedia://en/185427) whose [value](wikipedia://en/4095147) for any [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [argument](wikipedia://en/32842052) is the additive inverse of that argument. For example, if *x* = 3, then −*x* = −3, but if *x* = −3, then −*x* = +3. Similarly, −(−*x*) = *x*.
1. A [prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefix) of a numeric constant. When it is placed immediately before an unsigned number, the combination names a negative number, the additive inverse of the positive number that the numeral would otherwise name.

In many contexts, it does not matter whether the second or the third of these usages is intended: −5 is the same number. When it is important to distinguish them, a raised minus sign (¯) is sometimes used for negative constants, as in elementary education, the programming language [APL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APL_%28programming_language%29), and some early graphing calculators.

All three uses can be referred to as "minus" in everyday speech, though the binary operator is sometimes read as "take away".[^o24] In American English nowadays, −5 (for example) is generally referred to as "negative five" though speakers born before 1950 often refer to it as "minus five". (Temperatures tend to follow the older usage; −5° is generally called "minus five degrees", though both forms are seen.[^o25]) Further, a few textbooks in the United States encourage −*x* to be read as "the opposite of *x*" or "the additive inverse of *x*"—to avoid giving the impression that −*x* is necessarily negative (since *x* itself may already be negative).[^o26]

In mathematics and most programming languages, the rules for the [order of operations](wikipedia://en/212980) mean that −5<sup>2</sup> is equal to −25: [Exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491) binds more strongly than the unary minus, which binds more strongly than multiplication or division. However, in some programming languages ([Microsoft Excel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Excel) in particular), unary operators bind strongest, so in those cases −5^2 is 25, but 0−5^2 is −25.[^o27]

Similar to the plus sign, the minus sign is also used in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). (For more, see below.)

## Use in elementary education {#Use_in_elementary_education}

Some elementary teachers use raised minus signs before numbers to disambiguate them from the operation of subtraction.[^o29] The same convention is also used in some computer languages. For example, subtracting −5 from 3 might be read as "positive three take away negative 5", and be shown as

3 − <sup>−</sup>5 becomes 3 + 5 = 8,

which can be read as:

<sup>\+</sup>3 −1(<sup>−</sup>5)

or even as

<sup>\+</sup>3 − <sup>−</sup>5 becomes <sup>\+</sup>3 + <sup>\+</sup>5 = <sup>\+</sup>8.

## Use as a qualifier {#Use_as_a_qualifier}

When placed after a number, a plus sign can indicate an open range of numbers. For example, "18+" is commonly used as shorthand for "ages 18 and up" although "eighteen plus", for example, is now common usage.

In US [grading systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grading_in_education), the plus sign indicates a grade one level higher and the minus sign a grade lower. For example, B− ("B minus") is one grade lower than B. In some occasions, this is extended to two plus or minus signs (e.g., A++ being two grades higher than A).

A common trend in branding, particularly with streaming video services, has been the use of the plus sign at the end of brand names, e.g. [Google+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google+), [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+), [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+), and [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV+). Since the word "plus" can mean an advantage, or an additional amount of something, such "+" signs imply that a product offers extra features or benefits.

Positive and negative are sometimes abbreviated as +ve and −ve,[^o31] and on batteries and cell terminals are often marked with + and −.

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

In mathematics the [one-sided limit](wikipedia://en/379840) $x \to a^{+}$ means $x$ approaches $a$ from the right (i.e., right-sided limit), and $x \to a^{-}$ means $x$ approaches $a$ from the left (i.e., left-sided limit). For example, $\frac{1}{x} \rightarrow +\infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$ but $\frac{1}{x} \rightarrow -\infty$ as $x \to 0^{-}$.

When placed after [special sets of numbers](wikipedia://en/26691#Special_sets_of_numbers_in_mathematics), plus and minus signs are used to indicate that only positive numbers and negative numbers are included, respectively. For example, $\mathbb{Z}^+$ is the set of all positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) and $\mathbb{Z}^-$ is the set of all negative integers. In these cases, a subscript 0 may also be added to clarify that 0 is included.

### Blood {#Blood}

[Blood types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_types) are often qualified with a plus or minus to indicate the presence or absence of the [Rh factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh_factor). For example, A+ means [type A blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABO_blood_group_system) with the Rh factor present, while B− means type B blood with the Rh factor absent.

### Music {#Music}

In music, [augmented chords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_triad#Augmented_chord_table) are symbolized with a plus sign, although this practice is not universal (as there are other methods for spelling those chords). For example, "C+" is read "C augmented chord". Sometimes the plus is written as a [superscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superscript).

## Uses in computing {#Uses_in_computing}

As well as the normal mathematical usage, plus and minus signs may be used for a number of other purposes in computing.

Plus and minus signs are often used in [tree view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_view) on a computer screen—to show if a folder is collapsed or not.

In some programming languages, [concatenation](wikipedia://en/64474) of [strings](wikipedia://en/27701) is written "a" + "b", and results in "ab".

In most programming languages, subtraction and negation are indicated with the ASCII [hyphen-minus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen-minus) character, -. In [APL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APL_%28programming_language%29) a raised minus sign (here written using AF) is used to denote a negative number, as in ¯3. While in [J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_%28programming_language%29) a negative number is denoted by an [underscore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underscore), as in \_5.

In [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) and some other computer programming languages, two plus signs indicate the [increment operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Increment_operator) and two minus signs a decrement; the position of the operator before or after the variable indicates whether the new or old value is read from it. For example, if x equals 6, then y = x++ increments x to 7 but sets y to 6, whereas y = ++x would set both x and y to 7. By extension, ++ is sometimes used in computing terminology to signify an improvement, as in the name of the language [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++).

In [regular expressions](wikipedia://en/25717), + is often used to indicate "1 or more" in a pattern to be matched. For example, x+ means "one or more of the letter x". This is the [Kleene plus](wikipedia://en/16750#Kleene_plus) notation. Hyphen-minus usually indicates a range (\[A-Z\] - any capital from 'A' to 'Z'), although it can stand for itself (\[ABCDE-\] any capital from 'A' to 'E' or '-').

There is no concept of negative zero in mathematics, but in computing [−0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_zero) may have a separate representation from zero. In the [IEEE floating-point standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_floating-point_standard), 1 / −0 is [negative infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_infinity) ($-\infty$) whereas 1 / 0 is [positive infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824) ([$\infty$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞)).

\+ is also used to denote added lines in [diff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diff) output in the diff or the diff.

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

### Minus {#Minus}

- Dan/Godié/Karaboro/Krumen/Mwan/Nyabwa/Wan/Wè/Yaouré Languages: Tone letter in the orthographies of [Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_language), [Krumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krumen_language), [Karaboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaboro_languages), [Mwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwan_language), [Wan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wan_language), [Yaouré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaouré), [Wè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wè_language), [Nyabwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyabwa_language), and [Godié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godie_language).[^o38] The Unicode character used for the tone letter (2D7) is different from the mathematical minus sign.

### Plus {#Plus}

- [Botanical names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_name): A plus sign denotes [graft-chimaera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graft-chimaera).
- Catholicism: The plus sign before a last name denotes a [Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops_in_the_Catholic_Church), and a double plus is used to denote an Archbishop.
- Chess: In the [algebraic notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_notation_%28chess%29) used to record games of [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess), the plus sign + is used to denote a move that puts the opponent into [check](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check_%28chess%29), while a double plus ++ is sometimes used to denote [double check](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_check). Combinations of the plus and minus signs are used to evaluate a move (+/−, +/=, =/+, −/+).
- Huichol Language: The plus sign sometimes represents ɨ in the orthography of [Huichol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huichol_language#Orthography).[^o40]
- Linguistics: A superscript plus <sup>\+</sup> sometimes replaces the [asterisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterisk), which denotes unattested [linguistic reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_reconstruction).
- Telephone numbers: When prefixed to a telephone number + is used to indicate the form used for [International Direct Dialing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Direct_Dialing).[^o41] Its precise usage varies by technology and national standards.

### Plus and minus {#Plus_and_minus}

- Chemistry: Superscripted plus and minus signs are used to indicate an ion with a positive or negative charge of 1 (e.g., NH). If the charge is greater than 1, a number indicating the charge is written before the sign (as in SO).
- [International Phonetic Alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet): [subscripted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscript) plus and minus signs are used as diacritics to indicate [advanced or retracted articulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_articulation) of speech sounds.
- Physics: The use of plus and minus signs for different [electrical charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_charge) was introduced by [Georg Christoph Lichtenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Christoph_Lichtenberg).

## Alternative plus sign {#Alternative_plus_sign}

*Image: U+FB29.svg.*

A [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) tradition that dates from at least the 19th century is to write *plus* using the symbol ﬩, to avoid the writing of a symbol + that could look like a [Christian cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_cross).[^o47][^o48] This practice was adopted into [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) schools and was still commonplace in [elementary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_school) (including [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilonim) schools) but in fewer [secondary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school).[^o48] It is also used occasionally in books by religious authors, but most books for adults use the international symbol +. [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) has this symbol at position FB29.[^o49] This form of the plus sign is also used on the control buttons at individual seats on board the El Al Israel Airlines aircraft.

## Alternative minus signs {#Alternative_minus_signs}

*Image: Skjermbilete 2012-11-03 kl. 02.48.36.png.* "÷" being used as a minus sign (not as a division sign) in an excerpt from an official Norwegian trading statement form called «Næringsoppgave 1» for the taxation year 2010

There is a [commercial minus sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_minus_sign), ⁒, which is (or was) used in Germany and Scandinavia. The symbol ÷, still used in many Anglophone countries as a [division sign](wikipedia://en/276918), is (or was) used [to denote subtraction](wikipedia://en/63530168#In_mathematics) in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia).[^o51]

The [hyphen-minus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen-minus) symbol (-) is the form of [hyphen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen) most commonly used in digital [documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Document). On most keyboards, it is the only character that resembles a [minus sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minus_sign) or a [dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash) so it is also used for these.[^o52] The name *hyphen-minus* derives from the original [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) standard,[^o53] where it was called *hyphen–(minus)*.[^o54] The character is referred to as a *hyphen*, a *minus sign*, or a *dash* according to the context where it is being used.
