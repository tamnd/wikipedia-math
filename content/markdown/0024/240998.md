# CAST-128

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CAST-128"}

*Image: CAST-128-large.png.* Three rounds of the CAST-128 block cipher

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **CAST-128** (alternatively **CAST5**) is a [symmetric-key](wikipedia://en/53042) [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) used in a number of products, notably as the default [cipher](wikipedia://en/5244) in some versions of [GPG](wikipedia://en/38809) and [PGP](wikipedia://en/23080). It has also been approved for [Government of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Canada) use by the [Communications Security Establishment](wikipedia://en/231712). The algorithm was created in 1996 by [Carlisle Adams](wikipedia://en/9212118) and [Stafford Tavares](wikipedia://en/9546396) using the **CAST** design procedure.[^o3]

:::

Another member of the CAST family of ciphers, [CAST-256](wikipedia://en/612143) (a former [AES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AES_process) candidate) was derived from CAST-128. According to some sources, the CAST name is based on the [initials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initials) of its inventors, though [Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732) reports the authors' claim that "the name should conjure up images of randomness".[^o4]

CAST-128 is a 12- or 16-round [Feistel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feistel_network) with a 64-[bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit) [block size](wikipedia://en/294065) and a [key size](wikipedia://en/5749) of between 40 and 128 bits (but only in 8-bit increments). The full 16 rounds are used when the [key size](wikipedia://en/5749) is longer than 80 bits.[^o5]

Components include large 8×32-bit [S-boxes](wikipedia://en/240991) based on [bent functions](wikipedia://en/24465207), key-dependent rotations, [modular](wikipedia://en/20087) addition and subtraction, and [XOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR) operations. There are three alternating types of round function, but they are similar in structure and differ only in the choice of the exact operation (addition, subtraction or XOR) at various points.

Although [Entrust](wikipedia://en/16088938) holds a [patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent) on the CAST design procedure, CAST-128 is available worldwide on a [royalty-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalty-free) basis for commercial and non-commercial uses.
