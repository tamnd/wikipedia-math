# Multiplication sign

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplication sign"}

The **multiplication sign** (×), also known as the **times sign** or the **dimension sign**, is a [mathematical symbol](wikipedia://en/73634) used to denote the operation of [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845), which results in a [product](wikipedia://en/24977).[^o3]

:::

The symbol is also used in [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany), in botanical [hybrid names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_name_%28botany%29).

The form is properly a four-fold [rotationally symmetric](wikipedia://en/701096) [saltire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltire).[^o4] The multiplication sign × is similar to a lowercase [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X) (x).

## History {#History}

The earliest known use of the × symbol to indicate multiplication appears in an anonymous appendix to the 1618 edition of [John Napier](wikipedia://en/15993)'s [Mirifici Logarithmorum Canonis Descriptio](wikipedia://en/26291704).[^o8] This appendix has been attributed to [William Oughtred](wikipedia://en/475249),[^o8] who used the same symbol in his 1631 algebra text, Clavis Mathematicae, stating:

> Multiplication of species \[i.e. unknowns\] connects both proposed magnitudes with the symbol 'in' or ×: or ordinarily without the symbol if the magnitudes be denoted with one letter.[^o6]

Other works have been identified in which crossed diagonals appear in diagrams involving multiplied numbers, such as [Robert Recorde](wikipedia://en/310247)'s *[The Ground of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ground_of_Arts)*[^o7] and [Oswald Schreckenfuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Schreckenfuchs)'s 1551 edition of *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)*, but these are not symbolizations.[^o8]

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [symbol](wikipedia://en/37673) × has a number of uses, including

- [Multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) of two [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690), where it is read as "times" or "multiplied by"[^o3]
- [Cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) of two [vectors](wikipedia://en/32370), where it is usually read as "cross"
- [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two sets, where it is usually read as "cross"[^o11]
- Geometric dimension of an object, such as noting that a room is 10 feet × 12 feet in area, where it is usually read as "by" (e.g., "10 feet by 12 feet")
- [Display resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_resolution) in pixels, such as 1920 pixels across × 1080 pixels down. Read as "by".
- Dimensions of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859), where it is usually read as "by"
- A [statistical interaction](wikipedia://en/443083) between two explanatory [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109), where it is usually read as "by"
- the [optical magnification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnification#Size_ratio_%28optical_magnification%29) is sometimes referred to as "power" (for example "10× power")

In [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), the multiplication sign is used in a botanical [hybrid name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_name_%28botany%29), for instance *Ceanothus papillosus* × *impressus* (a hybrid between *[C. papillosus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceanothus_papillosus)* and *[C. impressus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceanothus_impressus)*) or *[Crocosmia × crocosmiiflora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocosmia_×_crocosmiiflora)* (a hybrid between two other species of *Crocosmia*). However, the communication of these hybrid names with a Latin letter "x" is common, especially when the actual "×" symbol is not readily available.

The multiplication sign is also used by [historians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian) for an event between two [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_date). When employed between two dates – for example 1225 and 1232 – the expression "1225×1232" means "no earlier than 1225 and no later than 1232".[^o12]

A [monadic](wikipedia://en/579061) × symbol is used by the [APL programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APL_%28programming_language%29) to denote the [sign function](wikipedia://en/505449).

:::

## Similar notations {#Similar_notations}

The lower-case Latin letter x is sometimes used in place of the multiplication sign. This is considered incorrect in mathematical writing.

In [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) notation, widely used in mathematics, a multiplication symbol is usually omitted wherever it would not cause confusion: "$a$ multiplied by $b$" can be written as $ab$ or $a\,b$.[^o3]

Other symbols can also be used to denote multiplication, often to reduce confusion between the multiplication sign × and the common variable $x$. In some countries, such as [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), the primary symbol for multiplication is the "[dot operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot_operator)" ⋅ (as in $a\cdot b$). This symbol is also used in compound [units of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement), e.g., N⋅m (see *International System of Units#Lexicographic conventions*). In algebra, it is a notation to resolve ambiguity (for instance, "$b$ times $2$" may be written as $b\cdot2$, to avoid being confused with a value called $b_{2}$). This notation is used wherever multiplication should be written explicitly, such as in "$ab = a\cdot2$ for $b = 2$"; this usage is also seen in English-language texts. In some languages, the use of [full stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_stop) as a multiplication symbol, such as $a.b$, is common when the symbol for the [decimal point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_point) is a [comma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comma).

Historically, computer language [syntax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntax_%28programming%29) was restricted to the [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) character set, and the [asterisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterisk) \* became the de facto symbol for the multiplication operator. This selection is reflected in the [numeric keypad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeric_keypad) on English-language keyboards, where the arithmetic operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication and division are represented by the keys +, -, \* and /, respectively.
