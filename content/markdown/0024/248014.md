# Ringed space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ringed space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **ringed space** is a family of ([commutative](wikipedia://en/61346)) [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) parametrized by [open subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) together with [ring homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/26411) that play roles of [restrictions](wikipedia://en/1589032). Precisely, it is a topological space equipped with a **[sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of rings** called a **structure sheaf**. It is an abstraction of the concept of the rings of [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122#Continuous_functions_between_topological_spaces) (scalar-valued) functions on open subsets.

:::

Among ringed spaces, especially important and prominent is a **locally ringed space**: a ringed space in which the analogy between the [stalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalk_of_a_sheaf) at a point and the ring of [germs of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_of_a_function) at a point is valid.

Ringed spaces appear in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) as well as [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry) and the [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

**Note**: In the definition of a ringed space, most expositions tend to restrict the rings to be [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), including [Hartshorne](wikipedia://en/6921017) and Wikipedia. *[Éléments de géométrie algébrique](wikipedia://en/471387)*, on the other hand, does not impose the commutativity assumption, although the book mostly considers the commutative case.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A **ringed space** $(X,\mathcal{O}_X)$ is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *$X$* together with a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) $\mathcal{O}_X$ on $X$. The sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X$ is called the **structure sheaf** of $X$.

A **locally ringed space** is a ringed space $(X,\mathcal{O}_X)$ such that all [stalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalk_of_a_sheaf) of $\mathcal{O}_X$ are [local rings](wikipedia://en/153391) (i.e. they have unique [maximal ideals](wikipedia://en/48164)). Note that it is *not* required that $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ be a local ring for every open set $U$*;* in fact, this is almost never the case.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

An arbitrary topological space *$X$* can be considered a locally ringed space by taking *$\mathcal{O}_X$* to be the sheaf of [real-valued](wikipedia://en/20646438) (or [complex-valued](wikipedia://en/5826)) continuous functions on open subsets of *$X$*. The [stalk](wikipedia://en/15879621) at a point $x$ can be thought of as the set of all [germs](wikipedia://en/352714) of continuous functions at *$x$*; this is a local ring with the unique maximal ideal consisting of those germs whose value at *$x$* is $0$.

If *$X$* is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with some extra structure, we can also take the sheaf of [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206), or [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) functions. Both of these give rise to locally ringed spaces.

If *$X$* is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) carrying the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744), we can define a locally ringed space by taking $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ to be the ring of [rational mappings](wikipedia://en/2783119) defined on the Zariski-open set *$U$* that do not blow up (become infinite) within $U$. The important generalization of this example is that of the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of any commutative ring; these spectra are also locally ringed spaces. [Schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) are locally ringed spaces obtained by "gluing together" spectra of commutative rings.

:::

## Morphisms {#Morphisms}

A [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) from $(X,\mathcal{O}_X)$ to $(Y,\mathcal{O}_Y)$ is a pair $(f,\varphi)$, where $f:X\to Y$ is a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) between the underlying topological spaces, and $\varphi:\mathcal{O}_Y\to f_*\mathcal{O}_X$ is a [morphism](wikipedia://en/245466#Morphisms) from the structure sheaf of $Y$ to the [direct image](wikipedia://en/3022744) of the structure sheaf of $X$. In other words, a morphism from $(X,\mathcal{O}_X)$ to $(Y,\mathcal{O}_Y)$ is given by the following data:

- a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) $f:X\to Y$
- a family of [ring homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/26411) $\varphi_V : \mathcal{O}_Y(V)\to\mathcal{O}_X(f^{-1}(V))$ for every [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) $V$ of $Y$ that commute with the restriction maps. That is, if $V_1\subseteq V_2$ are two open subsets of $Y$, then the following diagram must [commute](wikipedia://en/138484) (the vertical maps are the restriction homomorphisms):

There is an additional requirement for morphisms between *locally* ringed spaces:

- the ring homomorphisms induced by $\varphi$ between the stalks of *$Y$* and the stalks of *$X$* must be *[local homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/153391#Some_facts_and_definitions)*, i.e. for every *$x\in X$* the maximal ideal of the local ring (stalk) at $f(x)\in Y$ is mapped into the maximal ideal of the local ring at *$x\in X$*.

Two morphisms can be composed to form a new morphism, and we obtain the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of ringed spaces and the category of locally ringed spaces. [Isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) in these categories are defined as usual.

:::

## Tangent spaces {#Tangent_spaces}

Locally ringed spaces have just enough structure to allow the meaningful definition of [tangent spaces](wikipedia://en/30807). Let *$X$* be a locally ringed space with structure sheaf *$\mathcal{O}_X$*; we want to define the tangent space $T_x(X)$ at the point *$x\in X$*. Take the local ring (stalk) $R_x$ at the point $x$, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_x$. Then $k_x := R_x/\mathfrak{m}_x$ is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) and $\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2$ is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over that field (the [cotangent space](wikipedia://en/6620)). The tangent space $T_x(X)$ is defined as the [dual](wikipedia://en/7988) of this vector space.

The idea is the following: a tangent vector at *$x$* should tell you how to "differentiate" "functions" at *$x$*, i.e. the elements of *$R_x$*. Now it is enough to know how to differentiate functions whose value at *$x$* is zero, since all other functions differ from these only by a constant, and we know how to differentiate constants. So we only need to consider *$\mathfrak{m}_x$*. Furthermore, if two functions are given with value zero at *$x$*, then their product has derivative 0 at *$x$*, by the [product rule](wikipedia://en/248988). So we only need to know how to assign "numbers" to the elements of $\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_x^2$, and this is what the dual space does.

## Modules over the structure sheaf {#Modules_over_the_structure_sheaf}

Given a locally ringed space $(X,\mathcal{O}_X)$, certain [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) of modules on *$X$* occur in the applications, the *$\mathcal{O}_X$*-modules. To define them, consider a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) on *$X$*. If $\mathcal{F}(U)$ is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over the ring *$\mathcal{O}_X(U)$* for every open set *$U$* in *$X$*, and the restriction maps are compatible with the module structure, then we call $\mathcal{F}$ an *$\mathcal{O}_X$*-module. In this case, the stalk of *$\mathcal{F}$* at *$x$* will be a module over the local ring (stalk) *$R_x$*, for every *$x\in X$*.

A morphism between two such *$\mathcal{O}_X$*-modules is a [morphism of sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_of_sheaves#Morphisms) that is compatible with the given module structures. The category of *$\mathcal{O}_X$*-modules over a fixed locally ringed space $(X,\mathcal{O}_X)$ is an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063).

An important subcategory of the category of *$\mathcal{O}_X$*-modules is the category of *[quasi-coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaves)* on *$X$*. A sheaf of *$\mathcal{O}_X$*-modules is called quasi-coherent if it is, locally, isomorphic to the [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) of a map between free *$\mathcal{O}_X$*-modules. A [*coherent* sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) *$F$* is a quasi-coherent sheaf that is, locally, of [finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_of_finite_type) and for every open subset *$U$* of *$X$* the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of any morphism from a free *$\mathcal{O}_U$*-module of finite rank to *$\mathcal{F}_U$* is also of finite type.
