# Generalized permutation matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized permutation matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **generalized permutation matrix** (or **monomial matrix**) is a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) with the same nonzero pattern as a [permutation matrix](wikipedia://en/238849), i.e. there is exactly one nonzero entry in each row and each column. Unlike a permutation matrix, where the nonzero entry must be 1, in a generalized permutation matrix the nonzero entry can be any nonzero value. An example of a generalized permutation matrix is

:::

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 3 & 0\\ 0 & -7 & 0 & 0\\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \sqrt2\end{bmatrix}.$$

## Structure {#Structure}

An [invertible matrix](wikipedia://en/217122) *A* is a generalized permutation matrix [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it can be written as a product of an invertible [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080) *D* and an (implicitly [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122)) [permutation matrix](wikipedia://en/238849) *P*: i.e.,

$$A = DP.$$

### Group structure {#Group_structure}

The [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of *n* × *n* generalized permutation matrices with entries in a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* forms a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(*n*, *F*), in which the group of [nonsingular](wikipedia://en/217122) diagonal matrices Δ(*n*, *F*) forms a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918). Indeed, over all fields except [GF(2)](wikipedia://en/11137263), the generalized permutation matrices are the [normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) of the diagonal matrices, meaning that the generalized permutation matrices are the *largest* subgroup of GL(*n*, *F*) in which diagonal matrices are normal.

The abstract group of generalized permutation matrices is the [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625) of *F*<sup>×</sup> and *S*<sub>*n*</sub>. Concretely, this means that it is the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of Δ(*n*, *F*) by the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>*n*</sub>:

*S*<sub>*n*</sub> ⋉ Δ(*n*, *F*),

where *S*<sub>*n*</sub> acts by permuting coordinates and the diagonal matrices Δ(*n*, *F*) are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the *n*-fold product (*F*<sup>×</sup>)<sup>*n*</sup>.

To be precise, the generalized permutation matrices are a (faithful) [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of this abstract wreath product: a realization of the abstract group as a subgroup of matrices.

### Subgroups {#Subgroups}

- The subgroup where all entries are 1 is exactly the [permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_matrices), which is isomorphic to the symmetric group.
- The subgroup where all entries are ±1 is the [signed permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_permutation_matrices), which is the [hyperoctahedral group](wikipedia://en/24268961).
- The subgroup where the entries are *m*th [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) $\mu_m$ is isomorphic to a [generalized symmetric group](wikipedia://en/25186376).
- The subgroup of diagonal matrices is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974), normal, and a maximal abelian subgroup. The [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) is the symmetric group, and this construction is in fact the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) of the general linear group: the diagonal matrices are a [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136) in the general linear group (and are their own [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer)), the generalized permutation matrices are the normalizer of this torus, and the quotient, $N(T)/Z(T) = N(T)/T \cong S_n$ is the Weyl group.

## Properties {#Properties}

- If a nonsingular matrix and its inverse are both [nonnegative matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonnegative_matrices) (i.e. matrices with nonnegative entries), then the matrix is a generalized permutation matrix.
- The determinant of a generalized permutation matrix is given by $$\det(G)=\det(P)\cdot \det(D)=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\cdot d_{11}\cdot \ldots \cdot d_{nn},$$ where $\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)$ is the [sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_of_a_permutation) of the [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) $\pi$ associated with $P$ and $d_{11},\ldots ,d_{nn}$ are the diagonal elements of $D$.

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

One can generalize further by allowing the entries to lie in a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), rather than in a field. In that case if the non-zero entries are required to be [units](wikipedia://en/289666) in the ring, one again obtains a group. On the other hand, if the non-zero entries are only required to be non-zero, but not necessarily invertible, this set of matrices forms a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) instead.

One may also schematically allow the non-zero entries to lie in a group *G,* with the understanding that matrix multiplication will only involve multiplying a single pair of group elements, not "adding" group elements. This is an [abuse of notation](wikipedia://en/1698066), since element of matrices being multiplied must allow multiplication and addition, but is suggestive notion for the (formally correct) abstract group $G \wr S_n$ (the wreath product of the group *G* by the symmetric group).

:::

## Signed permutation group {#Signed_permutation_group}

A **signed permutation matrix** is a generalized permutation matrix whose nonzero entries are ±1, and are the [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) generalized permutation matrices with integer inverse.

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

- It is the [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) $B_n$, and has [order](wikipedia://en/493527) $2^n n!$.
- It is the symmetry group of the [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) and (dually) of the [cross-polytope](wikipedia://en/716401).
- Its [index](wikipedia://en/230581) 2 subgroup of matrices with determinant equal to their underlying (unsigned) permutation is the Coxeter group $D_n$ and is the symmetry group of the [demihypercube](wikipedia://en/6833695).
- It is a subgroup of the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954).

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

### Monomial representations {#Monomial_representations}

Monomial matrices occur in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) in the context of [monomial representations](wikipedia://en/2786447). A monomial representation of a group *G* is a linear representation *ρ* : *G* → GL(*n*, *F*) of *G* (here *F* is the defining field of the representation) such that the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) *ρ*(*G*) is a subgroup of the group of monomial matrices.

:::
