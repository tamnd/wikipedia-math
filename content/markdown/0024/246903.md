# Apologetics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apologetics"}

**Apologetics** (grc) is the defense of [religious doctrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_doctrine) through systematic [argumentation](wikipedia://en/13018310) and discourse.[^o2][^o3][^o4] Some sources identify apologetics as a separate branch of [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology), especially when applied to Christianity.[^o5][^o6] [Early Christian writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_early_Christian_writers) (c. 120–220) who defended their beliefs against [critics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_Christianity) and recommended their [faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith) to outsiders have become known as early examples of [Christian apologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_apologists).[^o7] (Compare the Hellenistic Greek word ἀπολογιστής, meaning "accountant", or "bookkeeper".[^o8])

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The term *apologetics* derives from the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) word grc (ἀπολογία).[^o3] In the Classical Greek legal system, the prosecution delivered the grc (κατηγορία), the accusation or charge, and the defendant replied with an *grc*, the [defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_%28legal%29).[^o10] The grc was a formal speech or explanation to reply to and rebut the charges. A famous example is [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates)' [Apologia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apology_%28Plato%29) defense, as chronicled in [Plato's *Apology*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apology_%28Plato%29).

In the [Koine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koine_Greek) of the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament), the [Apostle Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostle_Paul) employs the term *apologia* in his trial speech to [Festus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcius_Festus) and [Agrippa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrippa_I) when he says "I make my defense" in [Acts 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_26):2.[^o11] A [cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate) form appears in Paul's [Letter to the Philippians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_to_the_Philippians) as he is "defending the gospel" in Philippians 1:7,[^o12] and in "giving an answer" in 1 Peter 3:15.[^o13]

Although the term *apologetics* has Western, primarily Christian origins and is most frequently associated with the defense of Christianity, the term is sometimes used referring to the defense of any religion in formal debate involving religion.

## Apologetic positions {#Apologetic_positions}

### Baháʼí Faith {#Baháʼí_Faith}

Many [apologetic books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_literature#Apologia) have been written in defence of the history or teachings of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_Faith). The religion's founders wrote several books presenting proofs of their religion; among them are the [Báb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Báb)'s *[Seven Proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selections_from_the_Writings_of_the_Báb#Dalá'il-i-Sab'ih_%28The_Seven_Proofs%29)* and [Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahá'u'lláh)'s *[Kitáb-i-Íqán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitáb-i-Íqán)*.[^o16] Later Baháʼí authors wrote prominent apologetic texts, such as [Mírzá Abu'l-Fadl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mírzá_Abu'l-Fadl)'s *The Brilliant Proof* and [Udo Schaefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udo_Schaefer) et al.'s *Making the Crooked Straight*.[^o17]

### Buddhism {#Buddhism}

One of the earliest Buddhist apologetic texts is *[The Questions of King Milinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milinda_Panha)*, which deals with the Buddhist metaphysics such as [the "no-self" nature of the individual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatta) and characteristics such as wisdom, perception, volition, feeling, consciousness and the soul. In the [Meiji Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_%28era%29) (1868–1912), encounters between Buddhists and Christians in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Japan) as a result of increasing contact between [Japan and other nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_nationalism#Meiji_period_beginnings_1868–1912) may have prompted the formation of [Japanese New Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_modernism#Japan:_Neo-Buddhism),[^o19] including the apologetic Shin Bukkyō (新仏教) magazine.[^o20] In recent times, A. L. De Silva, an Australian convert to [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism), has written a book, *Beyond Belief*, providing Buddhist apologetic responses and a critique of Christian Fundamentalist doctrine.[^o21] Gunapala Dharmasiri wrote an apologetic critique of the [Christian concept of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Christianity) from a [Theravadin Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada) perspective.[^o22]

### Christianity {#Christianity}

*Image: Shield-Trinity-Scutum-Fidei-English.svg.* The [Shield of the Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield_of_the_Trinity), a diagram frequently used by [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) apologists to explain the [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity)

Christian apologetics combines [Christian theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_theology), [natural theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_theology),[^o24] and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) in an attempt to present a rational basis for the Christian faith, to defend the faith against objections and misrepresentation, and to show that the Christian doctrine is the only world-view that is faultless and consistent with all fundamental knowledge and questions.

Christian apologetics has taken many forms over the centuries. In the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), Christians were severely persecuted, and many charges were brought against them. Examples in the Bible include the Apostle Paul's address to the Athenians in the [Areopagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areopagus) ([Acts 17: 22-34](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2017%3A22-34&version=NIV)). J. David Cassel[^o25] gives several examples: [Tacitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacitus) wrote that [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero) fabricated charges that Christians started the [burning of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_Rome).[^o26] Other charges included [cannibalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_cannibalism) (due to a literal interpretation of the [Eucharist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist)) and [incest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incest) (due to early Christians' practice of addressing each other as "brother" and "sister"). [Paul the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle), [Justin Martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Martyr), [Irenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus), and others often defended Christianity against charges that were brought to justify persecution.[^o27]

Later apologists have focused on providing reasons to accept various aspects of Christian belief. Christian apologists of many traditions, in common with Jews, Muslims, and some others, argue for the existence of a unique and personal God. [Theodicy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodicy) is one important aspect of such arguments, and [Alvin Plantinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Plantinga)'s arguments have been highly influential in this area. Many prominent Christian apologists are scholarly philosophers or theologians, frequently with additional doctoral work in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology), [comparative religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_religions), and other fields. Others take a more popular or pastoral approach. Some prominent modern apologists are [Douglas Groothuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Groothuis), [Frederick Copleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Copleston), [John Lennox](wikipedia://en/19722743), [Walter R. Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Ralston_Martin), [Dinesh D'Souza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinesh_D'Souza), [Douglas Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Wilson_%28theologian%29), [Cornelius Van Til](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Van_Til), [Gordon Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Clark), [Francis Schaeffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Schaeffer), [Greg Bahnsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Bahnsen), [Edward John Carnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_John_Carnell), [James White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_White_%28theologian%29), [R. C. Sproul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._C._Sproul), [Hank Hanegraaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Hanegraaff), [Alister McGrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alister_McGrath), [Lee Strobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Strobel), [Josh McDowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_McDowell), [Peter Kreeft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kreeft), [G. K. Chesterton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._K._Chesterton), [William Lane Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lane_Craig), [J. P. Moreland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._P._Moreland), [Hugh Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Ross_%28creationist%29), [David Bentley Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bentley_Hart), [Gary Habermas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Habermas), [Norman Geisler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Geisler), [Scott Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Hahn), RC Kunst, [Trent Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Horn), and [Jimmy Akin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Akin).[^o28]

Apologists in the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) include [Bishop Robert Barron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Barron_%28bishop%29),[^o29] [G. K. Chesterton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._K._Chesterton),[^o30] [Dr. Scott Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Hahn), Trent Horn, [Jimmy Akin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Akin), [Patrick Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Madrid), Kenneth Hensley,[^o31] [Karl Keating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Keating), [Ronald Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Knox), and [Peter Kreeft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kreeft).

[St. John Henry Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Henry_Newman) (1801–1890) was an English convert to [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church), later made a [cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_%28Catholicism%29), [canonized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization) in 2019, and proclaimed a [Doctor of the Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church) by [Pope Leo XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIV) in 2025. In early life, he was a major figure in the [Oxford Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Movement) to bring the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England) back to its Catholic roots. Eventually his studies in history persuaded him to become a Roman Catholic. When John Henry Newman entitled his spiritual autobiography *[Apologia Pro Vita Sua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apologia_Pro_Vita_Sua)* in 1864, he was playing upon both this connotation, and the more commonly understood meaning of an expression of contrition or regret.

Christian apologists employ a variety of philosophical and formal approaches, including [ontological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontological_argument), [cosmological](wikipedia://en/6516), and [teleological arguments](wikipedia://en/30731).[^o32] The Christian presuppositionalist approach to apologetics uses the [transcendental argument for the existence of God](wikipedia://en/48044).[^o33]

[Tertullian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertullian) was an early [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) apologist. He was born, lived, and died in [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage). He is sometimes known as the "Father of the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church)". He introduced the term *[Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity)* (trinitas) to the Christian vocabulary[^o34] and probably the formula "three Persons, one Substance" as the Latin "tres [Personae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona), [una Substantia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consubstantial)" (from the [Koine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koine_Greek) "treis [Hypostaseis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypostasis_%28religion%29), [Homoousios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ousios)"), and the terms *Vetus Testamentum* ([Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament)) and *Novum Testamentum* ([New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament)).

### Deism {#Deism}

[Deism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deism) is a form of [theism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theism) in which God created the universe and established rationally comprehensible moral and natural laws but no longer intervenes in human affairs. Deism is a [natural religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_religion) where belief in God is based on application of reason and evidence observed in the designs and laws found in nature. The World Order of Deists maintains a web site presenting deist apologetics that demonstrate the existence of God based on evidence and reason, absent divine revelation.

### Hinduism {#Hinduism}

Hindu apologetics began developing during the British colonial period. A number of Indian intellectuals had become critical of the British tendency to devalue the Hindu religious tradition. As a result, these Indian intellectuals, as well as a handful of British [Indologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indology), were galvanized to examine the roots of the religion as well as to study its vast arcana and corpus in an analytical fashion. This endeavor drove the deciphering and preservation of [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit). Many translations of Hindu texts were produced which made them accessible to a broader reading audience.

In the early 18th century, Christian missionary [Bartholomäus Ziegenbalg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomäus_Ziegenbalg) engaged in dialogues with several [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language)-speaking [Malabarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabars) Hindu priests, and recorded arguments of these Hindu apologists. These records include German-language reports submitted to the Lutheran headquarters in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_%28Saale%29), and 99 letters written by the Hindu priests to him (later translated into German under the title *Malabarische Korrespondenz* from 1718 onwards).[^n1]

During 1830–1831, missionary [John Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilson_%28Scottish_missionary%29) engaged in debates with Hindu apologists in [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency).[^n2] In 1830, his protege Ram Chandra, a Hindu convert to Christianity, debated with several Hindu [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) apologists in public. Hindu [pandit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandit) [Morobhatt Dandekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morobhatt_Dandekar) summarized his arguments from his 1831 debate with Wilson in a Marathi-language work titled *Shri-Hindu-dharma-sthapana*.[^n3] Narayana Rao, another Hindu apologist, wrote *[Svadesha-dharma-abhimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svadesha-dharmabhimani)* in response to Wilson.[^n4]

In the mid-19th century, several Hindu apologist works were written in response to [John Muir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Muir_%28indologist%29)'s *[Mataparīkṣā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mataparīkṣā)*. These include *[Mata-parīkṣā-śikṣā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mata-parīkṣā-śikṣā)* (1839) by [Somanatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaji_Bapu) of [Central India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India_Agency), *[Mataparīkṣottara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mataparīkṣottara)* (1840) by Harachandra Tarkapanchanan of [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta), *[Śāstra-tattva-vinirṇaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śāstra-tattva-vinirṇaya)* (1844–1845) by [Nilakantha Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilakantha_Gore) of [Benares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benares),[^n5] and a critique (published later in 1861 as part of *Dharmādharma-parīkṣā-patra*) by an unknown [Vaishnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnava) writer.[^n6]

A range of Indian philosophers, including [Swami Vivekananda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swami_Vivekananda) and [Aurobindo Ghose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurobindo_Ghose), have written rational explanations regarding the values of the Hindu religious tradition. More modern proponents such as the [Maharishi Mahesh Yogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharishi_Mahesh_Yogi) have also tried to correlate recent developments from [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics) and [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness) research with Hindu concepts. The late Reverend [Pandurang Shastri Athavale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandurang_Shastri_Athavale) has given a plethora of discourses regarding the symbolism and rational basis for many principles in the [Vedic tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veda). In his book *The Cradle of Civilization*, David Frawley, an American who has embraced the Vedic tradition, has characterized the ancient texts of the Hindu heritage as being like "pyramids of the spirit".

:::

### Islam {#Islam}

**Ilm al-Kalām*, literally "science of discourse",[^o47] usually foreshortened to*kalam*and sometimes called*Islamic scholastic theology*, is an Islamic undertaking born out of the need to establish and defend the tenets of Islamic faith against skeptics and detractors.[^o48] A scholar of*kalam*is referred to as a*[mutakallim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalam)*(plural*mutakallimūn*) as distinguished from philosophers, jurists, and scientists.[^o49]***

### Judaism {#Judaism}

Jewish apologetic literature can be traced back as far as [Aristobulus of Paneas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristobulus_of_Paneas), though some discern it in the works of [Demetrius the Chronographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_the_Chronographer) (3rd century BCE) traces of the style of "questions" and "solutions" typical of the genre. Aristobulus was a Jewish philosopher of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) and the author of an apologetic work addressed to [Ptolemy VI Philometor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_VI_Philometor). [Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus)'s [Contra Apion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contra_Apion) is a wide-ranging defense of [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) against many charges laid against Judaism at that time, as too are some of the works of [Philo of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philo_of_Alexandria).[^o51][^o52]

In response to modern Christian missionaries, and congregations that "are designed to appear Jewish, but are actually fundamentalist Christian churches, which use traditional Jewish symbols to lure the most vulnerable of our Jewish people into their ranks",[^o53] [Jews for Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_for_Judaism) is the largest counter-missionary organization in existence, today. [Kiruv Organization (Mizrachi)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiruv_Organization_%28Mizrachi%29), founded by Rabbi Yosef Mizrachi, and Outreach Judaism, founded by Rabbi [Tovia Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tovia_Singer), are other prominent international organizations that respond "directly to the issues raised by missionaries and cults, by exploring Judaism in contradistinction to fundamentalist Christianity."[^o54][^o55]

### Pantheism {#Pantheism}

Some pantheists have formed organizations such as the World Pantheist Movement and the [Universal Pantheist Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pantheist_Society) to promote and defend the belief in [pantheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheism).[^o57]

### Native Americans {#Native_Americans}

In a famous speech called "Red Jacket on Religion for the White Man and the Red" in 1805, [Seneca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca_tribe) chief [Red Jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Jacket) gave an apologetic for [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) religion.[^o59]

## In literature {#In_literature}

Plato's *Apology* may be read as both a religious and literary apology; however, more specifically literary examples may be found in the [prefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preface) and [dedications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedication_%28publishing%29), which precede many [Early Modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period) plays, novels, and poems. Eighteenth century authors such as [Colley Cibber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colley_Cibber), [Frances Burney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Burney), and [William Congreve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Congreve), to name but a few, prefaced the majority of their poetic work with such apologies. In addition to the desire to defend their work, the apologetic preface often suggests the author's attempt to humble his- or herself before the audience.[^o61]
