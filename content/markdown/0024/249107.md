# Gaston Julia

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaston Julia"}

**Gaston Maurice Julia** (3 February 1893 – 19 March 1978) was a French mathematician who devised the formula for the [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434). His works were popularized by [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999); the Julia and Mandelbrot [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913) are closely related. He founded, independently with [Pierre Fatou](wikipedia://en/629100), the modern theory of [holomorphic dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_dynamics).

:::

## Military service {#Military_service}

Julia was born in the [Algerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria) town of [Sidi Bel Abbès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_Bel_Abbès), at the time governed by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Algeria). During his youth, he had an interest in mathematics and music. His studies were interrupted at the age of 21, when France became involved in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) and Julia was conscripted to serve with the army. During an attack he suffered a severe injury, losing his nose. His many operations to remedy the situation were all unsuccessful, and for the rest of his life he resigned himself to wearing a leather strap around the area where his nose had been.

## Career in mathematics {#Career_in_mathematics}

Julia gained attention for his mathematical work at the age of 25, in 1918, when his 199-page *Mémoire sur l'itération des fonctions rationnelles* ("Memoir on the Iteration of Rational Functions") was featured in the *[Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_de_Mathématiques_Pures_et_Appliquées)*.[^o5] This article gained immense popularity among mathematicians and earned him the *Grand Prix des Sciences Mathématiques* of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1918. But after this brief moment of fame, his works were mostly forgotten[^o6] until [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999) mentioned them in his works on [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913) in the French book *Les Objets Fractals: Forme, Hasard et Dimension*, later translated in 1977 as *Fractals: Form, Chance and Dimension*.[^o7]

On 19 March 1978, Julia died in Paris at the age of 85.

Julia was also father to [Marc Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Julia),[^o8] the French organic chemist who invented the [Julia olefination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_olefination).

## World War II and collaboration {#World_War_II_and_collaboration}

Julia [collaborated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany_and_Fascist_Italy) with [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) during the [occupation of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_France); recruiting French mathematicians to collaborate with the [Zentralblatt für Mathematik](wikipedia://en/1701961).[^o10] He was suspended for a few weeks after the [liberation of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_France),[^o11] but according to [Michèle Audin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michèle_Audin):[^o12] This was followed by no sanction, as the epuration committee was (unanimously...) too impressed by his status of "[gueule cassée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gueules_cassées)" to do anything. Then he resumed his normal activities, professor at the Sorbonne and l'Ecole Polytechnique, was president of the Académie des Sciences in 1950, etc.
