# Summation by parts

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Summation by parts"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **summation by parts** transforms the [summation](wikipedia://en/246160) of products of [sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequences) into other summations, often simplifying the computation or (especially) estimation of certain types of sums. It is also called **Abel's lemma** or **Abel transformation**, named after [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573) who introduced it in 1826.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose $\{f_k\}$ and $\{g_k\}$ are two [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838). Then,

$$\sum_{k=m}^n f_k(g_{k+1}-g_k) = \left(f_{n+1}g_{n+1} - f_m g_m\right) - \sum_{k=m}^n g_{k+1}(f_{k+1}- f_{k}).$$

Using the [forward difference operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_difference_operator) $\Delta$, it can be stated more succinctly as

$$\sum_{k=m}^n f_k\Delta g_k = \left(f_{n+1} g_{n+1} - f_m g_m\right) - \sum_{k=m}^{n} g_{k+1}\Delta f_k,$$

Or, equivalently, using [backward difference operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_difference_operator) $\nabla$:

$$\sum_{k=m}^n f_k\nabla g_k = \left(f_n g_n - f_{m-1} g_{m-1}\right) - \sum_{k=m}^{n} g_{k-1}\nabla f_k,$$

Both can be used to obtain a more symmetric version:

$$\sum_{k=m}^n \left(f_k \Delta g_k + g_k \nabla f_k\right) = f_n g_{n+1} - f_{m-1} g_m$$

Summation by parts is an analogue to [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252):

$$\int f\,dg = f g - \int g\,df,$$

or to [Abel's summation formula](wikipedia://en/9747155):

$$\sum_{k=m+1}^n f(k)(g_{k}-g_{k-1})= \left(f(n)g_{n} - f(m) g_m\right) - \int_{m}^n g_{\lfloor t \rfloor} f'(t) dt.$$

An alternative statement is

$$f_n g_n - f_m g_m = \sum_{k=m}^{n-1} f_k\Delta g_k + \sum_{k=m}^{n-1} g_k\Delta f_k + \sum_{k=m}^{n-1} \Delta f_k \Delta g_k$$

which is analogous to the [integration by parts formula for semimartingales](wikipedia://en/2143889#Semimartingales).

Although applications almost always deal with convergence of sequences, the statement is purely algebraic and will work in any [field](wikipedia://en/10603). It will also work when one sequence is in a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), and the other is in the relevant field of scalars.

:::

## Newton series {#Newton_series}

The formula is sometimes given in one of these - slightly different - forms

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{k=0}^n f_k g_k &= f_0 \sum_{k=0}^n g_k+ \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} (f_{j+1}-f_j) \sum_{k=j+1}^n g_k\\ &= f_n \sum_{k=0}^n g_k - \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \left( f_{j+1}- f_j\right) \sum_{k=0}^j g_k, \end{aligned}$$

which represent a special case ($M = 1$) of the more general rule

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{k=0}^n f_k g_k &= \sum_{i=0}^{M-1} f_0^{(i)} G_{i}^{(i+1)}+ \sum_{j=0}^{n-M} f^{(M)}_{j} G_{j+M}^{(M)} = \\ &= \sum_{i=0}^{M-1} \left( -1 \right)^i f_{n-i}^{(i)} \tilde{G}_{n-i}^{(i+1)}+ \left( -1 \right) ^{M} \sum_{j=0}^{n-M} f_j^{(M)} \tilde{G}_j^{(M)}; \end{aligned}$$

both result from iterated application of the initial formula. The auxiliary quantities are [Newton series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_series):

$$f_j^{(M)}:= \sum_{k=0}^M \left(-1 \right)^{M-k} {M \choose k} f_{j+k}$$

and

$$G_j^{(M)}:= \sum_{k=j}^n {k-j+M-1 \choose M-1} g_k,$$

$$\tilde{G}_j^{(M)}:= \sum_{k=0}^j {j-k+M-1 \choose M-1} g_k.$$

A particular ($M=n+1$) result is the identity

$$\sum_{k=0}^n f_k g_k = \sum_{i=0}^n f_0^{(i)} G_i^{(i+1)} = \sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i f_{n-i}^{(i)} \tilde{G}_{n-i}^{(i+1)}.$$

Here, ${n \choose k}$ is the [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668).

::: {#o7 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Method"}

## Method {#Method}

For two given sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$, with $n \in \mathbb{N}$, one wants to study the sum of the following series:

$$S_N = \sum_{n=0}^N a_n b_n$$

If we define $\displaystyle B_n = \sum_{k=0}^n b_k,$ then for every $n>0,$ $b_n = B_n - B_{n-1}$ and

$$S_N = a_0 b_0 + \sum_{n=1}^N a_n (B_n - B_{n-1}),$$

$$S_N = a_0 b_0 - a_1 B_0 + a_N B_N + \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} B_n (a_n - a_{n+1}).$$

Finally $\displaystyle S_N = a_N B_N - \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} B_n (a_{n+1} - a_n).$

This process, called an Abel transformation, can be used to prove several criteria of convergence for $S_N$.

:::

## Similarity with an integration by parts {#Similarity_with_an_integration_by_parts}

The formula for an integration by parts is $\displaystyle \int_a^b f(x) g'(x)\,dx = \left[ f(x) g(x) \right]_{a}^{b} - \int_a^b f'(x) g(x)\,dx$.

Beside the [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions), we notice that the first integral contains two multiplied functions, one which is integrated in the final integral ($g'$ becomes $g$) and one which is differentiated ($f$ becomes $f'$).

The process of the *Abel transformation* is similar, since one of the two initial sequences is summed ($b_n$ becomes $B_n$) and the other one is differenced ($a_n$ becomes $a_{n+1} - a_n$).

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

- It is used to prove [Kronecker's lemma](wikipedia://en/3759614), which in turn, is used to prove a version of the strong [law of large numbers](wikipedia://en/157055) under [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) constraints.
- It may be used to prove [Nicomachus's theorem](wikipedia://en/4924488) that the sum of the first $n$ cubes equals the square of the sum of the first $n$ positive integers.[^o11]
- Summation by parts is frequently used to prove [Abel's theorem](wikipedia://en/237305) and [Dirichlet's test](wikipedia://en/3141677).
- One can also use this technique to prove [Abel's test](wikipedia://en/7088921): If $\sum_n b_n$ is a [convergent series](wikipedia://en/1743431), and $a_n$ a bounded [monotone sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_sequence), then $S_N = \sum_{n=0}^N a_n b_n$ converges.

::: {#o12 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof of Abel's test"}

**Proof of Abel's test.** Summation by parts gives

$$\begin{aligned} S_M - S_N &= a_M B_M - a_N B_N - \sum_{n=N}^{M-1} B_n (a_{n+1} - a_n)\\ &= (a_M-a) B_M - (a_N-a) B_N + a(B_M - B_N) - \sum_{n=N}^{M-1} B_n (a_{n+1} - a_n), \end{aligned}$$

where *a* is the limit of $a_n$. As $\sum_n b_n$ is convergent, $B_N$ is bounded independently of $N$, say by $B$. As $a_n-a$ go to zero, so go the first two terms. The third term goes to zero by the [Cauchy criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_criterion) for $\sum_n b_n$. The remaining sum is bounded by

$$\sum_{n=N}^{M-1} |B_n| |a_{n+1}-a_n| \le B \sum_{n=N}^{M-1} |a_{n+1}-a_n| = B|a_N - a_M|$$

by the monotonicity of $a_n$, and also goes to zero as $N \to \infty$.

Using the same proof as above, one can show that if

1. the partial sums $B_N$ form a [bounded sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_sequence) independently of $N$;
1. $\sum_{n=0}^\infty |a_{n+1} - a_n| < \infty$ (so that the sum $\sum_{n=N}^{M-1} |a_{n+1}-a_n|$ goes to zero as $N$ goes to infinity)
1. $$\lim a_n = 0$$

then $S_N = \sum_{n=0}^N a_n b_n$ converges.

In both cases, the sum of the series satisfies:

$$|S| = \left|\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n b_n \right| \le B \sum_{n=0}^\infty |a_{n+1}-a_n|.$$

:::

## Summation-by-parts operators for high order finite difference methods {#Summation-by-parts_operators_for_high_order_finite_difference_methods}

A summation-by-parts (SBP) finite difference operator conventionally consists of a centered difference interior scheme and specific boundary stencils that mimics behaviors of the corresponding integration-by-parts formulation.[^o14][^o15] The boundary conditions are usually imposed by the Simultaneous-Approximation-Term (SAT) technique.[^o16] The combination of SBP-SAT is a powerful framework for boundary treatment. The method is preferred for well-proven stability for long-time simulation, and high order of accuracy.
