# Restricted product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Restricted product"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **restricted product** is a construction in the theory of [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315).

:::

Let $I$ be an [index set](wikipedia://en/393055); $S$ a [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of $I$. If $G_i$ is a [locally compact group](wikipedia://en/1651204) for each $i \in I$, and $K_i \subset G_i$ is an open [compact](wikipedia://en/750326) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) for each $i \in I \setminus S$, then the restricted product

$$\prod_i\nolimits' G_i\,$$

is the subset of the product of the $G_i$'s consisting of all elements $(g_i)_{i \in I}$ such that $g_i \in K_i$ for all but finitely many $i \in I \setminus S$.

This group is given the [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) whose [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) of [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) are those of the form

$$\prod_i A_i\,,$$

where $A_i$ is open in $G_i$ and $A_i = K_i$ for all but finitely many $i$.

One can easily prove that the restricted product is itself a locally compact group. The best known example of this construction is that of the [adele ring](wikipedia://en/247978) and [idele group](wikipedia://en/1168614) of a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358).
