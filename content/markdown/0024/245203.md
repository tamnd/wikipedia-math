# Sphenic number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphenic number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **sphenic number** (from σφήν, 'wedge') is a [positive integer](wikipedia://en/21474) that is the [product](wikipedia://en/24977) of three distinct [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).p. 147 For example, since 2, 3, and 73 are all prime, 438 is a sphenic number because $2 \times 3 \times 73 = 438$.p. 147 Because there are [infinitely many prime numbers](wikipedia://en/3143591), there are also infinitely many sphenic numbers.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A sphenic number is a product $pqr$ where $p$, $q$, and $r$ are three distinct prime numbers. In other words, the sphenic numbers are the [square-free](wikipedia://en/29525) 3-[almost primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_prime).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The smallest sphenic number is $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$, the product of the smallest three primes. The first few sphenic numbers are

[30](wikipedia://en/341668), [42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42_%28number%29), [66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/66_%28number%29), [70](wikipedia://en/343334), [78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/78_%28number%29), [102](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/102_%28number%29), [105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/105_%28number%29), [110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/110_%28number%29), [114](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/114_%28number%29), [130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/130_%28number%29), [138](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/138_%28number%29), [154](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/154_%28number%29), [165](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/165_%28number%29), ...

The largest known sphenic number at any time can be obtained by multiplying together the three [largest known primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_known_prime).

:::

## Divisors {#Divisors}

All sphenic numbers have exactly eight divisors. If we express the sphenic number as $n = p \times q \times r$, where $p$, $q$, and $r$ are distinct primes, then the set of divisors of $n$ will be:

$$\left\{ 1, \ p, \ q, \ r, \ pq, \ pr, \ qr, \ n \right\}.$$

The converse does not hold. For example, 24 is not a sphenic number, but it has exactly eight divisors.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

All sphenic numbers are by definition [squarefree](wikipedia://en/29525), because the prime factors must be distinct.

The [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) of any sphenic number is [−1.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-1)

The [cyclotomic polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_polynomials) $\Phi_n(x)$, taken over all sphenic numbers *n*, may contain arbitrarily large coefficients[^o9] (for *n* a product of two primes the coefficients are $\pm 1$ or 0).

Any multiple of a sphenic number (except by 1) is not sphenic. This is easily provable by the multiplication process at a minimum adding another prime factor, or raising an existing factor to a higher power.

:::

## Consecutive sphenic numbers {#Consecutive_sphenic_numbers}

The first case of two consecutive sphenic integers is $230 = 2 \times 5 \times 23$ and $231 = 3 \times 7 \times 11$. The first case of three is $1309 = 7 \times 11 \times 17$, $1310 = 2 \times 5 \times 131$, and $1311 = 3 \times 19 \times 23$. There is no case of more than three, because every fourth consecutive positive integer is divisible by $4 = 2 \times 2$ and therefore not squarefree .

The numbers $2013 = 3 \times 11 \times 61$, $2014 = 2 \times 19 \times 53$, and $2015 = 5 \times 13 \times 31$ are all sphenic. The next three consecutive sphenic years will be $2665 = 5 \times 13 \times 41$, $2666 = 2 \times 31 \times 43$ and $2667 = 3 \times 7 \times 127$ .
