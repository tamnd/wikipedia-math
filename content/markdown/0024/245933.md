# John Tate (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Tate"}

**John Torrence Tate Jr.** (March 13, 1925 – October 16, 2019) was an American mathematician distinguished for many fundamental contributions in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), and related areas in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He was awarded the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2010.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Tate was born in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis), on March 13, 1925. His father, [John Tate Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Torrence_Tate_Sr.), was a professor of physics at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) and a longtime editor of *[Physical Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review)*. His mother, Lois Beatrice Fossler, was a high school English teacher. Tate Jr. received his bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1946 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and entered the doctoral program in physics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He later transferred to the [mathematics department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Department_of_Mathematics) and received his PhD in mathematics in 1950 after completing a doctoral dissertation titled ["Fourier analysis in number fields and Hecke's zeta functions"](wikipedia://en/3149490) under the supervision of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351).[^o4] Tate taught at Harvard for 36 years before joining the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) in 1990 as a Sid W. Richardson Foundation Regents Chair. He retired from the Texas mathematics department in 2009 and returned to Harvard as a professor emeritus.[^o19]

Tate died at his home in [Lexington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Massachusetts) on October 16, 2019, aged 94.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

[Tate's thesis](wikipedia://en/3149490) (1950) on [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) in [number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822) has become one of the ingredients for the modern theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) and their [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283), notably by its use of the [adele ring](wikipedia://en/247978), its self-duality and harmonic analysis on it; independently and a little earlier, [Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa) obtained a similar theory. Together with his advisor [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351), Tate gave a cohomological treatment of [global class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) using techniques of [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) applied to the [idele class group](wikipedia://en/1168608) and [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315).[^o10] This treatment made more transparent some of the algebraic structures in the previous approaches to class field theory, which used central division algebras to compute the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) of a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358).

Subsequently, Tate introduced what are now known as [Tate cohomology groups](wikipedia://en/6070279). In the decades following that discovery he extended the reach of Galois cohomology with the [Poitou–Tate duality](wikipedia://en/33900144), the [Tate–Shafarevich group](wikipedia://en/25711736), and relations with [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500). With [Jonathan Lubin](wikipedia://en/33546642), he recast [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532) by the use of [formal groups](wikipedia://en/480335), creating the [Lubin–Tate local theory](wikipedia://en/31457610) of [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183).

He has also made a number of individual and important contributions to [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) theory; for example, Tate's invention of [rigid analytic spaces](wikipedia://en/5828941) can be said to have spawned the entire field of [rigid analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/5828941). He found a *p*-adic analogue of [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), now called [Hodge–Tate theory](wikipedia://en/34197327), which has blossomed into another central technique of modern [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).[^o10] Other innovations of his include the "[Tate curve](wikipedia://en/35189927)" parametrization for certain *p*-adic [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) and the [*p*-divisible (Tate–Barsotti) groups](wikipedia://en/29750969).

Many of his results were not immediately published and some of them were written up by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991), [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), [Joseph H. Silverman](wikipedia://en/2288703) and others. Tate and Serre collaborated on a paper on [good reduction](wikipedia://en/3141761#G) of [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152). The classification of abelian varieties over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) was carried out by [Taira Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taira_Honda) and Tate (the [Honda–Tate theorem](wikipedia://en/32458611)).[^o11]

The [Tate conjectures](wikipedia://en/3124804) are the equivalent for [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) of the [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518). They relate to the Galois action on the ℓ-adic cohomology of an algebraic variety, identifying a space of "[Tate cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_cycle)" (the fixed cycles for a suitably [Tate-twisted](wikipedia://en/3094946) action) that conjecturally picks out the algebraic cycles. A special case of the conjectures, which are open in the general case, was involved in the proof of the [Mordell conjecture](wikipedia://en/172199) by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226).

Tate has also had a major influence on the development of number theory through his role as a Ph.D. advisor. His students include [George Bergman](wikipedia://en/41052556), [Ted Chinburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Chinburg), [Bernard Dwork](wikipedia://en/1678313), [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863), [Robert Kottwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kottwitz), [Jonathan Lubin](wikipedia://en/33546642), [Stephen Lichtenbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lichtenbaum), [James Milne](wikipedia://en/36324636), [V. Kumar Murty](wikipedia://en/34926069), [Carl Pomerance](wikipedia://en/2134159), [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850), [Joseph H. Silverman](wikipedia://en/2288703), [Dinesh Thakur](wikipedia://en/40714911), and [William C. Waterhouse](wikipedia://en/26101498).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1956, Tate was awarded the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) for outstanding contributions to number theory. He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1958.[^o13] He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1969.[^o14] In 1992, he was elected as Foreign Member of the French Académie des Sciences. In 1995, he received the [Leroy P. Steele Prize for Lifetime Achievement](wikipedia://en/1252550) from the American Mathematical Society. He was awarded a [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2002/03 for his creation of fundamental concepts in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).[^o15] In 2012, he became a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.[^o16]

In 2010, the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters), of which he was a member,[^o17] awarded him the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285), citing "his vast and lasting impact on the theory of numbers". According to a release by the Abel Prize committee, "Many of the major lines of research in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) are only possible because of the incisive contributions and illuminating insights of John Tate. He has truly left a conspicuous imprint on modern mathematics."[^o18]

Tate has been described as "one of the seminal mathematicians for the past half-century" by [William Beckner](wikipedia://en/30357387), Chairman of the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin).[^o19]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Tate married twice. His first wife was Karin Artin, his doctoral advisor's daughter. Together they had three daughters, six grandchildren, and a great-grandson. One of his grandchildren, [Dustin Clausen](wikipedia://en/76038574), is a professor of mathematics at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques). After Tate divorced, he married Carol MacPherson.[^o21]
