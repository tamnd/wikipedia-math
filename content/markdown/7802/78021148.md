# Philippine Math Olympiad

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philippine Math Olympiad"}

The **Philippine Mathematical Olympiad** is a [secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school) [mathematics competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_competition) among secondary school students.[^o2] First held in 1984, the PMO was created as a venue for high school students with interest and talent in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) to compete in a very hard contest.

:::

It is the oldest and the most prominent nationwide mathematics competition among secondary school students in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines). It is regarded as the first step to representing their country at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) (IMO). It has three stages: two regional and one national.[^o3]

The [Mathematical Society of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Society_of_the_Philippines) (MSP) selects a team from the top 20 PMO national finalists to represent the Philippines at the [IMO](wikipedia://en/82208). The Philippines first participated in the IMO in 1988.

## Winners {#Winners}

Some PMO winners include:

- Andres Rico Gonzales III:[^o5] Highest country rank in 2020
- Albert John Patupat:[^o6] Highest country rank in 2018
- Farrell Eldrian Wu, a naturalized Filipino,[^o7] Highest country rank in 2016
  - Incidentally, Farrell graduated in [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) with a perfect GPA.[^o8]
