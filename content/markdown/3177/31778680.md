# Georg Scheffers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Georg Scheffers"}

*Image: Georg Scheffers.jpg.*

**Georg Wilhelm Scheffers** (21 November 1866 – 12 August 1945) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Scheffers was born on 21 November 1866 in the village of Altendorf near [Holzminden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzminden) (today incorporated into Holzminden). Scheffers began his university career at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) where he studied with [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) and [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010). Scheffers was a coauthor with Lie for three of the earliest expressions of [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681):

- *Lectures on [Differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) with known [Infinitesimal transformations](wikipedia://en/658068)* (1893),
- *Lectures on [Continuous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_group)* (1893), and
- *Geometry of [Contact Transformations](wikipedia://en/476398)* (1896).

All three are now available online through [archive.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archive.org).

In 1896 Scheffers became [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) at *Technische Hochschule Darmstadt* (now [TU Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Darmstadt)), where he was raised to [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 1900. From 1907 to 1935, when he retired, Scheffers was a professor at *Technische Hochschule Berlin* (now [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin)).

In 1901–1902 he published a famous two-volume textbook entitled *Anwendung der Differential- und Integralrechnung auf die Geometrie* (application of differential and integral calculus to geometry). The first volume subtitled *Einführung in die Theorie der Curven in der Ebene und in Raum* was published in 1901 and dealt with [curves](wikipedia://en/89246).[^o3] The second volume subtitled *Einführung in die Theorie der Flächen* (introduction to the theory of [surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28differential_geometry%29)) was published in 1902.[^o4] A second edition was published in 1910 (volume 2, 1913), and a third edition in 1922.[^o5]

In 1907 Scheffers published the first two volumes of his extensive revision and rewriting of [Georg Bohlmann](wikipedia://en/40245486)'s 1897–1899 revision of [Harnack](wikipedia://en/1925276)'s 1884 German translation of [Serret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Alfred_Serret)'s famous two-volume *Cours de calcul différentiel et intégral*,[^o6] which was first published by Gauthier-Villars in 1868.[^o7] In 1909 Scheffers published the third and last volume of his rewriting of Bohlman's version of Serret's two-volume work.[^o8] For a new edition, Scheffers added an appendix with 46 pages of historical notes for the first and second volumes.[^o9]

Another very successful book was prepared for students of science and technology: *Lehrbuch der Mathematik* (textbook of mathematics).[^o10] It provided an introduction to [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) as well as [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) of [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) and [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532). In 1958 this book was republished for the fourteenth time.

Scheffers is known for an article on special transcendental curves (including [W-curves](wikipedia://en/27126377)) which appeared in the [*Enzyklopädie der mathematischen Wissenschaften*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein's_encyclopedia) in 1903: "Besondere transzendenten Kurven" (special transcendental curves). He wrote on translation surfaces for [Acta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/6860708) in 1904: ["Das Abel'sche und das Lie'sche Theorem über Translationsflächen"](http://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=njp.32101044932000;view=1up;seq=81) (the theorem of [Abel](wikipedia://en/21573) and [Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) on translation surfaces).

Other books written by Scheffers are [*Lehrbuch der darstellenden Geometrie*](http://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000356983) (textbook on descriptive geometry) (1919),[^o11] *Allerhand aus der zeichnenden Geometrie* (1930), and *Wie findet und zeichnet man Gradnetze von Land- und Sternkarten?* (1934).

Georg Scheffers died 12 August 1945, in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin).

## Hypercomplex numbers {#Hypercomplex_numbers}

In 1891 Georg Scheffers contributed his article ["Zurückführung komplexer Zahlensysteme auf typische Formen"](http://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=hvd.32044102918125;view=1up;seq=317) to [Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381) (39:293–390). This article addressed a topic of considerable interest in the 1890s and contributed to the development of [modern algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). Scheffers distinguishes between a "Nichtquaternion system" (Nqss) and a Quaternion system (Qss). Scheffers characterizes the Qss as having three elements $e_1,\ e_2,\ e_3$ that satisfy (p 306)

$$e_1e_2-e_2e_1 = 2e_3, \quad e_2e_3-e_3e_2 = 2e_1, \quad e_3e_1-e_1e_3=2e_2.$$

In today's language, Scheffers' Qss has the [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) algebra as a [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239).

Scheffers anticipates the concepts of [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) of algebras and [direct sum of algebras](wikipedia://en/58899#Direct_sum_of_algebras) with his section (p 317) on reducibility, addition, and multiplication of systems. Thus Scheffers pioneered the structural approach to algebra.

Though the article covers new ground with its exploration of Nqss, it is also a [literature review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_review) going back to the work of [Hermann Hankel](wikipedia://en/932515). In §14 (p 386) Scheffers reviews both German and English authors on hypercomplex numbers. In particular, he cites [Eduard Study](wikipedia://en/663997)’s work of 1889. For volume 41 of *Mathematische Annalen* Scheffers contributed a further short note, this time including reference to 1867 work by [Edmond Laguerre](wikipedia://en/1011080) on linear systems, a rich source of hypercomplex numbers.
