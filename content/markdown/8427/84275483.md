# Wilhelmina Luten

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelmina Luten"}

**Wilhelmina Luten Seccombe Riley** (September 30, 1906 – October 1986) was an American statistician associated with federal government programs including the [Works Progress Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_Progress_Administration), the [Economic Cooperation Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Cooperation_Administration), the [Mutual Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_Security_Agency), and the [Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) (USAID).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Luten was born in [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana), the daughter of Daniel Benjamin Luten and Edith Heath Hull Luten.[^o4][^o5] Her father was a math and engineering instructor at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University).[^o6] She graduated from [Shortridge High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortridge_High_School) in 1923,[^o7][^o8] and from [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) in 1927,[^o9][^o10] with graduate studies at [Simmons College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmons_University).[^o11]

## Career {#Career}

Luten was a summer camp counselor after college,[^o13] and a fellow in the research department of the [Women's Educational and Industrial Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Educational_and_Industrial_Union) in Boston.[^o14][^o15] In 1932 she was statistician for the Detroit Department of Public Welfare.[^o21] In 1934 she was named supervisor of the [Federal Emergency Relief Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Emergency_Relief_Administration)'s field office in Louisiana.[^o16][^o17] She prepared reports on employment for the Works Progress Administration in the 1930s.[^o22][^o18] She was an analytical statistician at the Economic Cooperation Administration in the 1940s, and for the Mutual Security Agency[^o19] and the Agency for International Development (USAID).[^o27]

## Publications {#Publications}

- "Unemployment as a cause of family dependency" (1929)[^o14]
- "A Survey of 1200 Families, Detroit Department of Public Welfare" (1932)[^o21]
- *An analysis of employment of women on Works Progress Administration projects, December 1935 through May 1936* (1936, WPA report)[^o22]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Luten married twice. She married William T. Seccombe in 1949;[^o24] he died in 1961.[^o25] Her second husband was fellow statistician Donald C. Riley; he died in 1973.[^o26] She died in 1986, at the age of 80, at a hospital in northern Virginia.[^o27]
