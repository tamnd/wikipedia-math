# Boolean differential calculus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boolean differential calculus"}

**Boolean differential calculus** (**BDC**) (German: **Boolescher Differentialkalkül** (**BDK**)) is a subject field of [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/54476844) discussing changes of [Boolean variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_variable) and [Boolean functions](wikipedia://en/753349).

:::

Boolean differential calculus concepts are analogous to those of classical [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416), notably studying the changes in functions and variables with respect to each other.[^o2]

The Boolean differential calculus allows various aspects of [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632) such as [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356) on [finite automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_automata), [Petri net theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petri_net_theory),[^o22] and [supervisory control theory](wikipedia://en/3754843) (SCT), to be discussed in a united and closed form, with their individual advantages combined.

## History and applications {#History_and_applications}

Originally inspired by the design and testing of [switching circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switching_circuit) and the use of [error-correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error-correcting_code) in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), the roots for the development of what later would evolve into the Boolean differential calculus were initiated by works of [Irving S. Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_S._Reed),[^o7] [David E. Muller](wikipedia://en/35667779),[^o8] [David A. Huffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Huffman),[^o9] [Sheldon B. Akers Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_B._Akers)[^o10] and A. D. Talantsev (A. D. Talancev, А. Д. Таланцев)[^o11] between 1954 and 1959, and of [Frederick F. Sellers Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_F._Sellers),[^o12][^o13] [Mu-Yue Hsiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu-Yue_Hsiao),[^o12][^o13] and [Leroy W. Bearnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_W._Bearnson)[^o12][^o13] in 1968.

Since then, significant advances were accomplished in both, the theory and in the application of the BDC in switching circuit design and [logic synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_synthesis).

Works of [André Thayse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Thayse),[^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] [Marc Davio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Davio),[^o15][^o16][^o17] and [Jean-Pierre Deschamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Deschamps)[^o17] in the 1970s formed the basics of BDC on which Dieter Bochmann,[^o19] [Christian Posthoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Posthoff),[^o19] and Bernd Steinbach[^o20] further developed BDC into a self-contained mathematical theory later on.

A complementary theory of **Boolean integral calculus** (German: **Boolescher Integralkalkül**) has been developed as well.[^o19][^o26]

BDC has also found uses in [discrete event dynamic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_event_dynamic_system) (DEDS)[^o21] in [digital network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network) [communication protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_protocol).

Meanwhile, BDC has seen extensions to [multi-valued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-valued_function) variables and functions[^o19][^o23][^o24] as well as to [lattices](wikipedia://en/36565179) of Boolean functions.[^o25][^o27]

## Overview {#Overview}

Boolean [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886) play a significant role in BDC. They allow the application of [differentials](wikipedia://en/677191) as known from classical [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_%28mathematics%29) to be extended to logical functions.

The differential $dx$ of a Boolean variable $x$ models the relation

$$dx = \begin{cases} 0, & \text{no change of } x\\ 1, & \text{change of } x \end{cases}$$

There are no constraints in regard to the nature, the causes, and consequences of a change.

The differential $dx$ is binary and can be used just like common [binary variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_data).
