# Sally C. Morton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sally C. Morton"}

*Image: Sally C Morton portrait.jpg.* Sally C. Morton

**Sally C. Morton** is an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685) specializing in [comparative effectiveness research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_effectiveness_research). In 2021, Morton joined [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) as executive vice president of Knowledge Enterprise, the administrative subdivision of Arizona State involving university research. Morton is also a professor in the College of Health Solutions and the School of Mathematical Statistical Sciences and holds the Florence Ely Nelson Chair at Arizona State.

:::

Morton was educated at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics). Her PhD thesis, *Interpretable Projection Pursuit*, was completed at Stanford in 1990, under the tutelage of [Jerome H. Friedman](wikipedia://en/40671069). Before joining ASU, Morton was dean of the College of Science and professor of statistics at [Virginia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Tech). Prior to this role, she worked at [RTI International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTI_International), the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation) and the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh).

Morton was 2009 president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) (ASA) and 2013 chair of Section U (Statistics) of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (AAAS). She is a fellow of the ASA and of the AAAS, and an elected member of the Society for Research Synthesis Methodology and the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) (ISI). She won the [American Statistical Association Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224) in 2015 and is the 2017 winner of the Janet L. Norwood Award. She was elected to the [National Academy of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Medicine) in 2024.[^o2]
