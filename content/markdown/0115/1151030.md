# Nicolaus I Bernoulli

*Image: Epitaph für Nikolaus I Bernoulli.jpg.* Epitaph for Nikolaus I Bernoulli in the Peterskirche (Basel)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicolaus I Bernoulli"}

**Nicolaus Bernoulli** (also spelled **Nicolas** or **Nikolas**; 20 October in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel) – 29 November 1759 in Basel) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and was one of the many prominent mathematicians in the [Bernoulli family](wikipedia://en/199226).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Nicolaus Bernoulli was born on 20 October in Basel.[^n1] He was the son of Nicolaus Bernoulli, painter and Alderman of Basel. In 1704 he graduated from the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel) under [Jakob Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Bernoulli) and obtained his PhD five years later (in 1709) with a work on [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) in law. His thesis was titled *Dissertatio Inauguralis Mathematico-Juridica de Usu Artis Conjectandi in Jure*.[^o4]

In 1716 he obtained the [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo)-chair at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), where he worked on [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). In 1722 he returned to Switzerland and obtained a chair in [Logics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logics) at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel).

Nicolaus I Bernoulli was deeply influenced by his family, particularly his uncle [Jacob Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/64740) and his cousin [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784), both of whom were prominent mathematicians. Jacob Bernoulli, one of the early developers of calculus and a pioneer in the field of probability, had a significant impact on Nicolaus’s academic direction. Jacob’s work on the [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964) and the [Bernoulli theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli's_principle) provided a strong foundation for Nicolaus’s own research in probability theory.[^o5]

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) of London in 1714.

Nicolaus I Bernoulli had a rich array of personal interests that extended beyond his mathematical pursuits. Influenced by his father, who was a painter, Nicolaus developed a keen appreciation for the arts. This artistic inclination was reflected in his meticulous and creative approach to problem-solving in mathematics. He enjoyed engaging in intellectual discussions and debates, often with his family members, which helped him refine his [analytical skills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_skill). Additionally, Nicolaus had a passion for teaching and mentoring, finding great satisfaction in guiding his students and witnessing their academic growth. His diverse interests and talents made him a well-rounded individual, contributing to his legacy as a distinguished mathematician and educator.[^o7]

His most important contributions can be found in his letters, in particular to [Pierre Rémond de Montmort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Rémond_de_Montmort). In these letters, he introduced in particular the [St. Petersburg Paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Paradox). He also communicated with [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) and [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902).

Nicolaus I Bernoulli died on November 29, 1759, in Basel, [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland). The exact cause of his death is not well-documented, but it is generally believed that he suffered from a prolonged illness, possibly tuberculosis.[^n2][^n3]
