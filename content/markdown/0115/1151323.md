# Thue equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thue equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Thue equation** is a [Diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109) of the form

:::

$$f(x,y) = r,$$

where $f$ is an [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) bivariate [homogeneous polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_form) of degree at least 3 over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), and $r$ is a nonzero rational number. It is named after [Axel Thue](wikipedia://en/635533), who in 1909 [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that a Thue equation can have only finitely many solutions in [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $x$ and $y$, a result known as **Thue's theorem**.[^o2]

The Thue equation is [solvable effectively](wikipedia://en/250237): there is an explicit [bound](wikipedia://en/42693) on the solutions $x$, $y$ of the form $(C_1 r)^{C_2}$ where constants $C_1$ and $C_2$ depend only on the form $f$. A stronger result holds: if $K$ is the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) generated by the roots of $f$, then the equation has only finitely many solutions with $x$ and $y$ integers of $K$, and again these may be effectively determined.[^o3]

## Finiteness of solutions and diophantine approximation {#Finiteness_of_solutions_and_diophantine_approximation}

Thue's original proof that the equation named in his honour has finitely many solutions is through the proof of what is now known as [Thue's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thue-Siegel-Roth_theorem): it asserts that for any [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) $\alpha$ having degree $d \geq 3$ and for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists only finitely many [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) integers $p, q$ with $q > 0$ such that $|\alpha - p/q| < q^{-(d+1+\varepsilon)/2}$. Applying this theorem allows one to almost immediately deduce the finiteness of solutions. However, Thue's proof, as well as subsequent improvements by [Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602), [Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397), and [Roth](wikipedia://en/787827) were all ineffective.

## Solution algorithm {#Solution_algorithm}

Finding all solutions to a Thue equation can be achieved by a practical algorithm,[^o6] which has been implemented in the following [computer algebra systems](wikipedia://en/54427):

- in [PARI/GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PARI/GP) as functions *thueinit()* and *thue()*.
- in [Magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_%28computer_algebra_system%29) as functions *ThueObject()* and *ThueSolve()*.
- in [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica) through *Reduce\[\]*
- in [Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28software%29) through *ThueSolve()*

## Bounding the number of solutions {#Bounding_the_number_of_solutions}

While there are several effective methods to solve Thue equations (including using [Baker's method](wikipedia://en/18950800) and Skolem's *p*-adic method), these are not able to give the best theoretical bounds on the number of solutions. One may qualify an effective bound $C(f,r)$ of the Thue equation $f(x,y) = r$ by the parameters it depends on, and how "good" the dependence is.

The best result known today, essentially building on pioneering work of [Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475) and [Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_M._Schmidt),[^o8] gives a bound of the shape $C(f,r) = C \cdot (\deg f)^{1 + \omega(r)}$, where $C$ is an *absolute constant* (that is, independent of both $f$ and $r$) and $\omega(\cdot)$ is the number of distinct [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) of $r$. The most significant qualitative improvement to the theorem of Bombieri and Schmidt is due to [Stewart](wikipedia://en/29028436),[^o9] who obtained a bound of the form $C(f,r) = C \cdot (\deg f)^{1 + \omega(g)}$ where $g$ is a divisor of $r$ exceeding $|r|^{3/4}$ in [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991). It is [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that one may take the bound $C(f,r) = C(\deg f)$; that is, depending only on the *degree* of $f$ but not its [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203), and completely independent of the integer $r$ on the right hand side of the equation.

This is a weaker form of a conjecture of [Stewart](wikipedia://en/29028436), and is a special case of the [uniform boundedness conjecture for rational points](wikipedia://en/66851658). This conjecture has been proven for "small" integers $r$, where smallness is measured in terms of the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) of the form $f$, by various authors, including [Evertse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Hendrik_Evertse), [Stewart](wikipedia://en/29028436), and [Akhtari](wikipedia://en/70562226). Stewart and [Xiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Yao_Xiao) demonstrated a strong form of this conjecture, asserting that the number of solutions is absolutely bounded, holds on average (as $r$ ranges over the interval $|r| \leq Z$ with $Z \rightarrow \infty$).[^o10]
