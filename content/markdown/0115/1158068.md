# Mayer f-function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mayer f-function"}

The **Mayer f-function** is an auxiliary function that often appears in the series expansion of [thermodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic) quantities related to classical [many-particle systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-particle_system).[^o2] It is named after chemist and physicist [Joseph Edward Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Edward_Mayer).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider a system of classical particles interacting through a pair-wise potential

$$V(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})$$

where the bold labels $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ denote the continuous degrees of freedom associated with the particles, e.g.,

$$\mathbf{i}=\mathbf{r}_i$$

for spherically symmetric particles and

$$\mathbf{i}=(\mathbf{r}_i,\Omega_i)$$

for rigid non-spherical particles where $\mathbf{r}$ denotes position and $\Omega$ the orientation parametrized e.g. by [Euler angles](wikipedia://en/411492). The Mayer f-function is then defined as

$$f(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})=e^{-\beta V(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})}-1$$

where $\beta=(k_{B}T)^{-1}$ the inverse absolute [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050) in units of energy<sup>−1</sup> .

:::
